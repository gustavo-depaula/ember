The door into the library

Today the screen is an alphabetical run of author carousels. Starting over, I asked who opens it and why, and looked at what the 710 books actually are. The first round produced good rooms: ways to arrange and browse. You said the door was missing. So this page leads with four portal candidates, then keeps the seven rooms below, since any portal opens onto some of them.

Why someone opens it
J1"Where was I?"Going back to the book they're in. That's most visits.
J2"What should I read next?"Spiritual reading is one book at a time, ~15 min a day. The choice comes every few weeks and it's a serious one.
J3"I need something for…"A need of the soul, not a subject heading: praying better, a death, Our Lady, confession.
J4"Where's the Confessions?"A known title or author. Search, quickly.
J5"What does the Church say about…"Looking something up in the catechisms, the Summa, the Encyclopedia, papal documents. Consulted, not read.
J6"What fits now?"November is for the Holy Souls; Advent starts 29 Nov; St. Martin's day has his Life.
J7"Who are these people?"Getting to know the tradition: who wrote when, and who to read from each age.
Things the data says that the screen ignores

The plan of life already has "Leitura Espiritual", 15 minutes a day. Its flow says "Abra o livro escolhido", but nothing in the app chooses a book or links back to one. The library and the daily practice are strangers.

Books are used three different ways. Some you read through (Imitação, 4.3 h). Some you pray from, a page a day (Ligório's Meditações has "28 de Outubro. Festa dos Apóstolos São Simão e São Tadeu"). Some you consult (Trent, the Summa, 512 questions). One cover grid treats all three as the same.

Length is the honest unit. At 15 min a day, Imitação is 17 days, Introdução à Vida Devota 26, Confissões 43. Segredo de Maria is one evening. Nobody shows this today.

The portal: four candidates

Every arrangement in the first round answers "how do I find a book". None answers why I'd want to. A portal is a single gesture that does that before any shelf appears, the way the sentence did for Por perto. Four candidates, each grounded in something only this library has: the books' own words, and the saints who wrote and read them.

Portal A

Tolle, lege

You meet a book through a page, not a cover.

Augustine was converted by opening a book at random (Confissões VIII, 12, in the library). The screen opens on one page, set like paper, from a book you can read in your language. Who wrote it stays hidden until you ask. "Ler daqui" drops you into the chapter; swipe for another page. The shelves are one tap below. Tap the arrows.

Portal B

Pergunte aos livros

Bring what's on your heart; the library answers in its authors' own words.

One field: "O que você está vivendo?" The answers aren't books; they're passages. On sadness, Sales has a whole chapter, Da Tristeza e da Dor. Kempis and Ligório each have a paragraph. Every passage is a door into its chapter. Nothing is generated; it's retrieval over the 400 books readable in Portuguese.

Portal C

Escolha um mestre

You don't pick a book. You put yourself under a teacher.

The tradition's way into reading is a person: one becomes a disciple of St. Francis de Sales, or of St. Alphonsus. The portal is a deck of masters as holy cards, each with a line in their own words and where to begin. Choosing one makes their shelf your school, and the Leitura Espiritual follows them. Swipe the deck.

Portal D

Os livros que fizeram santos

Read what the saints read, at the page that changed them.

Many conversions turned on a book, and several of those books are here. The portal tells those stories, each a few lines long, and opens the book at the very place. St. Teresa of Ávila read Augustine's garden scene and saw herself in it. St. Thérèse knew the Imitação nearly by heart. It connects the saints' pages to the library, which nothing does today.

Behind the door: seven rooms (first round)

These are the arrangements from the first round. Read them as the rooms a portal leads into, not as the front page.

III

O itinerário

A road, in order: the reading list a spiritual director would hand you.

The tradition doesn't read everything at once. It goes from conversion, to prayer, to union. The classics are laid out as stations on a path, each with why it comes there and how many days it takes. You see where you are and what's next. The rest of the catalog sits off the road, under "Para ir além".

IV

O tempo da Igreja

The library follows the liturgical year, like the rest of Ember.

The page changes with the calendar. Late October, it leads with November, the month of the Holy Souls. Then come the saints of the month who wrote, or were written about: St. Leo's sermons on the 10th, St. Martin's Life by Sulpicius Severus on the 11th (50 min, in Portuguese). Next, what to start before Advent on 29 Nov. The calendar authority already exists.

V

O que a alma pede

You say what you need, in a sentence; the library answers like a director.

This is the Por perto sentence, but its first word is a need rather than a subject: rezar melhor, sofrer bem, amar Nossa Senhora, preparar a morte, conhecer a fé. The second word is how much time you have. Where the book has one, the answer quotes a line, so you hear the author before you commit. Try the words.

My pick for the door

A page, with a question above it. Portal A (Tolle, lege) is the gesture: the screen opens on words, not covers. It's cheap, it's unlike any other book app, and it has an Augustinian root. Its search field becomes Portal B's question, "O que você está vivendo?", answered in passages. A shows what the library is like; B lets you ask it something.

  1. Under the door: your book, tied to Leitura Espiritual (room I), and one band that follows the year (room IV).
  2. Portal C, the masters, becomes the row of faces that opens the tradition (room VII). That's where the Fathers, St. Thomas and Denis live.
  3. Portal D, the saints' books, is too finite to be a door. It fits as the passage pool for A: the garden page of the Confissões is the first page anyone should open.
  4. Build order: A first (passage pool + page view), then the bands from rooms I and IV, then B's passage index, which is the big build.

If you'd rather the door be a person than a page, swap A for C. The rest holds.
