The three witnesses

The Old Testament reached the Church in three forms: the Hebrew, the Greek of the Apostles, and Jerome’s Latin. Under each Hebrew verse stand the Septuagint and the Vulgate, and the reader’s own language, matched on the shared verse address the reader already uses.

  • Isaiah 7:14: the Hebrew הָעַלְמָה (“the young woman”), the Greek ἡ παρθένος and the Latin virgo, which St Matthew quotes (Mt 1:23). Tap the Hebrew word to study it.
  • Hebrew, Greek and Latin in full ink; under them the reader’s own language, muted, chosen with With … (Douay, Knox, CPDV, Matos Soares; the Ave Maria is read online, so it waits for the app), each saying what it was translated from.
  • Psalm 100:3 shows why it matters: the Vulgate and the Douay follow the written “and not we ourselves”; Knox and Matos Soares the read “his we are”.
  • Verse numbers are the Hebrew’s, the Vulgate’s beside them where they differ (Malachi 3:19 · Vulg. 4:1). Where the Vulgate divides the same words otherwise — Psalm 100:1–2, whose title it makes a verse of its own — the rows show the verse of the same number, and overlap.
