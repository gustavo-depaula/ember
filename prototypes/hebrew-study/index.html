Studying the Hebrew

The Westminster Leningrad Codex in the reader you already have: the page, a word taken apart to its root, the interlinear, and the Hebrew beside the Septuagint, the Vulgate and the Douay. All from real data: the WLC of Open Scriptures, TAHOT, Brown–Driver–Briggs, Strong’s, STEPBible, and Ember’s own Vulgate, Brenton, Douay, Knox, CPDV and Matos Soares.

❦
A

The page

A verse to a line, right to left, the codex’s paragraph letters and the Vulgate’s numbers where they differ; Psalm 23 in its accent lines; one remembered control for the marks: Letters · Vowels · Chant.

 B

Word study

A word on the sheet: its pieces with their glosses, its sound, its grammar in three registers, its accent, its root and the family of words built on it, and the dictionaries. Try בְּרֵאשִׁית, רֹעִי in Psalm 23, the Name, and the ° word in Psalm 100.

 C

Interlinear

Word over gloss, flowing right to left; cut into pieces, with sound and grammar if you like.

 D

Three witnesses

Isaiah 7:10–16: the Hebrew, and under each verse the Septuagint and the Vulgate, and your own language — Douay, Knox, CPDV or Matos Soares — saying what it was translated from.
