# Haydock's commentary in Brazilian Portuguese: conventions for this work

These conventions govern this work over the general ones wherever the two differ.

Fr. George Leo Haydock's notes on the Douay-Rheims Bible (1859 edition), a Catholic commentary compiled from Challoner, Witham, Calmet, the Fathers and others, with an introduction to each book of the Bible. This "book" is a working layout of the notes on one or more books of the Bible: each chapter file holds the notes on one chapter of the Bible (`…-c000` is that book's introduction), and each paragraph is one note. The notes are independent of one another: each explains one verse and is read on its own, under that verse, in a Bible reader. Translate each note so that it stands alone.

## What must not change (the app parses these)

- **The key.** Every paragraph opens with its key, `⟦3:16 #1⟧` or `⟦intro #1⟧`. Copy it exactly, at the start of the same paragraph.
- **The verse.** The first note on a verse carries, after its key, the verse in the Matos Soares Bible: `⟨MS 3:16: …⟩`. It is context, already in Portuguese: copy it character for character and never translate, correct or drop it. It is not part of the note.
- **Apparatus blocks.** A `{ … |}` block (Greek and Latin apparatus, hidden when the note is shown) is copied character for character, in the same place in the sentence. Never translate, repair or drop one.
- **Voices.** ` — ` (space, em dash, space) separates the voices of a note. Keep exactly as many as the source, in the same order. Do not introduce ` — ` anywhere else; for an aside use commas or parentheses.
- **Attributions.** The parenthesis that closes a voice, `(Witham)` or `(St. Chrysostom, hom. xiv.)`, stays where it is, closing the same voice.
- **No translator's notes.** The notes are stored one string per note, with no place for a footnote: add none.

## Language

- Brazilian Portuguese with full diacritics and post-1990 orthography. A clear, slightly formal register, as a Catholic commentary reads; modern syntax, not an imitation of nineteenth-century English.
- Say what the note says: its apologetics and its judgments on Protestant readings are translated as they stand, neither softened nor sharpened.
- Quotation marks are “ ” and, inside them, ‘ ’. The source rarely marks its quotations; add marks only where Portuguese needs them to be read.

## The lemma

Most notes open with the words of the verse being explained, ending in a full stop ("For the remission of sins."). The note's English lemma follows the Douay-Rheims; the `⟨MS …⟩` verse is the Matos Soares Bible, the Portuguese Bible the reader has open. Both translate the Vulgate. Later notes on the same verse have no `⟨MS …⟩` of their own: use the one on the verse's first note.

- Give the lemma in the Matos Soares wording when it has the same words.
- When the note's point depends on a wording Matos Soares does not have (a Latin or Greek turn of phrase, a word the note goes on to discuss), translate the lemma literally from the note instead, so the comment still makes sense.
- Words of the verse quoted again inside the note follow the same rule. Other Scripture quoted in a note is translated from the note's own English.

## Names and references

- Scripture references keep chapter:verse with a colon and get the Portuguese name of the book, written out as the source writes it out: `2 Corinthians 5:13` → `2 Coríntios 5:13`. The Douay's Vulgate names stay Vulgate names: 1–4 Kings → 1–4 Reis, 1–2 Paralipomenon → 1–2 Paralipômenos, 1–2 Esdras, Ecclesiasticus → Eclesiástico, Canticle of Canticles → Cântico dos Cânticos, Isaias → Isaías, Osee → Oseias, Apocalypse → Apocalipse, Acts → Atos. Psalm numbers are the Vulgate's; leave them.
- Saints and Fathers take their Portuguese names: St. Augustine → Santo Agostinho, St. John Chrysostom / St. Chrysostom → São João Crisóstomo, St. Jerome → São Jerônimo, St. Gregory → São Gregório, St. Ambrose → Santo Ambrósio, Ven. Bede → São Beda, St. Thomas → Santo Tomás, Origen → Orígenes, Tertullian → Tertuliano, Eusebius → Eusébio, Theophylactus → Teofilacto, Euthymius → Eutímio, St. Irenaeus → Santo Irineu, St. Cyril → São Cirilo, St. Basil → São Basílio, St. Hilary → Santo Hilário, St. Epiphanius → Santo Epifânio.
- Commentators known by a Latinised name take the form Portuguese scholarship uses: Menochius → Menóquio, Tirinus → Tirino, Estius → Éstio, Maldonatus → Maldonado, Cornelius a Lapide → Cornélio a Lápide, Jansenius → Jansênio, Nicholas of Lyra → Nicolau de Lira, Grotius → Grócio. Modern surnames do not change: Witham, Challoner, Calmet, Bristow, Worthington, Haydock, Du Hamel, Pastorini, Polus, Tillemont.
- Citations of works inside an attribution keep their Latin abbreviations (`hom. xiv.`, `lib. ii. de Trin.`, `in Matt.`); English ones are translated (`b. ii. chap. 3` → `liv. II, cap. 3`).
- Greek, Latin and Hebrew words quoted in the running text stay as the source has them.
- Dates: `about the year A.D. 29.` → `por volta do ano 29 d.C.`; `Year of the World` → `Ano do mundo`.

## Terms already settled (the New Testament groups)

The journals of the New Testament agree on these; keep them.

- Holy Ghost / Holy Spirit → Espírito Santo. The Church / the Catholic Church / the Fathers → a Igreja / a Igreja Católica / os Padres. Church militant / triumphant → Igreja militante / triunfante.
- faith, hope, charity, grace → fé, esperança, caridade, graça. works / good works / works of the law → obras / boas obras / obras da lei. penance / repentance → penitência / arrependimento.
- testament / covenant → testamento / aliança. Mosaic law / law of Moses → lei mosaica / lei de Moisés. circumcision / uncircumcision → circuncisão / incircuncisão. Gentiles / heathens → gentios / pagãos.
- Redeemer / Mediator / Advocate → Redentor / Mediador / Advogado.
- Septuagint → Septuaginta; the Seventy (its translators) → os Setenta. Latin Vulgate → Vulgata latina.
- A.D. → d.C.
