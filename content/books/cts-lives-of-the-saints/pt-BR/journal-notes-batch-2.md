## 2. Non-Obvious Translation Decisions

- **Measurements preserved as in source, not converted**: "twenty miles" (Cafasso) kept as "vinte milhas", "2,800 ft." kept as "2.800 pés", "16-stone man" and "200 lb. sacks" (Bernadette/Traynor) kept as "16 stones (cerca de 100 quilos)" and "200 libras" rather than being silently converted to metric — per the instruction to preserve factual details exactly as given, not "correct" or convert them. A parenthetical metric approximation was added only for "16 stones" since that unit is unfamiliar to PT-BR readers and the source itself is already giving a descriptive aside, not a precise clinical figure.
- **Saint Clare chapter**: "Sciffi family" kept as-is (proper name, untranslated). "generosi" (Latin) kept inline with dash-gloss as source does ("de alta linhagem"). The chapter's own Latin tags (*Pater ejus miles...*, *vivo sermone*, *Quem amor humanavit*, *non sibi soli vivere sed aliis proficere*, *sciebat enim quid potest amor*, *collegium divini amoris*, *egregius Domini Jaculator*) all kept in Latin with the source's own English gloss translated into Portuguese immediately after, preserving the source's consistent glossing pattern.
- St Clare's brother "Martin" → "Martinho", sisters "Agnes"/"Beatrice" → "Inês"/"Beatriz" (standard PT-BR devotional forms, consistent with how Agnes of Assisi is otherwise rendered as "Santa Inês de Assis" in Catholic PT-BR usage).
- "St Bonaventure" → "São Boaventura"; "Bd. Agnes of Bohemia" → "Beata Inês da Boêmia" (standard renderings).
- The closing historical NOTE in the Clare chapter (on the Perugia exile and the 1202 battle) was treated as part of the author's own text (not a later editor's apparatus) and translated in full, since it reads as authorial commentary integral to the piece, not detachable scholarly annotation.
- **St Dominic chapter**: kept the untranslated proper name spelling choices found in the source but normalized clearly garbled ones to standard historical French/Spanish forms consistent with how the book treats other names elsewhere (e.g. "Verfeuil" → "Verfeil", "La Penne d'Ajen" → "La Penne d'Agenais", "Raoul of Fontefroide" → "Raul de Fontfroide", "Foulques" → "Fulco") — these are recognized standard spellings of the same historical persons/places the source itself intends, not a change of fact.
- The asterisked footnote on the Dominican Inquisition (originally CTS editorial/explanatory, but integral to the author's own argument distinguishing Dominic personally from the later Inquisition) was kept in place at the same paragraph break, matching the source's own placement, rather than being dropped — judged as author-integral clarification, not a detachable editor's citation apparatus.
- "de iure Earl of Leicester" kept as "de iure Conde de Leicester" (Latin legal term kept inline, standard usage).
- Bartholomew de Martyribus quotation (Archbishop of Braga) kept as a blockquote-style translated paragraph in flowing prose to match the source's own un-blockquoted inline presentation (source does not use `>` there, so none was added).
- **St Bernadette chapter**: this is a first-person popular apologetic essay (not a scholarly hagiography); tone kept accessible/persuasive/rhetorical to match the source's own conversational register, including its rhetorical questions and short punchy sentences.
- Bernadette's own testimony (the long first-person quotation of what she saw) was translated as continuous quoted paragraphs preserving the source's own paragraph breaks exactly, including the quotation marks style.
- "M. Peyramale, the Dean of Lourdes" rendered "o vigário-geral Peyramale, o Deão de Lourdes" — added "vigário-geral" only because "Dean" alone reads ambiguously in PT-BR outside a cathedral-chapter context; this is a minor accessibility gloss, not an added fact (Peyramale's historical title, Dean of the Lourdes parish, is preserved).
- Scripture paraphrase "Neither will they believe if one should rise from the dead" (cf. Lk 16:31) translated directly rather than substituted with an official PT-BR Bible translation's exact wording, since the source itself paraphrases rather than quotes verbatim.
- Film title "The Song of Bernadette" → "O Cântico de Bernadete" (standard PT-BR release title for the Franz Werfel novel/film).
- Nihil obstat/Imprimatur block at the end of the Bernadette chapter kept in Latin/formulaic form per batch-1 convention (established in journal-notes-batch-1.md); only the date was localized to PT-BR date format ("30 de novembro de 1945") since this is formatting, not a factual change.
- **St Joseph Cafasso chapter**: "the new Aloysius" → "o novo Luís Gonzaga" (St. Aloysius Gonzaga's standard PT-BR name), since "Aloysius" alone is not a recognizable devotional reference in Portuguese; later in the same chapter "St. Aloysius Gonzaga" is likewise rendered "São Luís Gonzaga" for consistency.
- "Cure of Ars" → "Cura d'Ars" (consistent with batch 1).
- "Heroic Act" (in favor of the Souls in Purgatory) → "Ato Heroico" (standard Catholic devotional term, capitalized as in source).
- The two Latin/Scripture-flavored aphorisms quoted without attribution ("For venerable old age is not that of a long time...") — from the Book of Wisdom (Wis 4:8-13) — translated directly in the flowing prose style the source uses (not set off as a blockquote, since the source itself does not do so).
- **St Peregrine chapter**: this file is largely devotional apparatus (novena, litany, prayers, sick-call preparation) rather than narrative hagiography. Standard, highly recognizable liturgical formulas were rendered in their standard PT-BR Catholic forms: "Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory [be]" → "Pai-Nosso, Ave-Maria, Glória"; the Litany responses ("Lord have mercy", "Christ hear us", "Lamb of God...") rendered with the standard PT-BR litany wording used in Brazilian Catholic devotional books.
- Corrected the source's structural markdown to match its own convention exactly: only "Litany in Honour of St. Peregrine" is an actual `###` heading in the source; "Novena to St. Peregrine", "Prayer to St. Peregrine", "Preparation for a Sick Call", "Prayers For the Sick", "Act of Resignation...", "Prayer for a Happy Death" are all plain-text section titles with no `#`/`##`/`###` markup in the source — kept them as plain text (not converted to markdown headings) to preserve the source's actual structure.
- "Apostle of Emilia" → "Apóstolo da Emília" (region name, kept recognizable).
- The Litany's "V./R." versicle-response format kept as "V./R." (standard Latin liturgical abbreviation convention used in PT-BR devotional books too).
- Prayers addressed to God are all in the vós/vosso form (formal liturgical register standard for PT-BR devotional/prayer translations), consistent with how prayer text is rendered elsewhere in this book's other chapters.
- "sick call" (Catholic term for a priest's visit to bring the sacraments to someone gravely ill) rendered "Visita ao Doente" rather than a literal "chamado de doente", to match standard PT-BR pastoral terminology for this specific rite/occasion.
