## Translation Decisions

- **saint-camillus-de-lellis.md**: Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur block translated the ecclesiastical titles to their Portuguese equivalents (Censor Deputatus kept in Latin, "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis" kept in Latin as a formal Latin title matching the source's own choice to render it in Latin rather than English).
- **the-adventurous-nun.md**: The historical slur "nigger-lovers" (used by the author to describe how civil-rights defenders were mocked) was rendered as "amigo dos negros" used pejorativamente, avoiding reproduction of the actual slur or its Portuguese equivalent, while "negrophiles" (a period-appropriate, less inflammatory term) was translated literally as "negrófilos." Noted here per the modernization-disclosure instruction. Distances (miles) converted to approximate kilometers for readability (e.g. "50 miles" → "80 quilômetros").
- **saint-pius-x-460.md**: Character names (Father Lawrence, Frank, Mary, Tony, Margaret, Vin, Mrs. O'Shaughnessy) are contemporary Australian lay characters, not saints — kept untranslated. Distances converted to metric (four miles → seis quilômetros). The Latin liturgical/canonical phrases (Accedant qui ordinandi sunt, Instaurare Omnia In Christo, Haerent Animo, Ne Temere, Pascendi) kept in Latin with Portuguese glosses as the source does.
- **saint-joseph.md**: The two author footnotes (De La Broise and Orsini/Maldonatus/Prat citations) are the author's own scholarly apparatus supporting quoted material, not a later editor's annotations — kept, converted to markdown footnote syntax, with the cited book titles left in their original English/Latin per standard bibliographic practice.
- **poet-priest-martyr.md**: English/Elizabethan poetry by St. Robert Southwell translated for sense and register rather than attempting to preserve meter/rhyme, consistent with a prose biography quoting verse. "Persuivant" (Tudor-era term for a pursuivant/priest-hunter) has no compact Portuguese equivalent; rendered as "agente" (de captura de sacerdotes) with the concept explained on first use.
- **saint-joseph-calasanctius.md**: Encyclopedic Butler's-Lives register preserved (dense single paragraphs, formal tone). "Don"/"Donna" (Spanish honorifics) kept as "Dom"/"Dona." Place name "Petralta de la Sal" kept exactly as spelled in the English source (likely a variant/OCR form of Peralta de la Sal) rather than silently corrected.
- **saint-peregrine-the-cancer-saint.md**: Devotional material (novena, litany, prayers) rendered in standard modern Brazilian Catholic prayer register (formal "vós" address, matching how such prayers are conventionally printed in pt-BR devotionals) rather than colloquial "você," for consistency with the reverent tone of the rest of the book.
