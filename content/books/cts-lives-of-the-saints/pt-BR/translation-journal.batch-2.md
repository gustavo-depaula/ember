## Translation Decisions

- All eight files preserve paragraph structure, heading levels, and blockquote/italic conventions from the English source one-to-one.
- **saint-stanislaus-kostka.md**: Distances given in miles were converted to approximate metric equivalents (e.g. "a thousand miles" → "mil e seiscentos quilômetros", "three-hundred mile walk" → "quase quinhentos quilômetros") to read naturally in modern Portuguese, since the original numbers are round approximations rather than precise historical figures.
- **eugene-de-mazenod.md**: The piece has an unusual layout (title, then plain-text subtitle "Founder of the Oblates of Mary Immaculate", author byline, a bare roman numeral "I", then a plain-text section title "Missionary of Provence" — none of these use markdown heading syntax in the source). This structure was preserved exactly, translated as plain text, not converted to markdown headings.
- **saint-augustine-bishop-of-hippo.md**: This is a dense, quotation-heavy Butler's-Lives-style text. Long inline quotations (from the *Confessions*, from Augustine's letters and sermons) were translated in full, keeping the original's inline-quote-in-running-prose style (no blockquote markdown) since that matches the source's own formatting.
- **saint-patrick.md**: This chapter is a rhetorical oration/sermon (not a biographical narrative like the others), including an extended passage on Irish national history, the Penal Laws, and a polemical dialogue against "naturalism." Translated at full rhetorical register, preserving the emphatic repetition and direct-address style. The traditional hymn "St. Patrick's Breastplate" was translated as free verse rather than substituted with an existing standard Portuguese liturgical translation, to stay faithful to this source text's specific wording. The orphaned footnote marker after "Beside this church,*" (no footnote text follows in the source) was preserved as-is (asterisk kept, no note fabricated).
- **saint-brigid.md**: Old Irish/British units and measures ("two miles from Dundalk," "thirty miles") converted to approximate metric. Retained "gael" (lowercase, as in source) as the term for the Irish people/nation.
- **saint-cataldus.md**: This file had an author footnote embedded mid-paragraph via OCR ("*Tullabeg, Tullamore" identifying the Jesuit school near Rahan). Restored this as a proper markdown footnote (`[^1]`) at the correct anchor point rather than dropping it, since it is the author's own explanatory aside, not a later editor's scholarly apparatus. Distances converted to metric.
- **saint-benedict-abbot.md**: Classic Alban Butler hagiography text with the author's own footnotes embedded via OCR mid-sentence (on "Sublacum*" explaining the Italian form "Subiaco," and on "Vicovara*" explaining its ancient name "Varronis Vicus"). Both were restored as proper markdown footnotes at their correct anchor points and translated, per the rule that the author's own notes are kept (only later editors' scholarly apparatus is dropped). Miles converted to metric throughout. One stray, unexplained asterisk in the source after "wisely unlettered.*" (with no corresponding footnote text anywhere nearby) was silently dropped since there was no note content to preserve.
- **saint-lucy-of-syracuse.md**: Short devotional piece with prayers; translated the two closing prayers in full devotional register, matching the pattern used for prayers elsewhere in the corpus (formal "Tu"/"Vós"-style address, here rendered with formal but warm modern address consistent with other prayer translations in this book).
- No modernization of ethnonyms was needed in this batch (no "mahométans"-type terms encountered).
- No editor (as opposed to author) footnotes were encountered in this batch; the imprimatur/Nihil Obstat blocks at the end of several files (Latin ecclesiastical formalities, place names, censor names) were left largely untranslated/unmodified, consistent with how such standardized front/back matter is conventionally kept in its original administrative form.
