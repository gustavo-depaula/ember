## 2. Non-obvious translation decisions

- **Saints' names**: Standardized to Brazilian devotional forms per the instructions — "São João Maria Vianney, Cura d'Ars" (kept "Cura d'Ars" as the fixed epithet rather than "Pároco de Ars"), "São Tomás Moro" (not "Thomas More"), "Santa Rosa de Lima", "Santa Margarida Clitherow" (title used "Margarida" but body text mostly kept "Margaret" per how the original English narrative refers to her informally — on reflection I standardized to "Margaret" throughout the body since the source's own register treats her by her English first name colloquially; the title is Portuguized), "São Luís IX, Rei da França", "Santas Perpétua e Felicidade", "São Venceslau" (not "Wenceslas"), "São Gerardo Maiela" (not "Gerard Majella" — this is the standard Lusophone Redemptorist usage), "Santo Antônio" (of Padua, untitled in source file).
- **St. Martin de Porres**: the source text (Saint Rose of Lima) names him only as "St. Martin de Porres"; rendered as "São Martinho de Porres" (standard Portuguese form), not "de Lima" — corrected an initial drafting error before finalizing.
- **Place names**: Kept in original/local form per instructions (Ars, Dardilly, Écully, Chelsea, Muro, Lima, Prague/Praga [Portuguese exonym used, as it's extremely well established], Bohemia/Boêmia [likewise]). Czech place and person names in "Saint Wenceslas" were rendered with their standard Czech diacritics (e.g., Stará Boleslav, Přemyslides/Přemyslidas, Spytihněv, Bořivoj, Drahomíra, Radslav→ kept "Radslav" as source spells it, Přibislava) since the source itself is inconsistent/anglicized in spelling (e.g., "Boleslas" for "Boleslav") — I standardized toward the historically correct Czech forms used in modern Portuguese-language reference works, while keeping the English source's own English-style forms (Boleslas, Wenceslas) for the article's title and running English-derived narrative voice... **Correction/self-note**: on reflection this could be seen as inconsistent — see below.
- **Self-correction note on Wenceslas orthography**: I mixed anglicized narrative forms (Boleslas, kept from the source in most of the body) with corrected Czech diacritics for some proper nouns (Přemyslidas, Stará Boleslav, Drahomíra, Bořivoj). This was a judgment call to make place/dynasty names recognizable in standard Portuguese-language historical reference while preserving the felt register of the English source's transliteration for personal names central to the narrative (Wenceslas → Venceslau, Boleslas → Boleslau, per the standard Portuguese royal-name convention). Flagging this here in case a future editor wants full internal consistency one way or the other.
- **Latin/liturgical phrases**: Kept "Indignatio principis mors" (Latin) with its own English gloss translated into Portuguese, following the source's own bilingual pattern. Kept "peine forte et dure" (Law French) untranslated, as the source itself treats it as a foreign technical term and explains it in the surrounding English prose (translated).
- **Perpetua and Felicitas martyrdom account**: This is an ancient (2nd/3rd century) text with embedded quoted visions; no separate Latin liturgical formulas appear beyond the closing note that they are "mentioned in the canon of the Mass" (translated as "são mencionadas no cânone da Missa"). Footnote markers (endnotes citing Mabillon and Victor) were kept as author/editorial citations, since they are part of the source's own critical apparatus rather than being a later publisher's unrelated annotation.
- **Poetry/verse passages**: The "Good King Wenceslas" carol and the "Svatý Václave" canticle were translated as prose-like verse preserving line breaks and meaning rather than attempting rhymed metrical Portuguese verse, since a rhymed re-translation risked distorting the literal content; this matches how the source itself presents them as devotional/quoted material rather than requiring singable meter.
- **Footnotes**: All footnotes in Saint Rose of Lima (scripture citations, biographer citations, hymn sources) are the author's own apparatus, not later editorial glosses — kept and renumbered sequentially as markdown footnotes.
- **Currency**: "£10,000" in The Mothers' Saint (letter from "J.M.") rendered as "dez mil libras" (spelled out) rather than a bare figure, matching the informal, quoted-letter register of the passage.
- **Modernizations**: None of the outdated terms in this batch required modernization for offensiveness (no equivalent to "mahométans"/"Muslims" case arose). Left period vocabulary (e.g., "sarracenos" for "Saracens" in Saint Louis IX) as-is, since it reflects the medieval narrative's own historical vocabulary and is standard in Portuguese historical writing about the Crusades.
- **"Espelho" / "Vaso" / "Estrela" titles (Saint Anthony and You)**: Capitalized as fixed devotional epithets throughout (Espelho de Abstinência, Vaso de Pureza, Estrela de Santidade, Modelo de Obediência, Arca da Aliança, Mestre da Verdade, Plantador de Virtude, Médico das Almas, Guia dos que Erram, Pregador da Graça) to preserve their function as litany invocations repeated at the end of each section.
