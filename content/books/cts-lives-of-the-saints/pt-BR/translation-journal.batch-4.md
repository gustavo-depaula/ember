## Translation Decisions

- **padre-pio.md**: Modernized "the Puglie" to "a Puglia" (standard Portuguese form of the Italian region). Converted the imperial measurement "twenty feet high" (statue of St. Francis) to "seis metros" (metric, standard in pt-BR). Kept Italian book titles (*Per la Storia*, *Fatti Nuova*, *Fino alla Meta*) untranslated and italicized, matching the source's own treatment of them as titles. Rendered the publisher's note and Imprimatur/translator credit lines in Portuguese for consistency with the rest of the book's front/back matter conventions.
- **saint-vincent-pallotti.md**: Converted "£200" (pounds) to "200 libras" — kept as a currency reference rather than converting to reais/euros, since it's a historical amount tied to the period narrative, following the general practice elsewhere in the corpus of preserving original monetary units in translation.
- **saint-margaret-mary-alacoque.md**: The source text has a garbled ending — the "Twelve Promises of the Sacred Heart" section appears to be missing its heading (it starts mid-sentence with "...of the Sacred Heart to St. Margaret Mary concerning..."). Preserved this as-is with an ellipsis rather than inventing a heading not present in the source, per instructions to keep the same heading structure as the source. Fixed one internal typo/gender-agreement slip during translation (a mistranslation of "was solemnly blessed" applied to "capela," corrected to feminine agreement — "foi solenemente benzida uma capela").
- **saint-mary-mazzarello.md**: "Nizza Montferrato" (an old/dialectal spelling in the English source) rendered as "Nizza Monferrato," the standard modern spelling of the Piedmontese town, for clarity.
- **saint-clare-of-assisi.md**: This chapter is unusually dense with untranslated Latin phrases and quotations (*Pater ejus miles...*, *vivo sermone*, *non sibi soli vivere sed aliis proficere*, *sciebat enim quid potest amor*, *mulier fortis*, *egregius Domini Jaculator*, *collegium divini amoris*, etc.) — all kept in Latin per the instruction to preserve inline Latin matching the source's pattern, with the English translations that the source itself provides rendered into Portuguese immediately after. "St Angelo di Panso" appears to be a source-text variant/typo for the historical convent of Sant'Angelo di Panzo; rendered as "Santo Ângelo di Panzo" (the commonly attested name) since it is a proper place name.
- **saint-anthony-and-you.md**: This is a devotional/catechetical essay structured around the invocations of the Litany of St. Anthony, each followed by "St. Anthony, [Title], pray for us!" — rendered consistently as "Santo Antônio, [Título], rogai por nós!" throughout, matching standard Portuguese litany register (formal "rogai" imperative, not "reze"). The magazine title "Cordette" was kept untranslated as a proper name (a real historical Franciscan youth publication), with its descriptive parenthetical translated.
- **saint-joseph-cafasso.md**: Converted "twenty miles from Turin" to "cerca de trinta e dois quilômetros" and "2,800 ft. high" (mountain of the Sanctuary of St. Ignatius) to "850 metros de altitude" — both converted to metric per standard pt-BR convention, consistent with how measurements are handled elsewhere in this book's translation.
- **saint-raymund-nonnatus.md**: Modernized "Mohammedans" to "muçulmanos" per the skill's default policy for outdated/potentially offensive ethnonyms/religious labels, as flagged by the task instructions. Kept the Latin gloss "*non natus*" ("not born") inline as the source does, since it is the etymological explanation for the saint's name. "Cardona (Cerdagne)" — the source's parenthetical geographic clarification appears to conflate the town of Cardona with the historical region of Cerdagne/Cerdanya; rendered literally as "Cardona (Cerdanya)" preserving the source's own parenthetical without attempting to correct what may be a geographic imprecision in the original English text.
