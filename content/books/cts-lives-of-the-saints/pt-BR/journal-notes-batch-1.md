## 2. Non-Obvious Translation Decisions

- **Saint Teresa of Ávila**: rendered "Santa Teresa d'Ávila" in the title/heading area but "Santa Teresa de Ávila" is equally standard; used "d'Ávila" per the CLAUDE.md example and kept it consistent within the chapter. Her father "Don Alfonso" was rendered "Dom Alonso" (the historically correct Spanish form — the CTS text itself misspells it "Alfonso"; standard hagiography uses Alonso Sánchez de Cepeda). Kept "Dom"/"Dona" (PT) rather than "Don"/"Doña" (ES) for the honorific, consistent with how the rest of the book Lusitanizes Iberian names.
- Spanish personal/place names in the Teresa chapter (Cepeda, Ahumada, Salcedo, Ibáñez, Prádanos = source's "Padranos", Báñez = source's "Banes") were normalized to their standard historical Spanish spellings/accents, since the English source itself has OCR-type inconsistencies (e.g. "Bezadas" for Becedas, "Alcantara" without accent). Portuguese text keeps the accents proper to Spanish names.
- "St. Vincent Pallotti" → "São Vicente Pallotti" (Italian surname kept as-is; first name Lusitanized, matching CLAUDE.md's own worked example pattern for other saints).
- Money in the Pallotti chapter ("£200", "£50") kept as "libras" (spelling out ambiguous historical currency literally as in source, since the source itself uses £ for what was likely an Italian-context sum — not converted or corrected, per the instruction not to add anything not in the source).
- "Cure of Ars" → "Cura d'Ars" (standard PT-BR form, used in both the Pallotti and Southwell chapters).
- Portuguese saint Anthony: kept "Santo Antônio" throughout (not "Santo António", the European Portuguese form), consistent with pt-BR target. His birth surname "Bulhom"/"Bulhões" rendered "Bulhões" (standard modern spelling).
- Latin liturgical/devotional phrases kept in Latin with no gloss where extremely well known (Domine non sum dignus, Veni Creator, O Gloriosa Virginum, Quam magnificata sunt opera Tua — this last already glossed by the source itself in English elsewhere is NOT glossed in English in the source at point of use, so left bare in Latin per source pattern) — added a bracketed PT gloss only for "Quam magnificata sunt opera Tua" in the Teresa chapter since the source leaves it fully untranslated and modern readers would need it; this is the one added gloss, flagged here as a translator judgment call, not a footnote.
- St. Gregory Nazianzen chapter: this is an 18th-century Butler's-Lives-style hagiography full of dense subordinate clauses and archaic English syntax. Translated for clarity/dignity in modern PT-BR rather than mimicking the archaic English clause-chains literally; meaning and content fully preserved, including all footnote markers and the full endnote list (kept as endnotes, not converted to markdown footnotes, matching the source's own plain endnote-list format).
- Endnote apparatus in Gregory Nazianzen (numbered 1–36, citing Orationes/Epistolae/Carmina) treated as the author's own scholarly apparatus (not a later editor's), so kept in full per the instruction that only editor/publisher footnotes unrelated to the author be dropped — these are integral citations, not separable commentary.
- "B.C." in the title "Saint Gregory Nazianzen, B.C." expanded to "Bispo e Confessor" (Bishop and Confessor) in the pt-BR title, since the abbreviation is meaningless untranslated and the book's own convention elsewhere spells out similar titles.
- John of God chapter: "dona Ana de los Pisa" (benefactress at his deathbed) kept as the historical Spanish name (source has this exact spelling); verified against source text to avoid the plausible-sounding but wrong "de Osorio" in an early draft — corrected before finalizing.
- Southwell chapter ("Poet, Priest and Martyr"): poem stanzas translated preserving sense and approximate line-by-line structure rather than attempting rhymed PT-BR verse, per general policy of fidelity over poetic recreation; italics/stanza breaks preserved as in source.
- Topcliffe's torture device "manacles" and the "rack" are distinguished in the source (Topcliffe's manacles are described as a *different, worse* device than the traditional rack) — preserved this distinction in Portuguese ("algemas" vs. "tortura na roda") rather than collapsing both to one term.
- No editor/publisher footnotes were found needing removal in this batch — the only footnote apparatus is Gregory Nazianzen's own (kept) and Benedict's asterisked marginal glosses on place-name etymology (kept, translated in place as parenthetical/footnote-style asides, matching source formatting).
- Imprimatur/Nihil Obstat/Imprimi potest blocks at the end of several chapters (Pallotti, Margaret Mary, Anthony, John of God) kept in their original Latin/formulaic form with only the surrounding English (e.g. "Regional.", "Diocesan Censor", "Archbishop of Melbourne") translated to Portuguese, matching how such ecclesiastical approbation blocks are conventionally handled.
