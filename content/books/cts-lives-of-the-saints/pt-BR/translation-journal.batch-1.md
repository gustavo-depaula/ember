## Translation Decisions

1. **Modernization**: In *Santo Inácio de Loyola*, "Moresco, or Mahometan" was rendered "mourisco, ou muçulmano" — "muçulmano" replaces the archaic/pejorative "Mahometan," per the project's modernization rule. Similarly "the conversion of the Mahometans" → "a conversão dos muçulmanos" in the same file.
2. **Author footnotes kept**: The numbered footnotes in *Santo Inácio de Loyola* (Scripture citations, source citations to Bartoli, Orlandini, Bouhours, Raderus/Sacchini) are the original author's own citation apparatus (not a later editor's scholarly annotations), so they were translated and kept, renumbered identically.
3. **Author footnotes kept, reconstructed**: In *Santa Margarida Clitherow*, the source markdown had two footnotes (marked `*` and `**`) whose text had become interleaved mid-paragraph, apparently from an OCR/PDF-extraction artifact that merged running footnotes into body text. I reconstructed the main sentence flow, kept both footnotes' content (the note on Robert Middleton's martyrdom, and the note on the Council of the North's founding under Henry VIII), and placed them near their reference markers rather than reproducing the exact scrambled interruption — this preserves the author's meaning without carrying forward an OCR defect into the translation. The Lingard citation footnote near the end of the trial section was kept as a normal footnote.
4. **Editor footnote dropped**: In *São João Fisher*, footnote 1 ("The English of these quotations has been modernized wherever it seemed advisable") is a later editor's/publisher's note about English-language modernization of quotations — irrelevant once the text is translated into Portuguese, and not part of the author's own argument — so it was dropped, and the sentence it interrupted ("the man who deserved best of God") was reconnected smoothly.
5. **Ecclesiastical approval blocks kept**: *São Martinho de Tours* and *Santa Bernadete* both end with "Nihil Obstat" / "Imprimatur" blocks from the original CTS booklet. These were kept (Latin terms untranslated, as is standard), with the byline name and diocese translated ("Archiepiscopus Melbournensis" → "Arcebispo de Melbourne" as a translation gloss following the Latin, matching the treatment already used for "Censor Diocesan" → "Censor Diocesano"). The "@" glyph before the archbishop's name (an OCR rendering of the episcopal cross "†") was replaced with "†" in both files for correct typographic meaning.
6. **Quoted dialogue formatting**: The English source for *Santa Bernadete* and *São Maximiliano Kolbe* uses a mix of single quotes and stray unmatched quotation marks (apparent OCR/typesetting artifacts, e.g. unclosed `'` marks around dialogue). These were normalized to standard Portuguese double quotes (" ") for direct speech, without altering the wording, to avoid carrying forward the source's punctuation noise.
7. **Untranslated technical/proper terms kept with gloss**: "peine forte et dure" (Clitherow), "Bureau des Constatations" (Bernadette), "Il Giesu"/"Il Gesù" (Ignatius), "Suaviter in modo... fortiter in re" (Francis de Sales) were kept in their original language (French/Italian/Latin) as in the source, each followed by a bracketed Portuguese gloss on first occurrence, matching the instruction to keep Latin/foreign phrases inline with translation following.
8. **Names normalized for internal consistency**: Where the same person's name appeared with slightly different spelling variants across a single file (e.g., "Marie Vazous" vs. the historically correct "Marie Vauzous"; "Zdunska-Wola" vs. "Zduńska Wola"; "Ligug" vs. "Ligugé"; "Pilsudski" vs. "Piłsudski"; "Oswiecim" vs. "Oświęcim"), the correct diacritics/spelling were used consistently in the PT-BR text, since these are simple OCR-dropped-diacritic errors rather than meaningful source variants.
9. **Papal name correction**: "Pope Damasus V" in *São Martinho de Tours* was translated as "papa Dâmaso I" — there was no "Damasus V"; the pope who received the Spanish bishops' appeal regarding Priscillian in the historical record is Damasus I. Treated as a source OCR/transcription slip rather than a deliberate authorial claim.
10. **Saint-name forms applied per the project's rule** (São before consonant-initial masculine names, Santo before vowel-initial/exceptions, Santa for women): São Francisco de Sales, São João Fisher, São Martinho de Tours, Santo Inácio de Loyola, São Maximiliano Kolbe (kept "São" since "Maximiliano" starts with a consonant sound in PT), Santa Margarida Clitherow, Santa Bernadete.
11. **"Mass requisites" (Clitherow trial)**: rendered as "objetos da missa" / "hóstias do altar" contextually rather than a single fixed term, to keep the narrative natural.
12. Measurements were converted contextually where natural for readability without altering facts (e.g., "16-stone man" → "homem... de mais de cem quilos"; "200 lb. sacks" → "sacos de carvão de 90 quilos"). The "about one inch" trephine opening was translated literally as "cerca de uma polegada", matching the English source's own figure exactly (not converted to metric, and not altered against outside historical records), consistent with translating the narrative as given rather than correcting its reported numbers.
