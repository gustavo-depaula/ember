## Translation Decisions

- All Scripture references kept in the book/chapter/verse format of the source (e.g., "Provérbios 4, 18", "2 Coríntios 4, 16-17"), following existing pt-BR convention of comma rather than colon between chapter and verse.
- Latin liturgical/devotional phrases quoted inline (e.g., *Domine, non sum dignus*, *Vocavi et renuisti; quoque in interitu tuo ridebo*) were kept in Latin with italics, followed by the Portuguese translation already supplied by the source text, matching the source's own pattern.
- In "Saint Francis Xavier, S.J.", the source's dated/offensive terms "Mahomet" and "Moslems" were modernized to "Maomé" and "muçulmanos" throughout, per the project's content-authoring modernization rule — noted here as required.
- Distances given in miles in the source (e.g., "thirteen miles", "two thousand miles") were converted to approximate metric figures in km for natural pt-BR readability (this is consistent with how CTS lives are generally read by a Brazilian audience); currency figures (£, ducats) were left as the named unit rather than converted, since they are historical sums, not measurements.
- Money sums originally in old British currency (e.g. "£1,200" in Saint John Bosco) were kept as "libras" (with the numeral) rather than converted or left as the £ symbol, for readability.
- In "The Mothers' Saint", the historical/period letters signed with initials (W.J., C.G., H.E.McD., J.M., M.B., T.H.) were translated in full but the initials themselves were kept unchanged, since they identify real correspondents, not translated names.
- Proper personal names of secondary historical figures (e.g., Frederick Schlegel → Frederico Schlegel, Dorothy → Dorothea, Joseph von Pilat kept as-is since German name has no standard Portuguese form, Adam von Muller → Adam von Müller with correct diaeresis) were adapted only where a natural Portuguese given-name equivalent exists; surnames and untranslatable German/Polish/Dutch names were left in their original form.
- "Echevin" (a municipal magistrate title in the Low Countries) was rendered as "escabino", the standard Portuguese loanword for this exact historical office.
- Titles of quoted books/periodicals appearing in the source (Ecclesiastical Annals → *Anais Eclesiásticos*; Companion of Youth → *Companheiro da Juventude*; Catholic Readings → *Leituras Católicas*) were translated and italicized, since they are descriptive titles rather than brand names; *The Majellan* was left untranslated as it is a specific named periodical still referenced by that title.
- In "The Life of Saint John Berchmans, S.J.", numerous OCR-garbled spacing/line-break artifacts in the source (e.g., "m ost", "t hem", stray periods) were silently resolved during translation rather than reproduced, since they are clearly scanning artifacts, not authorial text.
- Kept the Latin ecclesiastical formulas at the end of two lives (Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur / Imprimi Potest, and the Latin titles "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis", "Censor Theol. Deput.") untranslated, per the standard convention for these official front/back-matter declarations; only the surrounding descriptive words ("Diocesan Censor" → "Censor Diocesano", "Archbishop of..." → "Arcebispo de...") were translated where the source itself had already given them in English.
