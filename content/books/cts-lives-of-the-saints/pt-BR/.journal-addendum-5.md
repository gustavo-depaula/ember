## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: For secondary historical figures (not the chapter's title saint) with common Latin-alphabet given names, I translated first names to their standard Portuguese equivalents for readability and to match the book's established convention of rendering "Don Bosco" as recognizably Portuguese (e.g., Giuseppe → José, Giovanni Battista → João Batista, Margherita → Margarida, Michael Ghislieri → Miguel Ghislieri, Dominica → Dominga, Guy → Guido, Gerard → Gerardo, Humbeline → Humbelina, Nivard → Nivardo, Peter Abelard → Pedro Abelardo, William of Champeaux → Guilherme de Champeaux). Surnames were left unchanged throughout (Sarto, Ghislieri, Mazzarello, Pestarino, etc.). The diminutive nickname "Bepi" (for young Giuseppe Sarto) was left untranslated — it is a regional Italian pet-name with no natural Portuguese equivalent, and the source itself glosses who it refers to.
- 2026-09-08: Italian secular-clergy title "Don X" (e.g. Don Antonio Costantini, Don Giuseppe Sarto) rendered as "Dom X" throughout saint-pius-x.md, matching the already-universal Portuguese convention "Dom Bosco" for the same title/period/region rather than "Padre X".
- 2026-09-08: In saint-john-of-god.md and saint-mary-magdalen.md, the closing ecclesiastical approbation block used English (not Latin) descriptors in the source — "Diocesan Censor", "Archbishop of Melbourne 1st July, 1973". Per the standing convention (keep Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur / Imprimi Potest labels in Latin, keep personal and place names unchanged), I translated the surrounding English descriptive words into Portuguese ("Censor Diocesano", "Arcebispo de Melbourne, 1º de julho de 1973") since they are not part of the fixed Latin formula and are ordinary descriptive text, unlike saint-pius-v.md and saint-bernard-abbot-of-clairvaux.md, which have no such block, and saint-mary-mazzarello.md, whose block was already in Latin/abbreviated Latin form ("Censor Dioc.", "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis") and was left as-is.
- 2026-09-08: Modernized "Mussulman"/"Mahomet" (saint-pius-v.md) to "muçulmano"/"Maomé" — standard modern Portuguese spelling, consistent with the earlier modernization decision logged for the True Devotion book. Not a change of meaning, only orthography/register.
- 2026-09-08: In saint-pius-x.md, the papal-conclave vote tally in the source spells the same cardinal's name inconsistently as "Goti" in the first scrutiny and "Gotti" in the second and third (an evident OCR/typesetting slip in the original pamphlet). Per the standing instruction not to silently "fix" OCR artifacts, I preserved the inconsistent spelling exactly as it appears at each occurrence rather than normalizing it.
- 2026-09-08: French title abbreviations "M. Loubet" and "M. Combes" (saint-pius-x.md, France section) were left as in the source ("M." for "Monsieur") rather than substituted with a Portuguese equivalent, since they are direct quotations/references to French officials of the era and the abbreviation is widely intelligible.
- 2026-09-08: "Cîteaux"/"Citeaux" was rendered as "Cister", its standard, long-established Portuguese name (the same root that gives "cisterciense"/Cistercian), rather than transliterating the French spelling.
- 2026-09-08: Saint Bernard's siblings carry the honorific "Bd." (Blessed) in the source for several of them (Bd. Guy, Bd. Gerard, Bd. Humbeline, Bd. Nivard) — rendered "Beato Guido", "Beato Gerardo", "Beata Humbelina", "Beato Nivardo".
- 2026-09-08: "Pippo buono" (Philip Neri's childhood nickname in Florence) was kept in Italian with an inline gloss, "Pippo buono — o bom Filipinho", since it is explicitly explained by the source text itself as a nickname, not simply narrated.
