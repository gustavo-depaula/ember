## 2. Translation Decisions

- **Saint Aloysius / Stanislaus (in Berchmans chapter):** rendered as "São Luís Gonzaga" and "São Estanislau" (standard BR-PT devotional forms), matching how they'd be referred to elsewhere in the corpus, even though the source only calls them "St. Aloysius"/"St. Stanislaus."
- **St. Mary MacKillop / "Santa Maria MacKillop":** per instructions, kept the English surname "MacKillop" (no Portuguese equivalent exists); note the source itself inconsistently spells it "McKillop" throughout the body text and "MacKillop" only in some external references — I preserved the body text's "McKillop" spelling as-is in running prose (matching the source's own usage) but used "MacKillop" in the H1 title per the task's naming instruction.
- **Saint Pius X (this chapter, "Pope of the Eucharist"):** this is explicitly a second, independent chapter about Pius X from elsewhere in the book (a narrative/dialogue framing device, distinct from any other Pius X chapter). Kept its own title exactly as given: combined "St. Pius X" + subtitle into one H1, "São Pio X, o Papa da Eucaristia," matching the source's single-heading treatment of title+subtitle.
- **Colloquial dialogue register (Saint Pius X chapter):** this chapter is a framed fictional dialogue (a priest telling children the story of Pius X at a family's home). Kept the spoken-word warmth and colloquialisms in natural conversational Brazilian Portuguese (contractions like "não sei," informal address "vocês"/"vós" mixed per character register — used "vós" forms for the priest's more formal exhortations and rhetorical high points, plain "vocês" register for the back-and-forth family banter) rather than a uniform literary register, to preserve the oral storytelling tone of the original.
- **"negrophiles" / "nigger-lovers" (The Adventurous Nun):** the source text itself is making a historical/rhetorical point by juxtaposing a period slur from Javouhey's era ("negrophile") with a mid-20th-century American slur used in the essayistic aside about the 1964 civil-rights murders. Translated both literally and directly (as the author intends the shock/contrast) rather than softening or euphemizing, since euphemizing would blunt the author's own argument about racism; flagged here for editorial awareness given the source's own dated and blunt language throughout that essay-like final third of the chapter.
- **Anne-Marie Javouhey's own name:** left as "Anne-Marie" / surname "Javouhey" (French, untranslated), no standard Portuguese devotional form exists for a beata not widely venerated in Lusophone tradition; nickname "Nanette" kept as-is.
- **"Reverend Mother" / "Mother Superior" / "Mother General":** rendered as "Reverenda Madre" / "Madre Superiora" / "Madre Geral" throughout both the Javouhey and MacKillop chapters, kept consistent across both chapters even though they are different congregations.
- **St. Camillus's motto/exclamations and the "five mercies of God":** kept literal ("as cinco misericórdias de Deus") — a personal turn of phrase from Camillus about his own chronic ailments, not a fixed liturgical term.
- **Latin phrases retained inline with translation following, per source's own pattern:** e.g. "Accedant qui ordinandi sunt" → glossed immediately after with "Let those to be ordained, come forward" / "Aproximem-se os que devem ser ordenados"; "Vocavi et renuisti; quoque in interitu tuo ridebo" glossed the same way John Berchmans's own letter glosses it; "Instaurare Omnia In Christo" kept in Latin with the Portuguese gloss following on first use, then referred to by the Portuguese gloss alone afterward, matching the dialogue's own repetition pattern.
- **"Te Deum" quotation (Camillus's deathbed picture):** kept Te Deum untranslated as a named prayer/reference, but translated the specific verse quoted ("Salvum fac servum tuum...") into Portuguese since the source itself renders it in English (not Latin) at that point — matched source's choice to give it in the vernacular there rather than reverting to Latin.
- **No editor/publisher footnotes encountered** in any of these nine chapters worth dropping; the only footnotes present were the author's own citations in "St. Joseph" (Bossuet, De La Broise, Orsini, Maldonatus, Prat) — these were kept and translated as part of the body text (inline, matching source formatting, since the source itself renders them as plain paragraph text rather than markdown footnotes).
- **Imprimatur/Nihil Obstat blocks:** left the ecclesiastical formulas, censor names, and Latin honorifics (Nihil Obstat, Imprimatur, Censor Deputatus, Archiepiscopus Melbournensis, etc.) untranslated/unmodified across all nine chapters, matching prior-batch convention of preserving these as official Latin/formal apparatus rather than translating them.
- **Modernizations:** none of the nine chapters required modernizing outdated ethnic/religious terminology (no "Mohammedans" etc. appeared in this batch); the one dated-language passage (see negrophile note above) was preserved deliberately rather than modernized, since the surrounding context makes clear the author is quoting/referencing the actual period term as part of a historical argument.
