## 2. Non-obvious translation decisions

- **Saints' names**: "St. Brigid" → "Santa Brígida"; "St. Patrick" → "São Patrício" (confirmed against `journal-notes-batch-3.md`, which already established this form); "Columcille" kept as-is (no common Portuguese exonym found in the existing corpus, and the source itself uses this Irish form consistently rather than "Columba"); "St. Vincent Strambi" → "São Vicente Strambi"; "St. John Bosco" / "Don Bosco" → "São João Bosco" / "Dom Bosco" (kept "Dom Bosco" throughout the body since the source uses this affectionate title, not his birth name, as its primary reference — "Dom" is the standard Lusophone rendering of the Italian honorific "Don" for a priest); "St. Rita of Cascia" → "Santa Rita de Cássia" (matching the existing `saint-rita.md` chapter's own rendering of the place name, "Cássia").
- **Place names**: Kept largely in original/Italian form (Civitavecchia, Bagnorea, Macerata, Tolentino, Vetralla, Morrovalle, Milão [Portuguese exonym, well established], Roma [likewise]). Irish place names in Saint Brigid kept in their English/anglicized source spelling (Faughart, Dundalk, Kildare) since no distinct Portuguese forms exist and the source itself doesn't use Irish-language forms consistently either.
- **"la mamma della misericordia"** (Italian, of St. Paul of the Cross): kept in Italian with the English gloss translated into Portuguese ("a mãe da misericórdia"), per the source's own bilingual pattern for untranslatable expressions.
- **"la santa cigala"** (Italian nickname for Bl. Anna Maria Taigi, "the saintly chatterbox"): kept in Italian with the source's own English gloss rendered into Portuguese ("a santa tagarela"), same pattern as above.
- **Giraldus Cambrensis**: rendered as "Giraldo de Gales" (Giraldus of Wales), the standard Portuguese-language historical reference form, rather than a literal transliteration of the Latin name.
- **Scripture quotations**: All Scripture quotations (e.g. Matthew 10:37, Matthew 19:29, Daniel 12:3, Song of Songs) were translated freshly from the source's own English wording rather than sourced from an existing Portuguese Bible translation, to match each essay's own paraphrase/quotation style; verse references preserved as given in the source (Dan 12:3 rendered "Dn 12,3" per Portuguese citation convention).
- **Currency**: "£1,200" (Don Bosco, cost of the shed and land) rendered as "1.200 libras" (numeral kept, matching the source's own numeral style elsewhere in this chapter, unlike the spelled-out "dez mil libras" convention noted for a different chapter in batch 8's journal — the two chapters differ in register, so each was matched to its own surrounding numeral usage).
- **Measurements**: "fifty miles" → "cinquenta milhas" (kept as historical distance figure, English miles, since no metric conversion was performed elsewhere for this era/text); "five-foot four" (Don Bosco's height) → "um metro e sessenta e três" (converted to metric, since this is a physical-description detail meant to give the modern reader an intuitive sense of stature, not a period-flavored quantity); "thirteen miles" (barefoot walk to school) → "cerca de vinte quilômetros" (converted to metric for the same reason — a felt sense of distance walked barefoot, not a formal figure).
- **"peace-makers" (St. Rita's parents)**: rendered "pacificadores", matching the existing `saint-rita.md` chapter's own choice of word for the same epithet.
- **Litany of St. Rita**: translated in full, following the standard Portuguese litany register and response formulas already used in the existing `saint-rita.md` prayers (e.g. "rogai por nós", "tende piedade de nós") for internal consistency across the two St. Rita chapters in this corpus.
- **Footnotes**: No editor/publisher footnotes appeared in any of the four source files; all content is the author's own narrative, prayers, and (in Saint Rita) a litany — nothing was dropped.
- **Imprimatur/Nihil obstat blocks**: Kept untranslated proper names and Latin formulas ("Nihil obstat", "Imprimatur", "Imprimi Potest") as in source; translated only the surrounding titles ("Diocesan Censor" → "Censor Diocesano", "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis" kept in Latin as in source, glossed as "Arcebispo de Melbourne" per the pattern already used in prior batches for these closing blocks).
