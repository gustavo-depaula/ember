## 2. Non-obvious Translation Decisions

- **Saints' names**: standard Lusophone devotional forms used throughout — São Pio X, São Pedro Luís Maria Chanel (Peter Aloysius Mary Chanel — "Aloysius" rendered as "Luís" per the traditional Portuguese equivalent, as in "São Luís Gonzaga"), Eugênio de Mazenod (no "São/Beato" used in the chapter itself since the source text calls him only "Blessed" once at the very end — kept as "Eugênio de Mazenod" throughout, matching the English original's own usage, which mostly omits a title), São Martinho de Tours, São Caetano (also known as Gaetano — kept both forms in the title, "São Caetano ou Gaetano," matching source), São Raimundo Nonato, Santa Filomena, São João Francisco Régis (French "Régis" kept with the acute accent, standard Portuguese spelling of the family name), Santa Maria Eufrásia Pelletier.
- **"The Dauntless Virgin of Siena"** — title kept as a translated literary nickname ("A Virgem Destemida de Sena") rather than swapped for the saint's formal name, per the guidelines; the saint is identified by her full name (Catarina de Sena / Catarina Benincasa) within the body text.
- **Latin phrases**: kept inline with Portuguese gloss following, matching the source's own pattern (e.g. *Pertransiit benefaciendo!* — "Andou fazendo o bem"; *non natus* — "não nascido"; *Habemus Pontificem* left untranslated, as it is virtually a technical/liturgical term recognizable to Portuguese readers).
- **Mohammedans / Islamic law** (saint-raymund-nonnatus.md): kept the source's own period vocabulary "maometanos" rather than modernizing to "muçulmanos," since the CTS text itself is not using it pejoratively but as the standard period English term; no editorializing was added. (Flagged here in case editorial policy prefers modernizing this term across the whole corpus for consistency with other translated books.)
- **Distances**: original English chapters give distances in miles; converted to metric (km) in the Peter Chanel and John Francis Regis chapters (e.g. "fourteen kilometers," "a hundred miles of stormy sea" → "mais de cento e sessenta quilômetros") and in Saint Martin of Tours ("nine miles long" → "quinze quilômetros," "3500 feet" → "1.070 metros"), consistent with general modernization for a Brazilian audience; imperial units were not simply transliterated.
- **Two apparent OCR/typographical errors in the source, silently normalized as a plain reading correction (not an editorial change of fact)**:
  - *saint-pius-x.md*: "Eucharistic Congress... August, 1879" — internally inconsistent, since Sarto only became Cardinal Patriarch of Venice in 1893; the real Venice Eucharistic Congress was in 1897. Rendered as "agosto de 1897."
  - *saint-pius-x.md*: Imprimatur date "Dublini, die 70, Jan., anno 1962" — "70" is not a valid day; rendered as "dia 30 de janeiro de 1962."
  - Pius V's date "(1712)" in the closing list of canonized Popes was kept as-is (verified correct: 1712 is Pius V's canonization year by Clement XI, not a typo).
- **saint-mary-euphrasia-pelletier.md**: the author's own footnote about the French conflating "Irish, English or Scotch" as "Anglish" was kept as a genuine author footnote (not dropped as an editorial gloss), translated directly, since it is integral to the text's own list ("I am Italian, English*, German...").
- **eugene-de-mazenod.md**: numbered section heading "I / Missionário da Provença" kept as in source (a bare roman numeral followed by a section title, apparently intended as part of a longer serialized work with further parts not included in this corpus).
- No editor/publisher footnotes were encountered in this batch beyond the one addressed above; the "Nihil Obstat"/"Imprimatur" blocks at the end of each chapter were kept and translated (titles of dignity translated — e.g. "Censor Deputatus" kept in Latin, "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis" → "Arcebispo de Melbourne," "Diocesan Censor" → "Censor Diocesano") since these are part of the original pamphlets' front matter, not later scholarly apparatus.
- **saint-peter-aloysius-mary-chanel.md**: kept the closing devotional hymn's verse structure and line breaks exactly as in the source (translated, not versified/rhymed, to preserve literal meaning over poetic meter).
