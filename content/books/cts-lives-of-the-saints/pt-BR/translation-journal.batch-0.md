## Translation Decisions

- Saints' and blesseds' names throughout rendered in standard Portuguese liturgical form per the São/Santo/Santa rule (e.g. Santo Agostinho, Santo Aleixo, São Basílio, São Domingos).
- In "Saint Teresa of Avila," the source text's Spanish family is given Spanish-form names with correct Spanish accents (Alonso, Sánchez, Dávila, Ahumada) rather than Portuguese equivalents, since these are historical Spanish proper names, not saints per se.
- In "St Dominic Savio," the line "St. Teresa of Avila wanted to go to convert the Moslems" was rendered "Santa Teresa de Ávila quis ir converter os muçulmanos" — modernization of "Moslems"/mahometan-type terms to "muçulmanos" per the project's default policy (no other instances of outdated ethnonyms were encountered in this batch).
- "Saint Gregory Nazianzen, B.C." and "Saint Philip Benizi, Conf." are dense Butler's-Lives-style entries with the original author's own scholarly citation apparatus (endnotes referencing Gregory's own Orationes/Carmina/Epistolae). These were judged to be the *author's own* source citations (not a later editor's annotations) and were kept, translated only where they contained explanatory English prose (e.g. note 17, "his will was comprised in these words...").
- The bibliographic headnote in "Saint Gregory Nazianzen" (naming Gregory of Caesarea, Hermant, Tillemont, Ceillier, Baronius/Alberici) was likewise kept and translated as the author's own source note, in brackets, matching the original's formatting.
- Formal Latin imprimatur/censor blocks at the end of "Saint Rita," "St. Dominic Savio," and "Saint Vincent Strambi" (Nihil Obstat / Imprimi Potest / Imprimatur, censor and bishop names in Latin form, e.g. "Gulielmus, Episcopus Fernensis") were left untranslated, as these are formal ecclesiastical Latin/name blocks, not narrative prose — consistent with treating Latin liturgical/legal formulas as inline Latin per the source's own convention.
- In "Saint Rita," the Cardinal-authored ecclesiastical exam reports (1626, 1637) were translated as direct quotations, preserving their formal register.
- In "Mary MacKillop," Australian and New Zealand place names (Fitzroy, Ballarat, Bendigo, Darebin, Kalgoorlie, Jindabyne, Temuka, etc.) and the informal decade-names "Roaring Forties"/"Roaring Fifties" were kept in English, as there are no Portuguese exonyms and these are historically specific references.
- In "Mary MacKillop," the closing prayer/quotation section headers (e.g. "True Charity," "The Institute God's Work," "A Welcome to the Cross," "Simple Obedience," "Respect for Priests," "All for God Only") were translated as section titles ("Verdadeira Caridade," "A Obra de Deus, o Instituto," "Um Acolhimento à Cruz," "Obediência Simples," "Respeito pelos Sacerdotes," "Tudo Somente por Deus").
- In "Saint Dymphna," the Litany was translated using the standard Portuguese litany-response conventions ("tende piedade de nós," "rogai por nós," "nós vos suplicamos, ouvi-nos," "perdoai-nos, Senhor") and the original's hard-line-break markdown formatting (trailing double-space at line end) was preserved so each invocation renders on its own line.
- Minor OCR-era spelling irregularities in the English source (e.g. "Bezadas" in the Teresa text, a known variant of "Becedas") were resolved to the standard historical spelling in translation.
