## 2. Non-obvious translation decisions

- **Saint Stanislaus Kostka**: "St. Stanislaus" → "Estanislau" (standard Lusophone form, cf. São Estanislau de Cracóvia). Kept "Estanislau Kostka" throughout, no honorific attached to informal narrative references (Paul thinking of "Stanislaus").
- **Saint John the Baptist**: "St. John the Baptist" → "São João Batista" (fully established form). Direct Scripture quotations (Gospel of Luke infancy narrative, the Baptist's preaching in Matthew/Luke/John) were translated freshly into fluent Portuguese rather than lifted from an existing Bible translation, to preserve the register and rhythm of the source's own (KJV-derived) wording; meaning was checked against the standard Gospel accounts. Kept "Magnificat!" untranslated as an interjection since the source itself uses it as an exclamation rather than quoting the full text.
- **Saint Rita**: "St. Rita of Cascia" → "Santa Rita de Cássia" — used the well-established Brazilian Portuguese devotional form "Cássia" for the place name (rather than literal "Cascia"), since this specific title is overwhelmingly the popular/liturgical form in Brazil. All other place names (Roccaporena, Perugia→Perúgia, Spoleto→Espoleto, Foligno→Fuligno, Assisi→Assis) were given their standard Portuguese exonyms where well established, otherwise kept as in source.
- **Saint Bernard, Abbot of Clairvaux**: "Clairvaux" → "Claraval" (standard, universally used Portuguese form for this specific abbey/title, "São Bernardo de Claraval" / "Abade de Claraval"). Other French place/monastery names (Cîteaux → Cister, Pontigny, La Ferté, etc.) — "Cîteaux" rendered "Cister" (standard Portuguese for the Cistercian motherhouse) but minor houses (Pontigny, La Ferté, Morimond, Fontenay, Foigny, Trois Fontaines) kept in French form, as there is no standard Portuguese exonym. French/English personal names of nobles and bishops (Hugh of Mâcon, William of Champeaux, Suger, etc.) were given light Portuguese adaptation only where the person is a canonized/beatified saint or extremely well known (e.g. "Peter Abelard" → "Pedro Abelardo", "Bd. Eugenius III" → "Bem-Aventurado Eugênio III"); lesser nobles' names (Josbert de la Ferté, Gaudry, etc.) kept as in source.
- **Saint Patrick**: "St. Patrick" → "São Patrício" (standard). Kept "Confissões" quotations translated fresh from the given English rather than sourcing an existing Portuguese translation of the Confessio, to match the essay's own paraphrase style. "St. Patrick's Breastplate" → "A Couraça de São Patrício" (this is the standard Portuguese title for the Lorica).
- **St. Edmund Campion**: "Edmund Campion" → "Edmundo Campion" (standard Portuguese form for canonized St. Edmund). Kept "Campion's Brag and Challenge" translated descriptively ("Desafio e Bravata de Campion") since it functions as a nickname/epithet in the source rather than a formal title with an established Portuguese name.
- **Saint John Fisher**: "John Fisher" → "João Fisher" (following the established Portuguese hagiographic convention for this saint, "São João Fisher" / "São João de Rochester", both of which appear in the source text itself — kept both forms as the author uses them).
- **Saint Edith Stein**: Kept "Edith Stein" unchanged per the guidelines (no traditional Portuguese form exists); "Sister Benedicta (of the Cross)" → "Irmã Benedita (da Cruz)", a natural Portuguese feminine form of the religious name, which is how it appears in existing Brazilian devotional sources. "Non-Aryan" kept literal/period rather than softened, since it is reported/quoted Nazi bureaucratic language, not the narrator's voice.
- **Saint Joseph Calasanctius**: title character's name modernized from the anglicized "Calasanctius" to the standard, universally used Portuguese/Spanish hagiographic form "José de Calasanz" (the filename was left unchanged as instructed: `saint-joseph-calasanctius.md`). "Piarists or Scolopii" → "Escolápios (Piaristas)" — Scolopii is an Italian colloquialism not normally rendered in Portuguese, so it was dropped in favor of the two names actually current in Portuguese (Escolápios, Piaristas).
- **Saint Lucy of Syracuse**: "St. Lucy" → "Santa Luzia" (fully established form); "Syracusa/Syracuse" → "Siracusa". This chapter is short and already close to a martyrology entry in style (drawn from Alban Butler); translated in the same terse, formal hagiographic register as the source, preserving its dense single-paragraph structure.
- Throughout this batch, blockquoted/verse material from Scripture, hymns, and prayers was translated fresh (not copy-pasted from an existing Bíblia Ave-Maria or similar) to match each source text's own paraphrase or KJV-era wording, per the instruction to preserve the author's own idiom rather than substituting a separate canonical translation.
