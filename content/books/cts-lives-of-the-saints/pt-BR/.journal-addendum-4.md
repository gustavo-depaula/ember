## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: In "Saint Martin of Tours," the English exonym "Treves" for the city of Trier was rendered as "Tréveris," the standard Portuguese name for the city, rather than transliterated literally — this follows the guideline of using well-known Portuguese equivalents for place names.
- 2026-09-08: In "Saint Stanislaus Kostka," all English distance figures (given in miles) were converted to approximate round-number metric equivalents in Portuguese (e.g. "a thousand miles" → "mais de mil e seiscentos quilômetros"; "three-hundred mile walk" → "caminhada de cerca de quinhentos quilômetros") for a natural reading experience for a Brazilian audience, since the original numbers carry no doctrinal or narrative precision requirement — they are illustrative of distance/hardship only.
- 2026-09-08: "Poet, Priest and Martyr" (St. Robert Southwell) contains several short poems by Southwell embedded in the text. These were translated for sense and imagery rather than rhymed or metered, preserving line breaks as in the source, since a rhymed Portuguese translation would require paraphrase that risks distorting the theological content of the verses.
- 2026-09-08: The torture device called "the manacles" in the Southwell chapter (a specific device distinct from "the rack") was rendered "as manoplas" throughout, with "roda (de tortura)" used for the generic "rack," to keep the distinction the source draws between the two.
- 2026-09-08: "Star Chamber" (the English royal court) was kept as an untranslated proper name ("a Corte da Star Chamber") since it names a specific historical English institution with no standard Portuguese equivalent.
- 2026-09-08: The Mass Propers quoted in full in "Saint Jude, Helper in Great Need" (Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Preface, Communion, Postcommunion) were translated using the traditional Portuguese-language missal section names (Intróito, Coleta, Epístola, Gradual, Evangelho, Ofertório, Secreta, Prefácio, Comunhão, Póscomunhão), and the embedded Scripture text was translated freely for readability rather than sourced from a specific existing Portuguese Bible translation, consistent with how the book's other embedded Scripture quotations have been handled.
- 2026-09-08: Ecclesiastical approbation blocks at the end of each chapter (Nihil Obstat / Imprimi Potest / Imprimatur, with censor and bishop names, dates, and titles such as "Diocesan Censor" or "Archbishop of Melbourne") were left exactly as in the English source, per the established convention already visible in previously translated chapters (e.g. the-life-of-saint-anthony.md, saint-bernadette.md) — these are archival records, not text to adapt, and mixing partial translation into them would be inconsistent.
- 2026-09-08: In "Saint Benedict, Abbot," the older, more formal/archaic register of this particular English source text (an 18th/19th-century Butler's-Lives-style hagiography, notably more antiquated than the other CTS pamphlets in this book) was rendered into dignified but readable modern Portuguese rather than imitating archaic Portuguese constructions — per the general house style of "clear, dignified, warm," not literally archaic.
- 2026-09-08: "A Virgem Destemida de Sena" (The Dauntless Virgin of Siena) — the essay's historical-narrative sections about the Avignon Papacy and the Black Death were translated closely, preserving the author's specific (sometimes historically imprecise, e.g. "Roman Empire fell... in 378") claims as given, per the book's general policy of translating the intended sentence rather than correcting the source.

## Uncertain Renderings (flag for review)

- saint-benedict-abbot.md: the source is unusually dense with obscure proper names and Latin bibliographic references (Dom Mège, Dom Germain Millet, Mabillon, Muratori) that are not explained in-line; these were kept as personal names, untranslated, consistent with the "don't invent content" instruction, but a subject-matter reviewer may want to double check the transliteration of a few historical/geographic names (Afile for "Afilum," Sublaco for "Sublacum," Vicovara/Varronis Vicus).
- saint-martin-of-tours.md: "Bolzano, Salerno, Trent, Verona, Mantua and Bologna" list in the Stanislaus Kostka chapter (not this file) includes "Salerno" where geographically "Salorno" (a town on that Alpine route) seems intended in the original English text — this is very likely an OCR/authorial error in the 1965 English pamphlet already present in en-US; translated literally as given ("Salerno") per the instruction not to silently "fix" the source.
