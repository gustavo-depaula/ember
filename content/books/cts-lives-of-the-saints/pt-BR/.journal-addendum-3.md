## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: "Saint Wenceslas" contains extensive Czech proper names (people, places, churches, art works). Rendered using the standard Portuguese/international transliteration of Czech names where a convention exists (Venceslau, Boleslau, Drahomíra, Ludmila) but kept less common Czech names in their Czech spelling with diacritics (Přemyslidas, Spytihněv, Kressenbrunn, Hněvsa, etc.) since no established Portuguese form exists for these — consistent with the guideline to keep personal/place names as in the source absent a well-known equivalent.
- 2026-09-08: The devotional canticle "Svatý Václave" in saint-wenceslas.md was translated stanza-by-stanza into Portuguese (not kept in Czech), matching the source's own English translation approach — the source itself presents an English rendering of the Czech hymn, not the Czech original, so the Portuguese follows suit.
- 2026-09-08: The English carol "Good King Wenceslas" (verses beginning "On the Feast of Stephen...") was translated into Portuguese prose-like verse, preserving line breaks as in the source, rather than attempting a rhymed/metrical Portuguese version — fidelity to meaning was prioritized over poetic form, consistent with the source's own presentation (the OCR text itself does not preserve clean verse lineation throughout).
- 2026-09-08: saint-pascal-baylon.md: title in the source is "St. Pascal Baylon" but the body text and journal convention favor "Pascoal." Used "São Pascoal Baylon" throughout for consistency (see Key Terms above).
- 2026-09-08: saint-joseph-calasanctius.md: rendered the Latin "d.C." for "a.d. 1648" (date of death) at the top of the file, translating the English gloss rather than leaving "a.d." untranslated, since it functions as a plain-language date marker (not a liturgical/Latin formula like Nihil Obstat) in this book's front matter.
- 2026-09-08: saint-gertrude.md: kept the author's footnote-in-parentheses about the Benedictine/Cistercian founding of Helfta inline exactly where the source places it (mid-paragraph, marked with an asterisk), per the instruction to keep such authorial asides as parentheticals — this is not an editor's scholarly footnote but part of the original devotional text.
- 2026-09-08: saint-rita.md: the source file uses "Saint Rita" as the title but "St. Rita of Cascia" in the opening paragraph. Followed the source's own pattern — title rendered "Santa Rita," first reference in body text as "Santa Rita de Cássia."
- 2026-09-08: saint-gemma-galgani.md and saint-peregrine-the-cancer-saint.md contain extended devotional material (novenas, litanies, prayers for the sick and for a happy death). Translated these in full following standard Portuguese Catholic prayer register (e.g., "tende piedade de nós," "rogai por nós") consistent with existing Ladainha/Novena conventions already in the journal.
- 2026-09-08: In saint-john-the-baptist.md, extensive Douay-Rheims-style Scripture quotations (Luke 1, Isaiah 40, Matthew 3 and 11, John 1 and 3, etc.) were translated freshly into natural Portuguese matching the register of the surrounding narrative prose, rather than sourced from an existing Portuguese Bible translation, since the book's presentation of Scripture is woven into the narrator's essay rather than set off as formal citations. Preserved the archaic-flavored English capitalization of divine pronouns ("He," "His") as "Ele"/"Sua" without special capitalization, per standard modern Portuguese practice.
- 2026-09-08: Minor OCR artifacts in the English source (e.g., "b asis," "th at," "l mprimatur," stray line breaks splitting sentences in saint-john-the-baptist.md, saint-wenceslas.md, and saint-gemma-galgani.md) were resolved by translating the evidently intended sentence, per the standing instruction not to attempt to "fix" the English, only to translate the intended meaning sensibly.
- 2026-09-08: Ecclesiastical approbation blocks at the end of saint-gertrude.md, saint-rita.md, and saint-john-the-baptist.md were preserved as in the source: Latin formula labels (Nihil Obstat, Imprimi Potest, Imprimatur) untranslated, censor/bishop names and dates unchanged.

## Expressions & Idioms

- "a burning and a shining light" (John 5:35, KJV-style, of John the Baptist) → "uma lâmpada que arde e resplandece" — standard Portuguese liturgical/biblical phrasing.
- "Behold the Lamb of God" → "Eis o Cordeiro de Deus" — standard liturgical form (as in the Agnus Dei).
- "a voice crying in the wilderness" → "voz do que clama no deserto" — standard Portuguese rendering matching Isaiah 40:3 / Advent liturgy phrasing.
