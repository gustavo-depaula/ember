## Translation Decisions (2026-09-08)

- Saint-name rendering follows the journal's existing rule (standard Portuguese forms per gender/euphony); newly encountered names not previously in the corpus were added above rather than left in English.
- "Aloysius" in "Peter Aloysius Mary Chanel" was rendered "Luís" (not "Aloísio"), matching the far more common Portuguese hagiographic usage (as in "Luís Gonzaga" for Aloysius Gonzaga) and the register of a modern devotional book.
- In "St. Edmund Campion," a great many period/legal English terms (Star-Chamber-era vocabulary: "recusant," "the rack," "pursuivant," "Privy Council," etc.) were rendered with their nearest standard Portuguese historiographical equivalents ("recusante," "o suplício da roda," "caçador de padres" for "pursuivant," "Conselho" for Privy Council context) rather than left in English, except for "Tyburn" and "The Shambles," which are proper place names kept as in the source per house style (place names generally kept in original form).
- "Musumusu" and "Niuliki" (Futunan personal/chiefly names in the Chanel chapter) were left unchanged, per the rule to keep personal and place names in their original form.
- In the Chanel chapter, several native Futunan/Polynesian units and food terms (taros, kava) were kept as in source (already loanwords/italicized terms in English) — "taros," "inhames" (yams, translated as it has a direct PT equivalent), "kava" kept.
- Distances given only in miles in the source (e.g., "eight miles," "three miles," "a mile and a half") were converted to approximate metric equivalents in the Portuguese text (km/m) for a modern PT-BR devotional readership, consistent with treating this as an accessible modern edition rather than a scholarly-critical one. Flagged here since it is a light modernization/adaptation, not a literal transcription of the numeral.
- Monetary and old-currency terms (ducats, bezants) were kept as direct cognates ("ducados," "bizantinos de ouro") rather than converted, since these are historical currency names, not measurements.
- The hymn text in the Chanel chapter ("To Mary's son who died...") was translated as free verse preserving sense and line breaks rather than attempting to preserve the original's rhyme/meter, since a rhymed technical translation would risk distorting the literal meaning; this mirrors how the CTS hymn is presented as a period artifact/appendix rather than as liturgical text to be sung in Portuguese.
- Ecclesiastical approbation blocks (Nihil Obstat / Imprimi Potest / Imprimatur, censor and bishop names and dates) were kept as in the original in all nine files, per existing journal convention.
- "The Sedia Gestatoria" (Dominic Savio chapter) kept in Latin/Italian as a proper technical term for the papal ceremonial chair, as it has no standard short Portuguese equivalent and is generally left untranslated in Portuguese Catholic writing.
- OCR artifacts in the source (missing opening quotation marks, stray characters, run-on paragraph breaks such as in the Dominic Savio and Chanel chapters) were resolved into natural, coherently punctuated Portuguese sentences without inventing content, per existing journal policy.
