## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: For "the-life-of-saint-anthony.md" and "saint-anthony-and-you.md" (both about St. Anthony of Padua), used identical renderings of his litany titles and epithets throughout to keep the two chapters consistent with each other, since they are companion texts in the source book.
- 2026-09-08: In "saint-ignatius-of-loyola.md", historical Spanish/French proper names were rendered in their standard modern forms where a well-known equivalent exists (e.g., "Don Bertram" → "Dom Beltrán", "Mary Saez de Balde" → "Maria Sáenz de Balda", "Antony Manriquez" → "Antônio Manrique", "Xaverius"/"Xavier" kept as "Xavier", "Peter Faber" → "Pedro Fabro", "James Laynez" → "Diego Laínez", "Alphonsus Salmeron" → "Afonso Salmerón"), consistent with how these Jesuit founders are conventionally named in Portuguese-language sources. Owens' Latin/French forms of Loyola family land titles ("Ognez and Loyola") were rendered "Oñaz e Loyola" (the standard historical spelling), a minor correction for readability, not a change of meaning.
- 2026-09-08: "saint-ignatius-of-loyola.md" and "saint-augustine-bishop-of-hippo.md" are both drawn from a Butler's-Lives-of-the-Saints-style source with the author's own scholarly footnotes (scripture and source citations, e.g. "Acts ii.", "Bouhours, lib. iv."). These are authorial citations, not later editorial apparatus, so they were kept and translated at the end of saint-ignatius-of-loyola.md as a "Notas" section, translating the surrounding words but preserving citation abbreviations and book/chapter numbers as in the source.
- 2026-09-08: Historical/geographic proper names in saint-ignatius-of-loyola.md were given their standard Portuguese exonyms where well established (e.g., "Germany" → "Alemanha", "Genoa" → "Gênova", "Trent" → "Trento", "Navarret" → "Navarrete"); place names without a common Portuguese form were left as in the source.
- 2026-09-08: In "saint-augustine-bishop-of-hippo.md", classical/patristic proper names were given their standard Portuguese forms (Patricius → Patrício, Monica → Mônica, Navigius → Navígio, Adeodatus → Adeodato, Ambrose → Ambrósio, Alipius → Alípio, Possidius → Possídio, Honoratus → Honorato, Faustus → Fausto), consistent with how these figures are conventionally named in Portuguese patristic literature.
- 2026-09-08: The famous "Tolle, lege!" episode in Augustine's conversion (saint-augustine-bishop-of-hippo.md) was kept in Latin with the traditional Portuguese gloss "Toma e lê!", following the pattern established elsewhere in the journal for keeping short recognizable Latin phrases inline with a vernacular gloss.
- 2026-09-08: "the-adventurous-nun.md" (Bl. Anne-Marie Javouhey) — the recurring nickname "Nanette" was kept unchanged as a personal nickname/diminutive, not translated, since it is a proper name form rather than a common noun.
- 2026-09-08: In "saint-camillus-de-lellis.md", the Order's various period-specific Latin bull titles ("Illius pro gregis," "Suprema dispositione") and the historical "Camillian Post" review title were kept in their original languages, per the general policy of preserving proper/formal titles.
- 2026-09-08: Distances given in miles/leagues in the English source (e.g., "fifteen kilometres" already metric in the-life-of-saint-anthony.md; "fifty miles", "eighty miles" in the-adventurous-nun.md) were converted to approximate round kilometre figures for a Brazilian readership, consistent with treating these as narrative color rather than precise historical data.
- 2026-09-08: "Mahometan"/"Moresco" (saint-ignatius-of-loyola.md) modernized to "muçulmano", per the journal's existing policy on outdated ethnic/religious terms (cf. True Devotion precedent noted in the skill).

## Files where renderings were less certain (flagged for review)
