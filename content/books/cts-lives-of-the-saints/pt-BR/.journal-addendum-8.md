## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: "St. Dymphna" was rendered "Santa Dinfna," the form used in existing Portuguese-language liturgical and parish naming (e.g. "Paróquia Santa Dinfna"), rather than a direct transliteration. The title "Lily of Eire" was rendered "Lírio da Irlanda" (Lily of Ireland) for clarity, since "Eire" as a poetic/Gaelic name for Ireland has no widely recognized Portuguese equivalent that keeps the same resonance.
- 2026-09-08: In the Dymphna litany, the opening "Christ, have mercy / Lord, have mercy / Christ, have mercy / Christ, graciously hear us" (an unusual, non-standard Kyrie ordering in the source) was preserved in that same order in Portuguese (Cristo/Senhor/Cristo/Cristo), rather than "corrected" to the more familiar liturgical Kyrie sequence.
- 2026-09-08: "Cura d'Ars" was kept untranslated as St. Vianney's customary epithet throughout his chapter (as the source itself does, capitalizing "Cure" almost as a title), rather than translating every instance to "pároco de Ars" or "vigário de Ars."
- 2026-09-08: Saint Thomas More's Early-Tudor-English verse ("And yet her brittle gifts long may not last...") and the punning epitaph rhyme ("When More some time had Chancellor been...") were translated into natural, dignified modern Portuguese prose/verse conveying sense and tone, not attempting to reproduce period English orthography or exact meter — consistent with the "not archaic, not casual" register directive.
- 2026-09-08: In Saint John Fisher, the source text contains a visible OCR/typesetting artifact where a footnote ("The English of these quotations has been modernized wherever it seemed advisable") is interleaved mid-sentence into "...the man who deserved best of [footnote text] God." The footnote was dropped (per the book's general no-footnotes policy) and the enclosing sentence translated as the evidently intended "o homem que mais merecesse a Deus."
- 2026-09-08: In the Kolbe chapter, the Polish/German camp vocabulary ("Wstawac," "brot") was kept in the original language with the source's own bracketed gloss translated into Portuguese (e.g., "Wstawac" — ("Levantar"); "brot" (pão)), matching the source's bilingual presentation.
- 2026-09-08: Maximilian Kolbe's mnemonic formula "w = W" (his own devotional shorthand for "what we want" / "the will of God") was kept as-is in Latin-alphabet form, with the surrounding explanation translated, since it is a personal symbolic notation rather than a phrase to translate.
- 2026-09-08: Personal and place names in the Kolbe chapter (Zdunska-Wola, Pabianice, Niepokalanów, Grodno, Zakopane, Amlitz, Ostrzeszów, Auschwitz/Oswiecim) were left as in the source; only the generic descriptive term "City of the Immaculate" for Niepokalanów was translated ("Cidade da Imaculada").
- 2026-09-08: In the Perpetua and Felicitas martyrdom account, the Roman/early-Christian personal names were rendered in their standard Portuguese hagiographic forms (Perpétua, Felicidade, Revocato, Saturnino, Secúndulo, Saturo, Dinócrates, Hilariano, Pudente, Rústico, Optato, Aspásio) as is customary in Portuguese-language martyrologies, while place and author names (Cartago/Cartago, Santo Agostinho, Santo Próspero, São Vítor) followed the same convention.
- 2026-09-08: Ecclesiastical front/back matter blocks (Nihil Obstat / Imprimi Potest / Imprimatur, censor and bishop names, dates) were kept as in the originals across all ten chapters, per the established convention — Latin labels untranslated, personal names and dates unchanged, including English-format dates (e.g. "1st March, 1949") left as printed.
