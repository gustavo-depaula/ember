## Translation Decisions

- **saint-thomas-more.md**: Kept English/Latin quotations' embedded Latin phrases (*Indignatio principis mors*, *Quod differtur, non aufertur*) inline with translation following, matching source pattern. English poem ("And yet her brittle gifts...") rendered as a free but faithful Portuguese verse translation rather than literal prose, to preserve register. Distances given in miles converted to approximate metric equivalents in two later chapters (see below) but not in this one, since only unitless references appear.
- **saint-gemma-galgani.md**: "some two hundred miles from Lucca" converted to "trezentos e vinte quilômetros" (approx. metric conversion) for a modern PT-BR reader; flagged as a judgment call rather than a literal transliteration.
- **saint-mary-euphrasia-pelletier.md**: The footnote about the French not distinguishing "Irish, English or Scotch — they were all 'Anglish' to them" was translated as "todos eram 'britânicos' para eles" — a necessary adaptation since the pun on "Anglish"/"English" doesn't carry over; the footnote's sense (the French lumped all these nationalities together) is preserved. "Emerald Isle" translated literally as "Ilha Esmeralda" (a recognized poetic epithet for Ireland, also used in PT-BR).
- **saint-philomena.md**: This CTS pamphlet predates the 1961 removal of St Philomena's cult from the universal calendar; translated the text faithfully as a historical devotional document without adding any editorial caveat, per the instruction to preserve the author's content and tone as-is. Hair length given in inches ("twenty-seven inches") converted to metric ("sessenta e oito centímetros"). French place names (Fourvières, Saint-Gervais, Hôtel de Ville) kept in their French form since they have no established Portuguese equivalent. "Communists" (1871 Paris Commune insurgents) rendered as "comunardos," the standard historical term in Portuguese, rather than a literal "comunistas," which would misleadingly suggest 20th-century Communism.
- **saint-john-francis-regis.md**: Distances in miles converted to approximate kilometers (e.g., "twelve miles" → "quase vinte quilômetros"; "3500 feet" → "mil e sessenta e sete metros"; "fourteen miles" → "cerca de vinte e dois quilômetros") for readability. French place names (Villeneuve-en-Berg → Villeneuve-de-Berg, Sommieres → Sommières, etc.) corrected to their standard modern French spelling/diacritics, since these are proper place names, not translated content. The graphic footnote on Huguenot atrocities was preserved as in the source (including the editorial doubt about its authenticity), per the instruction against dropping author content.
- **saint-jude-helper-in-great-need.md**: This chapter contains the Mass Propers (Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Preface, Communion, Postcommunion) and a Litany. Scripture text (Ephesians 4, John 15, Psalms, Jude's Epistle) was translated fresh from the English source rather than substituted with an existing Portuguese liturgical translation, to stay strictly faithful to this document's specific English wording, per the instruction that translations derive only from the given source. Liturgical formulas (Kyrie/"Lord have mercy", Agnus Dei) rendered in their standard Portuguese devotional form ("Senhor, tende piedade de nós", "Cordeiro de Deus, que tirais os pecados do mundo") since these are fixed, universally recognized liturgical texts, not free composition.
- **saint-edith-stein.md**: The pun "busserl"/"Husserl" (German diminutive for "little kisses") could not be preserved in Portuguese; rendered as "beijos (Küsschen)/Husserl" to keep the sense of the wordplay visible to the reader via the German gloss, with a brief note in-line. Edith Stein's post-profession religious name was expanded from "Sister Benedicta" to the fuller standard form "Irmã Teresa Benedita da Cruz" (matching her universally known religious name, Teresa Benedicta a Cruce / Teresa Benedita da Cruz), since the source itself introduces "Sister Benedicta of the Cross" as her name in religion — this is a completion of the source's own naming, not an addition of new information. Kept "blue-stocking" idiom as "intelectualóide" (a natural PT-BR equivalent for a woman with pretensions to bookish superiority) rather than a literal "meia azul," which would be meaningless in Portuguese.

## General Notes

- All saints' names rendered per the standard PT-BR liturgical/hagiographic forms (São/Santa/Santo per the house rules).
- No editor/scholarly footnotes were dropped in this batch — the one footnote present (in saint-john-francis-regis.md, on the authenticity of the Huguenot-atrocities passage) reads as an author/publisher's own explanatory note integral to the CTS pamphlet, not a later scholarly intrusion, so it was kept and translated in place.
- No offensive/outdated terms required modernization in this batch beyond the "Anglish"→"britânicos" and "Communists"→"comunardos" cases noted above.
