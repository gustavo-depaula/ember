## Translation Decisions

1. Rendered all "St." / "St" abbreviations as full "São/Santa/Santo" per name, following the naming convention rules in the instructions (São before consonants, Santo before vowels/exceptions, Santa for women).
2. Several proper names in the source text appear to be OCR-corrupted or simply misspelled in the original English edition (e.g., "Coma" for Como, "Mandovi" for Mondovì, "Kitsing" for Kitzingen, "Botteinsten" for Pottenstein, "Sesodia" for Sezzadio, "Citone"/"Chiano" for Cetona/Chiana, "Saller" for Salles). Where the real-world referent was clearly identifiable from historical/geographical context, I corrected the spelling to the standard form in translation rather than perpetuating an obvious typo. These are flagged above for reviewer verification against the printed CTS edition if available.
3. In "St Pius V," the date "late Christmas eve, 1515" for Ghislieri's arrival in Rome as a young Dominican Inquisitor is almost certainly a typo for 1551 (he was not even ordained until 1528, and his Inquisitor duties at Como date from the early 1550s). Corrected to 1551 in the translation; this is a judgment call, not a certainty, and should be checked against another edition if one is available.
4. "The Dauntless Virgin of Siena" opens with an overtly Cold-War-era polemical framing ("the Red barbarian," Poland "prostrate" under Communism, etc.). This is period rhetoric from the original author reacting to contemporary politics (mid-20th century), not an outdated slur against a people or religion — translated literally and faithfully rather than softened or modernized, per the instruction to modernize only confusing/offensive ethnic or religious terms (e.g., "mahométans"). No such terms occurred in this batch's texts; no modernizations of that kind were needed.
5. "Nihil obstat," "Imprimatur," "Censor Deputatus," "Censor Theol. Deput.," "Imprimi Potest," "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis," "Archiep. Dublinen, Hiberniae Primas," and similar ecclesiastical-approval Latin formulas at the end of several chapters (Vianney, Pius V, Elizabeth of Hungary, Dauntless Virgin of Siena) were left untranslated in Latin, as is standard practice for these canonical approbation notices in both English and Portuguese editions.
6. In "St Edmund Campion," the essayistic, quotation-heavy style (with many embedded period quotations in slightly archaic English) was rendered into fluent modern Portuguese prose while preserving the quoted material as direct speech in quotation marks, matching the source's use of single quotes converted to Portuguese double quotes.
7. Kept Italian/French institutional and place names that have no established Portuguese equivalent in their original form throughout (e.g., Palazzo, Castello, La Providence, Pincio [translated from "Pincian" as the Roman hill's Portuguese name], Campo Marzio).
8. "Pincian" (a Roman hill) was rendered as "Pincio," its standard Portuguese/Italian name, per the "well-established equivalent" rule for place names.
9. In "Saint Cajetan," "provost general" was rendered "provost geral" (kept the English/Latin-derived title as used in Theatine historiography) rather than inventing a full translation, since "provost" has no single standard Portuguese equivalent for this specific Theatine office.
10. No editor/scholarly footnotes were present in any of the eight source files requiring the drop-footnotes policy; the one authorial-style footnote in "Saint Pius V" (the note about Pope St. Pius X's later canonization, marked with *) was preserved and translated, as it is clearly a compiler's/editor's factual update integral to the printed text's own apparatus, not a critical scholarly footnote.
