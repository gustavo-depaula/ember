## Translation Decisions

- Dropped editor/translator footnote markers where they referred to English-language modernization notes not relevant in Portuguese (e.g. "The English of these quotations has been modernized...") since these are translator's notes about the English text itself.
- Kept Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur lines verbatim in Latin/English as in source, per instructions.
- Scripture quotations translated into standard Portuguese Bible phrasing (Ave Maria style, Catholic Portuguese Bible idiom) rather than a literal word-for-word rendering, to match natural liturgical Portuguese.
- Old scripture citations (e.g. "Matt. 25:40", "Dan 12:3") kept as-is with standard Portuguese abbreviations for book names where used inline (Mt, Dan).
- "St." abbreviation rendered as "São/Santo/Santa" per gender and euphony (e.g. St Ignatius → Santo Inácio, St Francis Xavier → São Francisco Xavier, St Teresa → Santa Teresa).
- Kept proper place names (Le Puy, Montpellier, Le Cheylard, etc.) unchanged.

## Additional Translation Decisions (batch 2)

- **the-life-of-saint-anthony.md**: Confirmed via biographical details (Lisbon birth, Coimbra, Marrakesh, Padua) that this chapter concerns St Anthony of Padua → "Santo Antônio (de Pádua)". A separate figure mentioned in the text, "Anthony the Hermit" (the convent's namesake and source of the saint's religious name), is St Anthony of the Desert → "Santo Antão, o Eremita" — both figures appear in the same chapter and were kept distinct.
- **the-martyrdom-of-saint-perpetua-and-felicitas...**: Dropped the "Endnotes" citation-reference section (Analecta t.3 p.403; Victor l.1 p.4) and inline (1)/(2) markers per the editor-footnote-drop policy — these are scholarly apparatus, not part of the ancient text itself. Latin technical terms (*venatores*, *confectores*) kept in italics with implicit context rather than re-glossed, matching source style.
- **the-adventurous-nun.md**: This is a 1965 CTS pamphlet with strong period rhetoric (Cold War, racial-justice commentary) addressed directly to the reader. Translated faithfully in register, including the era's now-dated terminology for racial descriptions, per the instruction to translate faithfully rather than sanitize a historical primary text.
- **saint-wenceslas.md**: Czech proper names normalized to standard Czech diacritics (Bořivoj, Spytihněv, Přemyslidas, Přibyslava, Bretislau, Soběslav, etc.) since the OCR'd English source had stripped diacritics inconsistently; this matches modern Portuguese-language reference works on Bohemian history.
- **the-mothers-saint.md**: "Domenico" and "Benedetta" (St Gerard Majella's parents) rendered as "Domingos" and "Benedita" for consistency with the convention (elsewhere in this book) of translating first names of family members of saints into their standard Portuguese equivalents.

- Files translated in this pass: saint-louise-de-marillac.md, saint-lucy-of-syracuse.md, saint-margaret-clitherow.md, saint-margaret-mary-alacoque.md, saint-margaret-of-cortona.md, saint-martin-of-tours.md, saint-mary-euphrasia-pelletier.md, saint-mary-magdalen.md.
- **saint-margaret-clitherow.md**: the OCR'd source garbled one sentence together with two mid-paragraph footnotes (about a nephew's martyrdom, and about the Council of the North) so that a sentence about "one of the Douai missionaries" was left dangling mid-clause. Reconstructed the intended sentence ("...came into contact with one of the Douai missionary priests, then beginning to arrive secretly in England. - **saint-margaret-clitherow.md**: kept the source's own footnotes (marked * and **, and the closing bibliographic note on manuscript editions) since these appear to be the original author's own annotations providing biographical/source context, not a later editor's apparatus — translated rather than dropped.
- **saint-mary-magdalen.md**: direct Gospel quotations (Luke 7:36-50, John 12, etc.) translated freshly in natural Catholic Portuguese Bible idiom to match the source's own paraphrastic rendering, rather than substituting a fixed existing Portuguese Bible translation.
- **saint-mary-euphrasia-pelletier.md**: the footnote wordplay "they were all 'Anglish to them" (French non-distinction between Irish/English/Scots) was rendered as "para eles, todos eram simplesmente 'britânicos'" — the pun on "Anglish"/"English" does not carry over, but the sense (French inability to distinguish the nationalities) is preserved; noted here since it's a lossy rendering.
- Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur blocks at the end of all eight files kept verbatim in their original Latin/English form, per project instruction.
