# Translation Journal — Lives of the Saints (CTS) (pt-BR)

Source: en-US
Target: pt-BR

Translated in 10 parallel batches (2026-09-03); this journal merges each batch's terminology notes into one reference. Batch working notes lived under a scratch directory during translation and are not shipped with the book.

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Book has 75 independent short biographies (CTS devotional pamphlets from several eras/authors — some warm mid-20th-century pamphlet style, a few in a denser Butler's-Lives essayistic register, one (Perpetua and Felicitas) an ancient martyrdom account translated in a deliberately more formal/archaic voice). Chapter-to-chapter continuity matters less than in a single-author treatise, but recurring formulas (Nihil obstat/Imprimatur footers, honorifics, saint names shared across chapters) were kept consistent throughout via this shared journal.
- **Fidelity over silent correction.** Several chapters contain apparent factual/date errors or OCR artifacts inherited from the source pamphlets (e.g. a Eucharistic Congress dated "1879" in `saint-pius-x.md` when the correct year is 1897; a canonization "1990" in `saint-rita-of-cascia.md` that should read 1900; an internally inconsistent "1515" in `saint-pius-v.md`; a garbled "Crimean"/"Hilberstadt" in `saint-dominic-confessor.md`/`saint-wenceslas.md`). Project convention settled on: **preserve the source's literal (possibly erroneous) reading rather than silently correcting it** — one chapter (Pius X) initially "corrected" 1879→1897 during translation; this was reverted to match the source and the other chapters' practice. These are flagged here for a future PDF/source-audit pass, not fixed as part of this translation.
- Genuine OCR noise (broken mid-word spacing, stray line breaks) was silently normalized during translation, since it's a scanning artifact and not part of the author's text — distinct from apparent factual errors, which were preserved as literal source-fidelity per the point above.
- Editor/publisher citation footnotes injected mid-paragraph by pagination (e.g. "* Moorman, op. cit., p. 56") were dropped per the default footnote policy; genuine author explanatory asides (place-name etymology, a relic's provenance, historical context an author clearly intended) were kept and translated in place, matching their original inline position rather than converted to markdown footnotes.
- A few chapters use the author's own bracketed citation-list apparatus (`[1]`, `[2]` with Latin work-abbreviations *Or.*, *Carm.*, *Ep.* at the end) rather than markdown `[^N]` footnotes (Gregory Nazianzen, Philip Benizi) — this structure was preserved as-is rather than converted.
- Approximate imperial measurements (miles, feet, stones) tied to descriptive/scene-setting prose (not exact records) were converted to approximate metric in a handful of chapters (Stanislaus Kostka, John Bosco, Padre Pio, Joseph Cafasso) for reader clarity; exact dates, page counts, and other precise figures were always left as given.
- Historical (non-saint) proper names were rendered in their standard Portuguese historical form where well established (Carlos V, Henrique VIII, Lutero, etc.); names with no established Portuguese form were left as in the source (Topcliffe, Cecil, Garnet, and similar English/period figures).
- Scripture quotations embedded in narrative prose were translated fresh from the English source's own wording (to preserve the exact phrasing/emphasis the author chose), not substituted with an existing official Portuguese Bible translation, since the sources give no verse numbers to cross-reference and are themselves quoting an older English translation (Douay-Rheims-flavored).
- The German wordplay in `saint-edith-stein.md` (Husserl/busserl, Stein/pedra) was kept with the German words as-is and the surrounding English explanation translated naturally — the source already glosses both puns inline, so no added translator's note was needed.
