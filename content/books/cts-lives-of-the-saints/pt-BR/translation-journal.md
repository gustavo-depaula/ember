# Translation Journal — Lives of the Saints (CTS) (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (Catholic Truth Society pamphlets, digitized by ecatholic2000.com)
Target: pt-BR

## Format Notes

- Chapters are plain narrative prose: `# Title` then `## Section` subheadings (not every chapter has subsections).
- No footnotes in the source — nothing to drop or preserve on that front.
- Quoted speech in the source uses straight single quotes as a loose approximation of dialogue punctuation (an OCR artifact of the original pamphlets); render as normal Portuguese dialogue/quotation punctuation, don't preserve the single-quote convention literally.
- Keep proper names, place names, and dates as in the source unless a well-known Portuguese form exists (below).

## Translation Decisions

- (seed) No editor footnotes present in this book — no drop/keep decision needed.
- A few chapters (e.g. saint-margaret-clitherow.md) DO contain the *original author's* own asterisk-marked bibliographic citations (not later editor apparatus) — these are kept and translated, since the "no footnotes" note only covers markdown `[^N]` editor-footnote style, which never appears in this book.
- "Don" (Italian religious title, e.g. Don Bosco, Don Cafasso) → "Dom" throughout.
- Mass propers (Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Preface, Communion, Postcommunion), where a chapter includes them in full (e.g. saint-jude-helper-in-great-need.md), use standard Portuguese Missal headings: Intróito, Coleta, Epístola, Gradual, Evangelho, Ofertório, Secreta, Prefácio, Comunhão, Pós-Comunhão.
- One-off untranslatable foreign legal/idiomatic phrases (e.g. French *peine forte et dure*, *les pauvres honteux*) kept italicized in the original with a short parenthetical Portuguese gloss.
- **saint-camillus-de-lellis.md**: the source has visibly OCR-garbled Peru/Ecuador place names ("Popogani, Quamanga, Quayquillo Truxillo", "Cattano" among Italian cholera cities). Rendered using the real, identifiable place names (Popayán, Huamanga, Guayaquil, Trujillo, Catânia) rather than transcribing the garbling — flagged here since it's an interpretive OCR correction, not a straight translation choice.
- **Strigonia** (old name for Esztergom, Hungary) kept as "Strigônia" (attested archaic Portuguese form), matching the source's own archaic usage rather than modernizing.
- Latin phrases the source itself glosses inline (e.g. *Tolle lege* / "Take up and read", *Doctor mellifluus*) are kept in Latin with the Portuguese gloss immediately following, mirroring the source's own bilingual presentation.
- **saint-gregory-nazianzen-b-c.md** and **saint-ignatius-of-loyola.md** are the two exceptions to "no footnotes in this book": both carry a genuine scholarly citation apparatus (bracketed numbers `[1]`–`[36]`/`[1]`–`[8]` referencing the saint's own Orations/Epistles/Carmina, Butler-style) — this is original bibliographic apparatus, not later editorial annotation, so it's kept as-is; only the one prose aside inside note 17 of the Gregory chapter was translated.
- "Mohammedan(s)" is used inconsistently across chapters in this 20th-century source (sometimes "maometano" fits the period diction of a given passage, sometimes "muçulmano" reads more naturally) — both rendered as attested modern Portuguese terms (não pejorativos); if the review pass wants one term project-wide, standardize on "muçulmano(s)/islamismo".

## Known Inconsistencies to Resolve in Review

- **the-adventurous-nun.md**: the source quotes the historical slur "nigger-lovers" (1960s U.S. civil-rights usage) as reported speech describing how such activists were labelled, paralleling the period term "negrophiles" used of Bl. Anne-Marie Javouhey. Rendered descriptively as "amantes de negros" rather than importing an equivalent Portuguese slur — preserves the passage's point (that defending persecuted people invites ugly labels) without reproducing a slur. Kept in quotes as reported speech, same as the source.

## Review Round 1 — Findings & Resolutions (all applied)

A full 10-agent, chapter-by-chapter cross-language audit was run against all 75 chapters. Systemic issues and their fixes, for future reference / consistency in later rounds:

1. **Imprimatur/Nihil Obstat blocks**: several chapters had translated the bishop's Latin title and see (e.g. "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis" → "Arcebispo de Melbourne", "Archiep. Dublinen., Hiberniae Primas" → "Arcebispo de Dublin, Primaz da Hibérnia"). **Policy: keep this Latin boilerplate in Latin always** — "Imprimatur", "Nihil Obstat", "Imprimi Potest", and the bishop's Latin title/see. Only a trailing plain-English date gets translated to Portuguese. Fixed across ~16 chapters.
2. **Lost `##` heading level** on the "@ Bishop Name," signature line before the imprimatur (en-US has it as a level-2 heading; several pt-BR chapters had flattened it to plain text). Fixed in 7 chapters.
3. **Unauthorized mile/feet→km/meter conversions**: found in 11 chapters, not just the originally-flagged ones. Reverted throughout to the original imperial units (in Portuguese: "milhas", "pés", "jardas", "libras"; British "stone" kept as "stone" since it has no natural PT equivalent). Only conversions NOT reverted: chapters where the **English source itself already uses metric** (e.g. saint-maximilian-kolbe.md's "42 kilometres", saint-pius-x.md's "fourteen kilometers", the-life-of-saint-anthony.md's "kilometres", saint-wenceslas.md's "six metres high", padre-pio.md's "five meters" well) — those are correct as translated and must NOT be reverted.
4. **Title-Case headings**: ~20 chapters had English-style Title Case on `#`/`##` headings (capitalizing "Da", "De", "Do", "Que", "É", "Para", "Sua", etc.). Portuguese heading convention in this corpus (confirmed against other translated books, e.g. morrow-my-catholic-faith): only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized; grammatical/function words stay lowercase even when content words keep Title-Case-style capitalization. Fixed mechanically across all chapters.
5. **São/Santo/Santa exceptions**: the phonetic rule (São before consonant, Santo before vowel/silent-H) has real lexicalized exceptions — "São Estanislau Kostka" (not "Santo", despite vowel-initial "E") is the corpus's established form (matches book.json + poet-priest-martyr.md). Conversely "Santo Alberico" and "Santo Estêvão" (vowel-initial) were wrongly rendered "São" in saint-bernard-abbot-of-clairvaux.md — fixed. "São Marcelino" (consonant-initial) was wrongly "Santo" in saint-augustine-bishop-of-hippo.md — fixed.
6. **Chapter-heading vs. book.json TOC-title mismatches**: found in ~10 chapters. Resolved case-by-case, sometimes fixing the chapter body to match the TOC, sometimes updating book.json's TOC entry to match the (better/more complete) chapter body — see book.json diff. Notable: the-martyrdom-of-saint-perpetua-and-felicitas-with-their-companions.md's companions include both men (Revocatus, Saturninus, Secundulus, Saturus) and women (Perpetua, Felicitas), so the grammatically correct Portuguese default for a mixed-gender group is masculine "**seus** Companheiros" — the TOC's earlier "suas Companheiras" was wrong and has been corrected.
7. **Standardized proper-name spellings** used inconsistently across chapters: "São Tomás Moro" (not "Morus" — fixed in poet-priest-martyr.md, saint-margaret-clitherow.md), "Santa Catarina de Siena" (not "de Sena" — fixed in saint-brigid.md), "São Camilo de Lélis" (accented; fixed ~8 occurrences in saint-camillus-de-lellis.md that used unaccented Italian "Lellis"), "Santa Gemma Galgani" (double-m, matches book.json; fixed ~76 occurrences that used "Gema" in saint-gemma-galgani.md).
8. **Missing author citations**: three chapters (saint-francis-of-assisi.md, saint-gertrude.md, mary-mckillop.md) had the *original author's* own bibliographic citation dropped during translation (these got mangled mid-sentence by OCR in the English source, and the translator reconstructed clean prose but silently dropped the citation). Restored as markdown footnotes (`[^1]`) at the equivalent point, per this book's existing precedent for preserving author citations (see saint-margaret-clitherow.md's asterisk notes).
9. **saint-dymphna.md**: the Litany's hard line-breaks (trailing double-space in markdown) were lost in translation, causing the litany to render as one run-on paragraph instead of one line per invocation. Restored.
10. **saint-dominic-confessor.md — SEVERE defect, now fixed**: roughly a third of this chapter's pt-BR text was not a translation of the English source at all — it was fabricated/contaminated content about St. Bernard of Clairvaux (Council of Troyes, founding of the Knights Templar, the 1130 papal election of Innocent II vs. antipope Anacletus), and a real paragraph (Dominic's motive for founding a new kind of preaching order) was missing entirely. The whole chapter was retranslated from scratch from the English original.
11. Assorted single-instance fixes: typos (petiiconou→peticionou, ungüento→unguento, dEle→d'Ele [4 files], atéológicas→ateológicas, patos→pathos, Ensuou-se→Seguiu-se, malabarística→malabarismo, bento/bento→abençoou/benta [gender & verb-form agreement]), factual/content errors (a twentieth mistranslated as "vintena" [a score] in saint-louis-ix-king-of-france-confessor.md — fixed to "um vigésimo"; a "hundredweight" mistranslated 1:1 as "libras" (pounds) in saint-margaret-clitherow.md, understating the weight ~112× — fixed to "quintais"), wrong/undocumented name changes reverted to match the source (Riggo, Coltselli, Monte Citorio, La Sapienza, Vincentians, Halberstadt, Abbé Baux, the parish of Saller, Father Leyssene, São Casimiro), and two citation numbering errors in saint-teresa-of-avila.md ("ii" misread as "11").
12. **the-life-of-saint-john-berchmans-s-j.md**: the Latin Vulgate quote "Vocavi et renuisti; quoque in interitu tuo ridebo" (source, itself a non-canonical singular paraphrase of Proverbs 1:24-26) was rendered "...quapropter et ego in interitu vestro ridebo" — plural "vestro" instead of the source's singular "tuo". Left as-is (not reverted): the whole letter this quote appears in addresses John Berchmans's father with the formal plural "vós"/"vosso" throughout, and "vestro" is consistent with that register, whereas literally matching the source's singular "tuo" would jar with the surrounding Portuguese. Documented here as a deliberate register-consistency choice, not an oversight.
