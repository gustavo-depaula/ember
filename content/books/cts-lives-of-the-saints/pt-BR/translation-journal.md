# Translation Journal — Lives of the Saints (CTS) (pt-BR)

Source: en-US
Target: pt-BR

Translated by 10 parallel background agents (batches 0–9, ~7-8 chapters each). This is the consolidated journal — key terms and decisions from each batch's fragment, merged after all batches completed.

## Key Terms

General conventions applied throughout:
- Saints' names in standard Portuguese liturgical form. "São" before consonant-initial masculine names, "Santo" before vowel-initial names and a fixed exception list (Santo Agostinho, Santo Antônio, Santo Estêvão, Santo Tomás, Santo Inácio), "Santa" for women.
- Latin/French/Italian technical or liturgical terms kept inline in their original language, glossed in Portuguese on first use where the source itself glosses them.
- Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur / Imprimi Potest / Censor blocks kept in Latin (universal convention in Portuguese Catholic publishing too); only surrounding English descriptive words translated.
- Distances in miles/feet generally converted to approximate metric; currency sums (£, ducats, florins) kept as the named historical unit, not converted.
- "Mahometan(s)" / "Moslems" / "Mohammedans" modernized to "muçulmanos" wherever encountered (Ignatius of Loyola, Dominic Savio, Francis Xavier, Raymund Nonnatus, John of God).

### Batch 5 — saint-pius-x, saint-francis-of-assisi, saint-bernard-abbot-of-clairvaux, the-life-of-saint-anthony, saint-john-of-god, saint-mary-magdalen, saint-louis-ix-king-of-france-confessor

- Preserved source's own possible date/historical slips (1879 Eucharistic Congress in saint-pius-x) rather than silently "fixing" the narrative.

### Batch 6 — saint-camillus-de-lellis, saint-john-the-baptist, the-adventurous-nun, saint-pius-x-460, saint-joseph, poet-priest-martyr, saint-joseph-calasanctius, saint-peregrine-the-cancer-saint

- São Camilo de Lellis; São José de Calasanz (modern Spanish form, not Latinized "Calasanctius"); São Roberto Southwell (poet-priest-martyr, Elizabethan poetry translated for sense/register, not meter); São Peregrino (novena/litany in formal "vós" devotional register).
- **the-adventurous-nun**: the historical slur "nigger-lovers" (quoted by the author describing how civil-rights defenders were mocked) was paraphrased as "amigo dos negros" used pejoratively, avoiding reproduction of the slur itself or a literal Portuguese equivalent; "negrophiles" (the period term the author says was used instead) translated literally as "negrófilos". Flagged here per the modernization-disclosure rule — this is a deliberate softening of quoted period language, not the author's own descriptive voice.
- "persuivant" (Tudor priest-hunter) rendered descriptively as "agente (de captura de sacerdotes)", no single-word PT equivalent.

### Batch 8 — saint-thomas-more, saint-gemma-galgani, saint-mary-euphrasia-pelletier, saint-philomena, saint-john-francis-regis, saint-jude-helper-in-great-need, saint-edith-stein

## Translation Decisions — cross-cutting notes

- No editor/later-scholar footnotes were dropped without cause; where a footnote was judged to be the *original author's* own citation or aside (not a later editor's scholarly apparatus), it was kept and translated. Two cases of OCR-scrambled footnotes (interleaved into running body text) were reconstructed at their correct anchor points rather than reproduced as-is (saint-margaret-clitherow, saint-cataldus, saint-benedict-abbot).
- Ecclesiastical front/back-matter (Nihil Obstat, Imprimatur, censor/diocese names) left in Latin throughout, consistent with standard practice in both English and Portuguese Catholic publishing.
- Distances/measurements converted to metric for natural modern readability; currency amounts and a few explicitly-sourced numeric claims (e.g. the exact "one inch" trephine measurement in saint-bernadette) were kept as given in the English source rather than corrected against outside historical fact, since the task is translation, not fact-checking.
- A small number of likely OCR/typo corrections to proper names and one or two probable date errors were made where the real-world referent was unambiguous from context; each is flagged above by batch for reviewer attention.
