## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: Book has no author-added footnotes as a rule; source is OCR'd from CTS devotional pamphlets and marked "not yet proofed against the printed originals" — translated the text as given, without silently correcting apparent OCR artifacts (missing quotation marks, stray characters, a few evident typos/place-name corruptions) beyond what's needed for a coherent Portuguese sentence. Several specific instances are logged by the batch that found them (see below).
- 2026-09-08: Each chapter is a self-contained biography/devotional essay (no shared narrative across chapters), so the book's 75 chapters were translated in 8 parallel batches rather than strictly sequentially. This journal was pre-seeded with common terms and merged afterward from each batch's addendum to keep saints' names and standard Catholic vocabulary consistent.
- 2026-09-08: Ecclesiastical approbation blocks (Nihil Obstat / Imprimi Potest / Imprimatur, censor and bishop names, dates) are kept as in the original throughout the book — Latin formula labels untranslated, personal/place names and dates unchanged — these are historical archival records, not text to adapt. Where the source's surrounding descriptive words are in plain English rather than Latin (e.g. "Diocesan Censor", "Archbishop of Melbourne, 1st July, 1973"), those descriptive words were translated into Portuguese while the Latin labels and proper names/dates stayed untouched.
- 2026-09-08: A handful of "St. Pius X"/"St. Rita" chapters exist as two separate, differently-titled chapters each (e.g. `saint-pius-x.md` "Scourge of Modernism" vs. `saint-pius-x-460.md` "Pope of the Eucharist"; `saint-rita.md` vs. `saint-rita-of-cascia.md`) — each was translated independently, keeping its own distinct title, without merging or cross-referencing content.
- 2026-09-08: Distances/measurements given in imperial units (miles, feet, gallons, stone, pounds, yards) were converted to approximate round-number metric equivalents throughout, for readability by a modern Brazilian audience, since these are narrative color rather than doctrinally precise figures. Historical currency (£, ducats, bezants) was left as named historical currency (libras, ducados, bezantes), not converted.
- 2026-09-08: Secondary historical figures (not the chapter's title saint) with common Latin-alphabet given names were generally translated to their standard Portuguese equivalents (e.g. Giuseppe → José, Peter Abelard → Pedro Abelardo), matching the book's own established convention of "Dom Bosco" for "Don Bosco" — surnames were left unchanged throughout. Personal nicknames with no natural Portuguese equivalent (e.g. "Bepi", "Nanette", "Pippo buono") were kept as in the source, glossed inline where the source itself glosses them.
- 2026-09-08: Well-known Portuguese exonyms were used for place names with an established Portuguese form (Treves → Tréveris, Cîteaux → Cister, Clairvaux → Claraval, Mantua → Mântua, Venice → Veneza, Padua → Pádua); place/personal names without a standard Portuguese form (Czech names in the Wenceslas chapter, English street/court names like Tyburn or Star Chamber, Futunan chiefly names in the Chanel chapter) were kept as in the source.
- 2026-09-08: Outdated ethnic/religious terms modernized: "Mahometan"/"Mussulman"/"Moresco" → "muçulmano", "Mahomet" → "Maomé" (orthography/register only, no change of meaning), consistent with the precedent set on the True Devotion book.
- 2026-09-08: Scholarly citation apparatus that is clearly *authorial* (Butler's-Lives-style bracketed reference numbers to a saint's own writings, e.g. in the Gregory Nazianzen, Ignatius of Loyola, and Augustine chapters) was treated like Scripture references and kept/translated as an end-of-file "Notas" section, rather than dropped as an editor's footnote — dropping it would leave orphaned bracketed numbers with no referent.
- 2026-09-08: Where the source embeds Scripture or extended biblical paraphrase inline as part of the narrator's own prose (rather than as a formal citation), it was translated freshly into natural Portuguese matching the surrounding register, rather than pulled from an existing Portuguese Bible translation, since no specific edition was specified for this task. Flagged for a subject-matter reviewer who may prefer substituting standard Brazilian Catholic liturgical/Scriptural wording.
- 2026-09-08: Embedded poems/hymns/carols (Southwell's poems, the "Svatý Václave" canticle, "Good King Wenceslas", the Chanel chapter's hymn, Thomas More's verse) were translated for sense and imagery, preserving line breaks, rather than forced into rhyme or meter — a rhymed technical translation risked distorting theological content.
- 2026-09-08: A few evident OCR/source errors were translated literally rather than silently corrected, per house style, and are flagged here for a possible follow-up fix: `saint-rita-of-cascia.md` gives her canonization year as 1990 (historically 1900); `saint-dominic-confessor.md` has a garbled place reference rendered literally as "na Criméia"; `saint-martin-of-tours.md`'s companion chapter `saint-stanislaus-kostka.md` lists "Salerno" where "Salorno" (an Alpine town) seems intended; `saint-pius-x.md`'s conclave tally spells one cardinal's name inconsistently ("Goti"/"Gotti") exactly as the source does; `saint-patrick.md` retains an orphaned footnote marker whose footnote text was lost to OCR.

## Expressions & Idioms

- "a burning and a shining light" (of John the Baptist) → "uma lâmpada que arde e resplandece" (standard Portuguese liturgical/biblical phrasing).
- "Behold the Lamb of God" → "Eis o Cordeiro de Deus" (Agnus Dei form).
- "a voice crying in the wilderness" → "voz do que clama no deserto" (Isaiah 40:3 / Advent liturgy phrasing).
- "a dead letter" → "letra morta" (existing Portuguese idiom with the same sense).
- Period English idioms and turns of phrase throughout were translated by sense rather than literal equivalents.
