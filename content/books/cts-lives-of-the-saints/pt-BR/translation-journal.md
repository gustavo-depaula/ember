# Translation Journal — Lives of the Saints (CTS) (pt-BR)

Source: en-US
Target: pt-BR

75 short, individually authored saint biographies from the Catholic Truth Society
pamphlet series (20th century). Correction to the initial scan: most files have no
footnotes, but a handful (saint-ignatius-of-loyola, saint-gregory-nazianzen-b-c,
saint-rose-of-lima, saint-pius-x, the-martyrdom-of-saint-perpetua-and-felicitas)
carry the *author's own* endnote-style citations (scripture/bibliographic refs,
e.g. "Acts ii.", "Exer. Spir. Max. 2, 3.") — these are not later editorial apparatus,
so per policy they were translated and kept, not dropped.
Register: modern devotional English prose — narrative biography, not scholarly.
Portuguese should match: clear, dignified, warm modern Brazilian Portuguese, not archaic.

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-15: Translated all 75 chapters via 10 parallel batch agents (en-US → pt-BR). Consolidated report below from all batches' final summaries.
- Saints' names rendered in standard Portuguese form throughout (e.g. "St Francis of Assisi" → "São Francisco de Assis", "St Teresa of Avila" → "Santa Teresa de Ávila", "St Augustine" → "Santo Agostinho"). Non-Portuguese-tradition saints (Mary MacKillop, Padre Pio, Edith Stein) kept their names as-is or only lightly localized (first name only where natural).
- "Mohammedans"/"Moslems"/"mahometans" modernized to "muçulmanos" wherever it appeared (St Francis Xavier, St Francis of Assisi, St Ignatius, St Raymund Nonnatus chapters), per the outdated-terms guideline.
- Distances/measurements given in miles/feet/yards in several chapters (Patrick, Chanel, Dominic Savio, John Francis Regis, John Bosco, and others) were converted to approximate metric equivalents for Brazilian readability rather than transliterated — a consistent judgment call across batches, not a literal-fidelity issue, but worth knowing when reviewing for "content changes."
- Several chapters had OCR artifacts in the English source (corrupted place/person names, a stray editorial fragment wedged mid-sentence in saint-john-fisher, garbled punctuation in the two Pius X chapters, a wrong canonization year in saint-rita-of-cascia "1990" vs the correct "1900" also given in the companion saint-rita chapter). Translators silently corrected these where the intended reading was unambiguous (documented per-chapter in translator batch reports) rather than propagating the corruption. One exception: saint-louis-ix-king-of-france-confessor and saint-margaret-clitherow have mid-sentence OCR footnote-marker artifacts that were preserved in place rather than corrected — worth a second look in review.
- "the-adventurous-nun" chapter (about Bl. Anne-Marie Javouhey) quotes period racist language critically; the translator rendered the era-appropriate pejorative rather than euphemizing it, since the passage's point is quoting/critiquing that racism — flagged for review to confirm the Portuguese rendering doesn't read as gratuitously offensive versus merely faithful.
- saint-rita.md and saint-rita-of-cascia.md, and saint-pius-x-460.md and saint-pius-x.md, are each two distinct source pamphlets about the same saint (not duplicates) — translated independently with distinct Portuguese titles.
