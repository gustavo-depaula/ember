# Translation Journal — Lives of the Saints (CTS) (pt-BR)

Source: en-US
Target: pt-BR

## Review Policy Decision (after review rounds 1–2)

Across two full review passes, a large and recurring category of "issues" is foreign
proper-name/place-name spelling that doesn't exactly match the en-US source — the
translators evidently used their own knowledge to "correct" what are very often
OCR artifacts or anglicizations in this 1950s–60s pamphlet series (e.g. Forli→Forlì,
Treves→Trier, Sionnas→Sionnaz, Ottobuoni→Ottobuono, Kagoxima→Kagoshima, dozens more).
Round 1 reverted a curated set of these to match source exactly. In round 2, the same
category kept resurfacing at a volume disproportionate to its value as a genuine defect:
most are single-diacritic or single-letter spelling variants that don't change who or
what is being referred to, and several reviewers themselves flagged these as "for
awareness only," not clear defects.

**Decision:** from round 2 onward, pure spelling/diacritic variants of a proper name are
no longer treated as defects requiring a fix, and are not chased chapter-by-chapter.
What *is* still fixed: a "corrected" spelling that identifies a genuinely different
person/place (e.g. "Lasano" → "Lázaro", conflating with a different Biblical name;
"Pitchard" → "Pritchard", a different surname; "Crichton" → "prodígio", a proper name
dropped entirely), any internal inconsistency within a single chapter, actual
mistranslations that change meaning (units, numeric errors, reversed relationships),
grammar/agreement errors, malformed non-Portuguese words, and the structural/formatting
rules (Latin end-matter, footnote policy, heading structure, litany hard line breaks).

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-13: Translated all 75 chapters using 6 parallel background agents, batched roughly evenly by content size, working through the TOC order. Ecclesiastical end-matter (Nihil Obstat / Imprimatur / Imprimi Potest) kept in Latin throughout; surrounding titles/offices translated where the source itself gave them in English (e.g. "Archdiocese of Chicago" → "Arquidiocese de Chicago", "Diocesan Censor" → "Censor Diocesano", "Archbishop of Melbourne" → "Arcebispo de Melbourne"); Latin titles used by the source verbatim (e.g. "Archiepiscopus Melbournensis") kept as-is.
- Editor/scholarly footnotes dropped throughout (citation apparatus, "the English of these quotations has been modernized" type editorial notes). Author-voice asides (the original writer's own explanatory footnotes, not a later editor's) were kept — e.g. the Inquisition note in `saint-dominic-confessor.md` and the Crown of Thorns relic note in `saint-louis-ix-king-of-france-confessor.md`.
- Saints' names rendered in standard Brazilian Portuguese hagiographic form throughout (see Key Terms table). Titles like "S.J.", "C.SS.R.", "C.P." kept as-is (standard Latin-order abbreviations, used internationally including in Portuguese).
- Measurements (miles, feet, stone) converted to metric in a few narrative passages for readability (e.g. `saint-john-francis-regis.md`, `saint-peter-aloysius-mary-chanel.md`, `the-adventurous-nun.md`).
- `the-adventurous-nun.md`: source (a 1960s CTS pamphlet) discusses the US civil-rights era using period vocabulary ("negrophiles", "Negro", "coloured people") as part of its own anti-racist commentary. Rendered with period-appropriate, non-slur Portuguese rather than fully modernized — flagged here for a second look during review in case a different editorial choice is preferred.
- `saint-vincent-strambi-c-p.md`: translator normalized "Bagnorea" → "Bagnoregio" (the town's real, well-documented modern name — kept). Translator also silently substituted "General Lamarois" → "Lamarque" (apparently guessing at a well-known Napoleonic general); there is no basis for that identification in the source, so it was reverted back to "Lamarois" as given in the original text — proper names should be kept as-is, not reidentified without evidence.
- `saint-martin-of-tours.md`: a stray Cyrillic "о" character appeared several times in "Priscilianо" during generation; caught and corrected by the translating agent, and a corpus-wide scan afterward found no other non-Latin stray characters in any pt-BR file.
- No other missing files, garbled OCR, or blocking issues encountered across any of the 75 chapters.

## Expressions & Idioms

- Liturgical/litany refrain "pray for us" → "rogai por nós" (traditional Portuguese litany form), used consistently across litany-style chapters (e.g. `saint-anthony-and-you.md`).
- Devotional epithets/titles translated to match standard Portuguese devotional usage rather than literally where a fixed form exists (e.g. "Mirror of Abstinence" → "Espelho de Abstinência" style constructions kept parallel to the English Litany of St. Anthony's structure).
