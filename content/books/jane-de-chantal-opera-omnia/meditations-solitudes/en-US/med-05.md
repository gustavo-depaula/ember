# Fifth meditation. Death

**First point.**

Kefleot, O mortals, that it would be a great offence against God to die without having thought of death. Death, which dominates over this perishable life, observes no fixed rules; at one time it takes this person, at another that, without choice or method, the good and the bad, the young and the old. O how blessed are they who live in a continual fear of death, and who are at all times prepared so to die, th#t they may live forever in that life in which there is no death!

**Second point.**

Alas! what will be the outcome of this reckoning? I do not know; for all this is hidden in the future.

**Third point.**

Reflect that the just man does not die an unprovided death, for he has amply provided for the dread moment by persevering in Christian integrity and in religious obedience. Moreover, holy Church does not pray that we may not die a sudden, "but an unprovided, death.
