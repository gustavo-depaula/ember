On the day of Pentecost, the Holy Apostles received, as we have seen, the graoe of the Holy Ghost. In accordance with the injunction of their divine Master, 1 they will soon start on their mission of teaohing all nations, and baptising men in the name of the Holy Trinity. It was but right, then, that the solemnity which is intended to honour the mystery of One God in Three Persons, should immediately follow that of Pentecost, with whioh it has .a mysterious connection. And yet, it was not till after many centuries, that it was inserted in the Cycle of the Liturgical Year, whose completion is the work of successive ages.

Every homage paid to God by the Churoh 's Liturgy has the Holy Trinity as its object. Time, as well as eternity, belongs to the Trinity. The Trinity is the scope of all Religion. Every day, every hour, belongs to It. The Feasts instituted in memory of the mysteries of our Redemption oentre in It. The Feasts of the Blessed Virgin and the Saints are but so many means for leading us to the praise of the God who is One in essence, and Three in Persons. The Sunday's Offioe, in a very speoial way, gives us, each week, a most explioit expression of adoration and worship of this mystery, which is the foundation of all others, and the source of all grace.

A Feast is the memorial of some fact which took plaoe at a certain time, and of whioh it is well to perpetuate the remembrance and influenoe. How could this be applied to the mystery of the Trinity P It was from all eternity, it was before any created being existed, that God liveth and reigneth, Father, Son, and Holy Ghost. If a feast in honour of that Mystery were to be instituted, it could only be by fixing some one day in the Year, whereon the Faithful would assemble for offering a more than usually solemn tribute of worship to the Mystery of Unity and Trinity in the one same divine Nature.

The idea of such a Feast was first oonceived by some of those pious and recollected souls, who are favoured from on high with a sort of presentiment of the things which th Holy Ghost will achieve, at a future period, in the Church. So far back as the 8th Century, the learned monk, Alcuin, had had the happy thought of oomposing a Mass in honour of the mystery of the blessed Trinity. It would seem that he was prompted to this by the Apostle of Germany, Saint Boniface. That this composition is a beautiful one, no one will doubt that knows, from Alouin's writings, how full its author was of the spirit of saored Liturgy ; but, after all, it was only a votive Mass, a mere help to private devotion, whioh no one ever thought would lead to the institution of a Feast. This Mass, however, became a great favourite, and was gradually circulated through the several Churches ; for instance, it was approved of for Germany, by the Council of Selingenstadt, held in 1022.

In that 11th Century, however, a Feast properly so called of Holy Trinity had been introduced into one of the Churches of Belgium, — the very same that was to have the honour, later on, of procuring to the Church's Calendar one of the richest of its Solemnities. (Stephen, Bishop of liege, solemnly instituted the Feast of Holy Trinity for his Churoh, in 920, and had an entire Office composed in honour of the mystery. The Church's law, which now reserves to the Holy See the institution of any new Feast, was not then in existenoe ; and Eiquier, Stephen's successor in the See of Liege, kept up what his predecessor had begun.

The Feast became gradually adopted. The Benediotine Order took it up from the very first. We find, for instance, in the early part of the 11th Century, that Berno, the Abbot of Beiohnaw, was doing all he could to propagate it. At Cluny, also, the Feast was established at the commencement of the same Century, as we learn from the Ordinarium of that celebrated Monastery, drawn up in 1091, and where we find mention of Holy Trinity day as having been instituted long before.

Under the pontificate of Alexander the Second, who reigned from 1061 to 1073, the Churoh of Rome, which has frequently sanctioned the usages of particular Churohes, by herself adopting them, was led to pass judgment upon this new institution.
