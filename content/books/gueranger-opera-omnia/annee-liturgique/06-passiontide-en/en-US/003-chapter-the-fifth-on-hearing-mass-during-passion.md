The holy Mass is the true sacrifioe, of whioh the sacrifices of the old Law were but figures. This sacrifioe was expeoted by mankind for four thousand years. It was during the present season that it was first offered up. It is now mysteriously renewed, eaoh day, upon our Christian altars.

No greater glory can be given to God than the celebration of this sacrifice, wherein God Himself is the Yiotim ; at the same time, nothing can be more advantageous to man than to partake of this divine Victim, to become himself this Viotim, by incorporating it with himself by holy Communion, whereby is realized that wonderful promise of our Redeemer: He that eateth My Flesh and drinketh My Blood, abideth in Me, and I in him.

Now, it is by the immolation of our Redeemer on the cross that the Flesh of this Lamb of God has beoome truly our food, and His Blood truly our drink. By the mysteries of His Incarnation and birth, we had Him as our Brother ; His Passion and death have made Him both our Saviour and our Food. Thus was realized that figurative sacrifioe whioh God prescribed to His people through Moses, and in which the viotim, after being immolated, was to be eaten by the priest who offered it, and by the person in whose name it was offered.
