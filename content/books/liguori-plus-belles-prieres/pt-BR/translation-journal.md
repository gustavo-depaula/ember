# Translation Journal — Les Plus Belles Prières de Saint Alphonse de Liguori (pt-BR)

Source: fr-FR
Target: pt-BR

## Footnote policy for this book

Unlike most books in the corpus, the footnotes here are **not** later editorial
apparatus — they are Père Saint-Omer's own citation system, giving the volume
and page in St. Alphonsus's *Œuvres complètes* (Casterman edition) from which
each prayer was drawn (explained by the compiler himself in `preface.md`,
footnote 34). **Keep all footnotes**, translating only the natural-language
text inside them (e.g. rubric-like editorial asides, indulgence notices).
Citation shorthand (`*III. p. 41.*`, `*Jo. 16. 24.*`, canon/session references)
stays as in the source — these point at a specific French print edition and a
Latin Vulgate numbering that don't change with the reader's language.
Indulgence-decree dates had their month name translated (e.g. "17 déc. 1886"
→ "17 dez. 1886") while day/year numerals were left untouched, since a date is
natural language, not a citation key.

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: Decided to preserve all footnote markers and citation shorthand
  verbatim (see policy note above) rather than dropping them as "editor
  footnotes," since they are integral to this compiler's own apparatus.
- 2026-09-02: `170^me^ ÉDITION` (French ordinal superscript) rendered as
  `170.ª edição` in Portuguese, using `^a^` superscript syntax consistent with
  Portuguese ordinal notation. This applies only to the book-title ordinal —
  citation-shorthand ordinals inside footnotes (e.g. `5^e Cons.`) are **not**
  modernized; they stay in French form since they're part of the citation key
  into the Casterman edition, not prose.
- 2026-09-02: `table-des-matieres.md` and `table-alphabetique.md` (the printed
  edition's table of contents and alphabetical index) are translated for
  structural completeness, but the page numbers are left as in the French
  source — they reference the original Casterman print edition's pagination,
  which has no equivalent in this digital, unpaginated corpus. Headings and
  entry text are translated to pt-BR. `table-alphabetique.md` was fully
  re-sorted alphabetically by the new Portuguese headword (only a modest
  fraction of entries changed initial letter), with a short translator's note
  at the top explaining the page numbers still reference the French edition.
- 2026-09-02: `calendrier.md` — French print abbreviations for saint titles
  (`év.`, `m.`, `conf.`, `v.`) spelled out as full Portuguese words (*bispo*,
  *mártir*, *confessor*, *virgem*), since the digital corpus has no
  print-column-width constraint. A few internal gender inconsistencies in the
  1878 source (feminine "sainte" paired with a male saint's name) were
  normalized to the correct grammatical gender in Portuguese rather than
  reproduced as an apparent typo. The Sunday-letter column (`L.`) values (A,
  b, c, CB, GF, etc.) were left untouched as structural/technical codes, same
  treatment as page numbers.
- 2026-09-02: Fixed/universal liturgical texts (Ordinary of the Mass, Te Deum,
  Marian antiphons, Litany of Loreto, Anima Christi, Ave Maris Stella, Stabat
  Mater, Salve Regina, Memorare) are rendered using the standard/traditional
  Portuguese liturgical wording a Catholic reader would recognize, rather than
  a fresh literal word-for-word translation of Père Saint-Omer's French
  gloss — a deliberate departure from strict literalism in favor of
  recognizability, consistent with the skill's guidance that liturgical
  formulas "may be given in the target language depending on how recognizable
  they are."
- 2026-09-02: The Penitential Psalms (`p2-confession.md`) and Song of Songs
  quotations (`p2-aspirations-soupirs.md`, the Visites files) were translated
  fresh from Père Saint-Omer's own French prose rendering rather than
  substituted with an existing standard Portuguese Bible/psalter translation,
  since his wording sometimes paraphrases rather than quoting the Vulgate
  directly and the goal is fidelity to his specific text.

## Flagged for the review pass

A handful of judgment calls from the translating agents are worth a second
look during review:

- `calendrier.md`: several obscure/minor saints rendered by best-effort
  phonetic transliteration due to uncertain identification ("Patrone" Apr 15,
  "Gite" Apr 27, "Théoline" Nov 5, "Aloïde" Oct 22, "Miron" Aug 17).
- `p3-visites-21-31.md`, footnote [^24]: source citation reads `*Jo.* 13. 24.`
  but context (Job's speech) suggests it should be Job, not John — kept
  verbatim per citation policy; possible source-original typo.
- `p4-saint-esprit.md`, section heading "L'amour est une lumière qui
  désaltère" — content is entirely about water/thirst, heading says "light"
  (probably a source typo for "eau"/"source"); translated literally as
  written rather than silently corrected.
- `p4-saints.md`, footnotes `[^10]`/`[^11]` (`*VIII. Neuv. de Ste Th.*`): kept
  verbatim as citation shorthand — ambiguous whether it's a volume/page
  citation or a novena-day reference.
- `p2-communion-5-6.md`: an odd numbered-list header "*Vis. 1.*" / "*2.*" in
  the French source reads like a truncated OCR of "*Avis 1.*" (an advisory
  note) — rendered as "Aviso 1." to preserve the apparent meaning.
- `p4-vierge-fetes.md`: a numbering gap in the French source's list ("2°...
  jumps past 3°") was filled in with "3º" for consistency — a minor
  correction, not a content change. A grammatical gap in "Autre prière au
  Cœur de Marie" (missing verb) was smoothed by supplying "sois".
- `p4-saint-esprit.md` / `p4-vierge-prieres.md` share a near-duplicate Novena
  to the Holy Spirit passage (apparently repeated verbatim by the compiler,
  with a minor wording variant) — preserved faithfully in both places.
