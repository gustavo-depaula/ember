# Translation Journal — Lives of the Saints (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (Alban Butler, Benziger Bros. ed. 1894, abridgment by John Gilmary Shea)
Target: pt-BR

## Heading Format

Source chapter headings read: `# Month Day.—ST. NAME, Title` (e.g. `# March 17.—ST. PATRICK, Bishop, Apostle of Ireland`).

Portuguese chapters use: `# Dia de Mês — SÃO/SANTA NOME, Título` (e.g. `# 17 de Março — SÃO PATRÍCIO, Bispo, Apóstolo da Irlanda`). Em dash with spaces, day before month (Brazilian convention), month name capitalized, no period after the day number.

`## Reflection` → `## Reflexão`

## Translation Decisions

- Saints' names rendered in their standard Portuguese hagiographic form (e.g. Patrick → Patrício, Bridget/Bridgid → Brígida, Francis → Francisco, Thomas → Tomás, Joseph → José, Catherine → Catarina, Jerome → Jerônimo). Where no standard Portuguese form exists (many minor/obscure saints), keep the name transliterated as closely as possible to Portuguese orthography or leave as in the original if it is already a Latin/international form.
- "Ss." / "Sts." (plural) → "Sãos"/"Santas" is unidiomatic; use "São ... e São ..." or "Santos ..." for groups of male saints, "Santas" for groups of female saints, mixed groups use "Santos".
- Numbered TOC-style short titles (e.g. "17. St. Patrick, Bishop, Apostle of Ireland") are mirrored per chapter to populate `book.json`.
- Editor apparatus: none present in this source (plain 1894 devotional abridgment) — no footnotes to drop.
- July 7–25 batch: saints rendered as Panteno, Efrém, Tiago (Bishop of Nisibis, Jul 11, and the Apostle, Jul 25 — both "São Tiago"), João Gualberto, Eugênio, Boaventura, Enrique (Emperor), Simão Stock, Aleixo (standard Portuguese hagiographic form "Santo Aleixo", not the literal transliteration "Alexio" — vowel-initial name takes "Santo"), Camilo de Lélis, Vicente de Paulo, Jerônimo Emiliani, Margarida, Vítor, Maria Madalena, Apolinário, Cristina, Isabel de Portugal, Felicidade (mother of the Seven Brothers). Persian king "Sapor II" kept as-is (standard Portuguese form). "Amidus" → "Amida". "Huneric"→"Hunerico", "Gontamund"→"Gontamundo", "Thrasimund"→"Trasamundo" (standard Vandal-king Portuguese forms).
- Dec 25–Feb 11 batch: "Candlemas" → "Candelária" (feast name kept traditional, not literal "Missa das Velas"); "Sanhedrim" → "Sinédrio"; St. Blase → São Brás; St. Dorothy → Santa Doroteia; St. Sylvester → São Silvestre; St. Ignatius (of Antioch) → Santo Inácio; St. Sabinus → São Sabino; St. Romuald → São Romualdo; St. Severinus → São Severino; St. John of Matha → São João de Mata; St. Scholastica → Santa Escolástica; St. Apollonia → Santa Apolônia; St. Bridgid → Santa Brígida; St. Jane of Valois → Santa Joana de Valois; Dioscorus → Dióscoro; Theophilus → Teófilo; Clovis → Clóvis.
- Feb 12–29 batch: Benedict of Aniane → Bento de Aniano; Catherine of Ricci → Catarina de Ricci; Faustinus/Jovita → Faustino/Jovita; John de Britto → João de Britto (kept Portuguese "Britto" spelling, his actual surname); Onesimus → Onésimo; Flavian → Flaviano; Eutyches → Êutiques; Dioscorus → Dióscoro; Simeon (son of Cleophas) → Simão; James the Lesser → Tiago Menor; Barbatus → Barbato; Romuald (Lombard prince) → Romualdo; Grimoald → Grimoaldo; Eucherius → Euquério; Charles Martel → Carlos Martel (kept, standard PT form); Severianus → Severiano; Scythopolis → Citópolis; Eudoxia → Eudóxia; Peter Damian → Pedro Damião; Fonte-Avellano → Fonte Avelana; Serenus → Sereno; Sirmium → Sírmio (rendered "Sirmio" per Pannonia usage); Pannonia → Panônia; Matthias → Matias; Barsabas → Barsabás; Tarasius → Tarásio; Eucratia → Eucrátia; Porphyry → Porfírio; Scete/Sceté → Cítis (desert of Scetis, standard PT "Cítis" or "Nítria region"; kept as Cítis); Leander → Leandro; Hermenegild → Hermenegildo; Leovigild → Leovigildo; Recared → Recaredo; Suevi → Suevos; Romanus/Lupicinus → Romano/Lupicino; Oswald (Bishop of Worcester/York) → Osvaldo; Odo → Odo (kept); Dunstan → Dunstano; Ethelwold → Etelwoldo; Wilfrid → Wilfrido.
- `feb-23-serenus-a-gardener.md` has no dated heading in the English source (secondary entry sharing Feb 23 with St. Peter Damian) — Portuguese mirrors this: `# SÃO SERENO, Jardineiro, Mártir` with no day/month prefix.
- `feb-27-leander.md` and `feb-28-sts-romanus-and-lupicinus.md` have no "## Reflection" section in the source — Portuguese versions omit it too, matching structure.
- `sep-09-omer.md`: en-US source says St. Omer died "on a pastoral visit to Wavre" — pt-BR reads "Wavrans" (Wavrans-sur-l'Aa, near Thérouanne, the historically correct place associated with St. Omer; "Wavre" is a different town in modern Belgium). Deliberate correction, consistent with this same file's other logged corrections (Luxen→Luxeuil, Terouenne→Thérouanne).
- `sep-25-firmin.md`: en-US source says "the river Lea" — pt-BR reads "rio Lee" (the actual river through Cork is the Lee; "Lea" appears to be a source typo). Deliberate correction.
- `jul-15-henry-emperor.md` (round 5 fix): en-US "his guardian, St. Wolfgang" was mistranslated as "seu anjo da guarda, São Wolfgango" ("his guardian angel") — St. Wolfgang was Henry's human mentor/tutor, not an angel. Corrected to "seu guardião, São Wolfgango".

## Post-merge review (round 8) — TOC/heading consistency sweep

A mechanical sweep comparing every `book.json` pt-BR TOC title against its chapter's own H1 found **31 divergences** that all seven prior review rounds missed. Prior rounds compared chapter files against en-US but never compared `book.json` against the chapter headings, so these were invisible to them.

- **26 São/Santo mismatches** where the TOC read "São X" but the chapter heading read "SANTO X" (Apolinário ×2, Aelredo, Hilário, Honorato, Euquério, Osvaldo, Eulógio ×2, Hermenegildo, Apolônio, Antonino, Epifânio, Ivo, Heliodoro, Efrém, Eugênio, Eleutério, Eduardo, Evaristo, Huberto, Estanislau ×2, Edmundo, Odão, Eusébio). All resolved in favour of the chapter heading ("Santo" before vowel-initial / silent-H names).
- **`jul-15-henry-emperor`**: TOC read "São Enrique" — the Spanish form, and inconsistent with the chapter's "Santo Henrique". → "Santo Henrique".
- **`nov-28-james-of-la-marca-of-ancona`**: TOC read "São Jaime da Marca" vs chapter "SÃO TIAGO DA MARCA". James → Tiago per the convention above. → "São Tiago".
- **`apr-07-hegesippus`**: TOC still read "Hegesipo" — the round-4 diacritic fix was applied to the chapter but never propagated to `book.json`. → "Hegésipo".
- **`jun-28-iren-us`**: TOC read "São Ireneu" vs chapter "SANTO IRINEU". → "Santo Irineu".
- **`mar-23-sts-victorian-and-others`**: TOC read "Victoriano" (Latin form) vs chapter "VITORIANO". → "Vitoriano".

**Convention clarification.** The round-4 note treated São/Santo as lexicalized per name and said to flag only when heading and body disagree *within* a chapter. That test is too narrow: it misses (a) `book.json` disagreeing with the chapter it names, and (b) the same saint taking different honorifics in *different* chapters. Both are user-visible. The standard is now: **one rendering per name across the whole book, TOC included.** Cross-file body fixes made under it: São→Santo Hilário (`jan-16-honoratus`), São→Santo Henrique ×3 (`mar-03-cunegundes`), Santo→São Paulino (`dec-24-delphinus`), São→Santo Edmundo ×3 (`apr-03-richard-of-chichester`), São→Santo Eusébio (`aug-14-eusebius`), SÃO→SANTO Estanislau (`may-07-stanislas`).

### Considered and deliberately not changed

- `jul-02-the-visitation-of-the-blessed`: en-US quotes St. Luke as "a city of **Judea**"; pt-BR reads "cidade de **Judá**". "Judá" is the standard Portuguese Catholic rendering of Luke 1:39 (Vulgate *in civitatem Iuda*). Kept — matching the en-US wording here would make the scripture quotation non-standard in Portuguese.
- `sep-25-firmin`: "rio Lee" was briefly "corrected" to the en-US "Lea" during this round and then **reverted**. The journal already logs "Lee" as a deliberate fix — the river at Cork is the Lee — and it stands. Reviewers: read this file's logged corrections before flagging a place name against en-US.
- `apr-03-richard-of-chichester`: "eight miles" → "treze quilômetros" is a correct unit conversion, not a numeric error.

### Content corrections (round 10)

| File | Was | Now | Why |
|------|-----|-----|-----|
| `nov-02-malachi` | "ele **mandou celebrar** muitas Missas por ela" | "ele **celebrou** muitas Missas por ela" | en-US "he said many Masses for her" — Malaquias, a bishop, offered them himself. "Mandou celebrar" hands the Masses to someone else, and contradicts the next sentence, where he is the one who "oferecera o Adorável Sacrifício" |
| `nov-19-elizabeth-of-hungary` | "criada na corte de **seu** pai" | "criada na corte do **pai dele**" | en-US "brought up in **his** father's court" — Louis's father. Portuguese "seu" attaches to the sentence subject (Isabel), reversing the fact: she was raised at the Thuringian court, not her own father's in Hungary |

Reviewer note: English possessives (`his`/`her`) collapse into Portuguese `seu`, which then reattaches to the subject. Any `seu` rendering a cross-referent possessive needs `dele`/`dela` or the name spelled out — this class survived four review rounds precisely because the Portuguese reads perfectly well.
