## Translation Decisions

- 2026-10-10: Address. Augustine writes to Valentinus and his monks together: vós throughout, except where the source turns to a singular "you" (ch. 15 "To a man who holds such views", ch. 20 "works which you suppose have their origin in yourself"), which takes tu. Scripture addressed to an individual keeps tu. God addressed in prayer and psalm takes lowercase vós ("Sede vós o meu auxílio", "Convertei-nos, ó Deus"), as in `augustine/enchiridion/pt-BR`. Pronouns for God are lowercase (ele, nele).
- 2026-10-10: Lord's Prayer as in `augustine/enchiridion`: "Perdoai-nos as nossas dívidas, assim como nós perdoamos aos nossos devedores", "E não nos induzais em tentação", "Santificado seja o vosso nome".
- 2026-10-10: Scripture quotations follow the source's wording clause by clause, not a Portuguese Bible (e.g. ch. 4 "a sua vontade foi a lei do Senhor"; ch. 13 Song 8:5 "apoiada no seu parente"; ch. 22 Rom 7:13 "se tornasse desmedido pelo mandamento"; ch. 31 "Fazei-vos um coração novo" against "Dar-vos-ei", since the argument turns on the pair). Every chapter:verse reference is mirrored with Portuguese book names (Sirach → Eclesiástico, Song of Songs → Cântico dos Cânticos); the reference sequence matches the source exactly (228 references). Quotations the source leaves without a reference stay without one; unquoted Scripture in the source (ch. 38 John 15:16, ch. 45 "I have hardened … Pharaoh's heart") stays unquoted.
- 2026-10-10: Ch. 42 "the 104th Psalm" (Vulgate numbering) → "no Salmo 104", unchanged.
- 2026-10-10: Headings keep the source's bracketed Benedictine section numbers, including the two without a period ("[XXI]", "[XXIII]"); the inline "[III.]" in ch. 5 is kept in place. Chapter titles in sentence case, without the closing period, per the Augustine siblings.
- 2026-10-10: The source carries no footnotes (editor or author); nothing dropped, no translator notes added. The *Retractations* extract and the dedication line are part of the source body and were translated.
- 2026-10-10: The *Retractations* extract quotes the opening words with straight quotes; the pt-BR uses curly quotes throughout, as the rest of the file and the siblings do.

## Source slips (mirrored, not corrected)

- Ch. 4 "Be not overcome of evil" cited as Romans 12:1 (the verse is 12:21, cited correctly in ch. 5 and 8).
- Ch. 4 "Give no heed to a worthless woman" cited as Proverbs 5:2; ch. 7 "Keep yourself continent" cited as 1 Timothy 5:22; ch. 37 cites Proverbs 3:16 for "law and mercy upon her tongue" (LXX numbering).
- Ch. 35 1 John 2:10 "abides in the right" (the verse reads "light"); rendered "permanece no que é reto". Possibly an r/l scan slip, but not unmistakable, so en-US was left alone.
- Ch. 39 "From God the Father … and the Lord Jesus Christ" is cited as John 1:5; it continues Ephesians 6:23.
- Retractations extract "monastry" (spelling); translated as "mosteiro".

### Round 2

Focus: a bilingual read, clause by clause, of the whole text (subtitle, *Retractations* extract, dedication line and 46 chapters), plus a rerun of the mechanical audit and the Key Terms frequency check. Verdict: 3 defects, all fixed. The mechanical audit and the Key Terms table are clean.

Fixed:
- Ch. 32, Ps 36:23: “e ele quererá o seu caminho” → “e ele quer o seu caminho”. The source has the present tense (“his way does He will”).
- Ch. 37, parenthesis: “(embora … tenha vindo a graça …, e a lei sem a graça seja a letra que mata, e na graça o Espírito que vivifica)” → “(embora … tenha vindo a graça …; mas a lei sem a graça é a letra que mata, e na graça é o Espírito que vivifica)”. In the source, “although” governs only the first clause (“but the law without grace is the letter which kills”). The subjunctive turned the two assertions into concessions.
- Ch. 24 (end) and ch. 25 (opening), Gal 5:4: “Vós que vos justificais pela lei” → “Vós que sois justificados pela lei”. The source is passive (“You who are justified by the law”), and the reflexive was the familiar Bible wording.

Rejected:
- Ch. 42, 2 Chr 25:7, “not with Israel, even with all the children of Ephraim” → “nem com todos os filhos de Efraim”. After a negation, *nem* reads as “not even”, so Ephraim stays the same group as Israel.
- Ch. 31, “Then is the will of use when we have ability” → “A vontade é útil quando temos a capacidade”. The *quando* clause carries the condition, so leaving out “then” changes no claim.
- Ch. 25, “the old fathers … who loved God” → “os antigos patriarcas”. This is a standard rendering of the Old Testament fathers.
- Ch. 20, “this therefore is also that which is given to it” → “esta, portanto, é também aquela que lhe é dada”. It mirrors the source's compressed syntax and keeps the sense that the reward is grace too.

### Round 4

Focus: function words, headings and forced commitments (gender, antecedents, *seu/sua*, an addressee trace for tu/vós). Verdict: 3 defects, all fixed. The Translation Decisions entry on address now records the singular exceptions.

Fixed:
- Ch. 20, “Now, hear and understand … the works which you suppose have their origin in yourself alone … God has moulded … you”: “ouvi e entendei … supondes … em vós mesmos … deveis … vos modelou (… vos formou …)” → “ouve e entende … supões … em ti mesmo … deves … te modelou (… te formou …)”. “Yourself” makes the addressee singular, and the Latin is too (*audi et intellige*). The address returns to vós at “caríssimos”.

Rejected:
- Ch. 3, “A estes diz o Apóstolo Tiago” after *pessoas*: agreement by sense, since the source's “such” is generic.
- Ch. 14 “a graça de Deus, que retribui” and ch. 27 “a graça de Deus que ouve” (source “who”): *que* can attach to *Deus*, and either reading is true.
- Ch. 36 heading, “pelo próprio nosso Senhor”: an attested word order. Style.
- Ch. 45, “uma é assistida … outra” (source “one … he dies”): the feminine agrees with *crianças*.
- Ch. 31, “ajuda a obedecer aquele a quem manda”: with no crase, *aquele* is the object of *ajuda*.
- Ch. 5, “como daquela de que está escrito”: refers back to *vontade*, as the source's “of which” does.
