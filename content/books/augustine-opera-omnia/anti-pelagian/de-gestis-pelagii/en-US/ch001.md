# Revisions II

## Chapter 47 (74): The Deeds of Pelagius, in one book

During that same period, in the East—that is, in Syria Palestine—Pelagius was brought before an episcopal proceeding by certain Catholic brothers, in the absence of those who had presented the petition against him, since they were unable to reach the synod by its appointed day. He was heard by fourteen bishops[^1], and when he condemned the very teachings that were read out against him from the petition as hostile to the grace of Christ, they pronounced him a Catholic.

But when these proceedings came into my hands, I wrote a book about them, lest, because he had as it were been acquitted, those teachings too should be thought to have been approved by the judges—teachings from which he would in no way have gone away except as a condemned man, had he not condemned them.

This book begins thus: *After there came into our hands.*

[^1]: See Contra Iul. 1, 5, 19. 7, 32; Opus imp. c. Iul. 2, 66; Ep. 186, 31.
