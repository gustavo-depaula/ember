# Preface

Against the Pelagian heretics, who say that Adam, even if he had not sinned, would have died in body, and that the human race was not corrupted in him (from which it follows that they contend that deaths, deadly diseases, and all the evils that we see even little ones suffer would have existed in paradise too, even if no one had sinned), I wrote a book to Count Valerius, the title of which book is *Marriage and Desire*; this was because I had learned that it had reached him that the Pelagians were saying that we condemn marriage. And so in that work I distinguished, with such argument as I could, the good of marriage from the evil of carnal desire, which conjugal chastity uses well. When he had received that book, the illustrious man I have mentioned sent me on a small sheet some statements excerpted from a work of Julian, the Pelagian heretic (in which work he thought he had answered, in four books, that one book of mine which I have recalled writing under the title *Marriage and Desire*), statements that had been sent to him by some person or other who arranged, as he wished, to have them excerpted from Julian's first book; and Valerius asked me to answer them as quickly as possible. And so it came about that I wrote a second book also under the same title, against which Julian, with excessive loquacity, composed eight more. To these I now reply, setting out his words and adding to them my response at each individual point, as the refutation of them seemed best to be supplied; for I had already, after they came into my hands, refuted his earlier four books clearly enough in six books.
