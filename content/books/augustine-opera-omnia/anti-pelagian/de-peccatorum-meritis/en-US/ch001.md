# Revisions II

## Chapter 33 (60): The Punishment and Forgiveness of Sins and the Baptism of Little Ones, to Marcellinus, in three books

There also came a necessity that compelled me to write against the new Pelagian heresy. Previously, when the need arose, I had dealt with it not in writings but in sermons and conversations[^1], as each of us was able or obliged to do. When, therefore, some questions of theirs were sent to me from Carthage for me to resolve by writing back, I first wrote three books whose title is *The Punishment and Forgiveness of Sins.*

There the discussion is chiefly about the baptism of little ones on account of original sin and about the grace of God by which we are justified, that is, made righteous, although in this life no one so *keeps the commandments*[^2] of righteousness as not to need to say in prayer for his own sins, *Forgive us our debts*[^3]. It was in opposition to all these views that they founded their new heresy.

In these books, however, I thought it best for the time being to keep silent about their names[^4], hoping that in this way they could more easily be corrected. Indeed, even in the third book—which is a letter, but reckoned among the books because I thought it should be joined to the two to which I judged it ought to be connected[^5]—I set down the name of Pelagius himself not without some praise, because his life was being commended by many, and I refuted those positions of his that in his own writings he had set forth not in his own person but as expounding what was said by others, positions that he later, now a heretic, defended with the most obstinate animosity. Caelestius, however, his disciple, had already, on account of such assertions, earned excommunication at Carthage in an episcopal judgment at which I was not present[^6].

In the second book, in a certain place I say: *This he will grant to some at the end, that they will not feel death because of a sudden transformation*[^7], reserving the matter for a more careful inquiry. For either they will not die, or, passing by a most swift transformation from this life into death and from death into eternal life as *in the twinkling of an eye*[^8], they will not feel death.

This work begins thus: *Although amid the great and feverish press of cares.*

[^2]: 1 John 3:24.
[^3]: Matt 6:12.
[^4]: De gestis Pelag. 23, 47.
[^5]: De pecc. merit. 3, 3, 5.
[^6]: De haeres. 88.
[^7]: De pecc. merit. 2, 31, 50.
