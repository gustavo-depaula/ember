# Revisions II

## Chapter 56 (83): The Nature and Origin of the Soul, in Four Books

At that same time a certain Vincent Victor, in Mauretania Caesariensis, came upon a small work of mine in the possession of a Spanish priest named Peter. In a certain passage there I had confessed that I did not know about the origin of the soul of each individual human being—whether souls are propagated from that one soul of the first man and then from the parents, or whether, as that one soul was given without any propagation, so individual souls are given to individuals[^1]—though I knew that the soul is not a body but a spirit. And against these statements of mine he wrote two books addressed to this same Peter, which the monk Renatus sent to me from Caesarea.

When I had read them, I gave my reply in four pieces: one to the monk Renatus, another to the priest Peter, and two to this same Victor. The one to Peter, however, although it has the length of a book, is nonetheless a letter, which I did not wish to separate from the other three. In all of these, in which many necessary matters are discussed, I defended my hesitation concerning the origin of the souls that are given to individual human beings, and I exposed his many errors and the perversities of his presumption. Yet I treated him—a young man who was not to be detested too hastily but still to be taught—with all the gentleness I could, and I received from him a reply containing his correction.

The book of this work addressed to Renatus begins thus: *Your sincerity toward us;*

and the one to Peter thus: *To the most beloved lord and brother and fellow priest Peter;*

and the first of the two most recent ones, addressed to Vincent Victor, begins thus: *What I thought I ought to write to you.*

[^1]: Retract. 1, 1, 3; 2, 45.
