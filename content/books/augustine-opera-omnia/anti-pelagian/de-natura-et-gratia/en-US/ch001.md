# Revisions II

## Chapter 42 (69): Nature and Grace, one book

There also came into my hands at that time a certain book of Pelagius, in which he defended human nature against the grace of God, by which the ungodly is justified and by which we are Christians, with as much argument as he could muster. The book, then, by which I answered him, defending grace not against nature but as that by which nature is set free and governed, I called *Nature and Grace*.

In it I defended certain words that Pelagius had set down as though they were those of Xystus, the Roman bishop and martyr, as if they really were the words of that same Xystus[^1]; for so I had thought. But afterward I read that they belong to Sextus the philosopher, not to Xystus the Christian.

This book begins thus: *The book that you sent.*

[^1]: De nat. et grat. 64, 77; see Jerome, Ep. 133, 3; Comm. in Ier 22; Comm. in Ezech 18.
