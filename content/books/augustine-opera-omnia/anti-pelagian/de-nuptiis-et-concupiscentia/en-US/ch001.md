# Letter to Count Valerius

Though I had long been troubled that I had written to you several times and had not been thought worthy of any reply from your Highness, I suddenly received three letters from your Kindness: one delivered to me, though not to me alone, through my fellow bishop Vindemialis, and not long afterward two through my fellow presbyter Firmus. He is a holy man, joined to us, as you could learn from him, by a most intimate love; and by speaking with us at length about your Excellence and truly conveying what sort of man he had come to know you to be *in the heart of Christ*[^1], he outdid not only the letters that the bishop I mentioned and that he himself brought, but even those that we complained of not having received. And so his account of you was the more pleasant to us because he said the very things that you could not have written back yourself, even had I inquired, lest you become a herald of your own praises, which Holy Scripture forbids. And yet I too am afraid to write these things to you, lest I incur the suspicion of a flatterer, my illustrious lord, deservedly most excellent and most dear son in the love of Christ.

See, then, what delight and joy it was for me to hear your praises in Christ, or rather the praises of Christ in you, from one who could neither deceive me, because of his faith, nor be ignorant of them, because of his friendship with you. But we have also heard other things from others, even if not so many or so certain: how sound and catholic your faith is, how devout your expectation of things to come, what your love of God and of the brethren is, how you are not proud in your lofty honors and do not set your hope on uncertain riches but on the living God, and how you are rich in good works; how your house is the rest and solace of the saints and the terror of the wicked; what great care you take that no one lay snares for the members of Christ under cover of the name of Christ, whether among his old enemies or his more recent ones; and how watchful you are for the salvation of those same enemies and how hostile to their error. These things and the like, as I said, we usually hear from others as well; but now through the brother mentioned above we have learned them more fully and with better testimony.

But of your conjugal chastity, so that we might praise and love this too in you, what could we hear except from some intimate of your household who knew your life not on the surface but thoroughly? About this good of yours, then, this gift of God, it also delights me to speak with you somewhat more intimately and at somewhat greater length. I know that I am no burden to you if I send something lengthy, by reading which you may be longer in our company; for I have learned this too, that amid your many and great cares you read easily and gladly, and take much delight in our little works, even those we have written to others, if any have been able to come into your hands. How much more, then, what is written to you, where I speak as though to one present, will you deign to attend to more closely and to receive more gladly? From this letter, then, go on to the book that I have sent along with it, which will itself, at its own beginning, make known to your Reverence more conveniently both why it was written and why it was sent to you in particular.

[^1]: See Phil 1:8
