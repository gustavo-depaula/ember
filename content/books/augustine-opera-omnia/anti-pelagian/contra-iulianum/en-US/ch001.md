# Revisions II

## Chapter 62 (89): Answer to Julian, in six books

Meanwhile, the four books of the Pelagian Julian[^1], which I mentioned above, also came into my hands. In them I found that the passages excerpted from them by the man who had sent them to Count Valerius had not all been written to that same count in the way Julian had stated them, but that some of them had been somewhat altered. I therefore wrote six books against those four of his[^2]; but the first two of mine refute the impudence of Julian by the testimonies of the saints who defended the catholic faith after the apostles. For he thought he could throw it in our face as a Manichean doctrine that we say original sin is contracted from Adam—a sin that *through the bath of rebirth*[^3] is taken away not only in adults but also in little ones. How much Julian himself helps the Manicheans by certain of his statements I showed in the latter part of my first book. My remaining four books reply to his four, one to one.

But in the fifth volume of this great and laborious work, where I recalled that a deformed husband, so as not to beget deformed children, used to set a beautiful picture before his wife during intercourse[^4], I gave the name of the man who used to do this as though it were certain, when it is uncertain, for my memory deceived me. Soranus, an authority on medicine, wrote that a king of Cyprus used to do this, but he did not give the man's proper name.

This work begins thus: *Your insults and abusive words, Julian.*

[^1]: Retract. 2, 53.
[^2]: Opus imp. c. Iul. 4, 5.
[^3]: Titus 3:5.
[^4]: C. Iul. 5, 14, 51.
