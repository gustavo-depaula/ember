# Revisions II

## Chapter 37 (64): The Spirit and the Letter, to Marcellinus, in one book

I had written him three books, whose title is *The Punishment and Forgiveness of Sins,* in which the baptism of infants is also carefully discussed[^1]; and he wrote back to me that he had been disturbed by my saying that it was possible for a man to be without sin, if his will did not fail with the help of God's aid, even though no one either has been or is or will be of such perfect righteousness in this life. For he asked how I could say that a thing could come about of which there is no instance. Because of this inquiry of his I wrote the book whose title is *The Spirit and the Letter,* taking up the apostolic saying where he says, *The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life*[^2]. In this book, with all the help God gave me, I argued sharply against the enemies of the grace of God by which the ungodly is justified.

Now when I was treating of the observances of the Jews, who abstained from certain foods according to the Old Law, I said, *the rites concerning certain foods*[^3]*,* a term that is not in use in the sacred Scriptures. It nevertheless seemed fitting to me, because I held in memory that *cerimoniae*[^4] were so called from "lacking" (carendo), as if *carimoniae*, in that those who observe them lack the things from which they abstain. But if there is another origin of this word, one that is at odds with true religion, then I did not speak according to that one, but according to the one I mentioned above.

This book begins thus: *Having read the little works that I recently composed for you, my dearest son Marcellinus.*

[^1]: Retract. 2.33.
[^2]: 2 Cor 3:6.
[^3]: De spir. et litt. 21.36; Ep. 82.18.
[^4]: Aul. Gell., Noct. Atticae 4.9.8.
