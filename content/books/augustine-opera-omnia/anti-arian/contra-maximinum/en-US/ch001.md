# Book I

## Preface.

In giving the reply I owe to the speech of Maximinus, the Arian bishop, whose great length used up the space of the day on which we held our discussion in person, I certainly ought to address him, whether he still thinks I must be contradicted when he has read this, or whether, with the Lord working marvelously in his heart, he consents to the truth that has been made manifest. What did you have in mind, Arian, in saying so much and yet saying nothing for the cause that is at issue between us, as if to be able to answer were the same as not being able to keep silent? First, then, I will show that you were unable to refute what I said; then, as far as will seem necessary, I myself will refute what you said.

## On Two Gods.

**1.** As to what you said about two gods, I certainly replied to your words, where you said that you worship one God: *It follows,* I said, *that either you do not worship Christ, or you worship not one God, but two.* Trying to answer this, you said much indeed, asserting that you also worship Christ as God; but although you did not deny that you worship two gods, you nonetheless did not dare to confess it. For you perceived that Christian ears cannot bear that two gods are to be worshiped. Oh, how near you came to correcting yourself, if only you feared to believe what you feared to say! For since the Apostle cries out, *One believes with the heart and so is justified, and one confesses with the mouth and so is saved*[^1], if you think that what you believe pertains to justification, why do you not also confess it with the mouth unto salvation? But if it does not pertain to salvation to confess that two gods are to be worshiped, then without doubt neither does it pertain to justification to believe it. Why, then, you who do not want your mouth held guilty by such a confession, do you not cleanse your heart of such a belief? Hold the right faith with the Catholic Church, and do not be ashamed to amend the perverse one. Hold with the Catholic Church that the Father is not he who is the Son, and the Son is not he who is the Father; and that the Father is God and the Son is God; and yet that both together are not two gods, but one. In this way alone will it come about that you worship both the Father and the Son and yet do not say that two gods are to be worshiped, but one, so that your conscience may not be stung by the guilt of impiety when the divine words sound in your ears, that *there is no God but one*[^2], and *Hear, O Israel; the Lord your God, the Lord is one*[^3]; and when you hear, *You shall worship the Lord your God and him only shall you serve*[^4], you may securely serve, with the service owed to the one God, not the Father only but also the Son. Remember, then, that you did not reply to my objection, namely, that you worship not one God, but two.

## On Human Contagions.

**2.** In the second place I dealt with you about your words, where you had said that *God the Father did not descend to human contagions*, as though Christ suffered those things in the flesh; and I pointed out how a contagion is usually understood, namely, only in some defect, from all of which we know Christ to be immune. Nor were you able to answer anything to this. For the divine testimonies that you cited could not help you at all, for by them you could not prove that Christ was tainted by any human contagion. For as to what you recall the Apostle to have said, *Since Christ, who was no sinner, was made sin for us*, read more carefully, and lest perhaps you have stumbled upon a faulty manuscript, or the Latin translator has erred, look at the Greek; and you will find that Christ did not make sin for us, but that Christ himself was made sin by God the Father, that is, a sacrifice for sin. For the Apostle says, *We beseech you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God: him who knew no sin he made to be sin for us*[^5]. Therefore he himself did not make sin, but God made him to be sin for us, that is, as I said, a sacrifice for sin. For if you recall or reread, you will find that in the books of the Old Testament sacrifices for sins are called sins. The likeness of sinful flesh, too, in which he came to us, was itself called sin: *God,* it says, *sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and from sin he condemned sin in the flesh*[^6], that is, from the likeness of sinful flesh, which was his own, he condemned sin in the flesh of sin, which is ours. For this reason it is also said of him, *For the death he died he died to sin, once for all, but the life he lives he lives to God*[^7]. For he died to sin once, because he died to the likeness of sinful flesh when in dying he was stripped of the flesh, so that through this mystery he might signify that those who are baptized into his death die to sin in order to live to God. So too through the cross he *became a curse for us*[^8]. For hanging on the tree, he hung upon the tree the death that had come from the curse of God, and thus *our old self was crucified together with him*[^9], so that what is said in the Law may be understood as said without falsehood, *Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree*[^10]. What is *cursed* but *You are earth, and to earth you shall go*[^11]? And what is *everyone* but that Christ himself also, who although he was life, nonetheless died with a true death, not a feigned one? If you understand these mysteries, you will at the same time understand that there are no contagions. But what is it to us if, speaking in your usual way, you perhaps wished to call the contact of mortals a contagion, since you hold with us that the Lord Jesus had no sin either in spirit or in flesh?

## On the Invisible God.

**3.** In the third place, when I dealt with the invisible God, I urged you to believe that not only the Father but also the Son is invisible according to his divinity, not according to the flesh, in which who denies that he appeared visibly to mortals? And on this I argued further in another place. But you, yielding to the most manifest truth, agreed that the Son is invisible; and thereby you destroyed what you had said, namely, that the Father alone is invisible. But again, disturbed by your own agreement, because you had agreed that the Son too is invisible, you dared to say that the lesser are seen by the greater, but the greater cannot be seen by the lesser, saying that the angels are seen by the archangels, and souls by the angels, but that the angels are not seen by souls; whence also, asserting that Christ according to the substance of his divinity is seen not only by no human beings but not even by the heavenly Powers, you said for that reason that the Father alone is invisible, because he has no superior by whom he may be observed. Tell us, I ask you, when did the archangels indicate to you that they see the angels but are not seen by the angels? From the report of which angels did you learn that they see souls, while souls do not see them? From whom did you hear these things? Where did you learn them? Where did you read them? Would you not do better to direct your mind to the divine Books, where we read both that angels were seen by human beings, when they willed and how they willed to be seen, at the command or with the permission of the Creator of all? Yet you who had said that the Father alone must be called invisible, *because he has no superior by whom he may be observed*, afterward confessed that he is visible to the Son, bringing forth against yourself the gospel testimony where the Son himself says, *Not that anyone has seen the Father except him who is from God; he has seen the Father*[^12]. Here indeed the truth most openly conquered you; but you, unwilling to be freed from error, did not want to be salutarily conquered. For when you had cited against yourself the gospel testimony by which it became clear that the Father is seen by the Son, the Son himself saying, *But he who is from God, he has seen the Father*, you straightway added on your own, *But he saw the incomprehensible one.* Meanwhile you lost what you had said, that the Father alone is invisible because he has no superior, since, conquered by the truth, you confessed that he is seen by an inferior. For you say that the Son is inferior, and yet, the Son compelling you to testify, you said that the Father is seen by him. About the incomprehensible we will see later, so that the truth may conquer you there too. For the question between us was not about the comprehensible and the incomprehensible, but about the visible and the invisible, in which question, if you yourself are visible to yourself, you see that you have been conquered.

## On the Immortal God.

**4.** In the fourth place I dealt with you about the immortal God, the Son as well. For that statement of the Apostle, *who alone has immortality*[^13], you wanted understood as if it were said of the Father alone, whereas he said this not of the Father but of God, which is Father and Son and Holy Spirit. So I showed that the Son too has immortality according to the substance of his divinity. For according to the flesh, who denies that he was mortal? But when you wished to answer me on this point, hemmed in by the plain truth, you also confessed that God the Son has immortality. You were therefore conquered in what you were saying, that the Apostle said of the Father only that he alone has immortality. Nor indeed do you slip out of the bonds of the truth because you say, *The Son does indeed have immortality, but received from the Father.* The question is not whence he has it, but whether he has it. For you want it understood of the Father alone that it is written, *He alone has immortality*[^14]. The Father has immortality received from no one, and the Son has immortality received from the Father; yet both the Father and the Son have immortality. Otherwise, if the Son does not have it, either the Father did not give it to the Son, or the Son lost what he received. But the Father gave it to the Son, and the Son did not lose it; nor did the Father in giving lose what he gave by begetting. Both the Father and the Son, therefore, have immortality, not the Father alone. You are thus compelled to confess that it was not said of the Father alone, *who alone has immortality*, since you have already been compelled to confess that the Son too has immortality. But the one who has this *alone* is God, which is not the Father alone, because this is the Son too, and each, with the Holy Spirit joined, is one God. But why God alone is said to have immortality, since the soul too in its own way is immortal, and so is the other spiritual and heavenly creation, we will see later; for now it is enough for us that to what I said you could answer nothing, and that you were compelled to confess that not the Father only, but the Son too, although from the Father, nonetheless has immortality.

## In What Way the Father Is Greater.

**5.** In the fifth place I showed in what way the Father is greater than the Son: that he is not greater as God, for in that respect the Son is coeternal with him, but he is greater than the man, because the Son was made in time. There I cited the apostolic testimony, *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^15]. For equality with God was his by nature, not by grasping. To this you replied, saying, *For who denies that the Son is in the form of God? That he is God, that he is Lord, that he is King, we have, I think, already set forth at length. And as to 'he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped,' the blessed apostle Paul instructs us in this, that he did not grasp it, and neither do we say so.* These words of yours not only have nothing against us; rather, they appear to be more for us. For if you confess the form of God, why do you not openly confess the Son of God equal to God? Especially since, regarding the Apostle's words where he says *he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*, you could not find anything to say for your side. And because you could not deny that the Apostle said this, you therefore said, *that he did not grasp it, and neither do we say so*, as though *he did not grasp* were the same as he did not have, that is, equality with God; and as though it were said, *He did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*, as if it were said: He did not think that equality with God was a thing to be grasped, because it was foreign to him. For one who grasps is a usurper of what belongs to another, as though the Son, when he could have, had been unwilling to grasp it. You see with how great folly this is understood. Understand, therefore, that the Apostle said *he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped* because he did not count what he was born to be as foreign to him; yet although he did not count equality with God as foreign but as his own, he *emptied himself*[^16], seeking not what was his own but what was ours. That you may know this is so, attend to how the Apostle arrived at it. For when he was enjoining on Christians the humility of love, he said, *each esteeming the other as superior to himself, each looking not to his own interests, but also to the interests of others*. Then, to exhort them by Christ's example to seek and look not to their own interests but also to the interests of others, he said, *Have this mind among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus: who, though he was in the form of God,* which was his own, *did not count it a thing to be grasped,* that is, did not count it foreign, *to be equal with God*; but rather, seeking what was ours, not his own, *he emptied himself,* not losing the form of God, but *taking the form of a servant*[^17]. For that nature is not changeable, so that he emptied himself by losing what he was, but by taking what he was not; not by consuming what was his own, but by assuming what was ours; and then, in the form of a servant, obeying as a man *unto death, even the death of the cross. Therefore God also exalted him and gave him the name which is above every name*[^18], and so on. He gave these things, then, to the man, not to God. For when he was in the form of God he was not without exaltation, nor did the things of heaven, of earth, and of the underworld fail to bend the knee to him. But when it is said, *Therefore he exalted him*, it is sufficiently clear why he exalted him, that is, on account of the obedience *unto death, even the death of the cross.* In the form, therefore, in which he was crucified, that very form was exalted, to it was given *the name which is above every name*, so that in that very form of a servant the only-begotten Son of God is named. Do not, then, make the form of God unequal to God, which cannot be said even among human beings. For when it is said, This man is in the form of that man, no one understands anything but equals. Or do you perhaps not want to take *since he was in the form of God* in such a way that you understand the Son in the form of God the Father, where nothing but the equality of the two appears; but rather you think *in the form of God* is to be understood as in his own form, because he too is certainly God? I do not much care if you understand it even in that way. For where the increments of age do not bring fullness of form, but the Son was born perfect by the Father's begetting, then without doubt, if the form of the Son is not equal to the form of the Father, he is not a true Son. But it is written, *that we may be in his true Son Jesus Christ*[^19]. The form, therefore, of the true Son cannot be unequal to the form of God the Father. Accordingly, neither to this passage of my discourse, where from the words of the Apostle I proved the Son equal to the Father, could you answer anything for your purpose.

## On the True Offspring of Animals.

**6.** In the sixth place, in order to show that the Son is of the same nature as the Father, I set even the offspring of mortal animals against the monstrousness of your error, rebuking your heart, you who deny that God the Son is of the same nature as the Father, although you do not deny that he is a true Son, since God himself has given to animals to beget what they themselves are; where I named not only a man the son of a man but even a dog the son of a dog, not as a likeness to God, but to the confusion of those who disparage the Son of God; who, although they see that corruptible and mortal natures nonetheless have unity of nature from their parents, are unwilling to grant to the Son of God the sharing of one nature from the Father, although he is inseparable from the Father, and incorruptible and eternal with the Father. Hence I also said that, according to you, the human condition is better, where it is granted to grow, so that by growing the children can attain to the strength of their parents; but the Son of God, as you say and teach, having been begotten lesser than the Father, so remained, and, since he could not attain to the form of the Father, did not grow. Here you, in order to make plain how great a mass of truth was pressing on you, did not answer to the point at all; but, as if panting with failing breath, you thought I should be reproached, saying that so foul a comparison, namely of the son of a man or a dog, ought not to be brought up into that great immensity. Is this to answer, or rather to show the poverty of an answer? As if I had brought forth these examples of earthly natures in order to equate corruption with incorruption, mortality with immortality, visible things with invisible, temporal things with eternal; and not rather in order to convict you, who err in great and highest matters, from small and lowliest things, you who do not see the good that the supremely good Creator has given even to the lowest and cheapest creatures, namely, that although they are far other than he is, they nonetheless beget what they themselves are. Nor do you attend to what an evil thing you say, that while human beings and dogs and the rest of this kind have true sons, whom truth creates as they beget, the very Truth should not be the true Son of God. Or if, compelled by holy Scripture, you allow that he is a true Son, we ask: allow that he is not degenerate. And degenerate how? Let Catholics hear that at which heretics should blush. The son of a brave man, if he does not have bravery, is called degenerate; yet he is a man, which his father also is; and in an unlike life he is nonetheless not of a foreign substance. But you so want the only-begotten Son of God to be degenerate that you deny him the very substance of the Father; you boast that he was born lesser and remained lesser; you grant him no age by which he can be increased, you grant him not the same form by which he can be made equal. Since you take away so much of his nature, I wonder with what face you call him a true Son; unless it be that, by a most wretched error, you think you cannot arrive at the glory of the one Father except through insult to the one Son.

## On the Greatness of the Son.

**7.** In the seventh place I said: *We acknowledge the Son to be so great a God that we call him equal to the Father. And so without cause,* I said, *you wanted to prove by testimonies and much speaking what we strongly profess.* And to these words of mine I added a discussion in which I gave the reason why the Son, although he is equal to the Father, nonetheless calls him his God, where he says, *I am ascending to my Father and your Father, my God and your God*[^20]. For you had cited this gospel testimony, by which you thought to prove that the Son was not equal to the Father. And so, answering you on these points, I said that the Father is for this reason the God also of the only-begotten Son, because he became man and was born of a woman; and that this is what he says in the Psalm, where he foretold what was to be: *From my mother's womb you are my God*[^21]; so that he might show that the Father is his God from this, that he became man. For man is born from the mother's womb, and according to the man God was born of the Virgin, so that not only would he be his Father who begot him from himself, but he would also be his God, who from the mother's womb created him a man. When you wished to answer these things, you said much, and brought forth many testimonies that help you not at all. Yet how it is said, *From my mother's womb you are my God*, although you mentioned the same words of holy Scripture, you could in no way find out. But why you put there the testimony of another psalm, where it is written, *With you is the principality in the day of your power, in the splendors of the saints; from the womb before the daystar I begot you*[^22], I do not see at all. For it is not the person of the Son speaking who says, From your womb, or, From your belly, you are my God. Even if that ineffable generation is taken as from the womb of the Father, this is what is signified: that from himself, that is, from his own substance, God begot God, just as, when he was born from the mother's womb, man begot man; so that we might understand that in each generation the substances of him who was born and of those from whom he was born are not different. The substance is indeed different, God the Father and man the mother; yet the substance is not different, God the Father and God the Son, just as the substance is not different, man the mother and man the son. But hear what this Son says in the prophecy: *From my mother's womb,* he says, *you are my God*[^23]. Do not try with many words unnecessary to the matter to cover clear things. He who is Father to the Son from his own womb is his God from the mother's womb, not from his own. To this, then, you could answer nothing at all.

## On the Subjection of the Son.

**8.** In the eighth place I answered you concerning the subjection by which the Son is subject to the Father, since you had said, *By his subjection he establishes one God.* I answered, then, that this too is rightly taken according to the man, namely, that the Son is subject to the Father. Nor is this to be wondered at, since according to that very form of a servant he is read to be subject even to his parents[^24]; and of him it is written, *You have made him a little lower than the angels*[^25]. To this you, as if answering, said that I had argued very well, since on account of the form of a servant he was subject even to his parents. Then, wishing to make it appear as if for you what you saw was against you, and so, before incautious and less attentive people, whoever should read these things, to make yourself appear to be answering where you had nothing to say, you add and say: *For if he is found subject to the parents whom he himself created, since all things were made through him—for we know the Son to have been begotten by the Father not after time, but before time—if then,* you say, *he was subject to his parents, as the authority of the divine Scriptures proclaims clearer than light, how much more is he subject to his own Begetter, who begot him so great and such as he is; according to what Paul says: 'When all things are subjected to the Son, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things under him'*[^26]? These words of yours could be thought to have been said by me, and to be entirely mine, were it not that, when they were said to those listening, and afterward to all who read them, it plainly appeared that you said them. For who would believe that you could agree with us that Christ is subject according to the form of a servant, and thereby not according to the form of God?

## Whether the Holy Spirit Adores the Father.

**9.** In the ninth place I asked of you that you show, through the divine Scriptures, if you could, whether the Holy Spirit adores the Father. For you had said this; but, as your very discourse, to which I replied, sufficiently indicates, you had not proved it. To this inquiry of mine, then, see what you answered in your later discourse. For when you had said as much as you wished about the judgment of the Son, which we too most faithfully believe, and about the subjection that we do not deny the Son renders to the Father according to the form of a servant, when you came to prove that the Father is adored by the Holy Spirit, you went back to those groanings, about which I had already answered you before, according to the manner of the holy Scriptures, in what manner of speaking it is said, *And the Spirit himself intercedes with unutterable groanings*[^27]; lest we believe that the Holy Spirit can never be without groanings, since there is found no day, no hour, no moment of time in which prayers are not poured out to God by holy ones somewhere, by some here, by others elsewhere. Since, then, no time is free of the prayers of the holy ones, seeing that day and night, when some are refreshing themselves with food and drink, others are doing some other thing, others are sleeping, there are surely never lacking those whom holy desire compels to pray; so it comes about that the Holy Spirit, who is present everywhere to all, is not allowed to cease even a little from groanings—which would be the height of misery, if he were compelled to groan for any who pray—unless to intercede with unutterable groanings be understood, as I said, that is, because he makes the holy ones intercede with the groanings of holy desires, into whom he pours the pious affection of spiritual grace. But I cited from the holy Scriptures similar modes of speech, where through what produces an effect that very effect is signified—as we call cold lazy, because it makes people lazy; and a day sad or glad, because it makes people sad or glad—as where God says to Abraham, *Now I have known*[^28], which is nothing other than: Now I have made you to know; for God is not then to be said to have come to know what he could never, before it happened, fail to know. These modes of speech, brought forth by me from the divine words, you did not find how to interpret otherwise; you ought by no means to have gone back to those groanings. For no one is so minded about the Holy Spirit except one who is minded according to the flesh, not according to the Spirit.

Yet even if it were granted to you that, as you suppose, the Holy Spirit so intercedes for the holy ones, to intercede or to pray is one thing, to adore another. Everyone who prays asks; not everyone who adores asks, nor does everyone who asks adore. Recall the custom of kings, who are very often adored and not asked, and sometimes asked and not adored. And so you could in no way demonstrate that the Father is adored by the Holy Spirit.

## In What Way the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit Are One God.

**10.** In the tenth place I dealt with you that you might understand how, through an ineffable conjunction, the Trinity itself is one God, which we say is of one substance; since even in different substances we find—that is, the spirit of man and the Spirit of the Lord—that, through the conjunction by which a man cleaves to the Lord, they are called one spirit, where the Apostle says, *But he who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit*[^29]. Answering this, or rather not keeping silent, you tried to show how the Father and the Son are one, not by unity of nature, but of will. This indeed you are accustomed to say, but you are accustomed to say it then, when there is objected to you what the Lord says, *I and the Father are one*[^30]. But in this place I did not wish to prove that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one—which indeed we most faithfully believe on account of the unity of substance—but that the same Trinity is one God. For it is one thing, they are one; another, he is one God. Distinguish: he is, and they are. Nor does the Apostle say, Those who cleave to the Lord are one, since the substance is different; but he says, *He who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit.* But if you, when of two it is said, He is one, and it is said what one—as the Apostle says, *he is one spirit*—think this is the same as when of two it is said, They are one, and it is not said what one—as the Savior says, *I and the Father are one*—why do you not say, The Father and the Son are one God? Why, when you hear, *Hear, O Israel; the Lord your God, the Lord is one*[^31], do you want it understood of the Father only? For surely the Father is Lord God, and the Son is Lord God; why with you is not each one Lord God, just as with the blessed Apostle the spirit of man and the Spirit of the Lord *are one spirit*? But what does it profit your cause that you say this comes about through agreement of will? It does indeed come about, but where there is a different nature, just as the nature of man and of the Lord is different; and yet *he who cleaves to the Lord*, surely through agreement of will, *is one spirit.* If, then, you will not have it through unity of substance, at least through agreement of will say—say it at some time, say it in whatever way—the Father and the Son are one God. But you do not say it, lest you be compelled to confess what you never wished, that it was said of both, not of the Father alone, *Hear, O Israel; the Lord your God, the Lord is one*[^32]; since you will not confess the Holy Spirit to be God, you will not confess him Lord. Say, I say, by whatever reasoning you wish, The Father and the Son are one Lord God, so that, serving the Father and the Son, you may serve not two gods and two lords against the precept of God, but the one Lord. But for now let this much be said about this matter. I think that when you read these things you could answer nothing to what I cited from the Apostle, *he who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit*[^33]; if you lay aside contention, you will not deny it.

## On the Temple of the Holy Spirit.

**11.** In the eleventh place I showed that the Holy Spirit is God, from his temple, which we ourselves are, on the testimony of the Apostle, where he says, *Do you not know that you are God's temple, and that God's Spirit dwells in you?*[^34]; and again, *Do you not know that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, which you have from God?*[^35] But to these things you answered nothing. For you said, *I accept what you brought forth: 'Do you not know that you are God's temple, and that God's Spirit dwells in you?' For God,* you say, *does not dwell in a man whom the Spirit has not first sanctified and purified.* And in this way you wanted it understood that the Holy Spirit was not said to be God, nor that we are the temple of the Holy Spirit, but of God; and that this is what was said, *You are God's temple.* But that for this reason was added, *God's Spirit dwells in you*, because the Holy Spirit purifies the temple of God, not his own, so that when he himself has purified it, then God may dwell there. What great absurdity follows this view of yours I do not wish to say now. For now I ought to show only how, by saying much, you said nothing to the point. For you abandoned the cause and proceeded to the praise of the Holy Spirit, and pursued it copiously against yourself. Against yourself, I said, because you will not call him God whose so great divinity you were compelled, by praising him, to confess; so that, since he is one, he is everywhere present, and is lacking to no one to be sanctified, wherever any Christian wishes to be and to pray to God, presenting himself to all at once, whether they are baptized in the east or in the west in Christ: this we too say. But the one whom we say is such and so great, far be it from us that we deny him to be God; which is also most quickly and easily shown by his temple, which we ourselves are. For unless he were our God, he could not have us ourselves as a temple; and that you might conceal this, and in your speech turn the minds of human beings away from the light of truth, you said whatever you said about the temple of God, whom you would not have understood to be the Holy Spirit; but about the temple of the Holy Spirit, which was most openly shown, you kept entirely silent. For when I had set before you two testimonies of the apostle Paul—one where he says, *Do you not know that you are God's temple, and that God's Spirit dwells in you?*[^36]; the other where he says, *Do you not know that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit within you?*[^37]—why did you act so fraudulently as to mention one of these, what was said, *You are God's temple*, and keep silent about the other, what was said, *Your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit within you*? Why did you do this, I ask you, unless because you could in no way argue how he would not be our God who has us ourselves as a temple, whom without doubt we would recognize as God if we were commanded by divine Scripture to make him a temple of wood and stones?

## On the Fact That the Father and the Son Are One.

**12.** In the twelfth place I urged you to bring forth, if you could, by what divine authority it was said that they are one where the substances are different. But you, wishing to answer this, could bring forth nothing of the kind; rather, constrained by great straits, you dared to affirm that the apostles are one with the Father and the Son. This Christ did not say at all; for it was said by you as though the Father and the Son and the apostles are one. But Christ did not say, That they and we may be one; he said, *That they may be one, as we also are one.* For, to set down the very words of the gospel, *Holy Father,* he says, *keep them in your name whom you have given me, that they may be one, as we also are one.* Did he say, That they may be one with us, or, That they and we may be one? Likewise, after a little: *I do not pray for these only,* he says, *but also for those who are to believe in me through their word, that they all may be one.* Neither here did he say, That they may be one with us. Then there follows, *As you, Father, are in me and I in you, that they also may be one in us.* And here he did not say, May we be one, or, May they be one with us; but, *May they be one in us*; so that those who are one by nature, because they are human beings, may also be one in the Father and the Son; not one with them, that is, not so that they and these are one. Further he adds and says: *That the world may believe that you sent me, and the glory which you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one, as we are one: I in them and you in me, that they may be perfected into one*[^38]. Although, then, he so often said, *That they may be one*, yet nowhere did he say, That they and we may be one, that is, that they may be one with us; but he said either *in us*, or *as we are*; that is, they according to their own nature, we according to ours. For he wanted those who were one by nature to be perfected in this very thing, that they were one. For because he says, *You therefore must be perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect*[^39], he does not want to join them to God by unity of nature, as though their nature and his were one and the same; but he wants them to be perfect in their own nature, just as God is perfect in his own, although a different nature, not one; and unless we are in him, we cannot exist at all. Not as all are in him, because he himself contains all that he created—on account of which he was said to be *not far from each one of us*, because *in him we live and move and have our being*[^40]—but as such are in him as those to whom it was said, *For once you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^41]. Hence also is that, *Let her marry whom she wishes, only in the Lord*[^42]. You could not, therefore, bring forth where it was said, They are one, of those whose substance is not one but different; and yet in an obscure passage you wished to creep up on us, so as to say that the apostles are one with the Father and the Son, as though the apostles and the Father and the Son were one, when it is manifest that the substance of the apostles is different from that of the Father and the Son. But since it is manifest that Christ nowhere said, That they and we may be one, or, May they be one with us, let it be manifest also that you could not answer us and wished to commit a fraud.

## On the Testimony the Father Bore to the Son.

**13.** In the thirteenth place I reminded you that the Father is not greater than the Son for the reason that the Father bore testimony to the Son. For I also recalled that the prophets bore testimony to him, whom you cannot say to be greater than he. For you had said that the Father bore testimony to the Son; which I took as though from this you wished to prove that he to whom testimony was borne is greater than the one who bore it: but since in your later discourse you kept entirely silent about this, I took your silence in place of agreement; although it can be that you mentioned the Father bearing testimony to the Son in order to prove from this that the one is one and the other is another, not in order to prove that the one is greater than the other. But that the Father is one and the Son is another, since the Father is not the same as the Son, is a teaching common to you and to us against the Sabellians. For they say that the Son is not another but the same as the Father; but we say that the Father is indeed one and the Son another; yet we say that what the Father is, this the Son is.

## On the Love of the Father and the Son.

**14.** In the fourteenth place, to what you had said—*I read of one who is loved, and I believe that the Father is the one who loves, and the Son the one who is loved*—I replied, saying: *But you say in this way that there is a diversity between the Father and the Son, because the Father loves and the Son is loved; as though you could deny that the Son too loves the Father.* Then I added: *If both love each other in turn, why do you deny that they are of one nature?* This I said, of course, so that you might not from this deny that the two are of one nature, because you yourself said that the one loves and the other is loved. Answering this, you agreed indeed that the Son too loves the Father, but you would not agree that they are of one nature; as though the Son loves the Father as a creature loves the Creator, not as the only-begotten loves his begetter, whom you want to be degenerate by a diversity of substance.

## On the Invisibility of the Trinity.

**15.** In the fifteenth place I said that the Trinity is equally invisible, not the Father only; but that the Son nonetheless appeared visible in the form of a servant, on account of which he said, *The Father is greater than I*[^43]. But since the divinity showed itself to the fathers, I said that this was done through a subordinate creature, not through its own nature, by which the Trinity is invisible. And to prove this, I recalled Moses saying to him, with whom he spoke face to face, *If I have found grace before you, show me yourself plainly*[^44]; that you might understand how he saw him whom he desired to be shown to himself; for surely, if he saw God in the substance by which God is, he would not have asked that he show himself to him. I also said that Christ is the creator of things visible and invisible, in order to prove that he is not visible through his own substance, by whom there could be created not only visible things but also invisible. Trying to answer these things, how many things you said that are not to the point, let those who read observe; and yet about Moses—why he wished God, with whom he spoke, to be shown to him, if he discerned his nature and substance—you dared to say nothing at all; and still you did not cease to affirm that the Son of God, the creator of invisible things, even before he took the form of a servant, was in the form of God visible; whom, above, you had yourself already confessed could be seen in the form of a servant, but in the substance of his divinity to be invisible.

## On the Only Wise God.

**16.** In the sixteenth place, because you had said that the Apostle spoke of the Father only, *To the only wise God*[^45], I said: *Then the Father alone is the wise God, and the very Wisdom of God, which is Christ, is not wise—of whom the Apostle says, 'Christ the Power of God and the Wisdom of God!'*[^46] Then I added: *It remains for you to say (for what do you not dare?) that the Wisdom of God is unwise.* To these things you said: *The blessed apostle Paul,* you say, *proclaims the Father alone wise, saying thus: 'To the only wise God.' But the reason must be sought,* you say, *how he is the only wise, not that Christ is not wise.* You then go on and add how you confess Christ too to be wise; for after some things which you wove in that are not to the point, in order to prolong your speech and the time, you also inserted this in your words, saying: *But truly the Father alone is wise*; as though the Apostle had said, To the only wise Father. But he said, *To the only wise God*; because the Son too is God, which you also want; the Holy Spirit too is God, though you do not want it; and this Trinity is the only wise God, who never could be at all unwise, nor will be able to be; not partaker of wisdom by grace, but wise by the immobility and immutability of his nature. For if I should say to you: Is it really so, O man who glory in the Christian name, that Christ is wise in such a way as not to be truly wise? Is Christ, then, who is true God, not truly wise? Would you not be so confounded by this question that you would immediately answer that Christ is truly wise? What is it, then, that you said: *But truly the Father alone is wise*? Surely you now perceive where you have arrived, and from how great a blasphemy you ought to recall yourself.

## On the Uncreated God.

**17.** In the seventeenth place I dealt with you about the fact that the Son too, not the Father only, is uncreated, that is, was not made. For you had said that for this reason one God is proclaimed by you, because *he is one above all, unbegotten, uncreated.* Answering, then, this audacity of yours, I said: *But you call the Father uncreated, as though the Son were made, through whom all things were made.* Then I added: *Know that the Son was made, but in the form of a servant. For in the form of God he was so far from being made that through him all things were made. For if he himself was made,* I said, *then all things were not made through him, but only the rest.* To these things you, with all the great length of your speech, found so little to say that you fell wholly silent about this matter, as though you had not heard it.

## On the Unbegotten Father.

**18.** In the eighteenth place I thought I should deal with you also about the unborn Father, that is, the unbegotten—since you had said this too—and I said: *I therefore do not call the Son unbegotten; but the Father the begetter, the Son the begotten. Yet the Father begot what he is; otherwise he is not a true Son, if the Son is not what the Father is; as we have already said above concerning the offspring of animals.* To this too you brought forth nothing, neither true nor false.

## On the Equality of the Holy Spirit with the Father.

**19.** In the nineteenth place, because you had demanded of me that I show the Holy Spirit to be equal to the Father, I answered you, saying: *But what is it that you demand, that I show you the Holy Spirit to be equal to the Father, as though you had shown the Father to be greater than the Holy Spirit, as you were able to show concerning the Son, on account of the form of a servant? For we know,* I said, *that the Father was said to be greater than the Son, because the Son was in the form of a servant; and the Son is still in human form, which he raised to heaven; for this reason it was said of him that even now he "intercedes for us"*[^47]. *And this same immortal form will be everlasting in the kingdom; on account of which it was said: 'Then the Son himself also will be subjected to him who put all things under him'*[^48]. *But of the Holy Spirit, who took up no creature into the unity of his person—although he too deigned to show himself visibly through a subordinate creature, whether in the appearance of a dove or in tongues of fire*[^49]*—the Father was never said to be greater than he; the Spirit was never said to have adored the Father; he was never said to be less than the Father.* To these things you, as if answering, nonetheless did not answer. For you could not show that anywhere the Father was said to be greater than the Holy Spirit, as the Son, on account of the form of a servant, said, *The Father is greater than I*[^50]. And when I had said that the Holy Spirit took up no creature into the unity of his person, you said that the Holy Spirit appeared in the dove and the fire just as Christ appeared in the man; as though the dove and the Spirit, or the fire and the Spirit, were one person, just as the Word and the man are one person. For those things appeared for an hour, to show visibly, by signifying, the invisible Holy Spirit—the dove on account of holy love, the fire on account of the light and fervor of love; and when the office of signification was completed, those bodily appearances passed away and ceased to be any longer, just as the pillar of cloud, cloudy by day, luminous by night[^51]. In short, lest the dove or the flame should be thought to belong to the substance of the Holy Spirit, or that a nature of such great majesty turned itself into these visible things, or took them up into the unity of his person, the Holy Spirit is never afterward read to have so appeared. But Christ, who did not take on a human appearance for an hour, in which he might appear to human beings and then that appearance pass away, but into the unity of his person, the invisible form of God remaining, took the visible form of a man; not only was he born in it of a human mother, but he also grew in it, and ate, and drank, and slept in it, and was slain in it, and rose again in it, and ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father in it, and is to come to judge the living and the dead in it, and in his kingdom will be subject in it to him who put all things under him. These things, briefly said in my reply, which I have now explained somewhat more at length so that you might at least so understand, you would not attend to and consider, rushing into so great a blasphemy that you said the divine nature of God and of the Holy Spirit was convertible—oh, the wickedness!—and changeable. For these are your very words: *The things,* you say, *which I argued about the invisibility of the omnipotent God, you yourself also, though with another purpose, nonetheless affirmed in your words: that the Holy Spirit was seen in the appearance of a dove, and also in the appearance of fire; the Son indeed in the form of a man; but the Father, neither in the appearance of a dove nor in the form of a man; nor did he ever turn himself into forms, nor will he ever be turned; of whom it is written: 'I am who I am, and I am not changed'*[^52]. Then you add and say: *The Son indeed, already established in the form of God, took, as you yourself brought forth, the form of a servant, which the Father did not; the Holy Spirit likewise took up the appearance of a dove, which the Father did not take up. Know therefore,* you say, *that the one is invisible, the one also incomprehensible and immense.* Would you say these things, if you could think according to the spirit, not according to the flesh, what you say? For you are a man who read in the holy Scriptures, *I am who I am, and I am not changed*. And although these words are not of the Father alone, but of the Trinity itself, which is one God, you, attributing them to the Father only, believe the Son to be changeable! You believe the Only-Begotten, through whom all things were made, to be changeable; him of whom the Gospel says, *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*; and, *All things were made through him*[^53], you believe to be changeable! What shall I now say of the Holy Spirit, when you believe him to be changeable whom you confess to be the true Son of God and true God? This you surely would not believe, if, like a Catholic, you believed that the form of a servant was taken up by the form of God, not that the form of God was changed into the form of a servant; and if you thought, not carnally but spiritually, that, the visible man being assumed, the invisible God remained; and if you did not, by contending, distrust, but, by understanding, behold; and if you could faithfully consider that the Holy Spirit, his invisible nature remaining, in no way changed and turned into the appearance of fire or of a dove, appeared through a subordinate creature, as he willed, visibly[^54]. Remember, however, that by no divine testimonies could you demonstrate, against my proposition, either that the Father is greater than the Holy Spirit, or that the Father was adored by the Holy Spirit.

## Though the Father Is Unbegotten, the Son Is Nonetheless Equal.

**20.** In the twentieth place, since you had said of the Son, *If he is equal to the Father, surely he is such as he; if such, surely unborn*, I answered you: But you say, I said, of the Son, *If equal, surely such*; that is, *that because he is not unbegotten, he does not seem such. You could say that he whom Adam begot is not a man, because Adam himself was not begotten but made by God. But if Adam could both not be begotten and yet beget what he himself was, will you not have it that God could beget a God equal to himself?* To these things I do not wonder that you found no answer; but I plainly praise that you did not even try to answer. And would that you had done this everywhere! For nowhere in our discourses could you find what to answer rightly, and yet almost everywhere you were unwilling to keep silent. But since in other matters you said so many things not necessary to the cause that is at issue between us, and consumed the time in speaking, thanks are to be given you where you saw that you could not refute certain things in such a way that you preferred to pass them over in deepest silence.
