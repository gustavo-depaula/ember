# A Sermon of the Arians

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten God, the firstborn of all creation.

**2.** Established by the will of God his Father before all ages.

**3.** By his will and command he made, by his own power, things heavenly and earthly, visible and invisible, bodies and spirits, out of things that did not exist, so that they might exist.

**4.** And before he made all things, he had been established as the God and Lord, king and creator of all that was to be, having in his nature foreknowledge of all that was to come, and in making all things awaiting in everything the command of the Father; he himself, by the will and command of the Father, came down from heaven and came into this world, as he himself says: *For I have not come from myself, but he sent me*[^1].

**5.** And because, of all the spiritual and rational ranks, man seemed, on account of the quality and frailty of the body, to have been made a little lower than the angels[^2], so that he might not think himself worthless and despair of his salvation, the Lord Jesus, honoring his own handiwork, deigned to take up human flesh; and he showed that man is not worthless but precious, as it is written: *A man is a great thing, and a precious thing is a man*[^3]. And therefore he deigned to make man alone heir to his Father, and indeed coheir to himself, so that what man had received less of in nature he might have more of in honor.

**6.** *When,* he says, *the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, born of a woman*[^4]. He who took up flesh by the will of the Father, he too dwelt in the body by his will and command, as he himself says: *I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^5]. He too, by the will of the Father, was baptized at thirty years of age, made manifest by the voice and testimony of the Father[^6], and by the will and command of the Father he preached the gospel of the kingdom of heaven, as he himself says: *I must preach the gospel to other cities also; for I was sent for this*[^7]; and: *He himself gave me a commandment, what I should say and what I should speak*[^8]. And so, by the will and command of the Father, he hastened to his passion and death, as he himself says: *Father, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not what I will, but what you will*[^9]. And the Apostle affirms and says: *He became obedient to the Father unto death, even death on a cross*[^10].

**7.** He too, hanging on the cross, by the will and command of the Father gave up into the hands of men the human flesh that he had taken from the holy virgin Mary, and commended his divinity into the hands of the Father, saying: *Father, into your hands I commend my spirit*[^11]. For Mary bore a body destined to die, but the immortal God begot an immortal Son. Therefore the death of Christ is not a diminishment of the divinity, but a laying down of the body. For just as his generation from the virgin was not a corruption of his deity but an assumption of a body, so too in his death there was no suffering or failure of his deity, but a separation of his flesh. For just as one who tears a garment does an injury to the one wearing it, so too those who crucified his flesh inflicted an insult upon his divinity.

**8.** He who by the will and command of the Father fulfilled the whole plan, by the will and command of the Father raised his own body from the dead; and with that very body, as a shepherd with his sheep, and a priest with his offering, and a king with his purple, and God with his temple, he was taken up by the Father into glory.

**9.** He who by the will of the Father came down and ascended, by the will and command of the Father sits at his right hand, hearing the Father say to him: *Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet*[^12]. He who by the will and command of the Father sits at his right hand, he by the will and command of the Father is to come at the consummation of the age, as the Apostle cries out and says: *The Lord himself, at the command, at the voice of an archangel, and at the trumpet of God, will descend from heaven*[^13]. He who by the will and command of the Father will come, by the will and command of the Father is to judge the whole world in equity and to render to each according to his faith and his works, as he himself says: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*; and again: *As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is true; for I do not seek my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^14]. Hence, even in judging, he puts the presence of the Father first and his own divine dignity and power second, saying: *Come, you blessed of my Father*[^15]. The Son, therefore, is the just judge; but the honor and authority of the one judging are the imperial laws of the Father; just as the dutiful advocacy and consolation of the Holy Spirit are the dignity of the only-begotten God, the just judge.

**10.** The Son, therefore, was begotten by the Father; the Holy Spirit was made through the Son.

**11.** The Son proclaims the Father; the Holy Spirit announces the Son.

**12.** The first and chief work of the Son is to reveal the glory of his begetter; the first and chief work of the Holy Spirit is to make manifest the dignity of Christ in the souls of men.

**13.** The Son is the witness of the Father; the Spirit is the witness of the Son.

**14.** The Son is sent by the Father; the Spirit is sent by the Son.

**15.** The Son is the minister of the Father; the Holy Spirit is the minister of the Son.

**16.** The Son is commanded by the Father; the Holy Spirit is commanded by the Son.

**17.** The Son is subject to the Father; the Holy Spirit is subject to the Son.

**18.** The Son does what the Father commands; the Holy Spirit speaks what the Son enjoins.

**19.** The Son adores and honors the Father; the Holy Spirit adores and honors the Son, as the Son himself says: *Father, I have honored you on earth; I have accomplished the work that you gave me*[^16]; and concerning the Holy Spirit he says: *He will honor me, for he will receive from what is mine and will announce it to you*[^17].

**20.** The Son can do nothing of himself[^18], but in all things awaits the nod of the Father. The Spirit does not speak of himself, but in all things awaits the command of Christ: *He will not,* he says, *speak of himself; but whatever he hears he will speak, and he will announce to you the things to come*[^19].

**21.** The Son intercedes for us with the Father; the Spirit makes petition for us to the Son.

**22.** The Son is the living and true, proper and worthy image of all the goodness and wisdom and power of the Father; the Spirit is the manifestation of all the wisdom and power of the Son.

**23.** The Son is not a part or portion of the Father, but the proper and most beloved, perfect and full only-begotten Son. The Spirit is not a part or portion of the Son, but the first and chief work of the only-begotten God above all others whatsoever.

**24.** The Father is greater than his Son; the Son is incomparably greater and better than the Spirit.

**25.** The Father is God and Lord to his Son; the Son is God and Lord to the Spirit.

**26.** The Father, willing immovably and impassibly, begot the Son; the Son, without labor and weariness, by his own power alone, made the Spirit.

**27.** The Son, as a priest, adores his God, and is adored by all as God and creator of all; but the Father alone adores no one, since he has no one greater, nor equal, whom he might adore; he gives thanks to no one, since he has received the benefit of no one; out of his goodness he granted to all that they might exist, while he himself received from no one what he is. Therefore there is this distinction of three substances, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and this difference of three realities, the unbegotten God, the only-begotten God, and the advocate Spirit; and the Father is God and Lord to his Son and to all things that were made through the power of the Son by his will; the Son is the minister and high priest of his Father, but he is the Lord and God of all his own works, by the Father's willing it.

**28.** And just as no one can pass to the Father without the Son, so too no one can adore the Son in truth without the Holy Spirit; therefore the Son is adored in the Holy Spirit.

**29.** Through the Son the Father is glorified.

**30.** The work and care of the Holy Spirit is to sanctify and to guard the saints; and to sanctify not only rational beings, as some suppose, but also many irrational ones; and to restore to their former state those who have fallen through their own negligence; to teach the ignorant, to admonish the forgetful, to reprove sinners, to exhort the slothful, to think and to act anxiously for their salvation, to lead the erring back to the way of truth, to heal the weak, to restrain the frailty of the body through the eagerness of the soul, to confirm them in the love of piety and chastity, and to enlighten all; above all, to bestow faith and love on each one, and, according to each one's zeal and diligence, according to the sincerity and simplicity of mind, according to the measure of faith and the merit of conduct, to apportion grace for their benefit, and to assign each one to that work and purpose for which he is fitted.

**31.** He is other than the Son both in nature and in order, in rank and in disposition, in dignity and in power, in might and in operation; just as the Son too, in nature and in order, in rank and in disposition, in divine dignity and in power, the only-begotten God, is other than the unbegotten God.

**32.** It is impossible, therefore, that one and the same should be Father and Son, the one begetting and the one being born, the one to whom witness is borne and the one who bears witness, the greater and the one who confesses the greater, the one who sits at the right hand and the one who gave the honor of the seat, the one who was sent and the one who sent; nor that disciple and teacher, as he himself taught when he said: *As the Father taught me, so I speak*[^20]; the like and the imitator and the one whose like and imitator he is; the one who prays and the one who hears; the one who gives thanks and the one who blesses; the one who received the commandment and the one who gave the commandment; and the minister and the one who commands, the suppliant and the eminent one, the subject and the superior, the firstborn and the everlasting one, the only-begotten and the unbegotten, the priest and the God—that these should be one and the same.

**33.** But God, who is without beginning, foreknew that he would be the father of his child, the only-begotten God; yet he never foreknew that he would become God, since he is unbegotten and never began either to foreknow or to know. And what is foreknowledge but the knowledge of things to come? Now the Father, by begetting the Son, was named by that very Son; and by the Son's revelation he was known by all Christians as the God and Father of the only-begotten God, and was made manifest as greater than the great and better than the good.

**34.** And because the Homoousians say that our Savior spoke all these things about the Father's foreknowledge and about his own subjection out of humility, whereas we Christians believe that he spoke all these things with the Father commanding and the Son complying, we say and prove that the heretics are convicted and refuted by their own statements. For if he humbled himself, that very humility of his shows his obedience; and that obedience itself declares that one is eminent above and another is subsisting beneath and subject, just as the Apostle says: *He humbled himself, becoming obedient to the Father unto death*[^21]. And that humility of his is truth, not falsehood. For who, or when, that is wise is content to humble himself, unless he has someone greater and better whom he is eager to please through humility? *And I,* he says, *always do the things that are pleasing to him*[^22]. For having once been born before all ages by the will of God, he does all things according to that one's will. But if he humbled himself and lied—which God forbid—and if the truth lies, which is impossible, where would anyone seek the truth? But the truth neither lies nor varies, he who came for this, that he might teach the truth; he who is not a teacher of ignorance but a master of truth, as he himself said: *Do not call yourselves masters on earth; for one is your master, Christ*[^23]. But if they say that, humbling himself on earth on account of his incarnation, he was speaking these things for the sake of men, let us show them that those testimonies which are set down in the Scriptures concerning the subjection of the Son are greater and firmer than those things which are done in the Gospel. For if he was humbling himself on earth for the sake of men, and not complying with his Father as an obedient and subject son with incomparable love and thanksgiving—since the more exalted he is in power, the more humble he is in compliance—why, before he took up flesh, did he obey when commanded, and why does he now, sitting at the right hand of God, intercede for us[^24], and, while established in a body on earth, did he promise that he would ask the Father in heaven, saying: *And I will ask my Father, and he will give you another advocate*[^25]? And if in all these things they still refuse to believe, because of the hardness and blindness of their heart, but dare to say that all these things are done for the sake of humility, then—after the consummation of the age, unless he knew himself to be subject and obedient by nature and by will, why would he humble himself, where humility is not necessary for the sake of men? For after the consummation of the age too, when all things will have been made subject to him[^26]—since now indeed all things are subject by nature, as a creation to its Creator, but by will, on account of free choice, we do not see all things subject to him; yet then, on the day of judgment, when at the name of Jesus every knee will bow, of things heavenly, earthly, and infernal, and every tongue will confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father[^27], all things will be subject to him without end, both by will and by nature; and he himself, after the subjection of all things to him, abiding in that subjection and love in which he always abides, he too as Son will be subject to the one who made all things subject to him; no Christian who hears is ignorant that faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ[^28]—so that God may be all in all, always holding the monarchy and power over all things, to whom be glory and honor, praise and thanksgiving, through his only-begotten Son, our Lord and Savior, in the Holy Spirit, now and through all ages of ages. Amen.
