# Epilogue

**1.** See how many heresies we have recounted, and yet we have not fulfilled the measure of your request. For "from the time the Christian religion"—to use your words—"received the name of the promised inheritance," how could I have recounted all the heresies that arose, when I could not know them all? This I think is because none of those whose writings on this matter I have read set down all of them. Since indeed I found in one author heresies that I did not find in another, and again in this one those that the other did not set down. But I have set down more than they did because I gathered from them all those which I did not find in each one separately, with the addition too of those which I, recalling them myself, could not find in any of them. Whence I rightly believe that I too have not set down all of them, because I could not read all who have written on this matter, nor do I see that any of those I read did this. Finally, even if I have perhaps set down all of them—which I do not think—I certainly do not know whether they are all. And so what you want to be brought to a close by my speaking cannot even be comprised by my knowing, because I cannot know all things.

**2.** I have heard that holy Jerome wrote about heresies, but that little work of his we could neither find in our library, nor do we know where it can be obtained. A most reliable, studious man, well acquainted with his books, when questioned about this, said that someone—I know not who—who spread this report did not know what he was saying. "For about heresies," he says, "holy Jerome composed no little work." But if you should learn of it, get to it, and perhaps you will have something better than this of ours—although I think that even he, learned man though he was, could not have tracked down all the heresies. And certainly the ABELOITES, heretics of our region, he did not, I suppose, know; and so perhaps elsewhere too others, hidden away in the most obscure places, escaping his notice by the very obscurity of their localities.

**3.** But now that which your letter contains—"that we say absolutely everything in which heretics dissent from the truth"—even if I knew everything, I could not do; how much less can I, who cannot know everything? For there are heretics, it must be confessed, who attack the rule of truth with single doctrines, or with not many more, like the Macedonians or the Photinians, and any others who are so disposed. But those, so to speak, fable-mongers—that is, those who weave together empty fables, and these long and intricate—are full of so many false doctrines that they themselves cannot count them, or can only with the greatest difficulty. Nor does any heresy easily become so known to any outsider as to its own members; whence I profess that I have neither stated nor learned all the doctrines even of those I have recounted. For who does not see how much labor this matter demands, and how many pages? Yet it is not for that reason of little profit to avoid these errors, once they are read and known, which I have woven into this work. For what the catholic Church thinks against these things, which you thought I should state, is asked to no purpose, since for this it suffices to know that the Church thinks against these things, and that one ought to receive none of them into one's faith. But how the truths to be asserted and defended against these things are to be stated exceeds the scale of this work. Yet it greatly helps a faithful heart to know what is not to be believed, even if it cannot refute it by a capacity for disputing. And so every catholic Christian ought not to believe these things. But not everyone who does not believe these things ought consequently to think or call himself a catholic Christian. For there can both be and arise other heresies which have not been recounted in this work of ours, any one of which, whoever holds it, is not a catholic Christian. What, then, makes someone a heretic is next to be inquired into, so that, when by the Lord's help we avoid this, we may avoid the heretical poisons not only of those we know but also of those we do not know, whether those that have already arisen or those that can still arise. But let there now be an end to this volume, which I believed should be sent to you before I completed this whole work, so that all of you who read it may help me by your prayers toward fulfilling what remains—which, however, you see to be great.
