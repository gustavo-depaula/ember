# Appendix

## [I. On the Timotheans]

The Timotheans say that the Son of God was indeed a true man, born of the Virgin Mary, but that he did not so render one person that he was not reduced to one nature; wanting the inner parts of the Virgin to have been a kind of smelting-furnace, through which the two natures, that is, God and man, dissolved and compacted into one mass, presented one form of God and man—the property of the natures effecting the union being, that is, changed. And to confirm an impiety of this kind, which asserts that God was turned from his own nature, they press into service the testimony of the Evangelist who says, *And the Word was made flesh*[^1], which they interpret thus: the divine nature was turned into the human. O abolition, impinging upon that inviolable substance! The beginner of this impiety, Timothy, now an exile at Biza, a city of Bithynia, deceives many by the image of a continent and religious life.

## [II. On the Nestorians]

The Nestorians, from the bishop Nestorius, who dared to lay down as doctrine against the catholic faith that Christ our Lord, God and man, was not—in that which was made the mediator of God and men—conceived in the womb of the Virgin by the Holy Spirit, but that afterward God was mingled with the man. Nor did he say that God suffered and was buried as man; contending to empty out our whole remedy, whereby the Word of God so deigned to take up man in the womb of the Virgin that there came to be one person of God and man, on account of which, having been so singularly and marvelously born, and having died too for our sins, paying back the things he had not seized, God and man, rising from the dead, ascended into heaven.

## [Iia. On the Nestorians]

The Nestorian heresy is named from its author Nestorius, bishop of the Church of Constantinople. Whose perversity was this: that he preached a man only, begotten of the blessed Virgin Mary, whom the Word of God had not received into the unity of person and into an inseparable fellowship. And on this account the Virgin Mother should be understood not as theotokos but as anthropotokos—which the ears of catholics could in no way bear, because such a birth asserted not one Christ in the truth of flesh and deity, but a twofold one, which is a sacrilege.

## [III. On the Eutychians]

The Eutychians arose from a certain Eutyches, a presbyter of the Church of Constantinople, who, while he seemed to refute Nestorius, crossed over into Apollinaris and Manichaeus, and, denying the truth of the humanity in Christ, ascribes whatever of our own nature was received by the Word to the divine essence only; so that, by denying our nature in Christ, he might dissolve the sacrament of human salvation, which is only in both substances—not perceiving, in his foolish impiety, that what had been lacking to the Head would be taken away from the whole body.

## [Iiia. On the Eutychians]

The Eutychians, from the presbyter Eutyches, who, while seeking to win royal patronage for himself through ambition, dared to lay down as doctrine that before the incarnation there were two natures in Christ, but that after the Word was made flesh there was one nature, that is, the divine, and the whole man translated into God; and that a true man was not conceived in the womb of the Virgin, nor was flesh taken from the body of Mary. But he maintained that a body, I know not where, was formed so subtle that it could pass through the inviolate virginal inner parts of the mother; and, affirming him to be wholly God, one nature, he held that not God-and-man, but the divinity alone, took up the passion, and bore it up into heaven—whereas he who was born of the Virgin Mary and made of the seed of David according to the flesh, crucified, dead, and buried, rose from the dead and bore up into heaven a perfect man, whom we await to come to judge the living and the dead.

This both the catholic faith confirms and all the authority of the divine Scriptures proclaims. Recoiling against this faith, the aforesaid Eutyches—when the synod of Ephesus, oppressed by royal power, and especially Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, followed his errors—not only deprived Flavian, bishop of the people of Constantinople, of his honor, but also had him driven from his homeland and given over to exile, with Hilary, deacon of the venerable apostolic see, standing by and protesting. They also, in a second session, deprived other priests, who were absent, of their honors; but this man the divine majesty itself, by a provident and swift and just judgment, consumed. For with the emperor Theodosius taken away and removed from this life, and likewise Chrysaphius, by whose patronage the oft-mentioned Eutyches, attacking the catholic faith, had brought in the aforesaid error—holy Flavian the bishop and confessor going before to God, so that together they might await the examination of God the just judge, as far as has been reported. By the authority, therefore, of the aforesaid apostolic see both the strength of the faith was expressed and the error of the abominable doctrine was extinguished. Indeed, the body of the confessor, brought back with glory, a worthy place of the holy Church received, the priests being released, that is, who, worthy by their confession, accepted by God and pleasing to men, were restored to the priesthood. The author of the abominable error, Eutyches, having been driven from the province, and the aforesaid synod detesting by its subscription the wrongly conceived errors, against the sound doctrine, the peace of holy mother Church recalls her priests.

[^1]: John 1:14.
