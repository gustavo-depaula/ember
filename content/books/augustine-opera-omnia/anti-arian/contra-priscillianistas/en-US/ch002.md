# The Inquiry of Orosius to Augustine: On the Error of the Priscillianists and Origenists

TO THE MOST BLESSED FATHER AUGUSTINE THE BISHOP, FROM OROSIUS

**1.** I had indeed already suggested this to your Holiness, but I was planning to present a memorandum on the matter I had suggested once I sensed that your mind was free from other obligations of dictation. But since my lords, your sons, the bishops Eutropius and Paul, moved by the same concern for the salvation of all that moves me too, your servant boy, have already produced a memorandum about a number of heresies, yet did not point out all of them, it became necessary for me to bring it out in haste and to heap together into one all the trees of perdition with their roots and branches, and to offer them to your fiery spirit[^1], so that, once you have viewed their array and discerned their wickedness, you may measure out what disposition of power you can bring to bear. Only do you, most blessed father, root out and cut down the malignant plantings or graftings of others[^2], and scatter the true seed for us from your own springs that will water it[^3]. I pledge God as my witness and hope for the increase of your work, because that land, which now, insincerely cultivated, yields ungrateful fruits, if you visit it by entrusting and refilling it from that hidden manna[^4] at my hands, will one day, poured out more freely, advance even to the hundredfold fruit in its abundance[^5].

Through you the Lord our God, through you, I say, blessed father, may he amend in word those whom he has chastised with the sword. I was sent to you by God; through him I hope for help from you, as I consider in what manner it came about that I came here. I recognize why I came: without willing it, without necessity, without consent I went out from my homeland, driven by a certain hidden force, until I was carried to the shore of this land. Here at last I came to my senses and understood that I was being directed to come to you. Do not judge me shameless if you receive me confessing it. Make me a fit merchant for my beloved mistress, having found the pearl[^6], that I may not return a runaway slave with his substance squandered. We have been torn apart more grievously by corrupt teachers than by the most bloodthirsty enemies. We confess the offense; you see through to the wound; the one thing that remains is this: with the Lord's help, bestow the medicine. Briefly, then, I will show both what was badly planted before and grew strong, and what was afterward grafted in worse and prevailed.

**2.** Priscillian, in the first place more wretched than the Manicheans in this, that he confirmed his heresy from the Old Testament as well, teaches that the soul, which is born of God, proceeds from a certain storehouse, professes before God that it will do battle, and is instructed by the exhortation of angels; then, descending through certain circles, it is captured by the malignant principalities and, according to the will of the victorious prince, is thrust into various bodies, and a bond is ascribed to them. From this he also affirmed that astrology prevails, asserting that Christ canceled this bond and fixed it to the cross through his passion[^7], as Priscillian himself says in one of his letters: "This is the first wisdom, to understand in the patterns of souls the natures of the divine powers and the arrangement of the body. In this both heaven and earth seem bound, and all the principalities of the world seem to be constrained to overcome the arrangements of the saints. For the patriarchs hold the first circle of God and the divine bond of the souls to be sent in flesh, fashioned by the consent of the angels and of God and of all souls, who possess it against the work of the formal warfare," and so on. He further taught that the names of the patriarchs are members of the soul, in that Reuben is in the head, Judah in the breast, Levi in the heart, Benjamin in the thighs, and the like. Conversely, in the members of the body the signs of heaven are arranged—that is, Aries in the head, Taurus in the neck, Gemini in the arms, Cancer in the breast, and the rest—wishing it to be understood that the eternal darkness, and from it the prince of the world, came forth. And he confirms this very thing from a certain book entitled "The Memoir of the Apostles," where the Savior seems to be questioned secretly by the disciples and to expound the gospel parable that reads, *A sower went out to sow*[^8], claiming that the sower was not good, asserting that, if he had been good, he would not have been careless, nor would he have let the seed fall either along the path or on rocky ground or on uncultivated land[^9], wishing it to be understood that this is the one who sows, who scatters the captured souls into whatever various bodies he pleases. In this book too many things are said about the prince of the waters and the prince of fire, wishing it to be understood that all good things are brought about in this world by art, not by the power of God. For he says that there is a certain virgin of light, whom God, wishing to give rain to men, shows to the prince of the waters, who, while he desires to seize her, is stirred up, sweats, and makes rain, and, deserted by her, rouses thunder with his bellowing. The Trinity, however, he spoke of in word only; for, asserting a unity without any existence or distinct property, by removing the "and" he taught that the Father, the Son, the Holy Spirit—this is the one Christ.

**3.** Then two of my fellow citizens, Avitus and another Avitus, when by now truth alone had laid bare so foul a confusion by itself, sought out foreign parts. For the one set out for Jerusalem, the other for Rome. On their return, the one reported Origen, the other Victorinus; of these two the one yielded to the other, yet both condemned Priscillian. Of Victorinus we know little, because, almost before his publications appeared, the follower of Victorinus gave way to Origen. So they began to put forward many magnificent things from Origen, which on slight occasion the truth itself would cut away. For we learned a doctrine concerning the Trinity sound enough, that all things which were made were made by God[^10] and were all very good[^11] and were made from nothing, and then, after that, interpretations of the Scriptures sober enough. All these things were at once received by the wise, with a faithful purging of the old errors. There remained only the offense regarding "from nothing." For it had been settled by persuasion that the soul exists; yet it could not be settled that it was made from nothing[^12], since they argued that the will of God cannot be nothing. This remains, almost down to the present. But those two Avitii, and with them the Greek Basil, who taught these things most blessedly, handed down certain things from the books of Origen himself that were not correct, as I now thoroughly understand. First, saying that all things, before they appeared as made, had always remained made in the wisdom of God, in these words: "For whatever God made, he did not begin by making it." Then they said that of angels, principalities, powers, souls, and demons there is one origin and one substance, and that a place was given to the archangel or to the soul or to the demon according to the quality of its merits, using these words: "A lesser fault earned a greater place." That the world was made last, in order that in it the souls might be purified that had sinned beforehand. The eternal fire, indeed, by which sinners are punished, they declared to be neither a true fire nor eternal, saying that "fire" was said to mean the punishment of one's own conscience, and that "eternal," according to the Greek etymology, is not everlasting—adding even a Latin proof, that it is said *forever* and *unto the age of the age*[^13], where it set "age" after "eternal"; and that thus all the souls of sinners, after the purification of conscience, would return into the unity of the body of Christ. They also wished to assert this concerning the devil, but they did not prevail, namely that, since the good substance made in him cannot perish, once the malice of the devil is wholly burned away his substance is at some time to be saved. Concerning the body of the Lord they handed down this, that the Son of God, coming all the way down to us after so many thousands of years, was not idle up to that point, but, preaching forgiveness to the angels, to the powers, and to all the higher beings—taking on the quality of the form of those whom he was visiting—thickened all the way to the palpability of flesh in the appearance of his assumption; determining this by his passion and resurrection, he again, until he should come to the Father, thinned it out by ascending; and so neither was the body laid down anywhere, nor is God, reigning in any body, circumscribed. They also said that the sun and the moon and the stars are the creation that is to be understood as subject to corruption unwillingly; and that these are not elemental fires but rational powers, and that they render their service to corruption *because of him who subjected it in hope*[^14].

**4.** This, so far as I have been able to retain it, has been briefly set forth, so that, with all the diseases observed, you may hasten to apply the medicine. *The truth of Christ is in me*[^15], that, out of the venerable reverence due to your holiness, I would not dare to be shameless, did I not recognize, by the evident judgment and ordinance of God, that remedies are to be brought forth for that great and excellent people, upon whom, as upon a sinner, a blow has been laid[^16], just as after the blow a cure is owed—and that you have been chosen and I have been sent. Be mindful of me, most blessed father, and of the many who, together with me, await your eloquence to descend upon them like dew. Deign to do so.
