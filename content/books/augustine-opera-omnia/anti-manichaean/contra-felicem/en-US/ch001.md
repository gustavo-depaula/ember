# Revisions II

## Chapter 8 (35): Answer to Felix, a Manichean, in Two Books

For two days I debated in church, before the people, against a certain Manichean named Felix. He had, in fact, come to Hippo to sow the same error there, for he was one of their teachers, though uneducated in the liberal arts, yet more cunning than Fortunatus[^1]. The proceedings are ecclesiastical records, but they are counted among my books. There are, then, two books, and in the second of them there is a discussion of the free choice of the will, whether for doing evil or for doing good. But concerning the grace by which people truly become free, of whom it is written, *If the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed*[^2], we were under no necessity to discuss it more carefully, given the sort of person with whom we were dealing.

This work begins thus: *In the sixth consulship of Honorius Augustus, on the seventh day before the ides of December.*

[^1]: See Confess. 5.6.11.
[^2]: John 8:36.
