# Book I

**1.** In the sixth consulship of Honorius Augustus, on the seventh day before the ides of December,

Augustine, bishop of the Catholic Church of the region of Hippo Regius, said: You know that yesterday you said that you could defend the writings of Mani and maintain that they have the truth. If it pleases you to do this today, or if you presume that you are able to, say so.

Felix the Manichean said: I do not deny that I said I would defend my law, if the authors of my law are brought forward in our midst.

And when Bishop Augustine had brought forward the *letter* of Mani that they call *The Foundation*, he said: If I read from this book that you see me carrying the *letter* of Mani that you call *The Foundation*, can you recognize whether it is the one?

Felix said: I recognize it.

Augustine said: Take it yourself, and read.

And when Felix had taken the book, he read: *Mani, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the providence of God the Father. These are wholesome words from the perennial and living fountain; whoever hears them and first believes them, and then keeps what they enjoin, will never be subject to death but will enjoy eternal and glorious life. For he is surely to be judged blessed who has been instructed in this divine knowledge, by which, set free, he will abide in everlasting life.*

Bishop Augustine said: You have certainly recognized the letter of your Mani?

Felix said: I have recognized it.

Augustine said: Prove to us, then, how this Mani is an apostle of Jesus Christ. For we nowhere read of him among the apostles in the Gospel; and we know who was ordained in the place of Judas the betrayer, namely, Saint Matthias[^1]; and everyone acknowledges who it was that was afterward called by the Lord's voice from heaven, namely, the apostle Paul[^2]. Prove to us, then, that this Mani is an apostle of Christ, which he dared to put at the head of his letter.

**2.** Felix said: And let Your Holiness prove to me what is written in the Gospel, where Christ says: *I am going to the Father, and I will send you the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, who will lead you into all truth*[^3]. Prove to me, apart from this writing, that this is the writing of the Holy Spirit, which Christ promised, in which all truth is to be found; and if I find the truth in other books that do not belong to Mani, then Christ handed those down too. For it was said by Christ that the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, will himself lead into all truth: according to Christ's word I reject the writings of Mani.

Augustine said: Since, then, you could not prove how Mani is an apostle of Christ, and you demand that I prove how Christ sent the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, whom he promised, so that you may then reject the writings of Mani if you find Christ's promise fulfilled apart from the writings of Mani—although you ought to have answered my questions first—nevertheless, see, I answer first myself, and I show you when the Holy Spirit was sent whom Christ promised.

(And he goes to the Gospel and to the Acts of the Apostles.)

**3.** And when he had taken the book of the Gospel, he read aloud: *Now while they were saying these things, Jesus stood in their midst and said to them, Peace be with you; it is I, do not be afraid.* And as he read, he said: This was after the resurrection. And when he had said this, he read: *But they were startled and frightened, and supposed that they saw a spirit. And he said to them, Why are you troubled, and why do questionings rise in your hearts? See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself; handle me, and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have. And when he had said this, he showed them his hands and feet. And while they still disbelieved and wondered for joy, he said, Have you anything here to eat? And they offered him a piece of broiled fish and some honeycomb. And when he had eaten before them, taking what was left he gave it to them, and said to them, These are the words that I spoke to you while I was still with you, that everything must be fulfilled that is written about me in the law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms. Then he opened their minds so that they might understand the Scriptures; and he said to them, Thus it is written, and thus it was necessary that the Christ should suffer and rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins should be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem. You are witnesses of these things. And I will send the promise of my Father upon you; but stay in the city until you are clothed with power from on high*[^4].

And when he had returned the book of the Gospel, he took up the Acts of the Apostles and said: We have heard in the Gospel that the holy Evangelist recorded the promise of Christ, which is in the Gospel according to John, whom Felix here has mentioned. For where the Lord said, *I send you the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete*[^5], John the Evangelist wrote it. But what I have just read, Luke the Evangelist attested, and it agrees with the truth of the apostle John. Let us also see how what the Lord promised was accomplished, and how it was fulfilled; so that, when the fulfillment of the Lord's promise has been set forth from the canonical books of the holy Church, we may not look for another Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, lest we fall into the snares of seducers.

**4.** And he read aloud from the Acts of the Apostles: *In the first book, O Theophilus, I have dealt with all that Jesus began to do and teach, on the day when he chose the apostles through the Holy Spirit and commanded them to preach the Gospel; to whom he presented himself alive after his passion by many proofs, appearing to them during forty days and speaking of the kingdom of God, and how he lived with them. And he charged them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise of the Father, which, he said, you heard from my mouth; for John baptized with water, but you shall begin to be baptized with the Holy Spirit, whom you are also to receive not many days from now, at Pentecost. So when they had come together, they asked him, saying, Lord, will you at this time present the kingdom to Israel? But he said, No one can know the time that the Father has fixed by his own authority; but you shall receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you shall be my witnesses at Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria and to the whole earth. When he had said these things, a cloud took him up, and he was lifted up from them. And as they were gazing while he went into heaven, behold, two men stood by them in white robes, who said to them, Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up into heaven from you, will come in the same way as you saw him going into heaven.*

*Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mount called Olivet, which is near Jerusalem, a sabbath day's journey away. And when they had entered, they went up to the upper room, where Peter and John, James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James the son of Alphaeus, and Simon the Zealot, and Judas the son of James were staying. And they all continued steadfastly of one mind in prayer, with the women, and Mary who had been the mother of Jesus, and his brethren. And in those days Peter stood up in the midst of the disciples and said (now the crowd was gathered in one place, about a hundred and twenty persons): Brethren, this scripture had to be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit foretold by the mouth of holy David concerning Judas, who was the guide to those who arrested Jesus, for he was numbered among us and had obtained a share in this ministry. Now this man bought a field with the reward of his iniquity, and he tied a noose around his neck, and falling headlong he burst open in the middle, and all his bowels gushed out. And it became known to all who lived in Jerusalem, so that the field was called in their own language Akeldama, that is, Field of Blood. For it is written in the book of Psalms, Let his habitation become desolate, and let there be no one to live in it, and let another take his office.*

*So one of the men who accompanied us during all the time that the Lord Jesus Christ went in and out among us, beginning from the baptism of John until the day when he was taken up from us, must become with us a witness to his resurrection. And they put forward two, Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also called Justus, and Matthias. And they prayed and said, You, Lord, who know the hearts of all, show which of these two you have chosen to take the place in this ministry and apostleship from which Judas turned aside, to go to his own place. And they cast their lots, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was enrolled with the eleven apostles as the twelfth*[^6]*.*

And when he had read, he said: We have heard who was ordained in the place of Judas the betrayer, which I mentioned a little while ago, so that no one may creep in by fraud into the number of the apostles and deceive the ignorant under the name of apostleship.

**5.** Now let us see what we promised, how Christ's promise concerning the Holy Spirit was fulfilled. And when he had said this, he read in the passage: *When the day of Pentecost was completed, they were all together in one place. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a violent wind rushing along, and it filled the whole house in which they were sitting. And there appeared to them divided tongues as of fire, which also rested upon each one of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in various tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter. Now there were dwelling at Jerusalem Jews, men from every nation under heaven. And when this sound was made, the crowd gathered and was confused, because each one heard them speaking in his own language and his own tongue. And they were amazed and astonished, saying to one another, Are not all these who are speaking Galileans by birth? And how is it that we recognize among them the language in which we were born? Parthians and Medes and Elamites, and those who dwell in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia, Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya around Cyrene, and the visiting Romans, both Jews and proselytes, Cretans and Arabs, we hear them telling in our own tongues the mighty works of God*[^7].

And when he had read it aloud, he said: Have you now heard how the Holy Spirit was sent? I have proved what you demanded of me; it remains for you also to do what you promised. Since we have found when he sent the Holy Spirit whom he promised, reject that writing which, under the name of the Holy Spirit, sought to defraud either the reader or the hearer.

**6.** Felix said: I do not deny what I said, that when it has been proved to me that the Holy Spirit taught the truth that I seek, I reject that writing. For Your Holiness read this to me, the passage where the apostles received the Holy Spirit, and among those apostles I seek one who will teach me about the beginning, the middle, and the end.

Augustine said: If you read that the Lord said this, I send you the Holy Spirit, who is to teach you the beginning, the middle, and the end, you would rightly bind me to show whom the Holy Spirit taught.

Felix said: Since Your Holiness says this, that the apostles themselves received the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, I say again: from among the apostles themselves let whichever one you wish teach me what Mani taught me, or let whichever of the twelve you wish set aside his teaching.

Augustine said: Far be it from the faith of the apostles that they should teach the doctrine of the sacrilegious Mani. But since you say that one of them ought to set aside the teaching of Mani, and yet the holy apostles are now not in the body, I, the least not only of all the apostles but of all the bishops (for when do I aspire to the merits of the apostles?), insofar as the Lord has deigned to grant me of his own Spirit, set aside the teaching of Mani, once the rest of that letter has begun to be read, which you yourself do not deny to be Mani's.

Felix said: You said that the apostles have departed from the body, yet their writings are valid even now; and since Your Holiness said it, and since you gave permission to say what we wished without any fear, you said, I set aside the law of Mani; and I say: *Every man is a liar, God alone is true*[^8]; the Scriptures of God speak.

**7.** Augustine said: You indeed are a man, and, as the matter itself is about to show, a liar. And since you have spoken what you wished, not from the Scripture of God, I too must speak what I wish. For if your Mani has the truth, it cannot be destroyed by me; if it is falsehood, what does it matter by whom it is destroyed? Yet since you spoke of the writings of the apostles, that even though they are not in the body, their writings are here: the apostles were taken up before the error of Mani was born into the world; for this reason the writings of the apostles are not found arguing explicitly against Mani. Nevertheless, that the apostle Paul, in that Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, whom he had received, foresaw what was to come concerning such people as Mani was, and as you too are, I read aloud, so that you may recognize it.

And taking up the Letter of the apostle Paul to Timothy, he read: *Now the Spirit expressly says that in the last times some will depart from the faith, giving heed to deceitful spirits and the teachings of demons, through the hypocrisy of liars whose conscience is seared, who forbid marriage and enjoin abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving by the faithful and by those who have known the truth; for every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving, for it is sanctified through the word of God and prayer. If you teach the brethren these things, you will be a good minister of Jesus Christ*[^9].

And when he had read it, he said: I say that this, concerning you and such as you who say that some creature of God is unclean, and who say that all intercourse, even that which takes place with a wife, is fornication—for this is what he means by *forbidding marriage*—was designated and expressed through the Spirit of prophecy. But if you deny that all intercourse is fornication, or if you say that all human food permitted and granted to people to eat is clean, then you are not among those of whom the Apostle prophesied. But if you are found among those of whom I have spoken, see, you have the apostle Paul destroying and setting aside the teaching of the Mani who was to come. So answer what I ask: Is all intercourse fornication, or is intercourse with a wife not a sin?

**8.** Felix said: Let what the Apostle said be read aloud to me again.

And it was read aloud: *Now the Spirit expressly says that in the last times some will depart from the faith, giving heed to deceitful spirits and the teachings of demons, through the hypocrisy of liars whose conscience is seared, who forbid marriage and enjoin abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving by the faithful and by those who have known the truth; for every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving, for it is sanctified through the word of God and prayer. If you teach the brethren these things, you will be a good minister of Jesus Christ*[^10].

Felix said: Mani did not depart from the faith, as Paul says, in the way that others departed from the faith into a sect of their own; but Mani departed from no sect, such that it could be said that he departed from the faith.

Augustine said: Since I see that you are unwilling to answer what you are asked, lest you be found either not to hold the Holy Spirit, whom we have already proved was sent to the apostles, or not to be among the number of those whom Paul designated as to come, where he also prophesied of you yourselves—I answer briefly: To take what you said according to your own understanding, They will depart from the faith—since none depart from the faith except those who were in some faith, while Mani was in no faith from which he might depart, but remained in the one in which he was—this I ask you: whether Mani, or rather the teaching of the lying demons that was in Mani, did not seduce any Catholic Christians, so that they departed from the faith. For if many are seduced by you, and through you, and through that teaching, so that they depart from the faith and give heed to deceitful spirits, such as was in Mani, and begin to say that all intercourse is fornication—whence the Apostle says, *forbidding marriage*—and begin to say that the flesh which people eat is not God's creature but a thing made by demons, and is an uncleanness, then it is manifest that the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, who was in the apostle Paul, foretold concerning these people that they were going to depart from the faith, giving heed to deceitful spirits, such as was in Mani.

Now, then, since I have answered, it is just that you answer what I have asked—whether you would not say that all intercourse is fornication; or, if you are unwilling to answer this, answer what I first asked, namely, to prove that Mani is an apostle of Christ; or, if you are unwilling to do this either, permit me to set aside his teaching, which I promised to do when his letter, which you call The Foundation, was being read.

**9.** Felix said: I answer to what Your Holiness says, that the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, came in Paul.

Augustine said: Not in him alone.

Felix said: I am dealing with him; because if he is in him, he is also in all. And if he is in him—for Paul says in another Letter: *We know in part, and we prophesy in part; but when that which is perfect has come, the things that were said in part will be done away*[^11]. When we heard Paul say this, Mani came with his preaching, and we received him according to what Christ said: *I send you the Holy Spirit*[^12]. And Paul came and said that he too was to come, and afterward no one came; therefore we received Mani. And because Mani came, and through his preaching taught us the beginning, the middle, and the end; he taught us about the fabric of the world, why it was made, and from what it was made, and who made it; he taught us why there is day and why there is night; he taught us about the course of the sun and the moon; because we did not hear this in Paul, nor in the writings of the other apostles, we believe this, that he is the Paraclete.

And so I say again what I said above: if I hear in another writing, where the Paraclete speaks, that is, the Holy Spirit, about what I wish to ask, and you teach me, I believe and renounce.

**10.** Augustine said: You say that you do not believe the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, was in the apostle Paul, because Paul says: *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part*[^13]; and from this you suppose that the Apostle, by these words, foretold that, as it were, another was to come after him, who would preach all the things that he himself could not, because he spoke in part; and this one you believe to be Mani.

First, then, from the very reading of the Apostle, I show why the Apostle said this. Next, since you said that Mani taught you the beginning, the middle, and the end, and how or why the world was made, and about the course of the sun and the moon, and the other things you have mentioned: it is not read in the Gospel that the Lord said, I send you the Paraclete to teach you about the course of the sun and the moon. For he wished to make Christians, not astronomers. It is enough that people know about these things, as far as they have learned in school, for human purposes. But Christ said that the Paraclete was to come who would lead into all truth; yet he does not there say, the beginning, the middle, and the end; he does not say, the course of the sun and the moon. Or if you suppose this teaching belongs to that truth which Christ promised through the Holy Spirit, I ask you how many stars there are. If you have received that Spirit of which you speak, to whom it belongs to teach these things, which I say do not pertain to Christian discipline and teaching, you ought to renounce and answer me.

For you have made yourself a debtor, so that if I ask anything about such matters and you do not answer, that Spirit does not appear in you of whom it was said, *He will lead into all truth*[^14]—if that is the truth to which these things pertain. So choose for yourself whether you wish and are prepared to answer me about these matters, as a man who has received the Spirit who leads into all truth, and you say that it pertains to that very truth to know these worldly things. So I can tell you the things that pertain to Christian teaching; but you, who suppose that it pertains to it how the world was made, and what is done in the world, must answer me about everything. But clearly, when you have answered, you are going to prove it. But before you begin to speak, in case you happen to have something that the man you followed has fabricated, let me first teach what I promised, namely, why the Apostle said, *We know in part, and we prophesy in part.*

**11.** For he was saying, as the reading itself will soon show, that while a person is in this life, he cannot attain all things, but attains in part in this life; while the Holy Spirit himself, who teaches in part in this life, will, after this life, lead into all truth. And that this may be taught most plainly, hear the Apostle.

And as he spoke, he read from the Apostle: *But whether there are prophecies, they will be done away; or tongues, they will cease; or knowledge, it will be done away; for we know in part, and we prophesy in part; but when that which is perfect has come, that which is in part will be done away. When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child; but when I became a man, I did away with the things of a child. For now we see through a mirror in an enigma, but then face to face*[^15].

And when he had read, he said: Now tell me this: if the Apostle was foretelling the future times of Mani, do you now see God face to face?

**12.** Felix said: I cannot do much against your power, for the episcopal rank is a wondrous power; and besides, against the laws of the emperors; and earlier I asked, by way of summary, that you teach me what truth is; and if you teach me what truth is, it will be evident that what I hold is falsehood.

Augustine said: It has become apparent that you could not prove Mani to be an apostle of Christ; and why you could not, I briefly explain. You said that you proved Mani to be an apostle of Christ on this ground: that Christ promised that the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, would come; and that, since you could not find when he came whom Christ promised, you therefore believed that this one was Mani. But when I proved through the holy, ecclesiastical, canonical Scriptures, most plainly, when the Holy Spirit came whom the Lord Christ promised would come, you turned and said that it ought to be shown you what he taught, and whether he set aside the teaching of Mani. So when I had answered that Mani came after the apostles in the flesh, and that their teaching had been preached by them before Mani was born, I said nevertheless that one of those apostles, Paul, through the Holy Spirit who had come upon all the apostles, prophesied also concerning this teaching of yours, that it was to come, and called it the teaching of lying demons; and there I showed that the things Paul said would be in that teaching pertain to your profession, namely, the forbidding of marriage—because you say that all intercourse, even with a wife, is fornication—and abstinence from foods that God created—because I know that you call certain foods unclean, although he goes on and says, *Every creature of God is good*[^16].

When I demanded here that you answer, you said that Mani teaches you the beginning, the middle, and the end, and the course of the sun and the moon, and other such things, which, when I showed that they do not pertain to Christian teaching, you answered that the apostle Paul himself said that he knows in part and prophesies in part. I said that we cannot know divine things while we are in this life, and here see through a mirror and in an enigma, but then face to face; and I asked you, if you suppose the apostle Paul said this—that Mani was to come who would teach what Paul could not, and that it pertains to you, who say you have received that same Spirit, to see God now face to face. Since you cannot do this, it is manifest that the apostle Paul spoke of that life of which John too says: *Beloved, we are children of God, and it does not yet appear what we shall be; but we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^17].

When you had heard this, you said that you were not strong against my power; which power is not mine, but if there is any, it was given me by him for refuting errors—the power that belongs to all his faithful who presume concerning him with a full heart. You said also that episcopal authority terrifies you; though you see with what peace we conduct ourselves between us, with what tranquillity we debate, how the people present do you no violence, strike no terror into you, but listen most calmly, as befits Christians.

You said also that you fear the laws of the emperors; one whom the Holy Spirit filled would not fear this for the sake of the true faith. For the apostle Peter too feared during the Lord's passion, and denied him three times; but when the Paraclete, whom Christ had promised, the Holy Spirit, filled him, he was crucified for the faith of the Lord; and he who at first had the denial of fear afterward fulfilled the most glorious passion of confession. You, therefore, from this very thing—that you said you fear the laws of the emperors—although it is sufficiently apparent from other matters as well, show that you have not found the Paraclete, the Spirit of truth; for even if you did not fear, you would be convicted by other things.

Felix said: The apostles too were afraid.

Augustine said: They were afraid so as to take precautions, not so as to hesitate, when arrested, to declare their faith. But you ought to have been afraid earlier, so that we would not find you here; now you are in the midst of us; why are you afraid, unless because you have nothing to say? For if you had feared the emperors, you would have kept silent beforehand.

But now, since yesterday you gave a petition to the Curator and publicly cried out that you were prepared to be burned along with your books if anything evil were found in them: whence is it that yesterday you demanded the laws as though brave, and today you flee the truth like a coward?

**13.** Felix said: I do not flee the truth.

Augustine said: Say, then, whether you see God face to face, according to your promise; for you said that the apostle Paul said of you that you were to receive truth so full—so that we may understand, or believe, that the Apostle had it in part, and you in whole.

Felix said: I do not flee the truth, but I seek the truth. What you say, that I do not hold it, let it be proved to me through the divine Scriptures; this is what I now ask.

Augustine said: First confess that you could not prove Mani to be an apostle of Jesus Christ; and, as far as I am able, when all the contrary things have been rooted out of your heart through my ministry, by the Lord's gift, I will instill in you what the knowledge of the truth is, which leads to God, beginning from faith.

Felix said: Since you say this, that I should deny my law and accept another—a better one, at least, which is what I seek—I do not answer in such a way as to deny my law before I accept another.

Augustine said: But first what a vessel holds that is harmful is poured out, and so what is good is poured in; or, if you still hesitate to pour it out, defend that with which you are full. For I will show you, as far as the Lord helps me, how full of uncleanness and blasphemies the teaching of Mani is, if you permit that letter to be read, whose beginning we have already discussed, and you could not prove Mani to be an apostle of Christ; but for this reason you contrive delays, so that the rest may not be read, where your sacrileges are plainly detected; or, if you permit it, let them be read.

Felix said: I permit it; since you said that all unclean things are poured out, and so good things are poured in—for this was said by Your Holiness. To which I too say: No one pours out water unless another has filled it.

Augustine said: See how inconsiderately you have spoken—to put it mildly, and not to call it insane. I certainly set before you the likeness of a vessel; no one can put anything into a full vessel unless that with which it was full is poured out.

Felix said: You spoke of one, and I spoke of two.

Augustine said: If you spoke of two vessels, do you wish us to pour out that one, your equal, and fill it, so that you can pour out what you have?

Felix said: We both have one water.

Augustine said: Since, then, you are both full of your own water, what shall we pour in, to teach you, unless one of you pours out what he has? Or, if it is good, and therefore that letter ought to be held so that it may be defended and read, let us see whether even the rest can be defended, if you have failed at its very beginning; or, if you say that you have not failed in that, prove to us how Mani is an apostle of Christ.

**14.** Felix said: Christ said that he would send the Holy Spirit, who would lead into all truth.

Augustine said: If you have him, attend to what I asked you a while ago. For you said that it pertains to his teaching to know also these worldly things; tell me how many stars there are, if you have been led into all that truth.

Felix said: And I say that, if the Paraclete spoke through the apostles, and through Paul, then I ask Your Holiness to show me those things that I have already mentioned.

Augustine said: Confess that you could not show what I asked, and I will show, according to the Scriptures, what pertains to the Christian faith.

Felix said: And I, if you bring me the writings of Mani, the five authors I told you of, will prove to you whatever you ask me.

Augustine said: This letter is from those very five authors—we have opened its beginning, and we found written there, *Mani, an apostle of Jesus Christ*; and I see that you do not expound that very beginning to me, because you do not prove how Mani is an apostle of Jesus Christ.

Felix said: If I do not prove it in this one, I prove it in the second.

Augustine said: In what second?

Felix said: In the Treasury.

Augustine said: This Treasury, which you call by such a name precisely in order to seduce the wretched—who wrote it? Mani? I do not want you to prove it to me from him; for that Mani lies, by saying that he is what he is not.

Felix said: You prove it to me through another.

Augustine said: What do you want me to prove to you?

Felix said: That Mani lies.

Augustine said: Because you cannot prove that Mani speaks the truth, must I prove that Mani lied?

Felix said: Why could I not prove it? Have the writings that I asked for been brought forward, and I have not proved it?

Augustine said: But you ask for the writings of Mani, in which we place no trust; prove it from elsewhere. I, however, prove to you from the very writing of Mani that Mani lied and that Mani blasphemed.

Felix said: Let the books be brought.

Augustine said: The letter of Mani that you call The Foundation is present. There is no beginning in a building except the foundation; if I show that he has caused a collapse in the foundation itself, why do we look for the rest of his construction?

**15.** Felix said: You say this, showing that there is a collapse in it; and I say, give as many arbiters as you too have, and I will prove to you that he does not lie.

Augustine said: Far be it from us and from the human race that you should see here as many Manicheans as you see people.

Felix said: Give, I said, what I asked of you.

Augustine said: Whom, then, do you want me to give you?

Felix said: Whomever you wish.

Augustine said: I give these; if you have better ones, ask for them.

Felix said: How do you give these?

Augustine said: Those present who are listening to us.

Felix said: These do not favor me.

Augustine said: So you are looking for people who favor you, not who favor the truth?

Felix said: People who will hear me, and not only me, but the writing itself, so that it may be proved whether it speaks the truth or lies.

Augustine said: You see, then, that they are listening. Let us read the rest; for you have confessed that this is the letter of Mani.

Felix said: I do not deny it.

Augustine said: Then let it be read.

**16.** Felix said: I have no arbiters. And he added: Let its opening be read.

And when it was being read, and they had come to the place where the same letter has it written: *May the peace of the invisible God and the knowledge of the truth be with his brothers and dearest ones, who believe in the heavenly commandments and likewise serve them; and may the right hand of light protect you and snatch you from every malign assault and from the snare of the world; and may the devotion of the Holy Spirit open the inmost parts of your breast, so that with your own eyes you may see your souls*;

Felix said: Give the writing that refutes these things.

Augustine said: So far we have heard nothing evil, except that Mani dared to name himself an apostle of Christ. For the things he said still belong to the plaster of deceit and to the sheep's skin, by which they first speak good words so that they can introduce evil ones. But let us see what he means to introduce through these words. For if he introduces something evil, then these words too will be evil and seductive; but if he says something good and true in what follows, we will, of necessity, acknowledge it. Permit, then, the following to be read.

Felix said: If, then, you assert that good things are put first, and afterward evil things are slipped in, how can I believe you, when you have first told me good things?

Augustine said: So far I have called these things neither good nor evil. I said, We have heard nothing evil so far; I did not say, We have already heard something good. For the only evil I named is that he dared to call himself an apostle of Christ. But the words he said in what follows will then be evil, if they introduce something evil; they will then be good, if they introduce something good. Permit, then, the following to be read: why are you afraid?

Felix said: I am not afraid.

Augustine said: Permit them, then, to be read.

Felix said: Let them be read.

**17.** And when it was being read, and they had come to the place where it has it written: *And his most splendid kingdoms were so founded upon the bright and blessed land that they can never be moved or shaken by anything*;

Augustine said: This land that he mentions—where did he get it? Did he make it, or beget it, or was it equal and coeternal with him? I mean this bright and blessed land that he speaks of.

Felix said: How is it written: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth, and the earth was invisible and able to be defiled and unformed*[^18]? I understand it thus: In the beginning God made heaven and earth, and the earth was; there seem to me to be, as it were, two earths, according to what Mani says about two kingdoms.

Augustine said: Since you have mentioned our Scripture, which you are accustomed to blaspheme, it falls to me to expound it and to show you that what is written is without blasphemy, and is in accordance with the truth and not according to Mani; so that then you may, at last, answer me what I asked.

Felix said: I answer.

Augustine said: As to what is written, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*, Scripture has briefly comprehended what God made. Then, because this earth that he had made did not yet appear—before he distinguished and adorned it—what sort of earth God had made, the Scripture that follows expounded what sort of earth it was of which it had said, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*; as if, when we had heard, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*, and were asking what sort of earth, it added and said: *But the earth*, that is, the one that God had made, *was invisible and unformed*. He did not, then, speak of two earths, but expounded what sort that one was. So now answer me what I briefly asked: this bright and blessed land that Mani speaks of, upon which the kingdoms of God were founded—was it made by God himself, or was it begotten of him, or was it coeternal with him? Answer one of the three, if it is not troublesome, without evasion.

Felix said: Scripture interprets itself.

Augustine said: If, then, you know that there is some passage here where Scripture says this—that God either begot that land, or made it, or that it was coeternal with him—open to the passage you know, and read it to me.

Felix said: It is not in this writing, but in another.

Augustine said: I believe, then, that if there is some such writing, you remember what it said there. So you answer me, you who know that writing; and if I say that it does not have it so, you will convict me. But if I find it to be so, I argue according to what you yourself have answered. Say, then, whether God made this land, or begot it, or it was coeternal with him—you who have read in some book or other, where you say this is written.

Felix said: Since Your Holiness has just now interpreted your Scripture as you wished, and I have accepted it, you too accept from me whatever I say.

Augustine said: For the time being, as to what I asked, if nothing moves me against it, I accept it; but if something moves me, I make my objection known to you, so that you may answer it.

Felix said: I have not answered your statements.

Augustine said: Since it is first that you answer me what I asked, I have nevertheless arranged even to answer you first; perhaps it did not move you, and so you did not answer; perhaps it will not move me either, when you have answered. Answer, then, what I asked.

**18.** Felix said: I answer. And he added: You asked, then, about that land in which God dwells, whether it was made by him, or he begot it, or it is coeternal with him. And I say that, just as God is eternal, and there is with him nothing made, all is eternal.

Augustine said: He neither begot it, then, nor made it?

Felix said: No, but it is coeternal with him.

Augustine said: But if he had begotten it, it would not be coeternal with him.

Felix said: What is born has an end; what is unborn has no end.

Augustine said: Of whom, then, was he a father, or whose father was he, whom he just now named father? For if he had begotten nothing, he could not be a father.

Felix said: But there are other things that he begot.

Augustine said: Are those things that he begot, then, not coeternal with him, or are they too coeternal?

Felix said: Whatever God begot is coeternal with him.

Augustine said: Then you were in error in what you said above: *Everything that is born has an end.*

Felix said: For the time being, since I spoke according to the generation of the flesh, I was in error.

Augustine said: Having most modestly confessed your error, may you deserve to understand the truth.

Felix said: May God grant it.

Augustine said: Attend, then, now, so that you may begin to recognize the error of this writing. If the things that God begot are not coeternal with him, then that land which God did not beget, where all the things that God begot dwell, is better than the land you say was not begotten by him.

Felix said: All things are made equal to one another, both those he begot and those he did not beget, that is, that land where he dwelt.

Augustine said: What of the one who begot—is he equal to all those things, or is he greater than they?

Felix said: Both the one who begot, and those he begot, and where they are placed—all are equal.

Augustine said: They are, then, of one substance?

Felix said: Of one.

Augustine said: What God the Father is, this his sons are, and this that land is too?

Felix said: All are this one thing.

Augustine said: Of this land, then, he is not the father, but the inhabitant?

Felix said: Yes.

Augustine said: If he neither begot it nor made it, I do not see how it pertains to him except by mere proximity, as when someone has a good neighbor; and there will now be two things, both unbegotten, the land and the father.

Felix said: Rather, there are three: the unbegotten father, the unbegotten land, and the unbegotten air.

Augustine said: Is all this one substance?

Felix said: One.

Augustine said: And so founded that it can never be moved or shaken by anything?

Felix said: Being moved and being shaken differ.

Augustine said: Let them differ; but still, were they such that they could never be moved or shaken by anything?

Felix said: There is a difference between being moved and being shaken.

Augustine said: That is not what I am asking you about.

Felix said: But that is where you want to trap me.

Augustine said: Take it however you wish: being moved—could it be moved?

Felix said: I do not say that it could not be moved, but being moved is a difference.

Augustine said: But I said it thus: neither moved nor shaken. I did not say, It can indeed be moved, but it cannot be shaken; or, It can indeed be shaken, but it cannot be moved; rather, I said both things, that it can neither be moved nor shaken.

Felix said: As to both things, there is a difference between being moved and being shaken.

**19.** Augustine said: Let us read, then, what follows, and let us see whether this God, who had his kingdoms founded upon the bright and blessed land, which can never be moved or shaken by anything, feared no one—as it was fitting that he should fear no one, whose kingdoms were so founded that they could be neither moved nor shaken by anything.

And he read what follows: *But beside one part and side of that illustrious and holy land.*

And as he read, he asked: Which side? The right, or the left?

Felix said: I cannot interpret this writing for you and expound what is not there; it is its own interpreter; I cannot speak, lest I perhaps fall into sin.

Augustine said: Let what follows, then, be read.

And as he read, and they had come to the place where it has: *But the Father of the most blessed light, knowing that a great ruin and devastation, which would rise up from the darkness, was threatening to fall upon his holy ages, unless he should set against it some surpassing and illustrious power, mighty in strength, by which he might at once overcome and destroy the race of darkness, which being extinguished, perpetual rest would be prepared for the inhabitants of the light.*

Augustine said: Now, since open blasphemies have begun, if you think them defensible, say what harm this race of darkness, which God seems to have feared—lest a great ruin and devastation from the darkness should rush into his kingdoms—could do to God; especially as you said above that his kingdoms were so founded that they could never be moved or shaken by anything. What, then, was this race going to do to him? Could it harm him, or could it not? Answer one of the two.

Felix said: I answer: If there is nothing against God, according to what the writing of Mani said—that there is another kingdom—for what reason was Christ sent, to free us from the snare of this death? Whose is this snare and death? If there is no adversary against God, why have we been baptized? Why the Eucharist, why Christianity, if there is nothing against God?

Augustine said: Since I see that you are unwilling to answer what I ask you, and that you ask me to answer you—and I do not refuse this, provided you remember that I answer you when you ask, whereas you were unwilling to answer me when I asked—but since you have asked me, hear the answer to what you asked. We, indeed, say that Christ the liberator has come, and we say that we are freed from our sins, because we are not begotten of the substance of God but made by God through his Word. Now there is a great difference between what is born of the substance of God and what is made by God and is not of the substance of God. Whatever, then, God made can be mutable; but God himself is not mutable, for works cannot be made equal to their maker and founder. But you, who a little while ago said, in answer to my question, that *both the Father who begot the sons of light there, and the air, and the land itself, and the sons themselves are of one substance and are all equal*, must tell me how the race of darkness could harm this substance, which is surely incorruptible. For if it could harm it, it was not an incorruptible nature; but if it could not, there was no reason why he should wage battle and send hither that power he speaks of.

Felix said: I ask for a delay, so that I can answer.

Augustine said: When? Is a recess until tomorrow enough?

**20.** Felix said: Give me a recess of three days, that is, today, and tomorrow, and the day after, or until the day that is after the Lord's day, that is, the day before the ides of December.

Augustine said: I see that you have asked for a delay in order to answer; it is human that it be granted you; but if you cannot answer by the appointed day, what will happen?

Felix said: I will be defeated.

Augustine said: What if you flee?

Felix said: I will be a guilty defendant before this city, and everywhere, and before my own law.

Augustine said: Rather, say this: If I flee, let me be regarded as though I had anathematized Mani.

Felix said: I cannot say this.

Augustine said: Then tell us openly that you are thinking of fleeing, and no one holds you.

Felix said: I am not fleeing.

Augustine said: As I see it, you do not wish to leave as though defeated; but at least say this: If I flee, I am defeated.

Felix said: I have said it.

Augustine said: And how will it be evident that you have fled, on the basis of the proceedings?

Felix said: Order that I be with whomever I choose.

Augustine said: Choose for yourself one of these brothers present, who stand at the rail.

Felix said: Let me be with the one who is in the middle.

Augustine said: As you have chosen for yourself, you will be with him until the day.

Felix said: Yes, and I consent to this.

Boniface said: Christ will grant that, if I come with him, he is a Christian.

I, Augustine, bishop of the Catholic Church of Hippo Regius, have subscribed to these proceedings in church before the people.

I, Felix, a Christian, an adherent of the law of Mani, have subscribed to these proceedings in church before the people.
