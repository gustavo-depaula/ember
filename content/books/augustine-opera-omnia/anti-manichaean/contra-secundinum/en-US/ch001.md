# Revisions II

## Chapter X (XXXVII): Answer to Secundinus, a Manichean, one book

A certain Secundinus, not one of those whom the Manicheans call elect but one of those they call "hearers"[^1], a man whom I did not even know by sight, wrote to me as a friend would, reproaching me in honorable terms because I was attacking that heresy in my writings, and admonishing me not to do so but rather exhorting me to embrace it, defending it and finding fault with the catholic faith.

I replied to him; but because I did not place at the head of that little work who was writing to whom, it is found not among my letters but among my books. There his letter too is set down from the beginning. The title of this volume of mine is *Answer to Secundinus, a Manichean,* which in my judgment I readily prefer to everything that I have been able to write against that pestilence.

This book begins thus: *The good will toward me that appears in your letter*.

[^1]: De haeres. 46; Confess. 3.10.18
