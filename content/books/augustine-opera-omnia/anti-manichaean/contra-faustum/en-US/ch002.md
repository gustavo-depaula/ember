# Book 1

**1.** There was a certain Faustus, an African by birth, a citizen of Milevis, pleasant in speech, clever in mind, a Manichean by sect, and on that account perverted by an unspeakable error. I knew the man myself, as I have recalled in the books of my Confessions. He published a certain volume against the right Christian faith and the Catholic truth. When it came into our hands and had been read by the brethren, they desired, and by the right of love through which we serve them they demanded, that we reply to it. This I shall now undertake, in the name and with the help of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, so that all who read these things may understand how worthless a sharp mind and a polished tongue are unless the steps of a man be directed by the Lord[^1]. This has been granted, by the hidden justice of divine mercy, even to many who are slower and weaker, while many who were most keen and most eloquent, abandoned by the help of God, ran swiftly and stubbornly to this end—that they might stray farther from the way of truth.

I think it fitting, moreover, to set his words under his name, and my reply under mine.

**2.** Faustus said: Now that errors have been more than sufficiently dragged into the light, and the deceit both of Jewish superstition and of the half-Christians has been amply exposed—by that most learned man, the only one besides our blessed father Mani worthy of our study, Adimantus—it has seemed not amiss, dearest brethren, to write for you these brief and well-ordered replies as well, on account of the crafty and cunning propositions of those who debate with us, so that when these same men, after the manner of their parent the serpent, try to entrap you with captious little questions, you too may be vigilantly equipped to answer them. For thus it will come about that, bound to the very things they have proposed, they will not be able to wander any further this way and that. And lest the minds of readers be flooded with diffuse or confused discourse, I have set their words and ours opposite each other, as briefly as distinctly.

**3.** Augustine replied: You think half-Christians are to be guarded against, which is what you say we are; but we guard against pseudo-Christians, which is what we show you to be. For what is half is in some part incomplete, yet in no part false. What then? If something is lacking to the faith of those whom you try to entrap, is what they have on that account to be destroyed, rather than what they lack to be built up? So, speaking to certain incomplete people, the Apostle says: *rejoicing and seeing your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ*[^2]. He certainly discerned a kind of spiritual structure, as he says elsewhere: *you are God's building*[^3]; and in it he discerned both what made him rejoice and what made him concerned. He rejoiced over what he already saw built up; he was concerned over what he perceived still had to be built, up to the summit of perfection. And so it is us—truly Catholics, not yet perfect, but in a way, as you said, half-Christians—whom you assail to deceive and seduce by your perversity.

But if there are still any such people, once they understand that you are pseudo-Christians, then even though, because of what is lacking to their faith, they cannot answer the captious propositions of your questions, they nonetheless recognize that you are to be avoided, not followed. Therefore, just as your aim is to seek out half-Christians whom you may deceive, so our aim is to show you to be pseudo-Christians—so that not only do the more skilled Christians expose you by refuting you, but the less skilled also profit by guarding against you.

But why did you call the serpent our parent? Or has it slipped your mind how you are accustomed to blame the God who gave the commandment to man in paradise[^4], and to praise the serpent because by its counsel it opened his eyes[^5]? I think it just that you should rather acknowledge as your own parent that serpent, the devil, whom you praise. For he, even now blamed by you, acknowledges you as his son.

[^1]: See Ps 37:23 (36:23).
[^2]: Col 2:5.
[^3]: 1 Cor 3:9.
[^4]: See Gen 2:16-17.
[^5]: See Gen 3:7.
