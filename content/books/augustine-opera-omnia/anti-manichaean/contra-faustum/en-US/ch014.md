# Book 13

**1.** Faustus said: How do you worship Christ, while repudiating the Prophets, from whose predictions it is accepted that he was to come?

Whether anyone could prove, when the matter began to be examined, that any of the Hebrew Prophets announced our Christ, that is, the Son of God, I do not know. But even if this is so, what is it to us? This reproach awaits those who, having perhaps been converted from Judaism to Christianity under the testimonies, as you maintain, of the Prophets, afterward neglected them, as ungrateful for benefits. But we, on the other hand, are Gentiles by nature, that is, what Paul calls the foreskin[^1], born under another law, and with other heralds whom the Gentile world calls seers, and from these we were afterward converted to Christianity: not having first been made Jews, so that we should rightly follow the faith of the Hebrew Prophets in going to Christianity, but roused only by the report, and the opinion of the virtues and the wisdom of our Liberator Christ Jesus. Hence, if to me, still abiding in my ancestral religion, a preacher had come and wished to introduce Christ to me from the Prophets, I would at once have thought him demented, who tried to build up for me, a Gentile and a man of a far different religion, things more doubtful out of things more doubtful.

What need was there, then, except that he should first persuade me that the Prophets are to be believed, and then, through the Prophets, Christ? But for this very thing to be done, there was need likewise of other Prophets, who should win faith for these. Therefore, if you think Christ is to be accepted through the Prophets, through whom will you accept the Prophets? Or will you say: Through Christ, so that in turn, that is, so that each commends the other, Christ the Prophets, and the Prophets Christ? But a pagan, free of the condition of both of them, would believe neither the Prophets speaking about Christ, nor Christ about the Prophets. So whoever becomes a Christian from among the Gentiles owes the whole to nothing other than his own faith.

And that what we say may be made plainer by an example, let us suppose someone, a Gentile, now being catechized by us, to whom, sitting beside him, we say: Believe in Christ, because he is God. But he says: Whence do you prove this to me? And we, answering, say: From the Prophets. Again, when he asks: What Prophets? we answer: The Hebrews. And he, smiling, says: But these I least of all believe. But we answer: What, that Christ confirms them? But he, laughing much more, says: What, that I do not believe him either? What will happen, when these things have so passed? Shall we not be stuck, and shall he not, having laughed at us as imprudent, return to his own?

So, as I said, the testimonies of the Hebrews confer nothing on the Christian Church, which consists more of Gentiles than of Jews. To be sure, if there are any predictions about Christ, as is reported, of the Sibyl, or of Hermes, whom they call Trismegistus, or of Orpheus, and of other seers in the Gentile world, these can help us somewhat toward faith, who are made Christians from among the Gentiles: but the testimonies of the Hebrews, even if they are true, are to us useless before faith, superfluous after faith; because before, indeed, we could not believe them, but now we believe them as a thing over and above.

**2.** Augustine replied: May the earlier, so lengthy reply profit us here for brevity of answering. For I think that whoever has read it now laughs at this man raving such things, and still saying that the Hebrew Prophets did not foretell Christ the Son of God: in which nation alone the very name that is called Christ was most sacred, both in the king and in the priest[^2]; nor was it taken away from there before he himself had come who was figured in them[^3].

But let them themselves answer whence they learned the name of Christ. If from Mani, I ask Mani himself how they believed—to pass over the others—men of Africa believing a man of Persia: when Faustus reproaches the Romans, and the Greeks, or other nations, if they have believed about Christ the Hebrew Prophets as though foreigners; and says that the prophecies of the Sibyl and of Orpheus, or any others perhaps of the Gentile seers, are more suited to them, that one may believe in Christ: and does not notice that those are recited in no Churches, while the Hebrew Prophets shine forth among all the nations, and lead so great swarms of the faithful to Christian salvation. But to say that Hebrew prophecy is not suited to the Gentiles, that they may believe in Christ, when he sees all the nations believing in Christ through Hebrew prophecy, is ridiculous insanity.

**3.** Such a Christ displeases you as has been foretold through the Hebrews: and yet all the peoples of the Gentiles, among whom you reckon Hebrew prophecy to have no weight of authority, believe in such a Christ as has been foretold through the Hebrews; accepting, namely, the Gospel which God had promised before, as the Apostle recalls, through his Prophets in the holy Scriptures, concerning his Son, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh[^4]. Hence the prophet Isaiah said: *There will be a root of Jesse, who will rise to reign over the nations: in him the nations will hope*[^5]; and: *Behold, a Virgin will conceive and bear a son, and they will call his name Emmanuel*[^6], which is interpreted, *God with us*[^7]:

nor let these men think that only a man, Christ, was foretold by the Hebrew Prophets; for Faustus seems to have touched on this, when he said "our Christ indeed, the Son of God"; as though the Hebrews did not call their Christ the Son of God. Behold, we have shown the God Christ, the Virgin's Son, from Hebrew prophecy. For surely, lest even the carnal Jews should think Christ to be only this, that he was made man for our sake of the seed of David, the Lord himself admonishes them from the prophecy of the same David, asking of them what seemed to them about Christ, whose Son they said he was. And when they had answered, David's; lest, as I said, this only be thought, and they not look to Emmanuel, which is interpreted, God with us, he said: *How, then, does David himself in the spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies the footstool of your feet*[^8]? Behold, I say, we have shown from Hebrew prophecy the God Christ: do you show some prophecy of yours, whence you learned the name of Christ.

**4.** For your Mani was not a prophet of Christ to come: for indeed he calls himself his apostle, with most impudent deceit; for it is established that this heresy arose not only after Tertullian, but even after Cyprian. Yet all his letters begin thus: "Mani, an apostle of Jesus Christ." Why did you believe him about Christ? What witness of his apostolate did he bring you? And the very name of Christ, which we know to have been established only in the kingdom of the Jews, in priests and kings, so that not only this or that man, but the whole nation itself and the whole kingdom should be made a prophet of Christ and of the Christian kingdom—why did this man invade it, why did he usurp it, who forbids you to believe the Hebrew Prophets, so that a false and deceitful apostle may make you the deceitful disciples of a false Christ?

Finally, lest it be said to him: You lie, he may bring forth to you some prophets too, foretelling Christ according to his assertion: what will you do with him, such a one as Faustus set forth as an example to be catechized, who would be willing to believe neither them nor him? Or will he call our Apostles as witnesses for himself? Not, I think, will he produce men, but he will open Books: which he will find opened not for him, but against him. For there we read and teach that Christ was born of the Virgin Mary, that the Son of God was made of the seed of David according to the flesh[^9].

But if he says they have been falsified, he himself will assail the trustworthiness of his own witnesses: but if he brings forth other manuscripts, which he calls those of our Apostles, how will he himself give them an authority which he did not receive through the Churches of Christ established by the Apostles themselves, so that from there it might run on to posterity with a confirmed commendation? How does he, whom I do not believe, bring forth to me Scriptures by which I should believe about him; and himself try to give them authority, when I do not believe him?

**5.** But if you believed about Christ on report; for Faustus too, constrained in great straits, touched on this in passing, lest, namely, he be either compelled to bring forth books whose authority is nothing, or be bound to those whose authority is contrary to him: if, then, you believed about Christ on report, see whether report is a suitable witness, consider diligently into what you hurl yourselves. For report spreads abroad many bad things about you, which you do not want to be believed. What reasoning, then, is it, to want this report about Christ to be true, which you want to be false about you? What of the fact that you even contradict the report about Christ?

For that report is clearer, that is more prevailing, which holds the ears and minds and tongues of all the nations, which, with Christ of the seed of David spread abroad according to the Hebrew Scriptures, fulfills what is written there, promised to Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^10].

What, then, do you answer, on whose word you believed about Christ, you to whom foreign witnesses are not pleasing? Further, the authority of our Books, strengthened by the consent of so many nations, through the successions of Apostles, bishops, and councils, is against you: but of yours there is none, because it is brought forth both by so few, and by those who worship a lying God and Christ. Hence it comes about, against their lying teaching, that they themselves too are held liars, as imitators of their God and Christ.

But report itself too, when consulted, both proclaims you to be most wicked, and does not cease to preach Christ of the seed of David against you. You did not hear the voice of the Father from heaven[^11], you did not see the works of Christ by which he bore witness about himself: the manuscripts in which these things are written you accept, as it were, so that you may deceive under a Christian appearance; but lest they be read against you, you say they have been falsified.

You bring forth from there Christ saying: *If you do not believe me, believe the works*[^12]; and: *I am one who bears witness about myself, and the Father who sent me bears witness about me*[^13]: and you do not want it brought forth from there against you: *Search the Scriptures, in which you think you have eternal life; they themselves bear witness about me*; and: *If you believed Moses, you would believe me also; for he wrote about me*[^14]; and: *They have there Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them*[^15]; and: *If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they believe him if someone rises from the dead*[^16]!

Whence have you come out here? Whence is your confidence? You reject the Scriptures confirmed and commended by so great authority, you do not work miracles: which if you did, we would beware of even those in you, the Lord forewarning us and saying: *Many false christs and false prophets will arise, and will do many signs and wonders, to deceive, if it can be done, even the elect: behold, I have foretold you*[^17]. So far did he want nothing to be believed against the confirmed authority of the Scriptures, which proves its trustworthiness by the facts themselves, which shows these things being fulfilled and brought about through the successions of times, which foretold them so long before they came to pass.

**6.** It remains that you say you offer a reasoning so sure and unconquerable that, with the truth declared through itself, it requires no authority of a witness, no truth of a miracle. What do you say? What do you bring forth? What reasoning, what truth? That fable is long and vain, a childish mockery, and a womanish jest, and an old wives' raving, containing a mutilated beginning, and a rotten middle, and a ruinous end.

For when it has been said to you, from its beginning: What was the race of darkness going to do to the immortal, invisible, incorruptible God, if he had been unwilling to fight with it? and from its middle: How is God incorruptible and incapable of contamination, whose members you crush by eating and digesting them in fruits and vegetables, so that you may purify them? and from its end: What did the wretched soul do, that it should be punished with a perpetual bond in the clod of darkness—the soul which, stained not by its own fault but by another's, could not be cleansed, with its God failing it, by whose sending it was polluted? while you hesitate, and find nothing to answer, your manuscripts, so many and so large and so costly, are looked upon; and much grief is felt for the labors of the copyists, and the purses of the wretched, and the bread of the deceived.

If, then, neither the antiquity of the authority of the Scriptures, nor the power of miracles, nor the soundness of morals, nor the truth of reasoning vouches for you; go away confounded, and return having confessed that he himself is the Christ, the Savior of all who believe in him: whose name and whose Church the present times so display as the past times announced; not through some man or other proceeding from cavernous hiding places, but through a certain nation, and a certain kingdom, propagated and established for this, that there all things about him might be marked beforehand in figures, which would now be recognized expressed in facts, and that there, through the Prophets, things might be written as foretold, which now, preached through the Apostles, might be presented?

**7.** Accordingly, set yourselves some Gentile to be catechized, in whom Faustus laughed that we failed: where it was not he who is to be laughed at, but he himself who is to be mourned that failed. For if we say to a Gentile man, Believe in Christ, because he is God; and he answers: Whence do I believe? and, the authority of the Prophets having been brought forth, he says he does not believe them, because they are Hebrews and he a pagan; we show the trustworthiness of the Prophets from the things which they sang as to come and are perceived to have come.

For I believe that it would not escape him how great persecutions the Christian religion first endured from the kings of this world: or if it did escape him, it would easily be proved to him through the very history of the nations and the imperial laws committed to writing and memory: which, when he learned it foretold so long before by the prophet who says: *Why have the nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things? The kings of the earth stood up, and the princes came together as one, against the Lord and against his Christ*—that this was not said about David himself easily appears in that same psalm. For there is said also that which confounds men, however most stubborn, by the very manifestation of things: *The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you; ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth*[^18]: which was not granted to the nation of the Jews in which David reigned; but that it has been fulfilled, with the name of Christ occupying all the nations far and wide, no one doubts.

I believe he would be moved, when he heard from there many other things too from the prophets, which it would now be long to pursue. He would also see the very kings of the earth now wholesomely subjected to the rule of Christ, and all the nations serving him: and there would be read to him from the Psalm, foretold so long before: *All the kings of the earth will adore him, all the nations will serve him*[^19]:

and that whole psalm itself, which is said figuratively as though of Solomon, if he were willing to read it, he would find Christ truly the peaceful king; for this is what the name Solomon is interpreted to mean: in whom he would recognize all the things said there fulfilled, far most remote from that man Solomon, king of Israel. Likewise that psalm where God is said to be anointed by God, and surely Christ is declared by that very anointing; and the same Christ is shown most plainly to be God, when the anointed God is introduced[^20]: if he were willing to consider the things said there about Christ, the things about the Church itself, the things he would read there foretold, but would behold fulfilled throughout the world;

he would see also the very idols of the nations so perishing from the world through the name of Christ, and would learn that this very thing was foretold by the Prophets: he would hear Jeremiah saying: *Thus you will say to them: Let the gods who did not make heaven and earth perish from the earth, and from under heaven*[^21]. Likewise in another place, when he heard the same prophet saying: *Lord, my strength, and my help, and my refuge in the day of evils: to you the nations will come from the end of the earth, and will say: How false are the idols our fathers possessed, and there is no profit in them! Shall a man make gods, and they themselves are not gods? Therefore, behold, I will show them at that time; I will show them my hand, and my power, and they will know that I am the Lord*[^22]. Hearing these things from prophetic Scripture, and perceiving them throughout the whole earth, what shall I say of how he would be moved to faith, when we prove this too by the facts themselves, since we recognize the hearts of the faithful so strengthened through a prophecy written before the times and fulfilled in these times?

**8.** But now, lest he should think Christ a man, such as certain great men have been, the same prophet would strike this from his thought. For there it follows and says: *Cursed is the man who has hope in man, and makes flesh his arm, and whose heart departs from the Lord: and he will be like the tamarisk that is in the desert; he will not see when good things come, and he will dwell among the wicked in a desert land, in a salt land that will not be inhabited*; and: *Blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord, and the Lord will be his hope: and he will be like a fruitful tree by the water, and will send its roots into the moisture; it will not fear when the heat comes, and there will be on it leafy shoots; in the year of drought it will not fear, and it will not cease to bear fruit*[^23].

Here surely, since he said that the man who places his hope in man is cursed, and explained that curse by prophetic likenesses; and that the man who trusts in the Lord is blessed, and likewise wove that blessing with fitting likenesses; that Gentile would perhaps be disturbed how we announce to him the God Christ, that he should not place his hope in a man, and again say that he is a man, not from his own nature, but from our mortality assumed. For thus some erred by believing Christ God and denying him man: and again some, by supposing him man and denying him God, either despised him, or, placing their hope in a man, fell into that curse.

Here, then, if this Gentile were disturbed, and said that this prophet had spoken against our faith: because we, according to apostolic teaching, would say that Christ is not only God, that hope may be most securely placed in him, but also the Mediator of God and men, the man Jesus[^24]; whereas this prophet had said only "God," but made no mention of human nature: in that very place he would hear the voice of the same prophet admonishing and correcting him: *The heart is heavy above all things, and he is a man, and who will recognize him?*[^25] For he is therefore a man, that those heavy of heart might be healed by faith through the form of a servant, and recognize him as God who was made man for their sake, that their hope might not be in a man, but in a man who is God.

And yet, *the heart is heavy above all things, and he is a man*: taking the form of a servant. *And who will recognize him?* who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God[^26]. *And he is a man*: because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us[^27]. *And who will recognize him?* because in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God[^28].

And truly the heart is heavy above all things; for even in his disciples that heaviness of heart was present, when he said to them: *So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me?* For what is, *So long a time I am with you*, except what is said here: *And he is a man*? And what is that, *And you have not known me*? except what is said here: *And who will recognize him?* Whom, except him who says: *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^29]?

That our hope be not in a man, on account of that curse uttered through the prophet: but that it be in a man who is God, that is, in the Son of God, the Savior Jesus Christ, the Mediator of God and men; both he than whom the Father is greater on account of the form of a servant, and he who is equal to the Father on account of the form of God.

**9.** Isaiah also says: *The reproach of men will be humbled and fall, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day: and they will hide all things made by hands in caves and in the clefts of the rocks and in the caverns of the earth, from the face of the fear of the Lord and from the majesty of his power, when he rises to shatter the earth. For in that day a man will cast away the gold and silver abominations which they made to adore, things superfluous and harmful*[^30]. And perhaps this Gentile whom we are catechizing, whom Faustus said would say with a laugh, "I do not believe the Hebrew Prophets," has hidden some idols made by hand in a cave, or in a cleft of rock, or in a cavern of the earth, or knows some friend of his to have done this, or knows it to have been done in his city or on his estate, from the face of the fear of the Lord, who, through the kings of the earth, according to that same prophecy, now serving and adoring him, shatters the earth by most severe laws, that is, breaks the audacity of the earthly heart. How, then, would he say, "I do not believe the Hebrew Prophets," when he perhaps recognizes fulfilled in himself what was foretold of old by the Hebrew Prophets?

**10.** It was rather to be feared that, surrounded by so great an evidence of things, he might perhaps say that, after these things began to happen throughout the world, the Christians composed these writings, so that they might be thought foretold beforehand by God, lest they be despised as though rashly done by men. This was to be feared, were there not the widely scattered and widely known people of the Jews: that Cain who received a mark, lest he be killed by anyone[^31], and that Ham, the servant of his brothers[^32], burdened by carrying the books by which those others are instructed.

For through their manuscripts we prove that these things were not written by us, as though warned of the outcome of events, but foretold of old and preserved in that kingdom, and now made manifest and fulfilled: in which even the things that are less clear there, because they happened to them in figure; but were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come[^33], are now illumined and resolved; and the things which were still shadowed by the shadows of future things are now manifested by the light of things accomplished.

**11.** Perhaps he would also say that he is moved by this, that those very people, in whose books these things are found foretold, which are now seen fulfilled, do not hold with us the fellowship of that same Gospel. But when he were taught that this too was foretold by those same Prophets, how greatly would he be moved to faith? Who is so demented as not to see it? Who is so impudent as to pretend not to see it?

For who would doubt that this was prophesied of the Jews, when Isaiah says: *The ox knows its owner, and the ass its master's manger; but Israel has not known me, and my people has not understood me*[^34]; or that which the Apostle recalls: *All day long I have stretched out my hands to a people not believing and contradicting*[^35]; and especially that: *God gave them a spirit of compunction, eyes that they should not see, and ears that they should not hear and should not understand*[^36]; and many things of this kind?

But if he said: "What, then, did the Jews sin, if God blinded them so that they should not recognize Christ?", we would show, as far as we could, in instructing the untaught man, that the just punishment of this blindness comes from other hidden sins known to God: that not only did the Apostle say of certain men: *For this God handed them over to the lust of their heart, or to a reprobate mind, to do the things that are not fitting*[^37], wishing to show that certain manifest sins come from the punishment of certain hidden ones; but that the Prophets themselves did not keep silent about this, we would demonstrate. For, not to go on at length, the same Jeremiah, in that very place where he says: *And he is a man, and who will recognize him?*[^38]—lest, as though by this very thing the Jews were excused, because they did not recognize him: *For if they had recognized him*, as the Apostle says, *they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^39]—goes on, and shows that it was from their hidden desert that they did not recognize him. For he says: *I the Lord, searching hearts, and testing reins, that I may give to each according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his pursuits*[^40].

**12.** Further, if that Gentile were moved by this—why even those who are called Christians depart into many and various heresies: neither was this passed over by the Hebrew Prophets, we would teach. For, as though it were consequent that, with the Jews shown in their blindness, this should come into his mind, that many too under the Christian name deviated from the Christian fellowship itself, Jeremiah himself, as though insinuating to us the order of catechizing, at once added: *The partridge cried, it gathered what it did not bear, making its riches not with judgment*[^41]. For the partridge is a too contentious animal; it is known with what greediness, in its very contentiousness, it runs into the snare. For heretics do not love to dispute, but in any way to overcome with most impudent obstinacy, that they may gather, as he said here, what they did not bear. For they find the Christians, whom they especially seduce by the name of Christ, already born through the Gospel of that same Christ, and make them their riches: not, indeed, with judgment, but with inconsiderate rashness. For they do not understand that there is the true and wholesome, and in a manner genuine and rooted, Christian fellowship, from which they separated those whom they gathered to their own riches.

And because of such the Apostle says: *For as Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses, so these too resist the truth; men corrupt in mind, reprobate concerning the faith: but they will go no further, for their madness will be manifest to all, as theirs also was*[^42]: this Prophet too goes on, and says of the partridge that gathered what it did not bear: *In the half of its days they will leave it, and in its latter end it will be foolish*[^43]: that is, he who at first seduced, as it were by the promise and display of surpassing wisdom, will be foolish, that is, will appear foolish. For to those to whom he was at first wise, he will then be foolish, when it will appear, because his madness will be known to all.

**13.** And as though that one whom we are catechizing should ask and say: By what manifest sign, then, while I am still a little one, and not yet able to discern clear truth from so many errors; by what manifest indication shall I hold the Church of Christ, in whom I am already compelled to believe by so great a manifestation of things foretold beforehand? The same prophet goes on, and, as though taking up the movements of his mind in the most orderly way, teaches him that the Church of Christ itself was foretold, which is eminent and apparent to all. For it is the throne of glory, of which the Apostle says: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^44]. Hence this prophet says: *But the throne of glory is exalted, our sanctification*[^45].

For on account of these movements of little ones, who can be seduced by men, the Lord too, foreseeing the manifestation of the Church's brightness, said: *A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden*[^46]: because surely the throne of glory is exalted, our sanctification, so that those may not be heard who draw men over to the rendings of religions, saying: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there*[^47]. For the parties point out, saying: Behold here, behold there. While that city is upon the mountain; which mountain, except that which, according to the prophecy of Daniel, grew from a small stone, and became a great mountain, so that it filled the whole earth[^48]? Nor let those be heard who, under the name as it were of a secret and apocryphal truth and of a fewness of men, say: *Behold, in the inner chambers, behold, in the desert*[^49]: because a city set on a mountain cannot be hidden, because the throne of glory is exalted, our sanctification.

**14.** When, then, this Gentile beheld, by these and other testimonies of this kind from the Prophets—about the persecution of kings and peoples, about the faith of kings and peoples, about the abolition of idols, about the blindness of the Jews, about the proof of the manuscripts kept by them, about the madness of heretics, about the excellence of the holy Church of true and genuine Christians—things foretold beforehand and now fulfilled; what would he find more worthy of faith than those Prophets, whom he might choose to believe concerning the divinity of Christ?

For indeed, if before these things were done I were to press the Hebrew Prophets upon a Gentile, by whom he should believe the things to come which he did not yet see done, he might perhaps rightly say: What have I to do with these Prophets, whose truthfulness, by what I should prove it, is not shown to me? But when so great and so many things which they foretold have now been brought to effect and manifestation; plainly that man, if he were not willing to be perverse, would in no way despise either those things which, so long before and with so great preparation, deserved to be commended as to be foreseen and foretold, nor those by whom they could be foreseen and foretold. For we believe no one more prudently, whether about past things which were done long ago, or about future things not yet done, than those who have proved to us the trustworthiness of their words by so many and so great things which, foretold by them, have now been done.

**15.** Further, the Sibyl, or the Sibyls, and Orpheus, and some Hermes or other, and any other seers or theologians or wise men or philosophers of the Gentiles who are said to have foretold or said true things about the Son of God, or about God the Father, avail something indeed for refuting the vanity of the pagans, yet not for embracing the authority of those men; since we show that we worship that God about whom even they could not keep silent, who partly dared to teach their fellow Gentile peoples that idols and demons were to be worshiped, partly did not dare to forbid it. But those holy authors of ours propagated and ruled, with God commanding and helping, that people, that commonwealth, such a kingdom, in which it was sacrilege which to those others was religion. And so if any there lapsed into the worship of images and demons, either they were punished, by the very laws of their commonwealth, or they were restrained by the most free thunder of prophetic voices. For one God was worshiped by them, who made heaven and earth, by a prophetic rite, that is, one significative of things to come; which rite would be abolished when those things had come which through it were signified to be coming: since the kingdom itself was a kind of great prophet, where the king and the priest were anointed with a mystical signification[^50]; which was not taken away before—with the Jews themselves too unknowing, and on that account unwilling—until there had come that God anointed with spiritual grace above his fellows, that Holy of holies[^51], both the true king by taking thought for us, and the true priest by offering himself for us. Wherefore, as great as is the distance, concerning Christ's coming, between the preaching of the Angels and the confession of the demons, so great is it between the authority of the Prophets and the curiosity of the sacrilegious.

**16.** By these and such things, which we now touch on briefly, then perhaps argued somewhat more at length according to the necessity of driving off the sluggish error, and asserted with the strength of a richer proof, that Gentile whom Faustus set before us to be catechized, if he preferred his salvation to his sins, would surely be moved to faith: imbued with which, and placed to be cherished in the bosom of the Catholic Church, he would consequently be admonished also what morals he ought to hold. Nor would he be disturbed by the multitude of those in whom he would not find the things he was commanded to observe: although they were gathered with him bodily into the Church, and received the same Sacraments.

He would know that the inheritance of God is to be shared with the few, but his seals with the many: to share with the few the holiness of life, and the gift of love poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^52], to which inner fount no stranger comes; but with the many the holiness of the Sacrament, which he who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself[^53], but he who despises to eat will not have life in himself[^54], and therefore will not come to eternal life. And those same few, in comparison with the multitude of the bad, are called few; but, considered in themselves, they are in a great number, spread throughout the whole world, growing among the tares, and with the chaff, until the day of harvest and winnowing[^55].

This was said in the Gospel, this foretold beforehand by the Prophets. For it was foretold: *As a lily among thorns, so is my beloved among the daughters*[^56]. It was foretold: *I dwelt in the tents of Kedar; with those who hated peace I was peaceable*[^57]. It was foretold: *Mark on the forehead those who groan and mourn over the iniquities of my people, which are done in their midst*[^58].

And so this Gentile, whom we would strengthen by such an address, now made a fellow citizen of the saints and of the household of God, and no longer a stranger to Israel[^59], but a true Israelite, in whom there was no guile[^60], would learn also to say from a simple heart these words, which the same Jeremiah goes on to weave: *O patience of Israel, Lord; let all who forsake you be terrified.* For when he had spoken of the partridge, which cries and gathers what it did not bear, he commended the excellence of the city set on the mountain, which cannot be hidden, so that heretics might not separate a man from the Catholic Church, saying: *But the throne of glory is exalted, our sanctification*[^61].

Then, as though it should come into mind: What, then, do we do about so many bad people, who are mixed in everywhere all the more, the more excellent the glory of Christ is in the unity of all the nations?, he at once added: *O patience of Israel, Lord.* For it must be patiently borne, what he says: *Let both grow until the harvest*[^62]:

lest, through impatience of tolerating the bad, the good be forsaken, who are properly the body of Christ; and therefore, when they themselves are forsaken, he is forsaken. Hence he subjoined and added: *Let all who forsake you be terrified: let those be confounded who have withdrawn into the earth.* For "earth" is the man who presumes upon himself, and leads others to presume upon him:

and therefore it follows: *They will be overthrown, because they have forsaken the Lord, the fount of life*[^63]. For what else does the partridge cry out, except that with itself, and from itself, is given the fount of life, so that those gathered to it may withdraw from Christ, deceived by the promise of Christ, with whose name they were already held imbued?

For it does not gather those it bore: but, in order to gather those it did not bear, it says: "The salvation which Christ promised is with me; I will give it." But see what this prophet says: *Heal me, Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved.* Hence the Apostle says: *Let no one glory in man*[^64]: and this prophet subjoins: *For you are my glorying*[^65]. In these ways we instruct a man from apostolic and prophetic teaching, that he may be built upon the foundations of the Apostles and Prophets[^66].

**17.** But how would Faustus persuade a Gentile man of the divinity of Christ, the man whom he set up as saying: I believe neither the Prophets about Christ, nor Christ about the Prophets? Would he believe Christ speaking about himself, when he would not believe him testifying about others? Truly it is ridiculous to think this. For one whom he did not once deem worthy of faith, he would either believe nothing at all, or rather would believe testifying for others than for himself.

Here perhaps Faustus, having been laughed at, would read to the man the Sibyls, and Orpheus, and others of this kind, if he could find any others of the Gentiles who foretold something about Christ? But he would not do it: for he confessed them to be unknown to him, saying: "To be sure, if there are any, as is reported, predictions about Christ of the Sibyl, or of Hermes, whom they call Trismegistus, or of Orpheus, and of other seers in the Gentile world." Not knowing, then, the writings of these men, since through report he supposes that there are some such, he surely would not read them to the man saying: I believe neither the Prophets, nor Christ.

What, then, would he do? Would he bring forth Mani, and from him commend Christ? Never did they do this: but rather, from Christ, whose name is now everywhere sweetly renowned, they always tried to commend Mani, so that with this honey, of course, they might smear the lips of their poisoned cup.

For since Christ promised his own that he would send the Paraclete, that is, the comforter or advocate, the Spirit of truth[^67]; through the occasion of this promise, saying that this Paraclete is Mani, or is in Mani, they creep into the minds of men ignorant of when that one promised by Christ was sent. But those who have read the canonical book which is inscribed the Acts of the Apostles see that promise of Christ recalled there again, and there most manifestly fulfilled[^68].

But now we ask, whence would he win faith for that Gentile about Christ? For I think no one is so blind as to be willing to say: I believe Mani, while he says: I do not believe Christ. Then, if not laughing, at least with irritation, he would say: Do you so bid me believe Persian books, you who told me not to believe the Hebrew ones? How, then, are you going to capture this man, heretic, unless you find him already in some way subject to the name of Christ; so that, because he no longer doubts that Christ is to be believed, he may, deceived, acquiesce in Mani as a better preacher of Christ? Behold, that is the partridge gathering what it did not bear. Do you not yet forsake him, you whom it gathers, and does he not yet appear foolish to you, he who says that the testimonies of the Hebrews are to us, even if they are true, useless before faith, superfluous after faith?

**18.** Let those, then, who have believed cast off all the books through which it came about that they believed. For if this is true, I do not see why even the very Gospel of Christ should be read by the faithful. For before faith it is useless, because that Gentile, whom Faustus, himself to be laughed at, or rather to be wept over, brings in laughing, does not believe Christ. But after faith it is superfluous, if even by true proclamations about Christ one believes now superfluously, when one has already believed in him. Here perhaps you will say: But one already faithful ought to read the Gospel, lest he forget what he believed. So, then, you mad men, also the true testimonies of the Prophets, lest he forget why he believed: which if he has forgotten, what he believed cannot be firm with him.

Or cast off now the books of Mani, by whose witness you believed that light fought with darkness, which light was God himself; and that, in order that the light might be able to bind the darkness, the light was first devoured by the darkness, and bound, and defiled, and torn apart: which you, by eating, restore, and loose, and purify, and heal, that a reward may be repaid to you, lest with that part which could not be freed you be condemned in the eternal clod. This little tale you sing daily both by your morals and by your voices:

why do you still seek in it the testimonies of books, so that on superfluous things and on producing your manuscripts another's substance is consumed, and your God is held bound? Burn all those parchments, and your elegant bindings sought out with handsome skins, so that neither may a superfluous thing burden you, and your God may from there be freed, who, as though by a servile punishment, is held bound even in a manuscript. For if you could eat your books even boiled, what a benefit would you bestow on the members of your God? Or would the uncleanness of flesh, even if it could be done, forbid the pages from your banquets? Then let the cleanness of the ink charge it against itself, which clung to the lambskin.

But this too you did, who, as though in your first war, affixed by writing what was clean in the reed to the uncleanness of the parchments: except that the colors convict you by a contrary testimony. For you rather came with the darkness of the ink to the light of the white pages. Ought you to be angry at us who say such things, or at yourselves who believe such things, that, whether you will or not, these consequences follow? But we, both for the recalling of our faith, and for the consolation of our hope, and for the exhortation of our love, read the prophetic and apostolic Books sounding together in mutual voices; and that harmony, as by a heavenly trumpet, both rouses us from the torpor of mortal life, and stretches us toward the prize of the supernal calling. For when the Apostle had recalled something from the prophetic Books, as it is written: *The reproaches of those who reproached you fell upon me*[^69], he at once subjoins the usefulness of divine reading: *For whatever things were written before*, he says, *were written for our instruction; that through the patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we may have hope toward God*[^70]. But Faustus contradicts. Let it be done to him, then, what Paul says: *If anyone preaches to you a gospel other than what you received, let him be accursed*[^71].
