# Book 7

**1.** Faustus said: Why do you not believe in the genealogy of Jesus?

There are indeed many causes, but the chief is this: that he himself nowhere with his own mouth confesses that he has a father, or a lineage, on earth; but on the contrary, that he is not of this world, that he proceeded from God the Father, that he descended from heaven, that he has no mother and brothers except those who do the will of his Father who is in heaven[^1]. Besides, those very men who ascribe these genealogies to him seem not to have known him before his birth; nor immediately when he was born, so that they might be believed to have written the things they saw done concerning him with their own eyes; but they were joined to him when he was already a young man and mature, that is, of about thirty years—if indeed age too can be ascribed to divine things without blasphemy.

Therefore, since in all testimony of truth this is always wont to be asked, whether someone heard or saw a thing, but these men confess neither that they heard from him this order of generation, or that he was born at all, nor that they saw it with their eyes, since they came to know him a long time afterward, that is, after his Baptism: to me, and to everyone who judges rightly, it seems as foolish to believe this as if someone should call a blind and deaf man as a witness in court.

**2.** Augustine replied: What he calls the chief cause why he does not accept the genealogy of Jesus Christ is there most plainly shown to be defeated, for those who read what we said above about the Son of man, which Christ so often testifies himself to be[^2], and about the Son of God, how the same one is also the son of man[^3]; how according to divinity he has no lineage on earth, but according to the flesh is of the seed of David, as apostolic teaching testifies[^4]. Hence he must be believed and understood both to have gone forth from the Father[^5], and to have come from heaven[^6], and yet here, the Word made flesh, to have dwelt among men[^7].

But if they think on this account that he had no mother or lineage on earth, because he said: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?*[^8]—it remains that they affirm that his disciples too, to whom he gave this example in himself, that they should despise the bond of earthly lineage for the sake of the kingdom of heaven, had no fathers, because he said to them: *Call no one on earth your father; for one is your Father, God*[^9]. What, then, he taught these men about fathers, he himself first did about his mother and brothers: as also in many other things, in which he deigned to offer himself to us, that we might imitate him, and to go before, that we might follow. Therefore this man, who is so defeated in what he thinks is his chief point, must be observed—how he lies fallen and entangled in the rest.

For he who says that the Apostles, who announced not only his divine but also his human birth, are not to be believed, because they joined him later when he was a young man, and neither saw him born nor said that they heard this from him—why, then, do they believe John when he says: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God; all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^10], and the rest which please them, although they do not understand it? Let them say where John saw this, or where he said that he heard this from the Lord himself. For whatever they say is the source from which John could know this, from that same source we believe that all the announcers of Christ's nativity could know what they announced.

Next I ask, on what grounds they believe the Lord said: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?* If it is because the Evangelist narrated this, why do they not also believe him in that: *that his mother and brothers were seeking him*[^11]? But if he lied about what they refuse to believe, how do they believe him that Christ said what they refuse to understand?

Next, if Matthew could not know Christ to have been born, because he had joined him already as a young man, whence could Mani, born so many years afterward, know Christ to have not been born? They will say: The Holy Spirit, who was in Mani, knew this. Indeed, if he were the Holy Spirit, he would have said true things. But why do we not rather believe about Christ his disciples, who clung to him even bodily—who could not only know, through the Holy Spirit imparted to them by him, if anything lay hidden in human affairs, but, with memory so fresh and present, even by human sense alone, gathered Christ's lineage according to the flesh and his whole origin?

And yet the Apostles are called blind and deaf witnesses. Would that you had been not only blind and deaf, lest you should learn such vain and sacrilegious things, but mute as well, lest you should say them!
