# Book 5

**1.** Faustus said: Do you accept the Gospel?

You ask me whether I accept it, when the very acceptance of it is plain, because I observe what it commands. Or ought I to ask of you whether you accept it, in whom no signs of one accepting the Gospel appear? I left father and mother, wife, children, and the rest, as the Gospel commands[^1]; and do you ask whether I accept the Gospel? Unless you still do not know what it is that is called the Gospel. For it is nothing other than the preaching and commandment of Christ. I rejected silver and gold, and ceased to keep copper in my belts, content with daily food, taking no care for the morrow, bearing no anxiety about where the belly might be filled or the body covered[^2]; and do you ask of me whether I accept the Gospel? You see in me those beatitudes of Christ that make up the Gospel[^3]; and do you ask whether I accept it? You see a poor man, you see a meek man, you see a peacemaker, pure in heart, mourning, hungering, thirsting, enduring persecutions and hatreds for the sake of righteousness; and do you doubt whether I accept the Gospel? It is then no longer surprising that John the Baptist, having seen Jesus and likewise heard of his works, still asked whether he was the Christ—to whom rightly and deservedly Jesus did not even deign to report that he himself was, but again sent back to him those same works which he had long since heard of: *The blind see, the deaf hear, the dead rise*[^4]. This I too might not undeservedly do regarding you, if to your repeated question whether I accept the Gospel I were to say: I have left all that is mine—father, mother, wife, children, gold, silver, eating, drinking, delights, pleasures. Consider this a sufficient answer to your questions, and consider that you will be blessed if you are not scandalized in me.

**2.** But to accept the Gospel, you say, is not this alone—to do what it has commanded—but also to believe all the things written in it, the first of which is that Jesus was born. But neither is this alone to accept the Gospel—to believe that Jesus was born—but also to do what it has commanded. Now if you think I do not accept the Gospel because I pass over the generation, then neither do you accept it; and much more do you not accept it, because you despise the precepts. And by this, for the time, we are equal, until we examine the parts as well. Or if that contempt of the precepts does not prejudge against you, so as to keep you from professing that you accept the Gospel, why should a condemned genealogy prejudge against me to that effect? But if to accept the Gospel consists in these two things, as you say—to believe the genealogies and to keep the commandments—why, then, do you, imperfect, judge the imperfect? Each of us needs the other.

But if, what is more certain, to accept the Gospel consists in the observance alone of the heavenly commandments, then you are doubly base, since, as the saying goes, you, a deserter, accuse a soldier. And yet come, let us grant, since you so wish, that there are these two parts of perfect faith—one of which consists in word, that is, to confess that Christ was born; the other in deed, which is the observance of the precepts. See, then, how arduous and more difficult a part I have chosen for myself; and you, see how very light and easy a part you have.

And not without reason does the crowd flock to you and flee from me, not knowing, of course, that the kingdom of God is not in word but in power. What, then, is the reason you challenge me, if, having undertaken the more difficult part of faith, I left the easier to you as the weaker one? But I, you say, think this part of faith—which you abandoned, namely, to confess that Christ was born—more effective for granting salvation to souls, and more suitable.

**3.** Come, then, let us ask Christ himself, and let us learn from his own mouth from which source above all the occasion of our salvation arises. Which of men will enter into your kingdom, Christ? *He who does*, he says, *the will of my Father who is in heaven*[^5]. He did not say: He who has professed me to have been born. And elsewhere to the disciples: *Go, teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to keep all things whatsoever I have commanded you*[^6]. He did not say: Teaching them that I was born, but to keep the commandments.

Likewise elsewhere: *You will be my friends, if you do what I command you*[^7]. He did not say: If you believe me to have been born. Again: *If you keep my commandments, you will abide in my love*[^8].

And many other things. Moreover, on the mountain too, when he was teaching: *Blessed are the poor in spirit*, he said, blessed are the meek, blessed are the peacemakers, blessed are the pure in heart, blessed are they who mourn, blessed are they who hunger, blessed are they who suffer persecution for the sake of righteousness[^9]; nowhere did he say: Blessed are they who have confessed me to have been born.

And in the separation of the sheep from the goats at the judgment, he says he will say to those who will be on the right: *I was hungry, and you fed me; I was thirsty, and you gave me drink*, and the rest: *therefore receive the kingdom*[^10]. He did not say: Because you believed me to have been born, receive the kingdom. Moreover, to the rich man seeking eternal life: *Go*, he says, *sell all that you have, and follow me*[^11]. He did not say: Believe me to have been born, that you may live forever.

Behold, then, to my portion, which I chose for myself out of the twofold faith, as you would have it, everywhere the kingdom, life, and blessedness are promised; but to yours, nowhere. Either teach where it is written that he is blessed, or will receive the kingdom, or will have eternal life, who has confessed him to have been born of a woman. Meanwhile, even though this too is a part of faith, it has no blessedness.

But when we have proved that it is not even a part of faith, what then? Surely you will be empty—which itself, of course, will be shown. But for the present this is enough for our purpose: that this portion of ours is crowned with the beatitudes. To it, however, there is added that other blessedness as well, from the confession of word also, in that we confess Jesus to be Christ the Son of the living God—which Jesus likewise testifies with his own mouth, saying to Peter: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, for flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^12]. Therefore we hold, not, as you supposed, one, but two parts of faith, both equally valid; and in both alike we are called blessed by Christ, since we exercise one of them by works and preach the other without blasphemy.

**4.** Augustine replied: I have already recalled above how often the Lord Jesus Christ calls himself the son of man, and with how great vanity the Manicheans bring in the fable of their unspeakable error, about some fabricated first man or other, whom they preach to be not earthy, but clothed in deceptive elements, against the Apostle who says: *The first man was of the earth, earthy*[^13]; and with how great care the same Apostle warned us, saying: *If anyone announces to you anything other than what we announced to you, let him be accursed*[^14]. It remains, therefore, that we believe Christ the son of man as he is preached by apostolic truth, not as he is fabricated by the falsehood of the Manicheans.

Since, then, the Evangelists announce him born of a woman, of the house of David, that is, of the family of David, and Paul writes to Timothy, saying: *Remember that Jesus Christ rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel*[^15], it appears clearly enough how we ought to believe Christ to be the son of man—he who, being the Son of God through whom we were made, was also made the son of man through the assumption of flesh, that he might die for our sins and rise for our justification[^16]. And therefore he calls himself both, the Son of God and the son of man. Hence, not to go through many passages, in one place in the Gospel according to John it is written thus: *Amen, amen I say to you, the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and they who hear will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he gave to the Son also to have life in himself; and he gave him power to do judgment, because he is the son of man*[^17]. He said: They will hear the voice of the Son of God; and he said: Because he is the son of man. For in respect of his being the son of man he received power to do judgment, since he will come to the judgment in that very form, so that he may be seen both by the good and by the wicked. In which form he also ascended into heaven, and that voice sounded to the disciples: *So he will come, as you saw him going into heaven*[^18]. For in respect of his being the Son of God, he is God equal to the Father, and one with the Father, and will not be seen by the wicked: *For blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^19].

Since, then, he promises eternal life to those who believe in him, and to believe in him is nothing other than to believe in the true Christ, such as he himself preaches and is preached by the Apostles—that is, both the true Son of God and the true son of man—you, Manicheans, who believe in a false and deceptive Christ, the son of a false and deceptive man, since you teach that God himself, terrified by the tumult of the opposing race, sent his members to be tormented and not afterward to be wholly purified,

you see how alien you are from the eternal life which Christ promises to those who believe in him. But, you say, he said to Peter, who confessed him the Son of God: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona*[^20]. Does he therefore promise nothing to those who would believe in him as the son of man, when he himself is both the Son of God and the son of man? You have eternal life plainly promised to those who believe in the son of man as well. *As Moses*, he says, *lifted up the serpent in the desert, so must the Son of man be lifted up, that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life*[^21]. What more do you want? Believe, then, in the son of man, that you may have eternal life; for he himself is also the Son of God, who can give eternal life; for he himself is the true God and eternal life[^22], as the same John says in his Epistle. Where he also says that he is the antichrist who denies that Christ has come in the flesh[^23].

**5.** Why, then, do you boast, as it were, of the perfection of his commandments, that you do the things commanded in the Gospel? For what would those things profit, where there is no true faith, even if they were truly fulfilled by you? Did you not hear the Apostle saying: *If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and deliver my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^24]? Why, then, do you boast, as it were, of Christian poverty, when you lack Christian love? For even robbers have among themselves what they call love, owing one another a criminal and shameful loyalty, but not the love that the Apostle commends. And in order to set it apart from the other things to be condemned and rejected, he says in another place: *But the end of the commandment is love, from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned*[^25]. Whence, then, can you have true love rising from feigned faith? Or when will you be ashamed to gloss your faith with lies, when you preach that even your first man fought with mutable deceit against his enemies, who remained in the truth of their own nature; and that Christ himself, who says *I am the truth*[^26], faked the appearance of flesh, the death of the cross, the wounds of the passion, the scars of the resurrection? Accordingly, you want to be set above your Christ, if, while he lies, you announce the truth. But if you want to follow your Christ, who would not be wary of falsehood in you, so that even in those commandments which you say you fulfill, there should be nothing but mere deceit?

For Faustus dared to say that you do not carry copper in your belts—unless perhaps he said the truth for this reason, that you have not copper in belts, but gold also in little chests and in purses. These things would not be blamed in you, were it not that you profess one thing and live in another way. There is still living in human affairs that Constantius, now already our brother, a Catholic Christian, who had gathered many of you at Rome into his house, for the fulfillment of Mani's precepts—quite vain and silly precepts indeed, but ones which you nonetheless reckon great. When your infirmity gave way before them, you scattered, each into his own way. Hence those who chose to persevere in them made a schism from your fellowship; and because they sleep on mats, they are called Mattarii. Far unlike their bedding were Faustus's feathers and goatskin coverlets, in which abundance of delights he loathed not only the Mattarii but even the house of his own father, a poor man of Milevis. Away, then, with your lost pretense, if not from your morals, at least from your writings; lest your tongue be seen to fight with your life, as that first man with the race of darkness, with lying—not elements, but words.

**6.** But lest anyone reproach me for saying these things against men who do not fulfill what is enjoined on them, rather than against the very sect of that most vain error, I say this: The very precepts of Mani are such that, if you do not do them, you are deceivers; but if you do do them, you are deceived. For Christ did not command you not to pull up a plant, lest you commit murder,

he who did not forbid his disciples, as they passed through the grainfield and were hungry, to pluck the ears of grain on the sabbath[^27], by which he might convict both the Jews then present and the Manicheans to come: the former, because it was done on the sabbath; the latter, because it was done at all. But Mani plainly commanded that, with your hands idle, you should live off the murders of others; and those murders indeed are false, but yours are true, when by such teaching of demons you slaughter wretched souls.

**7.** Yet there is that heretical conceit and intolerable pride. "You see," he says, "in me those beatitudes of Christ that make up the Gospel; and do you ask whether I accept it? You see a poor man, you see a meek man, you see a peacemaker, pure in heart, mourning, hungering, thirsting, enduring persecutions and hatreds for the sake of righteousness; and do you doubt whether I accept the Gospel?" If to be righteous were to justify oneself, this man would have flown into heaven by his own words while he was saying these things.

But I do not inveigh against the delights of Faustus, known to all the Hearers of the Manicheans, and especially to those at Rome. I set forth such a Manichean as Constantius was looking for, when he required those precepts to be fulfilled, not such as he refused to be seen as.

And yet, how do I see as poor in spirit one who is so proud that he believes his soul to be God, and is not ashamed that it is a captive? How meek, one who prefers to insult so great an evangelical authority rather than believe it? How a peacemaker, one who thinks that the divine nature itself, by which God is whatever he is, who alone truly is, could not have perpetual peace? How pure in heart, one in whom so many and so sacrilegious figments are in tumult? How mourning, unless his God, captured and bound, until he is loosed and escapes—yet curtailed in some part, which will be bound by the Father in the clod of darkness, and will not be mourned? How hungering and thirsting for righteousness, which Faustus did not add in his writings, I believe lest he should seem to lack it, if he confessed that he still hungered and thirsted for it?

But what righteousness do these men hunger and thirst for, whose perfect righteousness will be, when their brothers are condemned in the clod—brothers who sinned by no fault of their own, but were inexpiably infected by the enemy's corruption, against which the Father sent them—to triumph besides?

**8.** How do you endure persecutions and hatreds for the sake of righteousness, you for whom to preach and persuade these sacrilegious things is righteousness? Why do you not consider how little, and almost nothing, you suffer for this impious perversity, thanks to the mildness of Christian times? But, as though you were speaking to the blind and foolish, you would have it a great mark of your righteousness that you endure reproaches and suffer persecution. Further, if each is the more righteous the heavier the things he suffers, I forbear to say what is most easy to see—how much heavier the things suffered by others, defiled by all sorts of crimes and shameful deeds. This I say: if whoever suffers persecution for the name of Christ, however it has been usurped and taken up, is therefore to be said to hold the true faith and righteousness, grant that he is of truer faith and greater righteousness whom we can show to have suffered far heavier things than you. And now thousands of our martyrs meet you, and Cyprian above all, who by his very writings is shown to have believed in Christ born of the Virgin Mary. For this faith, which you detest, he went all the way to the sword and death, together with flocks of Christians who then believed thus, and who died thus and more grievously.

But Faustus, convicted or having confessed that he was a Manichean, when he and some others with him had been brought to the proconsular court, with those very Christians by whom they had been brought interceding for them, was relegated to an island with the lightest penalty—if indeed that is to be called a penalty: which the servants of God do daily of their own accord, desiring to remove themselves from the turbulent uproar of the peoples; and from which the public vows for earthly princes are wont, through clemency, to release the condemned. And then, not long after, all were dismissed from there by the same customary lot. Confess, then, that those held a truer faith and a more righteous life who deserved to endure for it things far more atrocious than you do; or cease boasting on the ground that you are detestable to many. But distinguish what it is to suffer persecution for blasphemy and to suffer persecution for righteousness; and consider, again and again diligently in your books, for which of these you suffer.

**9.** But how many in our communion truly do those more sublime evangelical precepts, with whose appearance you deceive the unskilled? How many people of both sexes, pure and untouched, abstaining from all intercourse; how many who have had experience of it and are afterward continent; how many distributors and renouncers of their possessions; how many subjecting the body to servitude by fasts frequent, or daily, or even incredibly continuous? How many fraternal congregations having nothing of their own, but all things in common, and these only what is necessary for food and clothing; melting one soul and one heart toward God in the fire of love? And in all these professions, how many false and lost ones are detected, how many also lie hidden, how many at first walking rightly soon fail through a perverse will; how many are found in temptations to have undertaken such a life in a sketched-out appearance with another intent, and how many, humbly and faithfully keeping their holy purpose, persevere to the end and are saved?

In whose society they appear, as it were, unequal; but yet they are joined by the same love, those who, for the sake of some bond, according to the Apostle's exhortation, have wives as though not having them, and buy as though not keeping, and use this world as though not using it[^28]. To these are joined, according to the abundant riches of God's mercy, even those to whom it is said: *Do not defraud one another, except by consent for a time, that you may be free for prayer; and come together again, lest Satan tempt you because of your incontinence. But this I say by way of indulgence, not by way of command*[^29]. To such the same Apostle also says: *Now indeed there is altogether a fault in you, that you have lawsuits among yourselves.* And bearing their weakness, he says a little later: *If, then, you have cases concerning secular matters, set those who are contemptible in the Church to judge them*[^30]. For not only those who, to be perfect, sell or relinquish all their goods and follow the Lord belong to the kingdom of heaven; but to this Christian warfare there is also joined, by a kind of exchange of love, a certain stipendiary multitude, to whom it will be said at the end: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^31], and the rest. Otherwise those would have to be condemned whose households the Apostle arranges with such diligent and careful concern, admonishing wives to be subject to their husbands, husbands to love their wives; children to obey their parents, parents to nurture their children in the discipline and correction of the Lord; servants to obey their carnal masters with fear, masters to render to servants what is just and equitable[^32].

But far be it that the Apostle should judge these to be alien from the evangelical commandments and to be separated from eternal life; for where the Lord says: *If anyone does not carry his cross and follow me, he cannot be my disciple*[^33], exhorting the stronger to perfection—there at once he also consoled these, saying: *He who receives a righteous man in the name of a righteous man will receive a righteous man's reward; and he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet will receive a prophet's reward.* Hence not only he who gave a little wine to Timothy on account of his stomach and his frequent infirmities[^34], but even he who has handed a cup of cold water to a healthier and stronger man, only in the name of a disciple, as there follows, will not lose his reward[^35].

**10.** But why do you deceive your Hearers, who serve you with their wives, and children, and households, and houses, and fields, if whoever has not left all these things does not accept the Gospel? But because you promise them not resurrection but a revolution back into this mortality, so that they may be born again and live the life of your Elect—as vain, and silly, and sacrilegious a life as the one you live, when you are highly praised; or, if they are of better merit, that they may come into melons and cucumbers, or into some other foods which you are going to eat, so that they may quickly be purified by your belchings—you rightly make them alien from the evangelical commandments; but you yourselves too, who think and persuade such things, ought above all to separate yourselves from them.

For if this vanity pertained to evangelical faith, the Lord ought not to have said: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^36]; but: You were hungry, and you ate me; or: I was hungry, and I ate you. For no one, according to your ravings, will now be received into the kingdom of God on account of that merit of ministering, because he gave the saints to eat; but because either he ate those he longed for, or was eaten by those who longed to breathe him out into heaven. Nor would those say: Lord, when did we see you hungry, and give you to eat? But they would say: When did we see you hungry, and you ate us? To whom he would not reply: *When you gave to one of the least of mine, you gave to me*[^37]; but: When one of the least of mine ate you, I ate you.

**11.** Thinking and teaching these monstrosities, and living according to them, do you dare to say that you keep the evangelical precepts, and to disparage the Catholic Church, in which there are so many little ones along with the great, both of whom the Lord blesses, keeping the evangelical commandments according to their grades, and hoping for the evangelical promises? But malevolent error leads your eye to the chaff alone of our harvest; for you would quickly see the wheat there too, if you were even willing to be wheat.

But among you, both those who are feigned Manicheans are wicked, and those who are not feigned are vain. For where the faith itself is feigned, both he who uses it with pretense deceives, and he who reckons it true is deceived; nor can a good life come to be from it; for each lives by his own love, whether well or badly. But if you burned with love of the spiritual and intelligible good, and not with desire for corporeal phantasms—to say quickly what is most well known about you—you would not worship this corporeal sun as the divine substance and as the light of wisdom.
