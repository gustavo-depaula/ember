# Book XXV

**1.** FAUSTUS said: Has God an end, or is he infinite? If your prayer does not deceive, which says, *the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^1], God has an end: unless perhaps this one about whom you question is one, and the one you pray to is another: otherwise the boundary of circumcision, which marks off Abraham and Isaac and Jacob from the society of the other nations[^2], bounds also the power of God himself with respect to them. But whose power is bounded, he himself too is not without an end. Finally, you do not even make mention in this prayer of the ancients who were before Abraham—I mean Enoch, and Noah, and Shem[^3], and the rest like them, whom you do not deny to have been just in uncircumcision, but because they lacked the mark of circumcision, you are unwilling that he be even their God, but of Abraham alone and his seed. Therefore, if God is one and infinite, what does this so diligent and solicitous caution of your invocation mean, that, not content to have named God, you add also "whose"—of Abraham, namely, and Isaac and Jacob: as though your prayer would go astray in some throng of gods, or suffer shipwreck, unless it sailed to the sign of Abraham? And that the Jews indeed pray thus is, for a certain reason, not absurd, inasmuch as they are circumcised; for thus they designate that they invoke the God of circumcision, on account of the gods of the uncircumcision: but why you do this very thing, I little understand; since you by no means bear the sign that Abraham had, whose God you invoke. For truly, as may be understood, for the sake of mutual recognition—lest, namely, they should stray from each other in turn—the Jews and the God of the Jews seem to have imposed such marks on each other reciprocally: and he indeed marked them with the obscene character of circumcision, by which, wherever in the lands they may be, wherever among the nations, they may yet be known by circumcision to be his; but they in turn marked their own God by the surname of their parents, by which, wherever he himself may be, in however great a throng of gods, when he has heard "the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob," he may at once recognize that he is invoked. Which is wont generally to be done in the case of many having one name, that none of them, when called, answers, unless the surname is heard. For so too the shepherd and the herdsman brand marks on their cattle, lest any of them seize another's for his own. Since you do likewise with these, saying "the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob," you not only show that God has an end, but that you yourselves too are foreign to him, having no part in his sign and sacrament, which is the mutilation of the male parts, by which he recognizes his own. Wherefore, if this is the God you worship, it is from this quite plain that he has an end. But if you wish God to be infinite, you must first renounce this one, and, your prayer changed, do penance for your past error. And this is said in such a way that we seem to have conquered you from your own resources: otherwise, if it is asked whether the highest and true God is himself infinite or not, on this point the contrariety of good and evil will be able briefly to instruct us. Since indeed, if there is no evil, God surely is infinite; but he has an end, if there is evil: but it is established that there is evil: therefore God is not infinite: for they take it that evils are from there, where there is the end of good things.

**2.** AUGUSTINE replied: Far be it that anyone who knows you should question you about this, or dispute about this matter at all with such men. For first you are to be cleansed, by devout faith and by whatever small reason of truth, from the figment of carnal and corporeal thoughts, that you may be able in some way and from however small a part to think spiritual things. Which, as long as you cannot (for your heresy knows nothing else than to stretch out both body and soul and God through spaces of places, whether finite or infinite, when body alone occupies such spaces, or is occupied by such spaces), you would do more advisedly if you did not mix yourselves into this question, where you can neither teach anything true, as neither in the rest; nor are you fit to learn, which perhaps you can in the rest, if you are not proud and quarrelsome. For when it begins to be asked, how God is finite, whom no place contains; how infinite, whom the Son wholly knows: how finite, the immeasurable; how infinite, the perfect: how finite, having no measure; how infinite, the measure of all: all carnal thought vanishes; and if it wishes to become what it is not yet, it first blushes for what it is. Wherefore this which you set before us about a finite and infinite God, you better end by being silent, until you cease to stray so far from the end of the Law, which is Christ. But about the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, since he himself is the true God of every creature, why he willed to insinuate himself to his people by that name, we have already said as much as was enough. About circumcision too, in the earlier parts of the work, we have often replied against ignorant slanders. But these people, if they thought with a Christian, not a heretical mind, how it was said, *To the clean all things are clean*, about that sign divinely given in a fitting part of the body, by which the stripping off of the flesh was figured, would in no way deride it. But because that too which follows is true—*but to the unclean and the unbelieving nothing is clean; but both their mind and their conscience are polluted*[^4]—deriding them, and wittily insulting them, we gently admonish that, if according to them circumcision is base, they have no reason there to laugh, but to mourn; because their god is bound and defiled, mingled both with that particle which is cut off, and with that blood which drips.

[^1]: See Exod 3:15.
[^2]: See Gen 17:9–14.
[^3]: See Gen 5.
[^4]: Titus 1:15.
