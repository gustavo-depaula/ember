# Book 8

**1.** Faustus said: Why do you not accept the Old Testament?

Because I have already been forestalled by the New; and the old does not go with the new, as Scripture testifies. For, *No one*, it says, *sews a new patch onto an old garment, otherwise a greater tear is made*[^1]. Since, then, I beware of making a greater tear, as you do, I do not mix Christian newness with Hebrew oldness. For who would not judge it even sordid, when one is clothed in new garments, not to give the old ones to one's inferiors? Therefore, even if I had been born a Jew, as the Apostles were, it would still have been worthy for me, having received the New Testament, to repudiate the Old, as they themselves did. But as it is, having both obtained by the benefit of nature that I was not born under the yoke of servitude, and Christ at once meeting me with the full gift of liberty, how wretched and foolish and, besides, ungrateful will I be if I further bind myself over to servitude? For Paul reproaches the Galatians for this, that, relapsing into circumcision, they went back to the weak and beggarly elements, which they wished to serve anew[^2]. How, then, should I commit that for which I see another reproached? It is base to return into servitude, but baser to go into it.

**2.** Augustine replied: We have already shown sufficiently above why and how we hold the authority of the Old Testament—not for the imitation of Jewish servitude, but for the witnessing of Christian liberty. For it is not my voice, but the Apostle's: *that all these things happened to them in figure; but they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^3]. Therefore we do not do in servitude the things commanded for foretelling us, but read in liberty the things written for confirming us. Who, then, does not now understand from what the Apostle calls back the Galatians—not those reading the Scripture of circumcision religiously, but those already wishing to be circumcised superstitiously[^4]?

Hence we do not sew a new patch onto an old garment; but we are instructed in the kingdom of heaven, after the likeness of that householder whom the Lord recalls bringing forth from his treasure new things and old[^5]. But he sews a new patch onto an old garment who wishes to have spiritual continence and has not yet laid aside carnal hope. For read diligently, and see that the Lord, asked about fasting, replied: *No one sews a new patch onto an old garment*[^6]. For the disciples still loved the Lord carnally, since they were even afraid of losing him by his being slain. Hence he calls Peter, who called him back from the passion, Satan, because he savored not the things that are of God, but the things that are of men[^7]. Accordingly, consider, in that fantasy of yours about the kingdom of God, from which you love and adore this light of the sun visible to the flesh, as though set before you for an example, what carnal hope you bear; and you will find how your fasts are sewn, as it were, to the prudence of the flesh, like an old garment.

But seeing that a new patch does not go with an old garment, how could the members of your God cohere with the princes of darkness—not joined and stitched, but, what is more violent, mixed and concreted? Or is each old, because each is false, and each is of the prudence of the flesh? Unless perhaps you wish to prove from this that one was new and the other old, because a greater tear was made; so that the wretched patch was cut off from the realm of light, and fixed with eternal punishment to the clod of darkness. And yet he thinks himself clever to prick, as it were with the needle of his tongue, the supports of the divine Scriptures—a mender, or a beggarly wearer, of the filth of such fables.

[^1]: Matt 9:16; Luke 5:36.
[^2]: Gal 4; 5.
[^3]: 1 Cor 10:11.
[^4]: See Gal 4; 5.
[^5]: See Matt 13:52.
[^6]: Matt 9:16.
[^7]: Matt 16:23.
