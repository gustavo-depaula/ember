# Book 4

**1.** Faustus said: Do you accept the Old Testament?

If there is an inheritance for me in it, I accept it; if there is not, I do not accept it. For it is indeed an excessive shamelessness to lay claim to a will that testifies one disinherited. Or do you not know that the Old Testament promises the land of the Canaanites[^1]—but to the Jews, that is, to the circumcised, and those who sacrifice, and abstain from pork and the other flesh that Moses calls unclean, who observe sabbaths and the solemnity of unleavened bread, and the rest of this kind, which its very testator commanded them to observe[^2]? Since these things have pleased no Christian—for none of us keeps them—it is fitting that, having renounced the inheritance, we should also hand back the will. This, then, is the first reason why I think the Old Testament must be cast off, unless you teach me something more prudent. But the second reason is that its inheritance is so wretched and so corporeal and so far from the soul's benefits that, after that blessed promise of the New Testament, which promises me the kingdom of heaven and perpetual life, I would loathe it even if its testator forced it on me for free.

2.1. Augustine replied: That promises of temporal things are contained in the Old Testament, and that it is therefore called the Old Testament, none of us doubts; and that the promise of eternal life and the kingdom of heaven pertains to the New Testament. But that in those temporal things there were figures of things to come, which would be fulfilled in us, upon whom the end of the ages has come, is not my surmise but the apostolic understanding, since Paul, when he was speaking of such things, says: *All these things were figures of us*; and again: *All these things happened to them in figure; but they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^3].

We accept the Old Testament, then, not for obtaining those promises, but for understanding in them the foretellings of the New Testament; for the witness of the Old wins faith for the New. Hence the Lord, after he had risen from the dead and offered himself not only to be seen by the eyes of his disciples but also to be touched by their hands, lest they should think anything was being done deceptively through their mortal and carnal senses, strengthened them all the more from the testimony of the old books, saying: *It was necessary that all things be fulfilled which were written in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms concerning me*[^4].

And so our hope is not fixed on the promise of temporal things; for we do not believe that even the holy and spiritual men of that time, the Patriarchs and Prophets, were devoted to these earthly things. For they understood, the Spirit of God revealing it to them, what suited that time, and in what ways God, through all those things done and said, was determining to figure and foretell things to come; and their desire was rather for the New Testament. But the present corporeal function was supplied to the old promises in order to signify the new things to come. Thus the tongue of those men was not the only thing prophetic, but their life as well. The carnal people, however, clung to the promises of the present life. Yet even by that people, no less, things to come were signified.

2.2. But you do not understand these things; because, as the prophet said: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^5]. For you are not learned in the kingdom of heaven, that is, in the true Catholic Church of Christ. If you were, you would bring forth from the riches of the holy Scriptures not only new things but old as well. For the Lord himself says: *Therefore every scribe learned in the kingdom of heaven is like a man, a householder, who brings forth from his treasure new things and old*[^6]. And so, while you suppose that you hold only the new promises of God, you have remained in the oldness of the flesh and brought in the newness of error. Of this newness the Apostle says: *Avoid profane novelties of words; for they contribute much to impiety, and their speech spreads like a cancer. Of these are Hymenaeus and Philetus, who have strayed concerning the truth, saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and have subverted the faith of some*[^7].

Recognize that you flow from this vein of falsehood, you who say that there is now only a resurrection of souls through the preaching of truth, while you deny that the resurrection of bodies, which the Apostles preached, is to come. And what can you think spiritually, according to the inner man who is renewed in the knowledge of God[^8], when, through the oldness of the flesh and through the images of carnal things in which your whole error is wrapped, you do not hold corporeal things by possession, but savor them by phantasm? For you boast that you despise and loathe the land of the Canaanites, which was manifest and was manifestly given to that people; as though you did not describe the land of light as split off on one side from the land of the race of darkness, as though by a wedge driven in, so that it is neither found in the truth of things and is believed in the falsehood of your imaginings—and so neither sustains your life by being given, nor corrupts your mind by being desired.
