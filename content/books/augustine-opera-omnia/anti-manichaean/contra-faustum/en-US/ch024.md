# Book XXIII

**1.** FAUSTUS said: As I was once disputing, a certain one of the numerous crowd, answering, said: Do you accept Jesus born of Mary? To whom I said: Which Jesus do you mean? For there were several Jesuses among the Hebrews: one indeed the son of Nun, the disciple of Moses[^1]; another the son of Jozadak the high priest[^2]; likewise another who was called the son of David[^3], but another the Son of God[^4]: which of these, then, born of Mary, do you ask me whether I accept? Surely, he said, the Son of God. To whom I said: And on whose authority, or by whose teaching, am I to accept this? But he: Matthew's, he said. To whom again I said: What, then, did Matthew write? And he: *The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham*, and so on[^5]. Then I said: I had supposed you were going to say, "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ the Son of God"; and I had prepared myself to forestall it: but since you have now faithfully recalled the chapter, you need only be admonished to consider what you have recited. For Matthew does not here confess that he had set out to write the generation of the Son of God, but of the son of David.

**2.** So that, to believe this man for the moment as he says, the son of David will be for me the one born of Mary: but still, of the Son of God, in this whole text of the generation no mention is made, up to the baptism, that is; and in vain do you heap a slander upon the writer, as though he shut up the Son of God in a woman's womb. But this man cries out, as it seems, and by his very title clears himself altogether of this sacrilege, declaring that he wrote that one sprung from that stock was a son of David, not the Son of God. For Jesus indeed, the one who is the Son of God, if you consider the mind and purpose of this writer, he does not so much wish us to take as procreated from the virgin Mary, as made at some time through baptism at the streams of the Jordan. For there he says that the one whom David at the outset designated a son was baptized by John and made at some time the Son of God[^6]—after about thirty years, that is, according to the testimony of Luke, where the voice too was then heard, saying to him, *You are my Son; today I have begotten you*[^7]. You see, then, that what was born of Mary, as this man thinks, thirty years before, is not the Son of God himself, but that which was made afterward from the baptism at the Jordan—that is, the new man, as in us when we believe, converted to God from the error of Gentilism: which thing itself, however, I do not know whether it agrees well enough with that faith which you call catholic; but for the moment so it seems to Matthew, if these things are his. For nowhere in Mary's childbearings is that read to have been said, *You are my Son, today I have begotten you*; or, *This is my most beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased*: but in his expiation at the Jordan. And if you too believe just as it is written, you will indeed already be a Matthean; for so I must say it: but catholic by no means. For we know the catholic faith; which is as far distant from this profession of Matthew as it is far from the truth. Since indeed your creed runs thus, that you believe in Jesus Christ the Son of God, who was born of the virgin Mary: it is yours, then, to take the Son of God from Mary; Matthew's, from the Jordan; ours, from God: and on this account Matthew, if these are for certain his, is as contrary to you in this profession of his, as to us; except that he was found a little more prudent than you, in that he ascribed the origin from the female sex to the son of David rather than to the Son of God. Wherefore of two things you must confess one: either that this is not Matthew, who seems to assert these things; or that you do not hold the apostolic faith.

**3.** But we—although no one at all should cast us down from this opinion, of taking the Son of God from God—yet, that we may grant much to imprudence, that we may be driven even to believe false things, Jesus the Son of God will have been made for us from the Jordan rather than born from a woman's womb. Although not even that very one whom Mary bore, if there was any, would rightly be called a son of David, unless it be established that he was sown by the father Joseph. Which, because you deny, you must necessarily confess this too, that not even he is the son of David. Since indeed the generation is drawn from Abraham to David, and from David all the way to Joseph, through all the Hebrew fathers, namely: of whom, because Jesus was not conceived, as is read, neither will he be the son of David; and far insane is he who, the one whom he was going to say was born of Mary without the intercourse of Joseph, set down at the beginning to be the son of David. Since, then, not even he who is begotten of Mary is rightly called the son of David, because he was not born of Joseph; how much more the Son of God?

**4.** Accordingly, neither is the origin of the virgin herself shown to have been from that tribe from which David is established to be—I mean Judah, from which were the kings of the Jews—but from the tribe of Levi, from which the priests: which is plain, because she had as father a certain priest named Joachim, of whom, however, no mention is made anywhere in this generation. How, then, will Mary be said to pertain to this lineage of Davidic kinship, in which you see her have neither father nor husband? And on this account neither will whoever is born of her be the son of David: unless you so apply this mother of his to Joseph that she be proved either his daughter or his spouse.

**5.** AUGUSTINE replied: The catholic faith—and likewise the apostolic—is that our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ is both the Son of God according to the divinity, and the son of David according to the flesh: which we so prove from the evangelical and apostolic writings that no one can contradict our proofs, unless one who contradicts the writings themselves: not as this Faustus set forth for himself, that I know not who said a few words, and afterward brought forth no testimonies against the cunning shifts of the same versatile Faustus. Which, when I have done it, nothing will remain for him to answer, except that by which he tries expressly to mock and evade the force of the most manifest truth in the holy Scriptures, namely, by answering that those things are false and inserted into the divine codices. Which madness, and frenzied presumption and audacity, I have already above in this work refuted, as much as seemed sufficient: nor is it needful to repeat the same, that we may also consider the measure of the discourse. What need is there, then, to seek and gather testimonies scattered through all the Scriptures, by which we might prove against this man, in the books of the greatest and divine authority, that the same is called the only-begotten Son of God, always God with God, who is also called the son of David on account of the form of a servant taken from the virgin Mary, the spouse of Joseph? Now for the moment, since he wished to dispute about Matthew, and the whole book of Matthew cannot be inserted by me into this discourse, let whoever wishes read, and see how Matthew, by narrating, leads the one whom he says his parents enumerate to be the son of David all the way to the passion and resurrection; nor does he say that any other than this same one was conceived and born of the virgin Mary by the Holy Spirit. To which thing he applies also a testimony from the prophet: *Behold, a virgin shall conceive in her womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which is interpreted: God with us*[^8]. And that this same one, baptized by John, heard from heaven: *This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased*[^9]: concerning whom it had already been said, from the virgin's childbearing, "God with us." Unless perhaps it seemed to Faustus less to be called God than to be called the Son of God. For from this he tried to conjecture that it seemed to Matthew that he was made the Son of God only from the baptism, because there the voice from heaven was made, *This is my Son*: when the same evangelist had already above applied the divine testimony from the prophet, where the very childbearing of the virgin was called "God with us."

**6.** This wretched, doting babbler we ought indeed to look at and observe, not omitting, where he could, to spread before the reader of his empty talk fogs of falsehood even from the testimonies of the Scriptures: as he said of Abraham, that he had not believed God promising him a son from Sarah, when he lay with the handmaid; though Scripture testifies that the childbearing of Sarah had not yet been promised to him[^10]: or that he lied that his wife was his sister; though he nowhere read Sarah's lineage in those writings to which credence is to be given on this matter[^11]: and about his son Isaac, that he too falsely said Rebecca was his sister; though her lineage is there openly written[^12]: and about Jacob, that there was a daily contest among his four wives, which should first seize him, coming from the field, for intercourse; which he is convicted of having nowhere there read at all. Behold what kind of man boasts that he hates lying writers of the divine books, who dares even concerning the evangelical summit of so great authority, known to all, so to lie that he wishes it to be thought that not Matthew—lest he be pressed down by the weight of the apostolic name—but I know not who else, under the name of Matthew, wrote concerning Christ what he is unwilling to believe, and what by slanderous craft he tries to refute.

**7.** Thus, then, it was said from heaven over the water of the Jordan, *This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased*; just as it was said also on the mountain[^13]. For not because there too that voice itself sounded from heaven was he not the Son of God before: since from the virgin's womb he took the form of a servant, who, *being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*[^14]. Finally, the same apostle Paul in another place says most openly: *But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law*[^15]—calling the woman a "woman" by the custom of Hebrew speech. He himself, then, is the Son of God, who is also the Lord of David according to the divinity; and the same is the son of David, of the seed of David according to the flesh. And if it did not profit us to believe this, the same Apostle would not so attentively commend it to Timothy, saying: *Remember that Christ Jesus rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my gospel*[^16]. Against which gospel whoever announces another, that he be anathema, with great care he admonished the faithful[^17].

**8.** What, then, should now disturb a follower of the holy Gospel, that Christ, born of the virgin without the intercourse of Joseph, is nonetheless called the son of David, since the evangelist Matthew leads the series of generations not all the way to Mary, but all the way to Joseph? First, because, as her husband, his person was the more to be honored on account of the male sex: for not because he was not mingled in intercourse is he therefore not a husband, since Matthew himself narrates that Mary was called his spouse by the angel—he who narrates that she conceived not by his intercourse, but of the Holy Spirit. But if not Matthew the apostle wrote these true things, but someone else under his name, as the Manicheans suppose, wrote them as false; would he so contradict himself in things most open and woven together so closely, that, the one whom he called the son of David, born of the virgin Mary without the intercourse of any man, enumerating his parents step by step, he should lead all the way, without any reason, to the one whom he himself had said was not mingled with Mary? For if one were to enumerate the forefathers of Christ from David to Joseph, calling him the son of David, and another were to say that he was born of the virgin Mary without the intercourse of any man, and not call him the son of David; not even so ought we at once to suppose that they spoke these things contrary to each other, so that either both or one of them were convicted of falsehood. For we ought to consider that it could be that both spoke true things, so that both Joseph was called the husband of Mary, having her as spouse continently, not by intercourse, but by affection; not by the mingling of bodies, but by the coupling—which is dearer—of minds: and that therefore the husband of the virgin mother of Christ ought not to be separated from the series of the parents of Christ; and that Mary herself drew some vein of blood from the stock of David, so that the flesh of Christ, even procreated from a virgin, could not be without the seed of David. But since one and the same narrator says both, commends both, both that Joseph was the husband of Mary and the virgin mother of Christ, and that Christ was of the seed of David and that Joseph was in the series of Christ's forefathers from David: what remains for one to believe who wishes to believe the divine Gospel rather than the fables of heretics, except both that Mary was not foreign to the kinship of David, and that she was not in vain called the spouse of Joseph, on account of the order of sex and the confederation of minds, although he was not mingled with her in the flesh; and that Joseph, rather on account of his male dignity, ought not to be separated from the order of those generations, lest by this very thing he seem separated from that woman to whom the affection of mind joined him; and lest the faithful of Christ should reckon that—which is the carnal mingling of spouses—so great a thing in marriage that they did not believe people to be spouses without it: but should rather learn that faithful marriages cleave to the members of Christ much more intimately, the more they could imitate the parents of Christ?

**9.** We, then, believe that Mary too was in the kinship of David, because we believe those Scriptures which say both, that Christ was of the seed of David according to the flesh[^18], and that his mother Mary was, not by lying with a man, but a virgin[^19]. Whoever, therefore, says that Mary did not pertain to the blood relationship of David, it is manifest that he struggles against the so excellent authority of these Scriptures: let him, then, prove that she did not pertain to the seed of David, and show this, not from any writings whatever, but from ecclesiastical, canonical, catholic ones. For others have with us no weight of authority on these matters: for those are the ones which the Church, spread throughout the whole world, receives and holds—the Church which was even prophesied through them; and, as it was promised, so rendered. And on this account that which Faustus set down concerning the generation of Mary, that she had a father from the tribe of Levi, a certain priest named Joachim, because it is not canonical, does not bind me: but even if I believed this, I would rather say that Joachim himself somehow pertained to the blood of David, and that somehow he was adopted from the tribe of Judah into the tribe of Levi, either he himself or some forefather of his, or at least was so born in the tribe of Levi that he drew some blood relationship from the stock of David: just as the same Faustus confesses it could have happened that Mary was of the tribe of Levi, who nevertheless, it is established, was handed over to a husband who was of the stock of David—that is, of the tribe of Judah; and thus he says it could be taken that Christ was the son of David, if Mary had been the daughter of Joseph. Accordingly, if the daughter of Joseph had married into the tribe of Levi, not absurdly would whoever was born of her, even in the tribe of Levi, be called the son of David: so, if the mother of that Joachim whom Faustus mentions as the father of Mary, of the tribe of Judah and the lineage of David, married into the tribe of Levi, not undeservedly are Joachim too, and Mary, and the son of Mary even so truly declared to be of the seed of David. This, then, or some such thing, I would rather believe, if I were held by the authority of that apocryphal writing in which Joachim is read as the father of Mary, than that the Gospel should lie, in which it is written both that Jesus Christ the Son of God our Savior was of the seed of David according to the flesh, and procreated through the virgin Mary. It suffices for us, then, that the Scriptures which say this, and which we believe, cannot be convicted of any falsehood by their enemies.

**10.** Let him not, then, say to me in turn: "Even if I do not show that Mary was not of the kinship of David, do you show that she was from there." For this I show by a most clear and plainly most strong proof, that the Scripture of most firmly established authority both says that Christ was of the seed of David, and that his mother was the virgin Mary, without anyone's intercourse. But how reverently Faustus seemed to be a detester of a certain as it were baseness, when he said: "In vain do you heap a slander upon the writer, as though he shut up the Son of God in a woman's womb!" The catholic faith plainly, which believes Christ the Son of God born according to the flesh of a virgin, in no way so shuts up the same Son of God in a woman's womb, as though he were not outside, as though he deserted the administration of heaven and earth, as though he withdrew from the Father: but you, Manicheans, with that heart by which you can think nothing but corporeal phantasms, do not at all grasp these things—how the Word of God, the Power and Wisdom of God, both remaining in himself and with the Father, and ruling the whole creation, reaches mightily from end to end, and disposes all things sweetly[^20]. In the marvelous and ineffable ease of which disposition he disposed for himself a mother too on earth, and, for the sake of his servants to be freed from the slavery of corruption, took in her the form of a servant—that is, a mortal body; showed it taken; and, when it was shown and laid low by death, raised it up by the resurrection, and, like a temple destroyed, built it again. You, nevertheless, who dread to believe these things as if sacrilegious, shut up the members of your god, not in the womb of a virgin, but in the wombs of all carnal females, from elephants to flies. Or does the true Christ therefore seem to you more vile, because we say the Word was so made flesh in the virgin's womb that, by no change of himself, remaining inviolably in his proper nature, he fitted to himself a man as a temple; and is therefore your god dear to you, because, bound by so many chains of flesh and defiled in that part in which he is also to be fixed in the globe, he begs for relief without cause, or even, utterly oppressed, is not allowed to beg?
