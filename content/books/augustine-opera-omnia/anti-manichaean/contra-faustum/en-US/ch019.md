# Book XVIII

**1.** FAUSTUS said: *I came not to abolish the Law, but to fulfill it*[^1]. But to believe that this was said by Christ—unless perhaps it means something else—you should know is no less contrary to you than to me. For each of us is a Christian under this opinion, because we have supposed that Christ came for the destruction of the Law and the Prophets. And if for the moment you are unwilling to confess it in word, you nevertheless indicate it by your works. For from this it comes that you yourself too despise the precepts of the Law and the Prophets; from this that both of us confess that Jesus established the New Testament—by which what else do we confess but the destruction of the Old Testament? Since this is so, how shall we believe that Christ said this, unless we first condemn ourselves of a foolish opinion in the past, and have recourse to repentance, and obey the Law afresh and the Prophets, and take care to observe their commandments, of whatever sort they may be? And when we have done this, then at last we shall truly have believed that Jesus said he came not to abolish the Law but to fulfill it. But as it is, it is false, because not even you believe it—of which you accuse me alone.

**2.** But be it so; suppose it was permitted to have erred in the past. What now, at last? Is it your pleasure to go under the Law, if Christ did not so much abolish it as fulfill it? Is it your pleasure to be circumcised—that is, to mark the private parts on the private parts—and to believe that God delights in such sacraments? Is it your pleasure to take up the leisure of the Sabbaths, and to insert your hands into the chains of Saturn? Is it your pleasure, to glut the appetite of a Jewish demon (for it is not God's), to slaughter with knives now bulls, now rams, now even goats—not to say men too?—and, on account of which we hate idols, to practice that now more cruelly under the Prophets and the Law? Is it your pleasure, finally, to reckon certain of these deadly foods clean, and to hold others among the unclean and contaminated, of which the Law and the Prophets assert pork to be the more defiling?

You will surely deny that any of these things must be done by us if we wish to persevere in being what we are: since indeed you hear Christ saying that he who has been circumcised becomes doubly a son of Gehenna[^2]. You see, too, that he himself neither kept the Sabbath nor anywhere commanded it to be kept. About foods likewise you hear him asserting that a man is defiled by none of the things that enter the mouth, but rather that the things which proceed from the mouth pollute him[^3]. About sacrifices likewise his frequent statement is that God wishes mercy, not sacrifice[^4]. If, then, these things are so, where will that be—that he came not to abolish the Law and the Prophets, but to fulfill them? And if he said it, either he said it meaning something else, or—which be far from us—he said it lying, or he did not say it at all. But that Jesus lied, let no one say, at least no Christian; and so either it was said otherwise, or it was not said at all.

**3.** And yet, for my part, the Manichean faith has already made me safe against the constraint of this chapter—the faith which from the beginning persuaded me not to believe indiscriminately all the things that are read as written in the Savior's name, but to test whether they are true, whether sound, whether uncorrupted: for there are very many tares which a certain night-roaming sower has scattered, to the contamination of the good seed, among almost all the Scriptures[^5]. And therefore not even here would this statement frighten me, although it carries before it the inscription of a revered name; because it is still permitted me, on principle, to test whether this too is the daytime and good sower's, or that nocturnal and most wicked one's. But you, who believe everything rashly, who condemn reason, nature's gift, out of men—you, for whom to judge between true and false is a scruple, and for whom to separate good from its contrary is no less a terror than madness is to infants—what will you do, when necessity drives you into the strait of this chapter? I mean, when a Jew, or someone else not ignorant of this statement, asks you why you do not keep the precepts of the Law and the Prophets, since Christ says he came not to abolish them but to fulfill them? You will surely be compelled either to succumb to a vain superstition, or to profess the chapter false, or to deny that you are a disciple of Christ.

**4.** AUGUSTINE replied: Since you repeat things already so often hissed off and convicted, we too, by whom we convicted them, will not be loath to repeat. Christians do not perform those things out of the Law and the Prophets by which were signified these things they do perform. For those were figures of things to come, which, once the things themselves had been revealed and made present through Christ, had to be removed—so that by this very fact too, that these were removed, the Law and the Prophets might be fulfilled.

For there too it is written that God would give a New Testament: *not such as I gave to their fathers*[^6]. For that people, on account of their stony heart, had received many precepts more suited to themselves than good, by which, nonetheless, things to come might be figured and prophesied; but at that time they were observed by men who did not understand them. But when the things signified to them came and were laid open, those things are no longer commanded to be done, but are read to be understood.

Hence there it is said, concerning this future matter too: *I will take from them the heart of stone, and give them a heart of flesh*[^7]—that is, not a heart without sense, but a heart with sense. Whence the Apostle drew what he says: *not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshly tablets of the heart*[^8]. For what else did he say but "a heart of flesh"? Since, then, this too had been foretold, it would rather be the case that, if these things were not removed from our observance, the Law and the Prophets would not be fulfilled, because what they foretold would not come to pass; but when this too happens, by that very thing they are understood to be fulfilled, by which they seem to you not to be fulfilled.

**5.** Nor does your insult frighten us, that you call the leisure of the Sabbaths "the chains of Saturn." For it is vain and silly; nor would it have come into your mind to say this, were it not that you worship the sun on the day they call the Sun's day. But just as we call that same day the Lord's day, and on it venerate not this sun but the resurrection of the Lord, so the leisure of the Sabbaths was observed by the fathers without any veneration of Saturn, at the time when it had to be observed thus: for it was a shadow of things to come, as the Apostle bears witness[^9]. For on these days, whose sevenfold number recurs in a cycle, the gentiles imposed the names of their gods—of whom the Apostle says that *they worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*[^10]. In this respect you too imitate them, except that with them you adore the two brighter luminaries, though not the other stars with them. But they imposed the names of their gods on the months also. For in honor of Romulus, since they believed him the son of Mars, they dedicated the first month to Mars and called it March. And then April—not from the name of any god of theirs, but from the thing itself, as it were Aperilis, because then most of the bud opens (aperitur) into flower. Then the third month, May, because they worship Maia, the mother of Mercury, as a goddess. Then the fourth, June, from Juno. Then the rest, up to December, they named from numbers. But of these, Quintilis and Sextilis were named after the names of men to whom they had decreed divine honors, July and August: for the seventh, September, and the rest, as I said, up to December, are designated by names of numbers in order. Further, January was named from Janus, February from the Februa, the sacred rites of the Luperci. Do you wish, then, that you too be said to worship Mars in the month of March? For in that month you celebrate your Bema with great festivity. But if you suppose it is permitted to you in the month of March to consider something else, not Mars, why do you try to import Saturn into the divine Scriptures from the seventh day—which was named the Sabbath from rest—because the gentiles called that day Saturn's day? Surely you now see with how great impiety you rave.

**6.** As for the sacrifices of animals, who of us is unaware that they were imposed as suited to a perverse people rather than offered to a God who desired them? But nonetheless even in these there were figures of us; for our cleansing, and God's propitiation toward us, is impossible without blood. But the truth of those figures is Christ, by whose blood we have been redeemed and cleansed. For in the figures of the divine words, he was called a bull on account of the power of the cross, with whose horns he tossed the impious; and a ram, on account of the primacy of innocence; and a goat, on account of the likeness of sinful flesh, that from sin he might condemn sin[^11]; and whatever other kind of sacrifice you mention more explicitly, in that too I will show you that Christ was foretold. Wherefore, whether circumcision, or the Sabbath, or the distinction of foods, or the immolation of sacrifices—all these were figures of us and prophecies, which Christ came not to abolish but to fulfill, since he fulfilled the things that were foretold by them. Consider whom you contradict: with the Apostle, from the Apostle I say it: *All these things were figures of us*[^12].

**7.** For just as Mani taught you an impious perversity, that from the Gospel, which does not hinder your heresy, you accept this, but what hinders it you do not accept, so the Apostle taught us a devout provision: that whoever announces to us beside what we have received, let him be anathema[^13]. Hence the catholic Christians number you too among the tares, because the Lord explained what the tares are—not certain false things inserted into the true Scriptures, as you interpret it, but men, sons of the evil one, that is, imitators of diabolical falsehood[^14]. Nor do they believe everything rashly; and therefore they certainly do not believe Mani and the other heretics. Nor do they condemn reason out of men; rather, what you call reason they convict of being error. Nor do they think it impious to judge between true and false; therefore they judge your sect most false, but the catholic faith most true. Nor do they fear to separate good from its contrary; rather, they understand that evil is not a nature, since it is against nature—not some nation of darkness or other, born and rebelling from its own beginning against the divine realms, which truly brought a greater terror upon your god than madness brings to infants; for you say that he, lest he behold his own members seized and laid waste by its onset, set up a veil against himself.

Accordingly, they suffer no straits from this chapter, as though they did not keep the precepts of the Law and the Prophets: for both, from the grace of Christ, they have the lawful love of God and neighbor, on which two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets depend[^15]; and whatever things there were figuratively prophesied—whether in deeds done, or in the celebrations of sacraments, or in modes of speech—they recognize as fulfilled in Christ and the Church. Whence we neither succumb to a vain superstition, nor say that the evangelical chapter is false, nor deny that we are Christ's disciples: because, by that reasoning of the truth which I have so often set forth according to my strength, he came not to abolish, but to fulfill, no other Law and no other Prophets than those which the catholic authority holds.
