# Book 2

**1.** Faustus said: Do you accept the Gospel?

Indeed, above all.

Then accordingly do you also accept that Christ was born?

It is not so. For it does not follow that, if I accept the Gospel, I therefore also accept that Christ was born.

Why?

Because the Gospel began both to exist and to be named from the preaching of Christ, in which, however, he nowhere says that he was born of men. But the genealogy is so far from being the Gospel that not even its writer dared to call it the Gospel. For what did he write? *The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David*[^1]. Not, then, the book of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, but the book of the generation—indeed, where a star too is introduced that confirms a horoscope[^2], so that this might more rightly be called a "little Genesis" than a Gospel. And then Mark, who did not trouble to write a generation but only the preaching of the Son of God, which is the Gospel—see how fittingly he began: *The Gospel*, he says, *of Jesus Christ, the Son of God*[^3]. So that from this it appears more than abundantly that the genealogy is not the Gospel. For in Matthew himself, after John had been shut up in prison, then it is read that Jesus began to preach the Gospel of the kingdom[^4]. Therefore whatever was narrated before this is established to be genealogy, not Gospel. Otherwise, what was to stop Matthew too from putting "the Gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God"—unless he thought it dishonest to call a genealogy the Gospel?

Therefore, if it is now sufficiently clear to you, what until now you did not know, that the Gospel is something far other than the genealogy, then know that I accept, as I said, the Gospel—that is, the preaching of Christ. About this, whatever you wish to question further, ask, leaving aside the generations. Or if you have a mind to confer about these too, I do not in fact refuse, since I have great abundance for answering about them as well; but you, learn to ask what comes first. For you seem to me now to want to know, not whether I accept the Gospel, but whether I accept the generations.

**2.** Augustine replied: You certainly questioned yourself, as though in our person, whether you accepted the Gospel; and you answered: Above all. And again you asked whether you accepted that Christ was born; and you answered: It is not so—adding this reason, namely, that the generation of Christ does not pertain to the Gospel. What then will you answer to the Apostle when he says: *Remember that Jesus Christ rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel*[^5]? You certainly see how you do not know, or pretend not to know, what the Gospel is; and you name the Gospel not from apostolic teaching but from your own error. But if you call "Gospel" what the Apostles called Gospel, you stray from the Gospel, since you do not believe that Christ is of the seed of David—which the Apostle testified is preached according to his Gospel. And what was the Gospel of the apostle Paul was also that of the other Apostles and of all the faithful stewards of so great a mystery. For he says elsewhere: *Whether then it was I or they, so we preach, and so you believed*[^6]. For not all of them wrote a Gospel, yet all of them preached the Gospel. Indeed, those who recounted the origin, the deeds, the words, and the sufferings of our Lord Jesus Christ were properly called Evangelists. For if we ask what the very name means when rendered in Latin: Gospel means "good tidings" or "good announcement." And although this can always be said whenever something good is announced, the word has nonetheless properly come to be used for that announcement of the Savior of which I spoke. But if you announce something else, it is plain that you are outside the Gospel.

Surely the little ones whom you call half-Christians are set against you, if they hear the voice of mother love sounding for them from the apostolic mouth: *If anyone announces to you anything other than what we announced to you, let him be accursed*[^7]. Since, then, Paul himself announced according to his Gospel that Christ is of the seed of David, you who deny this and announce something else, may you be accursed. And who does not see with how great a blindness it is said that Christ nowhere says he was born of men, when he almost never keeps silent about his being the Son of man?

**3.** But evidently, most learned men, you bring out to us from your storehouse some first man or other, who descended from the race of light to do battle against the race of darkness, armed with his own waters against the enemy's waters, and with his own fire against the enemy's fire, and with his own winds against the enemy's winds. Why, then, not also with his own smoke against the enemy's smoke, and with his own darkness against the enemy's darkness? Instead, against the smoke he was armed, as you say, with air, and against the darkness with light. Or is it because smoke and darkness are evil that his goodness could not have them? Then those three—water, wind, fire—are good. Why, then, could the malice of the opposing race have them? Here you answer: But that water of the race of darkness was evil, whereas the water the first man brought was good; and that one's wind was evil, but this one's good; and so this one's fire too, being good, fought against that one's evil fire. Why, then, could he not also bring good smoke against evil smoke? Or do your lies, like the smoke itself, vanish and fail in the smoke?

Surely your first man waged war against a contrary nature. Why, against those five elements which you invent in the contrary race, was one contrary thing brought from the divine realms—light against darkness? For the other four are not contraries. For air is not contrary to smoke, much less is water to water, wind to wind, and fire to fire.

**4.** And now, who could listen to those sacrilegious ravings of yours, that your first man, in accordance with the will of the enemy, in order to capture them, changed and turned the elements he carried, so that the realm which you call the realm of falsehood, remaining in its own nature, did not fight deceitfully, while the substance of truth appeared mutable, in order to deceive? You want our Lord Jesus Christ to be believed the son of this first man. You call truth the daughter of this fabricated tale. This first man whom you praise, because he fought against the opposing race with mutable and lying forms—if you tell the truth, you do not imitate him; but if you do imitate him, then you too are lying. Therefore our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, the true and truthful Son of God, the true and truthful son of man—both of which he himself testifies concerning himself—drew from true God the eternity of his divinity, and from true man the origin of his flesh.

Apostolic teaching knows nothing of your first man. Hear the apostle Paul: *The first man*, he says, *was of the earth, earthy; the second man is from heaven, heavenly. As was the earthy, such are they also that are earthy; and as is the heavenly, such are they also that are heavenly. As we have borne the image of the earthy, let us also bear the image of him who is from heaven*[^8]. So the first man, of the earth, earthy, is that Adam formed from the mud; but the second man, from heaven, heavenly, is the Lord Jesus Christ, because the Son of God came to the flesh, by assuming which he might both become man outwardly and remain God inwardly—so that he might both be the true Son of God through whom we were made, and become the true son of man through whom we were remade.

Why, then, do you bring forth your fabricated first man from who knows where, and refuse to acknowledge the first man whom apostolic teaching teaches? Or is it that there may be fulfilled in you what the Apostle himself says: *turning away their hearing from the truth, but turning aside to fables*[^9]? Paul announces a first man of the earth, earthy; Mani announces a first man not earthy, clothed in who knows what deceptive five elements. And Paul says: *If anyone announces to you anything other than what we announced to you, let him be accursed*[^10]. Therefore, lest Paul be a liar, let Mani be accursed.

5.1. But that you also slander the star by which the Magi were led to adore the infant Christ—you are not ashamed of this, although you place your own fabricated Christ, the son of your fabricated first man, not under the testimony of a star, but say that he is bound up in all the stars; because, you believe, he was mingled with the princes of darkness in that war in which your first man fought with the race of darkness, so that from those very princes of darkness, captured by such a mingling, the world might be fashioned. Hence these sacrilegious ravings compel you to say that Christ is fixed and bound and concreted not only in heaven and in all the stars, but also in the earth and in all things that are born from it—no longer your Savior, but one to be saved by you when you eat him and belch him up. 5.2. For deceived by this impious vanity, you also deceive your Hearers into bringing you food, so that the Christ bound in it may be helped through your teeth and bellies. For you preach that by such aid he is loosed and freed; and not even the whole of him, but you maintain that even still some scanty and filthy remnants of him remain in excrement, so that they are held entangled and implicated again and again in various corporeal forms; and that if, while the world stands, they cannot be loosed and purified, then by that final fire with which the world itself will burn[^11] they may be loosed and purified. And yet not even then, you say, can the whole Christ be freed, but the very last and remaining particles of his good and divine nature, which are so defiled that they can in no way be washed clean, are condemned forever, fixed to the dreadful clod of darkness.

See those who feign indignation that injury is done to the Son of God, because a star is said to have shown him as born, as though his birth were set under a fateful constellation—while they themselves set him not only under the conjunction of the stars, but in the bonds of all earthly things, and in the saps of all herbs, and in the rottenness of all flesh, and in the corruption of all foods, so bound and polluted that, in great part though not in whole, he cannot be loosed and purified unless he is belched up even from leeks and little roots, and that by men—namely, the Elect of the Manicheans.

5.3. We, for our part, place the horoscope of no man under the fate of the stars, so that, on account of the just judgment of God, we may vindicate from every bond of necessity the free choice of the will, by which one lives either well or badly. How much less, then, do we believe that the temporal generation of him who is the eternal Creator and Lord of all things was made under the condition of the stars? And so that star which the Magi saw, when Christ was born according to the flesh, did not rule over him by decree, but served as a testimony; it did not subject him to its command, but pointed him out in homage. Accordingly this star was not one of those which from the beginning of creation keep the order of their courses under the Creator's law; but by the new birth from a Virgin a new star appeared, which performed the ministry of its office even to the Magi themselves as they sought Christ, when it went before their face, until by going ahead it led them all the way to the very place where the Word of God was an infant. What astrologers, after all, have so set the fates of men being born under the stars as to assert that some star, when some man was born, left the order of its circuit and went over to him who was born? For they suppose that the lot of one being born is bound to the order of the stars, not that the order of the stars can be changed on account of the day of a man's birth.

Therefore, if that star was one of those which run their courses in the heavens, how could it decree what the newborn Christ was going to do, when at Christ's birth it was ordered to leave what it was doing? But if, as is more probably believed, it arose to point out Christ, and did not exist before, then Christ was not born because it appeared, but rather it appeared because Christ was born. Hence, if it had to be put so, we would say that the star was not the fate of Christ, but Christ the fate of the star. For he gave to it, not it to him, the cause of arising.

If, then, there are fates, which are named from *fari*, that is, from speaking—since Christ is the Word of God, in whom all things were spoken before they existed—then the company of the stars is not the fate of Christ, but Christ is the fate even of the stars: he who assumed the very flesh created under heaven by that will with which he also created heaven, and laid it down and took it up again by that power with which he also commanded the stars.

**6.** Why, then, should what is narrated about this generation not be the Gospel, since something so good is announced to us by it, that through it our infirmity is healed? Is it because Matthew did not begin by saying, *The beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ*[^12], as Mark says, but, *The book of the generation of Jesus Christ*[^13]? In this way, neither would John be said to have written a Gospel, since he too does not say "The beginning of the Gospel," or "The book of the Gospel," but, *In the beginning was the Word*[^14]. Unless perhaps Faustus was so elegant a fabricator of words that he would name this beginning of John's too a "little Word-book," on account of the Word, just as he dared to call the other a "little Genesis," on account of the horoscope. But why do you not rather consider with what impudence you call those long and impious fables of yours the Gospel? For what good is announced there, where it is said that God could not otherwise provide for and look after his kingdom against some rebellious, contrary, and alien nature, except by sending part of his own nature into its greedy jaws to be devoured, and so polluted that, after such labors and torments, it could not even be wholly purified? Is then so bad a tiding the Gospel? Surely all who know Greek even slightly translate "Gospel" as "good tidings" or "good announcement." But how is this a good tiding, when God himself is announced to you as mourning behind a veil set before him, until his members are restored and purified from that devastation and contamination? And if he should ever end his mourning, he will be cruel. For what evil did that part of him deserve from him, which will be bound in the clod—which surely must be mourned forever, since it will be condemned forever? But we have escaped: for whoever attends carefully to this tiding is not compelled to mourn that it is bad, but to laugh that it is false.
