# Book 12

**1.** Faustus said: Why do you not accept the Prophets?

Rather, you say instead, if you have anything, why we ought to accept the Prophets.

On account of the testimonies, you say, which they uttered beforehand about Christ.

I, for my part, have found none, although I have read them quite attentively and most carefully. But yet this too is the confession of an enfeebled faith—not to believe in Christ without a witness and an argument. Surely you yourselves are wont to teach that nothing is to be sought out more curiously, because Christian belief is simple and absolute. How, then, do you now destroy the simplicity of faith, by propping it up with proofs and witnesses, and that to the Jews? Or if the first position displeases you, and you therefore pass over to the other: who ought to be a more trustworthy witness to you than God himself concerning his Son, who, not through a seer, nor through an interpreter, but with a voice that of itself burst forth from heaven, said, when he sent him to the earth: *This is my Son, the most beloved; believe him*[^1]?

And also he himself about himself: *I proceeded from my Father, and came into this world*[^2]; and many other things of this kind. At which the Jews, snarling, said: *You testify about yourself; your testimony is not true.* To whom he himself said: *Even if I testify about myself, my testimony is true, because I am not alone. For in your Law too it is written: The testimony of two men is true. I am one who testifies about myself, and the Father who sent me testifies about me*[^3]: he did not say: The Prophets. To this he also calls his very works as testimony for himself: *If you do not believe me*, he says, *believe the works*[^4]. He did not say: If you do not believe me, believe the Prophets.

Therefore we need no testimonies about our Savior: we seek in the Prophets only examples of an honorable life, and prudence and virtue—none of which, I perceive, was in the Jewish seers, since it has not escaped you; for when I asked you why you thought they were to be accepted, you did not unwarily or discourteously pass over their works in silence, leaping only to their predictions; forgetting, of course, that it is written that a grape is never gathered from thorns, nor a fig from thistles[^5]. Therefore let me reply to your question, briefly and soberly for the time, by which you ask why we do not accept the Prophets. For the rest, that they prophesied nothing about Christ has already been amply shown in the books of our parents. But I will add this: that if the Hebrew seers, knowing and preaching Christ, lived so disgracefully, it can rightly be said of them too what Paul testifies of the wise of the Gentiles: *That when they had known God, they did not glorify him as God, or give thanks; but became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^6]. You see, then, that it is no great thing to have known great things, unless you have lived according to their dignity.

**2.** Augustine replied: By all these words Faustus is at pains to make us believe that the Hebrew Prophets neither foretold anything about Christ, nor, if they did foretell it, that their testimonies profit us, nor that those men themselves lived according to the dignity of those same testimonies. We, then, will demonstrate both their predictions about Christ, and how much provision has been made for us through them for the truth and firmness of faith, and that they lived fittingly and aptly in accordance with their prophecy. In this threefold disputation, what I set first it would be long to prove by bringing forward testimonies from all those books, by which I might show that Christ was foretold: but I will crush this man's frivolity with the weightiest weight of authority.

For he so does not accept the Hebrew prophets that he nonetheless professes that he accepts the Apostles. Let us hear, then, what the apostle Paul—about whom, when he had asked himself as though with an opposing voice whether he accepted him, he replied, "Indeed, above all"[^7]—says about their prophecy. *Paul*, he says, *a servant of Jesus Christ, called an apostle, set apart for the Gospel of God, which he had promised beforehand through his Prophets in the holy Scriptures, concerning his Son, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^8]. What more does he want? Unless perhaps he wishes this to be understood as said about some other prophets, not about our Hebrews? But although it were said through whatever prophets you please, yet about that Son of God the Gospel was promised, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh; into which Gospel the Apostle says he was set apart: and let this be contrary to the faithlessness of these men, that according to this Gospel we believe the Son of God was made of the seed of David according to the flesh; let them yet recognize more open things, in which the most evident testimony is borne to the Hebrew prophets through that Apostle, by whose authority the necks of these proud men may be broken.

**3.** *I speak the truth in Christ*, he says, *I do not lie, my conscience bearing me witness in the Holy Spirit, that there is to me great sorrow and continual grief to my heart. For I myself wished to be accursed from Christ for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh, who are Israelites; whose is the adoption, and the glory, and the covenants, and the giving of the Law, and the worship, and the promises; whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all things, God blessed forever*[^9].

What can be said more abundantly, what declared more expressly, what commended more holily? For what is the adoption of the Israelites, except through the Son of God? Hence he says to the Galatians: *But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law, that he might redeem those who were under the Law, that we might receive the adoption of sons*[^10]. And what is their glory, except especially that of which the same Paul says to the same Romans: *What advantage, then, has the Jew? Or what profit is there in circumcision? Much in every way. First indeed, because to them were entrusted the oracles of God*[^11]?

Let these men seek what are the oracles of God entrusted to the Jews, and show us others besides those of the Hebrew Prophets. And now, why did he say that the covenants pertain especially to the Israelites, except because both the Old Testament was given to them, and the New was figured in the Old? But the giving of the Law, which was given to the Israelites—not understanding its dispensation, because God now does not want us to be under the Law, but under grace—these men are wont to reproach with furious lack of skill.

Let them yield, then, to the apostolic authority, which, praising and commending the excellence of the Israelites, counted this too among it, that theirs is the giving of the Law. If it were evil, it surely would not be set among their praises. But if it did not preach Christ, the Lord himself would not say: *If you believed Moses, you would believe me also; for he wrote about me*[^12]; nor would he bear witness to it thus after the resurrection, saying: *It was necessary that all things be fulfilled which were written in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me*[^13].

**4.** But because the Manicheans preach another Christ, not the one whom the Apostles preached, but their own deceitful one deceitfully, the followers of whose falsehood themselves lie fittingly—except that they impudently want to be believed, when they profess themselves the disciples of a liar—there has befallen them what the Apostle says of the very unbelieving Jews: *When Moses is read, a veil is over their heart*; for this veil, through which they do not understand Moses, is not taken away unless they pass over to Christ: not such as they themselves have fashioned, but such as the Hebrew Fathers prophesied. For the same Apostle says thus: *But when you pass over to the Lord, the veil will be taken away*[^14].

For it is no wonder that these men refuse to believe Christ, who rose and said: *It was necessary that all things be fulfilled which were written in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me*: since Christ himself narrated what Abraham said to a certain merciless rich man, when he was tormented in the underworld and asked that someone be sent from there to his brothers, by whom they might be taught not to come, themselves too, into that place of torments. For this was said to him: *They have there Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them.* And when he had said that they would not believe unless someone rose from the dead, it was most truly answered: *If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they believe if someone rises from the dead*[^15]. Therefore these men, not hearing Moses and the Prophets, do not believe—not only in Christ rising from the dead, but that Christ rose from the dead at all. For how do they believe him to have risen, whom they do not believe to have died? For how do they believe him to have died, whom they do not believe to have had a mortal body?

**5.** But we do not believe them, the deceitful preachers, not of that Christ who was deceitful, but of one who did not exist at all. For we have a true and truthful Christ, foretold by the Prophets, preached by the Apostles, who adduce the testimonies of their preaching from the Law and the Prophets, as they show in innumerable places. This Paul embraced most briefly and most truly, saying: *But now, without the Law, the righteousness of God has been made manifest, witnessed by the Law and the Prophets*[^16]. What Prophets, except the Israelites, whose he said most plainly were both the covenants, and the giving of the Law, and the promises[^17]? The promises about whom, except about Christ? Which he briefly defines in another place, when he speaks of Christ, saying: *For as many as are the promises of God, in him they are yea*[^18]. Paul tells me that the giving of the Law is the Israelites'. He also says: *For Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness to everyone who believes*[^19]. He also says, speaking of Christ: *For as many as are the promises of God, in him they are yea*: and you tell me that the Israelite Prophets foretold nothing about Christ! What, then, remains, except that I choose whether I should believe Mani, telling a vain and long fable against Paul; or Paul, forewarning: *If anyone preaches to you a gospel other than what we preached to you, let him be accursed*[^20]?

**6.** Here perhaps they may say: Show us where Christ was foretold by the Israelite Prophets. As though that authority were a small thing, by which the Apostles say that the things we read in the writings of the Hebrew Prophets were completed in Christ; or that which the Lord himself says, that those things were written about him. Accordingly, whoever cannot show this does not himself understand: it is not the Apostles or Christ or the sacred manuscripts that lie. But yet, not to gather many things, and to recall this one—what the Apostle says consequently in the same passage: *But the word of God cannot fail. For not all who are of Israel are Israelites; nor are all sons who are the seed of Abraham; but: In Isaac shall your seed be called: that is, not those who are the sons of the flesh, these are the sons of God; but the sons of the promise are counted as the seed*[^21].

What will they answer to this, when it is plainly said about this seed to Abraham in another place: *In your seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed*[^22]? For if we were arguing this at the time when the Apostle was expounding it, saying: *To Abraham were the promises made, and to his seed: he does not say, And to seeds, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^23], perhaps someone would not so impudently be unwilling to believe this, before he saw all the nations believing in Christ, who is preached as of the seed of Abraham. But now, when we see this being fulfilled, which we read foretold so long before, when all the nations are now being blessed in the seed of Abraham, to whom it was said thousands of years ago: *In your seed shall all the nations be blessed*: who would so stubbornly be insane as either to try to bring in another Christ, who is not of the seed of Abraham; or to think that the Hebrew Prophecies foretold nothing about this true Christ, of whose nation Abraham is the father?

**7.** But who can recount—I do not say in one brief reply, such as we are constrained to have in this work, but in any vast volume whatever—all the proclamations of the Hebrew Prophets about our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ? Since all things that are contained in those books were either said about him or on account of him. But for the exercise of the seeker and the delight of the finder, far more things there are insinuated through allegories and riddles, partly conveyed by words alone, partly even narrated as deeds. Yet unless some things there were manifest, there could not be grasped the sense by which even the obscure things grow clear. And yet, even from those things which are wrapped in figures, if some are set down as though woven together under one view, they so join their voices in bearing witness to Christ that the deafness of any dull man would blush.

**8.** In Genesis God completed all his works in six days, and rested on the seventh[^24]. The works of God mark the human race in this world by six ages through the successions of times: of which the first is from Adam to Noah; the second, from Noah to Abraham; the third, from Abraham to David; the fourth, from David to the migration into Babylon; the fifth, from there to the humble coming of our Lord Jesus Christ; the sixth, which is now being passed, until the Most High comes to judgment; but the seventh is understood in the rest of the saints, not in this life, but in another, where the rich man saw the poor man resting, when he was tormented in the underworld[^25]; where no evening comes, because there is no failing of things there.

On the sixth day in Genesis man is formed to the image of God[^26]: in the sixth age of the world our reformation is made manifest in the newness of the mind, according to the image of him who created us, as the Apostle says[^27]. A spouse is made for the sleeping man from his side[^28]: the Church is made for the dying Christ from the mystery of the blood that flowed from the side of the dead one[^29]. She who was made from the side of her husband is called Eve, life and mother of the living: and the Lord says in the Gospel: *If anyone does not eat my flesh and drink my blood, he will not have life in himself*[^30].

And all the things that are read there, handled in detail and minutely, speak beforehand of Christ and the Church, whether in good Christians or in bad. For it was not in vain that the Apostle said: *Adam, who is a figure of him who was to come*[^31]; and that: *A man shall leave father and mother, and cleave to his wife; and the two shall be in one flesh. This*, he says, *is a great mystery; but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^32]. For who would not recognize that Christ in this way left his Father, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant[^33]: that he also left his mother, the synagogue of the Jews, clinging carnally to the Old Testament, and cleaved to his wife, the holy Church, that in the peace of the New Testament the two might be in one flesh? Because, though he is God with the Father, through whom we were made, he became, through the flesh, a partaker of us, that we might be able to be the body of that head.

**9.** Just as Cain's sacrifice from the fruits of the earth is rejected, while Abel's sacrifice from sheep and their fat is accepted; so the faith of the New Testament, praising God out of the innocence of grace, is preferred to the earthly works of the Old Testament: because, even though the Jews formerly did those things rightly, yet in this they are guilty of faithlessness, that, with Christ coming, they did not distinguish the time of the New Testament from the time of the Old. For God said to Cain: *If you offer rightly, but do not divide rightly, you have sinned.* If he had obeyed God, who said: *Be still; for its turning is to you, and you shall have dominion over it*, he would have turned his sin toward himself, attributing it to himself, and confessing to God; and so, helped by the grace of indulgence, he himself would have had dominion over his sin; and not, with sin having dominion over him, a slave of sin, would he have killed his innocent brother[^34].

So too the Jews, in whom these things were carried on as a figure, if they had been still from their disturbance, and, recognizing the time of salvation through grace unto the forgiveness of sins, had heard Christ saying: *The healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are ill: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance*[^35], and: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*; and: *If the Son frees you, then you will be truly free*[^36]: they would have turned their sin toward themselves in confession, as it is written in the Psalm, saying to the physician: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^37]; and they would, as free men through the hope of grace, have had dominion over that same sin, as long as it was still in their mortal body. But now, ignorant of God's righteousness, and wishing to establish their own[^38], lifted up by the works of the Law, not humbled over their sins, they were not still: and with sin reigning in their mortal body so as to obey its desires[^39], they stumbled on the stone of stumbling[^40], and blazed up with hatred against him,

whose works, seeing them accepted by God, they grieved over, while the man born blind who now saw said to them: *We know that God does not hear sinners; but whoever worships him, and does his will, him he hears*[^41]: as though he said this to them: He does not look upon the sacrifice of Cain, but he looks upon the sacrifice of Abel. And so Abel, the younger born, is killed by his elder brother: Christ, the head of the younger people, is killed by the Jewish people, the elder born: the one in the field, the other in the place of Calvary.

**10.** God asks Cain where his brother is—not as one ignorant who learns from him, but as a judge who punishes the guilty. He replies that he does not know, and that he is not his keeper. To this very day, what do the Jews answer us, when we question them with the voice of God, that is, with the voice of the holy Scriptures, about Christ, except that they do not know the Christ whom we name? For the ignorance of Cain is deceitful; the denial of the Jews is false. But they would in a manner be the keepers of Christ, if they had been willing to accept and keep the Christian faith. For he who keeps Christ in his heart does not say what Cain said: *Am I my brother's keeper?* God says to Cain: *What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries to me from the earth*[^42]. Thus the divine voice in the holy Scriptures accuses the Jews. For the blood of Christ has a great voice on the earth, when, received from all the nations, the answer is: Amen. This is the clear voice of the blood, which the blood itself expresses from the mouth of the faithful redeemed by that same blood.

**11.** God says to Cain: *And now you are accursed from the earth, which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand; for you shall work the earth, and it will not add its strength to give to you: groaning and trembling shall you be on the earth*[^43]. He did not say: Accursed is the earth; but: Accursed are you from the earth, which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand. For the faithless Jewish people is accursed from the earth, that is, from the Church, which opened its mouth in the confession of sins to receive the blood of Christ, which was poured out for the forgiveness of sins, from the hand of the persecutor unwilling to be under grace, but under the Law; so that he might be accursed from the Church, that is, that the Church might understand and show him to be accursed, since the Apostle says: *For whoever are of the works of the Law are under the curse of the Law*[^44].

Then, when he had said: *Accursed are you from the earth, which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand*, he did not say: For you shall work it; but he said: *For you shall work the earth, and it will not add its strength to give to you.* Hence it is not necessary to understand that Cain works the same earth which opened its mouth to receive his brother's blood from his hand: but he is understood to be accursed from this earth on this account, that he works an earth which will not add its strength to give to him; that is, the Church recognizes and shows the Jewish people to be accursed on this account, that, with Christ slain, it still works earthly circumcision, earthly sabbath, earthly unleavened bread, earthly Passover:

all of which earthly working has a hidden strength of understanding the grace of Christ, which is not given to the Jews persevering in impiety and faithlessness, because it has been revealed in the New Testament; and to those who do not pass over to the Lord the veil is not taken away, which remains in the reading of the Old Testament, because in Christ alone there is done away—not the reading of the Old Testament itself, which has a hidden strength, but the veil by which it is hidden[^45]. Hence, when Christ suffered on the cross, the veil of the temple was torn[^46], so that through Christ's passion the secrets of the mysteries might be revealed to the faithful, passing over to drink his blood with mouth opened in confession.

For this reason that people, like Cain, still works the earth, still carries on the working of the Law carnally, which does not give it its strength, because it does not understand in it the grace of Christ. For this reason, too, in the very earth which Christ bore, that is, in his flesh, they themselves worked out our salvation by crucifying Christ, who died for our sins. And that same earth did not give them its strength, because they were not justified by the strength of the resurrection of him who rose for our justification[^47]: because, even though he was crucified out of weakness, yet he lives by the power of God, as the Apostle says[^48]. This, then, is the strength of that earth, which it does not show to the impious and unbelieving. Hence not even when rising did he appear to those by whom he had been crucified; like Cain working the earth, that the grain might be sown, that same earth not showing him the fruit of its strength: *For you shall work the earth*, he says, *and it will not add its strength to give to you.*

**12.** *Groaning and trembling shall you be on the earth.* Now, behold, who does not see, who does not recognize, throughout the whole earth, wherever that people is scattered, how it groans with grief over its lost kingdom, and trembles with fear under innumerable Christian peoples? And so Cain answered and said: *My case is greater, if you cast me out today from the face of the earth, and I am hidden from your face, and I shall be groaning and trembling on the earth; and it will come to pass that everyone who finds me will kill me.*

Truly from this he groans and trembles, lest, his earthly kingdom now also lost, he be killed by that visible death. This he calls the greater case—greater than that the earth does not give him its strength—lest he die spiritually. For he savors carnally, and does not think it grievous to be hidden from the face of God, that is, to have God angry, except lest he be found and killed. He savors carnally, like one working the earth, whose strength he does not receive. But to savor according to the flesh is death[^49]. Not understanding this, he groans over his lost kingdom, and trembles at bodily death.

But what does God answer him? *Not so*, he says; *but everyone who kills Cain will pay seven penalties*[^50]: that is, not so as you say; the impious race of carnal Jews will not perish by bodily death. For whoever destroys them in that way will pay seven penalties; that is, will take away from them the seven penalties by which they are bound on account of the guilt of having killed Christ: so that throughout all this time which revolves in the sevenfold number of days—since the Jewish nation does not perish—it may appear sufficiently to the faithful Christians what subjection they have deserved who with proud kingdom killed the Lord.

**13.** *And the Lord God set a mark on Cain, that no one who finds him should kill him*[^51]. This indeed is very marvelous: how all the nations which were subjugated by the Romans passed over into the rite of the Roman sacred things, and undertook to observe and celebrate those sacrileges; but the Jewish nation, whether under pagan kings or under Christian ones, has not lost the mark of its Law, by which it is distinguished from the other nations and peoples; and every emperor or king who finds them in his realm finds them with that very mark, and does not kill them; that is, does not bring it about that they are not Jews, marked off from the fellowship of the other nations by a certain definite and proper sign of their observance: except whoever of them passes over to Christ, so that he is no longer found a Cain, nor goes out from the face of God, nor dwells in the land of Nod, which is said to be interpreted "Commotion."

Against which evil, God is asked in the Psalm: *Do not give my feet to commotion*[^52]; and: *Let not the hands of sinners move me*[^53]; and: *Those who trouble me will exult, if I am moved*[^54]; and: *The Lord is at my right hand, that I may not be moved*[^55]; and innumerable such things: which all suffer who go out from the face of God, that is, from the mercy of his love. Hence it is said in a certain psalm: *I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever.* But see what follows: *Lord, in your will you gave strength to my beauty; but you turned away your face, and I became troubled*[^56]. From which it is understood that every soul is beautiful, and comely, and strong in virtue, by participation in the light of God, not through itself. If these Manicheans too considered and understood this, they would not fall into so great a blasphemy, thinking themselves to be the nature and substance of God. But they cannot, because they are not still; for they do not understand the sabbath of the heart: for if they were still, as was said to Cain, they would turn their sin toward themselves, that is, attribute it to themselves, not to some race of darkness or other; and so, through the grace of God, they would have dominion over that same sin. But as it is, both they and all who, obstinate in various errors, by resisting the truth go out from the face of God, like Cain, like the lost Jews, dwell in the land of commotion, that is, in carnal disturbance, over against the delight of God, that is, over against Eden[^57], which is interpreted "Feasting," where paradise was planted. Now I will touch briefly on the few remaining things out of many, lest I impede the purpose of this work and my reply by too great a length of discourse.

**14.** For whom would these books not move to seek and understand Christ—to pass over those things which, although they are contemplated all the more sweetly the more they are drawn out from more hidden places, yet require length of discussion, because they need many corroborations: to pass over such things, then—whom would these not move to wholesome faith: that Enoch, the seventh from Adam, pleased God and was translated[^58]; and a seventh rest is foretold, to which everyone is translated who is formed, as on the sixth day in the sixth age of the world, by Christ's coming? That Noah is freed with his household through water and wood[^59]; just as the family of Christ is marked through the Baptism of the passion of the cross. That the same ark is built of squared timbers; just as the Church is constructed of saints, always ready for every good work[^60]. For a square, whichever way you turn it, stands firmly. That its length is six times its breadth, and ten times its height, in the likeness of the human body, shows that Christ appeared in a human body. That its breadth is spread out by fifty cubits; just as the Apostle says: *Our heart is enlarged*[^61]. By what, except by spiritual love? On account of which he likewise says: *The love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^62]. For on the fiftieth day after his resurrection Christ sent the Holy Spirit, by whom he enlarged the hearts of believers[^63].

That it is three hundred cubits long, so that six times fifty are completed: just as the whole time of this world is extended through six ages, in all of which Christ never ceased to be preached—in five foretold through Prophecy, in the sixth spread abroad through the Gospel. That its height rises to thirty cubits, which number the length of three hundred cubits has ten times over: because Christ is our height, who, bearing the age of thirty years, consecrated the evangelical teaching, testifying that he had come not to abolish the Law, but to fulfill it[^64]. But the heart of the Law is recognized in the ten commandments: hence the length of the ark is completed by ten times thirty; hence Noah too is counted the tenth from Adam[^65]. That the timbers of the ark are glued together with pitch within and without[^66]: so that in the joining of unity the endurance of love may be signified, lest, when scandals tempt the Church, whether from those who are within or from those who are without, the brotherly joining give way, and the bond of peace be loosed. For pitch is a most fervent and most violent glue, signifying the ardor of love, with great force of strength for holding the spiritual fellowship that endures all things[^67].

**15.** That all kinds of animals are shut in the ark: just as the Church contains all the nations, which that dish shown to Peter also signifies. That both clean and unclean animals are there[^68]: just as in the Sacraments of the Church both the good and the bad are engaged. That there are seven of the clean and two of the unclean[^69]: not because there are fewer bad than good; but because the good keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace. And the divine Scripture commends the Holy Spirit in a sevenfold operation: of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and piety, and of the fear of God[^70]. Hence too that number of fifty days, pertaining to the coming of the Holy Spirit, is completed in seven sevens, which make forty-nine, with one added; on account of which it was said: *Striving to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace*[^71]. But the bad are shown, in the number two, to be prone to schisms, and in a manner divisible. That Noah himself with his household is counted as eight: because in Christ the hope of our resurrection appeared, who on the eighth day, that is, on the first after the seventh of the sabbath, rose from the dead; which day is the third from the passion, but in the number of the days that revolve through all time it is both the eighth and the first.

**16.** That the ark is gathered above and finished to one cubit: just as the Church, the body of Christ, gathered into unity, is raised and perfected. Hence he says in the Gospel: *He who does not gather with me scatters*[^72]. That its entrance is made in the side: for no one enters into the Church except through the Sacrament of the forgiveness of sins; and this flowed from the opened side of Christ. That the lower parts of the ark are constructed with two and three chambers[^73]: just as the Church gathers from all the nations a multitude either twofold, on account of the circumcision and the foreskin; or threefold, on account of the three sons of Noah, by whose offspring the world was filled. And these were called the lower parts of the ark for this reason, because in this earthly life there is a diversity of nations, but at the summit we are all completed into one. And this is not a variety: because Christ is all things and in all, completing us, as it were, in one cubit above, by heavenly unity.

**17.** That after seven days from when Noah entered the ark the flood came: because we are baptized in the hope of future rest, which was signified on the seventh day. That outside the ark all flesh which the earth sustained was consumed by the flood: because outside the fellowship of the Church the water of Baptism, although it is the same, not only avails nothing for salvation, but rather avails for destruction. That it rained for forty days and forty nights[^74]: because all guilt of sins, which is committed against the ten commandments of the Law, throughout the whole world, which is contained in four parts—for ten multiplied by four make forty—whether that guilt, as pertains to the days, was contracted from the prosperity of things, or, as pertains to the nights, from the adversity of things, is washed away by the Sacrament of heavenly Baptism.

**18.** That Noah was five hundred years old when the Lord spoke to him to make the ark for himself, and was passing his six hundredth year when he had entered it[^75]—from which it is understood that the ark was built over a hundred years—what else do these hundred years seem to signify here, except the individual ages of the world? Hence this sixth age, which is signified from the completed five hundred up to six hundred, constructs the Church in the evangelical manifestation; and therefore let him who takes thought for himself unto life be, as it were, squared timber, ready for every good work, and let him enter into the holy structure: because the second month too of the six hundredth year, in which Noah enters the ark, signifies the same sixth age. For two months are concluded in the number sixty; and from the number six are named both sixty, and six hundred, and six thousand, and sixty thousand, and six hundred thousand, and six hundred times, and whatever thereafter, in larger sums, by the same principle, the number rises into infinite increases.

**19.** And that the twenty-seventh day of the month is recorded: this pertains to the signification of the same squaring, which has already been set forth in the squared timbers. But here more evidently, because the trinity perfects us, ready for every good work, that is, in a manner squared: in memory, by which we recall God; in understanding, by which we know him; in will, by which we love him. For three times three, and this three times, make twenty-seven, which is the cube of the number three. That in the seventh month the ark settled, that is, rested[^76]: the signification runs back to that seventh rest. And because the perfect rest, there too the number of that squaring is repeated: for on the twenty-seventh day of the second month this Sacrament was commended; and again on the twenty-seventh day of the seventh month the same commendation was confirmed, when the ark rested: for what is promised in hope is presented in fact. Further, because that very seventh rest is joined with the eighth resurrection: for the rest which receives the saints after this life is not ended when the body is restored, but rather it takes up the whole man—not now in hope, but already in fact—renewed in every part with the perfect salvation of immortality, both of spirit and of body, into the gift of eternal life:

because, then, the seventh rest is joined with the eighth resurrection; and this, in the Sacrament of our regeneration, that is, in Baptism, is a high and deep mystery. The water rose fifteen cubits, exceeding the height of the mountains[^77]: that is, this Sacrament transcends all the wisdom of the proud. For seven and eight joined make fifteen. And because seventy is named from seven, and eighty from eight, with both numbers joined, the water was raised for one hundred and fifty days, commending and confirming to us that same height of Baptism in consecrating the new man to hold the faith of rest and resurrection.

**20.** That after forty days the raven sent out did not return, either intercepted by the waters or enticed by some floating corpse, signifies men most foul with the uncleanness of desire, and on this account too intent on the things that are outside in this world, who are either rebaptized or seduced and held by those whom Baptism kills outside the ark, that is, outside the Church. That the dove sent out, finding no rest, returned; shows that through the New Testament rest in this world is not promised to the saints. For it was sent out after forty days; which number signifies the life that is lived in this world. Then, sent out after seven days, on account of that sevenfold spiritual operation, it brought back a fruitful sprig of olive: by which it might signify that some, even baptized outside the Church, if the richness of love has not been lacking to them, can in a later time, as at evening, be led, in the mouth of the dove as in the kiss of peace, into the fellowship of unity. That, sent out after another seven days, it did not return[^78]: this signifies the end of the world, when there will be the rest of the saints—no longer in the sacrament of hope, by which the Church is associated in this time, as long as that which flowed from the side of Christ is drunk; but now in the very perfection of eternal salvation, when the kingdom will be handed over to God the Father[^79], so that in that clear contemplation of unchangeable truth we may need no corporeal mysteries.

**21.** It is too long to touch on all things, even as briefly as I have run through these: why in the six hundred and first year of Noah's life, that is, when the six hundred years were completed, the covering of the ark is opened, and the mystery that was hidden is, as it were, revealed. Why on the twenty-seventh day of the second month the earth is said to have dried[^80], as though the necessity of baptizing were now ended in the fifty-seventh number of days. For it is the twenty-seventh day of the second month, which number, from that joining of spirit and body, has seven times eight, with one added on account of the bond of unity. Why those who had entered the ark separately come out joined. For it had been said thus, that Noah and his sons entered the ark, and his wife and the wives of his sons[^81]: the men were recorded apart, the women apart. For in this time of this sacrament, the flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh[^82]. But Noah and his wife, and his sons and the wives of his sons[^83], come out, the males and females now recorded jointly: because at the end of the world and in the resurrection of the righteous, the body will cling to the spirit with every kind of perfect peace, with no want or concupiscence of mortality resisting. Why the animals, although both clean and unclean had been in the ark, yet after the going out from the ark are offered to God in sacrifice only if clean[^84].

**22.** What, then, it means that, with God speaking to Noah and, as it were, again from the beginning—because the same things had to be signified in many ways—commending the figure of the Church, his offspring is blessed to fill the earth, and all animals are given them for food: just as in that dish it is said to Peter: *Slay and eat*[^85]. That they are commanded to eat with the blood cast out: lest the former life, as though suffocated, be held in the conscience, but have, as it were, an outpouring through confession. That God set a covenant between himself and men and every living soul, that he should not destroy it by a flood—the bow that appears in the clouds[^86], which never shines except from the sun: for those do not perish in the flood, separated from the Church, who in the Prophets and all the divine Scriptures, as in the clouds of God, recognize the glory of Christ, and do not seek their own. But lest the worshipers of this sun swell up the more, let them know that Christ is signified by the sun in this way, just as by a lion, by a lamb, by a stone, on account of some likeness, not by a property of substance.

**23.** And now that, from the vineyard he planted, Noah, drunk, was uncovered in his house[^87]: to whom would Christ not appear, having suffered among his own nation? For then the mortality of his flesh was uncovered—to the Jews a scandal, to the Gentiles foolishness, but to those who are called, both Jews and Gentiles, as Shem and Japheth, the power of God and the Wisdom of God: because what is foolish of God is wiser than men, and what is weak of God is stronger than men[^88].

Accordingly, in the two sons, the eldest and the youngest, two peoples are figured, carrying one garment behind their backs—namely, the sacrament of the Lord's passion now past and accomplished—who do not look upon their father's nakedness, because they do not consent to Christ's death; and yet they honor it with a covering, as those who know from whom they were born. But the middle son, that is, the people of the Jews—middle for this reason, because they neither held the primacy of the Apostles, nor believed last among the Gentiles—saw the nakedness of the father, because they consented to Christ's death; and they announced it outside to the brothers: for through him there was made manifest, and in a manner published, what was secret in the Prophecy; and therefore he becomes a servant of his brothers. For what else is that very nation even today, except a kind of book-keeper of the Christians, bearing the Law and the Prophets for the testimony of the Church's assertion, so that we may honor through the sacrament what it announces through the letter?

**24.** But whom would it not rouse, whom would it not either inform or confirm in faith, that those two who honored their father's nakedness, although turned away, are blessed thus, as those whom the deed of the wicked vineyard displeased? *Blessed*, he says, *be the Lord God of Shem.* For although he is the God of all nations, yet in a manner by a proper title, even now among the Gentiles themselves, he is called the God of Israel. And whence has this come about, except from the blessing of Japheth? For in the people of the Gentiles the Church has occupied the whole world. This very thing, this was foretold, when it was said: *May God enlarge Japheth, and may he dwell in the tents of Shem*[^89].

See, Manicheans, see; behold, the whole world is in your sight: this you are astonished at, this you grieve over in our peoples, because God enlarges Japheth. See whether he does not dwell in the tents of Shem, that is, in the Churches which the Apostles, the sons of the Prophets, constructed. Hear what Paul says to the now faithful Gentiles: *You who were*, he says, *at that time without Christ, alienated from the fellowship of Israel, and strangers to the covenants, and not having the hope of the promise, and without God in this world.* By these words it is shown that Japheth was not yet dwelling in the tents of Shem. But notice how a little later he concludes. *Therefore now*, he says, *you are no longer strangers and sojourners; but you are fellow citizens of the saints, and of the household of God, built upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone*[^90].

Behold how Japheth is enlarged and dwells in the tents of Shem. And yet you hold, read, and preach the Epistles of the Apostles, in which all these things are testified. Where should I reckon you yourselves, except in that accursed middle to whom Christ is not the cornerstone? For we recognize you neither in that wall which, from the circumcision, believed in Christ, whence the Apostles too were; nor in that which is from the foreskin, whence are all from the other Gentiles, running together into the same unity of faith, as into the peace of the corner.

But also all who accept and read any books whatever of our canon, where Christ is shown born and suffering in mortal flesh, and yet do not honorably veil that same mortality, uncovered in the passion, with the joined sacrament of unity, but without the knowledge of piety and love announce that from which we were all born—although they disagree among themselves, both the Jews from the heretics, and the heretics themselves one from another—are nonetheless, by the one condition of servitude, useful to the Church, whether for some attestation or for some proof.

For of heretics too it is said: *There must be heresies, that those who are approved may be made manifest among you*[^91]. Go now, and hurl your slanders at the old sacred writings; do this, servants of Ham; go, you to whom the uncovered flesh from which you were born has become worthless: for there would be nothing whereby you could in any way call yourselves Christians, unless Christ, as he was foretold by the Prophets, had come into the world, had drunk from his own vineyard that cup which could not pass, had slept in his passion as in the drunkenness of folly, which is wiser than men; and so the weakness of mortal flesh was uncovered through the hidden counsel of God, stronger than men—without which weakness, had not the Word of God assumed it, the Christian name itself, in which you too glory, would not be on the earth at all.

But do this, as I said; mockingly proclaim what we reverently honor: let the Church use you as those subject to it, that those who are approved may be made manifest in it. So far did those Prophets keep silent about nothing that the Church was either to have or to suffer, that we find you too there in your own places, in a vanity that is destructive for capturing the reprobate, but useful for making the approved manifest.

**25.** You say that Christ was not foretold by the Israelite Prophets: for the foretelling of whom all those pages keep watch, if you were willing to search them with the piety with which you harass them with frivolity. Who else, in Abraham, goes out from his land and from his kindred, to be enriched and made wealthy among foreigners[^92], except he who, having left the land and kindred of the Jews, from whom according to the flesh he was born, so prevails and is so powerful among the Gentiles, as we see? Who else, in Isaac, carried for himself the wood for the victim[^93], except he who himself carried for himself the cross for the passion? Who else, the ram to be sacrificed, clung by its horns in the thicket, except he who, to be offered for us, was affixed to the gibbet of the cross?

**26.** Who else, in the angel who wrestled with Jacob, when, being the weaker and conquered, he partly blesses the conqueror who prevailed over him as though stronger, and partly renders lame the broad part of his thigh[^94], except he who, suffering the people of Israel to prevail against him, blessed in them certain ones who believed? But the broad part of Jacob's thigh went lame in the multitude of the carnal people. Who else, the stone placed at Jacob's head, so that it might in a manner be expressed by name, was even anointed, except Christ the head of the man? For who does not know that "Christ" is named from anointing? He too, recalling this very thing in the Gospel, and testifying most plainly that it was figured of himself, when he had said that a certain Nathanael was truly an Israelite in whom there was no guile; and when that man, as one having that stone at his head, had confessed him the Son of God and King of Israel, in a manner anointing the stone by that confession, that is, confessing that he himself is the Christ; there the Lord opportunely recalled also that which Jacob then saw, who by the blessing was called Israel: *Amen I say to you*, he said, *you will see heaven opened, and the Angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man*[^95]. For this Israel had seen, when he had that stone at his head: a ladder from earth to heaven, by which the Angels of God ascended and descended[^96].

By which were signified the Evangelists, the preachers of Christ: ascending, of course, when, to understand his most supereminent divinity, they pass beyond the whole creation, that they may find it in the beginning, God with God, through whom all things were made[^97]; but descending, that they may find him made of a woman, made under the Law, that he might redeem those who were under the Law[^98]. For in him there is a ladder from earth all the way to heaven, from flesh all the way to spirit; because in him carnal people, by progressing, as by ascending, become spiritual: to whom, to be nourished with milk, even the spiritual themselves in a manner descend, when they cannot speak to them as to spiritual people, but as to carnal ones[^99].

Thus there is both ascending and descending upon the Son of man. For the Son of man is above, in our head, which is the Savior himself; and the Son of man is below, in his body, which is the Church. We understand that he is also the ladder, because he himself said: *I am the way*[^100]. To him, then, there is ascending, that he may be understood in the heights; and to him there is descending, that the little ones may be nourished in his members. And through him there is ascending and descending: for his preachers, following his example, not only raise themselves up to behold him loftily, but also humble themselves to announce him temperately. See the Apostle ascending: *Whether*, he says, *we are out of our mind, it is to God.* See him also descending: *Whether we are sober, it is for you.* Let him say also through whom he ascended and descended: *For the love of Christ*, he says, *constrains us; judging this, that if one died for all, then all died: and he died for all, that those who live may now live no longer for themselves, but for him who died and rose again for them*[^101].

**27.** Whom these holy spectacles in the holy Scriptures do not delight, he, not enduring sound doctrine, turns to fables[^102]. And those fables indeed solicit, with a certain varied delight, souls that are childish at whatever age of the body: but let us, now the body of Christ, recognize our voice in the Psalm, and say to him: *The unrighteous have told me delights; but not as your law, Lord*[^103]. Christ meets me everywhere in those books, everywhere in those Scriptures, as I travel and pant in that sweat of human condemnation, whether openly or hiddenly, and refreshes me: he both inflames in me, by some difficulty in finding him, the desire by which I may eagerly drink down what I have found, and, stored in the marrow, wholesomely hold it.

**28.** He is signified to me in Joseph, who, when his brothers persecuted and sold him, is honored in Egypt after his labors[^104]. For we have learned of the labors of Christ in the world of the nations, which Egypt signified, through the various passions of the martyrs; and now we see the honor of Christ in the same world, subjugating all things to himself by the dispensing of his grain. He is signified to me in the rod of Moses, which, made a serpent on the ground, figured his death coming from the serpent: but that, when its tail is seized, it is again made a rod[^105], signifies that afterward, when all things have now been accomplished at the end of the action, by rising it returns to what it had been, where, with death consumed through the restoration of life, nothing of the serpent appears.

We too, his body, are rolled along through the slippery places of the times in the same mortality: but at the very end, like the tail of the world, seized by the hand, that is, by the power of judgment, lest it slip further, we shall be restored, and, rising with the last enemy, death, destroyed[^106], we shall be at the right hand of God the rod of the kingdom.

**29.** Now of the departure of Israel from Egypt, let not me, but the Apostle speak: *But I do not want you to be ignorant, brethren, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ*[^107]. By expounding one thing, he introduced understanding into the rest. For if the rock is Christ on account of firmness, why is the manna not also Christ, as the living bread that came down from heaven[^108]? On which those who truly feed live spiritually. For those who took the old figure carnally died. But when the Apostle says, *They ate the same spiritual food*, he shows that this too is to be understood spiritually in Christ; just as he opened why he had called the drink spiritual when he added: *And the rock was Christ*: which once opened, all things shone forth. Why, then, is the cloud not also Christ, and the column, since he is upright and firm and supporting our weakness: shining by night, not shining by day; that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind[^109]? The Red Sea too is red; surely the Baptism consecrated by the blood of Christ. The enemies following behind die: past sins.

**30.** The people is led through the desert[^110]; all baptized, not yet fully enjoying the promised homeland, but, by hoping for what they do not see and by awaiting it through patience[^111], are, as it were, in the desert. And there are laborious and perilous temptations, lest they return in heart to Egypt. Nor yet does Christ forsake them there: for that column too does not withdraw[^112]. And the bitter waters grow sweet by wood: because the enemy peoples grow gentle when the sign of the cross of Christ is honored. And the twelve springs, watering seventy palm trees[^113], prefigure the apostolic grace, watering the peoples in the number seven multiplied tenfold, so that through the sevenfold gift of the Spirit the decalogue of the Law may be fulfilled. And the enemy, striving to block the way, is overcome, with Moses' hands stretched out, by the sign of the Lord's cross[^114]. And the deadly bites of the serpents are healed when the bronze serpent is lifted up and looked upon: which is declared by the very words of the Lord: *As Moses lifted up the serpent in the desert*, he says, *so must the Son of man be lifted up; that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life*[^115]. Do not these things too, then, cry out? Is the deafness in hard hearts so great?

The Passover is made in the slaying of a sheep: Christ is slain, of whom it is said in the Gospel: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world*[^116]. Those who make the Passover are forbidden to break the bones: the bones of the Lord are not broken on the cross. The Evangelist testifies that it was said on this account: *You shall not break a bone of him*[^117].

The doorposts are smeared with blood, that destruction may be driven off[^118]: the foreheads of the people are marked with the sign of the Lord's passion for the protection of salvation. The Law is given there on the fiftieth day after the Passover was kept[^119]: the Holy Spirit came on the fiftieth day after the Lord's passion[^120]. The Law there is said to be written by the finger of God[^121]: the Lord says of the Holy Spirit: *By the finger of God I cast out demons*[^122].

And Faustus cries out, with his eyes closed, that he has found nothing in those writings that pertains to the foretelling of Christ! But what wonder is it if he has eyes for reading and has not a heart for understanding, who, placed before the closed door of the divine secret, does not knock with the faith of piety, but insults with the elation of impiety? Plainly let it be so, let it so come to pass: for it is just. Let the door of salvation be closed to the proud: let the meek man come, whom the Lord teaches his ways[^123], and let him see both these things in those books, and the rest—either all of them, or certain ones, such as he may believe in all.

**31.** Let him see Jesus leading the people into the land of promise[^124]: for it was not rashly that he was called this from the beginning, but by that very dispensation, with his name changed, he was called Jesus. Let him see the cluster of grapes from the land of promise hanging on wood[^125]. Let him see in Jericho, as in this mortal world, a harlot—of such as the Lord says that they go before the proud into the kingdom of heaven[^126]—sending out through the window of her house, as through the mouth of her body, the scarlet thread[^127], which is surely the sign of blood, to confess unto salvation on account of the forgiveness of sins. Let him see the walls of that very city, as the mortal fortifications of the world, fall when the Ark of the Covenant has been carried around them seven times[^128]: just as now through the times, which slip by in the alternation of seven days, the Covenant of God goes around the whole world; that at the end of the times the last enemy, death, may be destroyed[^129], and from the destruction of the impious one single house, as the one Church, may be freed, cleansed from the shamefulness of fornication, through the window of confession, in the blood of forgiveness.

**32.** Let him see the times, first of the Judges, afterward of the Kings; just as there will be first the judgment, then afterward the kingdom: and in those very times of Judges and Kings, again and again, in many and various ways, Christ and the Church are figured. Who was it, in Samson killing the lion that met him, when he was making for the foreigners for the sake of seeking a wife[^130], except he who, about to call the Church from the Gentiles, said: *Rejoice, for I have overcome the world*[^131]? What does the honeycomb built in the mouth of that slain lion mean[^132], except that, behold, we behold the very laws of the earthly kingdom, which before had raged against Christ, now, with their ferocity destroyed, even providing defenses for the sweetness of the Gospel to be preached?

What is that woman full of confidence, transfixing the enemy's temples with wood[^133], except the faith of the Church, destroying the kingdoms of the devil by the cross of Christ? What is the fleece wet while the threshing floor was dry, and afterward the threshing floor wet while the fleece was dry[^134], except that first the one nation of the Hebrews held secretly in its holy ones the mystery of God, which is Christ, of which mystery the whole world was empty; but now, in manifestation, the whole world has it, while she has been emptied?

**33.** Now in the times of the Kings, to recall a few things from there too: does not the priesthood changed at the very outset to Samuel, with Eli rejected[^135], and the kingdom changed to David, with Saul rejected[^136], cry out that a new priesthood and a new kingdom are foretold, with the old rejected, which was a shadow of what was to come in our Lord Jesus Christ? Did not David himself, when he ate the loaves of the Presence, which it was not lawful to eat except for the priests alone[^137], figure both as to come in one person, that is, the kingdom and the priesthood in the one Jesus Christ? Does not the fact that ten tribes were separated from the temple, and two left behind[^138], sufficiently indicate what the Apostle says of that whole nation: *A remnant has been saved according to the election of grace*[^139]?

**34.** Elijah is fed in the time of famine, with ravens bringing bread in the morning and flesh in the evening[^140]: and the Manicheans do not understand Christ in those books, to whom, hungering as it were for our salvation, sinners confess, now having faith as the first fruits of the spirit, but at the end, as at the evening of the world, even the resurrection of the flesh. Elijah is sent to be fed to a foreign widow, who wished to gather two sticks of wood before she should die: here the sign of the cross is expressed not by the name of wood alone, but also by the number of the pieces of wood. Her flour and oil are blessed[^141]: the fruit and cheerfulness of love, which, when it is spent, does not fail; for God loves a cheerful giver[^142].

The boys who insulted Elisha and cried out, "Baldhead, baldhead," are eaten by beasts[^143]: those who with childish folly deride Christ crucified in the place of Calvary, invaded by demons, perish. Elisha sends his staff over the dead boy by his servant, and he does not revive; he comes himself, joins and fits himself to his death, and the boy revives[^144]: the Word of God sent the Law through his servant, and it did not profit the human race, dead in sins; yet it was not sent without cause; for he sent it who knew it was to be sent first: he came himself, conformed himself to us, made a partaker of our death, and we were given life.

When wood was being cut with axes, the iron, leaping off the wood, was sunk in the depth of the river, and, when wood was thrown over it by Elisha, returned[^145]; so when the presence of Christ, working through his body, was cutting down the impious Jews like unfruitful trees—because John had said of him, *Behold, the axe is laid to the roots of the tree*[^146]—he, with the passion intervening at their hands, deserted that very body, descending into the depths of the underworld, where, with it placed above in the burial, as the spirit returned to its handle, as it were, he rose. How many things I pass over, constrained by the necessity of brevity, those who read know.

**36.** Now the very migration into Babylon, to which the Spirit of God through the prophet Jeremiah commands them to go, and to pray for those very people in whose realm they sojourn, because in their peace there would be peace for them too, and to build houses, and plant new vineyards, and plant gardens[^147]—who would not recognize what he prefigured, if he had attended to the true Israelites, in whom there is no guile[^148], who through the apostolic dispensation, with the evangelical sacrament, migrated to the kingdom of the Gentiles? Hence the Apostle, as though repeating Jeremiah, says to us: *I will, therefore, that first of all supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks be made for all men, for kings, and for those who are in high places, that we may lead a quiet and tranquil life, in all piety and love: for this is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Savior, who wills all men to be saved, and to come to the knowledge of the truth*[^149].

From this, indeed, with those too believing, were built the dwellings of peace, the basilicas of the Christian congregations, and were planted the new vineyards of the faithful people, and the gardens; where, even among all the vegetables, that grain of mustard reigns, under whose shade stretched far and wide even the high-aspiring pride of the Gentiles, like the birds of heaven, takes refuge and rests[^150]. For as to the fact that even after seventy years, according to the prophecy of the same Jeremiah, there is a return from captivity, and the temple is renewed[^151]; what faithful man of Christ would not understand that, after the times have rolled away which run their course in the repetition of the sevenfold number of days, we too, that is, the Church of God, must return from the sojourn of this world to that heavenly Jerusalem? Through whom, except through Jesus Christ, the truly great priest, whose figure that Jeshua bore, the great priest of that time when the temple was built after the captivity? Whom the prophet Zechariah saw in filthy garb, and, with the devil conquered who stood for his accusation, the filthy garment taken from him, and a garment of honor and glory given[^152]: just as the body of Jesus Christ, which is the Church, with the adversary at the end of the times overcome through judgment, is taken up from the mourning of its sojourn into the glory of everlasting salvation. Which is also sung most plainly in the Psalm of the dedication of the house: *You have turned my mourning into joy for me, you have torn off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness; that my glory may sing to you, and I may not be pricked with sorrow*[^153].

**37.** Who can, on the occasion of another work, touch on, with however great brevity, all the things which in those old books of the Law and the Prophets announce Christ figuratively? Unless perhaps someone thinks it is done by ingenuity that the things which ran their course by the order of events through their own times are turned, by interpreting, into significations of Christ. This perhaps Jews can say, or pagans: but for those who wish to be thought Christians, the apostolic authority presses on the neck, saying: *All these things happened to them in figure*; and: *All these things were figures of us*[^154].

For if Ishmael and Isaac, men born, signify two Testaments[^155]; what is to be believed about so many deeds which were done by no natural use, by no necessity of business? Do they signify nothing? If any of us, who are ignorant of Hebrew letters, that is, the very characters of the words, were to see them written on a wall, in some place of honor; who would be so witless as to think the wall painted in that way? Or would he not rather understand it to be writing, so that, even if he were unable to read, he would yet not doubt that those marks signified something? So first all those things which are in the old document of the holy Scriptures—whoever reads them not with a perverse mind ought to be so moved that he does not doubt that they signify something.

**38.** For example: if a woman had to be made a help for the man, did any necessity compel, or any usefulness urge, even this, that she be made from the side of the sleeping man[^156]? If, for the sake of escaping the flood, there was need to build an ark; what need was there for its measurements either to be made just so, or even to be recorded in writings to be propagated to posterity for religion's sake? If, for the sake of restoring the species, the animals had to be shut in; what need was there for that particular number, seven of the clean, two of the unclean? Necessity surely compelled an entrance to be made to the ark; but what compelled it to be made in the side, or even to be committed to memory through writings[^157]?

Abraham is commanded to sacrifice his son: let him have been so commanded, that his obedience, proved even by such a test, might be made known to posterity; let the son more fittingly have carried the wood, lest the aged father carry it; let him not afterward have been permitted to strike his son, lest by a most grievous bereavement he strike himself: would Abraham have been any less proved, even if there were a return with no blood shed? Or, if the sacrifice now had to be completed, did it pertain to any increase of the victim that that ram should appear clinging by its horns in the thicket[^158]?

Thus all things, when they are considered, and are found woven together, as it were the superfluous with the necessary, admonish the human mind, that is, the rational mind, first that they signify something, and then to seek what they signify.

**39.** And so even the Jews themselves, who deride the Christ whose passion we have recognized, being unwilling for him to seem foretold by such figures of things—not only said, but also done—are compelled by us to learn what those things signify: which, unless they grant that they signify something, they do not defend books of such divine authority from the disgrace of foolish fables. A certain Philo saw this, a man most liberally learned, one of them, whose eloquence the Greeks do not hesitate to equal to Plato's: and he tried to interpret some things, not for understanding Christ, in whom he had not believed; but so that from this it might the more appear what a difference there is whether you refer all things to Christ, on whose account they were truly so said, or whether, apart from him, you pursue any conjectures whatever with any sharpness of mind whatever;

and how much that avails which the Apostle says: *When you pass over to the Lord, the veil will be taken away*[^159]. For, to recall something of this same Philo: wishing the ark of the flood to be understood as built according to the proportion of the human body, he handled all things, as it were, member by member. And as he most subtly consulted even the rules of numbers, all things fittingly came out for him, which would in no way hinder the understanding of Christ, since that Savior of the human race too appeared in a human body; yet they would not compel it, because a human body belongs surely to other men as well. But when it came to the door, which was made in the side of the ark, all conjecture of human ingenuity failed. Yet, to say something, he dared to believe, dared even to say, dared even to write, that by that door were signified the lower parts of the body, through which urine and dung are discharged. No wonder that, not finding the door, he so erred.

But if he had passed over to Christ, the veil being taken away, he would have found the Sacraments of the Church flowing from the side of that Man[^160]. For because it was foretold: *The two shall be in one flesh*[^161], therefore in the ark too some things are referred to Christ, but some to the Church, which whole thing is Christ. So too in the other interpretations of figures throughout the whole text of the divine Scripture one may consider, and compare, the senses of those who understand Christ there, and of those who, apart from Christ, try to twist them to other things.

**40.** Nor do the pagans clamor against us in this; for they do not dare to contradict our interpreting those things—not only said, but also done—taken figuratively, for the understanding of Christ: especially since the things we understand to have been foretold we also show to have been fulfilled; whereas they, in order somehow to commend their fables, try by interpreting to refer them to some physiologies or theologies, that is, natural or divine reasons: plainly indicating in part what sort they are; but in part dissembling, while they laugh in the theaters at what they venerate in the temples, too free in shamefulness, too servile in superstition.

**41.** But whoever says to us that those things were neither done nor written for Christ to be understood in them—apart from that great consonance of things prefigured and now fulfilled—he will be struck by other prophetic predictions, open and manifest: such as this: *In your seed shall all the nations be blessed.* This was said to Abraham, this to Isaac, this to Jacob[^162]. Hence not without reason he says: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^163]: surely about to fulfill, in the blessing of all the nations, what he promised from their seed. And not without reason did Abraham himself, when his servant swore to him, command him to put his hand under his thigh[^164], knowing that from there would come the flesh of Christ, in whom all the nations are blessed—which we now do not foretell, but what was then foretold, we now see.

**42.** I would like to know—or rather it would be better not to know—by what blindness of mind Faustus read where Jacob called his sons and said: *Gather, that I may announce to you the things that will befall you in the last days*[^165]: *gather and hear, sons of Jacob; hear Israel, your father.* Here surely no one doubts that the person of one prophesying is made clear.

Let us hear, then, what he says to his son Judah, from whose tribe Christ came of the seed of David according to the flesh, as apostolic teaching testifies[^166]. *Judah, your brothers will praise you; your hands upon the backs of your enemies; the sons of your father will adore you. A lion's cub is Judah: from the budding, my son, you have gone up, lying down; you have slept like a lion, and like a lion's cub—who will rouse him? A ruler will not be lacking from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until there come the things laid up for him; and he himself is the expectation of the nations, binding his colt to the vine, and to sackcloth the foal of an ass: he will wash his robe in wine, and his garment in the blood of the grape: his eyes shining from wine, and his teeth whiter than milk*[^167].

Let these things be false, let them be obscure, if they did not shine forth in Christ with the most evident light; if his brothers, the Apostles, and all his coheirs do not praise him, seeking not their own glory but his: if his hands are not upon the backs of his enemies; if whoever still oppose him are not pressed down and bent to the earth, while the Christian peoples grow: if the sons of Jacob did not adore him in the remnant which was saved according to the election of grace[^168]: if he himself is not the lion's cub, since by being born he was made a little one; for which reason it was added: *From the budding, my son.* For the reason was given why a cub, in whose praise it is written elsewhere: *A lion's cub is stronger than the beasts of burden*[^169]; that is, even as a little one stronger than the greater: if he did not go up on the cross lying down, when, with head bowed, he gave up the spirit: if he did not sleep like a lion, since even in death itself he was not conquered, but conquered; and like a lion's cub, for he died from there whence he was also born: if that one did not rouse him from the dead, whom no man has seen, nor can see[^170]. For by what was said, *Who will rouse him?*, the signification of one, as it were unknown, is sufficiently expressed.

If a ruler was lacking from Judah, and a leader from his thighs, until there came, at the suitable time, the things which had been promised as though laid up. For there are most certain writings of the very history of the Jews themselves, by which it is shown that a foreigner, Herod, was first king in the nation of the Jews, at which time Christ was born[^171]. So a king of the seed of Judah was not lacking, until there came the things laid up for him. But because what was promised profited not the faithful Jews alone, see what follows: *And he himself is the expectation of the nations.* He himself bound his colt to the vine, that is, his people, preaching and crying in sackcloth: *Do penance; for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^172]. And we recognize the people of the Gentiles subject to him compared to the foal of an ass, on which also he sat, leading it into Jerusalem[^173], that is, into the vision of peace, teaching the meek his ways.

If he did not wash his robe in wine: for that robe is the glorious Church, which he presents to himself, having no spot or wrinkle[^174]: to which it is also said through Isaiah: *If your sins are like scarlet, I will whiten them like snow*[^175]. Whence, except from sins forgiven? In what wine, then, except that of which it is said that it will be poured out for many unto the forgiveness of sins[^176]? For he himself is that cluster which hung on the wood[^177]. Therefore here too see what he adds: *And his garment in the blood of the grape.* And now that his eyes shine from wine, those members in his body recognize, to whom it has been granted, by a certain holy drunkenness of the mind alienated from the temporal things slipping away below, to behold the eternal light of wisdom. Whence we recalled something a little before, when Paul said: *For whether we are out of our mind, it is to God.* These are the eyes shining from wine. But yet, because it follows: *Whether we are sober, it is for you*[^178]; nor are the little ones, still to be nourished with milk[^179], left behind, because here too it follows: *And his teeth whiter than milk.*

**43.** What do you answer to these things, you mad men? Surely these things are manifest; surely they drive away all—I do not say slanders of contradiction, but even the mists of doubt. Seek such things first in those books, believe first such things, which now cannot all be recalled by me, because there are too many; nor many, because it would be long; nor would I wish to recall few, lest they be thought the only ones by those who do not read those books, and lest a faithful and diligent reader reproach me, when he finds many more evident ones, for having set down precisely these which could occur to me at present: for you will find many which do not need even such an admonition as I have just used in the words of Jacob. For who now seeks an expositor while he reads: *He was led like a sheep to the slaughter*[^180]; and all the things that are there said manifoldly and evidently: *That by his bruise we are healed*; *That he himself bore our sins*[^181]? Who would not think it sung as though a Gospel: *They have dug my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones; but they themselves considered and looked upon me: they divided my garments among themselves, and over my robe they cast lots?* Who, unless too blind, does not now perceive being fulfilled: *All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will adore in his sight*[^182]? What of that in the Gospel: *My soul is sorrowful even unto death*[^183]; and: *Now my soul is troubled*[^184]? Did it not first sound in the Psalm: *I slept troubled?* And whence did it come about that he slept?

Of those by whose voices it was shouted: *Crucify, crucify*[^185]; does he not also foretell, going on in the Psalm: *The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword?* But what did they do, what did they harm one who was to rise, and to ascend above the heavens, and to possess the whole world with the glory of his name? See whether the Psalm has not before been silent about this: for it follows: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and over all the earth your glory*[^186]. Who ever doubted that it was said of Christ: *The Lord said to me, You are my Son, today I have begotten you; ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth*[^187]? Whom else has anyone been permitted to understand, where Jeremiah says, doubtless of Wisdom: *He delivered her to Jacob his servant, and to Israel his beloved: after this she was seen on the earth, and conversed with men*[^188]?

**44.** Who would not recognize the same Savior in Daniel, when the son of man is presented to the Ancient of days, and receives a kingdom without end, that all the nations should serve him[^189]? And now, if that passage which the Lord recalled from the prophecy of the same Daniel: *When you see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of by Daniel, standing in the holy place; let him who reads understand*[^190]—if, with the times of the weeks reckoned, that number is handled, not only is Christ found, but also the time at which it behooved him to come to suffer. Although even without the computation of times we are wont to press the Jews with the manifest results of things—our dispute with whom is not whether our salvation is in Christ, but whether he has already come and suffered. And they are convicted by the most open facts themselves, not only concerning the faith of all the nations, which that same Scripture, to which they are compelled to yield, foretold would serve him—which Scripture so shines throughout the whole world that it strikes the eyes of all who shuffle—but also concerning the things which have now been done in the very nation of the Jews: that the sanctuary is overthrown, that the sacrifice, and the priest, and the former anointing have ceased: all of which Daniel then foretold would be, when he plainly prophesied that the Holy of holies would be anointed[^191]. Since, then, all those things have now been done, the anointed Holy of holies is demanded of them too, and they find nothing to answer. But how would they dispute with us, not about Christ, but only about his coming, unless they well knew that he was prophesied in their books? Why do they ask John whether he is the Christ[^192]? Why do they say to the Lord himself: *How long do you hold our soul in suspense? If you are the Christ, tell us plainly*[^193]? Why do Peter and Andrew and Philip say to Nathanael: *We have found the Messiah, which is interpreted Christ*[^194], except because this name was both known and awaited in that nation through those writings? For no other nation had and called its kings and priests christs, whose significative anointing it was not lawful to cease, except when he had come who was foretold in them[^195].

For the Jews so knew those christs of theirs that they nonetheless hoped for one, through whom at last they would be freed: but, blinded by the hidden justice of God, while they think only of his power, they did not understand the weakness in which he died for us. Hence we recognize those words foretold of them in the book of Wisdom: *Let us condemn him to a most shameful death; for there will be respect in his words: if he is truly the Son of God, he will take him up, and will free him from the hands of his adversaries. These things they thought and erred; for their malice blinded them*[^196]. Which can also be said most truly of these men, who, amid so great a multitude of testimonies, amid so great an arrangement of foretold things, amid so great a manifestation of fulfilled ones, still say that Christ was not prophesied in those Scriptures. And if they say it again and again, we can again and again bring forth documents, with the help of him who has supplied so great an abundance against the slanders of human error, so that we need not repeat the things we have already recalled.

**45.** Now further, that other shuffle of Faustus—which, I believe, when he too was being struck back by the most brilliant light of prophecy, he reckoned he had found most cleverly—it irks me even to refute, lest he be thought to have said something for the reason that it was deemed worthy to be answered. For who, even most demented, would say that it is the mark of an enfeebled faith not to believe about Christ without a witness? I would like these men to answer me, on whose word they themselves had believed about Christ: did they hear that voice from heaven: *This is my Son*[^197]? Faustus would rather have us believe that voice, he who does not want us to believe about Christ on the testimony of men, as though the knowledge of that voice too reached us without a human witness, when it is plain that it reached us in such a way

that the Apostle says: *But how will they call upon him in whom they have not believed? Or how will they believe him whom they have not heard? But how will they hear without a preacher? Or how will they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: How beautiful are the feet of those who announce peace, who announce good things!*[^198] You see, surely, how prophetic testimony accompanies the preaching of apostolic teaching. For lest the things the Apostles announced be despised, or be reckoned fabulous, these were shown to have been foretold beforehand by the Prophets: because, even though miracles bore witness, there would not have been lacking those (as even now some still mutter) who would attribute all those things to magical power, unless such a thought of theirs were overcome by prophetic attestation. For no one surely would say that by magical arts, long before they were born, they set up Prophets for themselves by whom they would be foretold. But evidently Faustus, who believed about a false Christ on the errors of the Persians, forbids us to believe about the true Christ on the testimony of the Hebrew Prophets.

**46.** But Catholic teaching teaches that the Christian mind ought first to be nourished by simple faith, in order to make it capable of understanding things heavenly and eternal. For thus the prophet too says: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^199]. And that very simple faith, by which we believe before we know the supereminent knowledge of the love of Christ, that we may be filled unto all the fullness of God[^200]—it is not without cause that the dispensation of his humility, by which he was humanly born and suffered, was foretold by the Prophets, through a prophetic nation, through a prophetic people, through a prophetic kingdom, so long before; except because in that folly which is wiser than men, and in that weakness which is stronger than men[^201], something great lies hidden for our justification and glorification. And there all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden[^202], which are opened to no one if he has despised the food conveyed to him through the maternal flesh, that is, the nourishment of milk through the apostolic and prophetic breasts; and, loathing as it were the food of infancy as though of an advanced age, has rushed into the poisons of heretics before the food of wisdom, for which he rashly reckons himself fit. Therefore what we say, that simple faith is necessary, is not contrary to what we say, that the Prophets are to be believed: for it pertains rather to this, that the Prophets be believed, before, with the mind purged and strengthened, he who spoke thus through the Prophets can be understood.

**47.** But, you say, if they prophesied Christ, they did not live worthily or fittingly in accordance with their prophecy. Whence do you know this? Or can you judge what it is to live either well or badly, you whose righteousness is that a melon that does not feel is rather to be helped, so that you may eat it, than a hungry beggar, so that you may give him something to eat? But for the Catholic little ones, before they know what is the perfect righteousness of the human soul, and how great a difference there is between that one for which one sighs and this one by which one lives here, it suffices to think this about those men, which the soundness of apostolic teaching commends, that the righteous man lives by faith[^203]. *And Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness. For the Scripture, foreseeing that God justifies the nations by faith, foretold to Abraham, saying: That in your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^204]. These are the Apostle's words. If, at this so clear and to all so well-known voice, you would awaken from your most deceitful dreams, you would follow the footsteps of our father Abraham, and would be blessed in his seed along with all the nations.

For he himself received, as the Apostle says, *the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which is in the foreskin, that he might be the father of all who believe through the foreskin, that it might be counted to them also for righteousness: that he might be the father of circumcision, not only to those who are of the circumcision, but also to those who follow the footsteps of the faith of our father Abraham, which is in the foreskin*[^205].

Whose righteousness of faith, then, is set before us to be imitated as an example, so that we too, justified by faith, may have peace toward God, we ought to understand how he lived, not to reproach it: lest, by abortion, we slip from the womb of mother Church before, formed and perfected by a stable conception, we are born.

**48.** This I would briefly answer Faustus, on behalf of the morals of the Patriarchs and Prophets, out of the voice of our little ones: among whom I would reckon myself too, provided that I do not reproach the life of the holy ancients, even if I do not understand how mystically they lived—whose life the Apostles laudably preached to us in their Gospel, just as those men by their prophecy foretold the Apostles to come, so that the two Testaments cry out to each other, like the two Seraphim: *Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of hosts*[^206]. But when Faustus has begun to accuse the Patriarchs and Prophets, not by a general and indefinite reproach, as he did here, but by recalling their deeds in particular, the Lord their God, who is also ours, will help me to answer each thing fittingly and aptly. But as it is, Faustus the Manichean reviles those men, while Paul the apostle praises them: let each choose whom to believe.
