# Book 15

**1.** Faustus said: Why do you not accept the Old Testament?

Because every vessel that is full does not receive what is poured over it, but pours it out: and a full stomach throws back what is put in. Accordingly the Jews too, sated with the Old Testament from being preoccupied with Moses, rejected the New: and we, filled with the New by being forestalled by Christ, reject the Old. You therefore accept both, because you are full in neither, but half: and one in you is not so much filled up by the other as corrupted; because half-full vessels are never filled from unlike material, but from the same and like to themselves,

as wine to wine, honey to honey, and vinegar to vinegar: over which, if you pour unlike things and not of their own kind, as gall to honey, and water to wine, and fish-sauce to vinegar: this will be called not a filling, but an adulteration. This, then, is the reason why we accept the Old Testament but little:

and because our Church, the bride of Christ, married to him poorer indeed but to a rich one, content with the goods of her husband, disdains the wealth of lowly suitors, the gifts of the Old Testament and of its author are sordid to her, and, a most diligent guardian of her own reputation, she accepts no letters but her bridegroom's. Let your Church, to be sure, usurp the Old Testament, which, like a wanton virgin forgetful of modesty, rejoices in both the gifts and the letters of another man. That lover of yours, finally, and corrupter of the modesty of the Hebrews, their God, by his stone tablet promises you gold and silver, the fullness of the belly[^1], and the land of the Canaanites[^2]. These so sordid gains have delighted you, so that you take pleasure in sinning after Christ, in being ungrateful for his so immense dowries. These entice you to perish for the God of the Hebrews after your marriage with Christ. Learn, then, even now, that you too are deceived and beguiled by his false promises. He is poor, he is needy, and he cannot even furnish what he promises: for if to his own proper spouse, I mean the Synagogue, he furnishes none of the things he promises, though she complies with him in all things, and serves him more submissively than a maidservant; what will he be able to furnish to you, who are strangers to him, and refuse the yoke of his commandments with a proud neck?

But do you indeed go on doing as you have begun, join a rough patch to an old garment, entrust new wine to worn-out wineskins[^3], serve two husbands, to please neither, make the Christian faith, in short, a Hippocentaur, neither a perfect horse nor a man: permit us to serve Christ alone, content with his immortal dowry alone, and imitating the Apostle who says: *Our sufficiency is from God, who has made us fit ministers of the New Testament*[^4]. The condition of the God of the Hebrews and ours is exceedingly different; because he cannot fulfill what he promises, and we loathe to accept it. The liberality of Christ has made us proud against his enticements.

And lest you think I have made these comparisons unfittingly, Paul first applied this likeness of conjugal discipline to us, saying: *The woman who is under a husband is bound by the law to her husband while her husband lives: but if her husband dies, she is freed from the law of her husband. Therefore, while her husband lives, she will be called an adulteress, if she is joined to another man: but if her husband dies, she will not be an adulteress if joined to another*[^5]. By this showing that those commit adultery in the spirit who, not first repudiating, and in a manner placing among the dead the author of the Law, then at last join themselves to Christ.

Which saying looks especially to those who have believed from among the Jews, namely, that they themselves should be forgetful of their first superstition. But what need is there for us of either precept in this, for whom, converted to Christ from the gentile state, the God of the Hebrews ought to seem not dead, but not even born? To be sure, to a Jew, if he believes, Adonai ought to seem deceased, but to a gentile an idol: and to each whatever he venerated before he knew Christ. For if, after the divorces of idolatry, anyone worships the God of the Hebrews and Christ alike, he differs in nothing indeed from a woman of worn-out modesty, who, after the death of one husband, has married two.

**2.** Augustine replied: Hear these things, you whose hearts Christ possesses; and see whether you bear them, except that he himself is your patience. Faustus, full of new honey, rejects the old vinegar, and Paul, full of old vinegar, poured out half, that he might take in the new honey poured over it; not to be preserved, but to be corrupted. For you see that what the apostle Paul says: *A servant of Christ Jesus, called an apostle, set apart for the Gospel of God*, is of the new honey. But what follows: *Which he had promised before through his Prophets in the holy Scriptures concerning his Son, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^6]; is of the old vinegar. Who would endure to hear this, unless the same one consoled us, saying: *There must be heresies, that those who are approved may be made manifest among you*[^7]? But what need is there to repeat the things already said sufficiently above?

For that the new patch and the old garment, and the new wine and the old wineskins[^8], do not signify the two Testaments, but two lives and two hopes: but that for understanding the two Testaments that likeness was given by the Lord: *Therefore every scribe learned in the kingdom of God is like a householder bringing forth from his treasure new things and old*[^9]—from the things we said before, let him recall who can, or at least let him review who will. For if anyone thinks two hopes are to be held, so that he serves God both on account of earthly felicity and on account of the kingdom of heaven; this hope does not hold that one, and when this one has been disturbed by some tribulation, the man, failing, will lose that one too. Hence is that also: *No one can serve two masters.* Which he expounded, saying: *You cannot serve God and mammon*[^10].

But the Old Testament, for those who understand rightly, is the prophecy of the New Testament. And so even in that first people the holy Patriarchs and Prophets, who understood what they were doing, or what was done through them, had in the New Testament that hope of eternal salvation: for they pertained to that which they understood and loved; because, although it was not yet revealed, it was already figured. But to the Old pertained those who there coveted nothing more than the temporal promises they thought of, in which they did not understand the eternal things figured and prophesied. But these things have already been said more than enough in our earlier replies.

**3.** But that is a marvelous impudence, when the sacrilegious and unclean fellowship of the Manicheans does not hesitate to boast itself even the chaste bride of Christ: in which, against the truly chaste members of the holy Church, what does it avail, except that there come to mind, against such men, that apostolic admonition: *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear, lest as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, so your minds too be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ*[^11]? For what do these men do, evangelizing to us beyond what we have received, except to corrupt us from the chastity which we keep for Christ, when they blame the law of God under the name of oldness, and praise their own error under the name of newness, as though all oldness were to be fled, or all newness to be sought; when both the apostle John sets the old commandment among things praised[^12], and the apostle Paul commands that profane novelties of words be avoided[^13]?

You, then, true bride of the true Christ, the Catholic Church, I too will address you according to my measure, such a son and servant of yours as I am, placed in you to dispense food to my fellow servants. Beware always, as you do beware, of the impious vanity of the Manicheans, now known by the peril of your own and proved by my deliverance.

That error once shook me out of your bosom: having had the experience, I fled what I ought not to have experienced. But may even my perils profit you, to whom my deliverance now serves. For unless your true and truthful bridegroom, from whose side you were made, had set in his true blood the forgiveness of sins for me, the abyss of deceit would have swallowed me up, and the serpent, with me made earth, would have devoured me beyond recovery.

Do not be deceived by the name of truth: this you alone have, both in your milk and in your bread; but in that fellowship there is only the name of it, the thing itself is not. And in your grown ones you are indeed secure: but I appeal in you to your little ones, my brothers, sons, and lords, whom you either cherish like eggs under anxious wings, or nourish like infants with milk, fruitful without corruption, virgin mother. These I appeal to in you, your tender offspring, lest they be seduced from you by chattering curiosity; but rather let them anathematize, if anyone evangelizes to them beyond what they received in you[^14]: and let them not forsake the true and truthful Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge[^15], and the great multitude of his sweetness, which he has hidden for those who fear him, but perfected for those who hope in him[^16]. But there, how can there be the words of a truthful one, in a deceitful preacher of Christ? Despise their insults: because you are well aware in yourself that you have come to love the promise of eternal life in the gifts of your bridegroom, that is, your bridegroom himself, because he himself is eternal life.

**4.** But you have not, as they in their folly suppose, been seduced to a strange god, promising fullness of the belly and the land of the Canaanites. For you understand that, even in those very promises, you, already then figured and prophesied, gave birth to the foreknowledge of the saints. Nor be moved by the stone tablet reproached with pitiable wit: because you do not have a stone heart, which those tablets signified in the earlier people. For you are the epistle of the Apostles: *Written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshly tablets of the heart*[^17].

At which words those vain men rejoice, thinking the Apostle reproached the dispensation of the Old Testament suited to that time, not understanding that he said this from a Prophet. For these words, which they unskillfully embrace, were foretold, long before they were said and fulfilled through the Apostles, by the Prophets, whom they reject. For the Prophet had said: *I will take away from them the heart of stone, and give them a heart of flesh*[^18].

Let them see whether this is not it: *Not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshly tablets of the heart.* For neither there, what was called a heart of flesh, nor here, fleshly tablets, mean this, that we should savor carnally: but because, in comparison with stone, which is without sensation, flesh feels; by the insensibility of stone is signified the heart that does not understand, and by the sensibility of flesh is signified the heart that understands.

You rather laugh at these men, who say that earth and wood and stones have sensation, and live with a more intelligent life, but that flesh lives a more stolid and obtuse one. Hence, not from truth, but from their own vanity, they are compelled to confess that the law written on tablets of stone is cleaner than their treasure on the skins of dead animals.

Or perhaps, because in their little tale they say that stones too are the bones of the princes, do they not hesitate to prefer the skins of lambs to them? Surely, then, that Ark of the Covenant covered the tablets of stone more cleanly than the goatskin covers the manuscript of these men. These things you mercifully laugh at, so that you may commend them as things to be laughed at and fled: for in that stone tablet you now understand, not with a stone heart, what suited that hard people; and yet in it you recognize the rock, your very bridegroom, him of whom Peter speaks: a living stone rejected by men, but chosen by God and honored. To them, then, he was a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal; but to you the stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner[^19]. All of which the same apostle Peter expounds, and recalls that all was foretold by the Prophets, from whom these men, condemned, are estranged. Read indeed even that tablet: do not fear, it is plainly your bridegroom's. To others that stone signified hard stolidity: but to you firm stability. By the finger of God those tablets were written[^20]: by the finger of God your bridegroom cast out demons[^21]: by the finger of God do you drive out the doctrines of lying demons searing the conscience[^22]. From this tablet you repel the adulterer who calls himself the Paraclete, that by a holy name he may seduce you. For on the fiftieth day after the Passover those tablets were given[^23]; and on the fiftieth day after the passion of your bridegroom, which that Passover prefigured, the finger of God was given, the Holy Spirit, the promised Paraclete[^24]. Do not, then, dread the tablet, by which things were once sent to you written, which you would now recognize:

only do not be under the law, lest through fear you not fulfill it; but under grace, that in you love may be the fullness of the law. For your bridegroom's friend was reviewing no other tablet, when he said: *For: You shall not commit adultery; You shall not kill; You shall not covet; and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. The love of neighbor works no evil. Love, therefore, is the fullness of the law*[^25]. For there are those two precepts, of the love of God and of the love of neighbor, set out on the individual tablets. This tablet, then, he sent ahead who, when he came, commended to you these two precepts, on which the whole Law and the Prophets hang[^26].

In the first precept is the chastity of your marriage, in the second the unity of your members: by the former you embrace divinity, by the latter you gather fellowship. These two precepts are the very ten: three pertain to God, and seven to the neighbor. O chaste tablet, in which, by an old figure, that lover and beloved of yours foretold to you a new song, on the ten-stringed psaltery[^27]: as though for you too he would stretch out his own sinews on the wood, that from sin he might condemn sin in the flesh, and the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in you[^28]. O conjugal tablet, which not without cause the adulteress hates!

**5.** For now my discourse is to you, deceitful Manichean congregation, wrapped in deceits. So, married to so many elements—or rather a harlot prostituted to demons, and impregnated with sacrilegious vanities—do you dare to tear the Catholic marriage of your Lord with the charge of unchastity? Show us your adulterers, the splendor-holder, the weigher, and Atlas the porter. For the one you say holds the heads of the elements, and suspends the world; the other, with knee fixed and strong shoulders, supports beneath such a mass, surely lest the former fail.

Where are these? Who, if they truly existed, when would they come to you, occupied with so great a business? When would they come in to you, that with your soft hand, and idle, plainly delicate, after so great a labor, you might rub the fingers of the one, the shoulders of the other? But evil demons deceive you, who play the harlot with you, that you may conceive lies and bring forth phantasms. Why, then, do you not reject the tablet of the true God, enemy to your parchments, by which you have come to love so many false gods with a mind wandering through the figments of your thoughts, beside which all the lies of the poets would be found graver and more honorable—or at least this: that among the poets the very profession of falsehood deceives no one, but in your books so great a crowd of deceits corrupts souls childish even in old men, enticed by the name of truth, with pitiable errors, when, itching in the ears, as the Apostle says, and turning their hearing away from the truth, they are turned to fables[^29]?

How, then, would you bear the sound doctrine of those tablets, where the first precept is: *Hear, Israel; the Lord your God is one God*[^30], when, delighted by the names of so many gods, you wallow in the fornication of a most base heart? Do you not recall your love-song, where you describe a supreme king reigning, eternally scepter-bearing, girt with flowery crowns and ruddy of face? Whom, if you loved him alone such, you would have to blush at: for even one husband girt with flowery crowns would displease a chaste wife. Nor can you say that this was so said or so shown by any mystical signification, since Mani is wont to be praised by you especially for no other reason than that, with the coverings of figures removed, he would speak to you the naked and proper truth itself. Properly, then, you sing of God as a scepter-bearing king, crowned with flowers. Let him at least lay aside the scepter, when he is girt with flowery crowns: that softness of luxury does not befit the severity of a royal rod. Add to this, that he is not loved by you alone: for you go on singing, and add twelve ages clothed with flowers, and full of songs, and casting their flowers into the face of the father. Where you also profess those twelve to be certain great gods, three in each of the four tracts, by which that one is surrounded. How you make him measureless, whom you say to be so surrounded, you have never been able to find. You add also innumerable kingdom-dwellers, and hosts of gods, and cohorts of angels: all of which you say God did not found, but begot from his own substance.

**6.** So you are convicted of worshiping innumerable gods, by not enduring the sound doctrine by which it is taught that one Son was born of one God, and the Holy Spirit of both. Yet these it is not lawful to call not only innumerable, but not even three gods: whose substance is not only one and the same, but whose operation also is one and the same, through that proper one and the same substance; but through the corporeal creature there is also a showing forth of each.

These things you do not understand, you do not grasp: I know, you are full, you are drunk, you are gorged with fabulous sacrilege. Digest sometime what you breathe out, and cease now to overwhelm yourself with such things: meanwhile sing what you sing, and look, if you can, at the disgrace of your fornication. For the doctrine of lying demons invited you to the feigned houses of angels, where a wholesome breeze blows, and to the fields where aromas teem, whose trees and mountains, seas and rivers, flow sweet nectar through all the ages. And you believed and feigned these things in your heart, where, made wanton and dissolute by vain recollections, you are tossed about. For when certain such things are said about the ineffable abundance of spiritual delights, they are surely said in a riddle; so that the mind which is exercised in such things may know that there is something else there to be sought and understood,

whether something of the kind is shown to the bodily senses in corporeal truth, like the fire in the bush[^31], and the serpent from the rod, and the rod from the serpent[^32], and the Lord's tunic not divided by his persecutors[^33], and the anointing of his feet or his head by the service of a woman[^34], and the branches of the multitude going before and following his colt[^35]:

or whether in the spirit, through images of bodies, they are shown figuratively, whether in a dream or in ecstasy, like the ladder to Jacob[^36], and to Daniel the stone cut without hands and grown into a mountain[^37], and to Peter that dish[^38], and to John so many things[^39]:

or whether by speech alone they are figured in the same manner, like the Song of Songs[^40], and that in the Gospel where a householder made a marriage for his son[^41], and a certain man had two sons, the frugal and the prodigal[^42], and a certain man planted a vineyard, and let it out to husbandmen[^43].

But you preach Mani especially for this, that he came last not to say such things, but rather to resolve them: so that, with the figures of the ancients opened, and his own narratives and disputations brought forth in evident light, he should hide himself in no riddle. You add as the cause of this presumption, that the ancients, in order to see or do or say such figures, knew that this one would come afterward, through whom all things would be made manifest: but he, who knew that no one would come after him, wove his opinions in no allegorical roundabout ways.

What, then, does your affection, sordid with carnal desires, do in the fields and wooded mountains, and the flowery crowns, and the teeming aromas? If they are not riddles of reason, they are phantasms of thought, or the madness of frenzy. But if they are said to be riddles, why do you not flee the adulterer, who promises open truth, in order to entice, and mocks with fabulous deceit those he has enticed? Do not his ministers, themselves wretched and poisoned with such vanities, usually set this bait on their hook from the apostle Paul, where he says: *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part; but when that which is perfect has come, that which is in part will be done away*; and: *We see now through a mirror in a riddle, but then face to face*[^44]? So that, forsooth, the apostle Paul knew in part, and prophesied in part, seeing through a mirror and in a riddle, all of which is to be done away when Mani comes, bringing what is perfect, where truth is seen face to face. O wanton, unclean one, without shame you still prattle these things, you still feed on the winds, you still embrace the idols of your heart!

Have you, then, seen face to face the scepter-bearing king reigning, girt with flowery crowns, and the hosts of gods, and the great splendor-holder, bearing six faces and mouths, and flashing with light; and another king of honor, surrounded by armies of Angels; and another, an adamant warrior-hero, holding a spear in his right hand and a shield in his left; and another, a glorious king driving three wheels, of fire, water, and wind; and the great Atlas bearing the world on his shoulders, and propping it on both sides with his arms, his knee fixed? These and a thousand other monstrosities, have you seen them face to face, or does the doctrine of lying demons sing these to you through the mouths of deceivers, and you do not know it? Woe to you, unhappy one! Behold by what phantasms you are defiled, behold what vanities you lick up in place of truth:

and drunk on serpentine cups, you dare, on account of a stone tablet, to insult the matronly modesty of the spouse of the only Son of God; because she, now not under the tutelage of the Law, but under the mastership of grace, neither proud of works nor broken by terrors, lives by faith and hope and charity, made an Israel in whom there is no guile[^45], and hearing what is written there: *The Lord your God is one God*[^46]: which you, not hearing, have poured out your fornication into so many feigned gods.

**7.** How would those Tablets not be enemies to you, in which the second precept is: *You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain*[^47]; since you have set even Christ himself—who, for the sake of cleansing the carnal from carnal vanity, truly arose even to fleshly eyes in the truth of the flesh—in the vanity of deceit? How would the third precept, about the rest of the sabbath, not be against you, who, a soul restless with the illusions of so many figments, are tossed about? These three precepts, how they pertain to the love of God, when will you grasp, when will you savor, when will you love? You are immoderate, and foul, and contentious: you have swelled, you have become vain, you have become worthless, you have exceeded your measure, you have defiled your beauty, you have disturbed your order. Such I was with you, I know you.

How, then, am I now to teach you that these three precepts pertain to the love of God, from whom, and through whom, and in whom are all things[^48]? How do you understand this, when you are not even permitted, by the detestable perversity of your error, to know and observe those seven which pertain to the love of neighbor, by which the fellowship of human life is held together?

Among which the first commandment is: *Honor your father and your mother*[^49]: which Paul too recalls as the first commandment with a promise, himself likewise enjoining the same[^50]. But you have learned, by demonic teaching, to reckon your parents enemies, because they bound you in flesh through intercourse, and in this way surely laid unclean fetters on your god. Hence too you violate the precept that follows, which is: *You shall not commit adultery*[^51], in such a way that in marriage you especially detest this, that children are procreated; and so, while your Hearers beware lest the women with whom they mingle conceive, you make them even adulterers of their own wives.

For they take them by the law of marriage, the tablets proclaiming it to be for the sake of procreating children: but by your law, fearing lest they affect a particle of their god with the filth of the flesh, they mingle with the women by a shameless union only to satisfy lust; but they take up children unwillingly, for the sake of which alone marriages are to be joined. How, then, do you not forbid marriage—which the Apostle foretold of you so long before[^52]—when you try to take away from marriage that from which marriage exists? With this taken away, the husbands will be basely lovers; the wives, harlots; the bridal chambers, brothels; the fathers-in-law, panderers.

And by this you do not keep even that precept which is: *You shall not kill*[^53], from the perversity of the same error. For while you fear lest a member of your god be bound in flesh, you do not give bread to the hungry: here dreading a false murder, there you perpetrate a true one. And so, if you run into a starving man who could die unless you help him by holding out food; you will now be held a murderer either by the law of God, if you do not give it; or by the law of Mani, if you do give it.

What? The rest of the precepts of the Decalogue, how will you keep them? Will you abstain from theft, so that some man or other should devour bread, or any food, to be slaughtered in his bowels, rather than that you, if you could, should snatch it from him, and run to the workshop of the belly of your Elect, so that by your theft your god might not fall into a heavier bond, and might be rescued from that into which it had fallen? Further, if you are caught in that same theft; will you not swear by your very god that you did not take it? For what will such a god do to you, to whom you say: I have sworn falsely by you, but for you; unless you wanted me to bring destruction on myself, while I rendered you honor?

Thus you will despise that commandment of the Law too: *You shall not bear false witness*, on account of the members of your god, so that you free them from their fetters not only by false witness, but even by a false oath.

And now what follows: *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*[^54], ought to be fulfilled in you: and this one alone I see that you are compelled by no necessity of your error to violate. But if it is wrong to covet another's wife, consider what it is to set oneself before others to be coveted: and recall your handsome gods and beautiful goddesses, offering themselves to be ardently coveted, the former by the female princes of darkness, the latter by the males: so that, roused into the lust of enjoying, and burning with longing for their embraces, they may rescue from them that god of yours fettered everywhere, and needing such great shamefulness of his own that he may be freed.

For as to not coveting a neighbor's property, which is the last commandment of the Decalogue, whence can you do it, wretched one? Does not your very god lie to you that he is fashioning for himself new ages in a foreign land, where, after a false victory, you may swell with a false triumph? Which, when you now desire it with insane vanity, and believe that same land of the race of darkness to be joined by the closest neighborhood to your substance, you surely covet a neighbor's property.

Rightly is the tablet an enemy to you, containing such good commandments much contrary to your error. For those three which pertain to the love of God, you are wholly ignorant of, you wholly do not keep: but these seven by which human fellowship is not harmed, if you ever keep them, you are either restrained by shame, lest you be confounded among men; or broken by fear, lest you be punished by public laws; or you shudder at the evil deed by some good habit; or by the very natural law you perceive that you do unjustly to another what you do not want done to yourself by another: yet you feel how greatly your error compels you to go to the contrary, both when you follow it and when you do not follow it, since you either do this which you do not want to suffer, or do not do it for the reason that you do not want to suffer it.

**8.** But this true bride of Christ, whom you insult, with most impudent face, about the stone tablet, understands what difference there is between the letter and the spirit[^55], which two are also called, in another way, the Law and grace: and now serving God not in the oldness of the letter, but in the newness of the spirit[^56], she is now not under the law, but under grace. For she is not blinded by being litigious; but, meek, she attends to the words of the Apostle, that she may understand what he calls the law, under which he no longer wants us to be, because it was set for the sake of transgression, until the seed should come to whom the promise was made[^57]: and because it entered in for this, that the offense might abound; but where the offense abounded, grace superabounded[^58].

Nor for this reason, however, does she call that same law sin, because without grace it does not give life; for it rather increases guilt with transgression added: *For where there is no law, neither is there transgression*[^59]; and therefore through itself, when it is letter alone without spirit, that is, law without grace, it only makes men guilty: but she sets before herself what those who understand less might think, and opens what she means, when she says: *What then shall we say? Is the law sin? Far be it: but I did not know sin except through the law. For I did not know covetousness, unless the law said: You shall not covet. So sin, taking occasion, deceived me through the commandment, and through it killed me. And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good. Did then that which is good become death to me? Far be it: but sin, that it might appear sin, worked death in me through what is good*[^60].

These things she understands, whom you insult; because, groaning, she asks, because, humble, she seeks, because, meek, she knocks: and so she sees that the law is not reproached, when it is said: *The letter kills, but the spirit gives life*[^61]; just as knowledge is not reproached, when it is said: *Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up.* For surely he himself had said: *We know that we all have knowledge*; and then he adds: *Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up*[^62]. Why, then, did he himself have whereby to be puffed up, except because, with love, knowledge not only does not puff up, but even strengthens?

So the letter with the spirit, and the law with grace, is no longer called letter and law in that way in which, through itself, it kills with the offense abounding. For thus the law was called also the strength of sin[^63], when it increases its noxious delight, through severe prohibition. Nor yet even so is it evil: but sin, that it might appear sin, worked death through what is good. So many things are noxious to some, although they are not evil. For you too, when your eyes hurt, close the windows even against your god the sun.

This bride of Christ, then, now dead to the law, that is, to sin, which is made more abundant by the prohibition of the law, when the law without grace commands but does not help: dead, then, to such a law, that she may be another's who rose from the dead, distinguishes these things without injury to the law, lest she commit sacrilege against its author:

which you do against him whom you do not understand to be the author of the good; when you hear the Apostle saying: *And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good.* Behold, the author of the good is he who seems to you one of the princes of darkness. Attend to the truth, it strikes your eyes. Behold, the apostle Paul says: *The law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good.*

Behold, whose author is he who sent ahead that tablet, which in your folly you deride, in the dispensation of a great mystery. For the same law, which was given through Moses, became grace and truth through Jesus Christ[^64], when the spirit was added to the letter, that the righteousness of the law might begin to be fulfilled, which, unfulfilled, made men guilty even by transgression. For it is not that one law is holy and just and good, and another that through which sin works death, to which we ought to die, that we may be another's who rose from the dead: but it is one and the same. Behold, follow on, read. *But sin*, he says, *that it might appear sin, worked death in me through what is good, that the sinner, or sin, might become beyond measure through the commandment.* Deaf, blind one, hear, see. *Through what is good*, he says, *it worked death in me.*

Therefore the law is always good; whether it harms those empty of grace, or profits those full of grace, it is always good: just as the sun is always good, because every creature of God is good[^65], whether it harms aching eyes, or soothes healthy ones. Accordingly, what health of the eyes is for seeing the sun, this grace is for minds for fulfilling the law. And just as healthy eyes do not die to the delight of the sun, but to those harsh strokes of the rays, by which, beaten back, they were driven into denser darkness: so the soul, made whole through the love of the spirit, is said to be dead, not to the righteousness of the law, but to that guilt and transgression which the law, through the letter, when grace was lacking, made. And so both are said of it: both: *The law is good, if one uses it lawfully*; and what follows: *Knowing this, that the law is not set for the just*[^66]; because he has no need of the terrifying letter whom righteousness itself delights.

**9.** This bride of Christ, rejoicing in the hope of her full salvation, wishes for you too a good conversion from fables to truth, lest, dreading Adonai as though an adulterer, you remain with the most cunning adulterer, the serpent. For "Adonai" is a Hebrew word, and is interpreted "Lord," in that manner in which God alone is called Lord: just as "latria," which is a Greek word, is interpreted "service," not any service whatever, but that by which God alone is served: just as "Amen" is interpreted "true," not anywhere and in any way, but by a mystical religion. And if it be asked of you whence you too have these words; besides the Hebrew writings, or those which are from the Hebrew, you find none. The Church of Christ, then, does not fear the casting of these names against it: it understands, and loves; nor does it care for the unskilled insulter: and the things it does not yet understand, it believes to be such as some it has experienced were, which, before being understood, were so.

Let anyone cast at her that she has come to love Emmanuel; she laughs at the ignorance of that man, she embraces the truth of this name. Let him cast at her that she has loved the Messiah; she repels the slain adversary, she holds the anointed master. So she desires that you too be healed of vain errors, and built upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets[^67].

Whom you call a Hippocentaur, not knowing what you say, nor attending to what your fable has made for you, when from part of your god and part of the land of darkness you fashioned a false world in your heart. Is not he, then, a Hippocentaur, half-beast and half-god? Truly, since he is not even to be called a Hippocentaur. But what he is, attend you, and blush and grow gentle, that you may shudder at your corruption by the adulterer serpent: whose cunning, if you did not think it was to be believed in Moses, you ought to have guarded against in Paul,

who, wishing to present the true Church a chaste virgin to Christ, says: *I fear lest, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, so your minds be corrupted from the simplicity and chastity which is in Christ*[^68]. Although you heard this, you have nonetheless so lost your wits, and been made mad by his poisoned incantations, that, while the same serpent persuaded many other heresies of one thing and another, to you he persuaded even that he himself is Christ. Further, if many wander, entangled in his various and manifold deceits, who yet confess this admonition of the Apostle to be true: how greatly are you adulterated, how far off prostituted, you who hold for Christ the very one by whom the Apostle cries out that Eve, the bride of Christ, was seduced and corrupted, so that by such an admonition he might guard the virgin bride from him for Christ? He has darkened your heart, who wallows with you in the phantasms of luminous groves. What are they, where are they, whence are they, his faithful promises? O drunken one, but not from wine[^69]!

**10.** Surely you have reviled, with sacrilegious impudence, the God of the Prophets, because, though the Jews themselves serve him, he has not furnished what he promised. Nor indeed did you say what he promised and did not furnish, lest there you be convicted either of something already furnished which you do not understand, or of something still to come which you do not believe. To you what has been promised and presented, whence faith might be made to you that you would receive triumphs of new ages over the land of darkness?

If you bring forth some prophets in whom we may read that future Manicheans were foretold with praise, so that on this very account you might think something already furnished to you, because we see that you exist; you must first prove that Mani himself did not feign prophets for you, who wished you to believe him. For he does not think a lie base: or can he doubt to show false prophets in sheep's clothing, who, praising Christ, asserts that he showed false scars in his members?

But I read that you were plainly foretold, not only by the prophets sometimes more obscurely, but even by the Apostle more expressly. But see how: *The Spirit*, he says, *says manifestly, that in the last times some will withdraw from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and to doctrines of demons in the hypocrisy of liars, having their conscience seared, forbidding to marry, abstaining from foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving by the faithful, and by those who have known the truth: for every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be cast away which is received with thanksgiving*[^70]. How these things have been fulfilled in you both touches, more clearly than light, the eyes of all who have known you, and we have shown above according to the time.

**11.** But this woman, whom apostolic teaching, presenting a chaste virgin to one husband, Christ, admonishes to beware of the deceit of the serpent by which you were corrupted, recognizes the God of the Prophets, the true God, her own God: in his last promise she securely believes, whose so great promises she already holds fulfilled; nor does anyone say that the Prophets whom she asserts to be in the books of the Jews were feigned for the present time. For what more incredible was being promised than that which was said to Abraham: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^71]. And what do we now hold more surely furnished?

That, surely, is his last promise, which the prophet briefly recalls thus: *Blessed are they who dwell in your house; for ever and ever they will praise you*[^72]. For with all want ended, and the last enemy, death, destroyed[^73], the perpetual praise of God will be the occupation of those at rest: to which no one will any longer come, from which no one will any longer depart.

Which the prophet recalls elsewhere thus: *Praise the Lord, Jerusalem; praise your God, Zion: for he has strengthened the bars of your gates, he has blessed your sons within you*[^74]. With the gates closed, none will enter, none will go out. Which the bridegroom himself too says in the Gospel, that to the foolish virgins, even knocking, he will not open[^75].

This Jerusalem, the holy Church, the bride of Christ, is described more copiously and richly in the Apocalypse of John. Let the chaste virgin not believe this prophetic promise, if she does not already hold what was promised her, through that same prophecy, to be in this time to come: *Hear, daughter, and see, and incline your ear, and forget your people, and your father's house; for the king has desired your beauty, for he himself is your God, and the daughters of Tyre will adore him with gifts: the rich of the people will entreat your face. All the glory of the king's daughter is within, in golden fringes, clothed about with varied things: virgins will be brought to the king after her, her companions will be brought to you, in joy and exultation they will be led into the king's temple. In place of your fathers, sons are born to you, you will set them princes over all the earth: they will be mindful of your name in every progeny and generation, therefore the peoples will confess to you forever and unto age upon age*[^76].

But you, unhappy one, corrupted by the serpent, when do you even try to think what is the beauty of the king's daughter within? For it is the chastity of the mind, where you have been violated, so that your eyes were opened to loving and adoring the sun and the moon; and so, by the just judgment of God, you were estranged from the tree of life, which is eternal and inner wisdom; and you called and thought nothing else truth and wisdom, except this light, which, drawn in through ill-opened eyes, and increased immeasurably, and varied manifoldly through fabulous images, you turned over in an unchaste mind. These are your fornications, too greatly to be abominated.

And yet patiently consider them, and return to me, says the Truth. Return to me, and you will be cleansed, you will be restored, if you are confounded at yourself, and poured back into me. Hear this, this the true Truth says, which neither fought with deceptive forms against the race of darkness, nor redeemed you with deceitful blood.
