# Book 9

**1.** Faustus said: Why do you not accept the Old Testament?

If the Apostles, born under it, were permitted to depart from it, why am I not permitted not to take up that under which I was not born? For we are all born Gentiles, not Jews, nor, in the end, Christians; but the Old Testament leads some to itself out of that same Gentile state, and makes them Jews; the New leads others, and initiates them as Christians: just as if two trees, a sweet one and a bitter one, transferred into themselves, by their roots, the strength of one earth, to be changed by their qualities. The Apostles, then, passing into the sweet from the bitter—how mad will I be, if I am turned into the bitter from the sweet?

**2.** Augustine replied: Why, then, does the Apostle, who you say, having left Judaism, passed from bitterness into sweetness, rather say that the branches broken off from there are those who, from that very people, refused to believe in Christ; and that in the very root of the olive, that is, the origin of the holy Hebrews, the nations were grafted, like a wild olive, that they might become partakers of the richness of the olive?

For when he warned the Gentiles, on account of the fall of the Jews, not to be proud, he spoke thus: *For I say to you Gentiles: as long indeed as I am the Apostle of the Gentiles, I will glorify my ministry, if by any means I can provoke to emulation my own flesh, that I may save some of them. For if their rejection is the reconciliation of the world, what will their acceptance be, but life from the dead? But if the first fruits are holy, so is the lump; and if the root is holy, so are the branches. But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, being a wild olive, were grafted in among them, and have become a sharer of the root and of the richness of the olive, do not boast against the branches. But if you boast, you do not bear the root, but the root bears you.*

You say, then: *The branches were broken off that I might be grafted in. Well. They were broken off because of unbelief. But you stand by faith. Do not be high-minded, but fear: for if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you. See, then, the goodness and the severity of God: toward those who fell, indeed, severity; but toward you, goodness, if you remain in his goodness; otherwise you too will be cut off. And they too, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in: for God is able to graft them in again.*

*For if you were cut from the wild olive that is natural to you, and grafted, against nature, into a good olive, how much more will these, who are according to nature, be grafted into their own olive? For I do not want you to be ignorant, brethren, of this mystery, that you may not be wise in your own eyes: that blindness has come in part upon Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles should come in, and so all Israel should be saved*[^1].

You see, then, that you, who are unwilling to be grafted into this root, are not indeed like the broken branches, as the carnal and impious people of the Jews is, but have remained in the bitterness of the wild olive. For what does it savor of, except the wild olive of the Gentiles, to adore the sun and the moon? Unless perhaps you think you are no longer in the wild olive of the Gentiles, because you have added thorns of a new kind, and have fashioned, not with a craftsman's hand, but with a perverse heart, a false Christ, whom you worship along with the sun and the moon.

Be grafted, then, into the root of the olive, to which the Apostle rejoices to have been restored, he who had been among the branches broken off through unbelief. For he says he was freed from there, when he rejoices to have passed from Judaism to Christ, because Christ was always preached in that root and in that tree: those who, when he came, did not believe in him, were broken off from there; and those who believed were grafted in there:

to whom, lest they be proud, it is said: *Do not be high-minded, but fear: for if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.* But lest there be despair of the broken ones themselves, he says a little later: *And they too, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in: for God is able to graft them in again. For if you were cut from the wild olive that is natural to you, and grafted, against nature, into a good olive, how much more will these, who are according to nature, be grafted into their own olive?*[^2]

Behold the source from which he too glories, freed from the breaking off, and restored to the richness of the root. Let those among you, then, whom impiety broke off from there, return and be grafted in again. But those who were never there, let them come, cut off from their natural barrenness, to be partakers of the fruitfulness.

[^1]: Rom 11:13ff.
[^2]: Rom 11:23-24.
