# Book XXXI

**1.** FAUSTUS: *To the pure all things are pure, but to the impure and defiled nothing is pure; rather, both their mind and their conscience are defiled*[^1]. This too must be considered—whether it is to your advantage to believe it was said by Paul; for thus far it has stood that Moses and the Prophets were not only driven by demons, when they brought forward such great laws about the differences of foods, but also that they themselves were impure and defiled in mind and conscience, so that what follows might fittingly fall upon them too: *they profess that they know God, but by their works they deny him*[^2]. And to whom does this better apply than to the Prophets and to Moses, who are proved to have lived far otherwise than befitted those who knew God? Yet until now I had supposed that, apart from adulteries and frauds and murders, there was nothing else by which Moses and the Prophets might seem to have had polluted consciences; but now, with this chapter showing it, it is given to know that they were of a defiled mind on this account too, because they supposed something to be defiled. To what purpose, then, do you suppose that even such men could attain the vision of the divine majesty, when it is written that none but those who are pure in heart can see God[^3]? But even if these had been chaste from unlawful deeds, this superstition alone of abstaining from certain foods, if it defiles the mind, could have denied them the sight of the divinity. Now therefore has that boasting of Daniel too and of the three boys vanished and been at once extinguished—who themselves, up to this preaching which holds nothing to be unclean, were reckoned in Judaism most chaste and of good mind, because, mindful of their fathers' traditions, they kept themselves unstained with all zeal from the banquets of the Gentiles, and especially from things offered to idols[^4]. For now at last it has appeared that they too were defiled in mind and conscience, then most of all, when they kept their mouth from blood and deadly foods.

**2.** But perhaps ignorance excuses them; for this Christian faith not yet having appeared, which would teach that all things are pure to the pure, they themselves supposed certain things to be not pure. But you—what excuse do you now use, if, when Paul cries out that nothing is not pure, and calls abstinence from foods a doctrine of demons[^5], and calls those defiled in mind who suppose anything to be defiled, you not only abstain, as we said, but even seize glory from it, and believe yourselves the more acceptable to Christ the more you will be abstinent from foods—that is, according to this verdict, the more defiled in mind and the more polluted in conscience? What of the fact that, since there are three religions in the world which place the purgation of the mind equally in chastities and abstinence, though by a most diverse rite—I mean the Jews, the Christians, and the Gentiles—it cannot be discovered from which of these religions this saying comes, which teaches that nothing is unholy? Surely not from Judaism; likewise not from Paganism, because it too distinguishes foods; nor is there any difference, except that the Hebrew differs from the Pagans in certain animals. There remains the Christian faith; and if you think it proper to this to reckon nothing polluted, the first thing is that you confess that there is no Christian among you. For all among you—to be silent about the rest—nonetheless reckon carrion and things offered to idols to be no small defilement[^6]. Or if this too is done by you by the right of Christianity, then it is not even the verdict of this religion, which takes away utterly all abstinence from unclean things. To what purpose, then, could this have been said by Paul, which suits no religion? For the Apostle did not so much put off religion as change the rite, when from a Jew he became a Christian. But he who wrote this chapter seems to me to have rested on no religion at all.

**3.** Therefore, if henceforth, seeking from the Scriptures something to the injury of our faith, you find anything, remember to bring it against us only then, when you have seen clearly that it is not contrary to you—as also that which you are accustomed to bring forward about Peter, namely that the same once saw a vessel let down from heaven, in which were all kinds of animals and serpents; and when he was astonished and wondering, a voice was heard saying to him: *Peter, whatever you see in the vessel, kill and eat*. And he, answering the same: *Lord*, he said, *I will not touch what is common and unclean*. And again the voice to him: *What I have sanctified, call not unclean*[^7]. Now although this very thing seems through allegory to signify something else, and not an indifference of foods, yet because it pleases you to understand this too in that way, you must therefore of necessity feed indiscriminately on all beasts, and on vipers and snakes, and on every other serpentine kind, according to Peter's vision. For thus far you will prove yourselves truly to be obeying the voice which it is reported he heard; and yet you must always remember this too, that hereby Moses and the Prophets were condemned, who supposed many things to be polluted of those which God had sanctified, according to the verdict of this voice.

**4.** AUGUSTINE: When the Apostle says, *To the pure all things are pure*[^8], he willed that the natures themselves be understood, which God created, according to what Moses wrote in Genesis: *And God made all things; and behold, they were very good*[^9]; not the significations, according to which God, through the same Moses, distinguished the clean from the unclean[^10]. Since on this we have already said many things and in many places, it will now suffice to have briefly noted it. Accordingly, those who, already in the time of the revelation of the New Testament, would still think that those shadows of things to come were so to be kept that without them the Gentiles could not receive the salvation which is in Christ, the Apostle calls impure, because they were carnal in their wisdom; and unbelievers, because they did not distinguish the time of grace from the time of the Law. To them he says nothing is clean, because both in the things they refused and in the things they took, they did not use them holily or justly—just as all unbelievers indeed, but you Manicheans especially, to whom nothing whatever is clean. For not even the food itself which you take, although you separate it with great care as if from the contagion of flesh, is clean to you, who say it was created by none but the devil. In it too you claim to purge, by eating, your god bound and polluted. At least you might seem clean to yourselves, by whose bellies he deserves to be purged. But you contend that your bodies too are the nature and workmanship of the race of darkness, and that your souls are defiled by those very bodies. What, then, is clean to you? Not the things you take, not that into which you pass the things you take, not you yourselves who purge what is taken. You see, then, on whose account the Apostle then said that verdict; yet he said such a verdict as would hold all unbelievers and impure persons, but would especially and most of all convict you. All things, then, are pure to the pure, according to the nature in which they were created; yet not all things were pure according to signification to the first people of the Jews; nor are all things suited to us, whether for the health of the body or for the custom of human society; but when each thing is rendered its own and keeps the natural order, all things are pure to the pure; but to the impure and unbelievers, such as you especially are, nothing is pure. The remaining words of the Apostle which follow, you yourselves would say to yourselves wholesomely, if you wished your seared conscience to be healed. For it follows: *but their mind and conscience are defiled*.
