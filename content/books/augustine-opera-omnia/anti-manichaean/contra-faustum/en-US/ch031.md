# Book XXX

**1.** FAUSTUS: Of you Paul long ago wrote: that *some will depart from the faith, giving heed to deceiving spirits and doctrines of demons, speaking lies in hypocrisy, having their conscience seared, forbidding to marry, abstaining from foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving by the faithful*[^1]. I will never agree that these things were said by the Apostle of you, unless you first confess that Moses and the Prophets brought forward doctrines of demons, and were interpreters of a deceiving and malign spirit, who carefully teach that one must abstain from pork and the other flesh which they call unclean. About these things, then, you must first deliberate, and think long and much: how these things ought to be taken—whether they seem to have spoken these things in God or in a demon. For thus far either Moses and the Prophets will be condemned along with us, or we will be absolved along with them. For this present verdict of yours is not just: that we, who hold that only the priestly class of men among the people ought to abstain from flesh, should seem to you to be followers of the doctrine of demons; but that your Prophets, and Moses himself indeed, who first forbade not only to the chief priests but to the whole human race indiscriminately swine's flesh, and hare, and hedgehog flesh, and cuttlefish and squid, and the other kinds of fish lacking scales[^2], you reckon to have spoken these things not in a deceiving spirit, nor in the doctrine of demons, but rather in God and in the Holy Spirit. Therefore, though for the moment I grant you that Paul said this, I will not have been overcome by you until you condemn Moses and the Prophets—so that what perhaps you would never have done, convinced by reason and truth, you now seem to do for the belly's sake: to blaspheme Moses.

**2.** There is also another thing among you about the three boys in Daniel, by which you will likewise be vehemently confounded, if it stands that to abstain from foods is a religion of demons. For they are read to have abstained not only from things forbidden by the Law, but even from things allowed[^3]—whom you are accustomed greatly to admire and to number among the martyrs; whereas they too followed a doctrine of demons, if it is established that this is the testimony of the Apostle. To this add that Daniel himself reports that he fasted for three weeks of days, and did not eat flesh, nor drink wine, while he prayed for his people[^4]. Why, then, does he too here magnify himself in a doctrine of demons, and seize glory from the contrivance of a deceiving spirit?

**3.** But of you what shall I say?—that is, of the more Christian among you, of whom some indeed abstain from pork, but very many from every quadruped, and others from absolutely every animal; and on this account the whole Church holds them in its eyes, and receiving them with the highest veneration, only does not reckon them gods. And you do not see, you unteachable ones, that if this testimony is true and was spoken by the Apostle, they too have been mocked by the doctrines of demons. But what, again, shall we say of that which one might surely dare to thwart or deny, since it is established among all and equally throughout the whole world celebrated every year with all zeal in the Catholic assembly? I mean Lent—which whoever among you thinks must be duly observed, must of necessity abstain from all those things which this chapter says were created by God for us to receive, and besides calls the abstinence from the aforesaid a doctrine of demons. What then of you also, dearest ones? Do you then live by the rite of demons, when these mysteries of Christ's passion are celebrated by you, and are you caught by the deceit of a deceiving spirit, and do you speak lies in hypocrisy, and have your conscience seared? But if none of these things apply to you—then neither to us. What, then, does this chapter mean, or by whom shall we suppose it was written, and against whom—since it confirms neither the traditions of the Old Testament nor the decrees of the New? Seeing that it teaches that this is to be abstained from by all, as appears from you yourselves, and that, indeed, by some though not by all; but this verdict of yours wishes all abstinence whatever from fleshly foods to be doctrines of demons. And if you too believe this, I will say the same thing again and again: condemn Moses, renounce the Prophets, bear an equal verdict about yourselves also—because, as they always abstain from certain foods, so you at times abstain from all.

**4.** But if Moses and the Prophets, when they judge between foods, seem to you to sanction the law of God, and not of demons; if Daniel observed three weeks in the Holy Spirit; if Ananias, Azariah, and Mishael, those boys, by the prompting of a divine mind, preferred to use vegetables and pulse; if, finally, among you as many as abstain do so not driven by demons; if Lent without wine and flesh is kept by you not superstitiously, but by divine law: see, I pray, see, that it would be the height of madness to suppose that Paul said that abstinence from all foods is a doctrine of demons, and to forbid marriage—just as neither is that which he says, that virgins too should be dedicated to Christ, a doctrine of demons; which you, reading likewise without consideration, as the rest, presently turn back upon us; and you do not see that hence both your virgins are marked as captured by a doctrine of demons, and you are shown to be the chief priests of demons, who ever vie to incite them to this profession by your persuasions, so that already in almost all the churches there is held among you a greater number of virgins than of married women. Why, then, do you too not at last desist from such undertakings? Why do you lead the wretched daughters of men into deceit, if in them not the will of Christ is fulfilled, but of demons? And yet I should like you first to answer us this: whether to make virgins at all is a doctrine of demons, or only to make them by forbidding marriage. If by forbidding, it is nothing to us; for we too judge it as foolish to hinder one who is willing, as wicked and impious enough to force one who is unwilling. But if to favor this purpose too, and not to resist one who is willing, you think this also a doctrine of demons—I say nothing now of your peril; I myself now fear for the Apostle, lest he seem to have brought a doctrine of demons into Iconium then, when he kindled Thecla, already pledged to the marriage chamber, into love by his discourse of perpetual virginity. But what shall we say even of the Master himself, and the author of all sanctity, Jesus, and the celibate bridegroom of the girls of this very profession, who in the Gospel, marking out three kinds of eunuchs—one by nature, another made, the third voluntary—nevertheless assigned the palm to these: *those who have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven*[^5], signifying virgins and boys who, with the very desire of marrying cut out from their hearts, dwell in his Church ever in the stead of eunuchs, as it were in a royal house? What then—does this too seem to you a doctrine of demons, and spoken in a deceiving spirit? And who else will there be speaking in God, if Paul and Christ are proved to have been priests of demons? For I pass over the rest of the Apostles of the same our Lord—Peter and Andrew, Thomas, and that one inexperienced in Venus among the rest, the blessed John, who in various ways sang of the profession of this good thing among virgins and boys with divine proclamation, leaving to us, and indeed to you yourselves, the pattern for making virgins. But these, as I said, I pass over, because you have excluded them from the canon, and you can easily, with your sacrilegious mind, impute to them doctrines of demons. Will you then be able to say the same of Christ too, or of the apostle Paul, who likewise everywhere, it is established, both always preferred in word the unmarried to the married, and showed this also by deed toward the most holy Thecla? But if this was not a doctrine of demons, which both Paul announced to Thecla, and the other Apostles to others, who can now believe that this was recorded by him as though the desire and doctrine of demons—even the persuasion to sanctity? There is, then, no reason for the moment why you should suppose that we make virgins by exhortations alone, and not by forbidding marriage. For this very thing is implanted in us too; and assuredly that man is to be thought, not only foolish, but mad, who supposes that what is publicly permitted can be forbidden by a private law—I mean this very thing, to marry. Therefore we too exhort the willing that they should persevere, yet we do not force the unwilling to come forward. For we know how much the will, how much even the very force of nature, avails against a public law, let alone against a private one, to which it is free to answer: I will not. If, then, to make virgins in this way is without crime, we too are beyond fault; but if in any way whatever to make virgins is a crime, you too are guilty. Now with what mind or purpose you bring forward this chapter against us, I do not see.

**5.** AUGUSTINE: Hear, then, what you confess you do not see—with what mind or purpose we bring forward this chapter against you. Not because you abstain from flesh, for some even of our first fathers did this, as you mention—yet not for the sake of condemning it, but of signifying something which you do not understand, and of which I have already spoken as much as seemed sufficient in the earlier parts of this work. And Christians—not heretics, but Catholics—for the sake of subduing the body, that the soul may be more humbled in prayer, not because they believe it to be unclean, abstain not only from flesh but even from certain fruits of the earth; either always, as a few do, or on certain days and seasons, as nearly all do through Lent—each one the more or the less as he wills or is able. But you deny that the creation itself is good, and you call it unclean, because the devil, you say, works flesh from the more dreggy matter of evil; and on this account you cast it away in horror, as the more unclean and more savage bonds of your god. Yet to your Hearers, whom you said were distinct from the class of priests, you grant that these things may be eaten by way of indulgence—just as the Apostle grants to some by way of indulgence, not all conjugal intercourse, even that which is done for the sake of begetting alone, but that which is done through incontinence, yet still with a spouse[^6]. For nothing is granted by way of indulgence except sin. This is what you hold about all food of flesh; this you yourselves learned from your heresy, and teach your Hearers; but to them you grant that it is to be pardoned, on account of which they minister necessities to you, as I said—not saying that it is not a sin, but bestowing pardon on those who sin; while you yourselves abstain from every such thing as from an evil and unclean contagion. And through this, that which follows those words of the Apostle, with which you ended your citation of this chapter, is precisely what makes us bring this testimony against you—which you too, I think, knew, so that you did not set down those words first, and said to us at the end: "With what mind or purpose you bring forward this chapter against us, I do not see"; whereas you preferred to be silent about this very purpose of ours rather than to mention it. For when the Apostle had said: *abstaining from foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving by the faithful*, he went on to say: *and by those who have known the truth; for every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be cast away which is received with thanksgiving; for it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer*[^7]. These are the things you deny; with this mind, this will, this opinion you keep from foods of this kind, namely that they are evil and unclean by nature, not by signification. In which matter you without doubt blaspheme their creator; this is what pertains to a doctrine of demons. Do not, then, wonder that this was prophesied of you by the Holy Spirit so long before.

**6.** Again, if you exhorted to virginity as the apostolic teaching exhorts—*he who gives in marriage does well, and he who does not give in marriage does better*[^8]—so that you would say that marriage is good, but virginity better, as the Church does, which is truly the Church of Christ, the Holy Spirit would not foretell you thus, saying: *forbidding to marry*[^9]. For he forbids who says this is evil, not he who sets another better thing before this good. Finally, you detest above all that intercourse which alone is honorable and conjugal, and which even the marriage tablets set forth, for the sake of begetting children; whence in truth you forbid, not so much intercourse, as marriage. For there is intercourse even for the sake of lust, but there is marriage only for the sake of children. And do not say that you do not forbid it, because you tolerate, without breach of friendship, many of your Hearers unwilling or unable to obey in this. For you have the one thing in the doctrine of your error, the other in the necessity of fellowship. Hence it is that I deferred a little while ago to say why Christ's death seemed to you to be preached as deceitful and simulated, but not also his birth. For you preach and praise death as the separation of the soul—that is, of the nature of your god—from the body of his enemies, that is, from the figment of the devil; and on this account you believed it a worthy thing which Christ might commend, even if not by dying, yet by simulating death. But in birth, because you believe that your god is not loosed but rather bound, you were unwilling to believe Christ even deceitfully to have imagined this—so that Mary would not so displease you if she had had intercourse and not conceived, as she displeases you because she did not have intercourse and yet gave birth. You see, then, how great a difference there is between those who exhort to virginity, setting a greater good before a lesser good, and those who forbid marriage, more vehemently accusing the intercourse of propagation, which alone is properly nuptial; how great a difference between those who abstain from foods for the sake of signifying a sacrament, or for the sake of chastising the body, and those who abstain from foods which God created by saying that God did not create them. Accordingly, the former is the teaching of the Prophets and Apostles, the latter of lying demons.
