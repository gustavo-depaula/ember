# Book XXVII

**1.** FAUSTUS: If Jesus was not born, neither did he suffer; but if he suffered, then he was also born. It is not to your advantage, believe me, to seek a consequence of nature in these matters; otherwise your whole faith will be weakened. For you too believe that Jesus was born of a virgin without intercourse with a man, and if prior things are to be proved from what follows, this will be false. For it could be answered to you in this way: that if Jesus was born of a woman, then he was also sown by a man; but if he was not sown by a man, then neither was he born of a woman. But he was able, as you believe, to be born though not sown; why, then, could he not also suffer though not brought to birth?

**2.** AUGUSTINE: No one puts to you what you put to yourself, except the inexperienced person whom you deceive, not the instructed person by whom you are refuted. For Jesus both could be born though not sown, and could suffer though not brought to birth; but one of these he willed, the other he did not will. For he willed to be born though not sown, but he did not will to suffer though not brought to birth, because, having been brought to birth, he suffered. You say to me: How do you know? Because I read this in the Gospel of truth. But if I should say to you: How do you know the things you say?—you set against me the authority of Mani, and say that there is falsehood in the Gospel. But I would not believe Mani when he says these things, not even if he did not praise Christ for having lied to me. This indeed he did not find in Christ, but he betrays what he himself loves.
