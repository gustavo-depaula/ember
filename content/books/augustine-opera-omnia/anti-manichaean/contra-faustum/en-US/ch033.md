# Book XXXII

**1.** FAUSTUS: If you accept the Gospel, you must believe all things which are written in it. For do you, because you accept the Old Testament, therefore believe everything indiscriminately which is written in it? Surely you excerpt from it only those prophecies which signified that a king would come to the Jews, because you suppose this to be Jesus; and a few common precepts of civil discipline, such as: *You shall not kill, you shall not commit adultery*[^1]; the rest you pass over, and reckon them to be no less, and the very same, as what Paul reckoned dung[^2]. What, then, is foreign in this, or what is a wonder, if I too, reading from the New Testament whatever is purest and suited to my salvation, pass over the things which, deceitfully introduced by your forebears, discolor both his majesty and his grace?

**2.** Or if the Testament of the Father has some things in which it ought to be little heeded (for you wish the Jewish law to be the Father's, of which we know how many things make for you horror, how many for shame, so that, as far as the mind goes, you yourselves have long since judged it to be not genuine, although partly the Father himself, as you believe, wrote it for you with his own finger, partly Moses wrote it, faithful and upright), do you think that the Testament of the Son alone could not be corrupted, alone has nothing in it which ought to be disapproved?—especially since it is established to have been written neither by him nor by his Apostles, but long afterward, by certain men of uncertain name, who, lest they should not be believed when writing things they did not know, set upon the fronts of their writings partly the names of Apostles, partly the names of those who were seen to have followed the Apostles, asserting that they wrote according to these the things they wrote. Whereby it seems to me all the more that they did grave injury to the disciples of Christ, because the dissonant and self-contradictory things they wrote, they referred to them, and professed that they wrote these Gospels according to them—Gospels so stuffed with errors, with such contradictions of narratives and at the same time of opinions, that they agree neither with themselves at all, nor among themselves. What, then, is this other than to slander good men, and to call the concordant company of Christ's disciples into the charge of discord? Since we, reading these things, observe this with a most sound gaze of the heart, we have judged it most equitable, having taken the useful things from them—that is, those which both build up our faith and propagate the glory of Christ the Lord and of his almighty Father God—to reject the rest, which befit neither their majesty nor our faith.

**3.** Accordingly, neither do you, as I had begun to say, believe that circumcision is to be received carnally from the Old Testament, although it is so written[^3]; nor that the Sabbath is to be kept in rest and leisure, although it is so read[^4]; nor that God is to be appeased by sacrifices, as it seems to Moses, and by immolations[^5]; but these things indeed, as wholly foreign and set far from the Christian rite, and in no part probable, you have despised. But certain of them you have even divided in two, and partly embrace, partly refuse: as the Passover, which is itself also an annual sacred rite of the Old Testament, in whose celebration, though it is written for you not only to kill a lamb to be eaten at night, but also to abstain from leaven for seven days, and to be content with unleavened bread along with bitter herbs[^6]—you indeed take up the Passover, but pass over that rite and custom by which it was provided that the same ought to be kept. Likewise the weeks of seven days, that is, Pentecost, which Moses likewise judged ought to be steeped in a certain kind and number of sacrifices[^7], you equally observe; but with the condemned part of it, that is, with the very oblations and sacrifices themselves, because these do not agree with the Christian faith. But of the commandment to abstain from common foods, it seemed to you, and was vehemently believed, that carrion indeed and things offered to idols are surely unclean[^8]; but you were unwilling to believe the same of pork, nor of hare or hedgehog, nor of mullets and squid, and the other kinds of fish which were to your taste—although Moses testifies that all these same are unclean[^9].

**4.** But those things I believe you are unwilling even to hear from the Old Testament, much less to admit—that is, fathers-in-law sleeping with daughters-in-law, like Judah; fathers with daughters, like Lot; Prophets with harlots, like Hosea; husbands selling the nights of their own wives to lovers, like Abraham; one husband mingled with two sisters by blood, like Jacob; rulers of the people, and those you would believe most filled with God, wallowing with thousands and hundreds of whores, like David and Solomon; or again that which is provided by the marriage law in Deuteronomy, that the wife of a deceased brother, if he should die without children, ought to marry the surviving brother, and that he should raise up offspring from her in the place of his brother; and that if a man should be unwilling to do this, the woman ought to lay before the elders a complaint about this impiety of her kinsman, so that they, having summoned him, may coerce him with censorial severity; and if he persistently refuses, and does not go unpunished before them, he should be stripped of the shoe of his right foot, struck in the face by the aforesaid woman, and depart spat upon and cursed, to have this reproach forever in his offspring[^10]. These, then, and others of this kind, are both the examples and the laws of the Old Testament; which, if they are good, why do you not imitate them? If evil, why do you not condemn their author, that is, the Old Testament itself? Or if you too think these things were inserted into it as false, as we believe about the New, then we are equal. Cease, then, henceforth to demand of us in the New Testament what you do not keep in the Old.

**5.** For my part, so far as I judge, insofar as you have willed it now to be persuaded that the Old Testament too is from God, it is more convenient enough and more excusable for you, who do not keep its commandments, if you confess it to be vitiated by unfitting additions, than if you spurn the same as inviolate and uncorrupted. Accordingly, I have always held this verdict about you, and hold it, as often as it is asked why you transgress the commands of the Old Testament: that you either despise it as wise men, as false, or neglect it as defiant and irreverent men, as true. Meanwhile, because you compel me to believe all things which are inserted in the monuments of the New Testament, if I should accept it, know that you too, as far as the mind goes, do not believe many things which are in the Old Testament, although you profess to accept it. For neither do you hold among the things confessed and credible from it that everyone is cursed who has hung on a tree[^11], because this surely applies also to Jesus; or that he too is held among the cursed who has not raised up seed in Israel, because this comprises also all virgins and the boys of God; or that he will utterly perish from his people who has not been circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin[^12], because this too applies to every Christian; or that he is to be killed with stones who has broken the Sabbath[^13]; or that he is not to be spared who has broken any single commandment whatever of the Old Testament. If you believed all these, and held it firm that these same were commanded by God, believe me, you would have laid hands on Christ first; nor would you now be angry at the Jews, who, having persecuted him with their minds and strength, fulfilled the commandments of their God.

**6.** For my part, I am not unaware that you do not dare to say these things are false, but that they were commanded to the Jews for the time then, namely until the coming of Jesus; who, since he has come, foretold, as you wish, by the same Old Testament, may himself now teach what is to be accepted by us from it, and what to be left. But whether the prophets foretold Jesus, we shall see afterward; meanwhile it behooves me now to answer this: that if Jesus, announced through the Old Testament, now judges and sifts, and teaches that a few things of it are to be accepted, but very many to be rejected; and the Paraclete promised to us from the New Testament likewise teaches what we ought to accept from it, and what to reject—of whom Jesus himself, when he promised him, says in the Gospel: *He will lead you into all truth, and he will announce to you all things, and bring them to your remembrance*[^14]. Therefore let it be permitted to us as much in the New Testament through the Paraclete as you show is permitted to you in the Old through Jesus—unless perhaps the Testament of the Son seems to you to be worth more than that of the Father, if indeed it is the Father's, so that, when so many things are rejected from that one, of this one alone nothing ought not to be approved; especially since it is established to have been written neither by Christ, as we said, nor by his Apostles.

**7.** Therefore, just as you admit from the Old Testament only the prophecies, and those precepts we mentioned above, civil and pertaining to the discipline of common life, but have set aside circumcision, and sacrifices, and the Sabbath and its observance, and unleavened bread—what is amiss if we too, accepting from the New Testament only those things which we find said, in honor and praise of the Son of majesty, either by him or by his Apostles, but now perfect and faithful ones, disregard the rest, which were either spoken then simply and ignorantly by the untrained, or thrown in obliquely and malignly by enemies, or imprudently affirmed by writers and handed down to posterity? I mean this very thing: born of a woman shamefully, circumcised in Jewish fashion, having sacrificed in Gentile fashion, baptized humbly, led about by the devil through the deserts, and tempted by him most miserably. These things, then, being excepted, and whatever was thrown in upon him by the writers from the Old Testament under false attestation, we believe the rest—especially the mystical fixing of his cross, by which the wounds of our soul's passion are shown; then his salutary precepts, then his parables, and the whole deifying discourse, which, setting forth most of all the distinction of two natures, does not come into doubt that it is his. There is, then, no reason now why you should suppose that I ought to believe all things which the Gospels contain, since you, as was shown above, scarcely touch with the tips, as they say, of your lips the topmost liquid of the Old Testament.

**8.** AUGUSTINE: We praise all the Scriptures of the Old Testament as true and divine, as is worthy; you assail the Scriptures of the New Testament as falsified and corrupted. We do not merely say, but in the apostolic writings also show and teach, that the things which we now do not observe from the Books of the Old Testament were nevertheless fittingly commanded at that time and to that people, and that in those very things which we do not observe, things were signified to us which we may understand and hold spiritually; but you reprehend altogether everything which you do not accept in the books of the New Testament, and assert that it was said or written neither by Christ nor by his Apostles. You see, then, how great a distance there is between us and you, as far as this matter goes. Since, then, it is asked of you why you do not accept everything in the books of the New Testament, but even in those books in which you approve some things, you refuse, reprehend, and accuse many things, contending that they were inserted by corrupters, do not take as your example the distinction of our faith or observance, but render an account of your own presumption.

**9.** For if it is asked of us why we do not worship God by the rite by which the Hebrew fathers worshiped in the time of the Old Testament, we answer that God commanded us something else through the fathers of the New Testament; and this not against the Old Testament, since in it too this was foretold beforehand. For this very thing was foretold through the prophet: *Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and I will accomplish over the house of Israel and over the house of Judah a New Covenant; not according to the covenant which I made with their fathers, in the day when I took their hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt*[^15]. Behold, this too was prophesied, that that Covenant would not endure, but that a New one would be. Here, if it should be objected that we do not belong to the house of Israel and the house of Judah, we defend ourselves from the apostolic teaching, because the Apostle teaches that Christ is the seed of Abraham, and says to us who belong to his body: *Therefore you are the seed of Abraham*[^16]. Further, if it should be asked of us why we hold the authority of that Covenant whose rite we do not observe, to this too we answer from the apostolic writings; for the Apostle says: *Let no one, therefore, judge you in food and drink, or in respect of a feast day, or a new moon, or sabbaths, which are a shadow of things to come*[^17]. For thus he shows both why these ought to be read and accepted—that is, lest we extinguish prophecy, because they were made in a shadow of things to come—and that we ought not to mind those who would judge us in this matter, because we no longer observe them bodily; just as elsewhere he said something of the kind: *These things happened to them in a figure; but they were written for our correction, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^18]. When, therefore, something is read in the document of the Old Testament such as is not commanded to be observed by us in the New Testament, or even is prohibited, what it signifies must be sought, not reprehended; because by the very fact that it is no longer observed, it is proved not condemned, but fulfilled—of which we have said many things, and often.

**10.** As, for instance, this very thing which Faustus, not understanding, charged as a crime against the commandments of the Old Testament: that a brother was commanded to marry his brother's wife to this end, that he might raise up offspring not for himself but for him, and call by his name what was born thence[^19]—what else does it foreshow in a figure, except that every preacher of the Gospel ought so to labor in the Church that he may raise up seed to his deceased brother, that is, to Christ, who died for us; and that what is raised up should receive his name? Finally, the Apostle, fulfilling this, no longer carnally in the foreshadowing signification, but spiritually in the completed truth, mentions those whom he has begotten in Christ Jesus through the Gospel[^20], is angry at them, and rebuking corrects them when they wish to belong to Paul: *Was Paul*, he says, *crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^21] As if he said: I begot you for my deceased brother; you are called Christians, not Paulinians. But he who, chosen by the Church, refuses the ministry of evangelizing, is by the Church itself deservedly and worthily despised. For this is what he is commanded to do, to spit in his face—not indeed without a sign of this reproach, that he be stripped of the shoe of one foot, lest he be in the lot of those to whom the Apostle himself says: *and your feet shod with the preparation of the Gospel of peace*[^22]; and of whom the prophet mentions: *How beautiful are the feet of those who announce peace, who announce good things!*[^23] For he who so holds the evangelical faith that it both profits himself and does not refuse to profit the Church, is rightly understood to be shod on both feet. But he who supposes that, because he has believed, enough provision has been made for himself, but flees the care of gaining others, will not signify that figured reproach of the man with one bare foot, but will bear it fulfilled in himself.

**11.** What of the fact that he charges that the Passover too is celebrated by us, and yet does not taunt us that we celebrate it as the Jews do, since we rather have the lamb in the reality of the present Gospel, not in the shadow of one to come, and signify its slaying, both daily and especially in the annual solemnity, not as one to come, but commemorate it as done? For this reason indeed the day of the solemnity of our Passover does not coincide with the day of the shadowy celebration of the Jews, that we might also embrace the Lord's day, on which Christ rose. But the unleavened bread, those who are Christians of right faith keep, not in the leaven of the old life, that is, of malice, but in the truth and sincerity of faith itself[^24]; not for seven days, but every day; which was signified by the sevenfold number of days, by which every day is daily revolved. And although this is somewhat laborious in this world, since narrow and strait is the way that leads to life[^25], yet a sure reward is kept; for the labor itself was signified by those bitter herbs, in that they are somewhat bitter.

**12.** Pentecost too, that is, the fiftieth day from the passion and resurrection of the Lord, we celebrate, on which he sent us the Holy Spirit the Paraclete, whom he had promised[^26]; which thing to come was also signified through the Passover of the Jews, when, on the fiftieth day after the celebration of the slain lamb, Moses received on the mountain the law written by the finger of God[^27]. Read the Gospel, and notice that there the Holy Spirit is called the finger of God[^28]. For those things are celebrated annually in the Church which were done notably and excellently on certain days, so that the festival celebrated together may keep their necessary and salutary memory. If, then, you wish to know why we celebrate the Passover: because then Christ was sacrificed for us. If you wish to know why we do not celebrate it by the Jewish rite: because that was a prefiguration of the true thing to come, this a commemoration of the completed thing; and a thing future and a thing past are not even by our own words enunciated in the same way; of which we have already said enough in this work.

**13.** But if you also ask this, why, of all the foods from which that people was prohibited in the shadow of things to come, we do not feed on carrion and on what is offered to idols—hear this too, and at some time set the truth before the slanders of vanity. For why it is not expedient for a Christian to feed on what is offered to idols, the Apostle says: *I do not wish you*, he says, *to become partners of demons*. For he does not reprehend the immolation itself which the fathers made, prefiguring the blood of the sacrifice by which Christ redeemed us; rather, *what the Gentiles immolate*, he says, *they immolate to demons, and not to God*. Then he added what I said: *I do not wish you to become partners of demons*[^29]. For if the very nature of the flesh offered to idols were unclean, it would surely defile even one who did not know. For it would be no less itself for being received by one who did not know; but on account of conscience, lest he seem to have had communion with demons. By "carrion" I think is meant that which the use of men did not admit for food, because it is the diseased flesh, not of slain, but of dead animals, nor suited to the health of the body, for the sake of which we take nourishment. For as to what was commanded the ancients in a figure about the pouring out of blood—that is, to Noah himself after the flood[^30]—what it signified we have already shown, and most people understand[^31]. And in the Acts of the Apostles this is read as commanded by the Apostles by way of a law, that the Gentiles should abstain only from fornication, and from things offered to idols, and from blood—that is, that they should eat no flesh whose blood had not been poured out. Which others understand not thus, but that it was commanded to abstain from blood, lest anyone defile himself with murder. To discuss this now would be long, and not necessary; because even if the Apostles then commanded this, that Christians should abstain from the blood of animals, lest they feed on strangled flesh, they seem to me to have chosen, for the time, a thing easy and in no way burdensome to those observing it, in which, along with the Israelites, the Gentiles too, on account of that corner stone joining the two in himself[^32], might observe something in common; and at the same time they might be admonished that, in the ark of Noah itself, when God commanded this, the Church of all nations was figured, the prophecy of which deed was now beginning to be fulfilled as the Gentiles came to the faith. But when that time had passed, in which those two walls—one coming from the circumcision, the other from the uncircumcision—although they harmonized in the corner stone, yet stood out more distinctly by certain properties of their own, and when the Church of the Gentiles became such that in it no carnal Israelite appears, what Christian now observes this, that he should not touch thrushes or smaller little birds except those whose blood has been poured out, or should not eat a hare if it was struck by hand on the neck and killed with no bloody wound? And those few who perhaps still dread to touch these things are laughed at by the rest; so has that verdict of truth held the minds of all in this matter: *Not what enters your mouth defiles you, but what comes out*[^33]—condemning no nature of food which human society admits, but the sins which iniquity commits.

**14.** But now concerning the deeds of the ancients, both those which seem sins to foolish and unlearned men, when they are not, and those which are truly sins—why they were written down, kept, and rather commended with the veneration of Scripture itself—we have already shown with sufficient discourse; likewise concerning the curse of him who hangs on a tree[^34], and of him who has not raised up seed in Israel, we have already answered in the first place, in its proper place, when we earlier dissolved these objections. And absolutely all things—whether those which we have already discussed one by one in the earlier parts of this work, or those similar things which Faustus has set down in this discourse of his to which we are responding—we defend by the one most firm account of truth, which we have received from the authority of the Holy Scriptures. Whatever is written in those books of the Old Testament, we praise, accept, and approve as all most truly and most usefully written for eternal life; but those commandments in them which we do not observe by bodily action, we both most rightly understand to have been commanded then, and have learned to be shadows of things to come, and now know to be fulfilled. And through this, whoever then did not observe even those deeds which were commanded to be done for the sake of signifying, paid the penalties divinely appointed by a most right judgment—just as now, if anyone with sacrilegious rashness should dare to violate the sacraments of the New Testament, distinguished according to the reason of the time. For just as then just men, who did not even refuse death for those sacraments, are rightly praised, so now the holy martyrs for these. And as a sick man ought not to reprehend the medicinal teaching, if it prescribes one thing for him today, another tomorrow, prohibiting even what it had prescribed before—for so stands the reason of healing his body—so the human race, from Adam even to the end of the age, as long as the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul[^35], sick and wounded, ought not to reprehend the divine medicine, if in some things it prescribes this same thing, but in some things prescribes that one thing be observed first and another afterward; especially since it promised that it would prescribe something else.

**15.** There is, then, no comparison which Faustus holds out—that the Paraclete chose for you from the New Testament what you should believe, and showed what you should reject, since he himself was prophesied by the New Testament, just as Christ did for us from the Old, by which he was likewise prophesied. For this would be said with some verisimilar reasoning, if there were something in the books of the Old Testament which we would say was not rightly said, not divinely commanded, not truthfully written. We say none of these things; but we accept all things, whether those we observe, that we may live rightly, or those we do not observe, that we may nevertheless see those very things, commanded and observed then in prophecy, now already fulfilled. Then, just as we read the Paraclete promised in those books of which you are unwilling to accept everything, so we read him also sent in that book which you are even afraid to name. For in the Acts of the Apostles, as I have both often and a little before mentioned, the Holy Spirit is most plainly read to have been sent on the day of Pentecost, who even by his work manifested who he was. For those who first received him spoke in all tongues[^36], so that by this sign too he might promise that the Church would be in all tongues, that is, in all nations, which would most truly preach him as it preaches the Father and the Son.

**16.** You, then, now say why you do not accept all things from the books of the New Testament: whether because they are not the Apostles' of Christ, or because the Apostles of Christ taught something depraved. You answer: "Because they are not the Apostles' of Christ. For that other voice is the Pagans', who say that the Apostles of Christ did not teach rightly." You, then—what do you say, whence do you show that those Scriptures were not ministered by the Apostles? You answer: "Because there are many things in them both contrary to one another and to themselves." This is utterly most false; you do not understand. For whatever such thing Faustus brought forward that seemed so to you, it was shown not to be so; and whatever such thing shall be brought forward by you, this we will teach. But who would bear that a reader or hearer should dare to blame a Scripture of such great authority more readily than the fault of his own slowness? Or do you say that the Paraclete taught this, that those Scriptures are not the Apostles', but were written under their names by others? At least teach this: that this very one is the Paraclete, from whom you learned that these are not the Apostles'. Or will you say: "Christ himself promised him, and sent him"? It is answered to you: Christ neither promised nor sent this one at all; and at the same time it is shown when he sent the one he promised. That Christ, then, sent him, prove this. Whence do you assert the person of your author, or rather deceiver? You answer that you prove it from the Gospel. From what Gospel? The one you do not accept whole, the one you say was falsified. Who, then, would himself first say that his own witness was corrupted by falsehood, and then produce him to testimony? For if we believe him in what you wish, and do not believe him in what you do not wish, we now believe, not him, but you. But if we wished to believe you, we would not require a witness of you. Then the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, was so promised that it was said: *He will lead you into all truth*[^37]; but how will he lead you into truth, who teaches you that Christ is deceitful? To this is added: that if you should show that all the things read in the Gospel about the promise of the Paraclete were such that they could not possibly be understood except of your Mani—as it is shown in the Prophets that those things were said of Christ which can fall upon no other at all—yet, since you would bring them forth from those codices which you say are not falsified, we would say that this very thing was false there and put in by your elder corrupters, namely that you read there written of Mani so that we could not understand it of another: what would you do, tell me, but cry out that you could in no way have falsified the codices which were already in the hands of all Christians? Because as soon as you had begun to do so, you would be convicted by the truth of the older copies. By the same cause, then, that they could not be corrupted by you, by this cause they could be corrupted by no one. For whoever first dared this would be confuted by the comparison of many older codices; especially because the same Scripture was contained, not in one language, but in many. For even now some faulty readings of codices are corrected either from older ones, or from the preceding language. So, then, you are either compelled to confess those codices truthful—and they at once overthrow your heresy; or, if you call them false, you will not be able by their authority to assert the Paraclete, and you yourselves overthrow your heresy.

**17.** To this is added that those things were said in the promise of the Paraclete which utterly exclude Mani, coming after so many years, from this suspicion. For because the Holy Spirit was to come immediately after the resurrection and ascension of the Lord, it was most plainly said by John: *For the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^38]. If, then, this was the cause that he was not given, because Jesus was not yet glorified, without doubt, Jesus being glorified, there was now cause that he should be given at once. For the Cataphrygians too said that they had received the promised Paraclete, and hence deviated from the catholic faith, trying to prohibit what Paul allowed, and to condemn second marriages which he permitted; lying in wait under these words, because it is written of the Paraclete: *he will lead you into all truth*—as if, forsooth, Paul and the rest of the Apostles had not taught all truth, and had reserved a place for the Paraclete of the Cataphrygians. To this they drew also that which Paul says: *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part; but when that which is perfect has come, that which is in part will be done away*[^39]; namely that the Apostle, knowing and prophesying in part, said: *Let him do what he wills; he does not sin if he marries*[^40]; and therefore the perfect thing of the Paraclete of Phrygia did away with this. To these things, when it was said to them that they were condemned by the authority of the Church promised so long before and diffused throughout the whole world, they answer that hence too that was fulfilled in them which was said of the Paraclete, that the world cannot receive him. Are not these the very things which you too are accustomed to say—that *he will lead you into all truth*; and *When that which is perfect has come, that which is in part will be done away*; and *The world cannot receive him*[^41]—were foretold of your Mani? And what heresy, then, could ever arise under the name of the Paraclete, which would not dare verisimilarly to fit all these things to itself? For is there any heresy which does not name itself the truth, and the prouder it is, the more it names itself even the perfect truth, so as both to promise that it will lead into all truth, and to try to do away with the teaching of the Apostles contrary to its error, as if through it had come that which is perfect? And since the Church holds what the Apostle more vehemently commended—*If anyone evangelizes to you beyond what you have received, let him be anathema*[^42]—when, announcing something else, it begins to be anathematized by the whole world, may it at once say: This is what was written, *The world cannot receive him?*

**18.** Whence, then, will you be able to prove what is required of you—that the Paraclete is the one from whom you learned that the evangelical writings are not the Apostles'? Since we too prove that the Holy Spirit is the Paraclete, none other than the one who came soon after Jesus was glorified. For he was not yet given for this reason, because Jesus was not yet glorified. We prove also that he himself leads into all truth, because one does not enter into truth except through love; but *the love of God*, says the Apostle, *is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to us*[^43]. We teach also that Paul did not say *When that which is perfect has come* except of that perfection which will be in the reception of eternal life. For when he was speaking of this, he said: *For now we see through a mirror in a riddle, but then face to face*[^44]. Here surely, unless you wish openly to be insane, you will confess that you do not see God face to face. That which is perfect, then, has not come to you. For in this way the Apostle has sufficiently explained what he meant about this matter; nor will this be for the saints until that has been done which John too says: *We are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be; but we know that, when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^45]; then, with the Holy Spirit leading into all truth, whose pledge we have now received. But what was said—*The world cannot receive him*—was said of those who are accustomed to be called in the Scriptures by the name of the world: lovers of the world, or the impious, or the carnal; of whom the Apostle says: *But the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God*[^46]. For they are said to be of this world as long as they cannot have wisdom of anything beyond these corporeal things, which they have perceived in this world—just as you too have wisdom of nothing else, who, when you marveled at this light of the sun and moon, fashioned for yourselves all divine things to be such, by the authorship of that vainspeaking one whom, deceived and deceivers, you call the Paraclete. Accordingly, being able in no way to show how he is the Paraclete, there is no source from which you may teach that you have discovered, by any most firm authority, how these evangelical writings, of which you are unwilling to accept everything, are not the Apostles' of Christ. It remains, then, that you say you discovered this by this reasoning, because there are such things there as discolor the glory of Christ: namely, because he is there narrated as born of a virgin, because circumcised, because for him was offered the sacrifice which was then offered, because baptized, because tempted by the devil.

**19.** But these things being excepted, and the testimonies from the Old Testament which were inserted into those writings, the rest, according to what Faustus says, you confess that you accept, and "especially the mystical fixing of his cross, by which the wounds of your soul's passion are shown. Then," he says, "his salutary precepts, and parables, and the whole deifying discourse, which, setting forth most of all the distinction of two natures, does not come into doubt that it is his." You see, then, that you are doing this: that all authority be taken away from the midst of the Scriptures, and that each one's own mind be the author of what he approves or disapproves in each Scripture; that is, that one be not subjected to the authority of the Scriptures unto faith, but himself subject the Scriptures to himself: not that something pleases him because he reads it written in lofty authority, but that it seems rightly written because it has pleased him. To what do you commit yourself, wretched soul, weak, wrapped in carnal mists? To what do you commit yourself? Remove, then, authority—let us see; remove authority, render a reason. Is your reason brought to this, that unless God's nature be believed violable and corruptible, that long fable of yours cannot find its theatrical exit? Finally, whence do you know that there are eight earths and ten heavens, that Atlas bears the world, and Splenditenens holds it suspended, and innumerable such things? Whence do you know these? "Plainly," you say, "Mani taught me." But, unhappy soul, you believed; for you did not see. If, then, for thousands of fabulous phantasms, with which you are basely pregnant, you submitted yourself to a most unknown and most furious authority, so as to believe all these things because they are written in those books which by a pitiable error you judged ought to be believed, when nothing is demonstrated to you, why do you not rather submit yourself to the evangelical authority—so founded, so established, so famed with such great glory, and commended by most sure successions from the times of the Apostles even to our times—so that you may believe, may see, may learn that all those things which offend you offend you out of a vain and perverse opinion; and that it is rather true that the unchangeable nature of God took on something of mortal creature, in which, remaining unchangeably, it would do and suffer, not deceitfully but truthfully, whatever it befitted that same creature to do and suffer for the salvation of the human race from which it was taken—rather than to believe the nature of God violable and corruptible, and, defiled and oppressed, unable to be wholly freed and purged, but condemned by the highest necessity of God to the eternal penalty of the lump?

**20.** "But," you say, "I believed the things he did not show me for this reason, because he evidently showed me two natures, namely of good and of evil, in this very world." But this very thing, unhappy soul, is whence you were deceived: because, as in that evangelical Scripture, so in this world you could think nothing evil except that by which your carnal sense was offended, such as a serpent, fire, poison, and the like; nor anything good, except what soothed that same carnal sense of yours with some pleasantness, such as the pleasantness of flavors, and the sweetness of odors, and the sight of this light, and whatever else perhaps similarly flattered your ears, or eyes, or nostrils, or palate, or touch. But if you first looked at the whole creation in such a way that you attributed it to God its author, as though reading a certain great book of the nature of things; and so, if anything there offended you, you believed rather that the cause, as being a man, could be hidden from you, than dared to reprehend anything in the works of God—you would never have fallen into sacrilegious trifles and blasphemous figments, by which, not understanding whence evil is, you try to fill God with all evils.

**21.** Here now, if you ask of us whence we know that those writings are the Apostles', we answer you briefly: we know it from the same source whence you too know that those writings are Mani's, which you pitiably set before this authority. For if here too someone should raise a question against you, and drive in the scruple of contradiction, saying that the books which you bring forward as Mani's are not Mani's, what will you do? Will you not rather laugh at the ravings of him who, against a thing confirmed by so great a series of connection and succession, sends forth the impudence of this utterance? Just as, then, it is certain that those books are Mani's, and he is wholly to be laughed at who, coming sideways, born so long afterward, should bring against you a suit of this contradiction; so it is certain that Mani, or the Manicheans, are to be laughed at, or even pitied, who dare to say any such thing against an authority so founded, kept and brought down by sure successions from the times of the Apostles even to these times.

**22.** Now, then, the authority of Mani must be compared with the authority of the Apostles. For that these are the writings of these men is as certain as that those are the writings of that man. But who compares Mani with the Apostles, except whoever separates himself from Christ, who sent the Apostles? Or who ever had the wisdom of two natures adverse to each other and from their own proper principles in the words of Christ, except one who has no wisdom of the words of Christ? The Apostles, therefore, as disciples of the truth, preach the birth of Christ and his true passion; but Mani boasts that he leads into all truth, and wishes to lead into such a Christ in whom he preaches the falsehood of his very passion. They preach Christ circumcised in the flesh which he took from the seed of Abraham; he, God cut up in his very nature by the race of darkness. They preach the sacrifice offered for the infant flesh of Christ, which was then piously done; he, a member, not of flesh, but of the very divine substance, brought to be immolated to all demons, the adverse nature of the race. They preach Christ baptized in the Jordan for the sake of giving an example; he, God himself, submerged through himself in the defilement of darkness, and not to emerge wholly, but, in respect of what could not be cleansed of him, to be punished with eternal damnation. They preach the flesh of Christ tempted by the leader of demons; he, a part of God possessed by the race of demons: and they indeed preach it tempted, that he might teach to resist the tempter; he preaches it possessed, so that it cannot be restored to the Father, nor to the victor. Finally, Mani announces something else out of the doctrine of demons, that he may seem the more eminent; but the Apostles commend, out of the doctrine of Christ, that whoever announces something else should be anathema[^47].
