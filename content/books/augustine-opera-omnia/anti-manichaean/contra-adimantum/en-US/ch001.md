# Revisions I

## Chapter 22 (21): Answer to Adimantus, a Disciple of Mani, One Book

**1.** At that same time there came into my hands certain *Disputations* of Adimantus, who had been a disciple of Mani, which he wrote against the Law and the Prophets, attempting to show that the evangelical and apostolic writings were opposed to them. I therefore replied to him, setting down his words and giving my own response to them. I confined this work to a single volume, and in it I responded to certain questions not once but twice, since what I had first responded had been lost and was then found after I had already responded a second time. Some of these same questions I did, to be sure, resolve in popular sermons preached in the church[^1]. There are also some that I did not yet answer; and some remained that were passed over because other matters were more pressing, with the further addition of forgetfulness.

**2.** In this work, then, I said: *For by certain shadows and figures of things, before the coming of the Lord, in accord with a marvelous and most orderly arrangement of the times, that people was held bound who received the Old Testament; yet in it there is such preaching and foretelling of the New Testament that there are found in the evangelical and apostolic teaching no precepts and promises, however lofty and divine, that are also lacking in those old books*[^2]*.* But I should have added *almost*, and said: *so that there are found in the evangelical and apostolic teaching* almost *no precepts and promises, however lofty and divine, that are also lacking in those old books.*

For what is it that the Lord says in the gospel sermon on the mount: *You have heard that it was said to the men of old this, but I say to you this*[^3], if he himself laid down nothing more than what was commanded in those old books? Then, we do not read that the *kingdom of heaven* was promised to that people among the things promised in the Law given through Moses on Mount Sinai[^4], which is properly called the Old Testament; this the Apostle says was prefigured by the slave girl Hagar and her son[^5]; but there too the New was figured by Sarah herself and her son.

Accordingly, if the figures are examined, all the things are found prophesied there which are presented or are awaited as to be presented through Christ. Yet on account of certain precepts that are not figurative but proper, which are found not in the Old Testament but in the New, it would be said more cautiously and more moderately that there are *almost none* here, rather than *none*, which are not also there—although there are those two precepts about the love of God and of neighbor[^6], to which most rightly all things are referred, whether of the Law or of the prophets or of the gospel or of the apostles.

**3.** Likewise, what I said—*In the holy Scriptures the name of 'sons' is taken in three ways*[^7]*—*was said with too little consideration. For we no doubt passed over certain other ways as well; as when one is called a *son of hell*[^8], or an *adopted son*[^9], which are surely said neither according to nature nor according to teaching nor according to imitation. We gave examples of these three ways as though they were the only ones: according to nature, as the Jews are *sons of Abraham*[^10]*;* according to teaching, as the Apostle calls those his sons whom he taught the Gospel[^11]; according to imitation, as we are sons of Abraham, whose faith we imitate[^12].

But what I said—*When it has put on incorruption and immortality, it will no longer be flesh and blood*[^13]*—*was said to mean that the flesh will not exist according to carnal corruption, not according to the substance by which the Lord's body, even after the resurrection, was called *flesh*[^14].

**4.** In another place I said: *Unless someone changes his will, he cannot do good, which he teaches to be placed within our power in another place where he says: 'Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree bad and its fruit bad'*[^15]. This is not against the grace of God that we preach. For it is within a person's power to change his will for the better; but that power is nothing unless it is given by God, of whom it is said: *He gave them power to become sons of God*[^16]. For since this is within our power which we do when we will it, nothing is so much within our power as the will itself; but *the will is prepared by the Lord*[^17]. In this way, then, he gives the power. So too must be understood what I said afterward: *that it is within our power that we should deserve either to be grafted in by the goodness of God or to be cut off by his severity*[^18]*,* because nothing is within our power except what follows our will, which, when it is prepared strong and powerful by the Lord, the work of piety is easily done, even that which was difficult and impossible.

This book begins thus: *Concerning what is written: 'In the beginning God made heaven and earth.'*
