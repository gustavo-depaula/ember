# Revisions I

## Chapter 18 (17): The Unfinished Literal Commentary on Genesis, in one book

When I had composed two books *On Genesis against the Manichees*[^1], since I had treated the words of Scripture according to their allegorical meaning and had not dared to set forth the great mysteries of natural things in their literal sense—that is, in what way the things said there could be understood according to the proper sense of history—I wanted to test, in this most laborious and difficult work as well, what I was capable of; but in expounding the Scriptures my inexperience collapsed under so great a weight of burden, and, before even one book was finished, I gave up the labor I could not sustain.

But in this present work, while I was revising my little compositions, this very book, unfinished as it was, came into my hands—a book I had neither published nor intended to discard, since I later wrote twelve books whose title is *The Literal Meaning of Genesis*[^2]*.* In those, although many things seem rather to be sought than found, this book is by no means to be compared with them. Yet after I had revised it, I decided to let this one stand as well, so that it might serve as an index—not, I think, a useless one—of my first attempts at unfolding and examining the divine words; and I decided that its title should be *The Unfinished Literal Commentary on Genesis.*

I found that it had been dictated up to these words: *the Father is only Father, and the Son is nothing other than the Son; because even when he is called the likeness of the Father, although this shows that no unlikeness intervenes, nevertheless the Father is not alone, if he has a likeness*[^3]*.* After these words I took up again, to be considered and treated afresh, the words of Scripture: *and God said, 'let us make man to our image and likeness*[^4].' Up to this point I had left the dictated book unfinished. What follows there I thought ought to be added when I was revising it; yet even so I did not finish it, but, with this too added, I left it unfinished. For had I finished it, I would at least have discussed all the works and words of God that belong to the sixth day.

In this book it seemed to me superfluous to note the things that displease me, or to defend the things that, not being well understood, may displease others. Rather, I briefly advise instead that those twelve books be read, which I composed long afterward as a bishop, and that this one be judged on the basis of them.

Here, then, it thus begins: *the obscure matters of natural things, which we perceive to have been made by Almighty God as their maker, must be treated not by asserting but by inquiring.*

[^1]: Retract. 1.10.
[^2]: Retract. 2.24.1.
[^3]: De Gen. ad litt. imperf. 16.60.
[^4]: Gen 1:26.
