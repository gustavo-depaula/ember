# Prologue.

**1. 1.** Since, by a discourse not brief and quite necessary, which we have embraced in one book, we have refuted the emptiness of those who think the disciples of Christ, in writing the Gospel, are to be despised because we bring forward no writings of Christ himself—whom, though not as God, yet as a man far excelling the rest in wisdom, they do not doubt is to be honored—and who wish him to be seen to have written such things as the perverse love, not such as, when read and believed, can correct men from perversity: now let us see how the things which the four evangelists wrote about Christ agree both with themselves and with one another, lest from this any stumbling block be suffered in the Christian faith by those who are more curious than capacious, who, thinking that they have found certain inconsistencies and contradictions—not in the Gospel books read in any fashion, but as if more carefully searched through—judge that these are rather to be objected contentiously than considered prudently.
