# On the marriage of the King's son, to which the crowds were invited; what order Matthew kept, in view of Luke, who says something of the kind elsewhere.

**71.** 139. Matthew continues: *and when the chief priests and the Pharisees had heard his parables, they perceived that he was speaking about them, and seeking to seize him, they feared the crowds, since they held him as a prophet. And Jesus answered and again spoke to them in parables, saying: 'The kingdom of heaven has become like a king who made a marriage feast for his son, and sent his servants to call those who had been invited to the marriage; and they would not come*[^1],' and so on down to where he says: *for many are called, but few are chosen*[^2].

This parable of those invited to the marriage Matthew alone narrates. Luke too records something of the kind[^3]; but it is not the same, as the order itself indicates, although that too bears some resemblance. But that after that parable about the vineyard and the slain son of the householder Matthew subjoins that the Jews perceived that the whole thing had been said against them, and began to contrive a plot, this Mark and Luke too attest, keeping the same order[^4]; but from there they proceed to something else, subjoining from this point what Matthew too, after this parable about the marriage which he alone interposed, inserted in order.

On the coin to be rendered to Caesar, whose image it bears, and on the woman who had married seven brothers, how these three agree.

**72.** 140. Matthew therefore continues: *then the Pharisees went away and took counsel how to entrap him in his speech. And they send to him their disciples with the Herodians, saying: 'Teacher, we know that you are truthful and teach the way of God in truth, and you care for no one, for you do not regard the person of men; tell us, therefore, what you think: is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not?'*[^5] and so on down to where he says: *and the crowds, hearing it, marveled at his teaching*[^6]. These two answers of the Lord — one about the coin, concerning the tribute to be rendered to Caesar, the other about the resurrection, concerning that woman who had married seven brothers in succession — Mark and Luke narrate in like manner, and they differ in no respect of order[^7]. For after that parable about the hirers of the vineyard and about the Jews, against whom it was spoken, preparing their plot, which all three recorded, these two, Mark and Luke, omit the parable about those invited to the marriage, which Matthew alone interposed, and now follow him in narrating these two matters, about the tribute of Caesar and about the woman of seven husbands one by one, in altogether the same order without any question of contradiction.

[^1]: Mt 21:45–46; 22:14.
[^2]: Mt 22:14.
[^3]: Lk 14:16–24.
[^4]: See Mk 12:12; Lk 20:19.
[^5]: Mt 22:15–17.
[^6]: Mt 22:33.
[^7]: See Mk 12:13–27; Lk 20:20–40.
