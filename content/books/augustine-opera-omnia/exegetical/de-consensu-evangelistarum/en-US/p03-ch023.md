# On the one who said to the Lord, "I will follow you wherever you go," and the other things which are near it: in what order they are narrated by Matthew and Luke.

**23. 54.** Then what he subjoins—*and a scribe, coming, said to him: 'Master, I will follow you wherever you go*,' up to where he says, *let the dead bury their own dead*[^1]—this Luke too narrates similarly. But he does so after many things, and not himself with the order of times expressed, but in the manner of one recalling: whether what he earlier omitted, or what, done even later than the things that follow, he anticipated, is uncertain.

For he says thus: *and it came to pass, as they were walking on the way, a certain one said to him: 'I will follow you wherever you go*[^2].' And he answered him the very same things which Matthew mentions. But that Matthew says this was done then, when he ordered them to go across the strait[^3], while Luke says, as they were walking on the way[^4], is not contrary, because they were surely walking on the way, to come to the strait.

And about the one who asks first to bury his father, Matthew and Luke agree entirely[^5]. For that Matthew first set down the words of the one asking this on account of his father, and then those of the Lord saying, *Follow me*, while Luke first set down those of the Lord saying, *Follow me*, and then those of the one asking this, makes no difference to the meaning. Luke mentioned also that another said, *I will follow you, Lord, but first allow me to take leave of those who are at home*[^6], about whom Matthew is silent. From there Luke now went on to something else, not to that which followed in the order of time. *And after these things*, he says, *the Lord appointed seventy-two others*[^7]. *After these things*, indeed, manifestly; but after how great an interval of time the Lord did that *after these things* does not appear.
