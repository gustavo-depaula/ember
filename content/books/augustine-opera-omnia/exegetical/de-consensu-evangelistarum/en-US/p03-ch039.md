# What he answered to those seeking a sign, about the prophet Jonah and the Ninevites, and about the Queen of the South, and about the unclean spirit which, when it has gone out of a man, returns and finds the house cleansed.

**39. 86.** Matthew goes on and says: *then certain of the scribes and Pharisees answered him, saying: 'Master, we wish to see a sign from you'*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says thus: *so shall it be also to this most wicked generation*[^2]. These things Luke too narrates, in that very place indeed, but in a somewhat different order[^3].

For that they sought a sign from the Lord from heaven, he mentioned above, after that miracle about the mute man, and did not there report what the Lord answered them to this; but afterward, when the crowds had run together, he says that this was answered to those whom he gives us to understand he had mentioned above, that they were seeking a sign from heaven; and this he attaches, after he interposed about the woman who said to the Lord, *Blessed is the womb that bore you*[^4].

But he interposes this woman, when he had mentioned, from the discourse of the Lord, that the unclean spirit, going out of a man, returns and finds the house cleansed. After that woman, then, when he said it was answered to the crowds about the sign which they sought from heaven, the similitude of the prophet Jonah being interposed, then, the discourse of the Lord himself being continued, he mentions what was said about the Queen of the South and about the Ninevites[^5]. Thus he rather mentioned something which Matthew was silent about than passed over anything of those things which the other narrated in this place. But who does not see that it is asked superfluously in what order the Lord said those things—since this too we ought to learn, through the most excellent authority of the evangelists, that it is no falsehood if someone has set out anyone's discourse not in the order in which it proceeded from him, when the order itself makes no difference to the matter, whether it be this way or that? And still Luke indicates this discourse of the Lord to be more extended, and in it mentions certain things such as Matthew has in that discourse which was held on the mountain[^6], which we understand were said twice, both there and here. But, this discourse ended, Luke goes on to something else, in which it is uncertain whether he kept the order of the things done. For he connects it thus: *and as he was speaking, a certain Pharisee asked him to dine with him*[^7]. But he does not say "as he was speaking these things," but *as he was speaking*. For if he had said "as he was speaking these things," he would necessarily compel us to understand that these things were narrated by him in this order, not only, but also done by the Lord in this order.

[^1]: Mt 12:38.
[^2]: Mt 12:45.
[^3]: See Lk 11:16–26.
[^4]: Lk 11:27.
[^5]: See Lk 11:17–32.
[^6]: See Lk 11:33–36; Mt 5:15; 6:22.
[^7]: Lk 11:37.
