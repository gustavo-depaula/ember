# Revisions II

## Chapter 16 (43): Agreement among the Evangelists, in four books.

During the same years in which I was gradually dictating the books *On the Trinity*, I also wrote others, inserting them within those years by continuous labor; among these are the four books *On the Agreement among the Evangelists*, written on account of those who level accusations against the evangelists as though they disagreed.

The first of these books was composed against those who either honor Christ, or pretend to honor him, as the supremely wise man, and for that reason refuse to believe the Gospel, because those things were not written by him but by his disciples, who they suppose mistakenly attributed to him the divinity by which he would be believed to be God.

In this book, what I said—that *the nation of the Hebrews began from Abraham*[^1]—is indeed credible too, so that the Hebrews would seem to be called, as it were, Abrahaeans.

But it is more truly understood that they were named after the one who was called Heber, as though Heberaeans[^2], a matter I have discussed sufficiently in the sixteenth book of *The City of God*[^3].

In the second book, when I was treating of the two fathers of Joseph, *I said that he was begotten by the one and adopted by the other.* But I should have said "adopted for the other"[^4]; for, after his death[^5]—which is the more credible view—he had been adopted according to the law[^6], since the man who begot him had married his mother, the wife of his deceased brother.

Likewise, where I said, *Luke ascends to David himself through Nathan, the prophet through whom God expiated David's sin*[^7]*,* I ought to have said "the prophet of that name," lest it be thought that it was the same man, when in fact it was another, although he too was so called[^8].

This work begins thus: *Among all the divine authorities.*

[^2]: QQ. in Hept. 1.24
[^3]: De Civ. Dei 16.3, 11
[^4]: De consensu evang. 2.3.5
[^5]: See Retract. 2.7.2
[^6]: Deut 25:5-6
[^7]: De consensu evang. 2.4.12; see Lk 3:31
[^8]: See Retract. 1.26 (q. 61)
