# That the Jews are questioned about the Christ, whose son he seems to be, whether Matthew is not opposed to the other two.

**74.** 143. Matthew continues: *now while the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus questioned them, saying: 'What do you think about the Christ? Whose son is he?' They say to him: 'David's.' He says to them: 'How then does David in the Spirit call him Lord, saying: 'The Lord said to my Lord, sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet'? If David then calls him Lord, how is he his son?' And no one could answer him a word, nor did anyone dare from that day to question him any further*[^1].

This Mark too records consequently and in the same order[^2]. Luke also is silent only about the one who questioned the Lord as to which was the first commandment in the Law[^3]; but, this omitted, he too preserves the same order, and likewise narrates that the Lord asked the Jews this about the Christ, how he is the son of David. Nor does it matter to the meaning that, according to Matthew, when Jesus had asked what they thought about the Christ, whose son he was, they answered: *David's.* Then at last he brought in how David called him Lord. But according to those two, Mark and Luke, they are found neither to have been questioned nor to have answered. For we ought to understand that, after their answer, the saying of the Lord himself was conveyed by the two evangelists as having been spoken by him in the hearing of those whom he wished to instruct usefully by his teaching and to estrange from the doctrine of the scribes, who about the Christ understood only this, that he had been made according to the flesh from the seed of David, but did not understand him to be God, on account of which he was the Lord of David himself. Therefore, as though concerning those who were in error, the Lord, making his discourse to these whom he wished to be freed from their error, is recorded according to these two evangelists, so that what was said to them: *how do you say,* as Matthew narrates, is to be taken thus, not as said to those, but rather as said about those to the ones whom he wished to instruct.

[^1]: Mt 22:41–46.
[^2]: See Mk 12:35–37.
[^3]: Lk 20:41–44.
