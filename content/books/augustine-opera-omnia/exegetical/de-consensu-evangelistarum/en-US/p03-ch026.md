# On the calling of Matthew too: whether this same Matthew agrees with Mark and Luke, who say Levi the son of Alphaeus.

**26. 59.** From here, then, Matthew goes on, saying: *and as Jesus passed by from there, he saw a man sitting at the tax office, named Matthew, and said to him: 'Follow me,' and rising he followed him*[^1]. Mark narrates this thus, he too keeping the same order after the healing of that paralytic: *and he went out*, he says, *to the sea, and all the crowd came to him, and he taught them. And as he passed by, he saw Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax office, and said to him: 'Follow me.' And rising he followed him*[^2]. Nothing here contradicts; for Matthew is the same one who is also Levi. Luke too subjoins this, after the same paralytic was healed, thus: *and after these things he went out and saw a publican named Levi sitting at the tax office, and said to him: 'Follow me.' And leaving all and rising, he followed him*[^3]. But from this it seems more probable that Matthew, by recalling, mentions these things which he had passed over, because Matthew is surely to be believed to have been called before that discourse held on the mountain. For on that mountain Luke then mentions that all twelve were chosen out of the many disciples, whom he also named apostles[^4].

[^1]: Mt 9:9
[^2]: Mk 2:13-14
[^3]: Lk 5:27-28
[^4]: See Lk 6:13
