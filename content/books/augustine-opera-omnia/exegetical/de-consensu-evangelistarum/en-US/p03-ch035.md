# On the man who had a withered hand, who was cured on the Sabbath: how the narration of Matthew agrees with Mark and Luke.

**35. 82.** Matthew goes on, then, narrating thus: *and when he had gone on from there, he came into their synagogue. And behold, a man having a withered hand*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says, *and it was restored to health like the other*[^2]. About this healed man who had a withered hand, Mark and Luke too are not silent[^3]. It could be thought that both about the ears of grain and about this healed man were done on the same day, since the Sabbath is mentioned here too, had Luke not disclosed that the healing of the withered hand was done on another Sabbath.

Accordingly, what Matthew says—*and when he had gone on from there, he came into their synagogue*—he did not indeed come except when he had gone on from there; but after how many days he came into their synagogue, after he had gone on from that field of grain, or whether he went there directly and immediately, is not expressed. And for this reason a place is given to the narration of Luke, who says that this man's hand was healed on another Sabbath. But it can trouble us how Matthew said that they questioned the Lord *whether it is lawful to cure on the Sabbath*[^4], wishing to find an occasion of accusing, while he set before them a similitude about a sheep, saying:

*what man shall there be among you who has one sheep, and if it falls into a pit on the Sabbath, will he not take hold of it and lift it out? How much better is a man than a sheep? Therefore it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath*[^5], when Mark and Luke report that they rather were questioned by the Lord: *is it lawful to do good on the Sabbath or to do evil? To save a soul or to destroy it?*[^6] And so it must be understood that they first questioned the Lord *whether it is lawful to cure on the Sabbath*, then that he, understanding their thoughts, who were seeking an entrance for accusing, set in the midst the one he was about to heal and asked what Mark and Luke mention that he asked, and then, while they were silent, set before them the similitude about the sheep and concluded that it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath; finally, having looked around at them with anger, as Mark says, grieved at the blindness of their heart, said to the man, *Stretch out your hand*[^7].

[^1]: Mt 12:9–10.
[^2]: Mt 12:13.
[^3]: See Mk 3:1–5; Lk 6:6–11.
[^4]: Mt 12:10.
[^5]: Mt 12:11–12.
[^6]: Mk 3:4; Lk 6:9.
[^7]: Mt 12:13; Mk 3:5; Lk 6:10.
