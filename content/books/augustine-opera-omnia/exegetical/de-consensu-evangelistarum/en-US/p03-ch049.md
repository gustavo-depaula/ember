# On the Canaanite woman who said, And the dogs eat of the crumbs that fall from their masters' table, how Matthew and Luke agree with each other.

**49.** 103. Matthew then continues, after that discourse of the Lord in which he dealt with the Pharisees about unwashed hands, and weaves together his narration thus, preserving the order, so far as the transition itself indicates, of the very events that followed: *And going out from there,* he says, *Jesus withdrew to the district of Tyre and Sidon. And behold, a Canaanite woman from those borders came out and cried, saying to him: 'Have mercy on me, Lord, son of David; my daughter is severely vexed by a demon.' But he did not answer her a word*[^1], and so on down to where he says: '*O woman, great is your faith; be it done for you as you wish.' And her daughter was healed from that hour*[^2].

This account of the Canaanite woman Mark too records, preserving the same order of events and raising no question of contradiction, except that he says the Lord was in a house when this same woman came to him pleading for her daughter[^3]. Matthew could indeed easily be understood to have been silent about the house yet to have recorded the same event; but since he says the disciples thus urged the Lord: *send her away, for she is crying after us*[^4], this seems to mean nothing other than that the woman uttered her pleading words behind the Lord as he walked. How then was it in a house, unless we are to understand that Mark indeed said she entered where Jesus was, since he had said beforehand that he was in a house? But since Matthew says *he did not answer her a word*[^5], he gave us to recognize that, in that silence which both passed over, Jesus had gone out of that house, and so the rest is woven together, which now is in no respect discordant. For what Mark records, that the Lord answered her about the children's bread not being thrown to the dogs, was said after those things which Matthew did not pass over in silence had been interposed, that is, that the disciples asked him on her behalf, and that he answered that he had been sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel, and that she came, that is, drew near, and worshiped him, saying: *Lord, help me*[^6], and then afterward was said what both evangelists record.

[^1]: Mt 15:21–25.
[^2]: Mt 15:28.
[^3]: See Mk 7:24–30.
[^4]: Mt 15:23.
[^5]: Mt 15:23.
[^6]: Mt 15:25.
