# On the calling of the fishing apostles.

**17. 34.** Matthew goes on, narrating: *But when he had heard that John had been handed over, he withdrew into Galilee*[^1]. This Mark too says, and Luke too[^2]; but Luke says nothing in this place about John being handed over. But John the Evangelist says that, before Jesus went into Galilee, Peter and Andrew stayed with him one day, and then the name was imposed on Peter, when before he was called Simon. Likewise, on the following day, when he now wished to go out into Galilee, he found Philip and said to him that he should follow him. From there it came about that he narrated also about Nathanael. And on the third day, when he was in Galilee, he did that miracle in Cana of the turning of water into wine[^3]. All of which the other evangelists passed over, weaving into their narratives that Jesus returned into Galilee. Whence it is understood that some days were interposed, in which those things about the disciples were done which are interposed by John. But it is not contrary to that place where Matthew narrates that the Lord said to Peter, *You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church*[^4]. For he is not to be understood as having received this name then, but rather then, when John records that it was said to him, *You shall be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter*[^5], so that the Lord called him by this name afterward, saying, *You are Peter*. For he does not say, *You shall be called Peter*, but, *You are Peter*, because it had already been said to him before, *You shall be called*.

**17. 35.** Then Matthew weaves his narrative and says: *And having left the city of Nazareth, he came and dwelt in Capernaum by the sea, in the borders of Zebulun and Naphtali*[^6], and the rest, until the discourse is concluded which he held on the mountain[^7]. In this weaving of the narrative Mark attests to him about the calling of the disciples Peter and Andrew, and a little after, James and John[^8]. But while Matthew immediately joined the narrative of that lengthy discourse which he held on the mountain, after he had healed many and a great crowd had followed him[^9], Mark interposed other things—because he was teaching them in the synagogue and they were astounded at his teaching[^10]. Then he himself said what Matthew too said after that lengthy discourse, that *he was teaching them as one having authority and not as the scribes*[^11]. He narrated also about the man from whom the unclean spirit was cast out, then about Peter's mother-in-law[^12]; and in these things Luke agrees with him[^13]; but Matthew narrated nothing about that demon; about Peter's mother-in-law, however, he was not silent, but afterward[^14].

**17. 36.** But in this place which we are now considering, the same Matthew, after the calling of the disciples—whom, while they were fishing, he commanded to follow him—narrates that he went about Galilee, teaching in the synagogues and preaching the Gospel and healing every illness; and, the crowds having gathered to him, that he went up onto the mountain and used that lengthy discourse[^15]. He gives, then, a place for understanding that the things were done then which Mark narrates after the choosing of the same disciples—when he went about Galilee and taught in their synagogues, then also about Peter's mother-in-law[^16]—but that he mentioned afterward what he had passed over, although he did not recall into the narrative all that had been passed over.

**17. 37.** It can indeed trouble one how John says that not in Galilee, but beside the Jordan, Andrew first followed the Lord with another, whose name is not mentioned; then that Peter received his name from him; thirdly, that Philip was called to follow him[^17]. But the other three evangelists say that they were called from their fishing, agreeing well enough among themselves, especially Matthew and Mark[^18]. For Luke does not name Andrew, who nevertheless is understood to have been in that boat, according to the narration of Matthew and Mark, who briefly touch on how it was done, which Luke set forth more openly, mentioning there also the miracle about the catch of fish, and that from the boat itself the Lord first spoke to the crowds[^19]. This too seems to differ, that he mentions said to Peter only by the Lord, *from now on you shall be catching men*[^20], which the others narrated was said to both brothers. But this could surely first be said to Peter, when he marveled at the great multitude of fish caught, which Luke intimated, and to both afterward, which those two mentioned. That, then, which we said about John must be carefully considered; for it cannot be thought a small contradiction, since there is much difference both of place and of time and of the calling itself. For if, beside the Jordan, before Jesus went into Galilee, at the testimony of John the Baptist, two followed him, of whom one was Andrew, who immediately brought his brother Simon to Jesus, when he also received the name that he should be called Peter—how is it said by the other evangelists that he found them fishing in Galilee and called them to discipleship, except that it is to be understood that they did not then so see the Lord beside the Jordan as already to cleave to him inseparably, but only came to know who he was, and, marveling, returned to their own affairs?

**17. 38.** For when he had made wine from water in Cana of Galilee, the same John says that his disciples believed in him. Which he narrates thus: *And on the third day a marriage was made in Cana of Galilee, and the mother of Jesus was there. And both Jesus and his disciples were invited to the marriage*[^21]. They surely, if they then believed in him, as he says a little after, were not yet disciples when they were invited to the marriage. But this was said in that manner of speaking by which we speak when we say that the apostle Paul was born in Tarsus of Cilicia[^22]—for he was not yet then an apostle. So, when we hear that the disciples of Christ were invited to the marriage, we ought to understand not yet disciples, but those who were to be disciples. For they were surely already disciples of Christ when these things were narrated and written down, and therefore the narrator of past times spoke thus about them.

**17. 39.** But as for what the same John says—*after this he went down to Capernaum, he and his mother and his brothers and his disciples, and they stayed there not many days*[^23]—it is uncertain whether Peter and Andrew and the sons of Zebedee had already attached themselves to him. For Matthew first narrates that he came and dwelt in Capernaum, and afterward that he called them, fishing, from their boats[^24]; but this one narrates that his disciples came to Capernaum with him. Or perhaps Matthew recapitulated what he had passed over, because he himself does not say, "after this, walking beside the sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers," but, without any difference of following time, *walking*, he says, *beside the sea of Galilee he saw two brothers*[^25], and the rest? Accordingly it can be that he afterward narrated not what was done afterward, but what he had earlier passed over, so that they are understood to have come to Capernaum with him, where John says that he himself and his mother and his disciples came. Or were they rather other disciples—just as Philip was already following him, whom he had so called as to say to him, *Follow me*[^26]? For in what order all twelve apostles were called does not appear in the narrations of the evangelists, since not only the order of the calling, but not even the calling itself of all is mentioned, but only of Philip and Peter and Andrew and the sons of Zebedee and Matthew the publican, who was also called Levi[^27]. Singly, however, and first and alone, Peter received his name from him[^28]. For the sons of Zebedee he called not singly, but both together, *sons of thunder*[^29].

**17. 40.** It must indeed be noted that the Gospel and apostolic Scripture calls not only those twelve his *disciples*, but all who, believing in him, were instructed by his teaching for the kingdom of heaven. Out of whose multitude he chose twelve, whom he also named apostles, as Luke mentions[^30]. For he himself a little after says: *and coming down with them, he stood in a level place, and a crowd of his disciples and a copious multitude of the people*[^31]. He would surely not call twelve men a *crowd of disciples*. In other places of the Scriptures too this evidently appears, that all those were called his *disciples* who learned from him what pertains to eternal life.

**17. 41.** But it can be asked how he called the fishermen two by two from the boats—first Peter and Andrew, then, having gone forward a little, the other two, the sons of Zebedee, as Matthew and Mark narrate—when Luke says that both their boats were filled with that great catch of fish, and mentions that the companions of Peter, James and John the sons of Zebedee, were called to help, when they could not draw out the nets full, and at the same time marveled at so great a multitude of fish which had been caught; and when he had said to Peter only, *Do not fear; from now on you shall be catching men*[^32], that nevertheless they together, the boats drawn up to land, followed him. Whence it is to be understood that this was done first which Luke intimates, and that they were not then called by the Lord, but only it was foretold to Peter that he would catch men. Which was not said as if he would never afterward catch fish; for even after the Lord's resurrection we read that they fished[^33]. It was said, then, that he would thereafter catch men; it was not said that he would no longer catch fish. Whence a place is given to understand that they returned, according to custom, to the catching of fish, so that afterward there was done what Matthew and Mark narrate, when he called them two by two and himself commanded that they should follow him—first the two, Peter and Andrew, then the other two, the sons of Zebedee. For then, not with the boats drawn up to land as though with care of returning, but so they followed him, as one calling and commanding that they should follow him.
