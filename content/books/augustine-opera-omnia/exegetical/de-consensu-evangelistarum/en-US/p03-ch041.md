# That from the boat he spoke to the crowds about him, of whose seed some fell on the path, etc., and about him on whom tares were sown, and about the grain of mustard, and about the wheat.

**41. 88.** Matthew goes on: *on that day, Jesus, going out of the house, sat beside the sea. And many crowds gathered to him, so that, going up into a boat, he sat, and all the crowd stood on the shore. And he spoke many things to them in parables, saying*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says, *therefore every scribe learned in the kingdom of heaven is like a man, a householder, who brings forth from his treasure things new and old*[^2]. That, after what was narrated about the mother and brothers of the Lord, this was done immediately, and that Matthew kept this order even in narrating, the matter intimates, because, when he passed on from there, he so connected it as to say: *on that day, Jesus, going out of the house, sat beside the sea, and many crowds gathered to him.*

For when he says *on that day* (unless perhaps *day*, in the manner of the Scriptures, signifies "time"), he sufficiently indicates either that this was done as a consequence or that not many things could be interposed, especially because Mark too keeps that order[^3]. But Luke, after what he narrates about the mother and brothers of the Lord, passes on to something else, nor by that transition does he make any connection[^4] by which he might seem to contradict this order.

Of all these things, then, which Matthew narrated that the Lord spoke, those which Mark and Luke said with him have no question of contradiction; and those which he himself alone said are much more without controversy. Nor in the order itself, although one keeps it somewhat different from another—partly of the things done, partly of his own recollection—do I see in what or to whom anyone contradicts.

[^1]: Mt 13:1–3.
[^2]: Mt 13:52.
[^3]: See Mk 4:1–34.
[^4]: See Lk 8:22–25.
