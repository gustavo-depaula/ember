# On John imprisoned, or even killed: in what order it is narrated by these three.

**44. 92.** Matthew, then, goes on and says: *for Herod had seized John and bound him and put him in prison on account of Herodias, his brother's wife*[^1], and the rest, up to that place where he says, *and his disciples, coming, took his body and buried it, and coming announced it to Jesus*[^2]. Mark narrates this similarly[^3]. But Luke recalls it not in the same order, but around the very baptism by which the Lord was baptized[^4]. Whence it is understood that he himself too, on the occasion, anticipated this, so as to narrate there what was done much later. For when he had mentioned John's words about the Lord, that *the winnowing fan is in his hand, and he will cleanse his threshing floor, he will store the wheat in his barn, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire*,

he immediately subjoined what John the Evangelist most openly sets forth was not done immediately, when he mentions that, after Jesus was baptized, he went into Galilee, when he made wine from water, and thence, after a few days' dwelling in Capernaum, returned to the land of Judea and there baptized many around the Jordan, before John was sent into prison[^5]. But who that is less learned in these writings would not think that, as it were, after those words about the winnowing fan and the cleansed threshing floor, Herod was immediately offended by John and put him in prison[^6]? But that these things were not narrated in the order in which they were done, we have both already proved elsewhere, and in this very place not anyone else, but the same Luke, proves it. For if, after those words, John was immediately put into prison, how, after the very mention of John put into prison, was Jesus baptized, according to Luke's own narration? Accordingly it is manifest that, on the occasion, recalling it, he anticipated it, and before many things which, before this happened to John, he was about to narrate as done, he set this, anticipated, in his narration. But neither did those two, Matthew and Mark, set down in their narration about John put into prison in that order of things[^7] in which it appears done even in their writings. For they too said that, John being handed over, the Lord went into Galilee, and after many things which he did in Galilee they come to Herod's admonition or perplexity, that John, whom he beheaded, had risen from the dead, and on this occasion they narrate all the things that happened concerning John, imprisoned and killed[^8].

[^1]: Mt 14:3.
[^2]: Mt 14:12.
[^3]: See Mk 6:17–29.
[^4]: See Lk 3:15–20.
[^5]: See Jn 2:1–12; 3:22–24.
[^6]: See Lk 3:17–20.
[^7]: See Mt 4:12; Mk 1:14.
[^8]: See Mt 14:1–12; Mk 6:14–29; Lk 9:7–9.
