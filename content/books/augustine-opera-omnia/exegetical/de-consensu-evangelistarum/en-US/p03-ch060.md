# On the little child whom he set forth to be imitated, on the scandals of the world, on the members of the body that cause scandal, on the angels of the little ones who see the face of the Father, and so on.

**61.** 119. This same Matthew, then, continues and says: *at that hour the disciples came to Jesus, saying: 'Who, do you think, is greater in the kingdom of heaven?' And Jesus, calling a little child, set him in their midst and said: 'Truly I say to you, unless you turn and become like little children, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^1],' down to where he says: *so also my heavenly Father will do to you, if each of you does not forgive his brother from your hearts*[^2].

Of this somewhat longer discourse of the Lord, Mark, following the same order, set down not all but certain parts, and he also inserted certain things himself which Matthew does not say[^3]. But the whole discourse, as far as we have undertaken to consider it, is interrupted by Peter alone, asking how often he ought to forgive his brother.

For the Lord was speaking such things that it is sufficiently clear that this too, which Peter asked and to which he was answered, belongs to the same discourse. Luke, however, records nothing in this preserved order except that about the little child whom he set before the disciples to be imitated, when they were thinking about their own greatness[^4]. For if he said any other like things which are also set down in this discourse, he recalled them as said elsewhere and on other occasions, just as John records as said by the Lord after the resurrection the matter of the forgiveness of sins, that they will be retained if they retain them for anyone, and forgiven if they forgive them for anyone[^5], whereas Matthew remembers that the Lord said this in this discourse, the same thing which Matthew himself attests was also said earlier to Peter[^6]. We ought therefore to remember that Jesus often said the same things in several places, lest we anywhere be troubled if the order of the sayings is thought by some to be contradictory, as we have so often pointed out, so that it need not be necessary to keep noting this.

[^1]: Mt 18:13.
[^2]: Mt 18:35.
[^3]: See Mk 9:33–49.
[^4]: See Lk 9:46–48.
[^5]: See Jn 20:23.
[^6]: See Mt 18:18.
