# Book I

## The authority of the Gospels.

**1. 1.** Among all the divine authorities contained in the Sacred Scriptures, the Gospel rightly stands preeminent. For what the Law and the prophets foretold would come to pass is shown, restored and fulfilled, in the Gospel.

The first preachers of the Gospel were the apostles, who saw our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ himself even present in the flesh. They not only remembered the words heard from his mouth and the deeds done by him before their eyes, but also took care, when the office of evangelizing had been laid upon them, to announce to the human race those things which—before they had attached themselves to him as disciples—had been divinely done at his nativity, in his infancy, or in his boyhood, and were worthy of remembrance, and which they had been able to seek out and learn, by the surest indications and the most trustworthy testimonies, whether from himself, or from his parents, or from any others whatsoever.

Some of them, namely Matthew and John, also published in separate books written accounts about him, such things as seemed fit to be written.

**1. 2.** And lest it should be thought to make any difference, as regards the receiving and preaching of the Gospel, whether those announce it who followed the same Lord here appearing in the flesh, serving him as disciples, or those who faithfully believed what they had learned from them,

it was brought about by divine providence, through the Holy Spirit, that to certain ones even among those who followed the first apostles there was granted the authority not only of announcing but also of writing the Gospel.

These are Mark and Luke. As for the other men who attempted or dared to write something about the deeds of the Lord or of the apostles, they were not such, in their times, that the Church would put faith in them or receive their writings into the canonical authority of the Sacred Books—not only because they were not the kind of men whose narratives ought to be believed, but also because they deceitfully inserted into their writings certain things which the catholic and apostolic rule of faith and sound doctrine condemns.

The order of the evangelists, and their manner of writing.

**2. 3.** These four evangelists, then, most widely known throughout the whole world—and perhaps four for this reason, that there are four parts of the world, throughout the whole of which they declared, by the very mystery of their number, that Christ's Church was to be spread—

are reported to have written in this order: first Matthew, then Mark, third Luke, last John. Hence one order belonged to their coming to know and preaching, but another to their writing.

For in coming to know and in preaching, the first were certainly those who, following the Lord present in the flesh, heard him speak and saw him act, and were sent from his own mouth to evangelize.

But in the writing of the Gospel—which must be believed to have been divinely ordained—two of them held the first and the last place out of the number of those whom the Lord chose before his passion: Matthew first, John last, so that the remaining two, who were not of that number but had nonetheless followed Christ speaking in them, might be embraced as sons and, being thus set in the middle place, be guarded by the two on either side.

**2. 4.** Of these four, indeed, Matthew alone is reported to have written in the Hebrew tongue, the others in Greek. And although each one seems to have kept some order of narration of his own, yet none of them is found to have wished to write as if ignorant of another who preceded him, or to have passed over in ignorance what another is found to have written; rather, as it was inspired in each, he added the not-superfluous cooperation of his own labor.

For Matthew is understood to have taken up the incarnation of the Lord according to the royal line, and most of his deeds and sayings according to the present life of men. Mark, following him, seems to be as it were his attendant and abbreviator.

For with John alone he said nothing,

by himself alone very few things,

with Luke alone fewer still,

but with Matthew very many things, and many of them in almost the same number and the very same words, in agreement either with him alone or with the rest.

Luke, however, appears more occupied with the priestly line and person of the Lord.

For he too ascends to David himself, yet not following the royal stem, but going up through those who were not kings,

he comes out at Nathan the son of David[^1], who was himself not a king either.

Not as Matthew, who, descending through Solomon the king[^2], follows out in order the other kings as well, preserving in them a mystical number of which we shall speak later.

Matthew, together with Mark, directed his attention to the royal person of Christ; Luke, to the priestly.

**3. 5.** For the Lord Jesus Christ, the one true king and the one true priest—the former to rule us, the latter to expiate our sins—declared that he himself enacted, in his own figure, these two persons that had been severally entrusted to the fathers,

whether by that title which was fixed upon his cross, *The King of the Jews*[^3]—whence, by a hidden prompting, Pilate answered, *What I have written I have written*[^4], for it had been foretold in the *Psalms*, *Do not destroy the inscription of the title*[^5]—

or, as regards the person of the priest, in that he taught us to offer and to receive, whence he sent ahead a prophecy concerning himself, saying, *You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek*[^6].

And by many other proofs of the divine Scriptures Christ appears as king and priest[^7]. Hence David himself—who not without reason is more frequently called his father than Abraham is, and whom Matthew and Luke held in common, the one tracing the descent through Solomon, the other the ascent to Nathan[^8]—although he was openly a king, nevertheless also prefigured the person of a priest, when he ate the loaves of the presence, which it was not lawful to eat except for the priests alone[^9]. To this is added that Luke alone also records that Mary was shown by the angel to have been a kinswoman of Elizabeth[^10], who was the wife of Zechariah the priest, of whom the same evangelist wrote that he had her as his wife from among the daughters of Aaron[^11], that is, from the tribe of priests.

**3. 6.** Since, therefore, Matthew directed his attention to the person of the king, and Luke to that of the priest, both especially commended the humanity of Christ. For it was according to his manhood that Christ was made both king and priest,

to whom God gave the throne of David his father, that of his kingdom there should be no end[^12], and that there might be, to intercede for us, a mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus[^13]. Luke, however, did not have an abbreviator joined to him as Matthew had Mark, and this perhaps not without some mystery, since it belongs to kings not to be without the attendance of a retinue. Hence the one who had undertaken to narrate the royal person of Christ had joined to him as it were a companion to follow in his footsteps in some fashion; but the priest, because he entered the Holy of Holies alone[^14], for that reason Luke, whose attention was upon the priesthood of Christ, did not have, as it were, an associate following after to abbreviate his narrative in some fashion.

John took care to express the divinity of Christ himself.

**4. 7.** These three evangelists, nevertheless, were chiefly occupied with those things which Christ did in time through his human flesh; but John especially has in view the very divinity of the Lord, by which he is equal to the Father, and took care above all to commend it in his Gospel, so far as he believed it could suffice among men.

And so he is borne far above those three,

so that you may see these in some fashion dwelling on earth with Christ the man, but that one transcending the cloud by which all the earth is covered[^15], and reaching the clear heaven, whence with the keenest and steadiest gaze of the mind he might behold, in the beginning the Word, God with God, through whom all things were made,

and might recognize him as made flesh, that he might dwell among us[^16]—that he took on flesh, not that he was changed into flesh.

For unless the assumption of flesh had been accomplished with the divinity kept unchangeable, it would not be said, *I and the Father are one*[^17]; for the Father and the flesh are not one.

And this testimony of the Lord concerning himself the same John alone has recorded—both, *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*, and, *I am in the Father and the Father is in me*[^18], and, *that they may be one, even as we are one*[^19], and, *whatever the Father does, these same things the Son does likewise*[^20];

and whatever other things there are that, to those who understand rightly, intimate the divinity of Christ, in which he is equal to the Father, John almost alone has set down in his Gospel—as one who, from the very breast of the Lord, upon which he was accustomed to recline at his supper[^21], drank the secret of his divinity more abundantly and, as it were, more intimately.

The two virtues: John is engaged with the contemplative, the other evangelists with the active.

**5. 8.** Accordingly, since two virtues are set before the human soul—one active, the other contemplative; the one by which one goes, the other to which one arrives; the one by which one labors, that the heart may be cleansed for seeing God[^22], the other by which one is at leisure and God is seen—

the former lies in the precepts for the practice of this temporal life, the latter in the teaching about that everlasting life. And for this reason the former works, the latter rests, because the former is in the purifying of sins, the latter in the light of the purified[^23].

And for this reason, in this mortal life, the former lies in the work of good conduct[^24]; the latter, rather, in faith, and—among very few—through a glass in a riddle and in part, in some vision of the unchangeable truth[^25].

These two virtues are understood to be figured in the two wives of Jacob[^26]. Concerning them I discoursed, according to my small measure, in the *Answer to Faustus the Manichean*, as far as seemed sufficient for that work.

For Leah is interpreted "laboring," but Rachel "the seen beginning." From which it is given to understand, if you attend carefully, that the three evangelists, who pursued more copiously the temporal deeds and sayings of the Lord which had the most force for forming the conduct of the present life, were engaged with that active virtue; but John, narrating far fewer of the Lord's deeds, yet recording more carefully and abundantly his sayings—especially those that would suggest the unity of the Trinity and the felicity of eternal life—held his intention and preaching to the commending of the contemplative virtue.

Some have understood the four living creatures from the Apocalypse of the four evangelists more fittingly than others have.

**6. 9.** Hence it seems to me that those who, from the *Apocalypse*, have interpreted those four living creatures[^27] as signifying the four evangelists attended to something more probable—those who understood the lion in Matthew, the man in Mark, the calf in Luke, the eagle in John—than those who assigned the man to Matthew, the eagle to Mark, the lion to John. For they wished to take a certain conjecture from the beginnings of the books, not from the whole intention of the evangelists, which ought rather to have been searched out. For it is far more fitting that he who especially commended the royal person of Christ should be taken as signified by the lion.

Hence also in the *Apocalypse* the lion is mentioned together with that very royal tribe, where it is said, *The lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered*[^28].

For according to Matthew the Magi too are related to have come from the east to seek and adore a king, who appeared to them as born by means of the star; and King Herod himself dreads the infant king and, that he might be able to kill him, slays so many little ones[^29].

But that Luke was signified by the calf, on account of the greatest victim of the priest, neither party has doubted. For there the discourse of the narrator begins with the priest Zechariah; there the kinship of Mary and Elizabeth is mentioned[^30]; there the rites of the first priesthood are narrated as fulfilled in the infant Christ[^31]; and whatever other things can be carefully noted, by which it appears that Luke had his intention upon the person of the priest.

Mark, then, who wished to narrate neither the royal nor the priestly line, neither kinship nor consecration, and yet is shown to be engaged with those things which Christ the man did, seems to be signified, among those four living creatures, only by the figure of the man.

Now these three living creatures—whether lion, or man, or calf—walk upon the earth; hence these three evangelists were chiefly occupied with those things which Christ did in the flesh, and with the precepts for practicing the mortal life which he handed down to those who bear flesh.

But John flies above the clouds of human infirmity, like an eagle, and beholds the light of the unchangeable truth with the keenest and steadiest eyes of the heart.

The reason for undertaking the work on the agreement of the evangelists. A reply is made to those who say that Christ wrote nothing, and that his disciples lied in proclaiming him God.

**7. 10.** These holy four-horse chariots of the Lord, by which, borne through the world, he subdues the peoples under his gentle yoke and his light burden[^32], certain men assail with calumnies, whether out of impious vanity or out of unlearned rashness,

so as to detract from the credit of the truthful narrative of those through whom the Christian religion, sown throughout the world, has sprung up with such fruitfulness that unbelievers now scarcely dare to mutter their calumnies even among themselves, restrained as they are by the faith of the nations and the devotion of all peoples.

Nevertheless, because they still, by their calumnious disputations, either hold some back from the faith so that they do not believe, or disturb others, who already believe, by harassing them as far as they can; and because some of the brethren, with their faith secure, desire to know what they may answer to such questions, whether for the advancement of their own knowledge or for refuting the empty talk of those men—by the inspiration and help of the Lord our God (which would that it might profit also their salvation), we have undertaken in this work to demonstrate the error or rashness of those who think they put forward sufficiently clever accusations against the four books of the Gospel, which the four evangelists each wrote.

That this may be done, it must be shown how these four writers do not contradict one another. For this is what they are accustomed to object as if it were the chief proof of their empty claim: that the evangelists disagree among themselves.

**7. 11.** But first that must be discussed which is wont to trouble some, namely, why the Lord himself wrote nothing, so that it is necessary to believe others who wrote about him. For this is said especially by those pagans who do not dare to fault or blaspheme the Lord Jesus Christ himself, and who attribute to him the most excellent wisdom, but only as to a man; yet they say that his disciples attributed to their master more than was the case, in calling him the Son of God and the Word of God, through whom all things were made, and saying that he and God the Father are one[^33], and whatever similar things there are in the apostolic *writings*, by which we have learned that he, together with the Father, is to be worshiped as the one God[^34]. For they think he is to be honored as a most wise man, but they deny that he is to be worshiped as God.

**7. 12.** When, therefore, they ask why he himself wrote nothing, they seem to have been prepared to believe about him what he himself had written, not what others proclaimed about him according to their own judgment.

Of these men I ask why they believed, concerning certain of their most noble philosophers, what their disciples left written in memory of them, when those philosophers had written nothing about themselves.

For Pythagoras, than whom Greece then had nothing more illustrious in that contemplative virtue, is reported to have written nothing, not only about himself, but about anything at all. As for Socrates, whom in turn they preferred to all in the active virtue, by which conduct is formed,

so much so that they do not keep silent that by the testimony also of their god Apollo he was pronounced the wisest of all—he treated Aesop's fables in a few little verses, applying his own words and meters to another's matter,

and was so far from wishing to write anything that he said he did even this under compulsion by the command of his daemon, as Plato, the most noble of his disciples, records—in which work, nevertheless, he chose to adorn another's opinions rather than his own.

What reason, then, is there why they believe about these men what their disciples committed to writing concerning them, yet are unwilling to believe about Christ what his disciples wrote about him,

especially since they confess that other men were surpassed by him in wisdom, although they are unwilling to confess that he is God?

Or were those men, whom they do not doubt to have been far inferior to him, able to make their disciples truthful about themselves, and he was not able?

But if it is most absurd to say so, then let them believe about him whom they confess to be wise, not what they themselves wish, but what they read in those who learned the things they wrote from that wise man.

If, on the report of fame, Christ is believed to be most wise, why, when a greater fame proclaims it, is he not believed to be God.

**8. 13.** Next let them say from what source they were able even to know or to hear that he was most wise.

If by the spreading of fame, is fame a surer messenger about him than his disciples, by whose preaching of him that very fame became fragrant throughout the whole world?

Finally, let them set fame against fame, and believe that fame about him which is the greater. For that fame which is scattered abroad with marvelous brightness concerning the Catholic Church, which they marvel to see spread over the whole world, incomparably surpasses the slight rumors of those men;

and that fame, so great, so renowned that, fearing it, these men gnaw their trembling and tepid little contradictions in their own bosom, now more afraid of being heard than wishing to be believed, proclaims Christ the only-begotten Son of God and God, through whom all things were made[^35].

If, then, they choose fame as a witness, why do they not choose this one, which shines forth with such brightness? If they choose a writing, why not the evangelical one, which prevails with such great authority? We, for our part, believe about their gods what both their own more ancient writing contains and a more renowned fame reports.

And if these gods are to be adored, why do they laugh at them in the theaters? But if they are to be laughed at, there is the more to laugh at when they are adored in the temples.

It remains that they themselves are willing to be witnesses about Christ, who take from themselves the merit of knowing what they say, by speaking what they do not know. Or, if they say they have certain books which they assert he wrote, let them bring them forth to us.

For surely they are most useful and most wholesome, which, as they themselves confess, a most wise man wrote. But if they are afraid to bring them forth, then assuredly they are bad; and further, if they are bad, a most wise man did not write them; but they confess that Christ was most wise: therefore Christ did not write any such thing.

Some pretend that Christ wrote books on the magical arts.

**9. 14.** But these men are so foolish as to say that in those books which they suppose he wrote are contained the arts by which they think he worked those miracles, the fame of which has spread everywhere:

by supposing which, they betray themselves as to what they love and what they aim at, since indeed they think Christ was most wise for this reason, that he knew some unlawful things or other, which not only Christian discipline but even the very administration of the earthly commonwealth rightly condemns. And surely, those who affirm that they have read such books of Christ, why do they not themselves do any such things as those at which they marvel that he did from such books?

Some rave that those same books were addressed to Peter and Paul.

**10. 15.** What of the fact that, by a divine judgment, some of those who either believe or wish it to be believed that Christ wrote such things err so far as to say that those same books were inscribed to Peter and Paul as if with the heading of a letter? And it may be that, whether the enemies of the name of Christ, or those who thought that by such execrable arts they could lend the weight of authority drawn from so glorious a name, wrote such things under the name of Christ and the apostles. In which most deceitful audacity they have been so blinded that they are deservedly laughed at even by boys who, still as boys, in the rank of lectors, know the Christian writings.

Against those who dream that Christ turned peoples to himself by the magic art.

**10. 16.** For when they wished to pretend that Christ had written some such thing to his disciples, they considered to whom he could most readily be believed to have written, namely to those who had attached themselves to him more intimately, to whom that as it were secret might worthily be committed; and Peter and Paul occurred to them—I believe because in many places they saw them painted together with him, since Rome solemnly and with great renown commends the merits of Peter and Paul, on account also of the same day of their passion.

Thus they have altogether deserved to err, who sought Christ and his apostles not in the holy books but on painted walls; nor is it any wonder if those who feigned were deceived by those who painted. For during the whole time in which Christ lived in mortal flesh with his disciples, Paul was not yet his disciple, whom—after his own passion, after the resurrection, after the ascension, after the Holy Spirit was sent from heaven, after the conversion and marvelous faith of many Jews, after the stoning of Stephen the deacon and martyr—while he was still called Saul and was grievously persecuting those who had believed in Christ, he called from heaven and made his disciple and apostle[^36]. How, then, could he write books—which they wish to be thought he wrote before he died—to disciples as the most intimate, namely Peter and Paul, when Paul was not yet his disciple?

**11. 17.** Let those also consider this, who rave that he could do such great things by the magic arts and that he consecrated his own name to peoples, to turn them to himself, by that very art:

whether he could, by the magic arts, fill so many prophets with the divine Spirit before he was born on earth—prophets who foretold concerning him such future things as we now read in the Gospel as already past, and such as we now see present throughout the world.

For even if by the magic arts he brought it about that he should be worshiped after death, he was not a magician before he was born—he to whose coming, foretold by prophecy, a single nation was assigned, the whole administration of whose commonwealth was a prophecy of that king who was to come and to found a heavenly city out of all nations.

Why the God of the Jews was not received by the Romans.

**12. 18.** Accordingly, that Hebrew nation, assigned, as I said, to the prophesying of Christ, had no other god but the one God, the true God, who made heaven and earth and all things that are in them.

When he was offended, they were often subjected to their enemies; and now, even for the most grievous crime of having killed Christ, they have been utterly uprooted from Jerusalem itself, which was the head of their kingdom, and subjugated to the Roman Empire.

Now the Romans were accustomed to propitiate the gods of the nations they subjugated, by worshiping them and taking up their sacred rites. But this they were unwilling to do with the God of the Hebrew nation, whether when they attacked it or when they conquered it.

I believe it was because they saw that, if they received the sacred rites of that God who commanded that he alone be worshiped, with the images even destroyed, they would have to give up all that they had earlier taken up for worship, by whose religions they supposed their empire had grown—in which the deceit of the demons greatly deceived them.

For surely they ought to have understood that it was by the hidden will of the true God, in whose hands the supreme power over things lies, that the kingdom was given and increased to them,

not by the favor of those gods, who, if they had had any power in this matter, would rather have protected their own worshipers, lest they be overcome by the Romans, or would have subdued and subjugated the Romans themselves to them.

**12. 19.** For neither can they say that their piety and morals were loved and chosen by the gods of the nations they conquered. They will never say this, if they recall their own beginnings—the asylum of criminals and the fratricide of Romulus.

For when Remus and Romulus established an asylum, so that whoever, guilty of any crime, had fled there should have his offense unavenged, they did not give precepts of repentance for the saving of the souls of the wretched[^37], but rather, for the price of impunity, armed an assembled band of the fearful against their own cities, whose laws they feared;

nor, when Romulus killed his brother, who had perpetrated no evil against him, did he think of the justice of avenging rather than of the rule of dominating. Did the gods then love such morals, by favoring the enemies of their own cities?

On the contrary, neither by deserting those cities did they oppress them, nor by going over to these others did they help them at all, because they do not have it in their power to give and to take away a kingdom; rather the one and true God does this by his hidden judgment,

not about to make at once happy those to whom he has given an earthly kingdom, nor at once wretched those from whom he has taken it away; but making men happy or wretched for another reason and from another source, he distributes temporal and earthly kingdoms to whom he wills and for as long as he wills, according to the predestined order of the ages, whether by permitting or by granting.

Why God allowed the Jews to be subjugated.

**13. 20.** Hence neither can they say this: "Why then did the God of the Hebrews, whom you call the supreme and true God, not only fail to subjugate the Romans to them, but not even help the Hebrews themselves, lest they be subjugated by the Romans?" For their manifest sins went before, on account of which the prophets had foretold so long beforehand that this would come upon them, and especially because in impious fury they killed Christ—in which sin they were blinded by the deserts of their other hidden sins. That his passion would be of profit to the nations was foretold by the same prophetic attestation. Nor did anything more clearly show that the kingdom and temple and priesthood and sacrifice of that nation, and that mystical anointing which in Greek is called *chrism*, whence the name "Christ" shines forth and on account of which that very nation called its kings christs (anointed)—that all this was assigned to nothing but the foretelling of Christ—than that, after the resurrection of the slain Christ began to be preached to the believing nations, all those things ceased, the Romans not knowing it, through whose victory, the Jews not knowing it, through whose subjugation, it came about that they ceased.

The God of the Hebrews, when they were conquered, showed that he himself was not conquered, by the overthrow of idols and the conversion of all nations to his worship.

**14. 21.** The few pagans who remain do not, indeed, observe a truly marvelous thing: that the God of the Hebrews, offended by the conquered and not received by the conquerors, is now proclaimed and worshiped by all nations.

For he is the God of Israel, of whom so long beforehand the prophet thus addressed the people of God: *and he who rescues you, the God of Israel, shall be called the God of the whole earth*[^38].

This came about through the name of Christ, who came to men from the seed of Israel himself, who was the grandson of Abraham, from whom the nation of the Hebrews began; for to Israel himself also it was said, *and in your seed all the tribes of the earth shall be blessed*[^39].

Hence the God of Israel is shown to be the one God, who made heaven and earth and who cares for human affairs justly and mercifully, so that justice does not shut out mercy, nor mercy hinder justice[^40]; and it is shown that he himself was not conquered in his Hebrew people, because he permitted his kingdom and priesthood to be assailed and destroyed by the Romans,

since indeed, through the Gospel of Christ, the true king and priest, which he prefigured as future by that kingdom and priesthood, the God of Israel himself now everywhere destroys the idols of the nations—which idols, precisely that they might not be destroyed, the Romans were unwilling to receive his sacred rites, as they had received the gods of the other nations they conquered.

Thus he also took away the kingdom and priesthood of the prophetic nation, because the one who was promised through them had already come; and he subjugated the Roman Empire, by which that nation was conquered, to his own name through Christ the king, and converted it, by the strength and devotion of the Christian faith, to the overthrowing of the idols for whose honor's sake his sacred rites had not been received.

**14. 22.** I think that Christ did not, by the magic arts, bring it about—before he was born among men—that future things concerning himself should be foretold through so many prophets and even through the kingdom and priesthood of a certain nation.

For the people of that now-destroyed kingdom, dispersed everywhere by the marvelous providence of God, although they have remained without any anointing of king or priest—in which chrism the name of Christ appears—

nevertheless still hold the remnants of certain of their observances; whereas Roman worship, even when conquered and subjugated, did not accept those sacred rites that belong to the cult of idols,

so that it might carry the prophetic books as a testimony to Christ, and thus, from the books of his enemies, the truth of the Christ who was foretold might be proved. Why, then, do the wretched, by praising Christ wrongly, still betray themselves?

If some magical things have been written under his name, then, since the teaching of Christ is vehemently hostile to these arts, let them rather understand from this how great that name is, by adding which even those who live contrary to his precepts try to lend honor to their nefarious arts.

For just as, by the various errors of men, many have founded various heresies as well against the truth under his name, so even the enemies of Christ feel that, for persuading men of what they put forward against the teaching of Christ, they have no weight of authority for themselves unless it has the name of Christ.

Pagans, compelled to praise Christ, are insulting toward his disciples.

**15. 23.** What of the fact that these vain praisers of Christ and crooked disparagers of the Christian religion do not dare to blaspheme Christ for this reason, that certain of their philosophers—as Porphyry the Sicilian reveals in his books—consulted their gods as to what they should answer concerning Christ, and those gods, by their own oracles, were compelled to praise Christ?

Nor is it any wonder, since in the Gospel too we read that the demons confessed him[^41]; and it is written in our prophets *that the gods of the nations are demons*[^42]. And for this reason these men, lest they strive against the responses of their own gods, hold back their blasphemies from Christ and pour them out upon his disciples;

but it seems to me that those gods of the nations, whom the pagan philosophers were able to consult, would also—if they were questioned about the disciples of Christ—be compelled to praise them too.

The apostles taught nothing about the overthrowing of idols different from what Christ or the prophets taught.

**16. 24.** Nevertheless, these men argue thus: that this overthrow of the temples, and condemnation of the sacrifices, and breaking of the images, does not come about through the teaching of Christ, but through that of his disciples, who, they contend, taught something other than what they learned from him—wishing in this way, while honoring and praising Christ, to tear down the Christian faith,

since indeed it is through the disciples of Christ that the deeds and sayings of Christ have been announced, on which the Christian religion stands—still hostile to these men, now very few in number, no longer attacking but still muttering. But if they are unwilling to believe that Christ taught such things, let them read the prophets, who not only commanded that the superstitions of idols be overthrown, but even foretold that this overthrow would come to pass in Christian times[^43]. If these prophets deceived, why is it fulfilled with such manifestation? If they spoke the truth, why is such great divinity resisted?

Against the Romans, who rejected the God of Israel alone.

**17. 25.** Nevertheless it must be asked of these men more carefully whom they think the God of Israel is. Why did they not receive him for worship, as they did the gods of the other nations which the Roman Empire subdued, especially since it is their opinion that all the gods are to be worshiped by the wise man?

Why, then, was this one rejected from the number of the rest? If he avails very much, why is he alone not worshiped by them? If he avails little or nothing, why, now that their images are crushed, is he alone worshiped by almost all nations?

They will never be able to be released from the bond of this question, who, while they worship gods greater and lesser—those whom they think to be gods—do not worship this God, who has prevailed over all whom they worship.

For if he is of great power, why was he judged to be rejected? If he is of little or no power, how could he do so much when rejected? If he is good, why is he alone separated from the rest of the good? If he is evil, why is he alone not overcome by so many good ones? If he is truthful, why are his precepts spurned? If he is a liar, why are his predictions fulfilled?

The God of the Hebrews was not received by the Romans, because he willed that he alone be worshiped.

**18. 26.** Finally, let them think about him what they will. Do not the Romans think that even evil gods are to be worshiped, who made shrines to Pallor and to Fever? Who counsel that demons are to be invited and demons to be appeased?

Whatever, then, they suppose about him, why did they judge that he alone was neither to be invoked nor to be propitiated by them? Who is this God, either so unknown that among so many gods he alone is still not found, or so well known that by so many men he alone is now worshiped?

Nothing, then, remains for them to give as a reason why they were unwilling to receive the sacred rites of this God, except that he willed that he alone be worshiped, and forbade the worship of those gods of the nations whom these men were already worshiping.

But this very thing is rather to be asked of them: whom, or of what kind, they think that God to be, who was unwilling that other gods be worshiped—those to whom these men made temples and images—and who could do so much that his will availed more for the overthrowing of their images than theirs availed for not receiving his sacred rites.

Surely the opinion of that philosopher of theirs is betrayed, whom they affirmed, even by an oracle, to have been the wisest of all men. For it is the opinion of Socrates that each god ought to be worshiped in the way that he himself has prescribed that he be worshiped.

Accordingly, a supreme necessity was laid upon these men of not worshiping the God of the Hebrews; for, if they wished to worship him in another way than he had said he was to be worshiped, they would surely not be worshiping him, but what they themselves had fashioned.

But if they wished to worship him in the way that he prescribed, they perceived that they must not worship the others whom he forbade to be worshiped.

And for this reason they spurned the worship of the one true God, lest they offend the many false ones, judging rather that the wrath of these would harm them than that the goodwill of that one would profit them.

That he is the true God.

**19. 27.** But granting that this was a vain necessity and a laughable timidity: now we ask what these men, to whom it is pleasing that all the gods are to be worshiped, think about this God. For if he is not to be worshiped, how are all worshiped, when he is not worshiped?

But if he is to be worshiped, then not all are to be worshiped, because, unless he alone is worshiped, he is not worshiped. Or will they perhaps say that he is not a god, while they call gods those who, as we believe, can do nothing except what they are permitted by his judgment—not so as to profit anyone, but so that they cannot even harm any except those whom he judges ought to be so harmed, who can do all things?

But, as they themselves are compelled to confess, they have certainly been able to do less than he. For if they are gods whose seers, consulted by men, gave responses near at hand to private matters—not to say deceived them—

how is he not God, whose seers not only gave fitting responses about the things that were consulted for the moment, but even, where they were not consulted, foretold so long beforehand those things concerning the whole human race and all nations which we now both read and see?

If they call him a god by whose inspiration the Sibyl sang the fates of the Romans, how is he not God who showed in advance that the Romans and all nations would believe in him as the one God through the Gospel of Christ, and would overthrow all the images of their fathers, just as he foretold?

Finally, if they call those gods who never dared, through their seers, to say anything against this God, how is he not God who, through his own seers, not only commanded that the images of these gods be overthrown, but even foretold that they would be overthrown among all nations by those who, having abandoned them, would be commanded to worship him as the one God and, being so commanded, would serve him[^44]?

Nothing is found foretold against the God of the Hebrews by the seers of the pagans.

**20. 28.** Or let them read, if they can, that any of the Sibyls, or any other of their seers, foretold that this would come to pass: that the God of the Hebrews, the God of Israel, would be worshiped by all nations; and that, because the worshipers of other gods had rightly spurned him before, there would also be writings of his prophets, set in an authority so lofty that, obeying them, even the Roman Empire would order the images to be destroyed; and that they had also given warning that such precepts should not be obeyed—let them read these things, if they can, from any books of their own seers.

For I forbear to say that the things which are read in their books bear testimony to our religion, that is, the Christian religion—things which they could hear from the holy angels and from our own prophets, just as even the demons were compelled to confess Christ present in the flesh[^45].

But I forbear these things, which, when we bring them forward, they contend were invented by our people. They themselves, altogether they themselves are to be pressed to bring forward something prophesied against the God of the Hebrews by the seers of their gods, just as we recite so many and such great things from the books of our prophets against their gods—both the commands we observe, and the predictions, and the deeds we point to as fulfilled[^46]. These things those who now remain, very few in number, prefer to grieve over as fulfilled than to acknowledge that God who was able to foretell what was to be fulfilled, since in their false gods, which are true demons, they seek nothing else as a great thing than to learn from their responses that something will come to pass for them.

This God alone is to be worshiped, who, while he forbids others to be worshiped, is not forbidden to be worshiped by others.

**21. 29.** Since these things are so, why do the wretched not rather understand that he is the true God, whom they see so separated from the fellowship of their gods that they, who profess that all the gods are to be worshiped, are compelled to confess him a God, while they are not allowed to worship him along with the rest?

Since, then, they cannot be worshiped together, why is not he chosen who forbids these others to be worshiped, with those others left aside who do not forbid him to be worshiped? Or, if they do forbid it, let it be read. For what ought rather to have been recited to their peoples in their temples, where nothing of the kind ever resounded? And surely the prohibition of so many against one ought to be better known and more powerful than that of one against so many.

For if the worship of this God is impious, the gods are useless who do not forbid men from impiety; but if his worship is pious, then, since in it it is enjoined that those others not be worshiped, the worship of those others is impious.

But if they so confidently forbid him to be worshiped that they fear more to be heard than they dare to forbid, by this very thing who would not be wise enough, who would not perceive, that he is rather to be chosen,

who so publicly forbids these gods to be worshiped, who ordered their images to be overthrown, foretold it, and overthrew them,

than these gods, whom we do not know to have commanded—that he should not be worshiped—whom we do not read to have foretold it, and whom we do not see to have availed anything?

I ask, let them answer: who is this God, who so pursues all the gods of the nations, who so betrays all their sacred rites, who so extinguishes them?

The opinion of the pagans about our God.

**22. 30.** Why should I question men who have become vain in their thinking, as to who this God is? Some say he is Saturn—I believe on account of the sanctifying of the Sabbath, because these men assigned that day to Saturn.

But Varro—than whom they find no one more learned among themselves—thought that the God of the Jews was Jupiter, holding that it made no difference by what name he was called, so long as the same reality was understood; I believe he was deterred by his supremacy.

For since the Romans are accustomed to worship nothing higher than Jupiter, as their Capitol clearly enough attests, and they judge him the king of all the gods, when Varro observed that the Jews worship the supreme God, he could suspect nothing other than Jupiter.

But whether they think the God of the Jews is Saturn or Jupiter, let them say when Saturn dared to forbid another god to be worshiped—not even Jupiter himself, who is reported to have expelled him from his kingdom, a son his father.

And if Jupiter, as the more powerful and the victor, pleased his worshipers, let them not worship Saturn, conquered and expelled. But Jupiter did not forbid even this Saturn to be worshiped, and the one whom he was able to conquer he nevertheless permitted to be a god.

What nonsense the pagans have talked about Jupiter and Saturn.

**23. 31.** "These," they say, "are fables, to be either interpreted by the wise or laughed at; but we worship Jupiter, of whom Maro says, *All things are full of Jupiter*[^47]"—that is, the spirit giving life to all things[^48]. With reason, then, did Varro also suppose that Jupiter was worshiped by the Jews, because he says through the prophet, *I fill heaven and earth*[^49]. But what of the fact that the same poet speaks of *aether*?

How do they take it? For thus he says: *then the almighty father Aether descended in fruitful showers into the lap of his joyful spouse*[^50]. For they say that aether is not a spirit, but a lofty body, by which the heaven is stretched out above the air. Or is it conceded to the poet to speak now according to the Platonists, so that God is not a body but a spirit, now according to the Stoics, so that God is a body?

What, then, do they worship in the Capitol? If a spirit, if in short the corporeal heaven itself, what is that shield of Jupiter doing there, which they call the Aegis? For the origin of this name is given thus: that a goat (capra) nursed Jupiter when he was hidden from his mother. Or do the poets lie about this too? Are the Capitols of the Romans, then, the works of the poets? But what does this not-poetic but plainly mimic inconsistency mean—to seek the gods in books according to the philosophers, and to adore them in temples according to the poets?

**23. 32.** But was that Euhemerus too a poet, who reveals so very plainly that Jupiter himself, and Saturn his father, and Pluto and Neptune his brothers were men, so that their worshipers ought rather to give thanks to the poets, because they invented many things not to dishonor them but rather to adorn them?

And yet Cicero records that this Euhemerus too was translated into the Latin tongue by the poet Ennius[^51]. Was Cicero himself a poet, who admonishes the one with whom he disputes in the *Tusculans*, as one privy to secrets, saying: *but if I should try to search out ancient things, and to dig out from them what the writers of Greece have handed down, those very gods who are held to be of the greater nations will be found to have set out from here to heaven. Ask whose tombs are shown in Greece; recall, since you have been initiated, what is handed down in the mysteries; then at last you will understand how widely this extends*[^52].

Here, certainly, he sufficiently confesses that the gods of these men were men, but he benevolently surmises that they reached heaven—although he did not hesitate to say publicly that even this honor of opinion was conferred upon them by men, when speaking of Romulus: *him who founded this city*, he says, *Romulus, we have raised to the immortal gods by goodwill and fame*[^53]. What wonder is it, then, if the more ancient men did this about Jupiter and Saturn and the rest, which the Romans did about Romulus, and which in still more recent times they wished to do even about Caesar?

To whom Virgil too added the adulation of his song, saying: *behold, the star of the Dionaean Caesar has come forth*[^54]. Let them therefore see whether perhaps historical truth shows the tombs of the false gods on earth, while poetic vanity does not fix their stars, but feigns them, in heaven.

For that star is not really Jupiter's, or that one Saturn's; but after their death, upon the stars founded from the beginning of the world, men imposed these names—men who wished those dead ones to be held as gods. Of these, what so great an evil did chastity deserve, or what so great a good did pleasure deserve, that among the stars which circle with the sun and moon Venus should have a star and Minerva should not?

**23. 33.** But suppose Cicero too, an Academic, was more uncertain than the poets, who dared to mention the tombs of the gods and to commit it to writing—although he did not presume this on his own opinion, but recorded it from the tradition of those very sacred rites. Does Varro too either feign it as a poet, or set it down doubtfully as an Academic, when he says that the sacred rites of such gods were composed from the life or death of each of them, by which they lived or died among men? Was that Leo, the Egyptian priest, a poet or an Academic, who, to Alexander the Macedonian, revealed an origin of these gods different indeed from the opinion of the Greeks, yet such that he declared them to have been men?

**23. 34.** But what is that to us? Let them say that they do not worship Jupiter as a dead man, nor have dedicated the Capitol to a dead man, but to the spirit that gives life to all things, by which the world is filled, and let them interpret his shield, made of goatskin in honor of his nurse, as they will.

What do they say of Saturn? Which Saturn do they worship? Is he not the one who first came from Olympus: *fleeing the arms of Jupiter, an exile with his kingdom taken away, who gathered together a race untaught and scattered upon the high mountains, and gave them laws, and chose that it be called Latium, since in these shores he had lain hidden in safety*[^55]?

Does not his very image, which is fashioned with the head covered, indicate, as it were, one in hiding? Does not he himself show agriculture to the Italians, which he points to by the sickle? "No," they say; "for see to it, if he was that man and a certain king of whom these things are told; we, however, interpret Saturn as universal time, which his Greek name too shows; for he is called by a word which, with an aspiration added, is also the name of time, whence in Latin too he is called Saturn, as if he were sated (saturetur) with years." What is now to be done with these men, I do not know, who, trying to interpret the names and images of their gods for the better, confess that their greater god and the father of the rest is "time." For what else do they indicate than that all their gods are temporal, whose father they make time itself?

**23. 35.** At this their more recent philosophers, the Platonists, who lived already in Christian times, blushed, and tried to interpret Saturn otherwise, saying that he was named Κρόνον as it were from the satiety of intellect, in that in Greek "satiety" is κόρος and "intellect" or "mind" is νοῦς. To this the Latin name too seems to lend support, as though composed of a first Latin part and a later Greek part, so that he should be called Saturn, as though satur νοῦς, "sated intellect."

They saw how absurd it would be if Jupiter, whom they either thought or wished to be thought an eternal god, were held to be the son of time. But according to this new interpretation—which, if their ancients had held it, it would be a wonder that it escaped Cicero and Varro—they call Jupiter the son of Saturn as a spirit flowing forth from that supreme mind, which they will have to be, as it were, the soul of this world, filling all heavenly and earthly bodies. Whence comes that line of Maro which I mentioned a little before: *All things are full of Jupiter*[^56].

Would not these men, if they could, change the superstition of men just as they change the very interpretation, and set up either no images at all, or Capitols to Saturn rather than to Jupiter? For they argue that no rational soul becomes wise except by participation in that supreme and unchangeable wisdom—not only the soul of any man, but even that of the world itself, which they call Jupiter; whereas we not only grant, but most of all proclaim, that there is a certain supreme wisdom of God, by participation in which whatever soul becomes truly wise becomes wise[^57]. But whether this whole corporeal mass which is called the world has a certain soul, or as it were its own soul—that is, a rational life by which it is governed as each animal is—is a great and hidden question, and this opinion ought neither to be affirmed unless it is found to be true, nor refuted unless it is found to be false. But what is this to a man, even if it always lies hidden from him, since indeed no soul becomes wise or blessed from any other soul whatsoever, but from that one supreme and unchangeable wisdom of God alone?

**23. 36.** The Romans, however, who founded the Capitol not to Saturn but to Jupiter, or the other nations which thought that Jupiter above all the other gods was to be worshiped, did not hold what these men held. For these men, according to this new opinion of theirs, would, if they had had any power in these matters, dedicate the highest citadels rather to Saturn; and they would above all destroy the astrologers and casters of nativities, who set Saturn—whom these men would call the maker of the wise—among the other stars as a maleficent god. This opinion has so prevailed against him in human minds that they are unwilling even to name him, calling him "the old man" rather than Saturn, with so timid a superstition that the Carthaginians have almost changed the name of one of their districts, calling it "the district of the old man" more frequently than "the district of Saturn."

Not all worship God who reject the God of Israel; nor do they worship him who worship others.

**24. 37.** It is known, then, what the worshipers of images are convicted of worshiping, and what they try to gloss over. But of these recent interpreters of Saturn too it must be asked what they think about the God of the Hebrews. For they too were pleased to worship all the gods along with the nations, since it shamed them, proud as they were, to be humbled under Christ for the remission of sins.

What, then, do they think about the God of Israel? If they do not worship him, they do not worship all the gods; but if they do worship him, they do not worship him as he commanded himself to be worshiped, because they also worship others whom he forbade to be worshiped. For through those seers he forbade these things, through whom he foretold that these very things would come to pass which their images now suffer from the Christians. For whether angels were sent to those prophets, to show them figuratively, through the fitting appearance of sensible things, that the creator of all is the one true God to whom all things are subject, and to indicate how he commanded himself to be worshiped; or whether the minds of some among them were so exalted by the Holy Spirit that they saw those things with that vision by which the angels themselves see—it is nonetheless established that they served that God who forbade other gods to be worshiped, and that they served him in the faith of piety, in the kingdom and priesthood of their own commonwealth, and in sacred rites signifying Christ who was to come as king and priest.

The false gods do not forbid others to be worshiped along with them. The God of Israel is convicted of being the true God by his works, both foretold and fulfilled.

**25. 38.** But further, among the gods of the nations—whom, while they wish to worship them, these men are unwilling to worship him who cannot be worshiped along with these—let them say what the reason is that none of them is found who forbids another to be worshiped, since they place them in various offices and functions and wish them to be presidents over the matters that pertain properly to each.

For if Jupiter does not forbid Saturn to be worshiped, because Saturn is not that man who expelled that human father from the kingdom,

but is either the body of heaven or the spirit filling heaven and earth, and therefore cannot forbid the supernal mind, from which he is said to have emanated, to be worshiped;

if for the same reason neither does Saturn forbid Jupiter to be worshiped, because he was not overcome by him in rebellion, as Saturn was by some Jupiter or other, fleeing whose arms he came into Italy, but rather the first mind favors the soul begotten of itself,

at least Vulcan would forbid Mars, the adulterer of his wife, to be worshiped; Hercules would forbid Juno, his persecutor. What is this so foul an agreement among them, that not even the chaste virgin Diana forbids the worship—I will not say of Venus, but of Priapus?

For if one man should wish to be both a hunter and a farmer, he will be the servant of both—though Diana blushes even to have her temple built near to Priapus's. But let them interpret Diana as whatever virtue they will, and let them interpret Priapus too as the god of fecundity—so that, indeed, in the breeding of females it would shame Juno to have such a helper—let them say what they please.

Let them interpret what they understand, so long as the God of Israel confounds all their arguments. For he, since he forbade all those gods to be worshiped, and was forbidden to be worshiped by none of them, and commanded, foretold, and accomplished the overthrow of their images and sacred rites, sufficiently shows that they are false and deceitful, and that he is the true and truthful God.

**25. 39.** But these now few worshipers of the many false gods—who would not marvel that they are unwilling to obey him concerning whom, when they are asked who that God is, they answer by supposing whatever they please, yet do not dare to deny that he is God, because, if they deny it, they are most easily convicted by his works, both foretold and fulfilled? For I do not speak of those things which they think they are free not to believe—that he in the beginning made heaven and earth and all things that are in them[^58]—nor of those very ancient things: that he translated Enoch[^59], that he destroyed the impious by the flood, that he delivered the just Noah and his household from it by means of the wood[^60]. I begin from Abraham with his deeds among men.

For to him was made, through an angelic oracle, a manifest promise, which we now see being fulfilled. For to him it was said, *In your seed shall all the nations be blessed*[^61]; from whose seed came the people of Israel, whence came the Virgin Mary, who bore Christ, in whom all the nations are blessed—let them now dare to deny it, if they can.

This promise was made also to Isaac the son of Abraham, and to Jacob the grandson of Abraham[^62], who was also called Israel, from whom the whole people was both propagated and named, so that the God of this people was called the God of Israel—not that he is not himself the God of all nations, whether of those who do not know him or of those who now know him, but because in this people he willed the power of his promises to appear more manifestly. For that people, first multiplied in Egypt and freed from that slavery through Moses with many signs and portents, with very many nations subdued, received also the land of promise, in which it reigned through its kings sprung from the tribe of Judah[^63]. This Judah was one of the twelve sons of Israel the grandson of Abraham, and thence the Jews, taking their name, did many things with God himself helping them, and suffered many things for their sins with him himself scourging them, until there should come the seed to whom the promise was made[^64], in whom all the nations would be blessed and would of their own accord break in pieces the images of their fathers[^65].

Idolatry overthrown through the name of Christ and the faith of Christians, in accordance with the prophecies.

**26. 40.** For it was not in Christian times, but so long beforehand, that what is fulfilled through Christians was foretold. The very Jews who have remained enemies of the name of Christ—concerning whose future faithlessness too those prophetic writings are not silent—they themselves have and read the prophet saying: *O Lord my God and my refuge in the day of evils, to you the nations shall come from the ends of the earth and shall say, 'Truly our fathers worshiped lies, the images, and there is no profit in them*[^66].' Behold, now it comes to pass; behold, now the nations come from the ends of the earth to Christ, saying these things and breaking the images. For this too is a great thing that God has granted to his Church spread everywhere: that the Jewish nation, deservedly subdued and dispersed through the lands, should carry everywhere the books of our prophets, lest these be thought composed by us, and, hostile to our faith, should become a witness of our truth. How, then, did the disciples of Christ teach what they did not learn from Christ—as the foolish boast in their folly—so that the superstition of the gentile gods and images should be destroyed? Can it even be said that the disciples of Christ feigned those prophecies which are now read in the books of the enemies of Christ?

**26. 41.** For who overthrew these things but the God of Israel? For to that very people it was said, through the divine voices made to Moses: *Hear, O Israel, the Lord your God is one God*[^67]. *You shall not make for yourself an idol, nor the likeness of anything, neither of what is in heaven above nor of what is on the earth below*[^68]; and that he might even overthrow these things, where he has received the power, it is thus enjoined upon him: *You shall not adore their gods, nor shall you serve them; you shall not do according to their works, but tearing down you shall tear down and breaking in pieces you shall break in pieces their images*[^69]. But who would say that Christ and Christians do not pertain to Israel, since Israel was the grandson of Abraham, to whom first, and then to Isaac his son, and then to Israel his grandson himself, it was said—what I have already mentioned—*In your seed shall all the nations be blessed*[^70]? Which we now see being done in Christ, since from there sprang that virgin of whom the prophet of the people of Israel and of the God of Israel sang, saying: *Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel*[^71]. And Emmanuel is interpreted, *God with us*[^72].

The God of Israel, then, who forbade other gods to be worshiped, who forbade idols to be fashioned, who commanded that they be overthrown, who through the prophet foretold that the nations from the ends of the earth would say, *Truly our fathers worshiped lies, the images, in which there is no profit*[^73]—he himself, through the name of Christ and the faith of Christians, commanded, promised, and brought about the overthrow of all these superstitions. In vain, then, do the wretched, because they have been forbidden to blaspheme Christ even by their own gods, that is, by the demons fearing the name of Christ, wish to make alien to him that teaching by which Christians dispute against the idols and uproot all those false religions, where they have been able.

He presses the remnants of the idolaters, that they may at last serve the true God who everywhere overthrows the idols.

**27. 42.** Let them answer concerning the God of Israel—whom not only the books of the Christians but also those of the Jews testify to teach and command these things; concerning him let them consult their own gods, who forbade Christ to be blasphemed; concerning the God of Israel, if they hear, let them give some insulting reply. But whom shall they consult? Or where shall they now consult? Let them read the books of their own. If they think the God of Israel is Jupiter, as Varro wrote (let me speak for the moment according to their opinion), why then do they not believe Jupiter that the idols are to be destroyed? If they think he is Saturn, why do they not worship him? Or why do they not worship him in the way that he prescribed himself to be worshiped, through those seers through whom he fulfilled what he foretold? Why do they not believe him that the images are to be overthrown and that other gods are not to be worshiped?

If he is neither Jupiter nor Saturn—because, if he were one of them, he would not say such great things against the sacred rites of their Jupiter and Saturn—who, then, is he, who alone among them is not worshiped on account of the other gods, and who, with the other gods overthrown, so evidently brings it about that he alone is worshiped, with every proud height humbled that had raised itself against Christ, persecuting and killing the Christians for the sake of the idols? Now, certainly, they seek where they may hide themselves when they wish to sacrifice, or where they may shut up their own gods, lest they be found by the Christians and broken. Whence comes this, except from fear of laws and kings, through whom the God of Israel exerts his power over those now made subject to the name of Christ, just as he promised long before, saying through the prophet: *and all the kings of the earth shall adore him, all the nations shall serve him*[^74]?

The foretold rejection of idols.

**28. 43.** Indeed, that is already being fulfilled which is sung again and again through the prophet: that he would forsake his impious people—not the whole of it, indeed, because many of the Israelites believed in Christ, for thence were even his apostles—and that he would humble every proud and injurious one, so that he himself alone might be exalted, that is, that he alone might be made manifest to men as high and powerful, until the idols should be cast away by the believers and hidden by the unbelievers, when, for fear of him, the earth is broken in pieces, that is, earthly men are broken by fear, fearing the law, whether of him himself or of those who, believing in him and reigning over the nations, would forbid such sacrileges.

**28. 44.** For these things, which—that they might be more easily understood—I have set forth briefly beforehand, the prophet states thus: *And now, you house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord. For he has forsaken his people, the house of Israel, because their land was filled, as from the beginning, with auguries, as that of foreigners, and many foreign children were born to them. For their land was filled with silver and gold, and there was no number of their treasures. And the land was filled with horses, and there was no number of their chariots. And the land was filled with the abominations of the works of their own hands, and they adored the things that their fingers had made. And man bowed himself down and the man humbled himself, and I will not forgive them. And now enter into the rocks and hide yourselves in the earth from the face of the fear of the Lord and from the majesty of his power, when he shall rise up to break the earth in pieces. For the eyes of the Lord are lofty, but man is lowly. And the loftiness of men shall be humbled, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day. For the day of the Lord of Sabaoth is upon every injurious and proud one and upon everyone lofty and elated*, *who shall be humbled; and upon every cedar of Lebanon, of the lofty and the elated, and upon every tree of Lebanon, of Bashan, and upon every mountain and upon every high hill, and upon every ship of the sea and upon every spectacle of beautiful ships. And the contumely of men shall be humbled and shall fall, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day*. *And they shall hide all things fashioned by hands in caves and in the clefts of the rocks and in the caverns of the earth, from the face of the fear of the Lord and from the majesty of his power, when he shall rise up to break the earth in pieces. For in that day man shall cast away the golden and silver abominations, which they made that they might adore vain and noxious things, that they may enter into the holes of the solid rock and into the clefts of the rocks, from the face of the fear of the Lord and from the majesty of his power, when he shall rise up to shatter the earth*[^75].

Why should the pagans not worship the God of Israel, if they suppose him to be even the one set over the elements?

**29. 45.** What do they say about this *God of Sabaoth*, which is interpreted "God of powers," or "of armies," because the powers and armies of the angels serve him? What do they say about this God of Israel, that he is the God of that people from whom came the seed in whom all the nations would be blessed[^76]? Why do they alone not worship him, who contend that all the gods are to be worshiped? Why do they not believe him, who has both shown and overthrown the other false gods? I heard one of them say that he had read in some philosopher or other, that from the things which the Jews did in their sacred rites he had understood which God they worshiped: *the one set over*, he said, *those elements out of which this visible and corporeal world is constructed*[^77]—whereas in the Holy Scriptures of his prophets it is openly shown that the God commanded to be worshiped by the people of Israel is the one who made heaven and earth[^78] and from whom is all true wisdom[^79]. But what need is there to dispute longer about this, when for what I am doing it suffices that, by whatever presumption they suppose about that God, they cannot deny that he is God? For if he is set over the elements out of which this world consists, why is not he alone rather worshiped than Neptune, who is set over the sea alone; or finally than Silvanus, who is set over fields and woods alone; than the Sun, who is set over the day alone, or even over all the heat of heaven; than the Moon, who shines forth with power over the night alone, or even over moisture; than Juno, who is reported to hold the air alone? For surely those who are set over parts, whoever they are, must of necessity be under him who bears the office over all the elements and over this whole mass. But he forbids them all to be worshiped. Why, then, do these men, against the command of the greater of them, not only wish to worship them, but, for the sake of them, refuse to worship him? They still do not find what they may pronounce, constantly and clearly, about this God of Israel, nor will they ever find it, until they find him to be the one true God, by whom all things were created.

The God of Israel, the prophecies being fulfilled, has now become known everywhere.

**30. 46.** Accordingly, a certain Lucan, a great declaimer of theirs in verse—himself too, I believe, long seeking, whether through his own thoughts or through the books of his own people, who the God of the Jews might be, and, because he did not seek piously, not finding him—nevertheless preferred to call the God whom he did not find an uncertain God rather than no God, since he felt such great evidences of him. For he says: *and Judea, given over to the sacred rites of an uncertain God*[^80]. And this God, the holy and true God of Israel, had not yet done, through the name of Christ, such great things among all the nations as have followed up to the present day after Lucan's times. But now, who is so hard as not to be bent? Who is so torpid as not to be set afire, when that is fulfilled which is written, that *there is no one who hides himself from his heat*, now that the things foretold so long before in that same *Psalm*, from which I have quoted that little verse, are made manifest in the clearest light? For by the name of *the heavens* the apostles of Christ were signified, because God presided in them, that they might announce the Gospel. Already, then, the heavens have declared the glory of God, and the firmament has announced the works of his hands. Day to day has uttered speech, and night to night has announced knowledge. Already there are no languages nor tongues whose voices are not heard. Already their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. Already in the sun, that is, in manifestation, he has set his tabernacle, which is his Church, because, that he might do this, he himself, as it follows there, came forth as a bridegroom from his chamber, that is, the Word joined to human flesh came forth from the virginal womb; already he has exulted as a giant and run his course; already his going forth was from the height of heaven, and his return even to the height of heaven[^81]. And therefore most rightly follows the verse which I mentioned a little before: *and there is no one who hides himself from his heat*[^82]. And still these frail ones, chattering their little contradictions, choose to be turned by this fire into ashes like stubble[^83] rather than to be purified of dross like gold[^84], when both the deceitful monuments of the false gods have already been frustrated, and the truthful promises of that uncertain God have already been made certain!

The prophecy about Christ fulfilled.

**31. 47.** Let the evil praisers of Christ, then, who are unwilling to be Christians, cease to say that Christ did not teach that their gods should be forsaken and their images broken in pieces[^85]. For the God of Israel, of whom it was foretold that he would be called the God of the whole earth[^86]—and the God of the whole earth he is now called—foretold through the prophets that this would come to pass, and fulfilled it through Christ at the fitting time. For surely, if the God of Israel is now called the God of the whole earth, it is necessary that what he commanded come to pass, since the one who commanded it has become known. But because it was through Christ and in Christ that he became known, so that his Church would be spread throughout the world and through it the God of Israel would be called the God of the whole earth—let those who wish read, a little above, in the same prophet; indeed, let it be recalled by me too. For it is not so long that it ought to be passed over. For many things are said about the presence and humility and passion of Christ, and about his body, of which he is the head, that is, his Church, when it is called as it were barren, that did not bear.

For through many years the Church, which was to be among all nations in her children, that is, in her saints, did not appear, since Christ had not yet been announced by the evangelists to those to whom he had not been announced by the prophets; and it is said that the children of the forsaken one would be more than those of her who has a husband—since by the name of *husband* was signified the Law or the king, which the people of Israel first received, while the nations had not received the Law at the time when the prophet spoke, nor had the king of the Christians yet appeared to the nations, from which nations nevertheless there came forth a far more fruitful and numerous multitude of the saints[^87]. So, then, says Isaiah, beginning from the humility of Christ and afterward turning himself to address the Church, up to that verse which we mentioned, where he says: *and he who rescued you, the very God of Israel, shall be called the God of the whole earth*[^88].

*Behold*, he says, *my servant shall be in understanding, and he shall be exalted and greatly honored. As many shall marvel over you, so too your appearance shall be seen as without honor by all, and your honor by men. For so shall many nations marvel over him, and kings shall keep their mouth shut, because they to whom it was not announced concerning him shall see, and they who have not heard shall understand*[^89]. *Lord, who has believed our report? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed? We announced him before him as a child, as a root in a thirsty land; there is no appearance in him nor honor. And we saw him, and he had no appearance nor comeliness; but his countenance was abject and the set of his form deformed beyond all men. A man in stripes and one who knows how to bear infirmities, on account of which his face was turned away; he was wronged and not greatly esteemed. He bears our infirmities and is in sorrows for us. And we esteemed him to be in sorrows and in stripes and in punishment. But he himself was wounded for our sins and made weak for our iniquities. The chastisement of our peace was upon him; by his bruise we have been healed. We all like sheep have gone astray, and the Lord handed him over for our sins. And he himself, because he was ill-treated, did not open his mouth; like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before the one who shears it he was without voice, so he did not open his mouth*. *In his humility his judgment was taken away. His generation, who shall declare it? For his life shall be taken from the earth. From the iniquities of my people he was led to death. I will therefore give the wicked for his burial and the rich for his death, because he did no iniquity nor deceit in his mouth. The Lord wills to purify him of the stripe. If you give your soul for your sins, you shall see a seed of the longest life. And the Lord wills to take away his soul from sorrows, to show him light and to form it through understanding, to justify the just one who serves many well, and he himself shall bear their sins. Therefore he himself shall inherit many and shall divide the spoils of the strong, because his soul was handed over to death and he was reckoned among the wicked, and he himself bore the sins of many and was handed over for their iniquities*[^90]. *Rejoice, O barren one, who do not bear; break forth and cry, you who do not travail, because the children of the forsaken one are more than of her who has a husband. For the Lord said, 'Enlarge the place of your tent and fix fast your courts; there is no reason to spare. Stretch out the cords further and set up strong stakes. Again and again extend to the right and to the left. For your seed shall inherit the nations, and you shall inhabit the cities that were forsaken. There is no reason for you to fear, for you shall prevail, nor be ashamed that you were detestable. For you shall forget your confusion forever, you shall not remember the ignominy of your widowhood, because I am the Lord who makes you, the Lord is his name; and he who rescued you, the very God of Israel, shall be called the God of the whole earth*[^91].'

**31. 48.** What can be said against this evidence and expression of things both foretold and fulfilled? If they think his disciples lied about the divinity of Christ, will they doubt about the passion of Christ? They are not wont to believe that he rose again; but they even gladly believe that he suffered all those human things from men, because they wish him to be believed a man only. He, then, who like a sheep was led to the slaughter[^92], who was reckoned among the wicked, who was wounded for our sins, by whose bruise we have been healed[^93], whose face was wronged and not greatly esteemed, struck with palms and smeared with spittle, whose form on the cross was deformed; he who was led to death from the iniquities of the people of Israel; he who had no appearance nor comeliness, when he was struck with blows, when he was crowned with thorns, when, hanging, he was mocked; he who, as a lamb before the one who shears it has no voice[^94], so did not open his mouth, when it was said to him by those insulting, *Prophesy to us, Christ*[^95]. Indeed, he is now exalted, now greatly honored. Indeed, many nations now marvel over him[^96]. Now the kings have kept their mouth shut, by which they promulgated the most savage laws against the Christians. Indeed, they to whom it was not announced about him now see, and they who have not heard now understand[^97]. For the nations, to whom the prophets did not announce, themselves see, rather, how true the things announced through the prophets are. And they who did not hear Isaiah himself speaking now understand, in his writings, of whom he spoke these things. For even in the very nation of the Jews, who believed the report of the prophets, or to whom was the arm of the Lord, which is Christ himself, who was announced through them, revealed, when with their own hands they committed those crimes against Christ which the prophets they had had foretold would come to pass[^98]? Indeed, he now possesses many by inheritance and divides the spoils of the strong[^99], since the things which the devil and the demons possessed, those expelled and betrayed, he distributes for the buildings of his churches and whatever necessary uses.

The teaching of the apostles against idolatry is vindicated from the prophecies.

**32. 49.** What, then, do the perverse praisers of Christ and disparagers of Christians say to these things? Did Christ, that these things should be foretold through the prophets so long beforehand, do this by the magic arts, or did his disciples feign them? Did the disciples of Christ feign that the Church—spread among the nations, once barren, now rejoicing in more children than that synagogue which had received the Law or the king as a husband—should so enlarge the place of her tent, occupying all nations and tongues, that further than the bounds of the Roman Empire are extended, even unto the Persians and the Indians and other barbarous nations, she should stretch out her cords; that to the right, through true Christians, and to the left, through feigned Christians, his name should be extended among so many peoples; that her seed should inherit the nations; that she should now inhabit the cities which had been forsaken by the true worship of God and by true religion; that she should not fear the threats and furies of men, when she was clothed in the blood of the martyrs as with a purple honor; that she should prevail over her so many and so vehement and powerful persecutors; that she should not be ashamed that she was detestable, when it was a great crime to become or to be a Christian; that she should forget her confusion forever, because where sin had abounded, grace did much more abound[^100]; that she should not be mindful of the ignominy of her widowhood, because, a little while forsaken and exposed to reproaches, she flourishes again with so eminent a glory? Did the disciples of Christ, finally, feign that the Lord, who made her and rescued her from the dominion of the devil and the demons, the very God of Israel, should now be called the God of the whole earth—which the prophets, whose books are now held in the hands of the enemies of Christ, foretold so long before Christ became the Son of man[^101]?

**32. 50.** From this, then, let them understand—what is left obscure or doubtful not even to the dullest and most obtuse—from this, I say, let the perverse praisers of Christ and execrators of the Christian religion understand that even the disciples of Christ learned and taught against their gods those things which the teaching of Christ contains; because the God of Israel, who is found to have commanded, in the books of the prophets, that all these things which these men wish to worship be abominated and overthrown, is himself now called, through Christ and the Church of Christ, the God of the whole earth, as he promised so long beforehand[^102]. For if, by a marvelous madness, they surmise that Christ worshiped their gods, and that through these he was able to do such great things, did the God of Israel too worship their gods—he who fulfilled what he promised concerning himself, to be worshiped among all nations, and concerning those gods, to be abominated and overthrown, through Christ[^103]? Where are their gods? Where are the prophecies of the fanatics and the divinations of the soothsayers? Where are the auguries or auspices or haruspices or oracles of the demons? Why is there not brought forward, from such ancient books, something admonished and foretold against the Christian faith and against the truth of our prophets, now so manifest and expressed among all the nations? "We offended our gods," they say, "and they forsook us; therefore the Christians prevailed against us, and the felicity of human affairs, wearied and diminished, slips away." Let them at least read, from the books of their own seers, that this would come to pass for them through the Christians; but let them read those passages where, if not Christ—since they wish him to have been a worshiper of their gods—at least the God of Israel was reproved and detested, who is established as the overthrower of theirs. But they will never bring this forward, except what they may perhaps just now have fabricated. And when they have brought it forward, it will be clear, by that very fact—that so great a thing, so unknown, is brought forward, which surely ought, before what was foretold had happened, to have been recited in the temples of the gods of all nations, so that those who now wish to be Christians might be forearmed and forewarned.

Against those who complain that the felicity of human affairs has been diminished through Christian times.

**33. 51.** Next, as for their complaint about the diminishing of the felicity of human affairs through Christian times—if they should read the books of their own philosophers reproving those things which are now withdrawn from them even as they refuse and murmur, then indeed they would find great praise for Christian times. For what of felicity is diminished for them, except what they were most basely and luxuriously abusing, to the great injury of the Creator? Unless perhaps the times are evil for this reason, that through almost all the cities the theaters are falling, the dens of foulness and the public professions of disgraceful deeds; and there are falling too the marketplaces or the walls in which the demons were worshiped. For why are they falling, except from the want of those things by whose wanton and sacrilegious use they were built? Did not their own Cicero, when he was praising a certain actor Roscius, say that he was so skilled that he alone was worthy to be allowed to go on the stage; and so good a man that he alone was worthy not to be allowed to go onto it[^104]? What else does he most openly show but that that stage is so foul that a man ought to be there so much the less, the more he is a good man? And yet their gods were appeased by such disgrace as he judged ought to be kept away from good men. There is also from this same Cicero an open confession, where he says that the goddess-mother Flora must be appeased by him through the celebration of games[^105]. In which games such great foulness was wont to be exhibited that, in comparison with them, the rest are decent—from the very performing of which, nevertheless, good men are forbidden. What is this mother Flora, what sort of goddess is she, whom the more celebrated foulness, given loose rein, wins over and propitiates? How much more decently did Roscius go onto the stage than Cicero worshiped such a goddess?

If, by the diminished abundance of the things that are lavished on celebrating these spectacles, the gods of the nations are offended, it appears what sort they are who are delighted by such things. But if they themselves, being angry, diminish these things, they are more useful in their anger than in their being appeased. Wherefore let them either accuse their own philosophers, who reproved such things in luxurious men, or break their own gods, who demanded such things of their worshipers—if indeed they now find either gods to break or gods to hide; but, in Christian times, let them cease to impute, with blasphemy, the failing of favorable things, by which they were flowing away into foul and noxious pursuits, lest they rather remind us of the source from which the power of Christ is the more to be praised.

Epilogue of the foregoing.

**34. 52.** I would say many things on this matter, did not the necessity of the work I have undertaken compel me now to conclude this book and to return to the purpose I set forth. For when I undertook to resolve, as I could, the Gospel questions where the four evangelists seem to some not to agree among themselves, with the intentions of each set forth, it first occurred to me that I must discuss what some are wont to ask, namely, why we bring forward no writings of Christ himself. For thus they wish both that he be believed to have written something else or other which they love, and to have thought nothing against their gods but rather to have worshiped them by a magic rite, and that his disciples not only lied about him in calling him God, through whom all things were made[^106]—when he was nothing but a man, though of the most excellent wisdom—but also did not teach, about their gods, what they had learned from him. Whence it came about that we pressed them rather concerning the God of Israel, who is worshiped, through the Church of Christians, by all the nations, and now everywhere overthrows their sacrilegious vanities, just as he foretold so long beforehand through his prophets, and, through the name of Christ in whom he had promised that all the nations would be blessed[^107], he fulfilled what he foretold.

From this they ought to understand that Christ could neither have known nor taught anything else about their gods than what the God of Israel commanded and foretold through the prophets—through whom he promised and sent Christ himself, in whose name, according to the promise which he made to the fathers that all the nations would be blessed, it came about that the God of Israel himself was called the God of the whole earth[^108]—and that his disciples did not deviate from the teaching of their master, when they forbade the gods of the nations to be worshiped, lest we either supplicate to senseless images, or have fellowship with the demons, or serve, by the obedience of religion, the creation rather than the Creator[^109].

The mystery of the Mediator is proclaimed to the ancients through prophecy, to us through the Gospel.

**35. 53.** Wherefore, since he is himself the Wisdom of God, through whom all things were created, and since no rational minds, whether of angels or of men, become wise except by participation in him—to whom we cleave through the Holy Spirit, through whom love is poured out in our hearts[^110], which Trinity is one God—it was provided by divine providence for mortals, whose temporal life was held occupied with things rising and setting, that this same Wisdom of God, with a man assumed into the unity of his person, in whom he might be born, live, die, and rise again in time, by saying and doing, by suffering and enduring things suited to our salvation, should become both, below, an example to men of returning, and, above, an example to angels of abiding. For unless something arose in time also in the nature of the rational soul—that is, began to be what it was not—it would never come from the worst and foolish life to the wise and best. And for this reason, since the truth of those who contemplate fully enjoys eternal things, while the faith of those who believe is owed to things that have arisen, man is purified through faith in temporal things, that he may perceive the truth of eternal things. For one of them too, a most noble philosopher, Plato, in that book which they call the *Timaeus*, says thus: *as much as eternity avails toward that which has arisen, so much does truth avail toward faith*[^111]. Those two are above, eternity and truth; these two are below, that which has arisen and faith. That we may therefore be called back from the lowest to the highest, and that which has arisen may receive eternity, we must come through faith to truth. And because all things that proceed to their contrary are brought back through some middle thing, temporal iniquity was alienating us from eternal justice; there was need, therefore, of a middle temporal justice, which middle should be temporal from the lowest, just from the highest, and thus, neither tearing itself away from the highest nor tempering itself to the lowest, should render the lowest to the highest. Therefore Christ was called the Mediator of God and men, between the immortal God and mortal man, God and man, reconciling man to God[^112], remaining what he was, made what he was not. He is for us faith in things that have arisen, who is truth in things eternal.

**35. 54.** This great and unutterable mystery, this kingdom and priesthood, was revealed to the ancients through prophecy; it is proclaimed to their descendants through the Gospel. For it was fitting that, at some time, there should be rendered among all the nations what was long promised through one nation. Accordingly, he who sent the prophets ahead before his descent, himself also sent the apostles after his ascension. And to all his disciples, through the man whom he assumed, he is the head, as it were of the members of his body. And so, when they wrote the things which he showed and said, it must by no means be said that he himself did not write them; since indeed his members did that which they came to know with the head dictating. For whatever he willed us to read concerning his own deeds and sayings, this he commanded them to write, as if with his own hands. Whoever understands this fellowship of unity and this ministry of the members, harmonious in their diverse offices under one head, will receive what he reads in the Gospel from the disciples of Christ narrating it no otherwise than if he had beheld the very hand of the Lord, which he bore in his own body, writing it. Wherefore let us now rather see of what kind are those things which they think the evangelists wrote contrary to one another (which can seem so to those who understand too little), so that, these questions resolved, it may from this too appear that the members of that head, not only by thinking the same thing, but also by writing things that agree, preserved, in the unity of the body itself, a genuine concord.
