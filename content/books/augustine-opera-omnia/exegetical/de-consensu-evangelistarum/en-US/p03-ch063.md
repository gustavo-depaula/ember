# Where he privately foretold his passion to the twelve disciples, and the mother of the sons of Zebedee with her sons asked that one of them might sit at his right hand and the other at his left, how Matthew is not opposed to the other two.

**64.** 124. Matthew continues and says: *and going up to Jerusalem, Jesus took the twelve disciples aside privately and said to them: 'Behold, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of man will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes, and they will condemn him to death, and will hand him over to the gentiles to be mocked and scourged and crucified, and on the third day he will rise.' Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee came to him with her sons, worshiping him and asking something of him*[^1], and so on down to where he says: *as the Son of man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many*[^2].

Mark too keeps this order with him, reporting that the sons of Zebedee said what Matthew expressed as having been said not by themselves but through their mother, since she had brought their wish to the Lord. Hence Mark briefly intimated that it was rather they than she who said what was said[^3]. Finally, the Lord too, both according to Matthew and according to Mark, answered them rather than their mother. Luke, however, after he has recorded from the same order what the Lord foretold to the twelve disciples about his passion and resurrection[^4], omits the things which these record, after which, with intervening matter, they met him at Jericho. But what Matthew and Mark said about the rulers of the gentiles, who lord it over their subjects, that it would not be so among them, but that he who would be greater would be the servant even of the others, Luke too says something of this kind, but not in that place; and the order itself indicates that the same meaning was again said by the Lord[^5].

[^1]: Mt 20:17–20.
[^2]: Mt 20:28.
[^3]: See Mk 10:32–45.
[^4]: See Lk 18:31–34.
[^5]: See Mt 20:25; Mk 10:42; Lk 22:24–27.
