# On the colt of the donkey, how Matthew agrees with the rest, who mention only the colt.

**66.** 127. Matthew continues and says: *and when he had drawn near to Jerusalem and had come to Bethphage at the Mount of Olives, then Jesus sent two disciples, saying to them: 'Go into the village that is opposite you, and immediately you will find a donkey tied, and a colt with her*[^1],' and so on down to that place where he says: *blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord; hosanna in the highest*[^2]. This too Mark narrates, keeping the same order[^3]. Luke, however, lingers at Jericho, recording certain things which these omitted, about Zacchaeus the chief of the publicans, and certain things said in parables[^4].

After these things he too meets them in recording this colt on which Jesus sat. Nor let it be a stumbling block that Matthew says both a donkey and a colt, while the rest are silent about the donkey. Indeed, that rule too is to be recalled which we suggested above concerning those who reclined in groups of fifty and of a hundred when the crowds were fed with the five loaves[^5]. Once this is suggested, it ought no longer to disturb the reader, not even if Matthew had been silent about the colt in the way that the others were silent about the donkey, so that it would seem most contradictory that one said a donkey, the others the colt of a donkey; how much less ought one to be disturbed because one so recorded the donkey, about which the rest are silent, that yet he did not pass over in silence the colt, about which they spoke. Where, then, both can be understood to have happened, there is no contradiction, not even if one records one thing and another another, how much less when one records one and another both.

**66.** 128. John too, though he is silent about how the Lord sent his disciples to bring him these animals, nevertheless briefly interposes this matter of the colt, with the testimony of the prophet as well[^6], which Matthew adduces. In this prophetic testimony too the somewhat diverse wording of the evangelists does not depart from the same meaning; but it can be a stumbling block that Matthew adduces it so as to say that the prophet mentioned a donkey[^7], whereas neither what John interposes nor the manuscripts of the customary ecclesiastical translation have it so.

The cause of this seems to me to be that Matthew is reported to have written his Gospel in the Hebrew language. Now it is clear that that translation which is called the *Septuagint* in some places differs from what those who know that language, and who have each translated the same Hebrew books, find in the Hebrew.

If, again, the cause of this divergence be sought — why so great an authority as the translation of the *Septuagint* differs in many places from that truth which is found in the Hebrew manuscripts — I think nothing more probable occurs to me

than that those *Seventy* translated by the same Spirit by which the very things they were translating had been spoken — which is confirmed by that marvelous agreement of theirs which is celebrated.

Therefore they too, by varying some things in their wording and yet not departing from that same will of God whose words those were and which the words ought to serve, wished to demonstrate nothing other than this very thing which we now marvel at in the concordant diversity, so to speak, of the four evangelists, by which it is shown to us that there is no falsehood if anyone narrates something in a manner so diverse that he does not depart from the will of him with whom one must be in accord and in agreement.

To know this is useful both for conduct, for the sake of avoiding and judging falsehoods, and for faith itself, lest we suppose that truth is, as it were, fortified by consecrated sounds, as though God commended to us the words to be spoken on account of a thing in the same way as he commends the thing itself; whereas rather the thing that is to be spoken is so to be preferred to the words by which it is to be spoken that we ought not to seek the latter at all, if we could know it without them, just as God knows it, and his angels in him.
