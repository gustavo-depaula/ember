# Where he foretold his passion to the disciples, what agreement there is among Matthew, Mark, and Luke.

**54.** 110. Matthew continues and says: *then he commanded his disciples to tell no one that he was Jesus Christ. From that time Jesus began to show his disciples that he must go to Jerusalem and suffer many things from the elders and scribes*[^1], and so on down to where he says: *you are not on the side of the things that are God's, but of those that are man's*[^2]. Mark and Luke append these things in the same order; but Luke is silent about Peter, that he contradicted the passion of Christ[^3].

Where these same three subjoin how the Lord commanded that whoever wishes should come after him, how they agree with each other.

**55.** 111. Matthew continues: *Then Jesus said to his disciples: 'If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me*[^4],' and so on down to where he says: *and then he will repay each one according to his work*[^5]. Mark too subjoins this, preserving the same order[^6]; but he does not say anything about the Son of man coming with his angels to repay each one according to his work. He records, however, that the Lord said this too: *for whoever is ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, of him the Son of man also will be ashamed when he comes in his glory with the holy angels*[^7]. This can be understood to belong to the same meaning by which Matthew said that he will repay each one according to his work. Luke too appends the same things, in the same order, in a not very different manner of words, yet most similar in the same truth of meaning[^8].

[^1]: Mt 16:20–21.
[^2]: Mt 16:23.
[^3]: See Mk 8:30–33; Lk 21–22.
[^4]: Mt 16:24.
[^5]: See Mk 8:34–38.
[^6]: See Mk 3:16–19.
[^7]: Mk 8:38.
[^8]: See Lk 9:23–26.
