# When the Jews asked the Lord by what authority he was doing these things, how these three agree with each other.

**69.** 132. Matthew continues and says: *and when he had come into the temple, the chief priests and the elders of the people came to him as he was teaching, saying: 'By what authority do you do these things, and who gave you this authority?' Jesus answered and said to them: 'I also will ask you one question; and if you tell me, I also will tell you by what authority I do these things. The baptism of John, where was it from?'*[^1], and so on down to where he says: *neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things*[^2]. This whole matter the other two, Mark and Luke, set forth in nearly the same number of words[^3].

Nor do they seem to differ from each other at all in order, except in what I spoke of above, that Matthew, having omitted certain things belonging to the other day, weaves his narration in such a way that, unless one attends, it might be thought that he is still occupied in the second day, while Mark is in the third. Luke, however, did not subjoin this as though following the days in order, but, when he had recorded the buyers and sellers driven out of the temple, omitted that he went out to Bethany and returned to the city, and what was done concerning the fig tree, and that, the disciples marveling, an answer was given about the power of faith,

and, these things omitted, he brought in the matter, saying: *and he was teaching daily in the temple. But the chief priests and the scribes and the leaders of the people sought to destroy him, and could not find what they might do to him; for all the people hung upon him, listening to him. And it happened on one of the days, as he was teaching the people in the temple and preaching the Gospel, that the chief priests and the scribes came together with the elders and said, speaking to him: 'Tell us, by what authority do you do these things?'*[^4], and the rest which those two also record. Hence it appears that nothing in them is contradictory even in the order itself, since what he says was *done on one of the days* is understood to be that day on which they too related that this was done.

[^1]: Mt 21:23–25.
[^2]: Mt 21:27.
[^3]: See Mk 11:27–33; Lk 19:47–48, 20:18.
[^4]: Lk 19:47–48, 20:12.
