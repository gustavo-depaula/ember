# It must be considered whether these three evangelists so depart from this man whose withered hand was healed that they contradict one another in nothing in the very order of the narration.

**36. 83.** Matthew goes on, weaving his narrative thus: *but the Pharisees, going out, took counsel against him, how they might destroy him. But Jesus, knowing it, withdrew from there, and many followed him, and he healed them all and charged them that they should not make him known, that what was said through Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, saying*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says, *and in his name the nations shall hope*[^2].

This one alone mentions; the other two went on to other things. Mark indeed seems to have kept somewhat the order of the thing done, who says that Jesus, having learned of the malignant plan of the Jews against him, withdrew to the sea with his disciples, and that the crowds flowed together to him, and that he healed very many of them[^3].

But whence he began to go on to something else—not what followed in order—scarcely appears: whether where he says that the crowds gathered to him (for this could be even at another time), or where he says, *he went up onto the mountain*.

Which Luke too seems to mention, saying: *now it came to pass in those days, he went out onto the mountain to pray*[^4]; for by saying *in those days*, he sufficiently shows that it was not done immediately.

[^1]: Mt 12:14–17.
[^2]: Mt 12:21.
[^3]: See Mk 3:6–12.
[^4]: Lk 6:12.
