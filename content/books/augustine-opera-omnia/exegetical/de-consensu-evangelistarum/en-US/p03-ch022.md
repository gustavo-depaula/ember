# On the order of the things which they narrate after this—whether Matthew, Mark, and Luke disagree among themselves in nothing.

**22. 53.** Matthew, then, goes on, saying: *and when evening had come, they brought to him many who had demons, and he cast out the spirits with a word and healed all who were ill, that what was said through Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: 'He himself took our infirmities and bore our sicknesses*[^1].' That this pertains to the time of the same day he sufficiently indicates, when he joins, *and when evening had come*. So too Mark, when he had said of Peter's mother-in-law, who was healed, *and she ministered to them*, subjoined this same thing: *and when evening had come, when the sun had set, they brought to him all who were ill and who had demons, and the whole city was gathered at the door. And he healed many who were troubled with various illnesses and cast out many demons and did not allow them to speak, because they knew him. And rising very early, he went out and departed into a desert place*[^2].

Here Mark seems to have kept the order, so that after what was said, *and when evening had come*, he then said, *and rising very early*. Although it is not necessary, where it is said *and when evening had come*, to take it as the *evening* of the same day, nor where it is said *early*, the early morning of the same night, nevertheless in some way the order of the things done can seem to be preserved here, on account of the arranged order of times. Luke too, when he had narrated about Peter's mother-in-law, does not himself say *and when evening had come*, but nevertheless added what means the same, saying: *and when the sun had set, all who had any sick with various illnesses brought them to him; and he, laying his hand on each one, cured them. And demons too went out from many, crying out and saying: 'You are the Son of God.' And rebuking them, he did not allow them to speak, because they knew that he was the Christ. And when day came, he went out and went into a desert place*[^3].

And here too we see the very same order of times kept which we found in Mark. But Matthew, who seems to have mentioned this about Peter's mother-in-law not in the order in which it was done, but as one recalling what he had passed over, after he had narrated what was done on that day, even in the evening, does not now subjoin the early morning, but narrates thus: *and Jesus, seeing many crowds around him, ordered them to go across the strait*[^4]. Now this is something else, not that which Mark and Luke weave, who place the early morning after the evening.

What was here said, then—*and Jesus, seeing many crowds around him, ordered them to go across the strait*—we ought to take as something else he interposed, recalling it: that on a certain day, when Jesus had seen many crowds around him, he ordered them to go across the strait.

[^1]: Mt 8:16-17; Isa 53:4
[^2]: Mk 1:32-35
[^3]: Lk 4:40-42
[^4]: Mt 8:18
