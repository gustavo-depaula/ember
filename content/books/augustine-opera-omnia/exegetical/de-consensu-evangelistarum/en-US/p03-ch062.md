# On the little children, on the rich man, on the vineyard, how Matthew is not opposed to the other two.

**63.** 123. Matthew continues: *then little children were brought to him, that he might lay his hands on them and pray. But the disciples rebuked them*[^1], and so on down to where he says: *for many are called, but few are chosen*[^2]. Mark kept this order with Matthew, but Matthew alone interposes the matter of the laborers hired for the vineyard[^3].

Luke, however, having recorded what he said to them when they were asking among themselves who was the greater, added the account of the one whom they saw casting out demons though he did not follow them; and from there he now departs from these two, where he says that he set his face to go to Jerusalem[^4], and after much intervening matter he again meets them in recording this rich man[^5], to whom it is said: *sell all that you have*[^6], whom these now record in the order in which they proceed together.

For there too Luke, before he makes mention of the rich man, does not omit the matter of these little children, just as these others do. As to that rich man, then, who asks what good thing he is to do that he may obtain eternal life, there can seem to be some difference, in that according to Matthew it is said: *why do you ask me about the good?*[^7] but according to the others: *why do you call me good?*[^8] For *why do you ask me about the good?* can rather be referred to what the questioner said: *what good thing shall I do?* For there he both named the good and it is a question, whereas *Good Teacher* is not yet a question. Most fittingly, then, both are understood to have been said: *why do you call me good,* and: *do you ask me about the good?*

[^1]: Mt 19:13.
[^2]: Mt 20:16.
[^3]: See Mk 10:13–31.
[^4]: See Lk 9:46, 51.
[^5]: See Lk 18:18–30.
[^6]: Lk 18:22.
[^7]: Mt 13:17.
[^8]: Mk 10:18; Lk 18:19.
