# On the one to whom the two commandments of love of God and of neighbor were commended.

**73.** 141. Matthew therefore continues and says: *but the Pharisees, hearing that he had imposed silence on the Sadducees, gathered together, and one of them, a doctor of the Law, asked him, testing him: 'Teacher, which is the great commandment in the Law?' Jesus said to him: 'You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind*[^1].' *This is the greatest and first commandment. And the second is like it: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^2].' *On these two commandments depend the whole Law and the prophets*[^3]. This Mark too records, preserving the same order[^4]. Nor let it be a stumbling block that Matthew says the one by whom the Lord was questioned was a tester, while Mark passes over this and at the end concludes thus, that the Lord said to him, when he had answered wisely: *you are not far from the kingdom of God*[^5].

For it can be that, although he came as a tester, he was nevertheless corrected by the Lord's answer. Or at least we may take the testing itself not as evil, as of one wishing to deceive an enemy, but rather as cautious, as of one wishing further to make trial of someone unknown to him. For not in vain is it written: *he who believes too readily is light of heart and will be diminished*[^6].

**73.** 142. Luke, however, interposes something of the kind not in this order, but far elsewhere[^7]. But whether he recalls this, or whether it is another with whom the Lord dealt in like manner about these two commandments, is altogether uncertain; yet from this it also rightly seems to be another, not only because of the great difference of order, but because he too is said to have answered the Lord's question and in his answer to have mentioned these two commandments;

and when the Lord had said to him: *do this, and you will live*[^8], that he might do what he himself had answered was great in the Law, the evangelist continues and says: *but he, wishing to justify himself, said: 'And who is my neighbor?'*[^9]. Then the Lord told the story of the man who was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho and fell among robbers.

Hence, because he was described as a tester, and he himself answered the two commandments, and after the Lord's admonition, saying: *do this, and you will live,* he is not commended as good, when it is said of him: *but he, wishing to justify himself,*

whereas the one whom Matthew and Mark record in a like order is so well commended that the Lord said to him: *you are not far from the kingdom of God*[^10], it is more probably believed that this one is another, not that one.

[^2]: Lev 19:18.
