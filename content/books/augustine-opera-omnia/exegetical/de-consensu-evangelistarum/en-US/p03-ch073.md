# When he foretold the overthrow of the temple, how he agrees with the other two in the order of narration.

**76.** 146. Matthew continues and says: *and Jesus went out from the temple and was going away. And his disciples came to point out to him the buildings of the temple. But he answered and said to them: 'Do you see all these things? Truly I say to you, there will not be left here one stone upon another that will not be thrown down*[^1].' Mark too records this in nearly the same order, after a certain digression made for this purpose, to record the matter of the widow who put two small coins into the treasury, which Luke alone records along with him[^2]. For according to Mark too, after the Lord dealt with the Jews as to how they should take the Christ to be the son of David, the things are narrated which he says about being on guard against the Pharisees and their hypocrisy[^3]. Which place Matthew followed out most fully and narrated more things said there. And therefore, after that same place which Mark touched briefly and Matthew set out copiously, Mark brought in nothing further, as I said, than the matter of that poorest and most bountiful widow, and then subjoined the things by which he might again cohere with Matthew, speaking about the future overthrow of the temple. Luke too, after that matter about the Christ, how he was the son of David, records a few things about being on guard against the hypocrisy of the Pharisees. From there, like Mark, he proceeds to the widow who put two small coins into the treasury. Then he subjoins about the future overthrow of the temple what Matthew and Mark do[^4].

[^1]: Mt 24:1–2.
[^2]: See Mk 12:41–44, 13:12; Lk 21:14.
[^3]: See Mk 12:35–40.
[^4]: See Lk 20:45–47; 21:1–16.
