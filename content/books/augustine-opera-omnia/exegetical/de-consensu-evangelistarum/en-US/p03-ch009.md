# How Matthew says that Joseph feared to go with the infant Christ into Jerusalem, on account of Archelaus, and yet did not fear to go into Galilee.

**8. 21.** Unless perhaps someone be again troubled by this: since Matthew said that Joseph feared, on the boy's return, to go into Judea, because Archelaus his son reigned there in place of his father Herod, how could he go into Galilee, where another son of his, Herod, was tetrarch, as Luke testifies[^1]? As if, indeed, the very times in which the boy was then feared for were the same as those which Luke now mentioned, which had so changed that in Judea itself Archelaus was now no longer king, but Pontius Pilate, who was not king of the Jews, but governor, in whose times the sons of the elder Herod, living under Tiberius Caesar, did not have a kingdom, but a tetrarchy. Which surely had not yet happened when Joseph, fearing Archelaus reigning in Judea, betook himself with the boy into Galilee, where his city Nazareth also was.

[^1]: See Mt 2:22; Lk 3:1
