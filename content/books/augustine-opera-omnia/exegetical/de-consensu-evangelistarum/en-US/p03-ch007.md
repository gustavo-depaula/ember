# On the order of the preaching of John the Baptist among all four.

**6. 18.** From here it begins to be narrated about the preaching of John, which all four mention. For Matthew, after those words which I set down as his last, where he mentioned the testimony from the prophet, *that he shall be called a Nazarene*[^1], goes on and adds: *Now in those days came John the Baptist, preaching in the desert of Judea*[^2], and the rest. And Mark, who narrated nothing about the nativity or infancy or boyhood of the Lord, took the beginning of his Gospel from here, that is, from the preaching of John. For he began thus: *The beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, as it is written in Isaiah the prophet: 'Behold, I send my angel before your face, who shall prepare your way*[^3]. *A voice of one crying in the desert: 'Prepare the way of the Lord, make straight his paths*[^4].'' *John was in the desert, baptizing and preaching a baptism of repentance for the remission of sins*[^5], and the rest. And Luke, after the words where he says, *And Jesus advanced in wisdom and age and grace before God and men*[^6], now goes on about the preaching of John, saying: *Now in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate governing Judea, Herod being tetrarch of Galilee, and Philip his brother tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias tetrarch of Abilene, under the chief priests Annas and Caiaphas, the word of the Lord came upon John the son of Zechariah in the desert*[^7], and the rest. John too, the apostle most eminent among the four evangelists, after he had spoken about the Word of God, who is the Son himself before all the ages of creation, because all things were made through him[^8], immediately brought in the matter of John's preaching and testimony, saying: *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John*[^9]. Whence it is now to be seen, concerning this John, how the narrations of the four evangelists do not disagree among themselves—not that this be required or demanded of us throughout, as we just now did concerning the beginnings of Christ born of Mary, how Matthew and Luke agree among themselves, so that we made one narrative out of both, showing to the slower that for either of them to mention what the other is silent about, or to be silent about what the other mentions, does not impede the understanding of the truthful narration of the other; so that by this example, whether as it was done by me or in some other more convenient way, each one may see that in the other such passages too there can be done what he has perceived to have been done here.

**6. 19.** Now, then, as I said, let us see the agreement of the four evangelists about John the Baptist. Matthew goes on thus: *Now in those days came John the Baptist, preaching in the desert of Judea*[^10]. Mark did not say *in those days*, because he had set forth no series of things in whose days it might be understood that he was speaking, if he said *in those days*. But Luke, through the earthly powers, expressed the times themselves of John's preaching or baptism more pointedly, saying: *Now in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate governing Judea, Herod being tetrarch of Galilee, and Philip his brother tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias tetrarch of Abilene, under the chief priests Annas and Caiaphas, the word of the Lord came upon John the son of Zechariah in the desert*[^11]. Yet we ought not to understand that Matthew signified these days, that is, this time of these powers, when he said *in those days*, but that he wished what he says, *in those days*, to be taken over a much longer space of time. For as soon as he had narrated that Christ returned from Egypt when Herod had died[^12]—which surely was done at the time of his infancy or boyhood, so that it can be consistent with what Luke narrated as done by him in the temple of Jerusalem when he was twelve years old[^13]—when, then, he had mentioned the infant or boy recalled from Egypt, Matthew immediately brought in: *Now in those days came John the Baptist*[^14], surely intimating not only the days of his boyhood, but all the days from his nativity up to the time at which John began to preach and baptize, by which time the age of Christ is found to be that of a young man, because he himself and John were of the same age, and he is narrated to have been about thirty years old when he was baptized by him.
