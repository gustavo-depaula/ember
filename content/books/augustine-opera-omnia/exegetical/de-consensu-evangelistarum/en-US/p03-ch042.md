# That he came into his own country, and they marveled at his teaching, while they despised his lineage; how Matthew agrees with Mark and Luke, especially whether the order of narrating contradicts the other in nothing.

**42. 89.** Matthew, then, goes on: *and it came to pass, when Jesus had finished these parables, he went on from there. And coming into his own country, he taught them in their synagogues*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says, *and he did not do many powers there, because of their unbelief*[^2]. From the foregoing discourse of parables he so passes on that he does not show the necessity of a consequent order, especially because Mark, from those parables[^3], tending not to that which this one does, but to something else, into which Luke too so connected the narration[^4] that it is shown more credibly that this was rather done as a consequence which those two continuously subjoin—namely about the boat in which Jesus was sleeping, and about that miracle of the demons cast out among the Gerasenes. Which two Matthew already above, recalling them, interposed[^5]. Now, then, let us see whether the things which the Lord said in his own country, or which were said to him, agree with the other two, Mark and Luke. For John, far off in the diverse and dissimilar places of his narration, mentions that such things either were said to the Lord or that he said such things[^6] as the other three here recalled.

**42. 90.** And Mark indeed mentions here almost all the same things which Matthew does, except that he says the Lord was called by his fellow citizens *the carpenter and the son of Mary*, not, as Matthew does, *the carpenter's son*[^7].

Nor is it to be wondered at, since both could have been said; for they believed him a carpenter for the same reason that they believed him the carpenter's son[^8]. But Luke indicates the same thing done more broadly and mentions more things there[^9]; nor does he insert it long after his baptism and temptation, without doubt anticipating what was done afterward, with many things interposed.

Whence anyone may observe—what is very necessary for this so great question, which, with God's help, we have undertaken to unravel, concerning the agreement of the evangelists—that, not being ignorant that they passed over something, or not being ignorant how the order of the things done stood, they rather kept another order, that of their own recollection.

Which can be most evidently understood from this: that Luke, before he narrated that the Lord did anything at Capernaum, anticipated this passage which we are now considering, where his fellow citizens both were astounded at the power of his might in him and despised the lowliness of his lineage. For he says that he said this to them: *You will surely say to me: 'Physician, cure yourself; as great things as we have heard done at Capernaum, do here too in your own country*[^10],' when, according to Luke's own narration, nothing is yet read that he had done at Capernaum. Which, because it is not long, and is very easy, and is very necessary, we insert in full, to show by what occasion, and how, he came to narrating this.

After he had indicated the Lord baptized and tempted, he goes on thus: *and when all the temptation was finished, the devil departed from him for a time. And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit into Galilee, and a report went out through the whole region about him. And he taught in their synagogues and was magnified by all. And he came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up, and entered, according to his custom, on the Sabbath day into the synagogue, and rose to read. And there was handed to him the book of the prophet Isaiah, and when he had unrolled the book, he found the place where it was written: 'The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me;*

*he has sent me to preach the Gospel to the poor, to proclaim to the captives release and to the blind sight, to send away the broken in release, to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord and the day of recompense*[^11].' *And when he had folded the book, he gave it back to the minister and sat down. And the eyes of all in the synagogue were fixed upon him. And he began to say to them that today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your ears. And all bore testimony to him and marveled at the words of grace that proceeded from his mouth, and said: 'Is this not the son of Joseph?' And he said to them: 'You will surely say to me this similitude: Physician, cure yourself; as great things as we have heard done at Capernaum, do here too in your own country*[^12],' and the rest, until he completes that whole passage of his narration. What, then, is more evident than that he, knowing it, anticipated this in narrating—since surely both he himself knows that great things had already been done by him at Capernaum, and he himself mentions things which he surely knows he has not yet narrated? For he has not advanced so far from his baptism that he should be thought to have forgotten that he has not yet mentioned anything of those things which had been done at Capernaum; for he has only just begun, after the baptism, to narrate something about the Lord.
