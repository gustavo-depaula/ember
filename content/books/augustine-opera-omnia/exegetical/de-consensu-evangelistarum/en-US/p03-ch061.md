# When he was asked whether it is lawful to dismiss one's wife, how Matthew and Mark agree with each other, especially regarding the questions themselves, whether of the Lord or of the Jews, and the answers, in which they seem to vary a little.

**62.** 120. Matthew continues, narrating thus: *and it happened that, when Jesus had finished these discourses, he departed from Galilee and came into the region of Judea beyond the Jordan. And great crowds followed him, and he healed them there. And the Pharisees came up to him, testing him and saying: 'Is it lawful for a man to dismiss his wife for any cause?'*[^1] and so on down to where he says: *let him who is able to receive it, receive it*[^2]. This Mark too records, keeping the same order[^3]. We must surely take care that it not seem contradictory that this same Mark says the Pharisees were asked by the Lord what Moses had commanded them, and that they thus answered his question about the bill of divorce permitted them[^4], whereas Matthew said that, in response to the words of the Lord by which he had shown that from the Law God had joined the male and the female and therefore they ought not to be separated by man, they returned the answer: *why then did Moses command to give a bill of divorce and to dismiss her?* To whom he again said: *because of the hardness of your heart Moses permitted you to dismiss your wives; but from the beginning it was not so*[^5]. For Mark too does not pass over this answer of the Lord, but places it after they had answered his question about the bill of divorce.

**62.** 121. In this matter, in the order or manner of words, we ought to understand that it makes no difference to the truth of the thing whether, when the Lord was forbidding separation and confirming his judgment from the Law, they themselves brought up the question about the bill of divorce permitted them through that same Moses through whom this too was written, that God joined the male and the female[^6], or whether they answered this same thing to him as he was questioning them about the command of Moses. For his intention was such that he would not give them the reason why Moses had permitted that unless they themselves had first mentioned it, which intention of his is signified by the question that Mark set down; and their own intention was, from the authority of Moses, since he commanded a bill of divorce to be given, as it were to conclude that he would without doubt forbid separation; for it was to say this that they had come, even testing him. This intention of theirs is so expressed by Matthew that he does not record that they were questioned, but that they brought up of their own accord the command of Moses, by which they might as it were convict the Lord, who was forbidding the separation of spouses. Since therefore the intention of the speakers, which the words ought to serve, has been shown by each evangelist, it makes no difference how diverse the manner of narrating was between the two, so long as neither departed from the same truth.

**62.** 122. This too can be understood: that, as Mark says, the Lord, when they first questioned him about dismissing a wife, in turn questioned them as to what Moses had commanded them; and when they had answered that Moses had permitted to write a bill of divorce and to dismiss her, he answered them concerning the very Law given through Moses, how God had instituted the marriage of male and female, saying the things which Matthew sets down, that is: *have you not read that he who made them from the beginning made them male and female?*[^7] and so on; and that, having heard these things, they repeated what they had answered to his first question, saying: *why then did Moses command to give a bill of divorce and to dismiss her?*[^8] Then Jesus showed the cause to be the hardness of their heart, which Mark, for the sake of brevity, sets down earlier, as returned to that former answer of theirs which Matthew omitted, judging that nothing is lost to the truth in whatever place that answer was returned in the very same words which they had said twice, since the Lord returned it in those very words.
