# On the blind men of Jericho given sight, how Matthew is not opposed either to Mark or to Luke.

**65.** 125. Matthew continues: *and as they were going out from Jericho, a great crowd followed him. And behold, two blind men sitting by the road heard that Jesus was passing by, and they cried out, saying: 'Lord, have mercy on us, son of David*[^1],' and so on down to where he says: *and immediately they saw and followed him*[^2]. This Mark too records, but as done with one blind man[^3]. This question is resolved in the same way as that about the two who were afflicted by a legion of demons in the region of the Gerasenes was resolved[^4]. For that one of the two blind men whom he just now interposed was very well known and most famous in that city is sufficiently clear from this also, that Mark recorded both his name and his father's name[^5], which does not readily occur among so many healed by the Lord above, except when he expressed by name Jairus the ruler of the synagogue, whose daughter Jesus raised[^6]. In which case too this sense appears all the more, since that ruler of the synagogue was surely a man of note in that place. Without doubt, therefore, this Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus, cast down from some great prosperity, was a man of most well-known and most famous misery, since he sat not only blind but also a beggar. This, then, is why Mark wished to record him alone, whose receiving of sight won for this miracle a fame as bright as his calamity was well known.

**65.** 126. Luke, however, though the deed was done in altogether the same manner, is to be understood as recording a like miracle, and a like manner of the same miracle, with a different blind man[^7].

For he says this happened as Jesus was drawing near to Jericho[^8], but these others say it was as he was going out from Jericho[^9].

But the name of the city and the likeness of the deed persuade one to think that it happened only once,

yet that the evangelists are opposed to each other in this, that one says *as he was drawing near to Jericho,*

the others *as he was going out from Jericho* — this surely persuades no one, except those who would more readily believe the Gospel to be lying than that Jesus did two similar miracles in a similar way.

But what is more credible, or rather what is true, both every faithful son of the Gospel most easily sees, and every contentious person, at least once he has been admonished, answers himself either by keeping silent or, even if he is unwilling to keep silent, by thinking it over.
