# A question between Matthew and Mark about the paralytic lying on the bed.

**25. 57.** From here, then, Matthew goes on, still preserving the order of times, and thus weaves his narrative: *and going up into the boat, he crossed over and came into his own city. And behold, they brought to him a paralytic lying on a bed*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says, *but the crowds, seeing this, feared and glorified God, who gave such power to men*[^2]. About this paralytic Mark and Luke too spoke[^3].

That Matthew, then, says the Lord said, *Take courage, son, your sins are forgiven you*[^4], while Luke did not say *son*, but *man*[^5], serves to intimate more expressly the meaning of the Lord: because the sins were being forgiven to a man, who by this very fact, that he was a man, could not say, "I have not sinned"; and at the same time so that the one who forgave a man might be understood as God. Mark indeed said this[^6], which Matthew too said, but he did not say *Take courage*. It could indeed be said thus: *Take courage, man; your sins are forgiven, son*, or, *Take courage, son; your sins are forgiven, man*, or in any fitting order of words.

**25. 58.** That, indeed, can trouble us, that Matthew narrates about this paralytic thus: *and going up into the boat, he crossed over and came into his own city. And behold, they brought to him a paralytic lying on a bed*[^7].

But Mark says this was done not in his city, which is surely called Nazareth, but in Capernaum, which he narrates thus: *and again he entered Capernaum after some days, and it was heard that he was in the house. And many gathered, so that there was no room, not even at the door, and he spoke the word to them. And they came, bringing to him a paralytic, who was carried by four. And when they could not bring him to him because of the crowd, they uncovered the roof where he was, and making an opening they let down the pallet on which the paralytic lay. But when Jesus saw their faith*, and the rest[^8]. But Luke does not mention in what place it was done, but says thus:

*and it came to pass on one of the days, and he himself was sitting, teaching, and there were Pharisees sitting, and doctors of the Law, who had come out of every village of Galilee and Judea and Jerusalem, and the power of the Lord was present to heal them. And behold, men carrying on a bed a man who was paralyzed, and they sought to bring him in and place him before him. And not finding by what way they might bring him in because of the crowd, they went up onto the roof and through the tiles let him down with his bed into the midst before Jesus. When he saw their faith, he said: 'Man, your sins are forgiven you'*[^9], and the rest.

There remains, then, a question between Mark and Matthew, that Matthew writes as though it was done in the Lord's city, but Mark in Capernaum. Which would be resolved with more difficulty if Matthew also named Nazareth. But now, since Galilee itself could be called the city of Christ, because Nazareth was in Galilee—just as the whole empire, set up in so many cities, is called the Roman city; and since the city is set up among so many nations, of which it is written, *Glorious things are said of you, city of God*[^10]; and since the earlier people of God too, dwelling in so many cities, was also called one house, the house of Israel[^11]—

who would doubt that Jesus did this in his own city, when he did it in the city of Capernaum, a city of Galilee, to which, by crossing the strait, he had returned from the region of the Gerasenes, so that, coming into Galilee, he would rightly be said to have come into his own city, in whatever town of Galilee he was—especially since Capernaum itself was so preeminent in Galilee that it was held as a metropolis? But if it were absolutely not allowed to take the city of Christ either as Galilee itself, in which Nazareth was, or as Capernaum itself, which stood out as the head of the cities of Galilee, we would say that Matthew passed over the things which were done after Jesus came into his own city, until he should come also to Capernaum, and added this about the healed paralytic—just as in many cases they do thus, passing over the things in between, as though what they subjoin, with no indication of their having passed anything over, follows immediately.
