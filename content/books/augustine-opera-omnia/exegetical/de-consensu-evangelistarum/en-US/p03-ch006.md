# How the order of Luke agrees with the order of Matthew in the things concerning the conception and the infancy or boyhood of Christ.

**5. 14.** After the generations have been enumerated, Matthew continues thus: *Now the generation of Christ was in this way. When his mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together, she was found to be with child by the Holy Spirit*[^1]. How this came about, which he here passed over, Luke set forth, after mentioning the conception of John, narrating thus: *And in the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent by the Lord into a city of Galilee whose name was Nazareth, to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David, and the name of the virgin was Mary. And the angel, having come in to her, said: 'Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you; blessed are you among women.' And when she had seen him, she was troubled at his word and considered what kind of greeting this might be. And the angel said to her: 'Do not fear, Mary, for you have found grace with God. Behold, you shall conceive in your womb and bear a son*[^2], *and you shall call his name Jesus; he shall be great and shall be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God shall give him the throne of David his father, and he shall reign in the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there shall be no end*[^3].' *And Mary said to the angel: 'How shall this be, since I do not know a man?' And the angel, answering, said to her: 'The Holy Spirit shall come upon you, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you; and therefore the Holy One who shall be born shall be called the Son of God*[^4],' and the rest, which do not pertain to what is now being treated. This, then, Matthew mentioned, saying of Mary, *she was found to be with child by the Holy Spirit*; nor is it contrary that Luke set forth what Matthew passed over, since both testify that Mary conceived by the Holy Spirit, just as it is not contrary that Matthew next connects what Luke passes over. For Matthew goes on and says: *But Joseph her husband, since he was just and unwilling to expose her, wished to send her away secretly. But as he was thinking these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying: Joseph, son of David, do not fear to take Mary your wife; for what is born in her is of the Holy Spirit. And she shall bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus; for he shall save his people from their sins*[^5]. *Now all this was done, that what was said by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: 'Behold, a virgin shall be with child and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel*[^6], *which is interpreted 'God with us.'' And Joseph, rising from sleep, did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him, and took his wife; and he did not know her until she had borne her firstborn son, and he called his name Jesus*[^7]. *Now when Jesus had been born in Bethlehem of Judah in the days of Herod the king*[^8], and the rest.

**5. 15.** About the city of Bethlehem, Matthew and Luke agree; but how and for what cause Joseph and Mary came to it, Luke sets forth[^9], while Matthew passes it over. On the other hand, about the Magi coming from the east, Luke is silent, but Matthew speaks, weaving it thus: *Behold, Magi from the East came to Jerusalem, saying: 'Where is he who has been born king of the Jews? For we have seen his star in the East and have come to adore him.' But Herod the king, hearing this, was troubled*[^10], and the rest, up to that place where it is written of the Magi themselves that *having received a response in a dream that they should not return by way of Herod, they went back to their own region by another way*[^11]. All this Luke passed over, just as Matthew did not narrate what Luke narrates: that the Lord was placed in a manger, and that an angel announced him born to the shepherds, and that a multitude of the heavenly host arose with the angel, praising God, and that the shepherds came and saw that what the angel had announced to them was true, and that on the day of his circumcision he received his name, and the things which the same Luke narrates after the days of Mary's purification were fulfilled: that he brought him to Jerusalem, and about Simeon and Anna, what they said about him in the temple, after they recognized him, filled with the Holy Spirit[^12]. All these things Matthew is silent about.

**5. 16.** Whence it is rightly asked when those things were done—either what Matthew passes over and Luke tells, or what Luke passes over and Matthew tells. For Matthew, after the Magi who had come from the east returned to their own region, goes on and narrates that Joseph was admonished by an angel to flee with the infant into Egypt, lest he be killed by Herod; then that Herod, not finding him, killed the children of two years old and under; and that, when Herod had died, he returned from Egypt, and, having heard that Archelaus reigned in Judea in place of his father Herod, dwelt with the boy in the region of Galilee, in the city of Nazareth[^13]—all of which Luke is silent about.

Nor can it on that account seem contrary that either this one tells what that one passes over, or that one mentions what this one does not tell. But it is asked: when could the things have happened which Matthew weaves together about the departure into Egypt and the return thence after Herod's death, so that they were now dwelling in the city of Nazareth, to which Luke records that they returned after they had performed in the Temple, regarding the boy, all things according to the Law of the Lord[^14]? Here, accordingly, that is to be recognized which will be of value for the rest of such matters, lest they similarly disturb and confuse the mind: that each evangelist so weaves his own narrative that the arranged series may seem as though it passes over nothing; for, while the things he does not wish to tell are left unsaid, he so joins the things he does wish to tell to those he was telling, that they seem to follow continuously.

But when one tells what the other was silent about, the order, carefully considered, indicates the place where he could leap over the things that were passed over by him, so as to couple the things he intended to tell to the foregoing, as though they followed with nothing interposed. And for this reason it is understood that Matthew, where he says the Magi were admonished in a dream not to return by way of Herod, and went back to their own region by another way[^15], there passed over the things which Luke narrated as done concerning the Lord in the temple, and said by Simeon and Anna; where again Luke, passing over the departure into Egypt which Matthew narrates, wove the return to the city of Nazareth as though continuous[^16].

**5. 17.** But if anyone wishes to order one narrative out of all the things which, concerning Christ's nativity and infancy or boyhood, are either told or passed over by one or the other in the narration of both, he can do so thus:

*Now the generation of Christ was in this way*[^17]. *There was, in the days of Herod the king of Judah, a certain priest named Zechariah, of the course of Abijah, and he had a wife of the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elizabeth. Now they were both just before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord without complaint. And they had no son, because Elizabeth was barren, and both had advanced in their days. Now it came to pass, when he was performing the priestly office in the order of his course before God, according to the custom of the priesthood it fell to him by lot to place the incense, having entered the Temple of the Lord; and all the multitude of the people was praying outside at the hour of incense. And there appeared to him an angel of the Lord, standing at the right of the altar of incense, and Zechariah, seeing him, was troubled, and fear fell upon him. But the angel said to him: 'Do not fear, Zechariah, because your prayer has been heard, and your wife Elizabeth shall bear you a son, and you shall call his name John; and there shall be joy and exultation for you, and many shall rejoice at his nativity; for he shall be great before the Lord, and shall not drink wine and strong drink, and shall be filled with the Holy Spirit even from his mother's womb, and shall convert many of the children of Israel to the Lord their God; and he himself shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah, that he may turn the hearts of the fathers to the children*[^18] *and the unbelieving to the prudence of the just, to prepare for the Lord a perfect people.'*

*And Zechariah said to the angel: 'How shall I know this? For I am an old man, and my wife has advanced in her days.' And the angel, answering, said to him: 'I am Gabriel, who stand before God, and I have been sent to speak to you and to announce these glad tidings to you; and behold, you shall be silent and unable to speak until the day on which these things come to pass, because you did not believe my words, which shall be fulfilled in their time.' And the people were awaiting Zechariah, and they marveled that he delayed in the Temple. But coming out, he could not speak to them, and they understood that he had seen a vision in the Temple. And he himself was making signs to them, and remained mute.*

*And it came to pass, when the days of his office were fulfilled, he went away to his house. But after those days Elizabeth his wife conceived, and she hid herself for five months, saying: 'Thus has the Lord done for me in the days in which he looked upon me, to take away my reproach among men.'*

*And in the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent by the Lord into a city of Galilee whose name was Nazareth, to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David, and the name of the virgin was Mary. And the angel, having come in to her, said: 'Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you; blessed are you among women.' And when she had seen him, she was troubled at his word and considered what kind of greeting this might be. And the angel said to her: 'Do not fear, Mary, for you have found grace with God; behold, you shall conceive in your womb and bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus; he shall be great and shall be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God shall give him the throne of David his father, and he shall reign in the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there shall be no end.' And Mary said to the angel: 'How shall this be, since I do not know a man?' And the angel, answering, said to her: 'The Holy Spirit shall come upon you, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you; and therefore the Holy One who shall be born shall be called the Son of God; and behold, Elizabeth your kinswoman has herself also conceived a son in her old age, and this is the sixth month for her who was called barren, because with God no word shall be impossible.' And Mary said: 'Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done to me according to your word.' And the angel departed from her.*

*And Mary, rising up in those days, went into the hill country with haste, into a city of Judah, and entered the house of Zechariah and greeted Elizabeth. And it came to pass, when Elizabeth heard Mary's greeting, the infant leaped in her womb. And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit and cried out with a loud voice and said: 'Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb. And whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me? For behold, as the voice of your greeting came into my ears, the infant leaped for joy in my womb.' And blessed is she who believed, for the things that were said to her by the Lord shall be accomplished. And Mary said: 'My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit has exulted in God my Savior, because he has looked upon the lowliness of his handmaid*[^19]; *for behold, from now on all generations shall call me blessed, because he who is mighty has done great things for me. And holy is his name*[^20], *and his mercy is from generation to generation upon those who fear him*[^21]. *He has shown might in his arm, he has scattered the proud in the thought of their heart, he has put down the mighty from their seat and exalted the lowly, he has filled the hungry with good things and sent the rich away empty*[^22]. *He has received Israel his servant, being mindful of his mercy, as he spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and his seed forever*[^23].' *And Mary stayed with her about three months and returned to her house*[^24] *and was found to be with child by the Holy Spirit.*

*But Joseph her husband, since he was just and unwilling to expose her, wished to send her away secretly. But as he was thinking these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying: 'Joseph, son of David, do not fear to take Mary your wife; for what is born in her is of the Holy Spirit. And she shall bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus; for he shall save his people from their sins.' Now all this was done, that what was said by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: 'Behold, a virgin shall be with child and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel*[^25], *which is interpreted 'God with us.'' And Joseph, rising from sleep, did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him, and took his wife and did not know her*[^26]. *But for Elizabeth the time of her bearing was fulfilled, and she bore a son.*

*And her neighbors and kinsfolk heard that the Lord had magnified his mercy toward her, and they congratulated her. And it came to pass on the eighth day, they came to circumcise the boy, and they called him by the name of his father, Zechariah. And his mother, answering, said: 'Not so, but he shall be called John.' And they said to her: 'There is no one in your kindred who is called by this name.' And they made signs to his father, what he wished him to be called. And asking for a writing tablet, he wrote, saying: 'John is his name.' And they all marveled. And immediately his mouth was opened, and his tongue, and he spoke, blessing God. And fear came upon all their neighbors, and throughout all the hill country of Judea all these words were spread abroad. And all who heard laid them up in their heart, saying: 'What, do you think, will this boy be?' For the hand of the Lord was with him.*

*And Zechariah his father was filled with the Holy Spirit and prophesied, saying: 'Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel*[^27], *because he has visited and accomplished the redemption of his people. And he has raised up a horn of salvation for us, in the house of David his servant*[^28], *as he spoke through the mouth of his holy prophets, who are from of old, salvation from our enemies and from the hand of all who hate us*[^29], *to show mercy to our fathers and to be mindful of his holy covenant, the oath which he swore to Abraham our father, that he would grant us*[^30], *that, without fear, delivered from the hands of our enemies, we should serve him in holiness and justice before him all our days. And you, child, shall be called the prophet of the Most High; for you shall go before the face of the Lord to prepare his ways*[^31], *to give knowledge of salvation to his people, for the remission of their sins, through the heart of the mercy of our God, in which the Dayspring from on high has visited us, to enlighten those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to direct our feet into the way of peace*[^32].' *And the boy grew and was strengthened in spirit, and was in the deserts until the day of his showing to Israel*[^33].

*Now it came to pass in those days, there went out a decree from Caesar Augustus that all the world should be enrolled. This first enrollment was made under Quirinius, the governor of Syria. And all went to be enrolled, each into his own city. And Joseph too went up from Galilee, from the city of Nazareth, into Judea, to the city of David which is called Bethlehem, because he was of the house and family of David, to be enrolled with Mary, his betrothed wife, who was with child. And it came to pass, while they were there, the days were fulfilled that she should bear, and she bore her firstborn son and wrapped him in swaddling clothes and laid him in a manger, because there was no place for them in the inn.*

*And there were shepherds in the same region, keeping watch and guarding the night watches over their flock. And behold, an angel of the Lord stood beside them, and the brightness of God shone round about them, and they feared with a great fear. And the angel said to them: 'Do not fear; for behold, I announce to you a great joy, which shall be to all the people, because there has been born to you today a Savior, who is Christ the Lord, in the city of David. And this shall be a sign to you: you shall find an infant wrapped in swaddling clothes and placed in a manger.' And suddenly there was, with the angel, a multitude of the heavenly host, praising God and saying: 'Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will.' And it came to pass, when the angels had departed from them into heaven, the shepherds said to one another: 'Let us go over to Bethlehem and see this word which has come to pass, which the Lord has shown to us.' And they came in haste and found Mary and Joseph and the infant placed in the manger. And seeing this, they understood concerning the word which had been spoken to them about this boy. And all who heard marveled, also at the things which had been told them by the shepherds; but Mary kept all these words, pondering them in her heart. And the shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all the things which they had heard and seen, as it was told to them. And after eight days were completed, that he should be circumcised, his name was called Jesus, which was called by the angel before he was conceived in the womb*[^34]. *Behold, Magi from the East came to Jerusalem, saying: 'Where is he who has been born king of the Jews? For we have seen his star in the East and have come to adore him.' But Herod the king, hearing this, was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him. And gathering together all the chief priests and scribes of the people, he inquired of them where Christ should be born. But they said to him: 'In Bethlehem of Judah, for thus it is written through the prophet: 'And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least among the princes of Judah; for out of you shall come forth a leader who shall rule my people Israel*[^35].'' *Then Herod, secretly calling the Magi, diligently inquired of them the time of the star which had appeared to them. And sending them to Bethlehem, he said: 'Go and inquire diligently about the boy, and when you have found him, report to me, that I too may come and adore him.' And when they had heard the king, they departed. And behold, the star which they had seen in the East went before them, until, coming, it stood over where the boy was. But seeing the star, they rejoiced with an exceedingly great joy. And entering the house, they found the boy with Mary his mother, and falling down they adored him, and opening their treasures they offered him gifts, gold, frankincense, and myrrh*[^36]. *And having received a response in a dream that they should not return to Herod, they went back to their own region by another way*[^37].

And when they had withdrawn, *after the days of her purification were fulfilled, according to the Law of Moses, they brought him to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord, as it is written in the Law of the Lord, that every male opening the womb shall be called holy to the Lord*[^38], *and to give a sacrifice according to what is said in the Law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons*[^39]. *And behold, there was a man in Jerusalem whose name was Simeon, and this man was just and devout, awaiting the consolation of Israel, and the Holy Spirit was in him. And he had received a response from the Holy Spirit that he would not see death unless he first saw the Christ of the Lord. And he came in the Spirit into the Temple. And when his parents brought in the boy Jesus, to do for him according to the custom of the Law, he too took him into his arms and blessed God and said: 'Now you dismiss your servant, Lord, according to your word, in peace, because my eyes have seen your salvation, which you have prepared before the face of all peoples, a light for the revelation of the nations and the glory of your people Israel.' And his father and mother were marveling at the things which were said about him. And Simeon blessed them and said to Mary his mother: 'Behold, this one is set for the fall and the rising of many in Israel, and for a sign that shall be contradicted*[^40], *and a sword shall pierce your own soul, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed.' And there was Anna, a prophetess, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Asher. She had advanced in many days and had lived with her husband seven years from her virginity, and she was a widow up to eighty-four years, who did not depart from the temple, serving with fastings and prayers night and day. And she, coming upon them at that very hour, gave thanks to the Lord and spoke about him to all who were awaiting the redemption of Jerusalem*. *And when they had performed all things according to the Law of the Lord*[^41], *behold, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph, saying: 'Rise and take the boy and his mother and flee into Egypt and be there until I tell you; for it shall come to pass that Herod will seek the boy to destroy him.' And rising, he took the boy and his mother by night and withdrew into Egypt. And he was there until the death of Herod, that what was said by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: 'Out of Egypt I have called my son*[^42].' *Then Herod, seeing that he had been deceived by the Magi, was greatly angered, and sending out, he killed all the children who were in Bethlehem and in all its borders, from two years old and under, according to the time which he had inquired of the Magi. Then was fulfilled what was said through Jeremiah the prophet, saying: 'A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and much wailing, Rachel weeping for her children and refusing to be consoled, because they are no more*[^43].' *But when Herod had died, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt, saying: 'Rise and take the boy and his mother and go into the land of Israel; for those who sought the boy's life are dead.' And rising, he took the boy and his mother and came into the land of Israel. But hearing that Archelaus reigned in Judea in place of his father Herod, he feared to go there. And being admonished in a dream, he withdrew into the parts of Galilee. And coming, he dwelt in a city which is called Nazareth, that what was said through the Prophets might be fulfilled: 'that he shall be called a Nazarene*[^44].' *And the boy grew and was strengthened, full of wisdom, and the grace of God was in him. And his parents went every year to Jerusalem on the solemn day of the Pasch. And when he had become twelve years old, as they went up to Jerusalem according to the custom of the feast day, and when the days were completed, as they were returning, the boy Jesus remained in Jerusalem, and his parents did not know it. But supposing him to be in the company, they went a day's journey and sought him among their kinsfolk and acquaintances. And not finding him, they returned to Jerusalem, seeking him. And it came to pass, after three days they found him in the temple, sitting in the midst of the teachers, hearing them and questioning them. And all who heard him were astounded at his prudence and his answers. And seeing him, they marveled. And his mother said to him: 'Son, why have you done so to us? Behold, your father and I have been seeking you in sorrow.' And he said to them: 'Why is it that you sought me? Did you not know that I must be about the things that are my Father's?' And they did not understand the word which he spoke to them. And he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was subject to them. And his mother kept all these words in her heart. And Jesus advanced in wisdom and age and grace before God and men*[^45].
