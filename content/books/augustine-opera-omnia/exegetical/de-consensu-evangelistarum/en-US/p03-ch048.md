# How Matthew and Mark are not opposed to John in what is narrated by the three of them about what happened after they had crossed over.

**48.** 102. Matthew continues, saying: *and when they had crossed over, they came to the land of Gennesaret. And when the men of that place recognized him, they sent into all that region and brought to him all who were sick, and begged him that they might only touch the fringe of his garment. And as many as touched it were made well*[^1]. *Then there came to him from Jerusalem scribes and Pharisees, saying: 'Why do your disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? For they do not wash their hands when they eat bread*[^2],' and so on down to where he says: *but to eat with unwashed hands does not defile a man*[^3].

Mark too records these things without any question of contradiction[^4]: for whatever is said in a different manner by another does not depart from the same meaning. John, however, after that boat to which the Lord came walking upon the sea, once they had gone out onto the land, in his usual way, intent on the Lord's discourse, records that he, taking occasion from the bread, spoke many and especially divine things; and after that discourse his narration is borne sublimely on again to one thing after another[^5]. Yet in the fact that he departs from these others, his transition to other matters is in no way opposed to the sequence of theirs. For what prevents us from understanding both that those were healed by the Lord of whom Matthew and Mark narrate, and that to those who followed him across the sea he spoke these things which John proclaims, since indeed Capernaum, to which, according to John, they are said to have crossed over, is near the lake of Gennesaret, to which land, according to Matthew, they are said to have gone out?

[^1]: Mt 14:34–36.
[^2]: Mt 15:1–2.
[^3]: Mt 15:20.
[^4]: See Mk 6:53–56, 7:1–23.
[^5]: See Jn 6:22–72.
