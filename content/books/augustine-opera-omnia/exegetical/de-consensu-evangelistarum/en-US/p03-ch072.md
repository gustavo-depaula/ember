# On the Pharisees sitting on the seat of Moses, and saying what they do not do, and the other things said by the Lord against the same Pharisees.

**75.** 144. Matthew continues, keeping the order of the narration thus: *then Jesus spoke to the crowds and to his disciples, saying: 'On the seat of Moses the scribes and Pharisees have sat down; therefore observe and do all things whatever they tell you; but do not act according to their works, for they say and do not do'*[^1], and so on down to where he says: *you will not see me from now on, until you say: 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^2].' Luke too records that the Lord had a similar discourse against the Pharisees and scribes and doctors of the Law, but in the house of a certain Pharisee who had invited him to a meal[^3]. To narrate which, he had departed from Matthew around that place where both had recorded what was said by the Lord about the sign of Jonah of three days and nights, and about the queen of the South, and about the Ninevites, and about the unclean spirit that returns and finds the house cleansed[^4].

After which discourse Matthew says: *while he was still speaking to the crowds, behold, his mother and his brothers were standing outside, seeking to speak to him*[^5]. Luke, however, in that discourse of the Lord, having recorded certain things too which Matthew omitted as having been said by the Lord, departs from the order[^6] which he had kept with Matthew, thus: *and as he was speaking,* he says, *a certain Pharisee asked him to dine with him, and he went in and reclined. But the Pharisee began to think within himself and to say why he had not been washed before the meal. And the Lord said to him: 'Now you Pharisees cleanse the outside of the cup and the dish*[^7].'

And from here he now says the rest against the same Pharisees and scribes and doctors of the Law, such things as Matthew says in this place which we have now undertaken to consider[^8]. Although, therefore, Matthew records these things in such a way that, though he does not name the house of that Pharisee, neither does he state the place where they were said, so that anything should conflict with that house,

yet because the Lord had already come into Jerusalem from Galilee, and after his arrival the foregoing matters down to this discourse are so woven together that they are probably taken to have been done in Jerusalem, while Luke narrates that while the Lord was still making his way to Jerusalem, it seems to me that there are two similar discourses, of which the one narrated the one and the other narrated the other.

**75.** 145. Surely it must be considered how it was said here: *you will not see me from now on, until you say: 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^9],' when according to the same Matthew they had already said this[^10]. For Luke too says that this was answered by the Lord to those who had warned him to go away from there, since Herod wished to kill him. There too he records that, against Jerusalem itself, those very words were said by him which Matthew has here.

For it is narrated thus according to Luke: *on that very day some of the Pharisees came, saying to him: 'Go out and depart from here, for Herod wishes to kill you.' And he said to them: 'Go, tell that fox: 'Behold, I cast out demons and accomplish cures today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am perfected'; nevertheless I must walk today and tomorrow and the following day, for it cannot be that a prophet perish outside Jerusalem.*

*Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kill the prophets and stone those who are sent to you, how often I have wished to gather your children as a bird gathers her nest under her wings, and you would not! Behold, your house will be left to you. But I say to you that you will not see me until it comes, when you will say: 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord'*[^11].

To this narration of Luke it does not seem opposed that the crowds said, as the Lord came to Jerusalem: *blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^12]; for according to Luke's order he had not yet come there and it had not yet been said. But since he does not say that he then departed from there, so as to come only at the time when this would be said (for he continues on his journey until he comes to Jerusalem,

and what he says: *behold, I cast out demons and accomplish cures today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am perfected*[^13], is understood to have been said by him mystically and figuratively — for he did not suffer on that day which is the third from this day, since he immediately says: *I must walk today and tomorrow and the following day*[^14]), this surely compels us to understand mystically too what he says: *you will not see me until it comes, when you will say: 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^15],'

signifying this of that coming of his by which he is to come in glory, so that what he says: *I cast out demons and accomplish cures today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am perfected,* may be referred to his body, which is the Church. For demons are cast out when the gentiles, having left their ancestral superstitions, believe in him; and cures are accomplished when men live according to his precepts, after they have renounced the devil and this world, until the end of the resurrection, by which, as it were on the *third* day, it will be perfected — that is, the Church will be brought to angelic fullness through the immortality of the body as well. Wherefore this order of Matthew is by no means to be thought to have departed to anything else; but Luke is rather to be understood either to have anticipated what was done in Jerusalem and to have interposed it by way of recollection, before his narration brought the Lord to Jerusalem, or to have related that, as he was now drawing near to that city, he answered such things to those warning him to beware of Herod as Matthew says he spoke even to the crowds, when he had already arrived in Jerusalem and all those things had been done which were narrated above.
