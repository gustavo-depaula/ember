# Where he calls them to take up his yoke and his burden: how Matthew does not differ from Luke in the order of narrating.

**33. 80.** Matthew goes on: *at that time Jesus, answering, said: 'I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent*,' and the rest[^1], up to where he says, *for my yoke is sweet and my burden light*[^2]. This passage Luke too mentions, but in part. For he himself does not say, *Come to me, all you who labor*[^3], and the other things that follow. But it is credible that this was said once by the Lord, and that Luke did not mention the whole of what was said. For Matthew says, *at that time Jesus, answering, said*, after he reproached the cities; but Luke, after that reproach of the cities[^4], interposes certain things, not many, however, and subjoins this thus: *in that very hour he exulted in the Holy Spirit and said*[^5]. Whence, even if Matthew did not say *at that time*, but *in that very hour*, Luke interposes so few things in between that it would not absurdly seem to have been said even in the same hour.

[^1]: Mt 11:25.
[^2]: Mt 11:30.
[^3]: Mt 11:28.
[^4]: See Lk 10:13–20.
[^5]: Lk 10:21.
