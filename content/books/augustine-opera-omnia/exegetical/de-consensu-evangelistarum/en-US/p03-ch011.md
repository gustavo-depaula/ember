# How Luke says, "His parents went every year to Jerusalem on the solemn day of the Pasch" with that boy, when Matthew says that out of fear of Archelaus they feared to go there as they returned from Egypt.

**10. 23.** Or does someone say this too: How, then, as Luke narrates, did his parents go every year of Christ's boyhood to Jerusalem[^1], if out of fear of Archelaus they were prevented from going there? It would not be difficult for me to resolve this, not even if any of the evangelists had expressed how long Archelaus reigned there. For it could happen that, during a feast day, amid so vast a crowd, they would go up secretly, soon to return, where nevertheless on other days they feared to dwell, so that they were neither irreligious by omitting the solemnity nor conspicuous by a continuous stay. But since all are silent even about the reign of Archelaus, how lasting it was, this understanding too lies open, that what Luke says—that they were accustomed to go up every year to Jerusalem—we take to have been done at the time when Archelaus was no longer feared. But if any history besides the Gospel, to which faith should seem due, reveals that the reign of Archelaus was somewhat longer, what I said above will suffice: that the parents of the boy so feared dwelling in Jerusalem that nevertheless, on account of the fear of God, they did not omit the solemn festival, in which they could most easily lie hidden. For it is not incredible that, opportune times of days or hours being seized, men should go to those places in which they dread to be.

[^1]: See Lk 2:41
