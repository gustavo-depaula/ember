# How, according to John the Evangelist, John the Baptist says, "I did not know him," when according to the others it is found that he already knew him.

**15. 32.** But what is said about the dove according to John is not narrated as to when it was done; rather the words of John the Baptist are reported, recalling what he saw. In which it is asked how it was said: *And I did not know him, but he who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me: 'Upon whomever you see the Spirit descending and remaining upon him, this is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*[^1].' For if he came to know him then, when he saw the dove descending upon him, it must be asked how he said to the one coming to be baptized, *I ought rather to be baptized by you*[^2]; for he said this to him before the dove descended. From which it appears that, although he already knew him (for he even leaped in his mother's womb when Mary had come to Elizabeth[^3]), he nevertheless learned something in him, which he did not yet know, by the descent of the dove: namely, that he himself baptizes in the Holy Spirit by a certain proper and divine power, so that no man who had received baptism from God, even if he baptized someone, could say that what he handed on was his own, or that the Holy Spirit was given by himself.

[^1]: Jn 1:33
[^2]: Mt 3:14
[^3]: See Lk 1:41
