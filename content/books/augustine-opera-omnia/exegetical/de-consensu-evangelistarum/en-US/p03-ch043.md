# How Matthew, Mark, and Luke agree among themselves about the words of Herod.

**43. 91.** Matthew goes on: *at that time Herod the tetrarch heard the fame of Jesus and said to his servants: 'This is John the Baptist; he himself has risen from the dead, and therefore powers work in him'*[^1]. Mark says this same thing and in the same way[^2], but not in the same order. For after the Lord sent the disciples, saying to them that they should carry nothing on the way except a staff only, that discourse being ended, as much as was mentioned by him, he subjoined this too, no necessity being made, however, by which we would be compelled to understand that this too was done as a consequence, just as neither did Matthew. For he said *at that time*, not "on that day" or "hour,"

except that Mark says not what Herod said, but: *they said*, he says, *that John the Baptist has risen from the dead*[^3]. But Matthew, about Herod himself: *he said*, he says, *to his servants*. Luke too, keeping the same order of narrating which Mark does, and he too not indeed compelling us to believe that this was also the same order of the things done, mentions this same thing thus: *now Herod the tetrarch heard*, he says, *all the things that were being done by him, and he was perplexed, because it was said by some that John had risen from the dead, but by some that Elijah had appeared, and by others that one of the ancient prophets had risen. And Herod said: 'John I beheaded; but who is this of whom I hear such things?' And he sought to see him*[^4].

In these words Luke too attests to Mark, at least in this, that others said, not Herod, that John had risen from the dead. But because he mentioned Herod perplexed, and afterward set down his words thus, saying, *John I beheaded; but who is this of whom I hear such things?*, it must be understood either that, after this perplexity, he confirmed in his own mind what was being said by others, when he said to his servants, as Matthew narrates: *and he said to his servants: 'This is John the Baptist; he himself has risen from the dead, and therefore powers work in him*[^5],' or that these words are to be pronounced so as to indicate him still perplexed. For if he said, "Is this perhaps," or, "Is this by chance John the Baptist?", there would be no need to admonish anything about the pronunciation, by which he is understood as doubting and perplexed.

Now, because those words are lacking, it can be pronounced in either way: so that we take him either as confirmed and believing, from the words of others, to have said it, or as still, as Luke mentions, perplexed—especially because Mark too, who had said above that it was said by others that John had risen from the dead, nevertheless at the end is not silent that Herod himself said: *this John, whom I beheaded, has risen from the dead*[^6]. Which words likewise can be pronounced in two ways, so that they are understood either of one confirming or of one doubting. But since Luke, after he mentioned this, passes on to something else, those two, Matthew and Mark, narrate, on this occasion, how John was killed by Herod.

[^1]: Mt 14:1–2.
[^2]: See Mk 6:14–16.
[^3]: Mk 6:14.
[^4]: Lk 9:7–9.
[^5]: Mt 14:2.
[^6]: Mk 6:16.
