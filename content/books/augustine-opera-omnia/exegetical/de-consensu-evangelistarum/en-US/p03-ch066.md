# On those who sold and bought being driven out of the temple, how these three are not opposed to John, who says this same thing far elsewhere.

**67.** 129. Matthew continues and says: *and when he had entered Jerusalem, the whole city was stirred, saying: 'Who is this?' And the crowds said: 'This is Jesus the prophet, from Nazareth of Galilee.' And Jesus entered the temple of God and cast out all who were selling and buying in the temple*[^1], and so on down to that place where he says: *but you have made it a den of robbers*[^2]. All record this matter of the crowds of sellers driven out of the temple[^3], but John in a far different order. For after the testimony of John the Baptist concerning Jesus, when he had recorded that he had gone into Galilee, when he turned water into wine, and then, after the mention of a few days at Capernaum, he says that he went up to Jerusalem, when it was the Passover of the Jews, and, having made a whip of cords, drove the sellers out of the temple. Hence it is clear that this was done by the Lord not once but twice, but that the first was recorded by John, this last by the other three.

On the withered fig tree, how Matthew is not opposed to the rest, and especially to Mark, regarding the order of the narration.

**68.** 130. Matthew continues: *and the blind and the lame came to him in the temple, and he healed them. But when the chief priests and scribes saw the wonderful things he did, and the children crying out in the temple and saying: 'Hosanna to the son of David,' they were indignant and said to him: 'Do you hear what these are saying?'*

*But Jesus said to them: 'Yes. Have you never read that 'out of the mouth of infants and nurslings you have perfected praise'?' And leaving them he went out of the city to Bethany and stayed there. But in the morning, returning to the city, he was hungry; and seeing a single fig tree by the road, he came to it and found nothing on it but leaves only, and he said to it: 'May fruit never be born of you forever'; and immediately the fig tree withered. And the disciples, seeing it, marveled, saying: 'How did it wither at once?' But Jesus answered and said to them: 'Truly I say to you, if you have faith and do not waver, you will not only do this to the fig tree, but even if you say to this mountain, 'Take yourself up and cast yourself into the sea,' it will be done; and all things whatever you ask in prayer, believing, you will receive*[^4].'

**68.** 131. This Mark too says consequently, but he does not keep the same order[^5]. For first, what Matthew said — that he entered the temple and cast out the sellers and buyers — Mark does not record, but says that, having looked round at everything, since it was now evening, he went out to Bethany with the twelve, and that on another day, as they were going out from Bethany, he was hungry and cursed the fig tree, which Matthew too records. And the same Mark subjoins that he came to Jerusalem, and when he had entered the temple, he cast out the sellers and buyers, as though it were done on a second day, not the first. But because Matthew connects it thus: *and leaving them he went out of the city to Bethany*[^6], from where, returning to the city in the morning, he records that he cursed the tree, it is more probably believed that Matthew rather kept the order of time regarding the sellers and buyers driven out of the temple.

For when he says: *and leaving them he went out,* who can be understood to have been left, except those with whom he was speaking above, who were indignant that the children were crying out: *hosanna to the son of David?*[^7] Mark therefore omitted what was done on the first day, when he entered the temple, and inserted it by way of recollection, when he had said that he found nothing on the fig tree but leaves[^8], which was done on the second day, as both attest.

But that the disciples marveled that the tree had withered, and that the Lord answered them about faith and about a mountain being moved into the sea, he says was not on that second day, on which he said to the tree: *let no one ever again eat fruit of you forever*[^9], but on the third day. For on that very second day the same Mark recorded the sellers driven out of the temple, which had been done on the first day, and which he had omitted.

On that very second day, then, he says that, evening having come, he went out of the city, and that as he was passing by in the morning the disciples saw the fig tree withered from the roots, and that Peter, recollecting, said to him: *Rabbi, behold, the fig tree which you cursed has withered*[^10], and that then he answered them about the power of faith. Matthew, however, relates this whole matter as if it were done on the second day — that is, both the saying to the tree, *may fruit never be born of you forever*[^11], and that it withered immediately, and that, the disciples seeing this and marveling, that answer about the power of faith was given.

Hence it is understood that Mark indeed recorded on the second day what he had omitted as done on the first day — namely, the sellers and buyers driven out of the temple — whereas Matthew, when he had recorded what was done on the other day concerning the cursed tree, when he was returning in the morning from Bethany to the city, omitted the things which Mark recorded — that he came into the city and went out in the evening, and that in the morning, as they were passing by, the disciples marveled that the tree was withered; and to what had been done on the second day, on which the tree was cursed, he joined what was done on the third day, that the disciples marveled at its withering and heard from the Lord about the power of faith,

joining these things in such a way that, unless we are compelled by Mark's narration to attend to where and what Matthew omitted, it could not be recognized. For when Matthew had said: *and leaving them he went out of the city to Bethany and stayed there. But in the morning, returning to the city, he was hungry, and seeing a single fig tree by the road, he came to it and found nothing but leaves only, and he said to it: 'May fruit never be born of you forever'; and immediately the fig tree withered,*

having omitted the rest that belonged to the same day, he immediately added: *and the disciples, seeing it, marveled, saying: 'How did it wither at once?'*[^12]. What they saw on one day they marveled at on another day. But it is understood that it did not wither then, when they saw it, but immediately when it was cursed. For they saw it not withering but utterly withered, and so understood that it had withered at once at the word of the Lord.
