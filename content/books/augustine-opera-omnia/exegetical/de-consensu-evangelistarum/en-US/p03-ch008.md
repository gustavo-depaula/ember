# About the two Herods.

**7. 20.** But, regarding Herod, it is wont to trouble some that Luke narrated that, in the days of John's baptism, when the Lord too was baptized as a young man, Herod was tetrarch of Galilee[^1], but Matthew says that, when Herod had died, the boy Jesus returned from Egypt[^2]; which both cannot be true, unless it be understood that there were two Herods. And since no one is ignorant that this could have happened, by what blindness do those rave who are more inclined to calumniate the truth of the Gospel than, considering it a little, to understand that two men were called by the same name?—of which thing all things are full of examples. For this later Herod is reported to have been the son of the earlier Herod, just as Archelaus, whom Matthew mentions as having succeeded his father in the kingdom of Judea when he had died; just as Philip, whom Luke intimates to be the brother of the tetrarch Herod and himself tetrarch of Ituraea. For that Herod who sought the life of the boy Christ was a king, but the other Herod, his son, was called not king, but *tetrarch*, which name, derived from a fourth part of a kingdom, sounds out in the prefix.

[^1]: See Lk 3:1-21
[^2]: See Mt 2:19-22
