# On the temptation of Jesus.

**16. 33.** Matthew goes on and says: *Then Jesus was led into the desert by the Spirit, to be tempted by the devil. And when he had fasted forty days and forty nights, afterward he was hungry. And the tempter, coming, said to him: 'If you are the Son of God, say that these stones become loaves.' He, answering, said: 'It is written: not by bread alone does man live, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God'*[^1], and the rest, up to where it is said: *Then the devil left him, and behold, angels came and ministered to him*[^2]. All this Luke narrates similarly, though not in the same order[^3]. Whence it is uncertain which was done first—whether the kingdoms of the earth were first shown to him and afterward he was set on the pinnacle of the temple, or this first and that afterward. Yet it is nothing to the matter, since it is manifest that all was done; and that Luke sets forth the same meanings in other words—is it not always to be remarked how nothing is lost to the truth? But Mark indeed attests that he was tempted in the desert by the devil forty days and nights[^4]; but he is silent about what was said to him or what he answered. Likewise, what Luke passed over, this one did not pass over in silence: that the angels ministered to him. But John passed over this whole passage.

[^1]: See Deut 8:3
[^2]: Mt 4:1-11
[^3]: See Lk 4:1-13
[^4]: See Mk 1:12-13
