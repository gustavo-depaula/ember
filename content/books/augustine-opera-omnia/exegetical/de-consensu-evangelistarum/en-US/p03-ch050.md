# When he fed the crowds with the seven loaves, whether Matthew and Mark agree with each other.

**50.** 104. Matthew continues, narrating thus: *and when Jesus had passed on from there, he came along the Sea of Galilee, and going up the mountain he sat down there. And great crowds came to him, bringing with them the mute, the blind, the lame, the maimed, and many others, and they put them down at his feet, and he healed them, so that the crowds marveled to see the mute speaking, the lame walking, the blind seeing; and they glorified the God of Israel. But Jesus, calling his disciples together, said: 'I have compassion on the crowd, because they have now continued with me three days and have nothing to eat*[^1],' and so on down to where he says: *now those who had eaten were four thousand men, besides children and women*[^2]. This other miracle of the seven loaves and a few fish Mark too records, in almost the same order, except that he interposes what no one else mentions, about the deaf man[^3] whose ears the Lord opened, spitting on his fingers and saying: *Ephphatha, that is, be opened*[^4].

**50.** 105. It will surely not be out of place to point out, in this miracle of the seven loaves which the two evangelists Matthew and Mark recorded[^5], that if some one of them had told it who had not told of those five loaves, he would be thought to contradict the rest. For who would not suppose that it was one and the same event, and that it had not been narrated wholly and truthfully either by him or by the others or by all, but that either he, being mistaken, had recorded seven loaves instead of five, or they five instead of seven, or that both had lied or been deceived by forgetfulness?

One would think the same a contradiction about the twelve baskets and the seven hampers, and the same about the five thousand and the four thousand who were fed. But because those who narrated the miracle of the seven loaves did not pass over in silence that of the five, this troubles no one, and all understand that both happened.

We have said this so that, wherever a similar deed of the Lord is found which in some point seems so to conflict with another evangelist that it cannot be resolved at all, nothing else may be understood than that both happened and that the one was recorded by the one and the other by the other, just as we pointed out concerning those who reclined in groups of a hundred and of fifty; for if we did not find both of these together in one writer, we would suppose that the several writers had said contradictory things.

[^1]: Mt 15:29–32.
[^2]: Mt 15:38.
[^3]: See Mk 7:31–37, 8:1.
[^4]: Mk 7:34.
[^5]: See Mt 14:15; Mk 6:35; Lk 9:12; Jn 6:5.
