# In what order the account of Peter's mother-in-law is narrated.

**21. 51.** Matthew goes on and says: *and when Jesus had come into the house of Peter, he saw his mother-in-law lying and feverish. And he touched her hand, and the fever left her, and she rose and ministered to them*[^1]. When this was done—that is, after what or before what—Matthew did not express.

For it is not necessary that what is narrated after a thing be understood to have been done also after it. Without doubt, however, this one is understood to have recalled what he had earlier omitted.

For Mark narrates it[^2] before he mentions that about the cleansed leper, which he seems to have interposed after the discourse held on the mountain, about which he himself was silent.

And so Luke too narrates about Peter's mother-in-law after this event[^3]—after which Mark too does—before the discourse, which he too interposed as lengthy, which can seem to be the same one which Matthew says was held on the mountain.

But what does it matter who places a thing in what place—whether what he inserts in order, or what, omitted, he recalls, or what, done afterward, he anticipates—provided only that he does not contradict, in narrating the same or other things, either himself or another?

For since it is in no one's power, however well and faithfully the things are known, in what order each one recalls them (for what comes into a man's mind earlier or later is not as we wish, but as it is given),

it is probable enough that each of the evangelists believed he ought to narrate in that order in which God had willed to suggest to his recollection the very things which he was narrating—at least in those matters whose order, whether it be this or that, takes nothing away from the authority and truth of the Gospel.

**21. 52.** But why the Holy Spirit—*distributing his own gifts to each one as he wills*[^4], and therefore also governing and ruling, without doubt, the minds of the saints in recalling what they were to write (for the sake of the Books to be placed on so great a summit of authority)—permitted one to order his narrative thus, another thus, whoever inquires with pious diligence will be able, divinely helped, to find. Yet this is not the task of this work which we have now undertaken.

It is enough that we demonstrate that the evangelists contradict neither themselves nor one another, in whatever order each of them could or wished to narrate either the same or other deeds and sayings of Christ. Wherefore, where the order of times does not appear, it ought to matter nothing to us what order of narrating any of them kept; but where it does appear, if anything troubles us that seems to contradict either itself or another, it must surely be considered and unraveled.

[^1]: Mt 8:14-15
[^2]: See Mk 1:29-31
[^3]: See Lk 4:38-41
[^4]: 1 Cor 12:11
