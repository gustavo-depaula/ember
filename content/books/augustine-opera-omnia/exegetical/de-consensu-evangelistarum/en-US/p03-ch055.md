# That the Lord showed himself to three disciples on the mountain with Moses and Elijah, how the three Evangelists agree with one another.

**56.** 112. Matthew continues: '*truly I say to you, there are some of those standing here who will not taste death until they see the Son of man coming in his kingdom*[^1].' *And after six days Jesus took Peter and James and John his brother, and led them up a high mountain apart*[^2], and so on down to where he says: *tell no one the vision until the Son of man is raised from the dead*[^3]. This vision of the Lord on the mountain before the three disciples, Peter, James, and John, where also the testimony of the Father's voice was borne to him from heaven, is recorded by the three evangelists in the same order and to altogether the same meanings[^4]; but the rest, differing according to those kinds of expression without any diversity of meaning, which we have demonstrated in many places above, can be seen by readers.

**56.** 113. But that Mark says it happened after six days, as Matthew does, while Luke says after eight, if this is a stumbling block to any, they are not to be despised but to be instructed by a reason given[^5]. For when we announce days, saying 'after so many days,' sometimes we do not count in the day on which we are speaking and the day on which the very thing is to be that we are foretelling or promising, but the days in between, after which, fully and entire, that thing is to be.

This Matthew and Mark did: excepting the day on which Jesus was speaking these things and the day on which he showed the vision mentioned on the mountain, and looking to the days in between, they said: *after six days*;

whereas the other, counting in the boundary days, that is, the first and the last, said: *after eight days,* by that manner of speaking in which the part is mentioned for the whole.

**56.** 114. Likewise, what Luke says about Moses and Elijah: *and it happened that, as they were parting from him, Peter said to Jesus: 'Master, it is good for us to be here*[^6],' and so on, is not to be thought contrary to what Matthew and Mark joined together, that Peter suggested this as if Moses and Elijah were still speaking with the Lord[^7]. For they did not expressly state that it was then; rather they were silent about what this writer added, namely, that as those two were parting, Peter suggested to the Lord the making of three tents. Luke also added that, as they entered into the cloud, a voice came from the cloud, which the others did not state, yet neither did they contradict it.

[^2]: Mt 17:1.
[^3]: Mt 17:9.
[^4]: See Mk 8:39, 9:1–9; Lk 9:27–36.
[^5]: See Mk 9:2; Mt 17:1; Lk 9:28.
