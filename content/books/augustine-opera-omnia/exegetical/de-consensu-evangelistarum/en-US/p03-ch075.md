# What Matthew and Mark record two days before the coming Passover.

**78.** 152. Matthew continues: *and it happened, when Jesus had finished all these discourses, that he said to his disciples: 'You know that after two days the Passover will take place, and the Son of man will be handed over to be crucified'*[^1]. To this the other two, Mark and Luke, bear witness, not departing from the same order. Nor do they convey this as said by the Lord; for they omitted to intimate this. Yet in his own person Mark too says: *now the Passover and the unleavened bread were two days off*[^2], and Luke: *now the feast of unleavened bread, which is called the Passover, was drawing near*[^3]. It was, then, drawing near in such a way that it was two days off, as the other two more openly agree. John, however, mentioned the nearness of that same feast in three places, while in the two earlier places he was narrating certain other things, but in the third place his narration appears to be occupied with those very times of which the three others too give intimation, that is, of the Lord's passion now impending[^4].

**78.** 153. But this can seem, to those who look at it without sufficient care, to be contradictory: that Matthew and Mark, after they had said the Passover would be two days off, then recorded that Jesus was in Bethany, where the account of that precious ointment is given[^5], whereas John says that six days before the Passover Jesus came to Bethany, as he is about to narrate the same matter of the ointment[^6].

How, then, according to those two, was the Passover going to be two days off, when, after they had said this, they are found with John in Bethany narrating that matter of the ointment which he too narrates, while he himself says the Passover would be six days off? But those who are thus troubled do not understand that Matthew and Mark set down by recapitulation what had been done in Bethany about the ointment — not after that announcement of theirs about the two days, but as already done before, when there were still six days to the Passover. For neither of them, when he had said the Passover would be two days off, so subjoined the account of that deed in Bethany as to say: 'after these things, when he was in Bethany';

but Matthew indeed says: *now when Jesus was in Bethany*[^7], and Mark: *when he was at Bethany*[^8], which is surely understood to have been even before those things were said which were said two days before the Passover. As therefore is gathered from John's narration, six days before the Passover he came to Bethany; there that supper took place where the mention of the precious ointment is made; from there he came to Jerusalem, sitting on the donkey,

and then afterward are done the things which they narrate as done at Jerusalem after this arrival of his. From that day, therefore, on which he came to Bethany and that matter of the ointment took place, down to the day on which all these things were done and said, we understand — though the evangelists do not record it — that four days were spent, so that the day arrived which the two defined as two days before the Passover. Luke, however, when he says: *the feast of unleavened bread was drawing near*[^9], did not state the two days, but this nearness which he recorded we ought to take as within that interval of two days. John, again, when he says: *the Passover of the Jews was near*[^10], does not wish these two days to be understood, but six days before the Passover.

Accordingly, when he had recorded certain things after this saying, then, now wishing to show how near the Passover was when he had said it, he says: *Jesus therefore, six days before the Passover, came to Bethany, where Lazarus had been dead, whom Jesus raised. And they made him a supper there*[^11]. This is what Matthew and Mark record by recapitulation, when they had already said the Passover would be two days off. By recapitulation, therefore, they return to that day in Bethany which was six days before the Passover, and narrate what John narrates about the supper and the ointment,

from where he was to come to Jerusalem and, when the things narrated there had been done, to arrive at the day which was two days before the Passover. From this point these two digressed, in order to record by recapitulation what had been done before in Bethany about the ointment; and when the narration of this matter is completed, they return again to the point from which they had digressed, that is, to narrate now the discourse of the Lord which he had two days before the Passover.

For if we remove from the middle what they narrated of the deed done in Bethany, digressing from the order to recall and recapitulate it, and weave together the order itself, the discourse runs thus according to Matthew, with the Lord saying: *you know that after two days the Passover will take place, and the Son of man will be handed over to be crucified. Then the chief priests and the elders of the people gathered together in the courtyard of the high priest, who was called Caiaphas. And they took counsel to seize Jesus by guile and to kill him. But they said: 'Not on the feast day, lest perhaps there be an uproar among the people*[^12].' *Then one of the twelve, who is called Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests*[^13], and so on.

For between what was said: *lest there be an uproar among the people,* and what was said: *then one of the twelve, who is called Judas, went,* there was interposed that matter of Bethany which they told by recapitulation[^14]; which we have omitted in order to weave the narration together, so as to convey that the order of times is not contradictory. According to Mark, again, with that same supper at Bethany, which he too interposed by recapitulation, likewise omitted, the order of the narration runs thus: *now the Passover and the unleavened bread were two days off, and the chief priests and the scribes were seeking how they might seize him by guile and kill him. For they said: 'Not on the feast day, lest perhaps there be an uproar of the people*[^15].' *And Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, went to the chief priests, that he might betray him*[^16], and so on.

Here too, between what was said: *lest perhaps there be an uproar of the people,* and what we have joined to it: *and Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve,* there is placed that matter of Bethany which they told by recapitulation. Luke, indeed, omitted the very event done in Bethany[^17]. We have said these things on account of the six days before the Passover which John mentioned[^18] when he narrated the event done in Bethany, and the two days before the Passover which Matthew and Mark mentioned, when, after this saying of theirs, they recorded that very thing in Bethany which John records.
