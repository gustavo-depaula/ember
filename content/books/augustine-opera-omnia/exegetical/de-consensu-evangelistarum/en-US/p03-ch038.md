# Where it was also said that he casts out demons by Beelzebul; whatever, on that occasion, he said about the blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, and about the two trees.

**38. 85.** Matthew goes on, saying: *and all the crowds were astounded and said: 'Is this not the son of David?' But the Pharisees, hearing it, said: 'This man does not cast out demons except by Beelzebul, the prince of demons.' But Jesus, knowing their thoughts, said to them: 'Every kingdom divided against itself shall be brought to desolation'*[^1] and the rest, up to where he says, *by your words you shall be justified, and by your words you shall be condemned*[^2]. Mark does not subjoin, on the occasion of that mute man, what was said about Jesus, that he *casts out demons by Beelzebul*, but after certain other things which he alone mentions, he too connects this—either recalling it elsewhere and adding it, or passing over something and then returning to this order[^3]. But Luke says, in almost the same words, what Matthew says; and that he calls the *Spirit of God* the *finger of God*, he does not depart from the same meaning[^4]—nay rather, he even teaches something, so that we may know how we are to understand, wherever in the Scriptures we read the *finger of God*. But in the other things which Mark and Luke do not here say, there is no controversy, nor in those which they say somewhat otherwise, because the meaning is the same.

[^1]: Mt 12:23–25.
[^2]: Mt 12:37.
[^3]: See Mk 3:22–30.
[^4]: See Lk 11:14–26.
