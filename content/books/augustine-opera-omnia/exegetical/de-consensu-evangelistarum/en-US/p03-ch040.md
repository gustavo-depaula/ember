# Where his mother and brothers were announced to him: whether the order itself does not differ from Mark and Luke.

**40. 87.** Matthew, then, goes on and says: *while he was still speaking to the crowds, behold, his mother and brothers were standing outside, seeking to speak to him*[^1], and the rest, up to where he says, *for whoever does the will of my Father who is in heaven, he is my brother and sister and mother*[^2]. This without doubt we ought to understand as done as a consequence.

For he premised, as he passed to narrating this: *while he was still speaking to the crowds*. But what is *still* except that which he was speaking? For he did not say, "and as he was speaking to the crowds, behold, his mother and brothers," but, *while he was still speaking*: which words, when he says them, compel us to understand, as he was speaking these things which he was indicating above. For Mark too, after what he reported the Lord said about the blasphemy of the Holy Spirit, *and there come*, he says, *his mother and brothers*[^3],

certain things being passed over which, in the same text of the Lord's discourse, both Matthew set down more at length than Mark, and Luke more at length than Matthew. But Luke did not keep the order of this thing done, but anticipated it and narrated it earlier, recalling it[^4].

In short, he interposed this so that it appears loosed from the connection both of the foregoing and of the following. For after he had mentioned certain parables of the Lord, he thus interposed this, recalled, about his mother and brothers: *now there came to him*, he says, *both his mother and his brothers, and they could not reach him because of the crowd*[^5]: he did not express when they came to him; again, when he passes on from here too, he speaks thus: *now it came to pass*, he says, *on one of the days, and he himself went up into a boat, and his disciples*[^6]. And here too, when he says, *it came to pass on one of the days*, he sufficiently indicates that it is not necessary that this day be understood as the one on which this was done, or that one which immediately follows. And so what Matthew narrates about the mother and brothers of the Lord has nothing of contradiction with the other two, neither in the words of the Lord, nor in the very order of the things done.

[^1]: Mt 12:46.
[^2]: Mt 12:50.
[^3]: Mk 3:31–35.
[^4]: See Lk 8:19–21.
[^5]: Lk 8:19.
[^6]: Lk 8:22.
