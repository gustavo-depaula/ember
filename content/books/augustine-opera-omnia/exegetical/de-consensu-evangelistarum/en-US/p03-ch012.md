# How they could, when the days of the purification of Christ's mother were completed, as Luke says, go up with him into the temple to perform the solemnities.

**11. 24.** Hence too that question is resolved, if it troubles anyone: since that elder Herod was already anxious, struck by the report of the Magi that a king of the Jews had been born[^1], how could they, when the days of his mother's purification were completed, go up safely with him into the temple, that there might be done concerning him, according to the Law of the Lord[^2], the things which Luke mentions? For who does not see that even that one day could lie hidden from a king occupied with many things? But if that does not seem probable—that Herod, who, very anxious, was awaiting what the Magi might report to him about the boy[^3], after so many days perceived that he had been deceived, so that, the time of his mother's purification having passed and the solemnity of the firstborn having been performed concerning the infant in the temple of Jerusalem, after even their departure into Egypt, it then came into his mind to seek the boy's life and to kill so many little ones—if, then, this troubles someone, I forbear to say with how many and how great occupations the care of a king could be distended, and turned aside altogether, or impeded, from that intention for very many days. For the causes by which this could have happened cannot be enumerated; yet no one is so inexperienced in human affairs as to deny or doubt that they could have been many and great. For to whose thought would it not occur how many other more terrible things, whether true or false, could be reported to a king, so that he who had feared the infant king as one who would, after some years, oppose him or his sons, agitated by the terrors of certain nearer dangers, would occupy his mind, snatched from that care, rather in guarding against other things close at hand? So then, to forbear these things, I say this: after the Magi reported nothing to Herod, he could believe that they had been deceived by the vision of a deceptive star, and that after they did not find the one whom they had thought born, they were ashamed to return to him, and thus, with his fear dispelled, he rested from inquiring after and persecuting the boy. When, therefore, after the purification of his mother, they had come with him to Jerusalem, and those things had been done in the temple which are narrated by Luke[^4]—because the words of Simeon and Anna prophesying about him, when they had begun to be proclaimed by those who had heard them, were about to recall the king's mind to its former intention—Joseph, admonished through a dream, fled with the infant and his mother into Egypt[^5]. Then, the things which had been done and said in the temple being divulged, Herod perceived that he had been deceived by the Magi; and then, desiring to bring about Christ's death, he killed many infants, as Matthew narrates.

[^1]: See Mt 2:3-16
[^2]: See Lk 2:22
[^3]: See Mt 2:8
[^4]: See Lk 2:22-39
[^5]: See Mt 2:3-16
