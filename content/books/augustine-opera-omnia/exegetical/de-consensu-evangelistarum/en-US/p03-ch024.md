# On his crossing of the strait, where he slept in the boat, and on the cast-out demons, whom he allowed to go into the swine: how the things done or said agree among Matthew, Mark, and Luke.

**24. 55.** *And as he went up into the boat, his disciples followed him. And behold, a great commotion arose on the sea*, up to where he says, *and he came into his own city*[^1]. These two things, narrated continuously by Matthew—about the sea made calm after Jesus, roused from sleep, commanded the winds, and about those who had a fierce demon and, their bonds broken, were driven into the desert—Mark and Luke narrate similarly[^2].

Certain sentences were spoken in different words by one and another, yet not different sentences—as that which Matthew says he said, *Why are you fearful, you of little faith?* Mark says it thus: *Why are you fearful? Do you not yet have faith?*[^3]

that is, that perfect faith, like the grain of mustard seed. This, then, is what the other too says, *of little faith*. But Luke: *Where is your faith?*[^4]

And the whole could indeed be said: "Why are you fearful? Where is your faith? You of little faith." Whence one mentions one thing here, another another. And as for what they said when they roused him,

Matthew thus: *Lord, save us, we are perishing*; Mark: *Master, does it not concern you that we are perishing?* Luke: *Teacher, we are perishing*[^5]: it is one and the same meaning of those rousing the Lord and wishing to be saved, nor is there need to ask which of these was rather said to Christ.

For whether they said one of these three, or other words which no evangelist mentioned but which nevertheless mean just as much toward the same truth of the meaning, what does it matter to the matter? Although this too could have happened: that, several rousing him at the same time, all these things were said, one by one and another by another.

Likewise, what they said when the storm was stilled, according to Matthew: *What kind of man is this, that the winds and the sea obey him?*[^6] According to Mark: *Who, do you think, is this, that the wind and the sea obey him?*[^7] According to Luke: *Who, do you think, is this, that he commands both the winds and the sea, and they obey him?*[^8] Who does not see that it is one meaning? For *Who, do you think, is this?* and *What kind of man is this?* mean just as much; and where *he commands* is not said, it is surely understood as a consequence, because the one who commands is obeyed.

**24. 56.** But as for the fact that Matthew says there were two who suffered that legion of demons which was allowed to go into the swine, while Mark and Luke mention one[^9], you may understand that one of them was a person of some more illustrious and famous standing, for whom that region especially grieved and for whose salvation it was most busy.

Wishing to signify this, two evangelists judged that he alone was to be mentioned, about whom the fame of this deed had spread more widely and more brilliantly. Nor does the fact that the words of the demons are stated differently by the evangelists have any difficulty, since they can either be reduced to one meaning or be understood as all said.

Nor that in Matthew he speaks in the plural, but in them in the singular, since they too narrate that, when asked what he was called, he answered that he was *legion*, because the demons were many. Nor that Mark says the herd of swine was around the mountain, but Luke on the mountain, contradicts at all. For the herd of swine was so great that some of it was on the mountain, some around the mountain. For there were two thousand swine, as Mark expressed.
