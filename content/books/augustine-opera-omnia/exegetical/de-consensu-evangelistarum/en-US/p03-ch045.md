# To the miracle of the five loaves: in what order by all, and how it was come to.

**45. 93.** Matthew, then, goes on, when he had said that it was announced to Christ that John was killed, and thus weaves his narrative: *and when Jesus had heard it*, he says, *he withdrew from there in a boat into a desert place apart. And when the crowds had heard it, they followed him on foot from the cities. And going out, he saw a great crowd and had pity on it and cured their sick*[^1]. He mentions that this was done immediately after the passion of John. Whence, after these things, were done those things which were narrated first, by which Herod, moved, said: *John I beheaded*. For those latter things must be understood, which the fame carried to Herod, so that he was moved and perplexed who this could be of whom he heard such things, when he himself had killed John.

But Mark, after he narrated the passion of John, mentions that the disciples who were sent returned to Jesus and reported to him all the things they had done and taught, and that the Lord said to them (which he alone mentions) that they should rest a little in the desert, and that he went up with them into a boat and went, and that the crowds, seeing this, came there before them, on whom the Lord had pity and taught them many things; and that, the hour now advancing, it came about that from five loaves and two fishes all who were present were fed[^2]. Which miracle all four evangelists mentioned. Luke too, who had already narrated, far above, about John's passion[^3], on the occasion which we have just spoken of, after he mentioned that perplexity of Herod about the Lord, who he might be, immediately subjoins this which Mark does—that is, that the apostles returned to him and narrated to him whatever they had done, and that, taking them, he withdrew into a desert place, and that the crowds followed him there, and that he spoke about the kingdom of God and healed those who needed cure[^4]. And thence he too, the day declining, mentions the miracle done about the five loaves.

**45. 94.** But John, who is far distant from those three evangelists in this, that he dwells more on the discourses which the Lord held than on the deeds which he marvelously did, after he mentioned that, leaving Judea, he went again into Galilee[^5]—which is understood to have been done then, when the other evangelists too say that, John being handed over, he went into Galilee[^6]—

after, then, John mentioned this, on his passage through Samaria he wove into his narrative many things which he spoke on the occasion of that Samaritan woman whom he found at the well[^7], and after two days he says that he went out thence into Galilee, then came into Cana of Galilee, where he had made wine from water, and healed the son of a certain royal official[^8]. But the other things which the others said he did and said in Galilee, John is silent about; but indeed, what they were silent about, he says: that he went up at a feast day to Jerusalem and did there that miracle about the man who had been thirty-eight years in infirmity and had no man by whom he might be put down into the pool, in which those affected with various ailments were healed; and on this occasion he mentions that he spoke many things[^9].

After these things he says that he went away across the sea of Galilee, which is of Tiberias, and that a great multitude followed; then that he went up onto the mountain and there sat with his disciples, the Pasch, the feast day of the Jews, being near; then, his eyes lifted up and a very great multitude seen, that he fed it with five loaves and two fishes, which the other evangelists too say[^10].

And for this reason it is certain that he passed over the things by which those, in narrating, came to the mention of this miracle; nevertheless, as it were from another road of narration—since they too were silent about what this one said—both those three, who walked almost alongside one another, and this one, who, pursuing the heights of the Lord's discourses, flew round about, in a manner, through other things which they were silent about, met one another at this miracle about the five loaves, to be mentioned alike, this one about to fly back from them, not long after, into higher things.
