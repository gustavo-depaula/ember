# Where he spoke to them about the coming of Elijah, what agreement there is between Matthew and Mark.

**57.** 115. Matthew continues: *and the disciples asked him, saying: 'Why then do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?' And he answered and said to them: 'Elijah indeed is to come and will restore all things; but I say to you that Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but did to him whatever they wished; so also the Son of man is to suffer at their hands.' Then the disciples understood that he had spoken to them about John the Baptist*[^1]. This same thing Mark too records, preserving this order, and with some diversity of words. Nowhere, however, did he depart from the truth of the same meaning[^2]; but he did not add that the disciples understood that the Lord had meant John in saying that Elijah had already come.

[^1]: Mt 17:10–13.
[^2]: See Mk 9:10–12.
