# It pertains to justice that an animal harmful to men be destroyed.

**81.** (21, 28) *But if an ox gores a man or a woman with its horn, and he dies, the ox shall be stoned with stones, and its flesh shall not be eaten; but the owner of the ox shall be innocent.* It pertains to justice that an animal harmful to men be destroyed; and what is set down about the ox is to be understood as the whole from a part—whatever among the animals subject to human use is dangerous to men. But if it is necessary that it be killed, why stoned? For what difference does it make to an animal, which is to be removed, by what death it is removed? Then what he added, that its flesh is not to be eaten, to what does it pertain, unless all these things signify something, which Scripture is especially accustomed to regard?
