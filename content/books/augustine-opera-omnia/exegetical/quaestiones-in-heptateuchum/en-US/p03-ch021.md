# On the words: And the rod of Aaron swallowed up their rods.

**21.** (7:12) *And the rod of Aaron swallowed up their rods*. If it had been said, "the serpent of Aaron swallowed up their rods," it would be understood that the true serpent of Aaron did not swallow up those phantasmal figments, but rods. For it could swallow up that which they were, not that which they seemed to be and were not. But since he said, *the rod of Aaron swallowed up their rods*; surely a serpent could swallow rods, not a rod. But the thing was called by the name from which it was changed, not into which it was changed, because it was changed back into it too: and therefore it had to be called that which it principally was.

What, then, is to be said of the rods of the magicians? Whether they too had been made true serpents, but were called rods by the same reasoning as the rod of Aaron? Or did they rather seem to be what they were not, by sorcerous trickery? Why, then, on both sides are they called both rods and serpents, so that as regards those figments the manner of speaking makes no difference? But it is difficult to show how, even if true serpents were made from the rods of the magicians, neither the magicians nor the evil angels by whose ministry those things were worked were yet creators of serpents. For there are present in corporeal things, throughout all the elements of the world, certain hidden seminal reasons, by which, when a temporal and causal opportunity has been given, they burst forth into the forms due to them, in their own modes and limits.

And so the angels who do these things are not called creators of the animals; just as farmers are not to be called creators of crops or trees or of any growing things on the earth, although they know how to provide certain visible opportunities and causes for these things to be born. But what these do visibly, the angels do invisibly; God alone, however, is the one Creator, who sowed in things the causes themselves and the seminal reasons. The matter has been briefly stated, which, if it were explained by examples and copious discussion, so as to be more easily understood, would require a long discourse, from which the reason of our haste excuses us.
