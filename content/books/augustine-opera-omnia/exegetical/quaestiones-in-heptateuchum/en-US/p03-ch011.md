# Whether the angel wished to kill Moses.

**11.** (4:24-26) As to what is written: *and it happened, on the way at the lodging place, that an angel met him, and sought to kill him; and Zipporah, taking a flint, circumcised the foreskin of her son; and she fell at his feet, and said: 'The blood of my child's circumcision has stood.' And he departed from him; on account of which she said: 'The blood of the circumcision has ceased'*; first it is asked whom the angel wished to kill, whether Moses, because it was said, *an angel met him, and sought to kill him*. For whom will he be thought to have met, except him who was at the head of the whole company of his people, and by whom the rest were led? Or did he seek to kill the boy, whom his mother aided by circumcising him; so that hence it is understood that he wished to kill the infant because he was not circumcised, and thus to ratify the precept of circumcision by the severity of the punishment?

But if this is so, it is uncertain about whom he first said, *he sought to kill him*; for it is not known whom, unless it is found from what follows: by a manner of speech indeed marvelous and unusual, that he should first say, *he met him, and sought to kill him*, about one of whom he had said nothing before.

But such is the case in the Psalm: *its foundations are in the holy mountains; the Lord loves the gates of Zion*[^1]. For thence the *Psalm* begins, nor had it said anything about that one—masculine or feminine—whose foundations he wished to be understood, saying, *its foundations are in the holy mountains*. But because there follows, *the Lord loves the gates of Zion*, the foundations are therefore either of the Lord or of Zion, and for an easier sense rather of Zion, so that the foundations of the city are understood. But because in this pronoun, which is *its*, the gender is ambiguous (for this pronoun is of every gender, that is, masculine, feminine, and neuter), but in Greek it is said in the feminine gender one way, in the masculine and neuter another, and the Greek manuscript has the masculine/neuter form; it compels us to understand not the foundations of Zion, but the foundations of the Lord, that is, those which the Lord established, of whom it was said: *the Lord building Jerusalem*[^2]. Yet he had named neither Zion nor the Lord before, when he said, *its foundations are in the holy mountains*: so here too, the infant not yet being named, it was said, *he met him, and sought to kill him*; so that whom he meant we recognize in what follows.

Although, even if anyone should wish to take it of Moses, it is not greatly to be resisted. Rather let that which follows be understood, if it can be: what it means that the angel therefore departed from killing any of them, because the woman said, *the blood of the infant's circumcision has stood*. For he did not say, "*he departed from him*, because she circumcised the infant," but "because *the blood of the circumcision stood*"; not because it ran, but because it stood: by a great sacrament, unless I am mistaken.

[^1]: Ps 87:1-2 (86:1-2).
[^2]: Ps 147:2 (146:2).
