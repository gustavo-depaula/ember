# What is the reason for the order in the words: We will do and we will hear.

**96.** (24, 3) It is to be noted that the people again answers thus: *all the words which the Lord has spoken we will do, and we will hear;* when the order would seem to require that it be said, "we will hear, and we will do." But it would be strange if some sense did not lie hidden here. For if *we will hear* is put for "we will understand," it is fitting first to render to the words of God the service of doing, so that to the understanding of those things which are done at his command he may himself lead them, by the merit of the devotion by which the commands were not despised, but done. But it is to be seen whether this people is found like that son who said to his commanding father: *I will go into the vineyard, and went not*[^1]. For the nations, which utterly despised the Lord, afterward justified through the obedience of one—those who did not pursue righteousness—*laid hold of righteousness*[^2].

[^1]: Matt 21:30.
[^2]: Rom 9:30.
