# God threatens Pharaoh that he will make great hail.

**33.** (9:19) What does it mean that God commanded Pharaoh, when he threatened that he would make great hail, to hasten to gather his flocks, and whatever he had in the field, lest they perish by the hail? For this seems to be an admonition not so much of indignation as of mercy. But this makes no question, since God, even when angry, tempers the punishment. That is what rightly raises the question: which flocks are now provided for, if all had died in the earlier plague, where it is written that God made a distinction between the flocks of the Hebrews and of the Egyptians, so that of the former none died, but all the flocks of the Egyptians died[^1]. Or is the question solved thus: that he had foretold that those would die which were in the field[^2], so that these are taken as *all*; but those that were in the houses are understood to have escaped, which even by the doubtful could be gathered and kept in the house, lest perhaps what Moses had foretold the Lord would do should be true: and of these there could again be some in the fields, which he now admonishes to be gathered into the houses, lest they perish by the hail; especially because Scripture follows and says, *he among Pharaoh's servants who feared the word of the Lord gathered his flocks into the houses; but he who did not attend in mind to the word of the Lord left his flocks in the field*[^3]? This, then, could happen, when God threatened the death of the flocks too, although Scripture was silent about it.

[^1]: See Exod 9:6.
[^2]: See Exod 9:3.
[^3]: Exod 9:20-21.
