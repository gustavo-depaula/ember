# What "and they shall be arches for the carrying-poles" is.

**132.** (30, 4) *And they shall be arches for the carrying-poles, so that it may be lifted by them.* What he had called *rings,* he calls *arches.* For he put *rings* for "round handles." And what else is a ring or circle, but an arch on every side? Therefore some, unwilling to say *arch,* rendered it *sheaths,* into which the carrying-poles would be inserted, saying: *and there shall be sheaths for the carrying-poles;* as though the Greek could not say this, since *sheaths* too is a Greek word: but he said *ψαλίδες,* which is rendered *arches.*
