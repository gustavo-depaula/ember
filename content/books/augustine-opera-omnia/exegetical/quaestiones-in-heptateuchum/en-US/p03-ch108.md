# How, and in what moral signification, hair-cloth coverings are commanded to be made over the tabernacle.

**108.** (26, 7) *And you shall make hair-cloth coverings to cover over the tabernacle; eleven coverings you shall make of them.* These hair-cloth coverings—that is, of sackcloth—are said to be eleven. For in sins there is transgression. And transgression is signified by the number eleven, since it transgresses ten, that is, the Law[^1]: therefore these eleven, multiplied by seven, make seventy-seven; where the Lord signified the universal remission of sins, saying: *not only seven times, but even seventy times seven*[^2]: as many generations as are found when Luke, counting from the Lord's Baptism, ascends upward, and arrives through Adam all the way to God[^3]. For the signification of sins is made in these coverings to this end, that through confession they may be expressed, and through the grace that has been given to the Church may be abolished, that is, covered: whence it is said: *blessed are those whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered*[^4]. Then he orders these coverings to be covered with rams' skins dyed red. And a ram dyed red—to whom does Christ not occur, made bloody by his Passion? By these too the holy martyrs are signified, by whose prayers God is made propitious to the sins of his people. Finally, hyacinth-colored skins are thrown over these, that eternal life may be signified by greenness, as by a perpetual vigor.

[^1]: See Gal 3:19.
[^2]: Matt 18:22.
[^3]: See Luke 3:23-38.
[^4]: Ps 32:1 (31).
