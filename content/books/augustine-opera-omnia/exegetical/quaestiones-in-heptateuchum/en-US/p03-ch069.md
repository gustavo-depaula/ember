# Jethro removed grave and dangerous occupations from Moses himself.

**69.** (18, 19-21) But what he further adds, saying: *be you for the people the things that pertain to God, and you shall bring their words to God; and you shall testify to them the precepts of God and his Law; and you shall show them the ways in which they are to walk in them, and the works which they are to do;* this he shows is to be done with the whole people. For he does not say: "bring the words of each one to God," but *their words,* when he had said above: *be you for the people the things that pertain to God.* After these things he admonishes that the affairs of individuals, which they have among themselves, not be neglected—powerful men, namely, having been chosen, men worshiping God, just, and who hate pride, whom he should set, some over thousands, others over hundreds, others over fifties, others over tens.

Thus he both removed grave and dangerous occupations from Moses himself, and did not burden these men. For the thousand themselves would have one over them, and under him others—ten; and under these others—twenty; and under these others—a hundred; so that scarcely anything would reach each of the individual overseers which they would need to judge. Here also an example of humility is suggested, in that Moses, with whom God spoke, did not disdain nor despise the counsel of his foreign father-in-law.

Although Jethro himself, since he was not an Israelite, is rightly asked whether he is to be counted among the men who worshiped the true God and were religiously wise—as Job too, since he was not from that people—rather, it is held the more credible. For the words are ambiguously set down, both as to whether he sacrificed to the true God among his people when he saw his son-in-law, and as to whether Moses himself worshiped him:

although, concerning the act of worship, even if it had been expressly stated, it would seem an honor rendered to the father-in-law, in the manner accustomed to be shown to men by the patriarchs for honor's sake; as it is written of Abraham that he bowed down to the sons of Heth[^1]. But whom he means by *γραμματοεισαγωγούς* after the "captains of tens" cannot easily be known, since we have this name in no use, either of offices or of teaching positions. For some have rendered it *teachers,* so that those of letters are understood, who introduce people to letters, as the Greek word sounds.

Here indeed it is signified that the Hebrews had letters before the Law was given: when these began to exist, I do not know whether it can be investigated. For to some it seems they began with the first men, and were carried down to Noah, and from there to the parents of Abraham, and from there to the people of Israel; but how this can be proved, I do not know.

[^1]: See Gen 23:7.
