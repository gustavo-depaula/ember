# How a lamb is called male, since every lamb is male.

**42.** (12, 5) *The lamb shall be for you unblemished, male, a yearling.* This expression can trouble one who does not know by what necessity it was so rendered, as though a lamb could be other than male. For "sheep" ought to have been the rendering, since the Greek has *probaton*; but in the Greek language *probaton* is of neuter gender, and all that follows could agree with it, as if he said: "the animal shall be for you unblemished, male, a yearling." For it could be said in Latin, "a male animal" (*masculum pecus*), just as one says "male frankincense" (*mascula thura*) in the neuter gender; but a "male sheep" could not be said, because "sheep" (*ovis*) is of feminine gender.

Likewise, if "a male sheep" (*ovis mascula*) were said, it would be more absurd still. But if "animal" (*pecus*) were used, something else too would be understood, and the mystery would not be preserved—the mystery whereby, though Scripture speaks of a sheep, it afterward says, *you shall take it from the lambs and the kids.*

In this matter Christ is rightly understood to be signified. For what need was there to direct that the sheep or lamb be taken from the lambs and the kids, unless he was being prefigured whose flesh has been propagated not only from the righteous but also from sinners? Although the Jews try to understand that a kid too is to be taken for celebrating the Passover, and they think this is the meaning of *to take from the lambs and the kids,* as if he said that either a lamb is to be taken from lambs or a kid from kids, if the one be lacking—yet, now that these things have been fulfilled in Christ, it is plain what was prefigured by that precept.
