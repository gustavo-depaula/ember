# On the words: Go, go up from here, you and your people whom you led out of the land of Egypt.

**149.** (33, 1) *Go up from here, you and your people, whom you led out of the land of Egypt.* God, angry, seems to say, *you and your people, whom you led out;* otherwise he would have said, "you and my people, whom I led out of the land of Egypt": but they, when they demanded the idol, spoke thus: *for this Moses, the man who led us out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him*[^1]; they had set their liberation in a man whom they were establishing. This is now thrown back at them when it is said, *you and your people, whom you led out of the land of Egypt*: which is a charge against them, not against Moses. For Moses wished nothing other than that they should set their hope not in him, but in the Lord, and should believe themselves freed from that slavery by the Lord's mercy, with thanksgiving: whose merit, however, with God, as of a most faithful servant, was so great through his grace, that God said to him: *let me alone, and in the anger of my wrath I will destroy them*[^2].

But whether this belongs to one commanding when he says, *let me alone,* or as it were to one asking, both seem absurd. For if God was commanding, the servant disobediently did not obey; and it was not fitting for God to ask this of a servant as for a favor, especially since he could destroy them even against his will. So that sense there is at hand, that by these words God signified that this people availed very much with him, because they were so loved by that man whom the Lord so loved; so that we might be admonished in this way, that when our own merits weigh us down so that we are not loved by God, we can be relieved before him by the merits of those whom he loves.

For when it is said by the Almighty to a man: *let me alone, and I will destroy them,* what else is said but: "I would destroy them, unless they were loved by you"? So, then, *let me alone* was said, as if it were said: "do not love them, *and I will destroy them;* for your love toward them intercedes with me, lest I do it." But the Lord saying, "do not love them," would have to be obeyed, if he had said this by commanding, and not rather by admonishing and expressing what recalled him from their punishment: nor yet, even with him interceding, did he leave the people without the scourge of discipline. For somehow, in order that Moses himself might so love them, God loved them more secretly, who openly terrified them by his voice.

[^1]: Ex 32:1, 23.
[^2]: Ex 32:10.
