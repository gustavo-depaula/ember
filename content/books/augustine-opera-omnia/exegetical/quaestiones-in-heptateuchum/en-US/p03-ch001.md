# On the lie of the midwives, by which they deceived Pharaoh.

**1.** (1:19-20) On the lie of the midwives, by which they deceived Pharaoh, so as not to kill the male Israelites when they were born, saying that the Hebrew women did not give birth as the Egyptians gave birth, it is commonly asked whether such lies have been approved by divine authority, since it is written that God did good to the midwives: but whether he was pardoning the lie out of mercy, or whether he judged the lie itself worthy of reward, is uncertain. For the midwives did one thing in keeping the little infants alive, another in lying to Pharaoh: for in keeping them alive there was a work of mercy; but that lie they used for themselves, lest Pharaoh harm them, which could pertain not to praise but to pardon. Nor does it seem to me that from this an authority for lying is set before those of whom it was said, *and no lie was found in their mouth*[^1]. For the life of certain people, far inferior to the profession of saints, if it has these sins of lies, is borne with by their very advancement and disposition, especially if they do not yet know how to await the divine heavenly benefits, but are occupied about earthly things. But those who so live that their manner of life, as the Apostle says, is in heaven[^2], I do not think ought to form the measure of their tongue—as far as concerns bringing forth truth and avoiding falsehood—by that example of the midwives. But this question must be discussed more carefully, on account of other examples that are found in the Scriptures.

[^1]: Rev 14:5.
[^2]: See Phil 3:20.
