# Jethro gives counsel to his son-in-law Moses.

**68.** (18, 18-19) In the matter of Jethro giving counsel to his son-in-law Moses, lest, occupied with the people's judgments, both he and the people be worn out with an unbearable wearing-out, the first question is why God allowed his servant—with whom he himself spoke such great and such things—to be admonished by a foreigner. In which Scripture admonishes us that the counsel of truth, by whatever man it be given, ought not to be despised. It must also be considered whether perhaps God willed Moses to be admonished by a foreigner in a matter in which pride might also tempt him: for he sat alone in judicial eminence, while the whole people stood. For this sense is indicated, since Jethro himself ordered that those be chosen to judge the people's cases who hated pride[^1]. Then how much it is to be observed what Scripture says elsewhere: *my son, let not your activities be in many things*[^2]—this appears well enough here too. Then the words of Jethro giving counsel to Moses are to be considered: for he says: *now therefore listen to me, and I will give you counsel, and God will be with you.* Here it seems to me to be signified that a mind too intent on human activities is in a manner emptied of God—whereby it becomes the fuller, the more freely it is stretched out toward heavenly and eternal things.

[^1]: See Exod 18:21.
[^2]: Sir 11:10.
