# That the spirit of understanding is meant, and what is understood by the robes with the horns.

**114.** 1. (28, 3) *And you, speak to all the wise in mind, whom I have filled with the spirit of understanding*: indeed the Greek has *aistheseos,* which in Latin we are accustomed to call *sense,* not "understanding"; but Scripture is accustomed to speak thus of the interior sense, which we call understanding, as it is in *Hebrews*: *but solid food is for the perfect, for those who by habit have their senses exercised to discern good and evil*[^1]. For there, where it put *senses,* the Greek has *aisthesis.* What spirit, then, ought we to take this to be, but the Holy Spirit?

**114.** 2. (28, 4) *And the robes which they shall make, the breastpiece, the shoulder-piece, and the ankle-length tunic, and the tunic with clusters.* It is to be noted that these are called robes, and the rest, when above he had proposed that one robe be made. But the Latin translators thought it more decent to say *tunic with clusters* than if they said *with tassels,* which, when well arranged, are accustomed to be an ornament to garments.

[^1]: Heb 5:14.
