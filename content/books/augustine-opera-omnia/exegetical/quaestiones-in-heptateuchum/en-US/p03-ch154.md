# On the words: Show me your glory, and: I will pass before you in my glory.

**154.** 1. (33, 14-23) When Moses had said to the Lord: *show me your glory;* the Lord answered him: *I will pass before you in my glory; and I will call by the name of the Lord in your sight; and I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will show mercy to whom I will have shown mercy*: when a little before he had said: *I myself will go before you, and will give you rest;* which Moses seems to have taken thus: *I will go before you,* as though not present to that people who would be on the journey: and therefore he says: *if you yourself do not come with us, do not lead me out from here,* etc. But God did not deny him this, saying: *this word too which you have spoken I will do.*

How, then, when Moses had said to him: *show me your glory,* does he again, as though about to go before and not to be with them, seem to say: *I will pass before you,* unless because this is something else? For he is understood to speak and say: *I will pass before you,* of whom the Gospel says: *when the hour had come that Jesus should pass from this world to the Father*[^1]: which passing is also said to be interpreted *Pascha.* This, therefore, is altogether a great prophecy. For he himself passes before all the saints to the Father from this world, to prepare for them the dwellings of the kingdom of heaven, which he will give them in the resurrection of the dead; since, about to pass before all, he was made *the firstborn from the dead*[^2].

**154.** 2. But he greatly commends his grace in that very thing when he says: *and I will call by the name of the Lord in your sight*: as though in the sight of the people of Israel, whose type Moses bore when he heard these things. For in the sight of that nation itself, dispersed everywhere, the Lord Christ is called upon among all nations. But he said *I will call,* not "I will be called," putting the active verb for the passive, by an unusual manner of speech; in which surely a great meaning lies hidden. For thus perhaps he wished to signify that he himself does this, that is, that by his grace it comes about, that the Lord is called upon among all nations.

**154.** 3. But what he added: *and I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will show mercy to whom I will have shown mercy;* there he plainly shows more expressly the calling by which he called us into his kingdom and glory: not for our merits, but for his mercy. For since he was promising that he would bring in the nations, saying: *I will call by the name of the Lord in your sight;* he commended that he does this mercifully, as the Apostle says: *for I say that Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers; but that the nations glorify God for his mercy*[^3]. This, then, was foretold: *I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will show mercy to whom I will have shown mercy.* By which words he forbade a man to glory as it were of the merits of his own virtues, *that he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^4]. For he does not say: *I will have mercy* on such and such, but, *on whom I will have been merciful;* so that he shows that no one merited the mercy of so great a calling by his own preceding good works. For indeed *Christ died for the ungodly*[^5].

**154.** 4. But whether he wished to repeat this same thing, when he added: *I will show mercy to whom I will have shown mercy,* or, as others rendered it, *to whom I will have been merciful;* or whether there is some difference, I do not know. For what in the Greek language was said in two words: *ἐλεήσω* and *οἰκτειρήσω,* which seems to signify one and the same thing, the Latin could not say in different words, and repeated the same mercy in different ways. But if it were said: "I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have mercy on whom I have mercy," or "I will have mercy on whom I will have had mercy"; it would seem not to be said suitably enough. And yet that very sense there is stronger, that either God showed the firmness of his own mercy by that repetition: as *amen, amen*; as *let it be done, let it be done*; as the repetition of Pharaoh's dream, and many similar things. Or in both peoples, that is, the nations and the Hebrews, God foretold in this way that he would show mercy. Which the Apostle says thus: *for as you also once did not believe God, but now have obtained mercy through their unbelief; so they also now have not believed in your mercy, that they too may obtain mercy. For God has shut up all in unbelief, that he may have mercy on all*[^6].

**154.** 5. Then, after this commendation of his mercy, he answered to that which had been said to him: *show me your glory,* or what Moses had asked above, saying: *show me yourself, manifestly, that I may see you*[^7]. *You will not be able,* he says, *to see my face. For man shall not see my face, and live*: showing that to this life, which is led in the mortal senses of corruptible flesh, God cannot appear as he is; that is, he can be seen as he is in that life, where, in order that one may live, one must die to this life.

**154.** 6. Likewise, a clause being interposed where Scripture says: *and the Lord said,* it continues and speaks: *behold, a place beside me.* For what place is not beside God, who is absent nowhere? But he signifies the Church by saying: *behold, a place beside me,* as though commending his temple: *and you shall stand,* he says, *upon the rock* (for *upon this rock,* says the Lord, *I will build my Church*[^8]), *as soon as my glory shall pass by*: that is: "as soon as my glory shall pass by, you shall stand upon the rock; for after the passing of Christ, that is, after the Passion and resurrection of Christ, the faithful people stood upon the rock." *And I will place you,* he says, *in a cleft of the rock*: he signifies a most firm protection. But others rendered it: *in a watchtower of the rock*; but the Greek has *open*; and this we more rightly render "hole" or "cleft."

**154.** 7. *And I will cover you with my hand, until I pass by; and I will take away my hand, and then you shall see my back parts; but my face shall not be seen by you.* When he had already said: *you shall stand upon the rock, as soon as my glory shall pass by,* where it is understood that after his passing he promised stability upon the rock; how is what he says to be taken: *I will place you in a cleft of the rock, and I will cover you with my hand, until I pass by; and I will take away my hand, and then you shall see my back parts*: as though, with him already set in the rock, he covers him with his hand, and then passes by; when he cannot be in the rock except after his passing?

But a recapitulation of a matter passed over is to be understood, such as Scripture is accustomed to use in many places. For he afterward said what in order of time is prior. Which order stands thus: *I will cover you with my hand, until I pass by, and then you shall see my back parts; for my face shall not be seen by you; and you shall stand upon the rock as soon as my glory shall pass by, and I will place you in a cleft of the rock.* For this came about in those whom the person of Moses then signified, that is, the Israelites, who afterward believed in the Lord Jesus, as the *Acts of the Apostles* indicate, that is, as soon as his glory passed by[^9]. For after he rose from the dead, and ascended into heaven, the Holy Spirit having been sent from above, when the Apostles spoke with the tongues of all nations, many of those who had crucified Christ were pricked in heart: and because they had not known him, and had crucified the Lord of glory, a blindness in part came upon Israel[^10], as had been said: *I will cover you with my hand, until I pass by.* Whence the *Psalm* says: *for day and night your hand was heavy upon me*[^11]: calling it *day,* when Christ did divine miracles; *night,* when as a man he was dying, when even those wavered who had believed in the day.

This, then, is: *when I have passed by, then you shall see my back parts.* When I have passed from this world to the Father, afterward those whose type you bear are to believe in me. For then, pricked in heart, they said: *what shall we do?* And they were ordered by the Apostles to do penance, and to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ, that their sins might be forgiven them[^12].

What follows in that *Psalm,* when it had been said: *day and night your hand was heavy upon me;* that is, so that I might not know (for *if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^13]): it continues and joins: *I was turned into wretchedness, while the thorn was being driven in*[^14]; that is, when I was pricked in heart. Then it added: *I acknowledged my sin, and I did not cover my crime*[^15]: after they saw with how great a wickedness they had crucified Christ. And because they took the counsel to do penance, and to receive in Baptism the remission of sins; *I said,* it says, '*I will declare against myself my offense to the Lord, and you forgave the impiety of my heart*[^16].'

**154.** 8. That this was rather a prophecy which the Lord spoke to Moses, the matter itself sufficiently indicates; since concerning the rock or its cleft, and concerning the placing of his hand over him, and the vision of his back parts, nothing afterward is read to have followed by a visible deed. For soon, a clause being interposed, Scripture adds: *and the Lord said to Moses;* when the Lord himself surely spoke also those things which were said above, and from there it weaves what the Lord says next: *cut for yourself two tablets of stone, like the first ones too*[^17], etc.
