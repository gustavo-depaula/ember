# Why the altar is commanded to be made three cubits in height.

**113.** (27, 1-2) Concerning the altar it is asked how he wished it to be three cubits high, since that is nearly the height of a man's stature. How, then, was the altar served, when in another place he forbids the altar to have steps? *Lest,* he says, *you uncover your nakedness upon it*[^1]. But there he was speaking of an altar that was to be constructed of earth or stones, where the steps themselves, built together with it, would surely pertain to the body of the altar: but now he prescribes that the altar be made of boards, so that, if something were set up at it for the hour of ministration where the minister of the altar might stand, and were removed when the ministry was completed, it would not pertain to the body of the altar. Likewise it is asked how the sacrifice that was offered could be burned upon a wooden altar; especially since he orders it to be made hollow, and a grating to be set down to the middle of it, that is, the middle of its hollow, made in latticework.

Or, since he said: *and you shall make horns on the four corners, the horns shall be of it, and you shall cover them with bronze,* is what he says, *you shall cover them with bronze,* to be referred not to the horns alone, but to all the things of which he was speaking, in prescribing concerning the making of the altar?

[^1]: Exod 20:26.
