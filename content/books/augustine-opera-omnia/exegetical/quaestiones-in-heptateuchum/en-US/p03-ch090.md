# On the words: You shall not boil a lamb in its mother's milk.

**90.** (23, 19) *You shall not boil a lamb in its mother's milk.* How it is to be understood according to the proper sense of the words, I do not know whether it can be found out. For if we take it that boiling a lamb in milk is forbidden for the sake of some signification, there is no use in boiling it so. But if it means in the days when it is suckled, who among the Jews ever held this in observance, that he should not boil a lamb except when it had ceased to suck? And what is *in its mother's milk;* as though, even if this were understood, it could be boiled without transgression of this precept, if, its mother having died when it was born, it were suckled by another ewe? Since no one doubts that the precept is surely for the sake of signifying something. But even those things which can be carried out in observance were not prescribed so without cause; for they signify something. But how this is to be observed according to the proper sense of the words either does not exist, or is not clear.

Yet I approve the understanding concerning Christ, that by this prophecy it was foretold that the infant would not be killed by the Jews, when Herod, seeking him to kill him, did not find him[^1]; so that *you shall boil,* which was said, pertains to the fire of suffering, that is, tribulation. Whence it is said: *the furnace tests the potter's vessels, and the testing of tribulation tests just men*[^2]. Because, then, the infant did not then suffer, when by Herod's seeking such a danger seemed to threaten, it was foretold in these words: *you shall not boil a lamb in its mother's milk.*

That too is perhaps not absurd which others say, that this was prescribed through the prophet, lest good Israelites associate themselves with evil Jews, by whom Christ suffered as a lamb in its mother's milk, that is, at that time when he was conceived. For females are said, from the time they have conceived, to gather milk: and that Christ was both conceived and suffered in that month, both the observance of the Passover and the day most well known to the churches of his nativity show. For he who was born in the ninth month on the eighth day before the kalends of January was surely conceived in the first month about the eighth day before the kalends of April, which time was also that of his Passion, in his mother's milk, that is, in the days of his mother.

[^1]: See Matt 2:13-15.
[^2]: Sir 27:6 (5).
