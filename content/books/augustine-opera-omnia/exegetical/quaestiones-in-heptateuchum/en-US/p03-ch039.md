# Whether the theft that God commanded the children of Israel can be taken as an example.

**39.** (11, 2) God said to Moses: *speak therefore secretly in the ears of the people, and let each one ask of his neighbor, and each woman of her neighbor, vessels of gold and silver and clothing.* From this no one should think an example is to be taken for despoiling one's neighbor in this way. For God commanded this, who knew what each one deserved to suffer; nor did the Israelites commit theft, but rendered a service to God who commanded it. Just as when an officer of the judge kills the man whom the judge has ordered killed: surely if he does it of his own accord, he is a murderer, even if he kills the very man whom he knows ought to have been killed by the judge.

There is also this question: if the Hebrews lived apart in the land of Goshen, where the plagues that afflicted Pharaoh's kingdom did not occur, how did each one ask of his neighbor, man or woman, for gold, silver, and clothing—especially since, where this is first commanded through Moses, it is put thus: *and a woman of her neighbor and roommate,* or *fellow-cell-dweller* (if it may be so said), or *fellow-inhabitant*[^1]. From this it must be understood that not only Hebrews lived in the land of Goshen, but that some Egyptians dwelt together with them in that land—Egyptians to whom those divine benefits could deservedly reach through the Hebrews, so that these same Egyptian neighbors both loved them and readily lent what they asked. Yet God did not judge them so far removed from the injuries and afflictions that the people of God endured as not to be struck by this loss too, these who had not been smitten by those plagues on account of which that land was spared.

[^1]: See Exod 3:22.
