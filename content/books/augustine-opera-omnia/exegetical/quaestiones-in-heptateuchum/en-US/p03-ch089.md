# On the words: For six years you shall sow your land and gather its fruit.

**89.** (23, 10-11) *For six years you shall sow your land, and gather its fruit; but in the seventh year you shall make a release, and give it rest; and the poor of your nation shall eat, and what is left over the wild beasts shall eat. So you shall do with your vineyard and your olive grove.* It can be asked what the poor gather, if in the seventh year he so spares the land that it is not even sown. For what was said, *the poor of your nation shall eat,* does not pertain to the vineyard and olive grove; because from unsown land they can take nothing, where crops cannot grow: for of the vineyard and olive grove he afterward says it is to be done likewise; and by this, that saying is understood of the fields that serve for grain.

Or is it to be taken thus: *for six years you shall sow your land, and gather its fruits,* that is, for six years you shall sow and gather; but in the seventh you shall not gather, so that *you shall sow* is understood even though it is not said: so that to the six years pertains sowing and gathering, but to the seventh, leaving what has been sown? For what will the poor have from it, whose leavings he gave even to the wild beasts—those, namely, which can feed on such crops, as are boars and stags, and any of this kind?

This, however, would not be said except for the sake of some signification. For if, as concerns the precepts that were given to men, *God has no care for oxen*[^1]—which is not so understood as though he himself does not feed those things which *neither sow, nor reap, nor gather into barns*[^2], but because he has no care to admonish a man by precept how he should provide for his ox: how much less has he care to prescribe concerning the wild beasts, how they are to be provided for by men, since he himself feeds them by the riches of a nature everywhere fruitful—he who feeds them also through the other six years, when what is sown is gathered?

[^1]: 1 Cor 9:9.
[^2]: Matt 6:26.
