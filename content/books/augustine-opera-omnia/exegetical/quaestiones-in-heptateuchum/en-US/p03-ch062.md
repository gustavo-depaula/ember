# The children of Israel ate nothing in the wilderness but manna.

**62.** (16, 35) *And the children of Israel ate manna forty years, until they came into an inhabited land. They ate manna until they came into the region of Phoenicia.* Scripture signified by prolepsis, that is, by mentioning in this place what was also done afterward, that the children of Israel ate nothing in the wilderness but manna. For this is what it means by *until the land that is inhabited,* that is, which is no longer wilderness: not that they ceased to eat manna immediately upon coming to habitable land, but that they did not before. For the manna is signified to have ceased once the Jordan was crossed, where they ate the bread of the land: so that when they entered the habitable land, before they crossed the Jordan, they could feed either only on manna or on both kinds of food; for this can be understood, since the manna is not said to have ceased except after the Jordan was crossed[^1].

But why, in the want of that wilderness, they also longed for flesh, when they had come out of Egypt with their very many cattle, is a great question. Unless perhaps it be said that, since there were not such great pastures throughout the wilderness, and the fruitfulness of the cattle seemed likely on that account to be less, they spared the cattle, lest, if all failed, the things necessary for sacrifices too should be lacking—or whatever else can be said by which this question may be resolved. Yet it is more fittingly believed that they did not long for the flesh which they could have from their cattle, but for that which was lacking, namely from the waters. For these they did not find in that wilderness: whence the *ortygometra* was given to them, that is, birds which many have rendered into Latin as "quail"[^2], though the *ortygometra* is a different kind of bird, although not altogether unlike the quail. For God knew what they longed for, and with what kind of flesh he might satisfy their longing. But because Scripture had said they craved flesh, and had not specified what kind of flesh, the question therefore arose.

[^1]: See Josh 5:12; Jdt 5:15.
[^2]: See Exod 16:13.
