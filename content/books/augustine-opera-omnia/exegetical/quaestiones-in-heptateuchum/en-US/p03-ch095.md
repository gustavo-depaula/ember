# Those are understood as ordinances of justice which were given to the people to observe.

**95.** (24, 1-3) *And to Moses he said*: *go up to the Lord, you, and Aaron, and Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel; and they shall worship the Lord from afar; and Moses alone shall draw near to the Lord, but they shall not draw near; but the people shall not go up with them. And Moses went in, and recounted to the people all the words of God and the ordinances of justice. And all the people answered with one voice, saying*: '*all the words which the Lord has spoken we will do, and we will hear.*' Up to this place of Scripture are understood the *ordinances of justice* which were given to the people to observe. And they begin, as the very words of Scripture indicate, where this name of "ordinances of justice" took its start, from that Hebrew slave whose ear is pierced against the doorpost[^1]. In all which ordinances of justice it is to be considered what among them can be drawn toward the leading of life and the keeping of good morals. For there are many things in them that are mysteries signifying something, rather than instructing our life. The Latin translators called *ordinances of justice*[^2] those which the Greeks call *dikaiomata.*

[^1]: See Exod 21:6.
[^2]: See Exod 21:1.
