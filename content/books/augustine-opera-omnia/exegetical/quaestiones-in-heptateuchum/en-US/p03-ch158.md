# How God is called jealous.

**158.** (34, 13-15) When God, speaking to Moses, prescribed that, the land having been given into their power, all idolatry should be overthrown, and foreign gods not be worshiped, he says: *for the Lord God, Jealous is his name, is a jealous God*: that is, the very name by which he is called Lord God is Jealous, because God is a jealous God. Which God does not do by the fault of human disturbance—being always and in every way unchangeable and tranquil—but by this word he indicates that his people will not with impunity commit fornication through foreign gods. For the word is drawn by the trope of metaphor from marital jealousy, which guards the chastity of a wife.

Which profits us, not God. For who could harm God by such a kind of fornication? But himself very much, so that he perishes. Which God forbids with the gravest terror, calling himself jealous: to whom it is said in the *Psalm*: *you have destroyed everyone who commits fornication away from you; but for me it is good to cleave to God*[^1]. Finally it continues: *lest perhaps you make a covenant with those who dwell upon the land, and they commit fornication after their gods.*

[^1]: Ps 73 (72):27–28.
