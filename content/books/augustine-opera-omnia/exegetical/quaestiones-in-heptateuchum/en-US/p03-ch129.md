# On the robe of the holy place.

**129.** 2. (29, 29-30) *And the robe of the holy place, which is Aaron's, shall be his sons' after him, to anoint them in them, and to consummate their hands. Seven days the priest who succeeds him from among his sons, who will enter into the tabernacle of the testimony to serve in the holy places, shall clothe himself with them.* These words have many questions. For first it is to be noted here how, when he has said *the robe* of the holy place, he afterward says in the plural *to anoint them in them,* as though in robes. For above too he had called them many robes, of which one was composed[^1].

Although it is ambiguous whether *in them* is from the fact that they are themselves of the neuter gender—whatever things they are by which that robe is completed, that is, the priestly garment: which is rather to be thought, from the fact that in what follows he says: *seven days the priest who succeeds him shall clothe himself with them;* namely all those things which he mentioned when he was describing the priestly garment. He repeated, indeed, what he had said above: *to consummate their hands*: whence I have set forth what seems to me[^2]. But what he says: *seven days the priest shall clothe himself with them,* will he not clothe himself on other days? But he wished it to be understood as those seven continuous days, in which his priesthood is in a manner dedicated, and there is a festival of a week at its beginning.

And he calls the successor of Aaron him *who enters into the tabernacle of the testimony, to serve in the holy places*: signifying, namely, him who could not be but one—not such as were the sons of Aaron while their father was living, but such as the successor of Aaron himself. How, then, does he say it is proper to this one alone, *to enter into the tabernacle of the testimony, to serve in the holy places,* when even those things which are outside the veil, by which the holy of holies are veiled, are called holy, and that too where the holy things are—that is, the table and the lampstand—is called the tabernacle of the testimony: when, since the succeeding priests too serve at the table and lampstand and the altar itself, how does he say there is one successor of Aaron, who *enters into the tabernacle of the testimony to serve in the holy places?* For if he had said, "to serve in the Holy of Holies," there would be no question. For to these, where the Ark of the testimony is, one alone, the high priest, entered: which is also very carefully commended in the *Letter to the Hebrews.*

Unless perhaps by the very fact that he says one *enters into the tabernacle of the testimony, to serve in the holy places,* he surely does not wish it to be understood except in the holy of holies; since these too are surely called holy. For not all things that are holy can also be called holy of holies: but those which are holy of holies are without doubt surely holy. And that this one, who once a year entered into the holy of holies, since he signified the Lord Christ, is most plainly commended in the aforesaid *Letter to the Hebrews*[^3].

But what was prefigured in the Holy of Holies—that over the ark which held the Law there should be the propitiatory, where the mercy of God is to be understood as signified, by which he is made propitious to the sins of those who do not fulfill the Law—this seems to me to be signified also in the priest's vestment itself: for what else does it signify but the mysteries of the Church? In that he set the judgments in the *λογίῳ,* that is, the *rational* placed on the priest's breast, but the sanctification and taking away of sins in the plate: as though the *rational* on the breast were like the ark in which the Law was, and that plate on the forehead like the propitiatory which was over the ark, and so that in both places what is written might be kept: *mercy exults over judgment*[^4].

[^1]: See Exod 28:4 and q. 2.114.2.
[^2]: See q. 2.125 on Exod 29:9.
[^3]: See Heb 9:7-11.
[^4]: Jas 2:13.
