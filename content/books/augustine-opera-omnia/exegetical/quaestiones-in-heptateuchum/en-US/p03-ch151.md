# How Moses, wishing to see God manifestly or knowingly, signifies that he has a twofold love.

**151.** (33, 12-13) *And Moses said to the Lord*: '*behold, you say to me*: '*lead this people.' But you have not shown me whom you will send with me. But you said to me*: '*I know you above all, and you have favor with me.' If, then, I have found favor in your sight, show me yourself, manifestly, that I may see you, that I may be one finding favor before you, and that I may know that this nation is your people.'* What the Greek has as *gnostos,* some Latins rendered *manifestly,* when Scripture did not say *phaneros.* So it could perhaps have been more aptly said: *if I have found favor in your sight, show me yourself, that I may see you knowingly*: by which words Moses sufficiently shows that he did not so see God in that great familiarity of converse as he desired to see him; since all those visions of God which were offered to the gazes of mortals, and from which there came a sound by which mortal hearing might be touched, were so displayed—a form being assumed as God willed, the form he willed—that in them the divine nature itself was perceived by no bodily sense, which is invisible, everywhere whole, and contained in no place.

And because on two precepts, that is, of the love of God and of neighbor, *the whole Law depends*[^1], therefore Moses showed his desire in both: in the love of God, namely, where he says: *if I have found favor in your sight, show me yourself, manifestly, that I may see you, that I may be one finding favor in your sight*; but in the love of neighbor, where he says: *and that I may know that this nation is your people.*

[^1]: Matt 22:40.
