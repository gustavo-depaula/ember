# The works of constructing the tabernacle and the priestly garment are commended.

**172.** (35, 1-19) After Moses descended from the mountain, the works of constructing the tabernacle and the priestly garment are commended; concerning the making of which, before he prescribed anything, he spoke to the people about the observance of the Sabbath. Here it not undeservedly stirs one—since he had received the ten words of the Law on tablets of stone again, which he himself cut, he himself wrote—why, after he descended, he admonished the people about the Sabbath alone. For if it was superfluous for the people to hear the ten precepts of the Law again; why was it not superfluous for them to hear about the Sabbath, when this too is read among those same ten precepts? Or is this too like the veil with which he covered his face, because the children of Israel could not behold the splendor of his face[^1]?

For of the ten precepts he prescribed to the people this alone, which was there said figuratively: for the other nine there, as they are precepts, we do not at all doubt are to be observed also in the New Testament. But that one about the Sabbath was so far veiled among the Israelites by the figurative observance of the seventh day, and was prescribed in a mystery, and was figured by a certain sacrament, that it is today not observed by us, but we only behold what it signified. But in that rest, where servile works are ordered to cease, there is a great depth of the grace of God.

For then good works are done with rest, when faith works through love[^2]: but fear has torment, and in torment what rest is there? Whence *fear is not in love*[^3]: but *love is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^4]. Therefore *the Sabbath is a holy rest to the Lord*: to be attributed, namely, to the grace of God, not to ourselves as if from ourselves. Otherwise our works will be such that they are human, or sins: either with fear, not with love; and therefore servile, without rest. But the fullness of the Sabbath will be in everlasting rest. For not in vain was the Sabbath of Sabbaths also instituted[^5].

[^1]: See Ex 34:34–35.
[^2]: See Gal 5:6.
[^3]: 1 John 4:18.
[^4]: Rom 5:5.
[^5]: See Lev 25.
