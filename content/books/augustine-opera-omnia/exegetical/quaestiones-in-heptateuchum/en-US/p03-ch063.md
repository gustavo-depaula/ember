# On the words: Until they came into the region of Phoenicia.

**63.** (16, 35) *They ate manna until they came into the region of Phoenicia.* He had already said: *until they came into the inhabited land*; but because he had not expressed properly which land he meant, by repetition he seems to have expressed a certain proper name by saying *into the region of Phoenicia.* But it is to be believed that that land was then so called; for now it is not called by this name. For that region of Tyre and Sidon which is called Phoenicia is another, which they are not read to have passed through. Although Scripture could perhaps have called "the land of Phoenicia" the place where palm trees had begun to be after the desolation of the wilderness, since in Greek the palm is so called. For at the beginning of their journey they found a place where there had been seventy palm trees and twelve springs[^1]; but afterward the length of the wilderness received them, where there was nothing of the kind, until they came to lands that were cultivated. But that sense is more probable, that we believe the land was then so called. For the names of many lands and places, as of rivers and cities, have been changed with antiquity for definite reasons.

[^1]: See Exod 15:27.
