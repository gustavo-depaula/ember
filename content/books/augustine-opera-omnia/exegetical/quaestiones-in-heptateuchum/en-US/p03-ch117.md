# Where it is commanded to place upon the rational the manifestation and the truth.

**117.** (28, 30) *And you shall place upon the rational of judgment the manifestation and the truth.* What this means, or on what kind of object or metal the *manifestation* and *truth* were to be placed upon the *rational*—since he says such things are made in the priest's vestment, which are made bodily—is difficult to find. Yet some relate as a tale that there was a stone whose color would change, either toward adverse or toward favorable things, when the priest entered the holy places: and that this is what he says: *and Aaron shall bear the judgments of the children of Israel upon his breast*; showing, namely, in that manifestation and truth, what the Lord had judged concerning them. Although *manifestation* and *truth* can be understood as set in letters upon the *λόγιον.*

On the making of the ankle-length tunic and the neck-opening.

**118.** (28, 31) *And you shall make an ankle-length tunic of hyacinth;* that is, hanging down to the ankles. *And there shall be a neck-opening in the middle of it,* that is, through which the head is put out: for this is what the Greeks call *peristomion.* *Having a border around the neck-opening, the weaver's work, a woven seam;* that is, lest the border itself be sewn on from outside: this he seems to mean by *a woven seam.* Whence he also added, *of itself, that it be not torn;* that is, that the border itself be woven of itself together with the garment.
