# How the people there are said to have seen the voice.

**72.** (20, 18) *And all the people saw the voice, and the lamps, and the sound of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking.* It is commonly asked how the people saw the voice, since a voice seems to pertain not to sight but rather to hearing. But just as I said a moment ago "seems," of all that has been said by me; so "to see" is commonly used for the general faculty of perception, not only of the body but also of the mind: whence is that saying, *when Jacob saw that there was food in Egypt*[^1]—from which he was surely absent. Although some have thought that "to see" a voice is nothing other than "to understand," which is the sight of the mind.

But since it had here to be said briefly that *the people saw the voice and the lamps, and the sound of the trumpet and the mountain smoking,* a greater question would arise as to how they heard the lamps and the smoking mountain, which pertains to the sense of sight. Unless someone should say that it ought not to have been said so briefly, but, that the whole might be said, "they heard the voice, and saw the lamps, and heard the sound of the trumpet, and saw the mountain smoking." For there were two kinds of sound, both from the clouds, like thunder, and from the trumpet; if indeed he called a "voice" that which was uttered from the clouds.

And by this it was better, in those things that pertained to the sense of hearing, that the general faculty was put, that is, of sight, when Scripture wished to embrace the whole briefly, than that in those things which pertain to the sense of sight, hearing should be understood: a manner in which we are not accustomed to speak. For "see what it sounds like," we are accustomed to say: "hear what shines," we are not accustomed to say.

[^1]: Gen 42:1.
