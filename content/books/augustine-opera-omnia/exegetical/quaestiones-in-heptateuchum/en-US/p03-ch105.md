# On the propitiatory which is placed over the ark.

**105.** (25, 17-20) What he calls the propitiatory, to be placed over the ark[^1], is commonly asked: but since he orders it to be made of gold, and expresses its length and breadth as great as that of the ark itself was said to be, without doubt he prescribes that a kind of golden plate of such a form be made, by which the ark would be covered; so that on the propitiatory itself there would be two cherubim, on this side and that attending to each other, so that their faces would be toward the propitiatory, and with their wings they would overshadow the propitiatory: which is a great mystery. For gold signifies wisdom, the ark signifies the secret of God. In the ark were commanded to be placed the Law, and the manna, and the rod of Aaron: in the Law are the precepts, by the rod power is signified, by the manna grace; because power to do the precepts does not exist except with grace. Nevertheless, because the Law is not fulfilled in every part by anyone making progress, the propitiatory is above. For there is need that God be propitious, and therefore it is placed above, because *mercy exults over judgment*[^2]. But the two cherubim with their wings overshadow the propitiatory, that is, honor it by veiling; since these mysteries are there: and they attend to each other, because they are in accord; for two Testaments are figured there: and their faces are toward the propitiatory, because they greatly commend the mercy of God, in which alone is hope. Finally, from here God promised he would speak to Moses, from the midst of the cherubim, from above the propitiatory[^3].

Furthermore, if the rational creature, in the multitude of knowledge—since the cherubim have this interpretation—is signified by those two living creatures; they are two for this reason, that they may commend the fellowship of love; with their wings they overshadow the propitiatory for this reason, because they attribute their wings to God, not to themselves, that is, they honor God by the virtues in which they excel: and their faces are only toward the propitiatory, because for whatever progress toward the multitude of knowledge there is no hope, except in the mercy of God.

[^1]: See Exod 25:21.
[^2]: Jas 2:13.
[^3]: See Exod 25:22.
