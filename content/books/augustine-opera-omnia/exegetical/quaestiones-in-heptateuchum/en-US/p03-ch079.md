# God kills only justly, but a man is worthy of punishment.

**79.** (21, 12-13) *If anyone strikes someone, and he dies, let him die the death. But he who did it unwillingly, but God delivered him into his hands—I will give you a place to which he may flee.* It is asked how this is said: *but he who did it unwillingly, but God delivered him into his hands;* as though, even if he killed willingly, he could not kill, unless God delivered into his hands. It is understood, therefore, that God alone did it, when someone is killed by one who is unwilling: and for this, that God alone did it, it is said, *but God delivered him into his hands.* But when one kills willingly, both he himself kills, and God delivered into his hands.

This, then, is the difference: that there God alone did it; but here both God and man, on account of the will of the one acting; yet not in the way that God did, so the man. For God acts only justly, but the man is worthy of punishment: not because he killed one whom God would not have killed, but because he did it through iniquity. For he did not render a service to God commanding, but served his own malignant desire. In one and the same deed, therefore, both God is praised for his hidden equity, and the man is punished for his own iniquity. For not because God did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all[^1], was Judas thereby excused, who delivered up that same Christ to death[^2].

[^1]: See Rom 8:32.
[^2]: See Matt 26:48.
