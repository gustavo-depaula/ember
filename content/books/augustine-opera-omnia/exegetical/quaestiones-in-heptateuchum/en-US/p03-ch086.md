# On what he said: You shall not curse gods.

**86.** (22, 28) *You shall not curse gods.* It is asked whom he called gods; whether the princes who judge the people, as it was said of Moses that he had been given as a god to Pharaoh[^1]: so that what follows was said by way of explanation, as though showing whom he called gods, where he says: *and you shall not curse the prince of your people*: which the Greek has as, *you shall not speak evil.*

Or is it to be taken according to that which the Apostle says: *for although there are those that are called gods, whether in heaven or on earth, as there are many gods and many lords*[^2]; for by adding, *as there are,* he wished those to be understood as gods who are even worthily so called: yet so, indeed, that what is called in Greek *latreia,* and is rendered in Latin *service*—but that which is understood to pertain to religion—is owed only to the one true God, who is God to us? But those who are called "gods," even if there are any who are rightly so called, are forbidden to be cursed; they are not commanded to be honored with sacrifices or any observances of latria.

[^1]: See Exod 7:1.
[^2]: 1 Cor 8:5.
