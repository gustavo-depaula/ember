# How the words that follow concerning the maidservant are obscure and need to be explained.

**78.** 1. (21, 7-11) *But if anyone sells his daughter as a servant, she shall not go away as the maidservants depart. But if she does not please her master, who has not betrothed her, he shall recompense her. But it is not for the master to sell her to a foreign nation, since he has spurned her. But if he betroths her to his son, he shall deal with her according to the right of daughters. But if he takes another for him, he shall not defraud her of what is needful, and clothing, and her companionship. But if he does not do these three things for her, she shall go out free without price.* Unusual words and expressions have made this passage most obscure, so that our translators almost did not find how to explain it. In the Greek diction itself, too, what is said here is very obscure. Yet I will, as I can, set forth what seems to be the case.

**78.** 2. *But if anyone,* it says, *sells his daughter as a servant,* that is, to be a servant, whom the Greeks call *oiketes*: *she shall not go away as the maidservants depart,* it is to be understood, she shall not depart as the Hebrew maidservants depart after six years. For one must understand that in the Hebrew woman too the Law was given which is observed in the males.

Why, then, will she not depart so, unless because in that servitude she is understood to have been humbled, in that her master had intercourse with her? For this becomes somewhat clear in what follows. For it continues and says: *but if she does not please her master, who has not betrothed her,* that is, did not make her his wife: *he shall recompense her,* this is what he said above, *she shall not go away as the maidservants depart.* For it is just that she receive something for the fact that she was humbled; because he had intercourse with her not in such a way as to make her his wife, that is, to betroth her to himself.

But what we have rendered, *he shall recompense her,* some translators have rendered, *he shall redeem her.* But if in the Greek it had been said *apolytrosetai,* it would have been written as it is written: *and he himself will redeem Israel*[^1]; for there too it is written *apolytrosetai.* But in this place is read *apolytrosei,* where it is understood that he receives something, rather than that something is given for her that she may be redeemed. For to whom will her master give, that he may redeem her whom he himself possesses as a servant? *But it is not for the master to sell her to a foreign nation, since he has spurned her*: that is, not "because he has spurned her, therefore it is for the master to sell her"—that is, he will so far have dominion over her as even to sell her lawfully to a foreign nation. For this is the meaning of *he has spurned her*—that is, "spurned her"; and "to spurn her" means to have "humbled" her, that is, by lying with her and not making her his wife. He said in Greek *ethetesen,* which we rendered *he has spurned,* a word which Scripture uses in *Jeremiah*: *as a woman spurns him with whom she is joined*[^2].

**78.** 3. Then it follows and says: *but if he betroths her to his son, he shall deal with her according to the right of daughters.* Here it begins to appear how he said above, *whom he has not betrothed.* For what else is *if he betroths her to his son,* but "if he joins her as a wife to his son"? Since indeed he says, *he shall deal with her according to the right of daughters,* that is, "so as to give her away as a daughter," providing her, namely, with a dowry. Then he adds: *but if he takes another for him,* that is, does not reckon this one as a wife for his son, but takes another for him: *he shall not defraud her of what is needful, and clothing, and her companionship;* he shall give her, by a like Law, what is fitting, since she did not remain a wife to his son, just as he would give her, if he had not betrothed her to himself, and yet had humbled her by lying with her.

But what we have rendered, *he shall not defraud her of her companionship,* the Greek has *homilia,* that is, *conversation,* by which name Scripture is understood to call intercourse more decently. But what is *he shall not defraud her of intercourse,* but "he shall give a payment for the intercourse"? For in Daniel the elders, bearing false witness against Susanna, say: *a young man, who was hidden in concealment, came to her, and lay with her*[^3]*.* But Daniel, questioning about this very thing, says: *under what tree did you see them conversing?*[^4]: where they had said, *he lay with her.*

Then, accusing and convicting the other, he said: *the seed of Canaan, and not of Judah, the beauty deceived you, and that desire perverted your heart: for so you used to do to the daughters of Israel, but they, fearing, complied with you*[^5]. But the Greek has *homiloun hymin,* which could be said in Latin word for word, *they conversed with you*: by which intercourse is signified. For where it is said, *under what tree did you seize them,* the Greek has, *you seized them conversing with each other*: and there too intercourse is signified.

**78.** 4. So what Scripture adds concerning this woman of whom it treats, saying: *but if he does not do these three things for her, she shall go out free,* is understood thus: "if he himself has not humbled her by intercourse, nor joined her to his son, nor, having brought another for his son, cast this one out, *she shall go away free,* that is, it shall suffice for her not to be held in servitude. For she shall go away receiving nothing, like a Hebrew slave. For it is not lawful for her master to join her to a non-Hebrew man, she whom it is not lawful to hand over to a foreign nation. But if he joins her to a Hebrew slave, this surely is understood, that she shall go out with him free, in no way separated from her husband."

[^1]: Ps 130:8 (129).
[^2]: Jer 3:20.
[^3]: Dan 13:37.
[^4]: Dan 13:58.
[^5]: Dan 13:56-57.
