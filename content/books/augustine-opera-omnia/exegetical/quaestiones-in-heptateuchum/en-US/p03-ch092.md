# How the promises are to be taken.

**92.** (23, 25-27) *And you shall serve the Lord your God; and I will bless your bread, and your wine, and your water, and I will turn away sickness from you. There shall be none who does not bear, nor barren upon your land. I will fill up the number of your days. And I will send fear to go before you; and I will make distraught all the nations into which you will enter,* etc. Although these promises can be understood also spiritually, yet when they are understood according to the temporal happiness of men, they pertain to the Old Testament: where, although the precepts—except those which signify something in a mystery—are found to be the same, pertaining to good morals, yet the promises are carnal and earthly. Whence in *Psalm 73 (72)* the man of God says that his steps had nearly slipped and been poured out, when he was envious, beholding the peace of sinners[^1].

For he saw the ungodly abound in those things which he himself, according to the Old Testament, awaited from the Lord God, whom he served for this reward. And when from this an ungodly thought had begun to steal upon him, in that he supposed God did not care for human things, he says he was corrected, while he did not dare to disapprove the authority of the saints, and he began to know, and says: *this is a labor before me, until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand in the last things*[^2]. For there will be given the rewards pertaining to the New Testament, which the ungodly will not receive: and then there will be the punishments of the ungodly, which none of the godly will feel.

[^1]: See Ps 73:2 (72).
[^2]: Ps 73:16-17 (72).
