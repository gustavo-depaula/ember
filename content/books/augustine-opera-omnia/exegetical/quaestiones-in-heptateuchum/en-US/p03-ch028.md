# On what Moses says: It cannot be done thus.

**28.** (8:26) As to what Moses says, *it cannot be done thus: for we will sacrifice to the Lord our God the abominations of the Egyptians*: that is, we are going to sacrifice these things which the Egyptians abominate, and therefore we cannot in Egypt: this the words which he adds make manifest, saying, *for if we sacrifice the abominations of the Egyptians openly before them, we will be stoned*. Some of our translators, not understanding this, so translated that they said: *it cannot be done thus: 'shall we sacrifice the abominations of the Egyptians to the Lord our God?'* Whereas Scripture rather said this, that they are going to sacrifice the abominations of the Egyptians. But other Latins have it thus: *it cannot be done thus, since we will not sacrifice the abominations of the Egyptians to the Lord our God*. The added negative particle makes a contrary sense, when Moses said: *it cannot be done thus: for we will sacrifice the abominations of the Egyptians to the Lord our God*: and therefore they said they wished to go into the desert, where the Egyptians would not see their abominations. But this is to be understood as mystically signified—what we said also of the shepherds, who were abominable to the Egyptians[^1]; and therefore the Israelites received a separate land when they came into Egypt. For so too the sacrifices of the Israelites are abominations to the Egyptians, as the life of the just is to the unjust.

[^1]: See Gen 46:34.
