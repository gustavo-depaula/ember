# It does not pertain to homicide if a nocturnal thief is killed.

**84.** (22, 2-3) *But if a thief is found breaking in, and being struck dies, it is not homicide for him. But if the sun rises upon him, he is guilty; he shall die for death.* It is understood, therefore, that it does not then pertain to homicide if a nocturnal thief is killed; but if a daytime one, it does pertain. For this is what it says, *if the sun rises upon him.* For it could be discerned that he had come to steal, not to kill, and therefore ought not to be killed. This is found also in the ancient secular laws—than which, however, this is more ancient—that a nocturnal thief may be killed with impunity in any manner, but a daytime one if he defends himself with a weapon: for then he is more than a thief[^1].

[^1]: See Cicero, *Pro Milone* 9; *Pro Tullio* 50.
