# What the golden moldings, turned all around, are.

**104.** (25, 11-12) *And you shall make on it golden moldings, turned all around.* He called *moldings* those things that project on the squared edge along the four sides, as square tables are usually made. Nor should we take what he says, *turned,* as "movable." For they are fixed, as I said tables are accustomed to have: but he said *turned* meaning "twisted," which in Greek are called *στρεπτά* or "channeled," as twisted columns are; or formed of two little rods interwoven in the manner of a rope, as torques too are accustomed to be made. But what he says: *and you shall make for it four golden rings, and set them upon the four sides; two rings on one side, and two rings on the second side;* at the four corners four rings come together, one at each, and by the number of the corners it comes about that what is placed on two sides is placed on all four. For one corner is common to two sides: otherwise it does not come out that two rings each are placed along four sides, since there are four rings; for there would have had to be eight, if we understand it otherwise than that it comes about by the number of corners, as I said. For the rings are placed at the corners for this purpose, that the carrying-poles or bearing-poles may be inserted, by which the ark may be carried by four men on this side and that.
