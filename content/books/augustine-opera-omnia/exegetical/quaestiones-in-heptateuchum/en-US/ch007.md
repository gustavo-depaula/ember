# Book VI: Questions on Joshua

## How Moses is shown to have died in grace.

**1.** (1:5) The Lord says to Joshua the son of Nun: *and as I was with Moses, so I will be with you also.* But not only by this testimony, but also in *Deuteronomy* by many proofs, it is proved that Moses so died as a servant of God and pleasing to God: although in him that punishment was fulfilled, that he should not enter the Land of Promise.[^1] From which it is given to understand that the Lord, being displeased in something even with his good servants, both punishes them temporally, and yet holds them in the number of those who are in his house vessels useful to the Lord for honor,[^2] to whom he will give the things promised to the saints.

## Concerning the crossing of the Jordan.

**2.** (1:11; 2:3-7) The question is, how, after the Lord had spoken to Joshua the son of Nun, exhorting and strengthening him, and promising that he would be with him, this same Joshua commanded the people through the scribes to prepare provisions, because after three days they were to cross the Jordan, when they are found to have crossed the Jordan after many more days.

For when he had commanded these things to the people, he sent spies into Jericho, since, the Jordan being crossed, that was the nearest city to meet them: but they turned aside to Rahab, a harlot woman, and, hidden by her, and sought by the king and not at all found, with her sending them away through the window and warning them to lie hidden for three days in the mountains,[^3] four days seem to have been consumed; thereafter, when they had reported what had been done concerning them, Joshua with all the people set out from the place where he was at daybreak; and when he had come to the Jordan, he turned aside and remained: then again the people are admonished to prepare themselves to cross the Jordan after three days, with the Ark of the Lord going before.[^4]

Hence, then, it is understood that it was a human arrangement which he caused to be announced to the people, that they should prepare provisions, as though they were to cross the aforementioned river after three days. For he could, as a man, hope that this could be done, if the spies returned quickly. With them delaying, it is understood—although Scripture is silent about it—that the rest was accomplished by divine arrangement, so that Joshua might now begin to be glorified among the people, and it might be shown that the Lord was with him, as he had been with Moses. For this too is said to him as he is about to cross the river, as it is written: *and the Lord said to Joshua: On this day I will begin to exalt you before all the children of Israel, that they may know that, as I was with Moses, so I will be with you.*

Nor ought it to seem incredible that even those with whom God spoke wished to do something by a human arrangement, in which they nonetheless trusted God to be their guide, and that their plans were changed by the providence of him by whom they were guided. For Moses himself, as a man, had certainly thought it ought to be done that he should hear the cases of the people in such a way that he could be of profit neither to himself nor to them, taking up an intolerable burden: and his arrangement was changed by God—this very thing being suggested and advised by his father-in-law, and God approving this advice.[^5]

## Why they are bidden to go far before the ark, and how the pillar of cloud no longer went before.

**3.** (3:3-4, 15) The scribes say to the people: *when you see the Ark of the testimony of the Lord our God, and our priests and Levites carrying it, set out from your places; and go after it: but let there be a long interval between you and it, you shall stand about two thousand cubits off, do not draw near to it, that you may know the way by which you are to go into it. For you have not gone the way from yesterday and the day before.*[^6]

The ark was bidden to go far ahead, so that it could be seen by the people. For so great a host, if it had gone close behind it, would not have seen it going before, nor known which way to follow. From this deed it is to be understood that that pillar of cloud, which was accustomed to give the signal for moving the camp, and to show the way[^7], had now withdrawn, and did not appear to them: hence it came about that even that three-day interval was foretold by human arrangement. Now, then, with Joshua as leader, they follow the Ark of the Lord, the cloud being taken away as a veil removed. *And the Jordan was full along its whole bank, as in the days of the wheat harvest.* This seems incredible for our regions: but there, as those who know it report, the wheat harvest is at the beginning of spring; and at that time that river is filled more than during winter.

## How the stones are said to be a memorial forever.

**4.** (4:7) *And these stones shall be to you a memorial for the children of Israel forever.*[^8] How *forever,* since heaven and earth pass away?[^9] Or is it because these stones signify something eternal, although they themselves cannot be eternal? Although what is in Greek, εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα, can be said in Latin also as "to the age," which does not follow that it should be understood as "eternal."

## The Ark of the covenant, or the Ark of the testimony.

**5.** (4:15-16) *And the Lord said to Joshua, saying: Command the priests carrying the Ark of the covenant of the testimony.*[^10] It is usually called the *Ark of the covenant,* or the *Ark of the testimony*[^11]: now it is called the *Ark of the covenant of the testimony,* so that not only the Ark, but the covenant itself was called *the covenant of the testimony.* For hence the Apostle says: *but now without the Law the righteousness of God has been manifested, having testimony through the Law and the Prophets*[^12]. For that which is called the Old Covenant had been given as a testimony of some other thing to come.

## How the children of Israel are bidden to be circumcised again.

**6.** (5:2-7) *The Lord said to Joshua: Make for yourself knives of stone, sharp,* or, as the Greek has it, *of sharp stone, and, sitting, circumcise the children of Israel again.* In this precept it is asked why he said *again.* For one man was not to be circumcised twice: but because there was one people, circumcised in some, uncircumcised in others, therefore it was said *again,* so that the circumcised should be circumcised again—not the man, but the people. For what follows shows this too.

For Scripture says: *and Joshua made stone knives, sharp for himself, and circumcised the children of Israel in the place which is called the Hill of Foreskins. And in this manner Joshua purified the children of Israel, those who at some time had been on the way, and those of them who had at some time been uncircumcised, of those who had gone out of Egypt; all these Joshua circumcised. For forty-two years Israel dwelt in the desert of Mabdaris, and therefore most of those fighting men were uncircumcised who had gone out of the land of Egypt, who had been disobedient to the commandments of God, concerning whom the Lord had also determined that they should not see the land which the Lord had sworn to their fathers to give them, a land flowing with milk and honey. But in their place he substituted their sons, whom Joshua circumcised, because they had been uncircumcised on the journey.*

It is clear, then, that it was not all, but some. For some of the sons of those who went out of Egypt were uncircumcised in that people, whom Joshua could circumcise; namely, the sons of those who begot them in the desert and neglected to circumcise them, because they were disobedient to the Law of God. There is, therefore, no reason why those who think that those who have the sacrament of Christian Baptism are to be rebaptized should think themselves helped by this testimony of the Law: because no one man was circumcised twice in number; but the people, which had already been circumcised in some, was still uncircumcised in others. And if it could in some way come about that God should bid a man to be circumcised twice; could they say that it was commanded for this reason, that those men had been circumcised by the Egyptians, or by some heretics separated from the fellowship of the Israelites? But since it appears clearly enough why it was said by God, men can find here no patronage for their error.

## Concerning the vision of the angel who appeared to Joshua in the field of Jericho.

**7.** (5:13-15) When Joshua saw a man *standing opposite him with a drawn sword*, and, with the man answering, he says that he was the commander of the army of the host of the Lord; and, prostrate on the ground, he said: *what do you command your servant?* it can be asked whether he prostrated himself to an angel, and called him lord; or rather, understanding by whom he had been sent, called him the Lord himself, and prostrated himself to him. Now Joshua was, as is read, in Jericho, not indeed in the city itself, whose walls had not yet fallen—which was soon to be, so that they could enter into it—but in the field belonging to it: for the translation which is from the Hebrew has it thus.

## Concerning the theft of Achan.

**8.** (7:1ff.) In the matter that came about, that Achan of the tribe of Judah stole from the devoted things of the city of Jericho against the command of the Lord; and on account of his sin the three thousand who had been sent to Ai turned their backs to the enemy, and thirty-six men were killed of them; and, with the people gravely terrified, Joshua with the elders prostrated himself to the Lord, and it was answered to him that this had happened because the people had sinned; God also threatened that he would not be with them, unless they removed the devoted thing from among themselves; and that the one who had done it was pointed out, and was not killed alone, but with all his household: it is usually asked how it is just that for the sins of one, others are punished; especially because in the Law the Lord said that neither fathers for the sins of sons, nor sons for the sins of fathers, were to be punished.[^13]

Is that a precept for men when they judge, that they should not punish anyone for another; but the judgments of God are not of this kind, who by his deep and invisible counsel knows how far to extend even the temporal punishment and salutary terror of men? For nothing dreadful, as far as concerns the administration of the whole world, befalls mortals when they die who were at some point going to die: and yet among those who fear such things, discipline is established, so that each one in the people may care not for himself alone, but they may apply diligence to one another, and, as members of one body and of one man, some may be solicitous for others.[^14]

Nor yet is it to be believed that, even in the punishments which are inflicted after death, one can be condemned for another; but that this penalty is inflicted only in those things which were going to have an end, even if they were not ended in that way. At the same time it is also shown how the totality itself is bound together in the fellowship of a people, so that individuals are reckoned not in themselves, but as parts in a whole. Through the sin of one, then, and the death of a few, the whole people was admonished to seek out in the whole body what had been committed. At the same time it was also signified how great an evil would be done if that whole assembly had sinned; since not even one man could be so judged that the rest could be secure on his account.

But indeed, if Achan, found and seized by someone, and as guilty of that crime brought to the judgment of Joshua, it is by no means to be thought that a human judge would have punished either for him or together with him any other who was in no way held by the fellowship of his deed. For it was not lawful for him to exceed the command of the Law, which is given to men, that they should not, by their judgment—which is commanded or permitted to man over man—decide that one is to be punished for the sin of another.

But God judges by a far more secret justice, who is able even after death—what man cannot—either to free or to destroy.[^15] The visible afflictions, then, or deaths of men, since they can both harm and profit those on whom they are imposed, the Lord knows, in the secret of his providence, how justly to dispense to each, even when he seems to avenge the sins of some upon others. But the invisible punishments, which only harm and cannot profit, no one suffers for the sins of others with God as judge, just as no one ought to suffer these visible ones with man as judge except for his own fault. For God commanded this to man the judge, in those things which pertain to human judgment to be punished, which he himself does in his own judgment, to which human power does not aspire.

## Why Joshua had Achan stoned, whom the Lord had bidden to be burned.

**9.** 1. (7:15, 25) It is rightly asked: since the Lord, on account of the one who had committed theft from the devoted things, commanded that the one who was pointed out should be burned with fire, why Joshua had the one pointed out rather stoned by the people. Or was it fitting that he should die in such a way as Joshua, who followed the Lord more closely, could understand the words of the Lord's command? For so another could not easily have done. Hence it is rather to be asked why the Lord called stoning "fire," than to be believed that Joshua did anything other than the Lord had commanded. For no one could be wiser for understanding the words of the Lord, nor anyone more obedient for doing them. Accordingly, that by the name of "fire" a punishment could be signified, Scripture is a witness in *Deuteronomy,* where it is said to the children of Israel: *and he led you out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt*[^16]: where, of course, he meant a hard tribulation to be understood.

**9.** 2. Two reasons occur to me (not that both hold, but that one of them does) why he was not, with all his household, burned with manifest fire. For if the Lord judged his sin to be such that, expiated by that punishment, he would not punish it eternally, then on account of that very expiation and purification that punishment fittingly received the name of "fire." Nor would anyone be admonished to reach toward this understanding, if visible fire properly burned him up; but each would remain in what he plainly saw fulfilled, and would seek nothing beyond. But now, since on account of the words of God and the deed of Joshua, by whom those words could not be transgressed, it is most rightly said that the stoning too was fire; it is elegantly recognized that the man was purified by that punishment, lest he perish hereafter by that sin. This is signified also by the vessels in *Leviticus,* which are bidden to be purified by fire.[^17]

But if that sin was such that even after this life Gehenna would receive him; then Joshua willed him to be stoned for this reason, that what was said by the Lord, *he shall be burned with fire,* might admonish it to be understood as something the Lord would do, not something to be done by them. For if the Lord had said: "you shall burn him with fire and all his goods"; there would be no place for this sense: but since it is set down in such a way that God seems rather to have foretold what would befall him than to have commanded what ought to be done to him by men; Joshua, who understood the divine words as so great a prophet, who did this very thing prophetically, could do no better than to kill him with stones rather than with flames, lest in those fires the words of the Lord should seem fulfilled, which he wished to be understood as said for another reason.

**9.** 3. Nor ought it to trouble that God foretold not only him, but also all that was his, to be burned with fire. For it says thus: *he shall be burned with fire, and all whatever is his. For all whatever is his* can be understood as his works, which he said were to be burned up together with him: not as the Apostle says of certain works consumed by fire, *but he himself shall be saved*[^18]: if this man's sin is to be so understood that it is punished even by eternal fire.

His sons, then, and daughters, with the cattle and all that he had, the people indeed, when it punished him, overwhelmed together with stones: yet Joshua did this not by human judgment, but by a prophetic spirit; whether understanding *all that is his* in such a way that he did not reckon even the sons to be excepted, inflicting the penalty of stoning too in place of fire; or signifying his works, which God was going to burn in him after death, not only through the rest of the things that were his, but also through his sons.

Nor, indeed, is it to be believed on this account that they, for the sin of their father, of which they were innocent, were burned even after death by the punishment of the fire of hell. For that death which awaits all, although it comes from the first sin,[^19] yet, because we are so born that there must necessarily be dying, is, when hastened, useful to some. Hence it is read of a certain man: *he was carried off, lest malice should change his understanding*[^20]. By what judgment of God, then, or mercy, it was inflicted whether on this man's sons, or on those thirty-six men[^21], since all were free from his sin, lies hidden with him with whom there is no injustice.[^22]

But this is plain: that the people too had terribly to seek out what had been committed; and the rest feared the more vehemently to imitate his deed, the more human weakness shrinks both from being given over to so great and so just a hatred of the people, and from dying, with one's posterity consumed together with oneself by one's own sin, among those whom one thought to leave behind for the hope of propagating one's line.

## How God commands an ambush to be made.

**10.** (8:2) That God, speaking to Joshua, bids him set an ambush for himself behind, that is, soldiers lying in wait to ambush the enemy, admonishes us from this that it is not done unjustly by those who wage a just war: so that a just man ought to consider nothing especially in these matters except to undertake a just war, for one to whom it is lawful to wage war; for it is not lawful for all. But when he has undertaken a just war, whether he conquers by open battle or by ambushes makes no difference to justice.

Now just wars are usually defined as those which avenge injuries, if some nation or city, which is to be attacked in war, has either neglected to punish what was wickedly done by its own, or to restore what was taken away through injuries. But also that kind of war is without doubt just which God commands, with whom there is no injustice,[^23] and who knows what ought to be done to each. In which war the leader of the army, or the people itself, is to be judged not so much the author of the war as the minister.

## Whether every will to deceive is to be called a lie.

**11.** (8:4-8) Joshua, sending thirty thousand warriors to subdue Ai, said to them: *you shall lie in ambush behind the city, and you shall not be far from the city, and you shall all be ready: and I and all the people who are with me will approach the city. And it shall be, when those who dwell in Ai come out to meet us, as before, that we will flee from their face. And when they come out after us, we will draw them away from the city, and they will say: These men flee from our face, as before. But you shall rise up from the ambush and go into the city. According to this word you shall do. Behold, I command you.*[^24]

It must be asked whether every will to deceive is to be reckoned a lie; and if so, whether a lie can be just by which one is deceived who is worthy to be deceived: and if not even this lie is found to be just, it remains that, according to some signification, this which was done about the ambush is to be referred to the truth.

## Whether the Gibeonites took sacks upon their shoulders or upon their asses.

**12.** (9:3-4, 13) As to the Gibeonites coming to Joshua with old bread and sacks, so that they might be thought, as they had pretended, to have come from a far land, in order that they might be spared (for it had been established by the Lord that they should spare no one inhabiting those lands into which they were entering): some manuscripts, both Greek and Latin, have: *and taking old sacks upon their shoulders;* but others, which seem more truthful, do not have *upon their shoulders,* but *upon their asses.* For the similarity of the word in the Greek language made an error easy, and therefore the Latin copies too were varied: for Ὤμων and ὄνων do not differ much from each other, the first of which is the word for "shoulders," the latter for "asses." But it is more credible that it was about asses, since they said they had been sent from their nation far away: hence it appears that they were envoys, and therefore could carry the necessary things rather on asses than on shoulders; both because they could not be many, and because Scripture mentions that they carried not only sacks but also wineskins.

## How the oath is said to have been kept toward them.

**13.** (9:19, 9) It can be asked how the Hebrews thought the oath was to be kept toward the Gibeonites, to whom they had sworn as though to people coming from a far land, as those had lied. For they knew they would be subdued, if it were known to the Hebrews that they dwelt in that land which they were going to retain, with the inhabitants killed, as it had been promised. To them, then, lying that they had come to them from a far land, the Israelites swore. But after they learned that they dwelt there, where all whom they found were, according to God's command, to be subdued, they were nevertheless unwilling to break the oath: and although they had learned that they had lied, they preferred to spare them for the sake of the oath; when they could indeed say that they had sworn as it were to those whom they had believed them to be, that is, coming from afar; but when they had learned otherwise, the Lord's command concerning them had to be fulfilled, that they should be assailed like the rest.

But God approved this, and was not angry with those who spared them: although they had not asked him[^25] who those men were, and therefore those had been able to deceive them. Hence they are surely not unreasonably to be believed, even if they wished to deceive men for their own safety, nevertheless to have feared God without deceit among his people: therefore the Lord was not angry with those who swore or spared, so that afterward he avenged the Gibeonites themselves, as men of his own people, upon the house of Saul, as the history of the kingdoms shows.[^26]

And since the oath was so kept, although among men who had lied, that the sentence was bent toward clemency, it did not displease God. For if, on the contrary, they had sworn that they would kill certain men whom they had thought to be Gibeonites in the Land of Promise, and afterward had learned that they were strangers to that land, and had come to them from afar, it is by no means to be thought that they would have subdued them for the sake of fulfilling the oath: since, for the sake of the very clemency of sparing, holy David, even after the words by which he had sworn that he would kill Nabal, certainly knowing whom he had been going to kill, preferred to spare, and not to fulfill the oath in a harsher matter[^27]; thinking it more pleasing to God if he did not do what, disturbed by anger, he had sworn to do for harm, than if he had carried it out.

## How the Lord, unconsulted, gives counsel.

**14.** (10:7-8) When the Gibeonites, besieged by the kings of the Amorites, had sent to Joshua to be helped, Scripture continues thus, and says: *and Joshua went up from Gilgal, he and all the warrior people with him, every mighty man in strength. And the Lord said to Joshua: Do not fear them; for into your hands I have delivered them: not one of them shall stand before you.* Here the Lord was not consulted whether one ought to go to them; but, to his people willing of their own accord rightly to help, he foretold the future victory. So, then, he could, though not consulted, have warned about the Gibeonites themselves[^28], who they were, when they lied that they were from afar, had not that oath, which compelled them to spare those subject to them, pleased him. For they had believed God, whom they had heard[^29] promise to his people that he would overthrow those nations, and that they would obtain their land; and he rewarded this faith of theirs in a manner by not betraying them.

## Concerning the five kings of the Jebusites, or the Amorites.

**15.** (10:5-6) It is asked how Adonizedek, king of the city of Jerusalem, and the other four with whom he besieged the Gibeonites, according to the *Septuagint* translators, are first called kings of the Jebusites when they came together to besiege them, but afterward kings of the Amorites by the Gibeonites themselves, when they sent messengers to Joshua to free them from the siege. But, as we have been able to inspect in that translation which is from the Hebrew, in both places they are called Amorites: although it is established that the king of the city of Jerusalem was of the Jebusites, because the city itself was called Jebus, as it were the metropolis of that nation[^30]; and Scripture very often mentions seven nations, which God promised that he would exterminate from before his people, of which one is said to be of the Amorites. Unless perhaps this name was the universal one of all, or rather of the greater part: so that not one, but several among these seven were comprised under this name; although there was also one of the seven which was properly called of the Amorites—just as there is a certain part which is properly called Libya, although this name applies to the whole of Africa; and a certain part which is properly called Asia, although some have reckoned Asia to be half the world, others a third part of the world. For, as is established, the Canaanites too are mentioned as one nation among those seven, and yet that whole land is originally called the land of Canaan.

## A command of the Lord, even if it seems cruel, is carried out without cruelty.

**16.** (11:14-15) *Joshua left in it nothing breathing, as the Lord commanded Moses his servant, and Moses likewise commanded Joshua, and so Joshua did: he transgressed nothing of all the things which the Lord appointed to Moses.*[^31] On this account that is in no way to be thought cruelty, that Joshua left nothing alive in the cities delivered to him, because God had commanded this. But those who think that on this account God himself was cruel, and on this account are unwilling to believe that he was the author of the true God of the Old Testament, judge as perversely about the works of God as about the sins of men, not knowing what each is worthy to suffer, and thinking it a great evil when things destined to fall are cast down, and mortals die.

## What cities the Hebrews were able to take.

**17.** (11:19) *And there was no city which was not delivered to the children of Israel.*[^32] It is asked how this is true, since neither in the later times of the Judges, nor in the times of the Kings, were the Hebrews able to take absolutely all the cities of those seven nations. But it is to be understood either thus, that Joshua approached no city in war which he did not take: or at least that none was not taken, but of those that were in the regions mentioned above. For the regions were enumerated in which were the cities about which this conclusion was made: *and he took all in war.*

## The heart of the enemy is said to have been made resolute, that they should go out to war against Israel.

**18.** (11:20) *Because it was done by the Lord that their heart was made resolute, that they should go out to war against Israel, that they might be exterminated; so that mercy should not be given them, but that they might be exterminated, as the Lord said to Moses.*[^33] It was said thus, *that this was done by the Lord, that their heart was made resolute,* that is, that their heart was hardened, as concerning Pharaoh[^34]; which is by no means to be doubted to be done justly by a divine and deep judgment, when God forsakes and the enemy takes possession: which is to be taken just as there too.

But here something else is troubling: how it was said that their heart was made resolute for this, that they should rise up to war against Israel, and that on this account mercy should not be shown them: as though it ought to be shown, if they did not make war; when God had commanded that none of them be spared; and on this account they spared the Gibeonites, because, pretending that they had come from a far land, they had held them to their oath.[^35] But since the Israelites of their own accord showed mercy to certain ones, although against God's command; it is to be understood that this was said for this reason: that these made war in such a way that they were not spared, nor were the Israelites themselves, neglecting God's command, bent toward mercy by them.

This indeed, under the leadership of Joshua, who carefully kept all the divine commands, is not to be believed to have been able to happen: nevertheless not even he himself would have destroyed these men so quickly, had they not most conspiringly opposed; and so it could come about that, with these not subdued by him who took care to fulfill God's commands, they remained until that time when those who did not do God's commands with such care could spare them after the death of Joshua.

For even while he was still living they spared certain ones, only subjugating them to their dominion: but some they could not even conquer. But these things were not done under his leadership; rather, when, now an old man, he was at rest from war, only dividing the lands for them; so that they themselves, with him now no longer making war, held the lands divided to them—partly already emptied of the enemy, partly to be taken by fighting. And that they were not at all able to conquer some, that this was of divine providence will appear opportunely in certain places of the Scriptures.

## What is narrated about the coming up of Pharaoh from Egypt.

**19.** (16:10) *And Ephraim did not destroy the Canaanite who dwelt in Gezer: and the Canaanite dwelt in Ephraim to this day, until Pharaoh king of Egypt came up, and took the city, and burned it with fire; and the Canaanites, and the Perizzites, and those who dwelt in Gezer he ran through; and Pharaoh gave it as a dowry to his daughter.*[^36] What was said of Pharaoh the king, I wonder whether we ought to understand it as said prophetically; since this history is believed to have been written down in those times in which those things were recent events. And what great thing could be chosen to be said prophetically, when past things are narrated, and greater future things, even most necessary ones, are passed over in silence?

Accordingly it is rather to be thought that the Seventy translators, who are reported to have translated by prophetic authority from that very marvelous agreement, added these things; not as foretelling future events, but because they themselves were in that time in which they remembered these things to have been done, and had read them in the books of *Kings*[^37]. For it was done in the times of the kings. This has seemed the more credible to us, since we have inspected the translation which is from the Hebrew, and have not found this there: just as neither that which was said about Jericho, that Hozah, who rebuilt it, incurred the curse which Joshua had spoken.

For it is written thus: *and Joshua adjured on that day: cursed be the man who will raise up and build that city: in his firstborn he will found it, and in his youngest he will set up its gates*[^38]: thus far it is found in the translation from the Hebrew. But that which follows: *and so did Hozah who was from Bethel; in Abiram his firstborn he founded it, and in his youngest, the one preserved, he set up its gates*[^39]: this is not read there. Hence it appears to have been inserted by the Seventy, who knew it had been done.

## How the Amorites were made tributaries to Ephraim against the command of the Lord.

**20.** (19:47) *And the Amorite remained to dwell in Elom and in Salumin, and the hand of Ephraim was heavy upon them, and they became tributaries to them.*[^40] This now was being done against the Lord's command, and Joshua was still living, but their leader in those battles was no longer present because of old age. On this account it was said that it was done by the Lord that the heart of those who conspired together to go to war against Joshua[^41] was made resolute, lest that mercy be shown them, even against God's command[^42], if they remained unsubdued, and, with Joshua growing old or dying, were left to be subdued by the children of Israel, who could spare them against the Lord's command, which he would not do.

## Israel did not root out the nations that held the land of promise.

**21.** 1. (21:41-43) It is rightly asked: since Israel, not only up to the day of Joshua's death, but even afterward, did not root out the nations that held the Land of Promise, although, those being in part subdued, they dwelt in that same promised inheritance, how is it to be understood what was said: *and the Lord gave Israel all the land which he had sworn to give to their fathers, and they inherited it, and dwelt in it. And the Lord gave them rest round about, as he had sworn to their fathers: not one of all their enemies stood before them; the Lord delivered all their enemies into their hands. There fell nothing of all the good words which the Lord spoke to the children of Israel; all came to pass.*

**21.** 2. All things, then, are to be carefully considered. And first it must be seen of how many nations the land was promised to the Israelites. Seven nations indeed seem to be constantly mentioned, as is read in *Exodus*: *and the Lord said to Moses: Go, and go up from here, you and your people, whom you led out of the land of Egypt, into the land which I swore to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, saying: To your seed I will give it: and at the same time I will send before your face my angel, and he will cast out the Amorite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, and the Girgashite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, and the Canaanite.*[^43] The land, then, of these seven nations God seems to have promised to the fathers.

It is also written in *Deuteronomy* much more expressly: *but if you approach a city to attack it, and call them out with peace, if indeed they answer you peacefully, and open to you, all the people who are found in the city shall be tributary to you and obedient: but if they do not obey you, and make war against you, then you shall besiege it; and the Lord your God will deliver it into your hands, and you shall kill every male in it by the slaughter of the sword, except the women; and the goods, and all the cattle, and all whatever is in the city, and all the utensils you shall plunder for yourself; and you shall eat all the plunder of your enemies, which the Lord your God gives you. So you shall do to all the cities which are very far from you, which are not of the cities of these nations. But of these cities, which the Lord your God gives you to inherit their land, you shall not keep alive anything living, but you shall utterly devote them to destruction; the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Canaanite, and the Perizzite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, and the Girgashite, as the Lord your God commanded you.*[^44]

And here it is plain that the land of these seven nations was promised as an inheritance, which the Israelites would possess once those same nations had been subdued to the point of utter destruction. For the others, which were found farther off beyond these nations, he willed to be made tributary to them, if they did not resist: but if they resisted, those too to be killed and given over to destruction, except the cattle and the things that could come as spoil.

Likewise, in another place in *Deuteronomy* it is read thus: *and it shall be, when the Lord your God has brought you into the land which you are entering there to inherit it, and has taken away great and many nations from before you, the Hittite, and the Girgashite, and the Amorite, and the Perizzite, and the Canaanite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, seven nations great and many and stronger than you; and the Lord your God will deliver them into your hands, and you will strike them, you will utterly exterminate them. You shall make no covenant with them, nor have mercy on them, nor join in marriages with them: you shall not give your daughter to his son, nor take his daughter for your son*[^45], and so on.

Accordingly, by these and other places of the Scriptures it is often shown that the children of Israel received the lands of these seven nations as an inheritance in such a way that they dwelt there not with those who held those same lands, but in their place. Nevertheless, in *Genesis* not these seven nations only, but eleven, are promised to the seed of Abraham. For it is read thus: *on that day the Lord God made a covenant with Abraham, saying: To your seed I will give this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates, the Kenites, and the Kenizzites, and the Kadmonites, and the Hittites, and the Perizzites, and the Rephaim, and the Amorites, and the Canaanites, and the Hivites, and the Girgashites, and the Jebusites.*[^46]

This question is resolved in such a way that we understand this to have been a prophecy in advance, that Solomon was going to stretch out and extend his kingdom to those bounds, concerning whom it is written thus: *and all the purpose of Solomon, which he had determined to build in Jerusalem and in Lebanon and in all the land of his power: all the people who were left of the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Perizzite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, who were not of Israel, of the sons of those who remained with them in the land, whom the children of Israel had not destroyed, Solomon subjugated them to tribute to this day.*[^47]

Behold, the remnants of the peoples who were to be subdued and altogether destroyed by God's command, Solomon subjugated to tribute, whom indeed, according to God's command, he ought to have destroyed: but yet, subjugated, they were possessed as tributaries.

A little later it is read thus: *and he was ruling over all the kings from the river to the land of the Philistines, and to the bounds of Egypt.*[^48]

Behold where was fulfilled what in Genesis God, speaking to Abraham and promising, had foretold. For *from the river* here is understood "from the Euphrates": for the "great river" in those places, even without the proper name added, can be understood. For this cannot be taken of the Jordan, since the Israelites had already, even before the reign of Solomon, obtained lands both on this side of the Jordan and beyond the Jordan. Therefore from the river Euphrates on the side of the east, to the bounds of Egypt, which was the part on the west for them, the Scripture of the Kingdoms said the kingdom of Solomon was stretched out. Then, therefore, more was subjugated than those seven nations held; and by this, then, not seven, but eleven nations were reduced to servitude.

For what is written in the books of Kingdoms, *to the bounds of Egypt from the river,* when Scripture meant to show how far from east to west the kingdom was stretched out; this same thing in *Genesis,* when it was defined from west to east, was said, *from the river of Egypt to the great river Euphrates.*[^49] For the river of Egypt, which is the boundary separating the kingdom of Israel from Egypt, is not the Nile, but is another, not a great river, which flows through the city of Rhinocorura, whence already toward the east the Land of Promise begins. So, then, it had been appointed for the children of Israel that they should themselves inhabit the lands of seven nations, those nations being exterminated and destroyed; but should reign over others, subject and tributary, up to the river Euphrates. And although in this they had not obeyed God, because even of those whom they ought to exterminate they had made some obedient; yet God in the times of Solomon fulfilled the faith of his promise.

**21.** 4. Now, then, in the book of *Joshua* the son of Nun, what we have undertaken to consider, how will it be true: *and God gave Israel all the land which he had sworn to give to their fathers, and they inherited it*? how, with Joshua still living, did he give all the land, when they had not yet even overcome the remnants of those seven nations? For what follows, *and they inherited it,* is true; because they were there, and had settled there.

Then what it adds: *and the Lord gave them rest round about, as he had sworn to their fathers,* is true; because, with Joshua still living, the remnants of those nations did not indeed yield to them, but none of them dared to provoke them in war in the lands where they had settled.

For this reason was said also what is then added: *not one of all their enemies stood before them.*

But what follows: *but the Lord delivered all their enemies into their hands;* he meant those to be understood as enemies who dared to go out to war against them.

Then what it says: *there fell nothing of all the good words which the Lord spoke to the children of Israel; all came to pass,* it means to be understood thus: that although they had now acted against the Lord's command, by sparing certain ones of those seven nations and making them obedient, yet they were still safe among them. Therefore, when it had said, *of all the words,* it added, *good;* because the curses appointed for despisers and transgressors had not yet come to pass.

It remains, then, that what it says, *the Lord gave Israel all the land which he had sworn to give to their fathers,* be understood according to this: that although there were still remnants of those nations to be crushed and exterminated, or of others up to the river Euphrates, either to be subjugated if they did not resist, or to be destroyed if they resisted; yet they were left for their use, that they might be exercised in them, lest, weak in carnal affections and desires, they should be unable to bear so great a sudden prosperity of temporal things with moderation and profit, but, puffed up, should the sooner perish—which will be shown opportunely in another place. All the land, then, was given to them; because even that part which had not yet been given into possession had already been given for a certain usefulness of exercise.

## Concerning the words: Not one stood before them . . .

**22.** (21:42) As for what it says: *not one of all their enemies stood before them,* it can be asked how this is true, since concerning the tribe of Dan it is written above that their enemies did not permit them to go down into the valley, and prevailed against them in the mountains.[^50] But what we said when Scripture mentioned the twelve sons of Jacob as born in Mesopotamia, where Benjamin had not been born[^51]; this too is to be understood here: that for the whole people eleven tribes were reckoned, by that rule which has become well enough known to us in other places of the Scriptures. But if the cause is asked, why this tribe, in the lot which fell to it, did not obtain sufficient lands, and was afflicted by those who held them; it is to be believed that this lies in the secret counsel of God. Nevertheless, when Jacob blessed his sons, he said such things about this Dan that from that very tribe the Antichrist is thought to be going to arise.[^52] Hence we have not now undertaken to say more, since this question can also be resolved thus: that *not one of all their enemies stood before them,* when they waged war together under the command of one leader, before the places to be defended were divided among the individual tribes.

## The sacrifice of our peace offerings is spoken of in the plural number.

**23.** (22:27) *And in the sacrifices of our peace offerings.*[^53] Because the sacrifices were spoken of in the plural, the peace offerings too are in the plural. Here it is to be noted more carefully how the sacrifice of a peace offering is usually spoken of; for if we take it as Christ, since he himself was called *the salvation of God*[^54], it does not appear how this word can be understood in the plural. For *one is our Lord Jesus Christ*[^55]: although men are called "anointed ones" through his grace, as is read in the *Psalm*: *do not touch my anointed ones*[^56]. But whether they can be called "savings" or "salvations" is not easily to be ventured: for he alone is *the Savior of the body.*[^57]

## What Joshua says about his approaching death.

**24.** (23:14) What Joshua says about his approaching death: *but I am running back the way, as also all who are upon the earth;* in the translation which is from the Hebrew we find, *I am entering the way.* So, then, what the Seventy said, *I run back,* is to be taken as it was said to man: *until you return to the earth from which you were taken*[^58]; so that it is understood as said with reference to the body.

But with reference to the soul, if we should wish to take it as it is set down in *Ecclesiastes*: *and the spirit will return to God who gave it*[^59]; I do not think it can be said of all, but of those who have so lived as to deserve to return to God, as to the author by whom they were created. For this cannot rightly be understood even of those of whom it is said: *a spirit going forth and not returning*[^60].

But this holy man, Joshua the son of Nun, if he had not added, *as also all who are upon the earth,* there would be no question; for we would believe nothing else of him than what we read he is worthy of: but since it was added, *as also all who are upon the earth;* it is surprising if this, which the Latin translator set down as *I run back,* is not rather to be said *I run through* or *I run out,* if this can be said which the Greek has, ἀποτρέχω. For all run through, or run out, the way of this life, when they have come to its end. But because this word is set down where the parents of Rebekah say to the servant of Abraham: *behold Rebekah; taking her, run back, and let her be the wife of your master*[^61]; therefore here too this word was so translated.

## The translation of the Seventy which is from the Hebrew.

**25.** (24:3) What the Seventy translators have: *and I took your father Abraham from across the river, and led him through all the land;* the translation which is from the Hebrew has: *and I brought him into the land of Canaan.* It is surprising, then, if the Seventy wished to set down *all the land* for *the land of Canaan,* unless looking to the prophecy, so that it may rather be taken, from the promise of God, as though done, what was being foretold as most certainly to come about in Christ and in the Church, which is the true seed of Abraham; not in the children of the flesh, but in the children of the promise.[^62]

## How those who were in Jericho are said to have made war.

**26.** (24:11) *And those who dwelt in Jericho made war against you.* It can be asked how this is true, since, with the gates shut, they protected themselves only by the circuit of the walls. But it was rightly said, because even to shut the gates against the enemy pertains to war. For they did not send envoys to ask for peace. Hence, if it had been said: "they fought against you," it would be false. For war does not have continuous fighting, but sometimes frequent, sometimes rare, sometimes none. Yet it is war when there is, in a manner, armed dissension.

## How wasps are said to have been sent, which is not read.

**27.** (24:12) What does it mean that, among the other things which Joshua the son of Nun mentions the Lord to have done toward the Israelites, he says: *he sent wasps before you, and cast them out from your face*: which is also read in the book of *Wisdom*[^63], and yet is nowhere found, among the things that were done, to have happened? Did he perhaps, by a transferred word, mean by *wasps* the very sharp stings of fear, by which, as it were with rumors flying about, they were stung to flee, or the airy hidden spirits, which in the *Psalm* he calls *through evil angels*[^64]? Unless perhaps someone should say that not all the things that were done are written down; and that this too was done visibly, so as to mean that real wasps are to be understood.

## Perfect servitude to the holiness of God is impossible for human frailty.

**28.** (24:19) What does it mean that Joshua said to the people: *you cannot serve the Lord, for God is holy.* Is it because to be in a manner attuned to his holiness by perfect servitude is impossible for human frailty? Hearing this, they ought not only to have chosen his servitude, but also to have presumed upon his help and mercy: which he understood who says in the *Psalm*: *enter not into judgment with your servant; for no one living will be justified in your sight.*[^65]

But these men chose rather to presume upon themselves, that they could serve God without any offense, so that even then they began what the Apostle expressed concerning them: *for being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they were not subject to the righteousness of God*[^66]. So for them now *the Law entered in, that the offense might abound,* and afterward *grace might more abound*[^67] through the Lord Christ, who is the end of the Law, unto righteousness to everyone who believes.[^68]

## How he bids foreign gods to be removed.

**29.** (24:23) What does it mean that the same Joshua, speaking to the people, says: *and now take away the foreign gods that are among you, and direct your hearts to the Lord God of Israel.* For they are not to be believed to have still had with them any images of the nations, since he had earlier proclaimed their obedience: or indeed, if they had them, after so many threatenings of the Law, would that prosperity have followed them; when such vengeance was taken upon them, because one of them had committed theft from the devoted things? Finally, Jacob said this to those who had gone out with him from Mesopotamia, where idols were so worshiped that even Rachel stole her father's[^69]: but after that admonition of Jacob, they gave up what they had[^70]; whence it appeared that this was said to them because the one who said it knew that they had them.

But now, after this admonition of Joshua the son of Nun, no one brought forth any such thing. Nor yet is it to be thought that he commanded this in vain; for he does not say: "and now take away the foreign gods if any are among you"; but altogether as though knowing they were there, he says, *that are among you.* Accordingly the holy prophet discerned in their hearts thoughts about God that were foreign to God, and admonished that these themselves be removed. For whoever thinks of God as God is not, certainly carries a foreign and false god in his thought. But who is there who thinks of God just as he is?

And by this it remains for the faithful, as long as they are pilgrims away from the Lord[^71], to remove from their heart the vain phantasms that rush in, which present themselves to those who think, as though God were such or such, as he certainly is not: and to direct the heart to him faithfully, so that, in the way and to the measure that he knows is expedient for us, he himself may make himself known through his Spirit, until all falsehood is taken away, whence it was said: *every man is a liar*[^72]; and, with not only impious falsity but even the glass and the dark manner itself done away, we may know face to face, as we also are known: as the Apostle says: *we see now through a glass in a dark manner, but then face to face: now I know in part; but then I shall know even as I am known.*[^73]

## Concerning the covenant established by Joshua the son of Nun, and concerning the stone set up by him under the terebinth.

**30.** 1. (24:25-27) *And Joshua made a covenant with the people on that day, and gave them a Law and a judgment in Shiloh before the tabernacle of the Lord God of Israel. And he wrote these words in the book of the Law of God; and he took a great stone, and Joshua set it up under the terebinth before the Lord. And Joshua said to the people: Behold, this stone shall be to you for a testimony, because it has heard all the things that were said by the Lord, whatever he spoke to you today, and it shall be to you for a testimony in the last days, when you have lied to the Lord your God.*

Those who hear these words not only on the surface, but search them out somewhat more deeply, ought by no means to think so great a man so foolish as to have believed that the words of God which he spoke to the people were heard by a lifeless stone: which, even if it had been fashioned by a craftsman into the likeness of a man, would certainly be reckoned among those of whom it is sung in the *Psalm*: *they have ears, and will not hear*[^74]. For not only do the idols of the nations, of gold and silver, not hear[^75], but, even if they are of stone, they hear.

But by this stone he certainly signified him who was a stone of offense to the unbelieving Jews, and a rock of scandal; who, when he was rejected by the builders, became the head of the corner[^76]: whom that rock also prefigured which, struck with wood, poured forth drink for the thirsting people[^77]; of which the Apostle says: *and they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them; and the rock was Christ.*[^78] Hence this excellent leader circumcised the people with stone knives[^79]: which stone knives were buried together with him too, that they might show a deep mystery that would profit those who came after. So, then, this stone too, although visibly set up, we ought to take spiritually, as a testimony that would come against the unbelieving Jews, that is, the lying ones; concerning whom the *Psalm* says: *the enemies of the Lord have lied to him.*[^80] For not in vain, when God's servant Moses, or rather God through him, had already made a covenant with the people[^81], which was in the Ark, which is called the Ark of the Covenant, and in the books of the Law written with so great a manifoldness of mysteries and precepts; yet here too it was said: *Joshua made a covenant with the people on that day.*

For the repetition of the covenant signifies the New Covenant; which *Deuteronomy* too signifies, which is interpreted "Second Law"; and which the renewed tablets too signify, the former being broken.[^82] For what was to be fulfilled in one way had to be signified in many ways. As for the stone being set up under the terebinth, this signifies the same as the rod to the rock, that water might flow forth; for neither here was the stone set up without wood. But it was set up beneath for this reason, that he would not have been lifted up on the Cross had he not been brought low by humility; or because at that time, when Joshua the son of Nun was doing this, the mystery was still to be kept in shadow. The wood of the terebinth, too, exudes a medicinal tear—a tree which was set down by the Seventy translators in this place, although according to other translators "oak" is read.

**30.** 2. It is indeed surprising that at least in his last words, with which Joshua the man of God addressed the people, he did not reproach them at all for sparing those nations which the Lord had commanded to be destroyed under the ban, to the point of utter slaughter. For it is written thus: *and it came to pass, after the children of Israel had grown strong, that they made the Canaanites obedient, but did not utterly exterminate them.*[^83] For Scripture testified that at first they were not able to do this[^84]: but now, after they had prevailed, so as to make them obedient, that they did not also exterminate them, this was certainly done against the Lord's command: which was done by no one while Joshua led the army.

Why, then, did he not reproach them in his last address, that in this they had neglected the Lord's commands? Is it perhaps because Scripture first said that they were not able—certainly before they prevailed—and that even when they had prevailed they are to be believed to have feared, lest perhaps, with the others ready to obey if they had been unwilling to spare, they should drive them to fight against themselves more sharply out of that very despair, and they should not be able to overcome them?

This human fear, then, the Lord was unwilling to impute to them, although in it there appears a certain falling short of faith, which, if it had been strong in them, those things would have followed which followed Joshua himself when he made war. But because there was not so great a faith in them, even when they had prevailed over their adversaries, they did not, out of fear, dare to fight with them to the point of their utter slaughter.

This fear, as I said, coming not from malice, nor from pride or contempt of the Lord's command, but from weakness of mind, the Lord was unwilling to impute to them, when he spoke to them his last words through Joshua. Hence the Apostle too says: *Alexander the coppersmith did me much harm; the Lord will repay him according to his works.* But of those who had deserted him in his peril, not from malice but from fear, he spoke thus: *at my first defense no one was present with me, but all forsook me; let it not be imputed to them.*[^85]
