# On the rings of the altar of incense.

**131.** (30, 3-4) When he was speaking of the rings of the altar of incense, which altar he ordered not to be overlaid with bronze, but with gold: *and you shall make two pure golden rings,* he says, *under its twisted crown, on the two sides you shall make them on the two flanks*: since the Greek has *eis ta dyo klite poieseis en tois dysi pleurois.* For *klite* are "sides," and *pleura* are "sides." Whence some Latins rendered thus: *on the two parts you shall make them on the two sides.* But the Greek does not say *mere,* which is "parts," but *klite,* which is "sides." For this word is in that *Psalm,* where it is written: *your wife like a fruitful vine on the sides of your house*[^1]. And by this only the case differs, since first he put the accusative, but afterward the ablative: *on the two sides you shall make them, on the two sides.* But what the sense is, is difficult to attain; unless perhaps, as Scripture is accustomed to love ellipsis, so that something is lacking and is to be understood, here too "they shall be" is to be understood: so that this is the sense: "*on the two sides you shall make them, on the two sides* they shall be"; that is, "you shall make the rings at the two sides, since they shall be on the two sides."

[^1]: Ps 128:3 (127).
