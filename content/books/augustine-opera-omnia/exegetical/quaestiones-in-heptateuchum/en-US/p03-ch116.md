# How it is called the "rational" on account of the poverty of the language.

**116.** (28, 22) *And you shall make upon the rational fringes, surrounding it, a chained work of pure gold.* What the Latins rendered *rational* the poverty of the language brought about. For the Greek has *logion,* not *logikon.* But we are accustomed to call "rational" that which the Greeks call *logikon.* But since *logos* in the Greek language is ambiguous, whether it signifies "word" or "reason," because it is the name of both things; where *logion* was thought to be derived from "word," our translators rendered it "oracle": for what we have, *the words of the Lord, chaste words*[^1], the Greeks have as *logia*; but here in the priestly vestment, that which was prescribed to be made of gold and hyacinth and purple and twice-twisted scarlet and twice-twisted fine linen, a double square, which was to be on the priest's breast, and to be called *logion*—it being uncertain whether it was derived from "reason" or from "word"—our translators, thinking it rather derived from "reason," called it *rational.*

[^1]: Ps 12:6 (11).
