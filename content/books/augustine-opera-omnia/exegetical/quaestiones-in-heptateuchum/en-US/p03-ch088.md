# Mercy is good, but it ought not to be against judgment.

**88.** (23, 3) *And you shall not show pity to a poor man in judgment.* Had he not added, *in judgment,* it would be a great question. But it would have to be understood, even if it were not written. For above he had said: *you shall not join with the multitude to turn aside with the many, so as to pervert judgment*[^1]: and by this, *and you shall not show pity to a poor man,* could be understood, *in judgment.* But since it was added, there is no question that this is prescribed, lest perhaps when we judge we see that justice is for the rich against the poor, and we seem to ourselves to do rightly if, against justice, we favor the poor man for the sake of mercy. Mercy, then, is good, but it ought not to be against judgment. And by "judgment" Scripture means that which is just. But lest anyone, on account of this statement, should think God forbids mercy, what follows very fittingly follows: *but if you come upon your enemy's ox, or his beast of burden, straying, leading it back you shall restore it to him*[^2]: so that you may know you are not forbidden to do mercy—do it even toward your enemies, when the power is free from judging[^3]; for when you lead back your enemy's straying ox and restore it, you do not sit as judge among any parties.

[^1]: Exod 23:2.
[^2]: Exod 23:4.
[^3]: See *Answer to an Enemy of the Law and the Prophets* 1.17.35.
