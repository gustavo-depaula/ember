# The causes of the hardening of Pharaoh's heart.

**24.** (8:15) *And Pharaoh saw that there was relief, and his heart was made heavy, and he did not heed them, as the Lord had said*. Here it appears that the causes of the hardening of Pharaoh's heart were not only that his enchanters did similar things, but also the very patience of God, by which he spared. The patience of God, according to the hearts of men, is useful to some for repenting, useless to some for resisting God and persevering in evil: yet it is not useless in itself, but according to the evil heart, as we have already said.

This too the Apostle says: *do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But according to the hardness of your heart and your impenitent heart, you store up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works*[^1]. For elsewhere too, when he said, *we are the good fragrance of Christ in every place*, he also added: *both in those who are saved and in those who perish*[^2].

He did not say that he was the good fragrance of Christ to those who are saved, but a bad one to those who perish; but he said only that he was a good fragrance. But those are such that they perish even by a good fragrance, according to the quality of their heart, as has often been said—which is to be changed by a good will in the grace of God, so that the judgments of God may begin to profit them, which harm evil hearts.

Hence that man, his heart changed for the better, sang: *my soul will live, and will praise you; and your judgments will help me*[^3]. He did not say "your gifts," or "your rewards," but *your judgments*. But it is a great thing that with sincere confidence it can be said: *prove me, Lord, and test me; burn my reins and my heart*. And lest he should seem to have attributed anything to his own powers, he immediately added: *for your mercy is before my eyes, and I have been well pleased in your truth*[^4]. He mentions the mercy done toward him, so that he can be well pleased in the truth; since *all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth*[^5].

[^1]: Rom 2:4-6.
[^2]: 2 Cor 2:15.
[^3]: Ps 119:175 (118:175).
[^4]: Ps 26:2-3 (25:2-3).
[^5]: Ps 25:10 (24:10).
