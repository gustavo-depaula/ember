# How God can be understood as being angry.

**10.** (4:14-16) *And the Lord, angry with anger, said*. How God can be understood as being angry—since it is not as man, through irrational disturbance—must be held throughout, wherever Scripture says anything of the sort, lest the same things have to be said often about this. But it is rightly asked why he here, being angry, said about his brother Moses that he himself would speak to the people for him: for he seems, as to one distrustful, not to have given the fullest faculty which he was about to give; and to have wished that to be done through two which could be done through one, if he had believed.

Nevertheless all these same words, considered more carefully, do not signify that the Lord, being angry, gave Aaron as a punishment. For he says thus: *is not Aaron your brother the Levite? I know that he, speaking, will speak*. By which words God is shown rather to have rebuked the man who feared to go because he was less suitable, when he had a brother through whom he could speak to the people what he wished; since he himself was of a thin voice and a slower tongue—although he ought to have hoped for everything from God.

Then he says the same things which he had promised a little before, even after he was angry. For he had said, *I will open your mouth, and instruct you*; but now he says, *I will open your mouth and his mouth, and instruct you what you are to do*; but since he added, *and he will speak for you to the people*, the opening of the mouth seems to have been granted, on account of which Moses says that he is of a slower tongue.

But concerning the thinness of voice the Lord willed to grant him nothing, but on account of this added the help of his brother, who could use that voice which would suffice for teaching the people. So when he says, *and you will put my words in his mouth*, he shows that he was about to give him words to be spoken: for if they were only to be heard, as by the people, he would say "in his ears."

Then, as to what he says a little after, *and he will speak for you to the people, and he will be your mouth*, here too "to the people" is understood. And when he says, *he will speak for you to the people*, he sufficiently indicates in Moses the headship, in Aaron the ministry.

Then, as to what he says, *but you will be to him as those things which are toward God*, here perhaps a great sacrament must be searched out, of which it bears the figure—as it were, Moses in the middle between God and Aaron, and Aaron in the middle between Moses and the people.
