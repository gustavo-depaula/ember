# Whether the hardening of Pharaoh's heart was necessary.

**18.** (7:3) God constantly says, *I will harden Pharaoh's heart*: and as it were brings in the cause why he does this: *I will harden*, he says, *Pharaoh's heart, and I will fulfill my signs and my portents in Egypt*; as if the hardening of Pharaoh's heart were necessary, so that the signs of God might be multiplied or fulfilled in Egypt.

God, then, makes good use of evil hearts, for that which he wishes to show to the good, or which he is about to do for the good. And although the quality of each heart in malice—that is, what kind of heart it has toward evil—comes about by its own fault, which has grown ingrained from the choice of the will; yet that by that evil quality it is moved this way or that—when, whether this way or that, it is moved evilly—comes about by causes by which the mind is driven: which causes, that they should exist or not exist, is not in the power of man; but they come from the hidden providence, plainly most just and most wise, of God who disposes and administers the whole that he created.

That Pharaoh, then, had such a heart that it was not moved by the patience of God to piety, but rather to impiety, was of his own fault: but that those things were done by which a heart so malignant by its own fault resisted the commands of God (for this is what is called *hardened*, because it did not consent flexibly, but resisted inflexibly), was of the divine dispensation, by which for such a heart there was prepared a punishment not only not unjust, but evidently just, by which those who fear God might be corrected.

For when, say, a gain is set before someone for the sake of which a murder might be committed, the greedy man is moved one way, the despiser of money another: the one, namely, to perpetrate the crime, the other to avoid it; yet the setting before them of that very gain was not in the power of either of them. So causes come to evil men, which are not indeed in their power, but make of them what they have found them already made by their own faults from a past will. It must surely be seen whether it can be taken also thus: *I will harden*, as if he said, "I will show how hard it is."
