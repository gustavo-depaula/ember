# Whether only the firstborn were destroyed.

**44.** (12, 30) *And there was a great cry in the land of Egypt. For there was no house in which there was not one dead.* Could there not have been some house that had no firstborn? Since, then, only the firstborn were dying, how was there none that did not have a dead one? Or had this too been divinely arranged by God's foreknowledge, that in absolutely all houses in which the Egyptians were to be struck there should be firstborn? From this plague, to be sure, the Egyptians who dwelt in the land of Goshen are not to be thought immune; for it belonged to men, or to animals, not to the land—that is, the firstborn men and animals were dying by a hidden, angelic stroke, and nothing was done in the land or in the sky, such as the frog, or the locust, or the darkness, by which the inhabitants might be afflicted. For since the land of Goshen had been exempt from such plagues, without doubt the benefit reached those Egyptians who dwelt in that same land with the Hebrews; but by this plague all their firstborn were struck.
