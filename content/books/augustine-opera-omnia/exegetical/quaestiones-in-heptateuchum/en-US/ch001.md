# Revisions II

## Chapter 55 (82): The Seven Books of Questions

**1.** At the same time I also wrote seven books of *Questions* on these same divine books, which I chose to call by this name because the matters discussed there I have set forth as things to be inquired into rather than as inquiries resolved—although it seems to me that many more of them have been so thoroughly treated that they may not unreasonably be judged to have been resolved and explained.

We had also begun in the same way to consider the *books of Kingdoms*; but, having made little progress, we turned our attention to other matters that were more pressing.

In the first book, however, where the discussion concerns the variegated rods that Jacob set in the water so that the sheep, having been placed there to conceive, might see them when they drank and so bear variegated offspring[^1], we did not give a good account of why he did not set them out again when the sheep conceived a second time—that is, when they conceived other offspring—but only at the first conception[^2]. For the explanation of another question[^3], where it is asked why Jacob said to his father-in-law, *and you have cheated my wage by ten ewe-lambs*[^4], having been unraveled with sufficient truth, shows that this one, though it ought to have been solved, was not solved.

**2.** In the third book also, where the discussion concerns the high priest[^5]—how he begot sons, since he was under the necessity of entering twice a day into the Holy of Holies, where the altar of incense was, to offer incense morning and evening[^6], into which, as the Law says, he could not enter while unclean, and since the same Law says that a man becomes unclean even from intercourse with his wife[^7], whom indeed it commands to wash with water but says that, even when washed, he is *unclean until evening*—I said: *it would follow that he must either be continent or that the incense must be omitted on certain days,* not seeing that this did not follow. For what is written, *he will be unclean until evening*, can be understood in this way: that by evening itself he was no longer unclean, but only up until it, so that at evening, now clean, he might offer the incense, since for the begetting of sons he had united with his wife after the morning incense.

Likewise, where it was asked how the high priest was forbidden to go in over the body of his father[^8], since he ought not to be made priest—when there was but one—except after the death of his priestly father, I said: *that for this reason it was necessary, while the father was not yet buried, that immediately after his death his son be appointed to succeed the father, on account also of the continual incense which had to be offered twice a day*[^9]*,* the very priest who is forbidden to go in over the death of his father not yet buried.

But I gave too little attention to the fact that this could have been commanded rather on account of those who were to become high priests not as successors to high-priestly fathers, but nonetheless from among the sons—that is, from the descendants of Aaron—if perhaps a high priest either had no sons, or had sons so reprobate that none of them ought to succeed his father, as Samuel succeeded the high priest Eli[^10], though he was not himself the son of the priest, but was nonetheless from the sons, that is, from the descendants of Aaron.

**3.** Concerning the thief also, to whom it was said, *today you will be with me in Paradise*[^11], I set down as though certain that he had not been visibly baptized, whereas it is uncertain and it is rather to be believed that he had been baptized, as I myself later argued elsewhere[^12].

Likewise, what I said in the fifth book, where the mothers are mentioned in the gospel genealogies, namely *that they are placed there only together with the fathers*[^13], is indeed true, but does not pertain to the matter under discussion. For the discussion was about those who married the wives of brothers or kinsmen who had died without children, on account of the two fathers of Joseph, one of whom Matthew mentions and the other Luke. I discussed this question carefully in this work, when we were revising our work *Answer to Faustus the Manichean*[^14].

This work begins thus: *When in reading the Holy Scriptures that are called canonical*.
