# Whether what is still being formed in the womb could be understood as animate.

**80.** (21, 22-25) *But if two men quarrel, and strike a woman who is with child, and her infant comes forth not yet formed; he shall suffer a penalty, as much as the woman's husband shall impose, and he shall pay upon demand.* It seems to me that these things are said for the sake of some signification, rather than that Scripture is occupied with deeds of this kind. For if it were attending to this, that a pregnant woman, when struck, not be driven to abortion, it would not have set down two quarreling men, since this can be committed also by one, who has quarreled with the woman herself, or even has not quarreled, but did it by wishing to harm another's posterity. But that he did not wish an unformed offspring to pertain to homicide, surely he did not reckon as a human being what is carried in the womb in such a state.

Here a question concerning the soul is commonly raised, whether what is not formed cannot even be understood as animate, and therefore it is not homicide, because it cannot be called killed, if it did not yet have a soul. For it continues and says: *but if it is formed, he shall give soul for soul.* Where what else is understood, but "and he himself shall die"? For from this occasion he now prescribes this in the rest too: *eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, burning for burning, wound for wound, bruise for bruise*: by the equity, namely, of retaliation.

Which Law established this in order to show what penalty is owed. For unless through the Law it were known what penalty was owed, how would it be known what pardon remits, so that it could be said: *forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^1]? Debtors, therefore, are shown by the Law, so that when pardon is granted, it may appear what is remitted. For we would not remit debts, unless we learned, with the Law as indicator, what was owed to us. If, then, that formless offspring already existed indeed, but was as yet in some manner formlessly animate (since the great question concerning the soul is not to be hurried by the rashness of an unexamined opinion), the Law therefore did not wish it to pertain to homicide, because a living soul cannot yet be said to be in that body which lacks sensation, if such a soul is in flesh not yet formed, and therefore not yet endowed with senses.

But what he said, *and he shall pay upon demand,* which he established was to be paid by the woman's husband when the formless offspring was expelled, is not easy to understand: for *axioma,* which the Greek has, is understood in several ways, and it was said *upon demand* more tolerably than if something else were said. For perhaps he will demand that he pay, so that in that way he may make sufficient satisfaction to God, even if the husband or wife does not require it.

[^1]: Matt 6:12.
