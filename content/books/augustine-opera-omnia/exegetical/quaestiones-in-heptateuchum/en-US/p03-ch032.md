# What weight those words have, whose meaning the Apostle too has.

**32.** (9:16) *And for this very thing you have been preserved, that I might show in you my power, and that my name might be announced in all the earth*. These words of Scripture the Apostle too set down, when he was engaged in that same most difficult passage. And there he says this too: *but if God, willing to show his wrath, and to demonstrate his power, bore in much patience the vessels of wrath*: sparing, that is, those whom he foreknew would be evil; which vessels he calls perfected for destruction. *And that he might make known*, he says, *the riches of his glory upon the vessels of mercy*[^1]. Hence the voice of the vessels of mercy is in the *Psalms*: *my God, his mercy will go before me; my God has shown me concerning my enemies*[^2].

God, then, knows how to make good use of the evil, in whom nevertheless he does not create human nature for malice, but bears with them patiently as long as he knows it ought to be; not in vain, but using them for the admonition or exercise of the good. For behold, that the name of God might be announced in all the earth, it surely profits the vessels of mercy. For their advantage, then, Pharaoh was preserved, as both Scripture testifies and the outcome teaches.

[^1]: Rom 9:22-23.
[^2]: Ps 59:10-11 (58:11-12).
