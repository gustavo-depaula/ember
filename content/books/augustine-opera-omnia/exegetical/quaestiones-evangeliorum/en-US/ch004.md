# Book One: Questions on the Gospel according to Matthew

Question 1. When he said, *no one knows the Son except the Father,* he did not add, "and anyone to whom the Father chooses to reveal him," as he did when he said, *no one knows the Father except the Son,* and added, *and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him.*

This is not to be understood as though the Son could be known by no one except the Father alone, while the Father could be known not only by the Son but also by those to whom the Son has revealed him.

It was rather said in this way so that we might understand that both the Father and the Son himself are revealed through the Son, because he himself is the light of our mind; so that in what he added afterward, *and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him,* you may take not only the Father but also the Son; for the addition was made with reference to the whole of what he had said.

For the Father declares himself by his Word; and the Word declares not only that which is declared through the Word, but also itself.

**2.** That the Lord's disciples *began to pluck ears of grain and eat them,* which they could not do without rubbing them, is this: *put to death your members which are upon the earth*[^1]*,* that is, because no one passes into the body of Christ unless he has been stripped of his carnal garments. Hence comes *put off the old man*[^2]*,* hence too comes *the circumcision made without hands in the stripping off of the flesh*[^3]*.*

**3.** In the *smoking flax* it is to be noted that the light, once forsaken, produces a stench.

**4.** *Then there was brought to him a blind and mute man who had a demon*, that is, one who does not believe and is subject to the devil, who does not understand and does not confess the faith itself, of which it is said: *with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^4], or one who does not give praise to God.

**5.** In that he said, *and if I cast out demons by Beelzebul*, then even according to your own opinion, *therefore the kingdom of God has come upon you*, because the kingdom of the devil cannot stand, which you confess to be divided against itself.

He now calls it *the kingdom of God*, by which the impious are condemned and separated from the faithful who are now doing penance for their sins. He calls that one *the strong man*, because he himself held people fast, so that they could not by their own strength rescue themselves from him, but only through the grace of God.

By *his goods* he means all unbelievers. *Unless he first binds the strong man.* By *he will have bound* he means that he will have taken from him the power of hindering the will of the faithful from following Christ and obtaining the kingdom of God.

**6.** He calls them a *brood of vipers* because he also calls them sons of the devil. For a man is a son of the devil to the extent that he imitates him by sinning.

**7.** *For as Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights, so will the Son of man be in the heart of the earth three days and three nights.* You are to take the part of the sixth day on which he was buried, together with the preceding night, as one night and day, that is, as a whole day; the sabbath as a night and a day; and the Lord's night with the same day dawning. And so, by taking the part for the whole, you have three days and three nights. For just as a pregnant woman is said to carry for ten months, nine are complete; but the beginning of the tenth is taken for the whole.

And that the Lord showed himself on the mountain, one evangelist says this happened after six days[^5], but another says after eight days[^6], reckoning the latter part of the first day, on which the Lord promised this would come to pass, and the earlier part of the last day, on which what he promised was fulfilled, as whole and complete days; so that you may understand that the one who said "after six days" mentioned only the days in between, which were truly whole and completely finished. For in *Genesis* the day begins from light and ends in darkness[^7], to signify the fall of man; but now it goes from darkness to light, as it is said, *that light might shine out of darkness*[^8]*,* because a man freed from sins comes to the light of righteousness.

**8.** *When the unclean spirit has gone out of a man* and so forth signifies that certain people will so believe that they cannot bear the labors of continence and will return to the world. What is said, *he takes with him seven other spirits,* is understood to mean that when someone has fallen away from righteousness he will also practice pretense.

For the desire of the flesh, driven out from its accustomed works through penance, when it *finds no* delights *in which to rest,* returns more greedily and again takes possession of the man's mind, if, when it was driven out, negligence followed, so that the Word of God was not brought in, through sound doctrine, as a dweller in the cleansed house. And since he will have not only those seven vices that are contrary to the seven spiritual virtues, but will also pretend through hypocrisy to have the very virtues themselves, therefore, *having taken with him seven other spirits more wicked*, that is, by this sevenfold pretense, that concupiscence *returns, so that the last state of the man is worse than the first.*

**9.** As to what he says, *one a hundredfold, another sixtyfold, another thirtyfold:* the *hundredfold* belongs to martyrs, on account of holiness of life or contempt of death; the *sixtyfold* to virgins, on account of interior repose, since they do not fight against the habit of the flesh, for repose is customarily granted to those of sixty years after their military service or after public affairs; the *thirtyfold* to the married, since this is the age of those who do battle, for they have the sharper struggle, lest they be overcome by their desires.

**10.** All uncleanness in the crop is called weeds. That the weeds are first separated is said because, with tribulation going before, the impious will be separated from the pious, which is understood to be done through the good angels, since the good can carry out the offices of punishment with a good mind, as a king does, as a judge does; but the wicked cannot carry out the offices of mercy.

**11.** The grain of mustard seed, by reason of the fervor of faith or because it is said to drive out poisons, becomes greater than all the herbs, that is, than the teachings. Now teachings are the tenets of the sects, that is, what has pleased each of the sects.

**12.** *The leaven that the woman took and hid in three measures of flour*: by the *woman* he means wisdom; by the *leaven,* love, which makes it ferment and rouses it; by the *three measures of flour*, either those three things in man: *with the whole heart and with the whole soul and with the whole mind*[^9], or those three fruitful measures: *a hundredfold, sixtyfold, thirtyfold*[^10], or those three kinds of men: *Noah, Daniel, and Job*[^11].

**13.** By the treasure hidden in a field he meant the two Testaments of the Law in the Church, which, when someone has touched them with a partial understanding, he perceives that great things lie hidden there. And *he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field*, that is, by contempt for temporal things he procures for himself leisure, so that he may be rich in the knowledge of God.

**14.** That the Jews said: *where did this man get this wisdom and these mighty works? Wisdom* in the things he was speaking, *mighty works* in the things he was doing. And so the Apostle too, when he said *Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God*[^12], referred the *power* to signs, on account of the Jews, but the *wisdom* to teaching, on account of the Greeks, that is, the gentiles.

**15.** That the disciples said he was a ghost signifies what was said: *will he find faith on the earth?*[^13], because certain people who have yielded to the devil will doubt about the coming of Christ. But that Peter implores the Lord's help lest he sink signifies that even after the last persecution the Church is to be purified by certain tribulations; which Paul also signifies when he says: *he will be saved, but only as through fire*[^14]. That it follows that all who were in the boat, worshiping, said: *truly you are the Son of God*, signifies that his glory will then be made manifest, through a clear vision, to those who now walk by faith.

**16.** *Whatever gift comes from me will profit you*, that is, the gift you offer for my sake will now belong to you. By these words the children signify that their parents' offerings on their behalf are no longer needed, since they had reached an age at which they could now make offerings for themselves. The Pharisees, then, denied that those who had come to this age, so that they could say this to their parents, were guilty if, after they had said it, they did not render honor to their parents.

**17.** *Every plant that my heavenly Father has not planted will be rooted up*, that is, carnal desire, in keeping with which those who thought as they did were offended at the omission of miraculous signs or even at their own traditions, and did not care for the precepts of life, which purify the mind from desire.

**18.** That he saves both the centurion's servant and the daughter of the Canaanite woman without coming to their houses signifies that the gentiles, to whom he did not come, would be saved through his word. That their children are healed at their request, the person of the Church is to be understood, which is to itself both mother and children; for all together, of whom the Church consists, are called the mother, while the individuals are called the children.

**19.** That the crowds brought to the Lord the *mute,* who do not praise God or do not confess the faith; the *blind,* who do not understand even if they obey those who command; the *deaf,* who do not obey even if they understand; the *lame,* who do not fulfill the precepts.

**20.** That the Lord said: *when it is evening, you say: 'it will be fair weather, for the sky is red,'* that is, by the blood of Christ's passion at his first coming the forgiveness of sins is given; *and in the morning: 'today there will be a storm, for the sky is red and lowering,'* that is, that at his second coming he is to come with fire going before him. *You know, then, how to interpret the face of the sky, but you cannot interpret the signs of the times?* By *the signs of the times* he spoke of his coming or his passion, which the rosy sky in the evening resembles, and likewise of the tribulation that is to come before his coming, which the rosy and lowering sky in the morning resembles.

**21.** That the Lord said: *Elijah indeed is to come and will restore all things,* that is, either those whom the persecution of the Antichrist will have thrown into confusion, or that he himself will restore by dying what he owes.

**22.** That he said: *he often falls into the fire and sometimes into the water.* The *fire* refers to anger, because it seeks the heights; the *water* to the pleasures of the flesh. Likewise, in that the disciples say: *why could we not cast it out?*, lest they be lifted up into pride by working such miracles, they were rather admonished, through the humility of faith, as through a grain of mustard seed, to take care that earthly elation, which is signified by the name of the *mountain*, should pass away.

**23.** That he said: *then the sons are free,* in every kingdom the sons are to be understood as free, that is, as not liable to tribute. Much more, then, ought the sons of that kingdom under which all earthly kingdoms stand to be free in any earthly kingdom whatever.

**24.** That the Lord says: *but whoever causes one of these little ones to stumble*, that is, of the humble, such as he wishes his disciples to be, by not obeying or even by opposing him, as the Apostle says of Alexander the coppersmith[^15], *it would be better for him to have a millstone hung around his neck and to be drowned in the depth of the sea*, that is, it befits him that the desire of temporal things, to which the foolish and the blind are bound fast, should drag him down to ruin, fettered by its own weight.

**25.** That *a debtor of ten thousand talents was brought to the lord, and he ordered him to be sold, along with his wife and children and all that he had, and payment to be made*, it is to be understood that he was a debtor of the ten precepts of the Law; and that, on account of his desire and his works, as a wife and children, he had to pay the penalty, which is his price; for the price of one sold is understood as the punishment of one condemned.

In that he said: *he refused to forgive his fellow servant, but went out and cast him into prison* and so forth, it is to be understood: he held this disposition against him, namely that he wished him punished. *But the fellow servants who told the lord what was being done* can be understood as the Church, which both looses that man and binds the other.

**26.** That the Lord says the rich man does not enter the kingdom of God, and the disciples say: *who can be saved?*, since the rich are few in comparison with the multitude of the poor, it is to be understood that all who desire such things noticed that they were reckoned in the number of the rich.

**27.** That the Lord tells two of his disciples apart that he is to suffer, he did this to confirm the testimony for the future, since he said: *by the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall stand*[^16]*;* for so that what he said might neither be made common nor lack the firmness of human testimony, he could not say it to fewer than two; or else to show forth the sacrament of love, for there cannot be love among fewer than two. Now he was to suffer not out of the necessity of a debt on account of his own sin, but out of love, to discharge our sins.

**28.** The Lord goes out from Jericho, now departing from this earth by his resurrection. Great crowds follow him; peoples and gentiles believe in him. But the two blind men sitting beside the way signify those from each people who already cling, through faith, to the temporal dispensation, in keeping with which Christ is the way, and who desire to be enlightened, that is, to understand something of the eternity of the Word, which they desired to obtain as Jesus passed by, that is, through the merit of faith, by which one believes that the Son of God was both born a man and suffered for us. For through this dispensation Jesus, as it were, passes by, because such action is temporal.

But it was fitting that they should cry out so loudly until they overcame the noise of the crowd resisting them, that is, that they should so perseveringly stretch out their mind in praying and knocking, until by the strongest effort they overcame the habit of carnal desires, which, like a crowd, clamors against the thought striving to see the light of eternal truth, or overcame the very crowd of carnal men hindering spiritual pursuits.

And so Jesus, who says, *to him who asks it will be given, and he who seeks will find, and to him who knocks it will be opened*[^17], hearing those coming to him, namely those reaching by the very ardor of their desire what they desire, stands still, touches them, and enlightens them. For the eternity of the Word does not pass by as that temporal dispensation does, since it, *remaining in itself, makes all things new*[^18].

Therefore, because faith in the temporal incarnation prepares us to understand eternal things, they were admonished, as Jesus passed by, that they should be enlightened, and they were enlightened by him as he stood still. For temporal things pass by, but eternal things stand.

**29.** That the Lord says to the disciples: *you will say to this mountain: be taken up and cast into the sea*, he spoke of pride, which belongs to the worldly: the servant of God ought to say this to himself, that he may drive it away from himself, since it does not befit him; or else that through their faith, since the Gospel was preached through them, the Lord himself, who has been called a mountain[^19], was taken away from the Jews, so as to be cast forth into the gentiles as into the sea.

**30.** That the Lord says: *and whoever falls on this stone will be broken to pieces; but on whomever it falls, it will crush him*, by those *who fall on it* he means those who now despise him or treat him with insult, and therefore do not yet perish utterly, but are nonetheless broken, so that they do not walk rightly. But on those upon whom it falls, it will come down from above upon them in judgment with the punishment of perdition; therefore he said it will crush them, so that the *impious are like the dust which the wind drives from the face of the earth*[^20].

**31.** That the Lord said: *the kingdom of heaven is like a man, a king, who made a marriage feast for his son:* by the *marriage* he meant the Word made flesh, because in that man whom he took on the Church was joined to God. In that he said: *my oxen and fatlings are killed:* by the *oxen* he meant the leaders of the peoples, by the *fatlings* all who are fattened. In that the lord said: *go to the partings of the ways, and whomever you find call to the marriage feast:* by the *ways* are understood the teachings of the gentiles, because from all of them they came to the marriage feast, that is, they believed in Christ.

**32.** That the Sadducees say to the Lord: *there were among us seven brothers, and one, having taken a wife, died, and the second, and the rest*, are understood as impious men, who could not bear the fruit of righteousness on earth through all the seven ages of the world, in which this earth subsists; for afterward this earth itself will pass away, through which all those, like seven husbands, passed barrenly.

**33.** That the Lord said: *on these two commandments depend the whole Law and the prophets:* by *depend* he meant, that is, it is referred to that point where it has its end.

**34.** That the Lord said: *for which is greater, the gold or the temple that sanctifies the gold?*, and likewise said: *for which is greater, the gift or the altar that sanctifies the gift?*, by the temple and the altar Christ himself is to be understood, and by the gold and the gift the praises and the sacrifices of prayers which we offer in him through him. For he is not sanctified by these, but these are sanctified through him.

**35.** That the Lord said: *straining out a gnat,* refers to what he said: *they tithe all the little things*. *Swallowing a camel* refers to what he said: *they have neglected the weightier matters of the Law, mercy and judgment and faith*, so that this is the meaning: you observe the least things, you despise the greatest.

For from this perversity that also happened to them by which these things can be referred allegorically, namely that they released Barabbas[^21], because of course he did not break the sabbath, which they observed carnally with great diligence, but killed the Lord, who set forth the sabbath spiritually *through judgment and mercy and faith*, which they most despised. For by the name *gnat* a seditious murderer is not absurdly figured, because this creature both disturbs by its buzzing and delights in blood; and by the name *camel*, on account of the greatness that humbles itself to undergo burdens, the Lord is fittingly understood.

**36.** That the Lord said to Jerusalem: *how often have I wished to gather your children, as a hen gathers her young under her wings, and you would not.* This kind of creature has great affection for its young, so that, affected by their weakness, it too grows weak; and, what you will find more rarely in other creatures, protecting its young with its wings, it fights against the hawk. So too our mother, the Wisdom of God, in a certain way made weak through the assumption of flesh, whence the Apostle says: *what is weak in God is stronger than men*[^22], protects our weakness and resists the devil, lest he carry us off. And in this defense what the hen attempts against the hawk by affection, Wisdom accomplishes against the devil by power.

**37.** That the Lord said: *pray that your flight may not be in winter or on a sabbath*, that is, that you be not held back by any hindrance, since in winter one was hindered from a journey by rains or cold, and on the sabbath, on which it was not permitted to set out; or else, in another sense, that no one be found, on that day, amid the sorrow or the joy of temporal things.

**38.** That the Lord said: *for as lightning comes from the east and shines as far as the west, so will the coming of the Son of man be:* by the name of the *east* and the *west* he wished to signify the whole world, throughout which the Church was to exist, the Gospel beginning from Jerusalem[^23], according to that sense in which he said: *from now on you will see the Son of man coming on the clouds*[^24]*.* For he fittingly named the Church now *lightning*, which especially is wont to flash out from the clouds. Therefore, the authority of the Church having been established throughout the world, bright and manifest, he consequently admonishes the disciples and all the faithful and those who shall wish to believe in him, not to believe schismatics and heretics. For each schism and each heresy either has its place in the world, holding some particular part, or deceives the curiosity of men by hidden and obscure gatherings. To this belongs what he says: *if anyone says to you: 'behold, here is the Christ, or there,'* which signifies the regions of lands and provinces, *or in the inner rooms or in the desert*, which signifies the hidden and obscure gatherings of heretics. Therefore, in that he said his coming would reach from the east to the west, it tells against those who are named after particular parts of the lands and say that the Christ is among them. And in that he says *as lightning,* it tells against those who gather secretly, as it were *in inner rooms*, and few, as it were *in the desert*. For the name *lightning* belongs to the manifestation and brightness of the Church, signifying also the night or the clouds of this age; for then the brightness of the lightning appears.

**39.** That the Lord said: *but from the fig tree learn its lesson:* by the *fig tree* understand the human race, on account of the itch of the flesh. *When its branch has now become tender,* that is, when the sons of men have, through faith in Christ, advanced to spiritual fruits, and in them the honor of adoption as sons of God has shone forth.

**40.** That the Lord said of Judas: *it would have been better for him not to have been born*, was this perhaps into this life, so that he spoke in the usual way? For nothing can be good for one who is not. And if anyone contends that there is some life before this one, it is proved that it was expedient not only for Judas, but for no one, to be born. Or does he mean it would have been good for the devil not to be born to sin? Or was it also good for that one not to be born to Christ through his calling, so that he might not be an apostate?

**41.** That the Lord was sold for thirty pieces of silver signified, through Judas, the wicked Jews, who, following carnal and temporal things, which belong to the five senses of the body, refused to have Christ. And because they did this in the sixth age of the world, it was signified that they received six times five as the price of the Lord sold. At which time the prophet rebukes such men, saying: *O sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?*[^25], so that, even if in the five ages there had been some excuse for following vanity, at least in the sixth they might grasp the truth, which was preached and shown through the Lord Jesus, just as on the sixth day man was made in the image of God[^26]. And because they refused, they have the image of the prince of this age impressed six times five, and they do not have Christ, through whom there has been sealed upon us *the light of your countenance, O Lord*[^27]. And because the word of the Lord is silver[^28], but they understood even the Law itself carnally, they held, as it were in silver, the image of worldly rule impressed, having lost the Lord.

**42.** That the Lord says: *where the body is, there the eagles will be gathered*, that is, into heaven, whither he raised the body with himself in the man he assumed, which was also called a corpse for this reason, that he was speaking these things as one about to die. *There the eagles will be gathered* is said of the spiritual, who, by imitating his passion and humility, are filled as it were from his body. For he took on a body for our sake, on account of humility, and likewise his passion.

**43.** That the Lord says to the disciples at the time of his passion: *I will not drink from this fruit of the vine from now on until that day when I drink it new with you in the kingdom of my Father*, he wishes us to understand this to be old, when he calls that one new. Therefore, since he had taken a body from the stock of Adam, who is called the old man, which he was to hand over to death in his passion, whence also through the sacrament of wine he commends his blood, what else ought we to understand by new wine than the immortality of renewed bodies? In that he says *I will drink with you*, he also promises to them the resurrection of their bodies for the putting on of immortality; for *with you* is to be taken as said not of the same time, but of the same renewal. For the Apostle says that we too have risen with Christ[^29], so that the hope of the thing to come may bring present gladness even now. And in that he says that this *fruit of the vine* too will be new, it signifies that these very bodies will rise according to the heavenly renewal, which now, according to their earthly oldness, are to die. But if you understand the *vine*, from whose oldness he drinks this cup of the passion, as the Jews themselves, it is signified that this very people will come to the body of Christ through newness of life, when, the fullness of the gentiles having entered in, all Israel will be saved[^30].

**44.** In that it was said: *they spat in his face*, it signified those who spurn the presence of his grace. Likewise, as it were, those *strike him with their fists* who prefer their own honors to him; those *give blows on his face* who, blinded by faithlessness, affirm that he has not come, as though banishing and repelling his presence.

**45.** That Peter, not yet firmly established in faith, denied the Lord three times, his very threefold denial seems to have designated the depraved error of heretics. For the error of heretics concerning Christ is bounded by three kinds: for they are deceived either about his divinity, or about his humanity, or about both.

**46.** That Peter follows at a distance the Lord going to his passion signifies that the Church will indeed follow, that is, imitate, the passion of the Lord, but in a far different way; for the Church suffers for itself, but he suffered for the Church.

**47.** Just as the temptation of desire is threefold, so too the temptation of fear is threefold. To the desire that lies in curiosity is opposed the fear of death; for as in the former there is a greed for knowing things, so in the latter there is a dread of losing such knowledge. To the desire of honors or praise is opposed the fear of disgrace and insults. To the desire of pleasure is opposed the fear of pain. It is therefore not absurd to understand that, on account of the threefold temptation of the passion, the Lord prayed three times that the cup might pass, but in such a way that the will of the Father might rather be fulfilled.
