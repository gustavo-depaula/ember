# Revisions II

## Chapter 12 (39): Questions on the Gospels, in Two Books

There are certain expositions of certain passages from the Gospel according to Matthew, and others likewise according to Luke; the former have been gathered into one book, the latter into another. The title of this work is *Questions on the Gospels.* But why only those passages from the said Gospel books were expounded that are contained in these books of mine, and what they are, my prologue sufficiently indicates, since the same questions are appended and numbered, so that anyone may find what he wishes to read by following the numbers.

In the first book, then, in the passage where it is set down *that the Lord, apart from the others, told two of his disciples of his passion*[^1], the faultiness of the manuscript misled us; for the text reads *twelve,* not "two[^2]."

In the second book[^3], in wishing to explain how Joseph, whose wife is said to be the Virgin Mary, could have had two fathers, with respect to *what is reported, that a man took the wife of his deceased brother in order to raise up offspring for him according to the Law*[^4], I said that *this explanation is weak, since the Law commanded that the child who was born should take the name of the deceased.* This is not true; for the Law decreed that the name of the deceased mentioned should have this effect, that the child be called his son, not that the child should bear that name.

This work begins thus: *this work was not written in such a way.*

[^1]: Quaest. Evang. 1, 27.
[^2]: Mt 20:17; Lk 18:31.
[^3]: Quaest. Evang. 2, 5.
[^4]: Deut 25:5-6; Mt 22:24; Mk 12:19; Lk 20:28.
