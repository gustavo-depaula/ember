# Revisions II

## Chapter 4 (31): Teaching Christianity, four books[^1]

**1.** Having found that the books *Teaching Christianity* were unfinished, I preferred to complete them rather than to leave them as they were and pass on to revising other works. I therefore finished the third book, which had been written only up to the point where mention is made of the testimony from the Gospel *about the woman who hid leaven in three measures of flour, until the whole was leavened*.[^2] I also added the final book, and so completed that work in four books. The first three of them help toward understanding the Scriptures, while the fourth deals with how the things we understand are to be presented.

**2.** In the second book, with regard to the author of the book that many call the *Wisdom of Solomon*—namely, that Jesus Sirach, who also wrote *Ecclesiasticus*, wrote it as well[^3]—I later learned that this is not as well established as I had stated, and I found it altogether more probable that he was not the author of this book.

And where I said that *with these forty-four books the authority of the Old Testament is brought to a close*,[^4] I called it the Old Testament according to the usage by which the Church now speaks. But the Apostle does not seem to call anything the Old Testament except what was given on Mount Sinai.[^5]

And in what I said, *that with regard to the history of the times holy Ambrose had resolved the question*,[^6] as though Plato and Jeremiah had been contemporaries, my memory deceived me. For what that bishop said on this matter is read in the book he wrote *On the Sacraments, or On Philosophy*.[^7]
