# Revisions II: Chapter 13 (40): Notes on Job, one book

As for the book whose title is *Notes on Job,* I could not easily say whether it ought to be reckoned mine, or rather the work of those who, as best they could or would, gathered into one volume the notes written in the margins of a manuscript.

For they are pleasing to very few who understand them, while many who do not understand are bound to be put off, since in many places even the very words that are being expounded have not been written out, so that it is not clear what is being expounded.

Then too the brevity of the remarks has been attended by such obscurity that the reader can scarcely bear it, since he is forced to pass over a great many things he does not understand.

Finally, I found the work itself so full of errors in our manuscripts that I could not correct it, nor would I wish it to be said to have been published by me, except that I know the brethren have it, whose zeal could not be denied.

This book begins thus: *and he had great works upon the earth.*
