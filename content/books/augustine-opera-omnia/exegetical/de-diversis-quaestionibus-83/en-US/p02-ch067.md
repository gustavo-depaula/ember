# On what is written: For I consider that — the sufferings of this present time are not worthy of the future — glory that will be revealed in us, up to that — which was said: For we are saved by hope

**67. 1.** This chapter is obscure, because it is not sufficiently apparent here what he is here calling "creation." Now, according to Catholic teaching, whatever God the Father made and founded through his only-begotten Son in the unity of the Holy Spirit is called creation. Therefore not only bodies, but also our souls and spirits are contained under the name of creation. But it was said: *creation itself will be freed from the slavery of corruption into the freedom of the glory of the sons of God*[^1]*,* as though we are not creation, but the sons of God, into the freedom of whose glory creation will be freed from slavery. Likewise he says: *for we know that all creation groans together and is in pain even until now; and not only it, but we ourselves also*[^2]*,* as though we are one thing and creation another. The whole chapter, then, must be considered piece by piece.

**67. 2.** *For I consider,* he says, *that the sufferings of this present time are unworthy of the future glory that will be revealed in us*[^3]*.* This is manifest. For he had said above: *but if by the spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live*[^4]*.* This cannot be done without trouble, for which patience is necessary.

To this pertains also what he said a little before: *if indeed we suffer together, so that we may also be glorified together*[^5]*.* And so, when he says, *for the expectation of creation awaits the revelation of the sons of God*[^6]*,* this, I think, is what he is saying. For this very thing in us that is in pain, when we put to death the deeds of the flesh—that is, when we hunger or thirst through abstinence, while we bridle the delight of intercourse through chastity, while we endure the lacerations of injuries and the stings of insults through patience, while, our pleasures neglected and rejected, we labor for the fruit of mother Church—whatever in us is in pain in this and similar wearing-down, is creation.

For the body and the soul are in pain, which is certainly creation, and it awaits the revelation of the sons of God, that is, it awaits when what has been called may appear, in that glory to which it has been called. For since the only-begotten Son of God cannot be called creation—seeing that through him were made all things whatsoever that God made—we too are distinctly called creation before that manifestation of glory, and are distinctly called sons of God, although we deserve this by adoption; for that Only-begotten is Son by nature.

*Therefore the expectation of creation,* that is, our expectation, *awaits the revelation of the sons of God*[^7]*,* that is, it awaits when what was promised may appear, when what we now are in hope may be manifest in the reality itself. For *we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be. We know that when he appears, we shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is*[^8]*.*

This is the revelation of the sons of God, which the expectation of creation now awaits—not that creation awaits the revelation of another nature, one that is not creation, but creation itself, such as it now is, awaits when it may be such as it is to be—as though it were said: while a painter is at work with the colors laid out before him and prepared for his work, the expectation of the colors awaits the manifestation of the image, not because there will then be other colors or that the colors will not be, but only that they will have another dignity.

**67. 3.** *For creation,* he says, *was subjected to vanity*[^9]*.* This is that: *vanity of vanities, and all is vanity. What abundance has a man in all his labor, which he himself labors under the sun*[^10]*?* To whom was it said: *in labor you shall eat bread*[^11]*? Creation,* therefore, *was subjected to vanity, not willingly*[^12]*.* It is well added, *not willingly.*

For man sinned willingly, but was not condemned willingly. And so it was something voluntary to act against the precept of truth, but the penalty of sin is to be made subject to deception. Therefore creation was not subjected to vanity willingly, *but on account of him who subjected it in hope*[^13]*,* that is, on account of his justice and clemency, who neither left sin unpunished nor wished the sinner to be incurable.

**67. 4.** *Because creation itself*[^14]*,* that is, man himself, who, when the seal of the image had now been lost on account of sin, remained only creation: *and creation itself,* therefore—that is, that which is not yet called the perfected form of the sons, but only called creation—*will be freed from the slavery of corruption*[^15]*.* And so, that he says, *it itself too will be freed,* makes it understood that *it itself too* is like us,

that is, that even of those who are not yet called *sons of God*, because they have not yet believed, but are only *creation*, one must not despair, because they too will believe and will be freed from the slavery of corruption, just as we who are now sons of God, *although it has not yet appeared what we shall be*.

They will therefore be freed from the slavery of corruption *into the freedom of the glory of the sons of God*[^16]*,* that is, they too will be free instead of slaves, and glorious instead of dead, in the perfect life that the sons of God will have.

**67. 5.** *For we know that all creation groans together and is in pain even until now*[^17]*.* All creation is numbered in man—not because all the angels and the supereminent virtues and powers are in him, or heaven and earth and sea and all things that are in them, but because all creation is partly spiritual, partly animal, partly corporeal.

And, to consider this from the lower things, corporeal creation is extended through places, the animal gives life to the corporeal, the spiritual rules the animal—and it rules well when it subjects itself to be ruled by God; but when it transgresses his precepts, it is entangled in labors and hardships through the very things it was able to rule.

He, therefore, who lives from the body is called a carnal or an animal man—carnal because he pursues carnal things, animal because he is carried along by the dissolute wantonness of his soul, which the spirit does not rule nor restrain within the bounds of the natural order, because the spirit itself does not subject itself to be ruled by God.

But he who by the spirit rules the soul, and through the soul the body—which he cannot do unless he too has God as his ruler, since just as the head of the woman is the man, so the head of the man is Christ[^18]—is called spiritual. This life is now lived with some trouble, but afterward will suffer none.

And since the highest angels live spiritually, the lowest animally, the beasts and all cattle carnally, while the body does not live but is given life, all creation is in man, because he both understands by the spirit and senses by the soul and is moved locally by the body. *All creation,* therefore, in man *groans together and is in pain.* For he did not say "the whole," but *all,* as if someone were to say:

"all men who are sound see the sun, but they do not see with the whole of themselves, because they see only with their eyes; so in man there is all creation, because he both understands and lives and has a body, but not the whole of creation is in him, because besides him there are also angels, who understand and live and exist, and cattle, which live and exist, and bodies, which only exist—since to live itself is more than not to live, and to understand itself more than to live without understanding."

When, therefore, wretched man groans and is in pain, *all creation groans together and is in pain even until now*. And he rightly said *even until now,* because even if there are now some in the bosom of Abraham[^19], and that thief, established with the Lord in paradise[^20], ceased to be in pain on the very day he believed, nevertheless even until now *all creation groans together and is in pain*, because in those who are not yet freed all creation is present, on account of the spirit and the soul and the body.

**67. 6.** *And not only it,* he says, *does all creation groan together and is in pain, but we ourselves also,* that is, not only in man do the body and soul and spirit together grieve from the difficulties of the body, but we ourselves also, the bodies excepted, *groan within ourselves, having the firstfruits of the spirit.*

And he rightly said *having the firstfruits of the spirit,* that is, those whose spirit has now been offered to God as a sacrifice and seized by the divine fire of love. These are the firstfruits of man, because truth first takes possession of our spirit, so that through it the rest may be taken hold of. He, then, now has firstfruits offered to God who says, *with the mind I serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin*[^21]*;* and who says, *God whom I serve in my spirit;*[^22] and of whom it is said, *the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak*[^23]*.*

But since he still says, *wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?*[^24] and it is still said to such people, *he will also give life to your mortal bodies on account of his Spirit remaining in you*[^25]; it is not yet a holocaust, but it will be when death is swallowed up in victory, when it will be said to it, *where, death, is your striving? Where, death, is your sting?*[^26]

Now, therefore, he says, not only *all creation*, that is, together with the body, *but we ourselves also, having the firstfruits of the spirit,* that is, we souls, who have now offered our minds as firstfruits to God, *groan within ourselves,* that is, apart from the body, *awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^27], that is, so that the body too, receiving the benefit of the adoption of sons to which we have been called, may show us, wholly freed, with all troubles passed, to be on every side the sons of God.

*For we are saved by hope; but hope that is seen is not hope*[^28]*.* Then, therefore, that will be a reality which is now hope, *when it has appeared what we shall be*, that is, *we shall be like him because we shall see him as he is*[^29].

**67. 7.** If this chapter is opened up in the manner in which it has been treated, we do not fall into those troubles by which very many people are compelled to say that all the angels and the sublime Virtues are in pain and groaning before we are fully freed, since it was said: *all creation groans together and is in pain*[^30]*.*

For although they help us in accordance with their sublimity, while they obey God—who deigned to send even his only Son for us—nevertheless they are to be believed to do this without groaning and pains, lest they be thought wretched, and lest that Lazarus from our own number, who now rests in the bosom of Abraham, be happier than they—especially since he said that this same creation which groans together and is in pain was subjected to vanity, which it is impious to believe of the highest and most excellent creatures among the virtues and powers. Then he said that it must be freed from the slavery of corruption, into which we cannot believe that those have fallen who live a most blessed life in the heavens.

Nevertheless nothing is to be affirmed rashly, but the Divine Words must be treated with pious diligence again and again, lest perhaps what groans together and is in pain, and was subjected to vanity, can be understood in some other way, so that it cannot impiously be supposed of the highest angels, as long as they help our weakness by the command of our Lord. But whether the exposition we have followed or some other exposition of this chapter is brought forward, this only must be guarded against: that it does not violate or wound the Catholic faith. For I know that empty-headed heretics have boasted many impious and inept things about this chapter.
