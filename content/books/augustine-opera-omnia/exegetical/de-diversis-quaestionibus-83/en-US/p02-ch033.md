# On Fear

**33.** It is doubtful to no one that there is no other cause for fearing than lest we lose what we love once attained, or not attain what we hope for. Wherefore, whoever has come to love and to have this very thing—not to fear—what fear will there be lest he be able to lose it?

For many things that we love and have, we fear to lose, and so we guard them by fear. But no one can guard by fearing the state of not fearing. Likewise, whoever loves not to fear, and does not yet have it but hopes that he will have it, ought not to fear that he will not attain it. For by this fear nothing else is feared than that same fear.

Furthermore, all fear flees something; and no thing flees itself. Fear, therefore, is not feared. But if anyone thinks it is not rightly said that fear fears something, since it is the soul rather that fears by fear itself, let him attend to what is easy to know: that there is no fear except of a future and impending evil.

But it is necessary that whoever fears something should flee it. And so whoever fears to fear is assuredly most absurd, because by fleeing he has the very thing he flees. For since nothing is feared except lest some evil happen, to fear lest fear happen is nothing other than to embrace what you reject.

But if this is contradictory, as it is, then whoever loves nothing other than not to fear fears in no way at all. And for that reason no one can love this alone and not have it. But whether this alone is to be loved is another question. Now one whom fear does not unnerve, neither does desire lay waste, nor sickness wear away, nor exultant and empty gladness toss about.

For if he desires—since desire is nothing other than love of passing things—he must needs fear lest he either lose them once attained or not attain them. But he does not fear; therefore he does not desire.

Likewise, if he is racked by grief of mind, he must needs also be agitated by fear, since where there is anxiety over present evils, there is fear of impending ones. But he is free from fear, therefore also from anxiety. Likewise, if he rejoices emptily, he rejoices over those things which he can lose, and so he must needs fear lest he lose them. But he fears in no way; in no way, therefore, does he rejoice emptily.
