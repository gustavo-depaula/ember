# On what the Apostle says: Death is swallowed up — in victory. Where, death, is your striving? — Where, death, is your sting? But the sting — of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law

**70.** It is commonly asked what this means: *then will come about what is written: 'Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, death, is your striving? Where, death, is your sting? But the sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the Law.'* I judge that death here signifies carnal habit, which resists the good will by the delight of enjoying temporal things.

For it would not be said, *where, death, is your striving?* if it had not resisted and fought back. Its striving is described also in that place: *the flesh desires against the spirit, the spirit against the flesh. For these are opposed to each other, so that you do not do what you wish*[^1]. It comes about, then, through perfect sanctification, that every carnal appetite is subjected to our spirit, illumined and given life—that is, to the good will.

And just as now we see that we are free of many childish delights which, if they had been denied us as children, tormented us most bitterly, so it must be believed will be the case with every carnal delight when perfect holiness has restored the whole person. But now, so long as there is in us something that resists the good will, we need the help of God through good men and good angels, so that, until our wound is healed, it may not so trouble us as to destroy even the good will.

But this death we deserved by the sin which was formerly altogether in free choice, when in paradise no pain of denied delight resisted a person's good will as it does now. For example, if there exists someone whom hunting has never delighted, he is altogether free as to whether he wishes to hunt or not, nor does anyone who forbids it torment him. But if, having used this freedom badly, he has hunted against the command of the one forbidding it, the delight, creeping in little by little, puts the soul to death, so that, if he should wish to abstain, he cannot do so without trouble and anguish, whereas before, in full health, he did not engage in it at all.

Therefore *the sting of death is sin*, because by sin there came to be a delight which can now resist the good will and be checked only with pain. And this delight, because it lies in the failing of a soul made worse, we rightly call death. *But the power of sin is the law*, because what the law forbids is committed much more wickedly and shamefully than if it were forbidden by no law.

And so death will be swallowed up in victory when, through sanctification perfected in every part of a person, carnal delight will be overwhelmed by the delight of spiritual things.

[^1]: Gal 5:17.
