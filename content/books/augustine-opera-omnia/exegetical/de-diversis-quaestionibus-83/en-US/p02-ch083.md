# On Marriage, in what the Lord says: — If anyone dismisses his wife, — except for the cause of fornication, and the rest 420

**83.** If the Lord admits only the cause of fornication for dismissing a spouse, and does not forbid a pagan marriage to be dismissed, it follows that paganism is reckoned as fornication. Now it is manifest that the Lord makes only the cause of fornication an exception, when he speaks in the Gospel about dismissing a spouse.

But a pagan marriage is not forbidden to be dismissed for this reason: that when the Apostle gave counsel on this matter—that a believer should not dismiss an unbelieving spouse who is willing to be with him—he says: *I say, not the Lord,*

so that the Lord is understood not indeed to command that it be dismissed—lest the Apostle seem to give counsel against his command—but nevertheless to permit it, so that no one is held in that matter by the necessity of a command, but acts freely by the will of counsel.

Nevertheless, if anyone asserts that the Lord admits, as a cause for leaving a spouse, only that fornication which is commonly called fornication—that is, which is perpetrated by unlawful intercourse—he can say this: that the Lord, when he spoke of this matter, spoke of both spouses being believers, both husband and wife, so that if both are believers, neither is permitted to leave the other except for the cause of fornication, where paganism cannot be understood, because each is a believer.

For thus the Apostle too seems to distinguish, when he says: *but to those who are in marriage I command, not I but the Lord, that a wife not depart from her husband; but if she departs, she should remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband*[^1].

Where it is understood that, even if for that one cause by which alone the leaving of marriage is permitted a woman has departed from her husband, she ought to remain unmarried, or, if she does not contain herself, to be reconciled rather to her husband—either corrected or at least tolerated—than to marry another.

And he continues and says: *and let the husband not dismiss his wife*[^2], briefly intimating in the husband the same form which he was prescribing in the woman.

These things being conveyed from the command of the Lord, there follows: *but to the rest I say, not the Lord: if any brother has an unbelieving wife, and she consents to dwell with him, let him not dismiss her; and if a woman has an unbelieving husband, and he consents to dwell with her, let her not dismiss her husband*[^3].'

Where he gives to understand that the Lord spoke of these so that neither should dismiss the other, if both were believers.

[^1]: 1 Cor 7:10-11.
[^2]: 1 Cor 7:11.
[^3]: 1 Cor 7:12-13.
