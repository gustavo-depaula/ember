# On the Ten Virgins

**59. 1.** Among the parables spoken by the Lord, that which is set down concerning the ten virgins is wont to exercise inquirers much. And many, indeed, have had many thoughts on it which are not beyond the faith; but how the exposition may fit all its parts, that is what must be worked out.

I have also read in a certain Scripture, of the kind that are called apocryphal, something that is not against the Catholic faith, but it seemed to me less fitting to this place, when I considered all the parts of this similitude. Yet about that exposition I dare rashly to judge nothing, lest perhaps it was not its unsuitability that made difficulties for me, but my own slowness that did not find its suitability. But what seems to me to be taken not absurdly in this place, I will expound as briefly and diligently as I can.

**59. 2.** Our Lord, then, having been asked privately by the disciples about the consummation of the world, among many other things which he said, said this also: *then the kingdom of heaven will be likened to ten virgins, who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. But five of them were foolish and five wise. But the five foolish, having taken their lamps, did not take oil with them; the wise, however, took oil with them in their vessels along with the lamps. And while the bridegroom delayed, they all slept.*

*But in the middle of the night a cry arose: behold, the bridegroom comes, go out to meet him. Then those virgins arose and trimmed their lamps. And the foolish said to the wise, 'give us of your oil, for our lamps are going out.' But the wise answered, saying, 'lest perhaps there be not enough for us and for you; go rather to those who sell and buy for yourselves.' And while they went to buy, the bridegroom came, and those who were ready went in with him to the wedding, and the door was shut.'*

*But last of all the remaining virgins come, saying, 'Lord, Lord, open to us.' But he, answering, said, 'amen I say to you, I do not know you. Watch, therefore, for you do not know the day nor the hour*[^1].'

That of ten virgins five are admitted and five excluded signifies the discernment of the good and the bad. Wherefore, if the name of virginity is honorable, why is it common to those received and those excluded? Then, what does the number five in each part mean? And what does the oil signify? It seems strange that the wise do not share with those who ask, since it is not right either to begrudge those who are so perfect that they are received by the bridegroom, by which name, with no one doubting, the Lord Jesus Christ is signified, and one ought to be merciful in giving from what one has, that sentence of the same Lord prescribing it, who says: *give to everyone who asks you*[^2].

But what is it that, by giving, could be not enough for both? These things especially increase the difficulty of the question; although, the rest too being diligently considered, great caution must be applied, so that all things may run together into one account, and nothing be said for one side that hinders another.

**59. 3.** It seems to me, then, that the five virgins signify the fivefold continence from the enticements of the flesh. For the appetite of the mind must be restrained from the pleasure of the eyes, from the pleasure of the ears, from the pleasure of smelling, of tasting, of touching.

But because this continence takes place partly before God, that he may be pleased in the inner joy of conscience, and partly before men, only that human glory may be seized, five are called wise and five foolish, yet both virgins, because both are continent, although they rejoice in a different kindling.

But the lamps, because they are carried in the hands, are the works that are done according to this continence. And it was said: *let your works shine before men*[^3]. But all *took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom*[^4]. It must be understood, then, that those of whom there is question are reckoned by the name of Christ; for those who are not Christians cannot go out to meet the bridegroom Christ.

*But the five foolish, having taken their lamps, did not take oil with them*[^5]. For many, although they hope for very much from the goodness of Christ, nevertheless have no joy, while they live continently, except in the praises of men. They do not, then, have oil with them. For I think that joy itself is signified by oil. *Therefore God, your God, has anointed you,* it says, *with the oil of gladness*[^6]. But one who does not rejoice for this reason, that he pleases God within, does not have oil with him.

*But the wise took oil with them in their vessels along with their lamps*[^7], that is, they placed the joy of good works in the heart and conscience. So the Apostle too admonishes: *but let a man test himself,* he says, *and then he will have glory in himself and not in another*[^8].

*But while the bridegroom delayed, they all slept*[^9], because, from both kinds of continent persons—whether of those who exult before God or of those who rest content in the praises of men—they die during this interval of time, until at the coming of the Lord the resurrection of the dead takes place.

*But in the middle of the night*, that is, with no one knowing or expecting—since the Lord himself says, *but of that day and hour no one knows*[^10], and the Apostle, *the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night*[^11], by which it is signified that it is utterly hidden—when he comes: *a cry arose: behold, the bridegroom comes, go out to meet him*[^12]. *In the twinkling of an eye and at the last trumpet* we shall all rise again[^13].

Therefore *all those virgins arose and trimmed their lamps*[^14], that is, the accounts of their works that must be rendered. For we must be presented before the tribunal of Christ, so that there each one may receive what he has done in the body, whether good or evil[^15].

*And the foolish said to the wise, give us of your oil, for our lamps are going out*[^16]. For those whose deeds are propped up by the praise of others fail when that same praise is withdrawn; and by habit they always seek out that from which the mind is wont to rejoice. And so they wish to have, with God, who is the inspector of the heart, the testimony of men, who do not see hearts.

But what did the wise answer? *Lest perhaps there be not enough for us and for you*[^17]. For each one will render an account for himself, nor is anyone helped by another's testimony with God, to whom the secrets of the heart are apparent; and each one scarcely suffices for himself, that his own conscience may bear him witness.

For who will boast that he has a clean heart[^18]? Hence it is that the Apostle says: *to me it is a very small thing that I be judged by you or by a human day; but neither do I judge myself*[^19]. Wherefore, since each one can either not at all or scarcely bring a true judgment concerning himself, how can he judge concerning another, since *no one knows what takes place in a man except the spirit of the man*[^20]?

*Go rather to those who sell and buy for yourselves.* They are not to be thought to have given counsel, but to have recalled, obliquely, the crime of those women. For flatterers sell oil, who, by praising things either false or unknown, send souls into error, and, by conciliating to them empty joys as to fools, receive from these some wage, whether of food or of money or of honor or of some temporal advantage—the souls not understanding what was said: *those who call you happy send you into error*[^21]. But it is better to be reproached by a just man than to be praised by a sinner. *The just man,* it says, *will correct me in mercy and reprove me; but let not the oil of the sinner anoint my head*[^22].

*Go,* then, *to those who sell and buy for yourselves,* that is, let us now see how those help you who were accustomed to sell you praises and to lead you into error, so that you sought glory not before God but from men.

*But while they went to buy, the bridegroom came,* that is, while they were bending down toward the things that are outside and seeking their wonted joys, because they did not know the inner joys, he came who judges, *and those who were ready,* that is, those to whom conscience bore good testimony before God, *went in with him to the wedding*[^23], that is, where the clean soul is coupled, to be made fruitful, with the pure and perfect and everlasting Word of God.

*And the door was shut,* that is, those having been received who are changed into the angelic life. *For we shall all rise again,* it says, *but we shall not all be changed*[^24]: the entrance to the kingdom of heaven is shut. For after the judgment there lies open no place for prayers or merits.

*But last of all the remaining virgins come, saying, Lord, Lord, open to us*[^25]. It is not said that they bought oil, and therefore they are to be understood, with no joy from others' praises now remaining, to return, in straits and great afflictions, to the imploring of God; but great is the severity, after the judgment, of him whose ineffable mercy was bestowed beforehand before the judgment.

And so, answering, he says: *amen I say to you that I do not know you*[^26], namely by that rule whereby the art of God, that is, the Wisdom of God, does not have it that those should enter into his joy who were seen to do something according to his precepts not before God, but in order to please men;

and thus he concludes: *watch, therefore, for you do not know the day nor the hour*[^27], not only of that last time at which the bridegroom is to come, but each one does not know the day and hour of his own falling asleep. But whoever is ready up to sleep, that is, up to the death which is owed by all, will be found ready also when that voice has sounded in the middle of the night, by which all are to be awakened.

**59. 4.** But that he said the virgins come to meet the bridegroom, I think is to be understood in such a way that she who is called the bride consists of the virgins themselves—as if, when all Christians run together into the Church, the children were said to run together to the mother, when she who is called the mother consists of the very children gathered together.

For now the Church is betrothed, and is a virgin to be led to the wedding, that is, when she keeps herself from worldly corruption; but at that time she will marry, when, all mortality passing away, she will be made fruitful by an immortal joining.

*I betrothed you,* he says, *to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ*[^28]. *You,* he says, *a virgin*, concluding from the plural to the singular. Therefore they can be called both virgins and a virgin.

But why they are said to be five has, as it seems to me, been expounded. But *we see now through a riddle, but then face to face*[^29]; and now in part, but then in whole. But that we now discern something through a riddle and in part in the Scriptures, which is nevertheless according to the Catholic faith, comes from that pledge which the virgin Church received at the humble coming of her bridegroom, whom she is to marry at that last coming, when he will come in glory, when now she will behold him face to face. For *he gave us the Holy Spirit as a pledge,* as the Apostle says[^30]. And therefore this exposition beholds nothing certain, except that it be according to the faith, nor does it prejudge other expositions which could no less be according to the faith.
