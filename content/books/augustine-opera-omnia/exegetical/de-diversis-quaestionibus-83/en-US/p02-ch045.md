# Against the Astrologers

**45. 1.** The ancients did not call "mathematicians" those who are now so called, but those who tracked out the numbers of the times by the motion of the heaven and the stars, of whom it is most rightly said in the Holy Scriptures: *again, neither are these to be pardoned. For if they could know so much as to be able to estimate the world, how did they not more easily find the Lord of it?*[^1]

For the human mind, in judging concerning visible things, can recognize that it is itself better than all visible things. Yet when it also confesses that it is changeable, on account of its falling away and advancing in wisdom, it finds that above it is unchangeable truth; and so, clinging after it, as it is said, *my soul clung after you*[^2], it is made happy, finding within even the creator and Lord of all visible things, not seeking outside the visible things, however heavenly, which either are not found, or are found in vain and with great labor, unless from the beauty of the things that are outside there be found the craftsman who is within and who works first in the soul the higher beauties, then in the body the lower ones.

**45. 2.** But against those who are now called mathematicians, who wish to subject our acts to the heavenly bodies and to sell us to the stars, and to receive from us the very price for which we are sold, nothing can be said more truly and more briefly than that they give no answer except upon receiving the constellations. Now in the constellations, degrees are noted, of which they say the zodiacal circle has three hundred sixty. And the motion of the heaven takes place, in one hour, over fifteen degrees, so that in such a span as one hour holds, fifteen degrees rise. And these degrees are said to have, each, sixty minutes. But the minutes of minutes, in the constellations from which they say they predict the future, they do not find.

But the conception of twins, since it is effected by a single intercourse—as the physicians attest, whose discipline is far more sure and manifest—happens in so small a point of time that it does not extend to two minutes of minutes. Whence, then, in twins is there so great a diversity of actions and outcomes and wills, who must of necessity have the same constellation of conception, and for both of whom one constellation is given to the mathematician, as though of one man?

But if they wish to hold to the constellations of birth, they are refuted by the very twins, who for the most part are poured forth from the womb one after the other in such a way that this interval of time again comes down to minutes of minutes, which they never take up to handle in the constellations, nor can they handle them.

But when it is said that they have predicted many true things, this comes about because people do not hold in memory the falsehoods and errors of those men, but, intent only on the things that have come about in accord with their answers, they forget those that have not come about, and they recall those that happen not by that art, which is no art at all, but by some obscure casting of lots.

But if they wish to attribute this to their skill, let them say that even dead written parchments, any whatever, divine by skill, from which a lot for the most part comes out according to one's wish. But if it is not by art that a verse foretelling the future often comes out of books, what wonder is it if even from the mind of one speaking, not by art but by lot, some prediction of future things comes out?

[^1]: Wis 13:8-9.
[^2]: Ps 63:9 (62:9).
