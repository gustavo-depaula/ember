# On what is written in the Gospel: — That Jesus baptized more than John, — although he himself did not baptize, but his disciples

The question is asked whether those who were baptized at the time when it is written that the Lord, through his disciples, baptized more than John received the Holy Spirit; for in another place in the Gospel it is said: *for the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^1]*.*

And the easiest answer, indeed, is this: that the Lord Jesus, who even raised the dead, could have allowed none of them to die until, after his glorification—that is, his resurrection from the dead and ascension into heaven—they received the Holy Spirit. But there comes to mind that thief to whom it was said, *amen I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise*[^2]*,* who had not even received baptism. And although Cornelius and those of the gentiles who had believed with him received the Holy Spirit even before they were baptized[^3], still I do not see how that thief either could have said without the Holy Spirit, *Lord, remember me when you come into your kingdom*[^4]*;* for *no one,* says the Apostle, *says 'Jesus is Lord' except in the Holy Spirit*[^5]*.* And the Lord himself showed the fruit of his faith, saying, *amen I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise.*

Therefore, just as by the ineffable power and justice of the sovereign God baptism was reckoned even to the believing thief, and what could not be received in his crucified body was held as received in his free soul, so too the Holy Spirit was given secretly before the Lord's glorification; but after the manifestation of his divinity, the Spirit was given more manifestly. And this is what was said: *but the Spirit had not yet been given,* that is, the Spirit had not yet so appeared that everyone would acknowledge that it had been given, just as the Lord too had not yet been glorified among men—and yet his eternal glorification never ceased to be, just as his coming is itself said to be that manifestation in mortal flesh, for he came to where he was, since *he came to his own,* and *he was in this world, and the world was made through him*[^6].

Therefore, just as the coming of the Lord is understood as a bodily manifestation, and yet before this manifestation he himself spoke in all the holy prophets as the Word of God and the Wisdom of God, so too the coming of the Holy Spirit is the manifestation of the Holy Spirit even to bodily eyes, when divided fire was seen over them and they began to speak in tongues[^7]. For if the Holy Spirit was not in men before the Lord's visible glorification, how could David say, *and take not your Holy Spirit from me*[^8]*;* or how was Elizabeth filled, and Zechariah her husband, so that they prophesied, and Anna and Simeon, of all of whom it is written that, filled with the Holy Spirit, they said the things we read in the Gospel[^9]?

Now, that God works some things secretly but others visibly through a visible creation belongs to the governance of his providence, by which all the divine actions are carried out with a most beautiful distinction in the orders of places and times, while the divinity itself is neither confined to nor moves among places, nor is stretched out or varied through times. And just as the Lord himself certainly had the Holy Spirit with him in that very man whom he bore, when he came to John to be baptized—and yet, after he was baptized, the Holy Spirit was seen to descend upon him in the form of a dove[^10]—so it must be understood that, before the manifest and visible coming of the Holy Spirit, whatever saints there were could have had the Spirit secretly. We have said this, of course, so that we may understand that even by that visible manifestation of the Holy Spirit, which is called his coming, the fullness of the Spirit was poured more abundantly into the hearts of men in a manner ineffable or even unthinkable.
