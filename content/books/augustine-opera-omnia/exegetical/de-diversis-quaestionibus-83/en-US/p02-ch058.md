# On John the Baptist

**58. 1.** John the Baptist, considering the Scripture which is read about him in the Gospel, is not absurdly believed, by many probable proofs, to bear the person of prophecy, and especially because of what the Lord says about him: *more than a prophet*[^1]. For he bears the image of the whole prophecy which, from the beginning of the human race to the coming of the Lord, was made concerning the Lord.

But the person of the Gospel is in the Lord himself, who was foretold through prophecy, whose preaching is increased throughout the whole world from the coming of the Lord himself, but prophecy is diminished after that which it foretold has come. And so the Lord says: *the Law and the Prophets were until John the Baptist; from now on the kingdom of God is preached*[^2]; and John himself says: *he must increase, but I must decrease*[^3].

And this was figured both in the days on which they were born and in the deaths by which they suffered. For John is born from the time when the days begin to diminish; the Lord is born from the time when the days begin to increase. The one is diminished in the head when he is killed; the other, indeed, is lifted up on the cross.

After, then, prophecy itself, established in John, pointed out with the finger as present him whom from the beginning of the human race it had sung as to come, it begins to diminish, and from then the preaching of the kingdom of God begins to grow. And therefore John baptized unto penance[^4]; for the old life is brought to an end up to penance, and from then the new begins.

**58. 2.** But not only in those who are properly called prophets, but in the very history of the Old Testament, it is gathered by those who devoutly seek, and are divinely helped to investigate these things, that prophecy is not silent. Yet it appears especially in those more evident figures of things: that the just Abel is killed by his brother[^5]

and the Lord by the Jews; that the ark of Noah is governed as the Church, as it were in the flood of the world[^6]; that Isaac is led to be sacrificed, and the ram in the briars is recognized in his place as, as it were, crucified[^7]; that in the two sons of Abraham, one of the slave woman, the other of the free woman, the two Testaments are understood[^8]; that the two peoples are foreshown in the twins Esau and Jacob[^9];

that Joseph, having suffered persecution from his brothers, is honored by strangers[^10], just as the Lord, killed by the persecuting Jews, was glorified among the gentiles. It would be long to recall them one by one, since the Apostle so concludes and says: *but these things happened to them in figure; and they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^11].

But the end of the ages, as the old age of the old man—when you have set up the whole human race as one man—is marked by the sixth age, in which the Lord came. For there are six ages even in one man: infancy, boyhood, adolescence, youth, maturity, and old age.

The first age of the human race, then, is from Adam to Noah; the second from Noah to Abraham, which are the most evident and most familiar joints; the third from Abraham to David, for thus the Evangelist Matthew divides it[^12]; the fourth from David to the migration to Babylon; the fifth from the migration to Babylon to the coming of the Lord.

The sixth is to be hoped from the coming of the Lord to the end of the world, in which the outer man, who is also called old, is corrupted as by old age, and the inner is renewed day by day[^13]. From then there is everlasting rest, which is signified by the Sabbath. To this it accords that man was made on the sixth day to the image and likeness of God[^14].

But no one is ignorant that the life of man, when it is now administering something, is supported by knowledge and action. For action is rash without knowledge, and knowledge sluggish without action. But the first life of man, to which no administration is rightly entrusted, is given over to the five senses of the body, which are sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. And therefore the first two ages of the human race are defined by ten generations each, as it were infancy and boyhood, that is, by five doubled, since generation is propagated by both sexes. There are, then, ten generations from Adam to Noah, and from there to Abraham another ten, which two ages we have said are the infancy and boyhood of the human race. But adolescence and youth and maturity, that is, from Abraham to David, and from there to the migration to Babylon, and from there to the coming of the Lord, are figured by four times ten generations, by seven doubled according to the same reckoning of both sexes, when action and knowledge have been added to the five which is in the senses of the body.

But old age is wont to hold even as much time as all the remaining ages. For since old age is said to begin from the sixtieth year, and human life can reach up to one hundred twenty years, it is manifest that old age alone can be as long as all the earlier ages. The last age, then, of the human race, which begins from the coming of the Lord to the end of the world, by how many generations it is to be reckoned is uncertain. And God willed this usefully to be hidden, as is written in the Gospel[^15], and the Apostle attests, saying that the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night[^16].

**58. 3.** But nevertheless, that the human race was visited in the sixth age by the humble coming of the Lord is taught by the generations distinguished above. By this visitation prophecy begins to be made manifest, which in the earlier five ages lay hidden. And because John bore the person of this prophecy, as was said above, therefore he is born of aged parents, as though, the world growing old, that prophecy begins to become known; and his mother hides herself for five months, as is written: *Elizabeth hid herself for five months*[^17].

But in the sixth month she is visited by Mary the mother of the Lord, and the infant leaps in the womb, as though, at the first coming of the Lord, in which he deigned to appear in humility, prophecy begins to be made manifest—but as though in the womb, that is, not yet so evidently that all confess it to be manifest as in the light, which we believe will happen at the second coming of the Lord, in which he is to come in glory, of whose coming Elijah is hoped to be the forerunner, as John was of this one.

And therefore it is said by the Lord: *Elijah has already come, and men did many things to him; and, if you wish to know, he himself is John the Baptist, who is to come*[^18]. Because in the same spirit and in the same power, as it were by the office of a preceding herald, both this one has already come and that one is to come. For this reason it is said that this John too, through the Spirit by which his father was filled as a seer, would be the forerunner of the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah[^19].

But when Mary had spent three months with Elizabeth, she departs[^20]. By which number it seems to me there is signified the faith of the Trinity and Baptism in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, by which the human race is imbued through the humble coming of the Lord and is perfected by the future coming of his glory.
