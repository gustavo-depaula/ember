# What Is to Be Loved

**35. 1.** Since whatever does not live does not fear, and no one would persuade us that life is to be done without so that we might be able to be without fear as well, it is to be loved to live without fear.

But again, because a life free from fear, if it also lacks understanding, is not to be sought, it is to be loved to live without fear and with understanding. Is that alone to be loved, or is love itself also to be loved?

But it is to be loved in such a way that the former things are not loved without it. Yet if love is loved on account of other things that are to be loved, it is not rightly said to be loved. For to love is nothing other than to seek a thing for its own sake. Is love, then, to be sought for its own sake, when, if what is loved is absent, this is undoubted wretchedness?

Next, since love is a certain motion, and there is no motion except toward something, when we ask what is to be loved we are asking what that is toward which one ought to be moved.

Wherefore, if love is to be loved, surely not every love is to be loved. For there is also a base love, by which the mind pursues things lower than itself, which is more properly called *cupidity*—namely, *the root of all evils*[^1]. And therefore that is not to be loved which can be taken away from the one loving and enjoying it.

The love of what thing, then, is to be loved, except of that which cannot be absent while it is loved? But that is what is nothing other to have than to know. Further, gold and every body is not such that to have it is to know it; it is not, therefore, to be loved.

And since something can be loved and not had—not only of those things which are not to be loved, such as some beautiful body, but even of those which are to be loved, such as the happy life—and since, again, something can be had and not loved, such as fetters, it is rightly asked whether anyone can fail to love that which to have is nothing other than to know, when he has it, that is, knows it.

But since we see that some people learn arithmetic, for instance, for no other reason than to become wealthy by that same discipline or to please men—and when they have learned it they refer it to the same end which they had set themselves when learning it—and since to have any discipline is nothing other than to know it, it can happen that someone has something which to have is the same as to know, and yet does not love it.

Although no one can perfectly have or know a good that is not loved. For who can know how great a good is that which he does not enjoy? But he does not enjoy it if he does not love it; therefore he who does not love does not have what is to be loved, even though he who does not have it may be able to love it. No one, therefore, knows the happy life and is wretched, since, if it is to be loved, as it is, to know it is to have it.

**35. 2.** Since this is so, what else is it to live happily but to have something eternal by knowing it? For that is eternal in which alone one rightly trusts, which cannot be taken away from the one loving it; and this very thing is what is nothing other to have than to know.

For the most excellent of all things is that which is eternal; and therefore we cannot have it except by that whereby we are more excellent, that is, the mind. But whatever is had by the mind is had by knowing; and no good is perfectly known that is not perfectly loved.

Nor, just as the mind alone can know, can it likewise love alone. For love is a certain appetite; and we see that appetite is present also in the other parts of the soul, which, if it agrees with the mind and reason, will be free, in such peace and tranquillity, for the mind to contemplate what is eternal.

Therefore the mind ought to love, with its other parts as well, this thing so great which is to be known by the mind. And since what is loved must of necessity affect the lover from itself, it comes about that what is eternal, when so loved, affects the mind with eternity.

Wherefore that life alone is happy which is eternal. But what is eternal that may affect the mind with eternity, except God?

But the love of things to be loved is better called love (caritas) or affection (dilectio). Wherefore that most healthful precept is to be considered with all the powers of thought: *you shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart and with your whole soul and with your whole mind*[^2]; and that which the Lord Jesus says: *this, however, is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you sent*[^3].

[^1]: 1 Tim 6:10.
[^2]: Matt 22:37.
[^3]: John 17:3.
