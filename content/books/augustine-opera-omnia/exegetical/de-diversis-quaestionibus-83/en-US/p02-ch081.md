# On Lent and Pentecost

**81. 1.** The whole discipline of wisdom, which pertains to the instruction of men, is to distinguish the Creator and the creation, and to worship the one as ruling and confess the other as subject. Now the Creator is God, from whom are all things, through whom are all things, in whom are all things[^1], and therefore the Trinity, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit.

But the creation is partly invisible, like the soul, partly visible, like the body. To the invisible the number three is assigned, which is why we are commanded to love God in a threefold way, *with the whole heart, with the whole soul, with the whole mind*[^2]; to the body the number four, on account of its most evident nature, that is, the hot and the cold, the moist and the dry; to the whole creation, therefore, the number seven.

For this reason the whole discipline that distinguishes and discerns the Creator and the creation is conveyed by the number ten.

This discipline, so long as it is signified temporally by bodily motions, consists in believing, and it nourishes little ones, as it were with milk, by the authority of deeds done that come and pass away, so as to make them fit for the contemplation which does not come and pass away but always remains.

In which discipline anyone—the things done temporally and divinely for the salvation of men having been narrated to him, or things to be done which are still proclaimed as future—if he has remained in faith and hoped for the things promised and taken care to fulfill with untiring love what the divine authority enjoins, will rightly conduct the life of this necessity and time, which is commended by the number forty.

For the number ten, which conveys the whole discipline, led out four times—that is, multiplied by the number assigned to the body, because the administration is carried on through bodily motions, which has been said to consist in faith—produces the number forty.

Thus it also obtains the stable wisdom that lacks any time, which is commended by the number ten, so that ten is added to forty—because the equal parts of the number forty, led together, also come to fifty. Now the number forty has these equal parts: first forty in single units, then twenty in twos, ten in fours, eight in fives, five in eights, four in tens, two in twenties. Therefore one and two and four and five and eight and ten and twenty, led together, make fifty. For this reason, just as the number forty, when its equal parts are computed, brings forth a further ten and becomes fifty, so the time of faith in the things done and to be done for our salvation, conducted with equity of life, obtains the understanding of stable wisdom, so that the discipline is made firm not only by believing, but also by understanding.

**81. 2.** And therefore the Church that now is—although we are sons of God, yet before it appears what we shall be—conducts itself in labors and afflictions, and in it the just man lives by faith[^3]. For *unless you believe,* he says, *you will not understand*[^4]*.* And this is the time in which we groan and are in pain, awaiting the redemption of our body[^5], which is celebrated as Lent. *But we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is*[^6]: when the ten is added to the forty, so that we may deserve not only to believe the things that pertain to faith, but also to understand the truth clearly. Such a Church—in which there will be no mourning, no admixture of evil men, no iniquity, but gladness and peace and joy—is prefigured by the celebration of Pentecost.

For this reason, after our Lord rose from the dead, the forty days having been spent with his disciples—namely, that same temporal dispensation which pertains to faith being conveyed through this number—he ascended into heaven[^7], and, ten other days having been spent, he sent the Holy Spirit[^8]: that is, so that the ten might be added to the forty by a certain breathing and burning of love and charity, for beholding not human and temporal things, but divine and eternal ones. And therefore this whole, that is, the number fifty of the days, is now to be marked by a celebration of gladness.

**81. 3.** These two times—that is, one of labor and anxiety, the other of joy and security—our Lord also signifies by the nets cast into the sea. For before his passion it is said of the net cast into the sea that they caught so many fish that they barely brought them to shore by dragging, and the nets were breaking[^9]. For they were not cast on the right side—for the Church of this time has many bad people—nor on the left—for it has good people too—but at random, so as to signify the admixture of good and bad.

And that the nets were broken signifies that, love having been violated, many heresies went out. But after the resurrection, when he wished to foreshow the Church of the future time, where all will be perfect and holy, he commanded the nets to be cast on the right side, and huge fish were caught, one hundred fifty-three, the disciples marveling that, although they were so big, the nets were not broken[^10].

Their size signifies the greatness of wisdom and righteousness, but the number signifies the discipline itself, made perfect both by the temporal dispensation and by the eternal regeneration, which we said is commended by the number fifty.

For since there will then be no need of bodily aids, and faith and wisdom will be contained in the mind—because we said that the number three is assigned to the mind—we lead fifty three times, and they make one hundred fifty. To which number the Trinity is added, because all this perfection is consecrated in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit; so they make one hundred fifty-three, which is found to be the number of the fish caught on the right side.
