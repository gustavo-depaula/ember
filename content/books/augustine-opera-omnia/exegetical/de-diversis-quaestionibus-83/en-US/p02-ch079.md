# Why did the magicians of Pharaoh perform certain — miracles like Moses the servant of God? 378

**79. 1.** Every soul partly bears the power of something private to itself, and is partly constrained and ruled by the laws of the whole, as by public ones. Since, then, each visible thing in this world has an angelic power set over it, as the divine Scripture testifies in several places, it acts upon the thing over which it is set in one way as by private right, and in another way is compelled to act as it were publicly.

For the whole is more powerful than the part, since even that which it does there privately it is allowed to do only as far as the law of the whole allows. But each soul is the more purified by piety the less it delights in what is private to itself and the more it looks to the law of the whole and obeys it devoutly and gladly.

For the law of the whole is divine wisdom. But the more it rejoices in what is private to itself, and, neglecting God—who usefully and wholesomely presides over all souls—wishes to be as a god to itself or to others over whom it can have power, loving its own power over itself or over others rather than his over all, the more sordid it is, and the more it is compelled, as a punishment, to serve the divine laws, as it were public ones.

Therefore, the more the human soul, having deserted God, has been delighted by its own honors or its own power, the more it is made subject to such powers, which rejoice in what is private to themselves and desire to be honored by men like gods.

To whom it is often granted by divine law that, to those whom they have subjugated to themselves according to their merits, they may by that private right grant even something of miracles to be displayed in those things over which they are set, in the lowest yet most ordered rank of powers.

But where the divine law, as it were a public one, commands, it certainly overcomes the private license, although even that private license would be nothing except by the permission of the universal divine power.

And so it comes about that the holy servants of God, when it is useful for them to have this gift, command the lowest powers, according to the public and in a way imperial law—that is, the power of the most high God—to perform certain visible miracles.

For in them God himself commands, whose temple they are, and whom they love most ardently, content with his private power. But in magical commands, for the enticement of deception—so that they may subjugate to themselves those to whom they grant such things—they grant effect to their prayers and rites, bestowing by that private right what they are allowed to bestow on those who honor them and serve them and keep certain pacts with them in their rites.

And when the magicians seem to command, they terrify the lower ones through the names of more sublime ones, so as to display to those who marvel certain visible things—which, because of the weakness of the flesh, seem great to men unable to behold eternal things, things which the true God grants through himself to those who love him.

But God permits these things, justly moderating all things, so that he may distribute their servitudes and freedoms according to their desires and choices; and if at any time, by the invocation of the most high God, they obtain something for their evil desires, that is vengeance, not grace. For not in vain does the Apostle say: *God gave them up to the desires of their heart*[^1]. For the facility of perpetrating certain sins is the punishment of other preceding ones.

**79. 2.** But as for what the Lord says—*Satan cannot cast out Satan*[^2]—lest perhaps anyone, using the names of certain lowest powers, when he has cast out a demon, should think this statement of the Lord false, let him understand it to be said in this sense: that in this way Satan, even if he spares the body or the bodily senses, spares them for this reason: that, through the error of impiety, he may rule over the will of the man himself with a greater triumph. But in this way Satan does not go out, but rather enters into the inmost parts, so as to work in him as the Apostle says: *according to the prince of the power of this air, who now works in the sons of distrust*[^3]. For he was not disturbing and tormenting the senses of their body, or dashing their bodies together, but reigning in their will, or rather in their desire.

**79. 3.** But as for what he says—that false prophets will perform many signs and wonders, so as to deceive even, if it were possible, the elect[^4]—he certainly admonishes us to understand that even wicked men perform certain miracles such as the saints cannot perform. Nor, however, are they on that account to be thought to be of a better standing with God. For the magicians of the Egyptians were not more acceptable to God than the people of Israel, because that people could not do what they did, although Moses, in the power of God, could do greater things[^5]. But these things are not bestowed on all the saints for this reason: lest the weak be deceived by a most pernicious error, supposing that there are greater gifts in such deeds than in the works of righteousness, by which eternal life is acquired. For this reason the Lord forbids the disciples to rejoice in this, when he says: *do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven*[^6].

**79. 4.** When, therefore, the magicians do such things as the saints sometimes do, they indeed appear to be such visibly, but they are done both with a different end and by a different right. For the former do them seeking their own glory, the latter seeking the glory of God; and the former do them through certain things granted to the powers in their order, as by private dealings or favors, but the latter through public administration, by the command of him to whom all creation is subjected.

For in one way a possessor is compelled to give a horse to a soldier, in another way he hands it over to a buyer, or gives or lends it to anyone. And just as very many bad soldiers, whom the imperial discipline condemns, terrify certain possessors with the standards of their emperor, and extort from them something that is not publicly commanded, so sometimes bad Christians or schismatics or heretics, through the name of Christ or Christian words or sacraments, exact something from the powers, which have been enjoined to yield to the honor of Christ.

But when they willingly yield to bad men commanding them, they yield in order to seduce men, in whose error they rejoice. For this reason the magicians perform miracles in one way, good Christians in another, bad Christians in another: the magicians through private contracts, good Christians through public justice, bad Christians through the signs of public justice.

Nor is it surprising that these signs have force when they are employed by them, since even when they are usurped by outsiders, who have not at all given their name to this service, they nevertheless have force on account of the honor of the most excellent emperor.

Of whom was that man about whom the disciples reported to the Lord that he was casting out demons in his name, although he did not follow with them[^7]. But when powers of this kind do not yield to these signs, God himself forbids it in hidden ways, when he judges it just and useful.

For in no way do any spirits dare to despise these signs; for they tremble at them, wherever they have beheld them. But, with men unaware, something else is divinely commanded—whether to confound the bad, when they ought to be confounded, as we read of the sons of Sceva in the *Acts of the Apostles*, to whom the unclean spirit said: *Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are you?*[^8]; or to admonish the good, that they may advance in faith and be able to do these things not boastfully but usefully; or to distinguish the gifts of the members of the Church, as the Apostle says: *are all workers of miracles? Do all have gifts of healings?*[^9] For these reasons, then, as has been said, it is often divinely commanded, with men unaware—that is, that when these signs are employed, powers of this kind do not obey the will of men.

**79. 5.** But so that the bad often harm the good temporally, they receive power over them for the greater benefit of the good, on account of the exercise of patience. And so let the Christian soul always be watchful, in its tribulations, to follow the will of its Lord, lest by resisting the ordering of God it acquire for itself a heavier judgment.

For what the Lord himself, acting as a man, said to Pontius Pilate, this Job too could say to the devil: *you would have no power over me unless it had been given to you from above*[^10]. Therefore not the will of the one to whose malice power is given over the good, but the will of him by whom this power is given, ought to be most dear to us. *Since tribulation works patience, and patience proof, and proof hope; but hope does not confound, since the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^11].
