# On the Father and the Son

**23.** Everything chaste is chaste by chastity, and everything eternal is eternal by eternity, and everything beautiful by beauty, and everything good by goodness. So too everything wise is wise by wisdom, and everything like is like by likeness.

But a thing is said to be chaste by chastity in two ways: either because it begets it, so that it is chaste by the chastity which it begets and to which it is the beginning and cause of its being; or, in another way, a thing is chaste by participation in chastity, which can at some time be not chaste. And so it is to be understood of the rest.

For the soul, too, is understood or believed to attain eternity, but it becomes eternal by participation in eternity. God, however, is not eternal in this way, but is so because he is the author of eternity itself. This may be understood also of beauty and of goodness.

Wherefore, when God is called wise, and is called wise by that wisdom without which it is impious to believe that he either ever was or could be, he is not called wise by participation in wisdom, as the soul is—which can be both wise and not wise—but because he himself begot that wisdom by which he is called wise.

Likewise, those things which are chaste or eternal or beautiful or good or wise by participation receive, as has been said, the capacity to be neither chaste nor eternal nor beautiful nor good nor wise. But chastity itself, eternity, beauty, goodness, and wisdom in no way admit either corruption or, so to speak, temporality, or baseness, or malice. So too those things which are like by participation admit unlikeness. But likeness itself can in no way be unlike in any part.

Hence it comes about that when the Son is called the likeness of the Father—because it is by participation in him that whatever things are like, whether to one another or to God, are like; for he is the first form, by which they are, so to speak, given form, and the shape by which all things are shaped—he can in no part be unlike the Father. He is, therefore, the same as the Father, so that this Son is that Father—that is, this is the likeness, that is the one whose likeness it is; this is substance, that is substance, from which there is one substance.

For if it is not one substance, the likeness admits unlikeness, which all most truthful reason denies can happen.
