# On the Cross of Christ

**25.** The Wisdom of God took on a man as an example by which we might live rightly. But it belongs to a right life not to fear the things that are not to be feared. And death is not to be feared. It was fitting, therefore, that this very thing be shown by the death of that man whom the Wisdom of God took on. But there are men who, although they do not fear death itself, nevertheless shudder at some kind of death. Yet, just as death itself is not to be feared, so no kind of death is to be feared by a man living well and rightly. None the less, therefore, this too had to be shown by the cross of that man. For among all kinds of death there was none more execrable and dreadful than that kind.
