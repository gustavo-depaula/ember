# On What Was Said, I Repent That I Made Man

**52.** The Divine Scriptures, raising us from an earthly and human sense to a divine and heavenly one, have come down even to those words which the custom of even the most foolish people uses among themselves.

And so the names even of those affections which our mind undergoes—which one who is wiser now understands to be most far removed from God—those men through whom the Holy Spirit spoke did not hesitate most fittingly to set down in the books;

so that, for example, because it is most difficult for a man to take vengeance without anger, they judged that the vengeance of God, which takes place altogether without that disturbance, should nevertheless be called anger;

likewise, because men are accustomed to guard the chastity of a wife by being jealous, they called that providence of God—by which it is enjoined and brought about that the soul not be corrupted and, following now these gods and now those, in a way play the harlot—the jealousy of God;

so too the hand of God means the power by which he works; and the feet of God, the power by which he reaches out to guarding and governing all things;

and the ears of God or the eyes of God, the power by which he perceives and understands all things; and the face of God, the power by which he manifests himself and is recognized, and the rest in this manner,

for this reason, namely, because we, to whom the discourse is made, are accustomed both to work with our hands, and to walk with our feet, and to arrive where the mind carries us, and to perceive corporeal things with our ears and eyes and the other senses of the body, and to be made known by the face, and whatever else pertains to this, as it were, rule.

In this manner, then, since we are not accustomed easily to change something begun and to carry it over into something else except by repenting—although divine providence appears, to those who behold it with a serene mind, to administer all things in a most certain order—nevertheless, most fittingly adapted to lowly human understanding, those things which begin to be and do not persist as long as they were hoped to persist are said to be taken away, as it were, by the repentance of God.
