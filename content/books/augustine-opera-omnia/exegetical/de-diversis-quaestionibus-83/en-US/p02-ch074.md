# On what is written in Paul's Letter to the Colossians: — In whom we have redemption and the remission of sins, — who is the image of the invisible God

**74.** Image and equality and likeness must be distinguished: for where there is an image, there is at once likeness, but not at once equality; where there is equality, there is at once likeness, but not at once an image; where there is likeness, there is not at once an image, not at once equality.

Where there is an image, there is at once likeness, but not at once equality: as in a mirror there is the image of a man; because it is expressed from him, there is also necessarily likeness, but not equality, because many things are lacking to the image which are present in that thing from which it is expressed.

Where there is equality, there is at once likeness, but not at once an image: as in two equal eggs, because there is equality, there is also likeness; for whatever is present in one is present in the other; yet there is no image, because neither is expressed from the other.

Where there is likeness, there is not at once an image, not at once equality; for every egg is like every egg, insofar as it is an egg; but the egg of a partridge, although insofar as it is an egg it is like the egg of a hen, is nevertheless not its image, because it is not expressed from it, nor equal, because it is both smaller and of another kind of living thing.

But where it is said "not at once," it is certainly understood that it can sometimes be so. There can therefore be some image in which there is also equality, so that in parents and children there would be found image and equality and likeness, if an interval of time were lacking; for from the parent the likeness of the child is expressed, so that it is rightly called an image, and it can be so great that equality too is rightly spoken of, except that the parent precedes in time. From which it is understood that equality too can sometimes have not only likeness but also an image, which was made manifest in the earlier example.

There can also sometimes be likeness and equality, although there is no image, as we said of equal eggs. There can also be likeness and an image, although there is no equality, as we showed in the mirror. There can also be likeness where there is both equality and an image, as we recalled of children, except for the time by which the parents precede. For thus we call one syllable equal to a syllable, although the one precedes and the other follows.

But in God, because the condition of time is absent—for God cannot rightly be seen to have begotten in time the Son through whom he founded times—it follows that not only is there his image, because it is from him, and likeness, because it is an image[^1], but also equality so great that not even an interval of time is an impediment.

[^1]: See Col 1:15.
