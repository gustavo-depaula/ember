# The Opinion of a Certain Wise Man

**12.** "Take action," he says, "O wretched mortals, take action that the malignant spirit may never pollute this dwelling, that it may not, mingled with the senses, defile the holiness of the soul and overcloud the light of the mind.

This evil creeps in through all the avenues of the senses: it gives itself to shapes, adapts itself to colors, clings to sounds, lies hidden in anger, in the deceit of speech; it submits itself to smells, pours itself into tastes, and, by the flood of turbid motion, darkens the senses with shadowy affections; with certain mists it fills all the passages of the understanding, through which the ray of the mind is wont to spread out the light of reason. And because it is a ray of ethereal light, and in it is a mirror of the divine presence—for in it God, in it the innocent will, in it the merit of right action shine back.

God is present everywhere. But he is present to each one of us at the moment when the unimpaired purity of our mind has supposed itself to be in his presence. For just as the sight of the eyes, if it has been damaged, supposes that whatever it cannot see is not present—for in vain does the present image of things stand round the eyes if soundness is lacking to the eyes—so too God, who is nowhere absent, is present in vain to polluted souls, since the blindness of the mind cannot see him."
