# Whether One Person Understands Any Thing More Than Another, and — Whether the Understanding of the Same Thing Thus Proceeds to Infinity

**32.** Whoever understands any thing otherwise than that thing is, is deceived; and everyone who is deceived does not understand that in which he is deceived. Whoever, therefore, understands any thing otherwise than it is, does not understand it. Nothing, then, can be understood except as it is.

But we understand something just as it is—as this very thing, that nothing is understood which is not understood as it is. Wherefore it is not to be doubted that there is a perfect understanding, than which none more excellent can be, and therefore that the understanding by which each thing is understood does not proceed to infinity, nor can one person understand it more than another.
