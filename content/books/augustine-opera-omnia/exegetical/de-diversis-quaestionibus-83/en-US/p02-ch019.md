# On God and the Creature

**19.** What is unchangeable is eternal; for it is always of the same kind. But what is changeable is subject to time; for it is not always of the same kind, and therefore is not rightly called eternal. For what is changed does not remain; what does not remain is not eternal.

And this is the difference between the immortal and the eternal: that everything eternal is immortal, but not everything immortal is, to speak precisely, called eternal; because even if something always lives, yet if it undergoes changeableness, it is not properly called eternal, since it is not always of the same kind—although it may rightly be called immortal, because it always lives.

Yet sometimes even what is immortal is called eternal. But that which both undergoes change and is said to live by the presence of a soul, though it is not itself a soul, can in no way be understood as immortal, and much less as eternal. For in the eternal, when the word is properly used, neither is anything past as though it had passed away, nor anything future as though it were not yet, but whatever is, simply is.
