# But of That Day and Hour No One Knows, — Neither the Angels of Heaven, nor the Son of Man, — but the Father Alone

**60.** Just as God is said to know even when he makes someone know, as it is written: *the Lord your God tests you, that he may know whether you love him*[^1].

For it was not said as though God did not know, but so that they themselves might know how far they had advanced in the love of God, which is not fully recognized by men except by the temptations that befall them; and the "tests" is put for "allows to be tested."

So too, when he is said not to know, it is either put for the fact that he does not approve, that is, does not recognize in his discipline and teaching, as it was said, *I do not know you*[^2], or for the fact that he usefully makes them not know what it is useless to know.

Therefore it is rightly taken both that what was said—that the Father alone knows—was said in this way because he makes the Son know, and that what was said—that the Son does not know—was said in this way because he makes men not know, that is, does not disclose to them what they would know uselessly.

[^1]: Deut 13:3.
[^2]: Matt 25:12.
