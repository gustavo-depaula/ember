# On the One Hundred Fifty-three Fish

**57. 1.** *All things are yours, but you are Christ's, and Christ is God's*[^1]. If they are counted from the head, there are found one, two, three, four. Likewise: *the head of woman is man, the head of man is Christ, and the head of Christ is God*[^2].

If they are counted in the same way, there are found likewise one, two, three, four. Now one and two and three and four brought together make ten. Wherefore the number ten rightly signifies the teaching that introduces God the founder and the founded creature. And when the perfect and indestructible body is made subject to the perfect and indestructible soul, and that in turn is made subject to Christ, and he to God—not as though unlike or of another nature, but as the Son to the Father—this whole is rightly signified by the same number ten, which is hoped to be, after the resurrection of the body, forever.

And perhaps for this reason those who are hired for the vineyard receive a denarius under the name of wage[^3]. But as one and two and three and four together make ten, so one and two and three and four taken four times make forty.

**57. 2.** But if the number four rightly signifies the body on account of the four most familiar natures of which it consists—the dry, the moist, the cold, and the hot—and because the progression from a point to length, from length to breadth, from breadth to height makes the solidity of the body, which again is contained in the number four, not absurdly is the number forty understood to signify the temporal dispensation which was carried out for our salvation, when the Lord took on a body and deigned to appear visibly to men.

For one and two and three and four, which signify the Creator and the creature, taken four times—that is, demonstrated temporally through the body—make forty. For between "four" and "four times" there is this difference, that four is in a state of rest, four times in motion. Therefore, as four is referred to the body, so four times is referred to time; and the mystery carried out bodily and temporally is intimated, for the sake of those who were entangled in the love of bodies and subject to times. The number forty, then, as was said, is believed not incongruously to signify that temporal dispensation itself. And perhaps this is why the Lord fasted forty days[^4], showing the want of this world, which is carried on by the motion of bodies and by times; and he was forty days with his disciples after the resurrection, intimating to them, I believe, this very temporal dispensation which he carried out for our salvation.

But the number forty, its parts that measure it being reckoned, comes up to the number fifty, commending the same thing, since indeed those very parts which measure it are equal to one another—because corporeal and visible action, administered with equity in time, procures perfection for man. And this perfection, as was said, is signified by the number ten, just as the number forty, its equal parts being brought into a sum, brings forth ten, by which it comes to fifty, as was said above. For the one, of which forty has forty; and the two, of which it has twenty; and the four, of which it has ten; and the five, of which it has eight; and the eight, of which it has five; and the ten, of which it has four; and the twenty, of which it has two—brought together make fifty. For no other number can measure the number forty by equal parts except these which we have enumerated and, reckoned, have brought up to the number fifty. When the forty days after the resurrection had been completed with his disciples—that is, commending to them what was carried out for us temporally—the Lord ascended into heaven; and after another ten days he sent the Holy Spirit[^5], by whom those who had believed visible and temporal things might be perfected spiritually for grasping invisible things—in those very ten days, after which he sent the Holy Spirit, indicating by the number ten that same perfection which is conferred through the Holy Spirit, which the number forty, its equal parts being reckoned, brings forth, and it becomes fifty; just as by the temporal dispensation administered with equity one arrives at the perfection which the number ten signifies, which ten together with forty makes fifty. Therefore, since the perfection that comes about through the Holy Spirit, so long as we still walk in the flesh, although we do not live carnally, is coupled with that temporal dispensation itself, the number fifty rightly seems to pertain to the Church—but now purified and perfect, which embraces in love both the faith of the temporal dispensation and the hope of future eternity, that is, couples, as it were, the number forty with ten.

But this Church, to which the number fifty pertains, whether because it is strained out from three kinds of men—Jews, gentiles, and carnal Christians—or because it is imbued with the mystery of the Trinity, by the number that signifies it taken three times comes up to one hundred fifty; for fifty taken three times makes one hundred fifty. And when you have added to it the three themselves, because what is washed in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit by the bath of regeneration ought to be marked and eminent[^6], they make one hundred fifty-three.

Which number of fish is found, because the nets were cast into the right-hand part, and therefore it holds great fish[^7], that is, perfect ones and fit for the kingdom of heaven. For that other similitude of the little net, not cast into the right hand, caught good and bad together, who are separated on the shore[^8].

For now, within the nets of the precepts and sacraments of God, good and bad dwell together in the Church that now is. But the separation comes about at the end of the world, as at the end of the sea, that is, on the shore, when the just reign, first temporally, as is written in *Revelation*, then forever in that city which is there described[^9], where, the temporal dispensation now coming to rest, which is signified by the number forty, the ten remains, which the saints who labor in the vineyard are to receive as their wage.

**57. 3.** It can also, if this number is considered, point to the holiness of the Church, which was brought about through our Lord Jesus Christ; so that, since the creature consists of the number seven—the three being attributed to the soul and the four to the body—the taking on of the man himself is said to be three times seven, because both the Father sent the Son, and the Father is in the Son, and by the gift of the Holy Spirit he was born of the Virgin.

And these are three, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit; but the man himself, seven times, was taken on by the temporal dispensation, so as to become everlasting. The sum of the number, then, becomes twenty-one, that is, three times seven. And this taking on of the man availed for the liberation of the Church, of which he is the head[^10].

But the Church itself, on account of soul and body, is found in the same number seven. Let twenty-one, then, likewise be taken seven times, on account of those who are freed through the Lord's Man, and they make one hundred forty-seven. To this is added the number six, the sign of perfection, because it consists of its parts that measure it, in such a way that nothing less and nothing more is found. For one measures it, of which it has six; and two, of which it has three; and three, of which it has two; which brought together, one and two and three, make six. And this perhaps pertains also to that mystery, that God on the sixth day perfected all his works[^11].

When, therefore, you have added six to one hundred forty-seven, which is the sign of perfection, they make one hundred fifty-three. Which number of fish is found, after, at the Lord's command, the nets were cast into the right-hand part, where the sinners, who pertain to the left, are not found.
