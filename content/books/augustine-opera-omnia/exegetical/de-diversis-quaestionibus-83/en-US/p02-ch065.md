# On the Resurrection of Lazarus

**65.** Although, according to the gospel history, we hold with full faith that Lazarus was raised, yet I do not doubt that it also signifies something by way of allegory; nor, when deeds that were done are allegorized, do they lose the credit of the deed performed—just as when Paul expounds the allegory of Abraham's two sons as being the two Testaments[^1]. Was it therefore the case either that Abraham did not exist, or that he did not have those sons?

Therefore, in the allegory too, let us take Lazarus in the tomb to be the soul buried under earthly sins, that is, the whole human race, which in another place the Lord signifies by the lost sheep, for whose freeing he says that, leaving the ninety-nine in the mountains, he came down[^2].

And that he asks, saying, *where have you laid him?*, I judge to signify our calling, which takes place in secret; for the predestination of our calling is hidden. A sign of this secret is the Lord's question, as though he did not know, when it is we who do not know—as the Apostle says, *that I may know, as also I am known*[^3]; or because the Lord shows in another place that he does not know sinners, saying, *I never knew you*[^4]*,* which the buried Lazarus signified, because in his discipline and precepts there are no sins. Similar to this question is that one in *Genesis*: *Adam, where are you?*[^5], since he had sinned and had hidden himself from the face of God.

This hiding the burial here signifies, so that the one dying may have the likeness of one sinning, the one buried of one hidden from the face of God. And that he says, *take away the stone*[^6], I think signifies those who wished to impose the burden of circumcision on those coming to the Church from the gentiles, against whom the Apostle writes in many ways[^7], or those who live corruptly in the Church and are a stumbling block to those wishing to believe. *Martha says to him: 'Lord, it is already the fourth day, and he stinks*[^8].' The last of the four elements is earth; it therefore signifies the stench of earthly sins, that is, of carnal desires.

For *you are earth,* said the Lord to Adam when he had sinned, *and into the earth you shall go*[^9]*.* And when the stone had been taken away, he came out of the tomb with hands and feet bound, and his face was covered with a cloth. That he came out of the tomb signifies the soul withdrawing from carnal vices; but that he was wrapped in bindings is because we who withdraw from carnal things and serve the law of God with the mind, while still established in the body, cannot be free from the troubles of the flesh, as the Apostle says: *with the mind I serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin*[^10].

And that his face was covered with a cloth is because in this life we cannot have full knowledge, as the Apostle says: *now we see through a glass in a riddle, but then face to face*[^11]*.* And that Jesus said, *loose him and let him go*[^12]*,* is because after this life all veils are taken away, so that we may see face to face.

And how great a difference there is between the man whom the Wisdom of God bore, through whom we have been freed, and other men, is understood from this: that Lazarus is not loosed except by coming out of the tomb—that is, even the reborn soul cannot be free from all sin and ignorance except by the dissolution of the body, so long as it sees through a glass in a riddle; but the Lord's linen cloths and the face cloth—he who committed no sin and was ignorant of nothing—were found in the tomb[^13].

For he alone in the flesh not only was not so confined by the tomb that any sin should be found in him[^14], but was not even wrapped in linen cloths, so that anything should be hidden from him or hold him back from the journey.
