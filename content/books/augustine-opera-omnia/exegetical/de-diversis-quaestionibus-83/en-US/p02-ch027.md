# On Providence

**27.** It can happen that divine providence both punishes and brings help through an evil man. For the impiety of the Jews both supplanted the Jews and was for the salvation of the gentiles. Likewise it can happen that divine providence both condemns and assists through a good man, as the Apostle says, *to some we are the fragrance of life unto life, but to others the fragrance of death unto death*[^1].

But since every tribulation is either a punishment of the impious or a training of the just—because the same threshing-sledge both cuts up the chaff and strips the grain from the chaff, from which "tribulation" took its name—and since, again, peace and quiet from bodily troubles both profit the good and corrupt the wicked, divine providence orders all these things according to the deserts of souls.

Yet the good do not choose for themselves the service of tribulation, nor do the wicked love peace. Wherefore these too, through whom is brought about what they do not know, receive the wage not of the justice that is referred to God, but of their own ill will. Just as it is not imputed to the good that harm is done to someone when they wish to be of profit, but the reward of good will is bestowed on a good disposition.

So too the rest of creation is, according to the deserts of rational souls, either felt or hidden, either troublesome or convenient. For since the most high God administers well all the things he has made, there is nothing disordered in the universe and nothing unjust, whether we know it or not.

But the sinful soul gives offense in a part; yet because it is, according to its deserts, there where it is fitting for such a one to be, and suffers those things which it is just for such a one to suffer, it disfigures the whole kingdom of God with no foulness of its own.

Wherefore, since we do not know all the things that the divine order does well concerning us, in good will alone do we act according to the Law; but in the rest we are acted upon according to law, while the law itself remains unchangeable and orders all changeable things by a most beautiful governance. *Glory,* therefore, *to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^2].

[^1]: 2 Cor 2:16.
[^2]: Luke 2:14.
