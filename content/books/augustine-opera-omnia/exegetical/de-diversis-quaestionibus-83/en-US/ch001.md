# Revisions I

## Chapter 26 (25): Miscellany of Eighty-three Questions, in One Book

**1.** Among the things we have written there is also a certain lengthy work, which is nevertheless reckoned as a single book, whose title is *Miscellany of Eighty-three Questions.* But since they had been scattered across many sheets of paper, because from the very first time of my conversion, after we came to Africa, they were dictated as the brothers questioned me, whenever they saw me at leisure, with no order being preserved by me, I gave orders, once I was already a bishop, that they be collected and that one book be made out of them, numbers being added, so that whatever anyone wished to read he might easily find.

**2.** The first of these questions is *whether the soul is from itself.*

The second: *on free choice.*

The third: *whether, with God as its author, a human being is made worse.*

The fourth: *what the cause is that a human being is made worse.*

The fifth: *whether an irrational animal can be happy.*

The sixth: *on evil.*

The seventh: *what in a living being is properly called soul.*

The eighth: *whether the soul is moved of itself.*

The ninth: *whether truth can be perceived by the senses of the body.* In it, what I said—*that everything which a bodily sense reaches, which is also called sensible, is changed without any interval of time*[^1]*—*is undoubtedly not true of the incorruptible bodies of the resurrection; but for now no sense of our body reaches them, unless perhaps something of the sort is revealed by God.

The tenth: *whether the body is from God.*

The eleventh: *why Christ was born of a woman.*

The twelfth, in the place where the title is *the opinion of a certain wise man,* is not mine. But because it became known to certain brothers through me, who at that time were most diligently collecting these things from me, and it pleased them, they wished to write it among our own works. It belongs to a certain Fonteius of Carthage, *On Cleansing the Mind in order to See God*, which he wrote indeed as a pagan, but he died a baptized Christian.

The thirteenth is *by what proof it is established that human beings excel the beasts.*

The fourteenth: *that the body of our Lord Jesus Christ was not a phantasm.*

The fifteenth: *on the intellect.*

The sixteenth: *on the Son of God.*

The seventeenth: *on the knowledge of God*.

The eighteenth: *on the Trinity*.

The nineteenth: *on God and the creature.*

The twentieth: *on the place of God.*

The twenty-first: *whether God is not the author of evil.* Here it must be seen that what I said be not misunderstood—*he who is the author of all the things that are is not the author of evil, because insofar as they are, to that extent they are good*[^2]*—*and that it not therefore be thought that the punishment of the wicked is not from him, which is surely an evil for those who are punished.

But I said this in the way in which it was said, *God did not make death*[^3], when elsewhere it is written, *death and life are from the Lord God*[^4]. The punishment of the wicked, then, which is from God, is indeed an evil for the wicked, but it is among God's good works, since it is just that the wicked be punished, and surely everything that is just is good.

The twenty-second is *that God does not suffer necessity.*

The twenty-third is *on the Father and the Son.* Here I said *that he himself begot that wisdom by which he is called wise*[^5]*;* but I treated this question better later in the book *On the Trinity*[^6].

The twenty-fourth is *whether both sin and right action lie within the free choice of the will.* That this is so is altogether most true; but in order that one may be free for acting rightly, one is set free by the grace of God.

The twenty-fifth is *on the cross of Christ*.

The twenty-sixth: *on the difference of sins.*

The twenty-seventh: *on providence*.

The twenty-eighth: *why God willed to make the world.*

The twenty-ninth: *whether there is anything above or below in the universe*.

The thirtieth: *whether all things have been created for the use of man.*

The thirty-first is itself not mine either, but Cicero's[^7]. But because this too became known to the brothers through me, they wrote it among the things they were collecting, wishing to know how the virtues of the mind were divided and defined by him.

The thirty-second is *whether one person understands any thing more than another, and whether the understanding of the same thing thus proceeds to infinity.*

The thirty-third: *on fear.*

The thirty-fourth: *whether nothing other than to be free from fear is to be loved.*

The thirty-fifth: *what is to be loved.* In it, what I said—*that that is to be loved which to have is nothing other than to know*[^8]*—*I do not sufficiently approve.

For those to whom it was said, *do you not know that you are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwells in you?*[^9] did not lack God; yet they did not know him, or did not know him as he is to be known.

Likewise what I said—*no one, therefore, knows the happy life and is wretched*[^10]*;* by *knows* I meant in the way in which it is to be known. For who is wholly ignorant of it, at least among those who already use reason, since they have known that they wish to be happy[^11]?

The thirty-sixth is *on nourishing love.* Here I said, *God and the mind, then, when they are loved, are properly called the most purified and consummated love, if nothing else is loved*[^12]*.* But if this is true, how then does the Apostle say, *no one ever hated his own flesh*[^13], and from this admonish that wives be loved? But it was said for this reason: *it is properly called love,* since the flesh is indeed loved, yet not properly but for the sake of the soul to which it is subject for use. For even if it seems to be loved for its own sake, when we do not want it to be deformed, its comeliness must be referred to something else, namely to that from which all things are comely.

The thirty-seventh is *on him who is ever born.*

The thirty-eighth: *on the conformation of the soul.*

The thirty-ninth: *on nourishment.*

The fortieth: *since the nature of souls is one, whence come the diverse wills of human beings?*

The forty-first: *since God made all things, why did he not make them equal*?

The forty-second: *how the Wisdom of God, the Lord Jesus Christ, was both in his mother's womb and in the heavens.*

The forty-third: *why did the Son of God appear in a man, and the Holy Spirit in a dove?*[^14]

The forty-fourth: *why did the Lord Jesus Christ come so long afterward?* Here, when I was recalling the ages of the human race as though of a single man, I said, *nor was it fitting that a Teacher come from God, by imitation of whom one might be formed in the best morals, except at the time of youth*[^15].

And I added *that what the Apostle says bears on this*, that *under the Law the little ones were guarded as under a guardian*[^16].

But it may give pause why elsewhere I said that Christ came in the sixth age of the human race, as though in its old age[^17]. So what was said about youth refers to the vigor and fervor of faith, *which works through love*[^18]; but what was said about old age refers to the number of the times. For both can be understood in the totality of human beings, which cannot be in the ages of individuals; just as in the body youth and old age cannot exist at the same time, but in the mind they can, the one on account of eagerness, the other on account of seriousness.

The forty-fifth is *against the astrologers.*

The forty-sixth: *on the ideas.*

The forty-seventh: *whether we can ever see our own thoughts.* Here, as for what I said—*it must be believed that the angelic bodies, such as we hope to have, are most luminous and ethereal*[^19]*—*if this is taken to mean without the members we now have and without the substance of flesh, however incorruptible, it is an error. But this question, about seeing our own thoughts, was treated much better in the work *The City of God*[^20].

The forty-eighth is *on things to be believed.*

The forty-ninth: *why did the children of Israel offer visible sacrifices of animal victims.*

The fiftieth: *on the equality of the Son*.

The fifty-first: *on man made to the image and likeness of God*[^21]. Here, what is it that I said—*a human being is not rightly called such without life*—when even a man's corpse is called a man? At least, then, I should have said "is not properly called," where I said *is not rightly called.*

Likewise I said, *nor is it unskillfully distinguished that the image and likeness of God is one thing, and to the image and likeness of God another, as we understand man to have been made*[^22]. This is not to be understood as though man is not called the image of God, since the Apostle says, *a man indeed ought not to veil his head, since he is the image and glory of God*[^23],

but he is also said to be made to the image of God, which is not said of the Only-begotten, who is image only, not to the image.

The fifty-second: *on what was said, I repent that I made man*[^24].

The fifty-third: *on the gold and silver that the Israelites received from the Egyptians*[^25].

The fifty-fourth: *on what is written, but for me it is good to cling to God*[^26]. Here, what I said—*but that which is better than every soul we call God*[^27]*—*should rather have been said: *better than every created spirit*.

The fifty-fifth: *on what is written, there are sixty queens, eighty concubines, and young maidens without number*[^28].

The fifty-sixth: *on the forty-six years of the building of the Temple*[^29].

The fifty-seventh: *on the one hundred fifty-three fish*[^30].

The fifty-eighth: *on John the Baptist*.

The fifty-ninth: *on the ten virgins*[^31].

The sixtieth: *but of that day and hour no one knows, neither the angels of heaven nor the Son of man, but the Father alone*[^32].

The sixty-first: *on what is written in the Gospel, that the Lord fed the crowds on the mountain with five loaves*[^33]. Here, what I said—*that the two fish signify those two persons, namely the royal and the priestly, to whom that most sacred anointing also pertained*[^34]—should rather have been said: "*pertained* especially," since we read that the prophets too were sometimes anointed[^35]. Likewise, what I said—*Luke, who presented Christ as a priest ascending after the abolition of sins, ascends through Nathan to David, because Nathan was the prophet who had been sent, by whose rebuke David, by doing penance, obtained the abolition of his own sin*[^36]—is not to be taken as though it was the prophet Nathan himself[^37] who was the son of David, because here too it was not said: because he himself, the prophet, had been sent, but it was said: *because Nathan the prophet had been sent*[^38], so that the mystery is understood not in the same man but in the same name.

The sixty-second is *on what is written in the Gospel, that Jesus baptized more than John, although he himself did not baptize but his disciples did*[^39]. Here, what I said—*that thief to whom it was said, amen I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise*[^40]*, who had not received baptism itself*[^41]—this indeed we find that other governors of the holy Church before us also set down in their writings[^42]; but by what proofs it can sufficiently be shown that that thief was not baptized, I do not know. On this matter there has been more careful discussion in certain of our later little works, especially in what we wrote to Vincentius Victor *On the Origin of the Soul*[^43].

The sixty-third: *on the Word.*

The sixty-fourth: *on the Samaritan woman*[^44].

The sixty-fifth: *on the resurrection of Lazarus*[^45].

The sixty-sixth: *on what is written, do you not know, brothers—for I speak to those who know the Law—that the Law has dominion over a person as long as he lives?*[^46] *down to the place where it is written:* *he will give life also to your mortal bodies through his Spirit dwelling in you*[^47]. Here, wishing to expound what the Apostle says—*but we know that the Law is spiritual, but I am carnal*[^48]—I said, *that is, I consent to the flesh, not yet set free by spiritual grace*[^49].

This is not to be taken as though a spiritual man, already established under grace, cannot say this also of himself, and so on down to the place where it was said, *wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?*[^50]—which I learned later, as I have already confessed before[^51]. Again, expounding what the Apostle says—*the body indeed is dead because of sin*[^52]—I said, *he calls the body dead so long as it is such that the lack of temporal things troubles the soul*[^53]. But it seemed to me much better afterward[^54] that the body was called dead because it now has the necessity of dying, which it did not have before sin.

The sixty-seventh: *on what is written, for I reckon that the sufferings of this time are not worthy of the glory to come, which will be revealed in us*[^55] *down to what was said, for in hope we have been saved*[^56]. Here, when I was expounding what is written—*and the creation itself will be freed from the slavery of corruption*[^57]—I said, *and the creation itself, that is, man himself, when, the seal of the image having now been lost on account of sin, he remained merely a creature*[^58]. This is not to be taken as though man lost the whole of what he had of the image of God.

For if he had lost it altogether, there would be no reason for it to be said, *be reformed in the newness of your mind*[^59], and, *we are transformed into the same image*[^60]; but again, if he had lost the whole of it, nothing would remain on account of which it might be said, *though man walks in an image, yet he is troubled in vain*[^61].

Likewise, what I said—*that the highest angels live spiritually, but the lowest live in the manner of the soul*[^62]—was said more boldly about the lowest than can be shown either from the Holy Scriptures or from the things themselves, because even if perhaps it can be, it can only be with the greatest difficulty.

The sixty-eighth: *on what is written, O man, who are you to answer back to God?*[^63] Here I said, *that even if anyone, by lighter sins, or certainly though by graver and many ones, has nevertheless by great groaning and grief of repentance become worthy of God's mercy, this is not from himself, who if he were left would perish, but from the merciful God, who comes to the aid of his prayers and griefs. For it is too little to will, unless God shows mercy; but God, who calls to peace, does not show mercy unless the will has gone before toward peace*[^64]. This was said about repentance. For there is also a mercy of God that goes before the will itself, and if it did not exist, *the will would not be prepared by the Lord*[^65]. To that mercy belongs also the calling itself, which goes even before faith[^66]. When I dealt with this a little later I said[^67]: *but this calling, which works through the opportunities of the times whether in individual human beings or in peoples or in the human race itself, is of a high and deep ordering. To this belongs also that saying, in the womb I sanctified you*[^68]*, and, when you were in the loins of your father I saw you*[^69]*, and, Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*[^70], and so on; although that testimony, *when you were in the loins of your father I saw you,* from where it occurred to me as though it were written, I do not know.

The sixty-ninth: *on what is written, then the Son himself will also be subject to him who subjected all things to him*[^71].

The seventieth: *on what the Apostle says, death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your striving? Where, O death, is your sting? Now the sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the Law*[^72].

The seventy-first: *on what is written, bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^73].

The seventy-second: *on the eternal times.*

The seventy-third: *on what is written, and in habit found as a man*[^74].

The seventy-fourth: *on what is written in the Letter of Paul to the Colossians, in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins, who is the image of the invisible God*[^75].

The seventy-fifth: *on the inheritance of God*.

The seventy-sixth: *on what the Apostle James says, but do you wish to know, O empty man, that faith without works is idle?*[^76]

The seventy-seventh: *on fear, whether it is a sin.*

The seventy-eighth: *on the beauty of images.*

The seventy-ninth: *why did Pharaoh's magicians work certain miracles just as Moses the servant of God did?*[^77]

The eightieth: *against the Apollinarists*.

The eighty-first: *on Lent and Pentecost.*

The eighty-second: *on what is written, for whom the Lord loves he rebukes; and he scourges every son whom he receives*[^78].

The eighty-third: *on marriage*[^79], on what the Lord says: *if anyone dismisses his wife, except for the cause of fornication*[^80].

This work begins thus: *whether the soul is from itself.*
