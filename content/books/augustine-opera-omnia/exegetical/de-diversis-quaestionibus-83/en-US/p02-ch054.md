# On What Is Written, But for Me It Is Good to Cling to God

**54.** Everything that is either is always in the same way or is not. And every soul is better than every body; for everything that gives life is better than that which is given life, and that the body is given life by the soul, not the soul by the body, no one doubts.

But what is not a body and yet is something is either a soul or something better than a soul. For nothing is worse than every body, because, even if one should call it the matter from which the body itself comes to be, rightly, since it lacks all form, it is said to be nothing.

Again, between body and soul nothing is found that is better than body and worse than soul.

For if there were anything intermediate, it would either be given life by the soul, or give life to the soul, or neither; and it would either give life to the body, or be given life by the body, or neither. But whatever is given life by the soul is a body; and if anything gives life to the soul, it is better than the soul.

Again, that by which the body is given life is the soul; what is given life by the body is nothing. But neither—that is, that which needs no life and bestows no life—is either altogether nothing, or something better than both body and soul. But whether there is any such thing in the nature of things is another question.

For now, in the meantime, reason discovers that there is nothing between body and soul which is better than body and worse than soul. But that which is better than every soul, we call God; to whom whoever understands him is joined. For what is understood is true, and not everything that is believed is true.

But whatever is true and is separated from the senses and from the mind can only be believed, yet cannot be sensed or understood. To God, therefore, is joined that which understands God. But the rational soul understands God. For it understands that which is always of the same kind and undergoes no change.

But both the body, through time and places, and the rational soul itself, in that it is at one time wise, at another foolish, undergo change. But what is always in the same way is assuredly better than that which is not so. And nothing is better than the rational soul except God. When, therefore, it understands something that always holds itself in the same way, it understands him without doubt. And this is truth itself. And because the rational soul is joined to it by understanding, and this is the good of the soul, it is rightly taken that this is what was said: *but for me it is good to cling to God*[^1].

[^1]: Ps 73:28 (72:28).
