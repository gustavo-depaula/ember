# On what is written: Then the Son himself — will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him

**69. 1.** Those who contend that the Son of God is not equal to the Father are accustomed to make rather free use of this testimony, where the Apostle says: *but when all things have been subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, that God may be all in all.*

For an error cloaked with the name "Christian" could not arise for them except from Scriptures not understood; for they say: "if he is equal, how will he be subjected?" This is certainly like that gospel question: "if he is equal, how is the Father greater?" For the Lord himself says: *because the Father is greater than I*[^1].

The rule of the Catholic faith stands thus: that when some things are said in Scripture of the Son such that he is less than the Father, they are understood according to his taking on of man, but when those things are said by which he is shown to be equal, they are taken according to that which he is as God. It is apparent, then, how it was said both, *the Father is greater than I*, and, *I and the Father are one*[^2]; and, *the Word was God*, and, *the Word was made flesh*, and, *he did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*, and, *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^3].

But since many things are said of him also according to the property of the person—apart from what pertains to the taking on of man—in such a way that the Father must be understood as nothing other than the Father, and the Son as nothing other than the Son, the heretics think that in these things which are so said and understood there cannot be equality. For it is written: *all things were made through him*[^4], namely through the Son, that is, through the Word of God—by whom, if not by the Father?

But nowhere is it written that the Son made any creature through the Father. Likewise it is written that the Son is the image of the Father[^5]; but nowhere is it written that the Father is the image of the Son;

then, that the one is begetter, the other begotten, and other things of this kind, which pertain not to an inequality of substance but to the property of the persons—in which things they say there cannot be equality, since they bring rather coarse minds to penetrating them, they must be pressed by the weight of authority.

For if equality could not be understood in these things—of him through whom all things were made and of him by whom they were made, of the image and of him whose image it is, of the begotten and of the begetter—the Apostle, closing the mouths of contentious people, would in no way have put down the very word, saying: *he did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*[^6]*.*

**69. 2.** Since, then, the things that are written for the distinction of the Father and the Son are written partly on account of the properties of the persons, partly on account of the taking on of man—while nevertheless the deity and unity and equality of the divine substance of the Father and the Son remains—

it is rightly asked in this place whether the Apostle said according to the properties of the persons or according to the taking on of man: *then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*[^7].

The context of Scripture is accustomed to illuminate a statement, when the things written round about, touching the present question, are treated with diligent discussion. We find, then, that this place was reached in such a way that above he said: *but now Christ has risen from the dead, the firstfruits of those who sleep*[^8]; for he was treating of the resurrection of the dead. This came about in the Lord according to the taking on of man; though most plainly he continues and says: *for since through a man came death, also through a man came the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive. But each in his own order: the beginning, Christ. Then those who are Christ's, at his coming (parousia). Then the end, when he hands over the kingdom to God and the Father, when he has emptied out every rule and every power and might. For he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet. The last enemy to be destroyed is death. For he subjected all things under his feet. But when he says that all things are subjected, it is manifest that this excepts him who subjected all things to him. But when all things have been subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, that God may be all in all*[^9]*.* It is manifest, then, that this was said according to the taking on of man.

**69. 3.** But other things in this chapter, whose whole text I have recalled, are accustomed to present a question: first, that it was said, *when he hands over the kingdom to God and the Father*, as though the Father does not now hold the kingdom.

Then, that it was said, *for he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet*, as though afterward he will not reign, and to this purpose serves what was said above: *then the end.* Which by a sacrilegious opinion they take as though he called the end the consummation of his kingdom, when in the Gospel it is written: *and of his kingdom there will be no end*[^10].

Finally, that it was said, *but when all things have been subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*, they wish to be understood as though now either something is not subjected to the Son, or he himself is not subjected to the Father.

**69. 4.** The question, then, is resolved by the manner of speaking. For Scripture often speaks in such a way that what always is, is said then to come about in someone, when it has begun to be known by him. For thus we say in prayer: *hallowed be your name*[^11], as though it were at some time not holy.

Therefore, just as *hallowed be* means "let it be made known to be holy," so too: *when he hands over the kingdom to God and the Father*, that is: when he has shown the Father to reign, so that what is now believed by the faithful and not thought to be so by unbelievers may grow clear through the manifestation and revealing.

And he will empty out every rule and power precisely by manifesting the kingdom of the Father, so that it may be known to all that none of the princes and powers—whether heavenly or earthly—had anything of rule and power through itself, but from him from whom are all things, not only that they exist, but also that they are ordered.

For in that manifestation no hope will remain to anyone in any prince or in any man. This is even now sung prophetically: *it is good to hope in the Lord rather than to hope in man; it is good to hope in the Lord rather than to hope in princes*[^12], so that by this meditation the soul may now rise up into the kingdom of the Father, neither making much of anyone's power except his, nor most perniciously flattering itself with its own.

He will therefore hand over the kingdom to God and the Father, when through him the Father will be known through manifestation. For his kingdom is those in whom he now reigns through faith. For the kingdom of Christ is spoken of in one way according to the power of his divinity—that all creation is subjected to him—

and in another way his kingdom is called the Church, according to the property of the faith that is in him, according to which he prays who says: *possess us*[^13]. For it is not that he does not himself possess all things; according to which that too is said: *when you were slaves of sin, you were free from righteousness*[^14]. He will therefore empty out every rule and every power and might, so that no one who beholds the Father through the Son may have need or take pleasure in resting in the power of any creature or in his own.

**69. 5.** *For he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet*[^15], that is, his kingdom must be manifested to such a degree until all his enemies confess that he reigns; for this is what is understood by his enemies being under his feet. And if we take this of the just, the word *enemies* was used because from being unjust they are justified and made subject to him by believing. But of the unjust, who do not belong to the future blessedness of the just, it must be taken thus: that they too, confounded by the very manifestation of his kingdom, will confess that he reigns. Therefore, *he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet*, was not said as though, when he has put his enemies under his feet, he will not reign afterward, but: *he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet* means—he must, he says, bring his kingdom to such evidence until his enemies can in no way dare to deny that he reigns.

For it is also written: *our eyes are toward the Lord our God, until he has mercy on us*[^16]; nor for that reason, however, when he has had mercy on us, ought we to turn our eyes from him; for our blessedness is precisely in proportion as we enjoy the contemplation of him. So, then, this too was said. The intention of our eyes is not directed toward the Lord only up to the obtaining of his mercy, so as afterward to be turned away from it, but so as to seek nothing further from it. *Until*, therefore, is put for "so that you understand nothing further."

For to what further—that is, up to what greater manifestation—will the kingdom of Christ be manifested, except until all his enemies confess that he reigns? It is one thing, then, not to be manifested further, another not to remain further. Not to be manifested further is not to become more manifest; not to remain further is not to become more enduring. But when will the kingdom of Christ be more manifest than when it has grown clear to all his enemies?

**69. 6.** *The last enemy to be destroyed is death*[^17]. For there will be nothing else to be destroyed, after this mortal has put on immortality. *For he subjected all things under his feet*: this is, that he may also destroy death. *But when he says that all things are subjected* (the prophet certainly said this in the *Psalms*[^18]), *it is manifest that this excepts him who subjected all things to him*:

he wishes the Father to be understood as having subjected all things to the Son, just as in many places the same Lord commends and proclaims in the Gospel,

not only on account of the form of the servant, but also on account of the principle from which he is and from which he is equal to him from whom he is.

For he loves to refer all things to one principle, as his image[^19], but one in whom dwells all the fullness of divinity[^20].

**69. 7.** *But when all things have been subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*[^21]. Not as though it is now not so, but then it will be manifest, according to the manner of speaking treated above, *that God may be all in all*.

He himself is the end which he recalled above, when he wished first to conclude the whole briefly. Then, as it were, to unfold and expound it piece by piece; for he was speaking of the resurrection where he said: *the beginning, Christ; then those who are Christ's at his coming; then the end.*

He himself, namely, is the end, *that God may be all in all*. For an end that pertains to consummation is spoken of in one way, an end that pertains to consumption in another; for a tunic is finished in one way by weaving, food in another by eating. But God is said to be all in all, so that none of those who cling to him may love his own will against him, and so that it may be manifest to all what the same Apostle says in another place: *but what do you have that you have not received?*[^22]

**69. 8.** There are likewise those who understand this place thus: *he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet*, so as to say that *reign* is here put in another sense, not in the sense in which *kingdom* is put, of which he says: *when he hands over the kingdom to God and the Father*,

so that he called *kingdom* that by which God rules the whole creation, but called *reign* in such a way that it is understood as leading an army against an enemy or defending a city—so that for this reason he said, *he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet*, because there will be no occasion for such a reign as the commanders of armed men have, once the enemy is so subjected that it cannot rebel.

For it was certainly said in the Gospel: *and of his kingdom there will be no end*[^23], according to which he reigns forever; but according to that by which warfare is waged under him against the devil, this warfare will certainly last *until he puts all his enemies under his feet*; but afterward it will not be, when we shall enjoy perpetual peace.

These things, however, have been said so that we may know that this too must be considered more diligently: in what sense the Lord now reigns by the dispensation of his mystery, through the incarnation and passion.

For according to that which he is the Word of God, his kingdom is as much without end as without beginning and without interruption; but according to that which the Word was made flesh[^24], he began to reign in believers through faith in his incarnation. From this comes also that: *the Lord has reigned from the tree*[^25]. And from this he emptied out every rule and every power and might, since it is not through his brightness, but through his humility, that those believing in him are saved.

This is what is hidden from the wise and prudent and revealed to little ones[^26], since it pleased God through the foolishness of preaching[^27] to save those who believe. And the Apostle says that he knows nothing among the little ones except Christ Jesus, and him crucified[^28]. This preaching is needed for as long as it takes until all his enemies are put under his feet—that is, until all worldly pride yields and is made subject to that humility of his, which I judge is signified by the name of feet—as has already for the most part been done, and as we see being done daily. But to what end do these things come about?

That he may hand over the kingdom to God and the Father, that is, that he may lead those nourished by faith in his incarnation to the vision by which he is equal to the Father. For he was already speaking to those who had believed when he said: *if you remain in my word, you are truly my disciples, and you will know the truth, and the truth will free you*[^29].

For he will hand over the kingdom to the Father when he reigns in those contemplating the truth by that whereby he is equal to the Father, and through himself, the Only-begotten, will make the Father seen through the vision; for now he reigns in believers through that whereby *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^30].

But then he will hand over the kingdom to God and the Father, *when he has emptied out every rule and power and might*. By what will he empty it out except by humility, patience, and weakness?

For what rule is not emptied out, when the Son of God reigns in believers for this reason: that the princes of the world judged him?

What power is not emptied out, when he through whom all things were made reigns in believers for this reason: that he was so subjected to the powers that he said to a man, *you would have no power over me unless it had been given to you from above*[^31]?

What might is not emptied out, when he himself, through whom the heavens were made firm, reigns in believers for this reason: that he was made weak even unto the cross and death? But in this way the Son properly reigns in the faith of believers. For the Father cannot be said or believed to be either incarnate or judged or crucified.

But through the vision by which he is equal to the Father, he reigns with the Father in those contemplating the truth.

And that he will hand over the kingdom to God and the Father, leading those who now believe in him from faith in his incarnation to the vision of his deity, he will not himself lose, but each will offer himself as one for the enjoyment of those who contemplate.

But for as long as it is needed, in people not yet able to behold the equality of the Father and the Son with the clear light of the mind, Christ reigns through that which such people can grasp, and through what he himself properly took on—that is, the humility of the incarnation—*until he puts all his enemies under his feet*, that is, until all worldly pride is made subject to the humility of his incarnation.

**69. 10.** Of course, what was said—*then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*[^32]—although it is said according to the taking on of man, since the question arose from that, while he was treating of the resurrection of the dead, nevertheless it is rightly asked whether it was said only according to him who is the head of the Church[^33], or according to the whole Christ, his body and members being counted in.

For when he says to the Galatians: *it does not say, 'and to seeds,' as in many, but as in one, 'and to your seed,' which is Christ*, lest in this place we should understand only that Christ who was born of the Virgin Mary, he afterward says: *for you are all one in Christ Jesus. But if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed*[^34].

And to the Corinthians, when he spoke of love, drawing a comparison from the members of the body: *for as the body is one*, he says, *and has many members, but all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body, so also is Christ*[^35]; he did not say, "so also are Christ's members," but, *so also is Christ*, showing that the whole is rightly called Christ, that is, the head together with his body, which is the Church.

And in many places of the Scriptures we find Christ called even in this way, so that he is understood together with all his members, to whom it was said: *you are the body of Christ and its members*[^36]. Not absurdly, then, do we understand thus: *then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*, so that by the Son we understand not only the head of the Church, but all the saints together with him, who are one in Christ, one seed of Abraham—but subjected according to the contemplation of eternal truth, for the obtaining of blessedness, with no movement of the mind, no part of the body resisting, *so that* in that life, no one loving his own power, *God may be all in all*.
