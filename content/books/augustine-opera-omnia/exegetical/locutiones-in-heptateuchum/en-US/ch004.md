# Book III: Expressions on Leviticus

**1.** (1:2) *If a man of you offers gifts to the Lord from the cattle, from the oxen and from the sheep you shall offer*; that is, if you offer from the cattle, you shall offer from the oxen, from the sheep. By the name of sheep it included also the kind of goats, as it does in other places too.

**2.** (2:6) *And you shall break them into fragments*; that is, by breaking you shall make them fragments.

**3.** (4:23) *And the sin which he sinned in it become known to him*. Two manners of expression are here to be noted: both *he sinned a sin*, and what it added: *in it*. For in what but in the very thing, that is, the sin?

**4.** (5:1) *But if a soul sins, and hears the voice of one swearing, and is himself a witness, or has seen, or has been aware, if he does not report it, and he shall take on sin;* the word *and* seems superfluous; for with it removed there follows complete: *he shall take on sin*.

**5.** (5:1) *But if a soul sins, and hears the voice of one swearing, and is himself a witness, or has seen, or has been aware, does not report it*; *and* is put for "that is"; for it could be said thus by the custom of our expression: "*but if a soul sins*, that is, *hears the voice of one swearing*," and the rest.

**6.** (5:3) *Or touches anything from the uncleanness of a man, from all his uncleanness, by which if he touches it he is defiled, and it was hidden from him, but after this he comes to know it and has transgressed*; whereas it should have been said in the right order: *and has transgressed, but after this comes to know it*.

**7.** (5:15-16) *A soul, if it be hidden from him by forgetfulness and he sins unwillingly.* It did not say: "if it be hidden from her," since it puts *soul* for "man," as it does in other places, where it first said *soul*, which is of feminine gender, and afterward brings in the masculine gender, referring to the man.

But here this expression is much more compressed, since the two genders are placed in immediate sequence, so that it is said: *a soul if it be hidden from him*. This expression frightened the Latin translators and they did not wish to render it,

but they put it thus: *a soul, if any be hidden and sin unwillingly*, whereas it is certainly one thing if a soul be hidden, another if something be hidden from a soul; but this is what Scripture says: *if it be hidden from her*, not: "if she herself be hidden."

But in another Greek copy we found: *a soul, if it be hidden from her by forgetfulness*; but even it in what follows brought in the masculine, saying: *the priest shall make entreaty for him, and it shall be forgiven him*, which there has αὐτῷ. Whence it appears that the writer feared a solecism, yet could not persevere in the feminine gender, overcome by the evidence of the following Scripture.

**8.** (6:9) *This is the burnt offering upon its burning upon the altar all the night until morning, and the fire of the altar shall burn upon it*; it could lack *and*, and so be said: "*all the night until morning the fire of the altar shall burn*." But this conjunction, when added, makes obscurity for those who are not accustomed to such expressions of the Scriptures.

**9.** (6:14) *This is the law of the sacrifice, which the sons of Aaron the priests shall offer it before the Lord*; nothing would be lacking if it did not have: *it*.

**10.** (6:17) *It is the holy of holies* (singular). So the Greek has it; but it is a Greek expression, which certain of ours, unwilling to render, said: *they are the holy of holies* (plural).

**11.** (6:32) *They shall slay the ram which for the offense before the Lord*. The Latin translators added: *is*, and said: *which is for the offense*, which the Greek does not have.

**12.** (7:16) *And if he sacrifices his gift as a vow or a freewill offering, on whatever day he offers the sacrifice, it shall be eaten on the morrow*. In place of this: "on the next day"; whence others rendered: *on the other day*.

**13.** (8:31) *Cook the flesh in the court of the tabernacle of testimony in the holy place, and there you shall eat it, and the loaves that are in the basket of consecration, as it was commanded me, saying: "Aaron and his sons shall eat it."* This expression certain ones, unwilling to render, said: *as he commanded me, saying*; because this seems consistent, but the former is a solecism in the custom of our expression.

**14.** (8:35) *And at the door of the tabernacle of testimony you shall sit seven days, day and night*; in place of this: "you shall dwell."

**15.** (9:7) *And Moses said to Aaron: "approach the altar, and do what is for your sin, and the burnt offering; and make entreaty for yourself and your house, and make the gifts of the people, and make entreaty for them as the Lord commanded Moses."* It did not say: *as the Lord commanded me*; but it spoke as though there had been one Moses whom the Lord commanded, and this other one who was speaking this to Aaron.

**16.** (10:8-9) *And the Lord spoke to Aaron, saying: "wine and strong drink you shall not drink,"* and the rest. Which words of the Lord it concludes thus: *all the ordinances that the Lord spoke to them by the hand of Moses*; whereas the Lord was speaking, it did not say: "which I spoke to them by the hand of Moses," but used that expression which Moses himself used above.

**17.** (10:9) *An eternal ordinance throughout your generations, to distinguish between the midst of the clean and of the defiled*, and the rest. It is to be noted in what way it says *eternal*, which certainly will not be without end.

**18.** (10:14) Moses, speaking to Aaron, Eleazar, and Ithamar his sons, among other things says also this: *from the sacrifices of the peace offerings of the children of Israel they shall offer the arm of the offering and the little breast of the setting-apart upon the offerings of the fat, to set apart a setting-apart before God. And it shall be for you and for your sons and for your daughters with you an eternal ordinance*; though all this was to have an end.

**19.** (11:9) When it was giving commands concerning the living creatures that are in the waters, which of them are clean or unclean: *in the waters*, it says, *and in the sea, and in the streams*.

But the Latin manuscripts have *in the sea* (singular), because it was too unusual to render the plural number from the Greek and to say not *in the sea* but *in the seas*, especially on account of the ambiguity, lest not "seas" but "males" be understood, that is, the masculine sex;

for it is no less unusual—what nevertheless was rendered—in the case of "bloods," because the Latin language does not admit a plural number of "blood," even in the nominative case itself. For although from the word "sea" (mare) no one says *maribus*, yet *maria* ("seas") are spoken of, whereas "bloods" are not spoken of, and yet it is written: *deliver me from bloods*[^1]; and: *I will not gather their assemblies from bloods*[^2].

Therefore *maribus* too could be said thus, were it not, as I said, that the ambiguity was avoided. But *streams* in this place Scripture put for "rivers," although *streams* are properly winter torrents that dry up in summer, and therefore cannot have fish. Whence some of our translators preferred to render not "streams" but "rivers." But that "streams" is put in the Scriptures for "rivers" that passage of the *Psalm* sufficiently shows where it is read: *and you will give them to drink of the stream of your delight*. For it did not wish anything to be understood by the name of stream that would flow for a time and then dry up, since there follows and it says: *for with you is the fountain of life*[^3]; which is certainly eternal and unfailing.

**20.** (11:21) *But these you shall eat of the creeping flying things that walk upon four feet, which have upper legs of its feet*; it did not say: "of its feet" (plural).

**21.** (11:44) *And you shall be holy, because holy I*; "am" is supplied, whence most of ours rendered: *because I am holy*.

**22.** (12:1) *And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying*. This is a most customary and most frequent expression in the Scriptures: *he spoke, saying*. But the one that follows is more rarely found, and it constrains the poverty of Latin speech; for the Greek has: καὶ ἐρεῖς πρὸς αὐτοὺς λέγων, which could be expressed in Latin, "and you shall say to them, saying";

but it offends less if it is said, "and you shall speak to them, saying"; and it is more like the Greek, because that too did not say λέξεις πρὸς αὐτοὺς λέγων, but ἐρεῖς πρὸς αὐτοὺς λέγων.

**23.** (12:2) *Whatever woman receives seed and bears a male, and she shall be unclean seven days*; most of ours were unwilling to render it, but said thus: *whatever woman receives seed and bears a male, she shall be unclean seven days*. That expression, then, since it is unusual even in Greek speech, could also not be rendered in the Greek; but since it did not irk the Greeks to render it, why it should have irked the Latins, I do not know.

**24.** (12:4) *Thirty-three days she shall sit in her clean blood*. Similarly of her who bore a female it is said: *she shall sit in her clean blood*, but with the same days doubled, that is, sixty-six days. *She shall sit*, then, is said for "she shall remain," for it was not the case that for so many days she was not allowed to rise from her seat.

**25.** (13:2) *If on any man there is made in the skin of his body a bright scar of a sign*. It is to be noted that Scripture calls a "scar" not the mark of a "wound," but even the mark of color alone.

**26.** (13:2) *And there is in the skin of his color a touch of leprosy*; it calls the spot itself the *touch*, because the man himself is touched by leprosy.

**27.** (13:3) *And the priest shall see him, and shall defile him*; in place of this: "shall pronounce him defiled."

**28.** (13:3) *And let the hair that is in the touch be turned white*; that is: "be turned into white."

**29.** (13:6) *And the priest shall cleanse him; for it is a sign;* he shall pronounce him cleansed, just as: *he shall defile*, which it said above, "he shall pronounce defiled."

**30.** (13:7-8) *And the priest shall see him, and behold the appearance is changed in the skin, and the priest shall defile him*; it has the extra *and*; for with that conjunction removed the sense is complete in this way: *but if the appearance is changed in the skin, after the priest saw him, in order to cleanse him, and he is seen again by the priest, and the priest sees him, and behold the appearance is changed in the skin, the priest shall defile him*.

**31.** (13:9-10) *And if a touch of leprosy is on a man, he shall come to the priest, and the priest shall see, and behold a white scar in the skin, and this has changed the hair white, and from the sound of the living flesh in the scar*; that is, it has changed the hair into a white color in the scar, *but it has changed it from the sound of the living flesh*, that is, because it does not have such hair as is sound in living flesh.

**32.** (13:45-46) Speaking of the leper, it says: *and unclean unclean he shall be called*, as though it were not enough to say once: *he shall be called unclean*. Although some manuscripts have it so, in another Greek copy we found it said once: *he shall be called unclean*. Likewise a little after it says: *since he is unclean, he shall be unclean*;

which could not be expressed into Latin from the Greek as it stands; for the Greek says ἀκάθαρτος ὢν ἀκάθαρτος ἔσται as if it said: "being unclean, he shall be unclean"; but "being" (existens) is not what the Greek says, ὤν, but, if it could be said, "essing" (essens), from the verb "to be" (esse), not from "to exist" (existere).

**33.** (13:47) *And a garment if there is in it a touch of leprosy*; it could say customarily thus: "and in a garment if there is a touch of leprosy."

**34.** (13:51) *Or in any vessel of leather, in whatever there is in it a touch*; it could be enough: *in whatever there is a touch*.

**35.** (13:55) *And behold the touch has not changed its appearance*; that is, the color in which it is seen; for not the sight by which it itself sees, since by *touch* it means the spot itself.

**36.** (14:15) *And the priest taking of the pint of oil, shall pour it upon the left hand of the priest*; it did not say: "upon his own left hand," although he certainly does it onto his own.

**37.** (15:2) *To any man, any man who has a discharge*.

**38.** (15:16) *And any man from whom goes out the lying-down of seed*.

**39.** (15:21) *And everything upon whatever he lies upon it.*

**40.** (15:21) *And everything upon which he sits upon it, shall be unclean*.

**41.** (16:21) *And he shall send it out by the hand of a man made ready into the wilderness*, that is: "he shall send it out into the wilderness by the hand of a man made ready for this"; he was speaking of the scapegoat when he said this: but it is to be noted in what way Scripture says: *by the hand*.

**42.** (17:3) *A man, a man of the children of Israel*; that is: "of the children of Israel"; but this repetition seems to signify any man whatever, that is, this one or that one.

**43.** (18:7) *The shame of your father and the shame of your mother you shall not uncover*; by this expression it forbade intercourse with these persons.

**44.** (18:14) *The shame of your father's brother you shall not uncover, and to his wife you shall not go in; for she is your kinswoman*; it put *and* for "that is"; for it calls this the shame of his father's brother, that is, the shame of the paternal uncle, the private parts of the uncle's wife.

**45.** (18:25) *And the land abhorred those who settle upon it*; that is: have their seats, which is: "dwell."

**46.** (19:9) *And when you (plural) reap the harvest of your land, you (singular) shall not finish reaping your field*. This—that it began from the plural and ended with the singular number—most Latin were unwilling to render, but said: *of your (plural) field*, where it was said: *of your (singular) field*, as though the Greek could not say this; therefore the expression was to be noted rather than emended.

**47.** (20:17) *Whoever takes his sister from his father or from his mother, and sees her shame, and she sees his shame, he has uncovered the shame of his sister, they shall take on their sin*; it put *sin* for "the penalty of the sin."

**48.** (20:25) *And you shall separate yourselves between the midst of clean animals and the midst of unclean animals, and the midst of clean and unclean birds. You shall separate yourselves*, it said, *between the midst of the clean and the unclean*, since the clean are separated from the unclean, or the unclean from the clean; it is an altogether novel expression. For it is one thing: "you shall separate between the midst of clean animals and the midst of unclean animals," as it most often is accustomed to speak; another is what it now says: *you shall separate yourselves*, as though, by separating those things from one another, they separate themselves from both, as though judging between the two.

**49.** (21:1) *Speak to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and you shall say to them: "in souls they shall not be defiled among their people, except in a kinsman who is nearest to them"*; it speaks of the mourning that is owed to the souls of the deceased; for one mourns because they have departed.

**50.** (21:5) *And you shall not shave a baldness over the dead, and over their flesh they shall not cut cuttings*; the customary expression would be: "they shall not cut their flesh with cuttings."

**51.** (21:7-8) *A harlot and profane woman they shall not take, nor a woman cast out by her husband; because he is holy to the Lord his God*; it did not say: "because they are holy," but as though it spoke of one, though it began from the plural. Then it follows in the singular: *he has sanctified him, the gifts of the Lord your God this one shall offer; he is holy, because holy am I the Lord who sanctify them*; again it concluded with the plural.

**52.** (22:11) *But if a priest possesses a soul bought with money, this one shall eat of his loaves*; it did not say: this one (feminine) shall eat, although it said *soul*, which is of feminine gender; but it rather looked to that which it wished to signify by *soul*, that is, the man.

**53.** (22:12) *And the daughter of a priest, a man, if she is to a foreign man*; that is: "if she marries a foreign man."

**54.** (22:26-27) *And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying: "a calf or a sheep or a goat, when it is born, and it shall be seven days under its mother"*; it seems to have the extra *and*, according to the most customary expressions in the Scriptures, which most Latin were unwilling to render.

**55.** (22:32) *And I will be sanctified in the midst of the children of Israel*; which is: "I will be held holy"; for the Lord is not without holiness even apart from the children of Israel. In that sense it is said also in the Lord's Prayer: *hallowed be your name*[^4], that is: "let it be held holy by men."

**56.** (23:2) *Speak to the children of Israel, and you shall say to them: "the feasts of the Lord which you shall call called holy, these are my feasts; six days you shall do works"*; though it said it was to be spoken to many, it afterward speaks as though to one.

**57.** (23:15-16) *And you (plural) shall count for yourselves from the day after the Sabbath on which he offered the sheaf of the laying-on, seven full weeks you (singular) shall count*; it did not say: "you (plural) shall count," although it was speaking above to many.

**58.** (24:11) *And when the son of the Israelite woman had named, he cursed the name*; although it did not add here "of God," it is nevertheless clear that the name of God is to be understood, which he cursed.

**59.** (24:15) *A man, a man if he curses God, shall take on his own sin*. See where it sufficiently appears to be a matter of expression, when it is said: *a man, a man*, as if it said: "this man or that one," that is, any man whatever; not as some have supposed, that *a man, a man* is said as though one were named praiseworthily, as if it were said: "a man, but a man"; that is, not just any sort like the beasts, but one who is truly a man. That this sense is not the true one, but that this is a matter of expression of the Scriptures, is here openly shown, since it is said of the one who is blamed, not of the one who is praised.

**60.** (25:46) When it was treating of the slaves whom it was permitted to the Israelites to have: *and they shall be for you*, it says, *as a possession forever*; although they certainly could not, being mortal, be either masters or slaves eternally; it said *forever*, then, where there is no fixed measure of time how long they should serve, as there is set for those whom in the year of remission it commands to be released into their freedom.

**61.** (26:3) *If you walk in my precepts, and keep my commandments, and do them, and I will give you rain in its season*; the *and* is superfluous to the custom of our expression, but added in the manner of the Scriptures; for there followed: *I will give you*.

**62.** (26:18) *And if even still you do not obey me, and I will go on to chastise you seven times for your sins*; here too the *and* is superfluous, for there followed: *I will go on to chastise you*; and what it says, *seven times*, is to be taken for any number whatever.

[^2]: Ps 16:4 (15:4)
[^3]: Ps 36:8–9 (35:9–10)
[^4]: Matt 6:9
