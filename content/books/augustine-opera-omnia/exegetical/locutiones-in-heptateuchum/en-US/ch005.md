# Book IV: Expressions on Numbers

**1.** (1, 4) *Et vobiscum erunt unusquisque secundum caput uniuscuiusque principum* (and they shall be with you, each one according to the head of each one of the leaders).

**2.** (1, 22. 20) *Filiis Symeon secundum propinquitates eorum, secundum populos eorum, secundum domos familiarum eorum, secundum numerum nominum eorum, secundum caput eorum, omnia masculina a viginti annis et supra, omnis, qui procedit in virtute, recognitio eorum* (to the sons of Simeon, according to their kindreds, according to their peoples, according to the houses of their families, according to the number of their names, according to their head, all the males from twenty years old and upward, everyone who goes forth in the host, their census). This expression—that it does not say "the sons of Simeon" or "of the sons of Simeon," but *to the sons of Simeon*, in the dative case, and it keeps this thereafter in the remaining tribes—was not rendered this way by the Latin translators that we have been able to examine. In the tribe of Reuben alone, which is counted first, this expression does not occur; for it does not say "to the sons of Reuben, according to their kindreds," and so on, but says *and the sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel were, according to their kindreds*, and then likewise as in the others.

**3.** (1, 22. 54) *Et fecerunt filii Israel secundum omnia, quae mandavit Dominus Moysi et Aaron, ita fecerunt* (and the sons of Israel did according to all that the Lord commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did).

**4.** (3, 3) *Sacerdotes qui uncti sunt quorum consummaverunt manus eorum sacerdotio fungi* (the priests who were anointed, whose hands they consecrated to perform the priestly office).

**5.** (4, 14) *Et imponent super illud omnia vasa eius, quibus ministrant in ipsis* (and they shall place upon it all its vessels, with which they minister therein).

**6.** (5, 7) *Et reddet cui deliquit ei* (and he shall restore to whom he wronged, to him). Some translators, unwilling to render this expression as it stands, corrected the order and translated *et reddet ei cui deliquit* (and he shall restore to him whom he wronged).

**7.** (5, 6) *Vir aut mulier quicumque fecerit ab omnibus peccatis humanis* (a man or a woman, whoever [masculine] shall have committed any of human sins). It does not say "whoever [feminine] shall have committed"; rather it is an expression in which the masculine gender claims the pronoun, even though the feminine is placed afterward—an expression that is not customary.

**8.** (5, 12) *Viri, viri si praevaricata fuerit uxor eius* (of a man, of a man, if his wife shall have transgressed). The expression is to be noted not only because it doubled the noun, saying *viri, viri* (of a man, of a man), but also because it added *eius* (his).

**9.** (5, 14) *Si superveniet illi spiritus zelandi. Illa autem non fuerit inquinata, et adducet homo uxorem suam ad sacerdotem* (if a spirit of jealousy shall come upon him. But she shall not have been defiled, and the man shall bring his wife to the priest). The word *et* (and) is superfluous, but it is an expression customary to the Scriptures.

**10.** (5, 18) *Erit aqua argutionis quae maledicitur haec* (it shall be the water of reproof which is accursed, this). This expression was made unusual by the order of the words; for it could be said in the customary way thus: "this shall be the water of reproof which is accursed," or "this water of reproof shall be that which is accursed," or in any other way that ordinary words can be arranged.

**11.** (5, 19) *Innocens esto ab aqua argutionis quae maledicitur haec* (be innocent of the water of reproof which is accursed, this). In this expression not only does the word order sound unusual, but the case is also changed; for it would be complete and customary to say "be innocent of this water of reproof which is accursed," or "of this water of reproof, which is accursed."

**12.** (5, 27) *Et inflabitur ventrem* (and she shall swell as to the belly), for "she shall swell in the belly." This expression is frequent even among Latin authors; but our translators, who were unwilling to render it, said *et inflabitur venter eius* (and her belly shall swell).

**13.** (6, 2) *Vir vel mulier quicumque magne voverit votum* (a man or a woman, whoever [masculine] shall greatly vow a vow): it did not say "whoever [feminine]."

**14.** (6, 9) *Et radetur caput suum* (and he shall shave his head [accusative]): such an expression as the one above, *inflabitur ventrem* (she shall swell as to the belly).

**15.** (7, 3) *Sed vehicula tecta et duodecim boves, vehiculum a duobus principibus et vitulum a singulis* (but covered wagons and twelve oxen, a wagon from two leaders and a calf from each one). In this expression I thought it worth noting that he calls "calves" the same ones he had called *boves* (oxen); but this too is reported to be a Greek idiom.

**16.** (7, 11) *Princeps unus quotidie, princeps quotidie offerent dona sua* (one leader each day, a leader each day, they shall offer their gifts), instead of saying "the leaders one by one on single days."

**17.** (7, 15) *Vitulum unum pro bobus et hircum ex capris unum* (one calf for the oxen and one he-goat from the goats).

**18.** (8, 19) *Et non erit in filiis Israel accedens filiorum Israel ad sancta* (and there shall not be among the sons of Israel any of the sons of Israel approaching the holy things). It seems it could have sufficed to say "and there shall not be among the sons of Israel anyone approaching the holy things," or at least "there shall not be any of the sons of Israel approaching the holy things."

**19.** (8, 20) *Et fecit Moyses et Aaron et omnis synagoga filiorum Israel Levitis; secundum quae praecepit Dominus Moysi de Levitis, ita fecerunt eis filii Israel* (and Moses and Aaron and the whole assembly of the sons of Israel did to the Levites; according to what the Lord commanded Moses concerning the Levites, so the sons of Israel did to them).

**20.** (9, 13) *Et homo, homo quicumque mundus fuerit et in viam longinquam non est et defuerit facere Pascha. Exterminabitur anima illa de populo suo.* (and a man, a man, whoever shall be clean and is not on a far journey and shall have failed to keep the Passover, that soul shall be cut off from his people.) Therefore *homo*, *homo* (a man, a man) is an expression as if it said "whoever, a man," this one or that one.

**21.** (9, 17) *Et cum ascendisset nubes a tabernaculo, et postea promovebant filii Israel* (and when the cloud went up from the tabernacle, and afterward the sons of Israel would set out). It could have been complete: "and when the cloud went up from the tabernacle, the sons of Israel would set out."

**22.** (10, 14) *Et promovebant ordo castrorum filiorum Iuda primi* (and the rank of the camp of the sons of Judah set out first).

**23.** (10, 17) *Et promovebunt filii Gerson et filii Merari* (and the sons of Gershon and the sons of Merari shall set out). He used a verb in the future tense, though he is narrating a past event, as in the earlier passages when he was speaking of the cloud[^1], at whose motion or halt the camp would set out or stay; and so thereafter, in the many instances of setting out, he uses this tense.

**24.** (10, 29) *Et dixit Moyses Hobet filio Raguel Madianitae genero Moysi* (and Moses said to Hobab the son of Raguel the Midianite, the kinsman of Moses): it did not say "his kinsman."

**25.** (10, 30) *Et dixit ad eum: 'non ibo, sed ad terram meam et ad progeniem meam*' (and he said to him: "I will not go, but to my own land and to my own offspring"): "I will go" is understood.

**26.** (11, 4) *Et promiscuus qui erat in eis concupiverunt concupiscentiam* (and the mixed multitude that was among them lusted a lust). He used the singular for the plural, saying *promiscuus* (the mixed multitude, singular), not "promiscui" (plural); and yet he rendered it as plural when he said *concupiverunt* (they lusted).

**27.** (11, 6) *Nunc autem anima nostra arida, nihil praeter in manna oculi nostri* (but now our soul is dry, our eyes are on nothing but the manna). The words "is" and "are" are missing; for it would be complete: "our soul is dry, our eyes are on nothing but the manna." For some translators rendered it thus and added words that are not in the Greek.

**28.** (11, 8) *Et molebant illud in mola* (and they would grind it in a mill).

**29.** (11, 21) *Sescenta millia peditum in quibus sum in eis* (six hundred thousand footmen, among whom I am, among them).

**30.** (11, 25) *Et abstulit de spiritu qui super ipsum* (and he took of the spirit that was upon him). Either "was" or "shall be" is understood. But the Latin translators were reluctant to render this expression, which is called ellipsis—most familiar to Greek diction and, as I think, to Hebrew as well—even though it is frequent in the Latin language too, though less than in Greek.

**31.** (11, 33) *Et percussit Dominus populum plagam magnam valde* (and the Lord struck the people a very great plague). The expression was to be noted: *percussit plagam* (struck a plague, accusative), not "percussit plaga" (struck with a plague, ablative), which is the customary form.

**32.** (12, 3) *Et homo Moyses lenis valde* (and the man Moses very meek): "was" is understood.

**33.** (12, 9) *Et ira animationis Domini super eos* (and the wrath of the Lord's anger upon them): "came to be" is understood.

**34.** (12, 10) *Et ecce Maria leprosa sicut nix* (and behold, Miriam leprous like snow); and here too "became" is understood.

**35.** (13, 20) *Et quae terra in qua isti insident super eam; et quae civitates in quibus isti habitant in ipsis* (and what the land is in which these dwell upon it; and what the cities are in which these live in them).

**36.** (13, 25) *Locum ipsum quem nominaverunt vallem Botrui* (that very place which they named the Valley of the Cluster), although he had said above *venerunt in vallem Botrui* (they came into the Valley of the Cluster). It is said, then, by anticipation, not because it was already so called when they came, but because, when this book was being written, it was already so called.

**37.** (13, 32) *Quoniam fortior nobis est magis* (for it is stronger than we, more). The expression would be customary if it did not have *magis* (more).

**38.** (13, 33) *Et protulerunt pavorem terrae quam exploraverunt eam* (and they brought forth a dread of the land which they explored it).

**39.** (13, 33) *Terram quam transivimus eam explorare, terra comedens qui habitant super eam est* (the land which we passed through it to explore, a land devouring those who live upon it it is). But it should have been said in the customary way: "the land which we passed through to explore."

**40.** (14, 7) *Terra quam exploravimus eam bona est valde valde* (the land which we explored it is very, very good).

**41.** (14, 10) *Et ait omnis synagoga lapidare eos in lapidibus* (and the whole assembly said to stone them with stones). It was not said "with stones [ablative without preposition]," since *lapidare* (to stone) by itself could have sufficed in the customary expression.

**42.** (14, 11) *Et quousque non credunt mihi in omnibus signis quibus feci in ipsis?* (and how long do they not believe me in all the signs which I did among them?): it did not say "which I did among them [with the relative in another case]."

**43.** (14, 24) *Puer autem meus Chaleb, quoniam spiritus alius in eo et assecutus est me, et inducam eum in terram* (but my servant Caleb, because another spirit is in him and he has followed me, and I will bring him into the land). It would be customary if it did not have *et* (and).

**44.** (14, 24) *Et inducam eum in terram, in quam intravit illuc* (and I will bring him into the land, into which he entered thither). It would suffice in the customary expression: "into which he entered."

**45.** (14, 26) *Et ait Dominus ad Moysen et Aaron dicens* (and the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, saying). This was introduced although the Lord was speaking in the passage above as well. Scripture often does this: when it wishes to say something further, it again introduces as speaking the one who was already speaking.

**46.** (14, 29) *Quotquot murmuraverunt super me* (as many as murmured upon me): it did not say "about me" or "against me."

**47.** (14, 31) *Et hereditate possidebunt terram, quam vos abscessistis ab ea* (and by inheritance they shall possess the land, which you departed from it). The customary form would be "from which you departed"; but as it stands, both *quam abscessistis* (which you departed) is said in an uncustomary way, and *ab ea* (from it) is added, as the Scriptures are accustomed to speak.

It is like the line *penitusque sonantes accestis scopulos* (and you drew near the deep-resounding crags)[^2], that is, "you approached the crags," he did not say "to the crags"; and *devenere locos* (they came down to the places)[^3], he did not say "to the places." So too *quam abscessistis* (which you departed), he did not say "from which you departed."

**48.** (14, 35) *Ego Dominus locutus sum: 'nisi ita fecero synagogae malignae isti*.' (I the Lord have spoken: "unless I shall do thus to this wicked assembly.") It must indeed be asked what it is that in Greek is placed ἦ μήν; for our translators have almost everywhere rendered this as *nisi* (unless).

**49.** (15, 18) *In terram in quam ego induco vos illuc* (into the land into which I am bringing you thither).

**50.** (15, 27-28) *Si autem anima una peccaverit non sponte, offeret capram unam anniculam pro peccato. Et exorabit sacerdos pro anima invita et quae peccaverit nolenter ante Dominum exorare pro eo; et remittetur ei* (but if one soul shall sin unwillingly, it shall offer one she-goat a year old for the sin. And the priest shall make atonement for the unwilling soul and the one who shall have sinned unwillingly before the Lord, to atone for him; and it shall be forgiven him). In this sentence both that expression was to be noted, that it says *et exorabit sacerdos* (and the priest shall make atonement) and afterward said *exorare* (to atone)—for both could be said together thus: "shall make atonement to atone"—and that other one, that, beginning from the feminine gender, it closed with the masculine.

For when it had said *si anima una peccaverit* (if one soul shall sin), it concluded by saying *exorare pro eo* (to atone for him); where "for that man" is understood. For also that which it added, *et dimittetur ei* (and it shall be forgiven him), in the Latin indeed does not show, because this pronoun is of every gender; but in the Greek the masculine shines out, where it has αὐτῷ, which is here placed *ei* (to him).

But what is here said, *pro anima invita* (for the unwilling soul), is understood as "which shall have sinned unwillingly," not that it is unwilling to have an offering made for it. For he explained this as a consequence too when he said *et quae peccaverit nolenter* (and the one who shall have sinned unwillingly).

And *invita* (unwilling) is set down for this reason, because what was said in Greek could not be expressed in Latin: ἀκουσιασθείσης; for one could not say "having been made unwilling," or anything else from the fact that one acted unwillingly.

**51.** (15, 30) *Et anima quaecumque fecerit in manu superbiae* (and whatever soul shall act in the hand of pride): "sin" is understood. It is also to be noted that it says *manu superbiae* (the hand of pride), putting "hand" for "deed" or "power."

**52.** (15, 35) *Morte moriatur homo; lapidate eum lapidibus omnis synagoga.* (let the man die the death; stone him with stones, the whole assembly.) Both *morte moriatur* (let him die the death) and *lapidate lapidibus* (stone with stones) and *lapidate omnis synagoga* (stone, the whole assembly) are expressions unusual to us.

**53.** (16, 17) *Et accipite singuli turibulum suum* (and take, each one, his own censer).

**54.** (16, 33) *Et descenderunt ipsi et omnia quae sunt eis* (and they went down, they themselves and all that are theirs). He put the present for the past, *sunt* (are) for "were" or "had been."

**55.** (16, 34) *Et omnis Israel qui in circuitu eorum fugerunt a voce eorum* (and all Israel who round about them fled at their voice): "was" is understood; that is, "all Israel who was round about them."

**56.** (16, 37) *Quoniam sanctificaverunt turibula peccatorum horum in animabus suis* (because they sanctified the censers of these sinners at the cost of their souls). It did not say "because these sinners sanctified the censers at the cost of their souls"; but, as if it were being asked "whose censers," instead of saying "their own," it said *peccatorum horum* (of these sinners), and left it to be understood who had sanctified them, namely that they themselves had.

**57.** (17, 8) *Et ecce germinavit virga in domum Levi;* (and behold, the rod budded into the house of Levi;) Latin usage would require it to be said "in the house of Levi [ablative]."

**58.** (18, 6) *Et ego accepi fratres vestros Levitas de medio filiorum Israel dationem datam Domino* (and I have taken your brothers the Levites from the midst of the sons of Israel, a giving given to the Lord). This expression has been rendered into Latin thus out of necessity; for the Greek has δόμα δεδομένον. And if anyone should wish to express it in Latin, he will say "a given thing given," because δόμα too means a given thing, according to what the Apostle says: *non quia quaero datum, sed requiro fructum*[^4] (not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit), which in the Greek is written δόμα.

And in *Genesis*: *dedit Abraham data filiis suis*[^5] (Abraham gave gifts to his sons), the Greek has δόματα; and in the Gospel: *nostis bona data dare filiis vestris*[^6] (you know how to give good gifts to your children), in the Greek there is δόματα, and δεδομένον is "a given thing," not a noun but a participle. And on this account "a given thing given" could be said, as a given thing that is given, were it not that the ambiguity must be avoided.

Some of our translators, trying to avoid this ambiguity, said "a gift given"; but "gift" in Greek is called δῶρον, not δόμα. Hence in this place it would not be said rightly, because God does not say that this has been given to him as a gift by the sons of Israel, but rendered to him in place of the firstborn owed to him; hence both what is rendered and what is "given as gifts" can be called given things. And therefore every gift is also a given thing, but not every given thing is also a gift, because not everything that is given is at once also given as a gift.

**59.** (18, 12) *Omnis primitia olei et omnis primitia vini et tritici primitia eorum quaecumque dederint Domino tibi dedi ea* (every firstfruit of oil and every firstfruit of wine and the firstfruit of their wheat, whatever they shall give to the Lord, to you I have given them). It did not say "every firstfruit, to you I have given it"; but, when it had said *omnis primitia* (every firstfruit) and enumerated of what things it consisted, it added *tibi dedi ea* (to you I have given them [neuter plural]), not even "to you I have given it [feminine singular]" or "to you I have given them [feminine plural]," that is, the firstfruits,

but it introduced the neuter gender, which indeed is wont to be done by an absolute expression in the Latin language too. Nor does this which was said, *primitia* (firstfruit), seem Latin; for "firstfruits" is wont to be said only in the plural number. But if our translators could render what they found in the Greek, *non congregabo conventicula eorum de sanguinibus*[^7] (I will not gather their assemblies out of bloods), and in another place *virum sanguinum et dolosum abominabitur Dominus*[^8] (the man of bloods and deceitful the Lord will abhor), although "blood" in the Latin language is of the singular number only, why should it have irked them here too to express the singular number *primitia* (firstfruit), as the Greek has it? Yet some translators rendered *primitias* (firstfruits) and in the accusative case, so as to say *omnes primitias tibi dabo* (all the firstfruits I will give to you). Thus they were unwilling to render either expression, unusual to us, as it was found in the Greek.

**60.** (19, 2) *Loquere filiis Israel, et accipiant ad te iuvencam rufam* (speak to the sons of Israel, and let them take to you a red heifer), instead of as if it said "let them bring to you," or at least both would be said in the customary way: "let them take and bring to you a red heifer."

**61.** (19, 7) *Et lavabit corpus suum aqua* (and he shall wash his body with water). This would be understood even if it did not have *aqua* (with water).

**62.** (19, 9) *Et ponet extra castra in locum mundum* (and he shall put it outside the camp into a clean place). It did not say "in a clean place [ablative]," which belongs to the Latin expression.

**63.** (19, 15) *Omne vas apertum quaecumque non habent alligaturam alligatam super ea* (every open vessel which do not have a binding bound upon them).

**64.** (20, 9) *Et accepit Moyses virgam quae ante Dominum* (and Moses took the rod which before the Lord): "was" is understood.

**65.** (20, 12) *Quia non credidistis sanctificare me in conspectu filiorum Israel* (because you did not believe, to sanctify me in the sight of the sons of Israel), that is, "to declare my holiness." According to this expression it is said: *et pro eis sanctifico me ipsum*[^9] (and for them I sanctify myself). And in the Lord's Prayer: *sanctificetur nomen tuum*[^10] (hallowed be your name).

**66.** (20, 15) *Et incolae fuimus in Aegypto dies plures* (and we were sojourners in Egypt many days), "for many years."

**67.** (20, 18) *Et dixit ad eum Edom* (and Edom said to him)—that is, the nation itself (for Edom, who was Esau, was no longer living)—just as those who sent messengers were called "Israel," which name had earlier been that of one man.

**68.** (20, 19) *Et dicunt ei filii Israel: 'iuxta montem transibimus; si autem de aqua tua biberimus ego et pecora mea, dabo pretium tibi*.' (and the sons of Israel say to him: "we will pass along by the mountain; but if we drink of your water, I and my cattle, I will give you a price.") Quite elegantly it passes from the plural to the singular number, as though Israel were speaking, when it had said *filii Israel* (the sons of Israel) and had begun with verbs of the plural number, which is *transibimus et biberimus* (we will pass and we will drink).

**69.** (20, 30) *Et vidit omnis synagoga, quia dimissus est Aaron* (and the whole assembly saw that Aaron was released). This word *dimissus* (released) is not easily found in the Scriptures for what is "dead"; this occurs also in the Gospel, where Symeon says: *nunc dimittis servum tuum in pace*[^11] (now you release your servant in peace). Yet it has ἀπολύεις in the Greek, which sounds more as derived from "loosing" than from "releasing"; for it did not say ἀφεῖς, which without doubt is "you release."

**70.** (20, 30) *Et fleverunt Aaron triginta dies omnis domus Israel* (and the whole house of Israel wept for Aaron thirty days). It did not say "wept [singular]," nor did it say "the entire" or "the whole house," but *omnis* (all), as though they were many; just as "every man," which is said of all, is something far different from "the whole man," which is said of one. But Scripture constantly puts "every" for "whole."

**71.** (21, 5) *Et detrahebat populus ad Deum et adversus Moysen* (and the people spoke against God and against Moses). It did not say "of God," but *ad Deum* (to God). However, some translators, unwilling to render this expression, translated *detrahebant de Deo* (they spoke against God).

**72.** (21, 7) *Ora ergo ad Dominum ut auferat a nobis serpentem* (pray therefore to the Lord that he take away from us the serpent). Just as in *Exodus* it was said *ranam*[^12] (the frog), the singular for the plural.

**73.** (21, 9) *Et factum est quando momordit serpens hominem, et aspexit in aeneum serpentem, et vivebat* (and it came to pass, when the serpent bit a man, and he looked upon the bronze serpent, and he was living). This expression not only has this, that it changes the mood of the verb and from the perfect form of the past tense closes with the imperfect, but also that it has an extra *et* (and).

**74.** (21, 11) *Et elevantes ex Obot castra collocaverunt in Acalgai trans in eremo* (and setting out from Oboth, they pitched camp in Acalgai across in the desert). This is "in the farther desert."

**75.** (22, 23) *Et percussit asinam in virga* (and he struck the donkey in the rod). It did not say "with the rod," but *in virga* (in the rod).

**76.** (23, 11) *In maledictionem inimicorum meorum vocavi te, et ecce benedixisti benedictionem* (for the cursing of my enemies I called you, and behold, you have blessed a blessing); it did not say "with a blessing," but, as though it said, "behold, you have spoken a blessing."

**77.** (23, 12) *Quaecumque miserit Deus in os meum, hoc observabo loqui* (whatever God shall put into my mouth, this I will be careful to speak). It did not say "these things I will be careful."

**78.** (23, 13) *Veni mecum adhuc in locum alium de quo non videbis eum inde* (come with me still to another place from which you will not see it from there).

**79.** (25, 4) *Et ait Dominus ad Moysen: 'accipe duces populi et ostenta eos Domino contra solem, et avertetur ira animationis Domini ab Israel*.' (and the Lord said to Moses: "take the leaders of the people and display them to the Lord before the sun, and the wrath of the Lord's anger will be turned away from Israel.") Although the Lord was speaking, it did not say "*and display them* to me," and "*the wrath* 'of mine' *will be turned away from Israel*."

**80.** (25, 15) *Domus familiae est Madian* (she is a house of a family of Midian). He said this of the woman who was slain together with the adulterous Israelite. By which words, as I think, he wished it to be understood that she was noble, so as to call her *domus familiae* (a house of a family). So now in common speech both "father of a family" and "mother of a family" are said as a mark of riches.

**81.** (26, 1) *Et locutus est Dominus ad Moysen et Eleazar sacerdotem dicens: 'accipe principium totius synagogae filiorum Israel a viginti annis et supra*.' (and the Lord spoke to Moses and Eleazar the priest, saying: "take the beginning of the whole assembly of the sons of Israel from twenty years old and upward.") It is to be noted what he called *principium* (the beginning), namely the vigor of age among the people.

**82.** (27, 20-21) *Ut exaudiant eum filii Israel* (that the sons of Israel may obey him), and *in ore eius exibunt* (at his mouth they shall go out), and *in ore eius introibunt* (at his mouth they shall come in), that is, "when he shall have commanded."

**83.** (27, 22) *Et fecit Moyses secundum quae praecepit illi Dominus; et assumens Iesum statuit eum ante Eleazarum sacerdotem* et cetera (and Moses did according to what the Lord commanded him; and taking Joshua he set him before Eleazar the priest), where this was to be noted, that, when it had already said *et fecit Moyses secundum quae praecepit illi Dominus* (and Moses did according to what the Lord commanded him), it repeats the very same words.

**84.** (27, 23) *Et commendavit eum secundum quae praecepit Dominus Moysi* (and he commended him according to what the Lord commanded Moses). It did not say "*according to what* the Lord commanded 'him.'"

**85.** (28, 13) *Decimam decimam similaginis consparsam in oleo agno uni* (a tenth, a tenth of fine flour mingled with oil for one lamb); that is, "single tenths for single lambs." For when it says *decimam decimam* (a tenth, a tenth), it signified that the tenths are to be repeated for the individual lambs.

**86.** (28, 16-17) *Et in mense primo quarta decima mensis huius dies festus, septem dies azyma edetis* (and in the first month, on the fourteenth of this month, a feast day; seven days you shall eat unleavened bread). It is to be noted that the feast is celebrated over several days, and yet it is called *diem festum* (a feast day, singular)—it does not call the celebration of that festival "feast days," however many days it has lasted.

**87.** (29, 1) *Et mense septimo una mensis* (and in the seventh month, on the one of the month). According to this expression it is said in *Genesis*: *et facta est vespera et factum est mane, dies unus*[^13] (and there was evening and there was morning, one day); for there, as here, the Greek has ἡμέρα μία, whence some, here interpreting "on the first day of the month," passed over the necessary expression, although they did not depart from the sense of the words.

**88.** (29, 2) *Vitulum unum ex bobus, arietem unum* (one calf from the oxen, one ram). It did not say "from the sheep," as it did *ex bobus* (from the oxen), though that too, if it were absent, would be complete.

**89.** (29, 2) *Agnos anniculos sine vitio septem* (seven lambs a year old without blemish). This which it placed with the lambs, *sine vitio* (without blemish), is understood throughout—both, that is, with the calf and with the ram.

**90.** (29, 4) *Decima decima agno uni septem agnis* (a tenth, a tenth for one lamb, for seven lambs), that is, "single portions for single lambs."

**91.** (29, 39) *Exceptis votis vestris et voluntaria vestra et holocaustomata vestra et sacrificia vestra et libamina vestra et salutaria vestra* (apart from your vows and your freewill offerings and your burnt offerings and your sacrifices and your drink offerings and your peace offerings). This expression, even if it were Greek, I would not note. For it does not say "apart from your vows and your freewill offerings," and so on, as it had begun; but neither "apart-from your vows" [in the nominative], so as to begin with a solecism, as it were,

but, when it had first said in the correct and customary expression *exceptis votis vestris* (apart from your vows), it then added the rest in another case, where "apart from [agreeing with the ablative]" cannot be understood, but rather "excepted [agreeing with a nominative]"; which neither Greek nor Latin expression admits.

**92.** (30, 3) *Homo, homo quicumque voverit votum Domino* (a man, a man, whoever shall vow a vow to the Lord), instead of "every man."

**93.** (30, 4) *Cum autem mulier voverit votum Domino aut definierit definitionem in domo patris sui in iuventute sua* (but when a woman shall vow a vow to the Lord or shall determine a determination in the house of her father in her youth). Here it calls a female *mulierem* (a woman), even if she be a virgin, after the manner of the Scriptures; whence it is said also of Christ, what the Apostle says: *factum ex muliere*[^14] (made of a woman).

**94.** (30, 5) *Et audierit pater eius vota eius et definitiones eius, quas definivit* (and her father shall hear her vows and her determinations which she determined). *Definitiones definivit* (she determined determinations) is an expression.

**95.** (30, 5) *Definivit adversus animam suam* (she determined against her soul); that is, "against the delights of her soul."

**96.** (30, 5) *Et tacuerit pater eius, et stabunt omnia vota eius* (and her father shall be silent, and all her vows shall stand). Here it has an extra *et* (and); for the complete sense is "if her father shall hear and be silent, all her vows shall stand."

**97.** (30, 7) *Si autem facta fuerit viro* (but if she shall have been made to a man), it is understood "shall have married"; Scripture speaks thus.

**98.** (30, 7) *Et vota eius super eam* (and her vows upon her): "are" is understood, which some translators even added.

**99.** (30, 13) *Omnia quaecumque exierint ex labiis eius secundum vota eius et secundum definitiones quae adversus animam eius, non manet ei* (all whatever shall have gone forth from her lips according to her vows and according to the determinations which against her soul, does not remain for her), when one would expect the plural number, so as to say "do not remain for her"; for thus some translated, avoiding this seeming solecism.

**100.** (31, 4) *Mille ex tribu et mille ex tribu, ex omnibus tribubus Israel* (a thousand from a tribe and a thousand from a tribe, from all the tribes of Israel). Did it really say *mille* (a thousand) twelve times? And yet this repetition signifies "a thousand from each."

**101.** (31, 8) *Interfecerunt in gladio* (they slew in the sword), which would customarily be said "they slew with the sword."

**102.** (31, 10) *Et omnes civitates eorum quae in habitationibus eorum* (and all their cities which in their dwellings): "were" is understood.

**103.** (31, 10) *Et villas eorum succenderunt in igni* (and their villages they burned in fire), which would customarily be said "they burned with fire."

**104.** (31, 18) *Et omnem multitudinem mulierum, quae non novit concubitum masculi, vivificate* (and all the multitude of women who has not known the bed of a male, keep alive). Nowhere does it appear more certainly that by Hebrew expression even virgins are wont to be called "women."

**105.** (31, 28-29) *Et a dimidia parte eorum accipietis, et dabis ea Eleazar sacerdoti primitias Domini* (and from their half part you [plural] shall take, and you [singular] shall give them to Eleazar the priest as firstfruits of the Lord). It did not say "you [plural] shall give."

**106.** (31, 35) *Et animae hominum a mulieribus, quae non cognoverunt concubitum viri, omnis anima duo et triginta milia* (and the souls of persons from among the women who had not known the bed of a man, every soul thirty-two thousand). Here too the earlier expression is confirmed, that even those who had not known the bed of a man, that is, virgins, are called "women."

**107.** (31, 54) *Et accepit Moyses et Eleazar sacerdos aurum a chiliarchis et a centurionibus et intulit ea in tabernaculum testimonii* (and Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold from the captains of thousands and from the centurions and brought them into the tabernacle of testimony). It seems it ought to have said "and brought it into the tabernacle of testimony," since it had stated that gold had been taken, and most Latin manuscripts have it so; but it seems to have been brought in as a plural, so as to say *intulit ea* (brought them), because many vessels had been mentioned above, in which this gold was.

**108.** (32, 1) *Et pecora multitudo erat filiis Ruben et filiis Gad, multitudo copiosa valde* (and cattle, a multitude, there was to the sons of Reuben and to the sons of Gad, a very abundant multitude); it did not say "and there was a multitude of cattle."

**109.** (32, 1) *Et viderunt regionem Iazer et regionem Galaad, et erat locus locus pecoribus* (and they saw the region of Jazer and the region of Gilead, and there was a place, a place for cattle). What it said twice, *locus* (a place), is not to be joined in pronunciation; rather a slight pause is to be made: *et erat locus* (and there was a place), and then to be added *locus pecoribus* (a place for cattle). For, as though one waited to learn what sort of place, the repetition is elegantly introduced.

**110.** (32, 2) *Et accedentes filii Ruben et filii Gad dixerunt ad Moysen et Eleazar sacerdotem et ad principes synagogae dicentes* (and the sons of Reuben and the sons of Gad, drawing near, said to Moses and Eleazar the priest and to the leaders of the assembly, saying). This is neither a Greek nor a Latin expression, *dixerunt dicentes* (they said, saying), but seems Hebrew.

**111.** (32, 5) *Detur terra ista famulis tuis in possessionem* (let this land be given to your servants for a possession); as though they spoke of others, when they were asking for themselves.

**112.** (32, 12) *Non enim secuti sunt post me praeter Chaleb filius Iephone* (for they did not follow after me, except Caleb the son of Jephone). It seems it ought to have said "except Caleb the son of Jephone [accusative]"; but it introduced it in the nominative, because *secuti sunt* (they followed) precedes. This expression is also Latin, but rare.

**113.** (32, 13) *Donec consumeretur omnis generatio facientes maligna in conspectu Domini* (until the whole generation should be consumed, doing [nominative plural] wicked things in the sight of the Lord). It did not say "doing [nominative singular] wicked things," nor "of those doing [genitive plural] wicked things."

**114.** (32, 16) *Et accesserunt ei et dicebant* (and they approached him and were saying). It did not say what is customary, "they approached to him."

**115.** (32, 24) *Et aedificabitis vobis ipsis civitates impedimentis vestris* (and you shall build for yourselves cities for your baggage). As though it said "you shall build for yourselves cities on account of your baggage."

**116.** (32, 26-27) *Impedimenta nostra et uxores nostrae et omnia pecora nostra erunt in civitatibus Galaad; pueri autem tui transibunt omnes armati* (our baggage and our wives and all our cattle shall be in the cities of Gilead; but your servants will pass over, all armed). They did not say "but we will pass over, all armed," but as though they spoke of others.

**117.** (32, 28) *Et commendavit eis Moyses Eleazar sacerdotem et Iesum filium Nave et principes familiarum tribuum Israel* (and Moses commended to them Eleazar the priest and Joshua the son of Nun and the leaders of the families of the tribes of Israel). It did not say "Moses commended them to Eleazar the priest," which the order of the customary expression certainly required; for through these men they were brought into the land they were seeking. Hence the latter rather seem commended to the former, by whom they were being brought into the land they had asked for, than the former, who were doing the bringing, commended to the latter, whom they were bringing in.

**118.** (32, 30) *Si autem non transierint armati vobiscum in bellum in conspectu Domini, et transferte impedimenta eorum* (but if they shall not pass over armed with you into battle in the sight of the Lord, then transfer their baggage); it has an extra *et* (and).

**119.** (32, 33) *Et dedit eis Moyses filiis Gad et filiis Ruben* (and Moses gave to them, to the sons of Gad and to the sons of Reuben); it has an extra *eis* (to them).

**120.** (33, 14) *Et non ibi erat aqua populo bibere* (and there was no water there for the people to drink); instead of what is "for drinking."

**121.** (33, 51) *Loquere filiis Israel et dices ad eos* (speak to the sons of Israel and you shall say to them). It seems to suffice: *loquere filiis Israel* (speak to the sons of Israel).

**122.** (33, 52) *Et omnia idola fusilia eorum perdetis ea* (and all their molten idols you shall destroy them). It has an extra *ea* (them).

**123.** (34, 6) *Hoc erit vobis fines maris* (this shall be for you the borders of the sea). It did not say "these shall be for you."

**124.** (34, 7) *Et hoc erit vobis fines ad aquilonem* (and this shall be for you the borders toward the north); the same expression is repeated.

**125.** (35, 3) *Et erunt civitates eis habitare* (and the cities shall be for them to dwell), instead of what is wont to be said, "for dwelling."

**126.** (35, 11) *Refugia erunt vobis fugere illo homicidam* (they shall be refuges for you, for the manslayer to flee thither); which can customarily be said "that the manslayer may flee thither."

**127.** (35, 15) *Et incolae qui in vobis istae civitates in refugium* (and the sojourners who among you, these cities for a refuge). *Incolae* (sojourners) is set in the singular number, that is, "for this sojourner who is among you," since it lacks "is." For it did not say "who is among you," by a most familiar expression, but by one rarer in the Latin language than in Greek.
