# Revisions II

## Chapter 54 (81): Expressions, in Seven Books

I composed seven books on the seven books of the divine Scriptures—that is, the five of Moses, one on Joshua son of Nun, and another on Judges—noting in each the expressions that are less customary to our language,

expressions in which those who read the divine words, paying too little attention, seek a meaning, when it is in fact a manner of speech; and sometimes they dig out some meaning that does not, indeed, depart from the truth, yet it does not turn out to be what the author who wrote it meant, but rather it appears more credible that he said this by a manner of speech.

Now many obscure passages in the holy Scriptures become clear once the manner of speech is recognized. For this reason these same manners of speech ought to be learned where the meaning is plain, so that even where it is hidden, the very knowledge of them may come to one's aid and open them up to the reader's intention.

The title of this work is *Expressions on Genesis,* and so on for each of the books.

But as for what I set down in the first book[^1] as being written—*and Noah did all the words whatsoever the Lord commanded him, so he did*[^2]—and which expression I said was similar to the one that occurs at the founding of creation,

where, after it is said *and so it was made,* there is added *and God made it*[^3]*,* this does not seem to me to be similar in every way. For there, even the sense lies hidden, whereas here there is only the expression.

This work begins thus: *The expressions of the Scriptures.*

[^1]: Locut. in Hept. 1.18
[^2]: Gen 6:22; 7:5
[^3]: Gen 1:15, 24
