# Book I: Expressions on Genesis

The expressions of the Scriptures that appear to follow the peculiarities—what are called in Greek *idiomata*—of the Hebrew or Greek language.

**1.** (1:14) *And let them divide between the midst of the day and the midst of the night*.

**2.** (1:20. 26) *The flying things that fly over the earth according to the firmament of heaven*. It is asked how *according to the firmament* is to be taken. So too: *let us make man according to the image and according to the likeness*, which many Latin manuscripts have as: *to the image and likeness*.

**3.** (1:28) *Fill the earth, and have dominion over it*. For the Latin expression is: *dominamini ei* ("have dominion over it").

**4.** (2:5) *And there was no man to work the earth*; which the Latin manuscripts have as: *who might work the earth*.

**5.** (2:8) *God planted a Paradise according to the East*; which the Latin have as: *toward the East*.

**6.** (2:9) Whereas many Latin manuscripts have: *and the tree of knowing good and evil*, or: *the tree of the knowledge of good and evil*, or: *the tree of knowing of good and evil*—and whatever other variants of the translators there may be on this matter—the Greek has: *and the tree for knowing the knowable of good and evil*; and I do not know whether this is a matter of expression and does not rather suggest some definite meaning.

**7.** (2:16) As to what the Latin manuscripts have: *of every tree that is in Paradise you shall eat for food*—it is not to be punctuated *for food in Paradise*, but: *you shall eat for food*; for the Latin expression admits the saying *esca edes* ("you shall eat as food"), since in expressions of this kind they are accustomed to put, for the Greek dative case, the "ablative" or what they call the "seventh" case. Or else the order of the words is: *of every tree for food*.

**8.** (3:1) *The serpent was the most prudent of all the beasts*, which many Latin have; for in the Greek it is written φρονιμώτατος, not σοφώτατος.

**9.** (3:7) As to what is written of Adam and Eve: *their eyes were opened*—since it would be absurd to believe that they had previously wandered about in Paradise blind or with their eyes shut, this is a matter of expression, by which it is also written of Hagar: *she opened her eyes, and saw a well*[^1]; for she was not sitting with her eyes previously shut; and likewise that at the breaking of bread the eyes were opened of those who recognized the Lord after the resurrection[^2]; for they were not walking along the road with him with their eyes shut.

**10.** (3:15) Whereas many manuscripts have: *and I will put enmity between you and the woman*, the Greeks have: *in the midst of you and in the midst of the woman*, which is a matter of expression; for it amounts to just as much as when one says: *between you and the woman*. This holds also for what follows: *in the midst of your seed, and in the midst of her seed*.

**11.** (3:17) Whereas many Latin manuscripts have: *because you have listened to the voice of your wife, and have eaten of the tree of which I had commanded you, of that alone, not to eat*, the Greeks have: *you have eaten of the tree of which I had commanded you, of that alone, not to eat of it*; but other Greek translators have *manducasti* or *edisti* ("you have eaten"), so that according to them the sense is this: "because you have listened to the voice of your wife, and as for the tree of which I had commanded you, of that alone, not to eat—you have eaten of it."

**12.** (4:2) *And she went on to bear his brother Abel*; the expression "went on to say" is frequent in the Scriptures.

**13.** (4:8) *And it came to pass, while they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel, and slew him*—this is a matter of expression; for even if it did not have *and it came to pass*, the sense could be complete: *and when they were in the field*.

**14.** (6:6) As to what is written in certain Latin manuscripts: *and God repented, and said, 'I will blot out the man whom I made from the face of the earth'*—in the Greek is found διενοήθη, which is said to signify rather *he reconsidered* than *he repented*; which word some Latin manuscripts also have.

**15.** (6:14) Whereas very many Latin manuscripts have: *you shall make nests into the ark*—since the Latin expression is not *into the ark* but "in the ark"—the Greeks have neither *into the ark* nor "in the ark," but: *you shall make nests the ark*; which is understood to mean: so that the ark itself should be the nests.

**16.** (6:16) Whereas very many manuscripts have: *you shall make the door of the ark in the side*, some have: *crosswise*; for they wished thus to translate what is said in Greek: ἐκ πλαγίων.

**17.** (7:4) As to what is written: *for in seven more days, I will bring a flood of water upon the earth*, which some Latin manuscripts have, in the Greek is found: *I will bring rain upon the earth*; the Greek expression has the genitive case, not the accusative, so that if we express this in Latin it would be said thus: *for in seven more days I will bring rain upon the earth*.

**18.** (7:5) As to what is written: *and Noah did all things whatsoever the Lord God commanded him, so he did*, this is an expression similar to the one that occurs at the founding of creation: after it is said *and so it was made*, there is added *and God made it*[^3].

**19.** (7:4) As to what is written: *I will blot out every uprising*—it is to be noted that it does not say "creation": for in Greek it is written ἀνάστασις,

a word which is also regularly used for "resurrection" in the Greek Scriptures, when ἐξανάστασις could be said, so that "uprising" is ἀνάστασις, "resurrection" ἐξανάστασις.

The Apostle too used this word: *if by any means I may attain to the resurrection of the dead*[^4]; for there the Greeks do not have ἀνάστασις, but ἐξανάστασις.

**20.** (7:14) As to what is written: *and all the beasts according to their kind, and all the cattle according to their kind, and everything creeping that moves upon the earth according to its kind, and every flying thing according to its kind went in to Noah into the ark, two of all flesh, in which is the spirit of life*—the phrase *in which* does not refer to anything unless you supply "kind," that is, "in which kind"; for if we were to supply *flesh*, it would have to be said *in which* (feminine) it was; which only the translator Symmachus said.

**21.** (7:23) As to what is again said: *and every uprising was blotted out*, it is to be noted to be a matter of expression, as if it said "the founding" or "the creation" of flesh.

**22.** (7:23) When he had said: *and every uprising that was upon the face of all the earth was blotted out, from man even to cattle and to the creeping things and the flying things of heaven*, he then added: *and they were blotted out from the earth*; it is to be noted that this is an expression of repetition, which Scripture commonly employs.

**23.** (8:6-7) As to what is written: *he sent forth the raven to see whether the water had ceased, and going forth it did not return, until the water dried up from the earth*—this is an expression customary in the Scriptures, which may by now begin to be noticed; for it did not return afterward either, because it was said not to have returned until the water dried up.

**24.** (8:9. 11) As to what is written: *and he stretched out his hand, took it, and brought it to himself into the ark*—this is an expression which I think to be Hebrew for the reason that it is also most familiar to the Punic language, in which we find many things consonant with Hebrew words; for it would certainly suffice to say *and he stretched out the hand*, even if it did not add *his*. Such too is what he says a little after: *it had olive leaves, a twig in its mouth*.

**25.** (8:12) *And it did not go on to return to him any more*—this is a most familiar expression in the Scriptures.

**26.** (8:21) As to what is written: *and I will not add further to curse upon the earth*, it is similar to the one above: *and it did not go on to return to him*.

**27.** (8:21) *And I will not add to strike all living flesh*—this is the same expression.

**28.** (9:5) *For indeed the blood of your souls*; when it would suffice to say either *your blood* or *the blood of your souls*.

**29.** (9:12) *This is the sign of the covenant which I will set in the midst of myself and you*; which is: "between me and you."

**30.** (10:9) *He was a giant hunter against the Lord God*—it is uncertain whether it can be taken as "before the Lord God"; because that is how what is said in Greek is usually understood: ἐναντίον.

**31.** (10:14) *Whence came forth thence the Philistines*, when it would suffice: *whence came forth the Philistines*.

**32.** (11:1) *And the whole earth was one lip*; which we customarily say: "one language."

33.\* (11:1) *And the whole earth was one lip*; it is to be noted that the whole earth means all the men who then existed, although not yet in all the earth.

**34.** (11:3) *And they had bricks for stone*; the Greek has: *and they had bricks into stone*; which, if it were said in Latin, the expression would be less understood.

**35.** (11:4) *Come, let us build for ourselves a city and a tower whose top will reach unto heaven*; this is said by hyperbole, if a manner of speech is to be taken here; but if *unto heaven* is taken as said literally, let it be considered among the *Questions*.

**36.** (11:10) Whereas certain Latin manuscripts have: *Shem the son of Noah was a hundred years old when he begot Arphaxad*, the Greeks have: *Shem the son of a hundred years when he begot Arphaxad*, where there is an ellipsis, because *was* is lacking. But the fact that they do not have *son of Noah*, but only *son*, is a novel expression.

**37.** (11:30) *And Sarah was barren, and she did not bear*, when it could suffice: *and Sarah was barren*.

**38.** (12:12) *Therefore it will be that when the Egyptians see you, they will say: that this is his wife*—the word *that* is added by the manner of speech, for it could have sufficed: *this is his wife*.

**39.** (12:14) As to what Scripture says: *and it came to pass, as soon as Abraham entered into Egypt*, it would suffice: *as soon as Abraham entered into Egypt*.

**40.** (12:18) *Why have you done this to me, that you did not tell me that she is your wife?* When it would suffice: *you did not tell me*. And the very word *told* is said after the manner of the Scriptures; for most Latin have: *you did not say*.

**41.** (13:1) *And Abraham went up out of Egypt, he and his wife and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the desert*; "they went up" is to be supplied; yet it is not properly said that what he had that was lifeless—such as gold and silver and all the household goods—went up with him; and through this an expression is here understood which is called in Greek ζεῦγμα κατ’ ἔλλειψιν.

**42.** (13:7) *And there was strife in the midst of the herdsmen of Abraham's cattle and of Lot's cattle*—on which account almost all the Latin manuscripts did not render that expression, but speak as our custom has it, and we have decided not to note it henceforth; for it occurs throughout the Greek Scripture wherever something of this kind is said.

**43.** (13:8) *For we are men, brothers*, Abraham said to Lot; whence it is understood to be Scripture's custom to speak thus, so that those of one kinship are called "brothers" even if they differ in degree of blood, so that one is in the higher and the other in the lower, as in this place; for Abraham was his uncle.

**44.** (14:1) *And it came to pass in the reign of Amraphel king of Shinar*; according to the custom of our manner of speech it would suffice to begin thus: *now in the reign of Amraphel*; therefore in saying *and it came to pass*, Scripture spoke after its own manner.

**45.** (14:5) *Now in the fourteenth year Chedorlaomer and the kings who with him*—"were" is to be supplied; whence by some Latin it was indeed added.

**46.** (14:6) *And the Horites who in the mountains of Seir*; "were" is to be supplied.

**47.** (14:13) *And there coming a certain one of those who had escaped, he announced to Abraham the man from across the river, and he was dwelling by the oak of Mamre, of the Amorite, brother of Eshcol and brother of Aner; who were in league with Abraham*—an obscure hyperbaton.

For the order is: *there coming a certain one of those who had escaped, of the Amorite, brother of Eshcol and brother of Aner, who were in league, he announced to Abraham the man from across the river; and he was dwelling by the oak*. This hyperbaton becomes still more obscure through an ellipsis;

for when it had said *a certain one of the Amorite, brother of Eshcol and brother of Aner*, it did not say what of the brother, but "son" is understood; just as when it is said *James of Alphaeus*, although "son" is not said, nothing else is understood, and there are many such expressions in the Scriptures where "son" is left unsaid and understood.

**48.** (14:22) As to what certain Latin manuscripts have: *and Abraham said to the king of Sodom: 'I stretch out my hand to God the Most High, who created heaven and earth. If from a thread even to the strap of a sandal'*—the translator was misled by what the Greek has, σπαρτίου, which is understood in Latin as "thread," and this is an expression of the Scriptures.

**49.** (14:22-23) *I stretch out my hand to God the Most High, who created heaven and earth, if I take anything of all that is yours*; for if he said *I stretch out my hand to God the Most High* in place of saying "I swear," it is not a customary expression in Latin, unless it be said thus: "I stretch out my hand to God the Most High, that I will not take anything of all that is yours."

**50.** (15:13) *Knowing you shall know, that your seed will be a stranger in a land*; this is indeed a most customary expression of the Scriptures, but the Greeks have: *knowing you shall know*, which is almost the same.

**51.** (16:3) *And Abraham gave her to her husband, to him as a wife*—it has the extra word: *to him*.

**52.** (16:5) *But when she saw that she had conceived, I was despised before her*. The Greeks have in this place a participle, which the Latin language does not have, that is, ἰδοῦσα, but as if it were said: "seeing that I had conceived, I was despised before her"; it sounds like a solecism; so too through that participle which was said, ἰδοῦσα, for which we say: "seeing."

**53.** (17:6) Whereas the Latin have: *I will increase you exceedingly greatly*, the Greeks have: *greatly, greatly*.

**54.** (17:8) *And I will give to you and to your seed after you the land in which you dwell, all the land of Canaan for an everlasting possession*—it is to be considered whether in saying *everlasting* it is a matter of expression; the Greeks have αἰώνιον; and as for its saying *and to your seed after you*, there it wished to be understood what it had said: *to you*.

**55.** (17:9) *But you shall keep my covenant, and your seed after you in their generations*; *you shall keep*, in place of "keep"; he put the future indicative for the imperative mood.

**56.** (17:12) *And a child of eight days shall be circumcised among you, every male*; in place of "every male," as if any but a male could be circumcised.

**57.** (17:17) *And Abraham fell upon his face, and said in his heart, saying: 'Shall a son be born to me who am a hundred years old, and shall Sarah at ninety years bear?'* There is no doubt that this is the expression of one marveling, not of one doubting.

**58.** (17:24) *Now Abraham was ninety-nine years old when he was circumcised the flesh of his foreskin*; he did not say "in flesh" or "in the flesh."

**59.** (18:7) *And Abraham ran into the cattle*; he did not say "to the cattle."

**60.** (18:11) *Now Abraham and Sarah, elders advanced in days*, which the Greeks have as: *advanced of days*.

**61.** (18:20) *And the Lord said:* '*the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is fulfilled, and their sins are very great*.' Scripture is accustomed to use "cry" for such shamelessness and licentiousness of iniquity that it is concealed neither by modesty nor by fear.

**62.** (18:28) *And he said:* '*that I will not destroy if I find there forty-five*,' the word *that* seems superfluous, and so in some Latin manuscripts it is not read.

**63.** (18:30) *Will you, O Lord, if I speak*—"be angry" is supplied, or something of this kind.

**64.** (19:29) *When the Lord overthrew the cities, in which he dwelt in them*.

**65.** (20:13) *In every place where we enter there*.

**66.** (21:19) *And God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of living water*—this is a matter of expression, for she was not with her eyes shut. We have already spoken of this at the beginning of the book, where it is written: *and their eyes were opened*.

**67.** (21:23) *And the land in which you have dwelt in it*.

**68.** (21:27) *And they both arranged a covenant*, or both made a covenant; Scripture loves to call a "pact" by the name of "covenant."

**69.** (22:2) *Take your beloved son*—the word *take* is said by that expression by which it is also said to Hagar concerning her son.

**70.** (22:4) *And Abraham looking up with his eyes*, when it would suffice: *looking up*.

**71.** (22:16) *By myself I have sworn, unless blessing I will bless you*; as if it said: "by myself I have sworn that blessing I will bless you"; or, with no word added, simply: "by myself I have sworn, blessing I will bless you."

**72.** (22:17) *And multiplying I will multiply your seed*, when it could suffice: *I will multiply*.

**73.** (22:20) *And it was announced to Abraham, saying* (plural), whereas the custom of speaking has: "they announced to Abraham saying," or: "it was announced by those saying."

**74.** (23:3-4) *And Abraham rose up from his dead* (masculine), he did not say "from his dead" (feminine). And again concerning the same woman: *and I will bury*, he says, *my dead*, which the Greek scripture shows to be said not in the neuter as though a dead body, but in the masculine gender.

**75.** (24:3) *And I will adjure you by the Lord God of heaven and the God of earth*; the Greeks do not have *by*, but: *I adjure you the Lord God of heaven*.

**76.** (24:3) *Among whom I dwell in them*.

**77.** (24:5) *Lest perhaps the woman be unwilling to go with me*: to call a woman "female" is proper to that language.

**78.** (24:5) *Into the land from which you went out thence*.

**79.** (24:6) *Take heed to yourself, that you do not bring my son back there*: by these words a warning is customarily expressed.

**80.** (24:9. 8) *And the servant put his hand under Abraham's thigh, and swore to him concerning this matter*. Therefore this is the expression by which Abraham had said *I adjure you*, as if he had said: "swear to me";

but we are not accustomed to speak thus, and it is to be observed whether there are in the Scriptures other similar expressions; for the fact that Abraham said *if the woman be unwilling to come with you, you will be free from this oath* made it clear that he had said *I adjure you* as though he were saying: "swear to me."

**81.** (24:16) As to what is written of Rebekah: *now the virgin was very beautiful in countenance; she was a virgin, no man had known her*, this repetition conveys a commendation of virginity. But why it is added *no man had known her*—unless it be a matter of expression—it would be remarkable if it could be taken so that "virgin" be a name of age, not of integrity. The Greeks, however, do not have *had known*, but: *knew*; which seems inconsistent.

**82.** (24:26) *He worshiped to the Lord*: which it is customary for us to say: "he worshiped the Lord."

**83.** (24:27) *Because he has not forsaken justice and truth from my lord*: that is, the justice and truth that is from my lord, as if it said: "which my lord has done."

**84.** (24:28) *And the girl, running, announced it into her mother's house*: as though it were not also her father's house.

**85.** (24:32) *And water to wash with his feet, and with the feet of the men who were with him*.

**86.** (24:40) *The Lord, whom I have pleased before him, he will send his angel with you*; as though it would not suffice: *whom I have pleased*; or could he please him except *before him*?

**87.** (24:42) *If you prosper my way, which I now enter upon it*.

**88.** (24:43-44) When the servant of Abraham was recounting his own words, which he said as he was about to approach the spring, he recounted them thus: *and the virgin to whom I shall say, "give me a little water to drink from your jar," and she will say to me, "drink, you too, and I will draw for your camels"; this is the woman whom the Lord has prepared for his servant Isaac*.

By these words it is expressed clearly enough that even those who were virgins were called "women" by the Hebrew manner of speech.

**89.** (24:48) *And I blessed the Lord God of my lord Abraham;* this is an expression of honor, and most familiar to the Scriptures, like *the God of Elijah*.

**90.** (24:49) *Report to me, that I may turn to the right or the left*. By the right hand he signified prosperity, by the left adversity; that is, the right, if they consent; the left, if they do not consent; for he was in any case going to return by the way he had come; which expression of the Scriptures the rest of their passages will also indicate, since the right hand is named in all good things, the left in evil ones—whether happiness and unhappiness, or justice and injustice, and sometimes the right hand in eternal things, the left in temporal.

**91.** (25:13) *These are the names of the sons of Ishmael according to the names of their generations*; as if it had said: "according to which names their generations were called."

**92.** (25:20) *He took Rebekah the daughter of Bethuel the Syrian of Mesopotamia, the sister of Laban the Syrian, to himself as a wife*; when it could be said merely: *as a wife*; or: *to himself a wife*.

**93.** (25:24) *And there were twins in her womb*; when what was said could be left unadded: *her*.

**94.** (25:27) *And the young men grew up*. There is such an expression even among secular authors, as is: *and they build hidden shields*[^5]; that is, by building they make them hidden. So too here: *the young men grew up*—since they were infants, it is understood that by growing up they became young men.

**95.** (25:31) Jacob to Esau: *sell me today your birthright to me*; for so the Greek manuscripts have it.

**96.** (26:28) *Seeing we have seen, that the Lord is with you.*

**97.** (26:28) *And we will arrange a covenant with you*: the Scriptures love to put *covenant* for "pact," that is, διαθήκην. As the Latin have: *and we will arrange a covenant with you that you do us no harm*; as if it were said: "that you make a pact not to do us harm."

**98.** (27:1) *And he called his elder son Esau, and said*; in this place the word "elder" does not signify advanced age, but the greater by comparison.

**99.** (27:3) *Now therefore take your vessel, both quiver and bow*; he did not say "vessels," but: *vessel*; what he wished to be understood is not clear, unless perhaps it is an expression, so that by "vessel" he wished the quiver to be understood, and in explaining what he had said by *vessel* he added *both quiver and bow*, as if he had said: "take your vessel and the quiver"; as if he were to say: "take your vessel, that is, *the quiver*; then another thing, *and the bow*," which would not belong to that vessel which is the quiver. Or else he wished to call the quiver and bow a "vessel," putting the singular number for the plural; as if he were to say: "take your garment," by which name we would understand several garments, just as we understand "soldier" for "soldiers," and there are many such cases.

**100.** (27:3) *Go out into the field, and hunt me a hunt*.

**101.** (27:9) *And go to the sheep, and take me two tender and good kids*; by the name of *sheep* he signified both kinds of livestock, because they were together in the same pastures.

**102.** (28:4) *And may he give you the blessing of your father Abraham*; Isaac says this to his son concerning the boy's grandfather, his own father.

**103.** (28:5) *And he went out into Mesopotamia of Syria*; as though *Mesopotamia* were spoken of as anything but *of Syria*, although the *Septuagint* is said not to have this word *of Syria*, but it is written with an asterisk.

**104.** (28:15) God to Jacob: *I will not forsake you, until I do all the things I have spoken with you*; as though he would let him go once he had done them; but not so—it is a matter of expression.

**105.** (28:16) *Jacob rose up from his sleep, and said: 'that the Lord is in this place, and I did not know it'*; the sense is complete, even if it does not have *that*.

**106.** (28:16) *The Lord is in this place*; as if it said: "there is here a manifestation of the Lord"; for the Lord is not in a place.

**107.** (29:5) Jacob asking, in that he says: *do you know Laban the son of Nahor?* When he was the son of Bethuel; it is to be understood that Nahor was the more noble, and that by the merit of his standing it came about that he asked about him. That the one who is descended from someone should be called the "son" of grandfather and great-grandfather and beyond, of some greater ancestor, is a most customary expression. Hence it is that Isaac also called his son's father Abraham, as I mentioned a little before.

**108.** (29:7) *The day is still long, it is not yet the hour for gathering the flocks*.

**109.** (30:4) *And she gave him Bilhah her maidservant, to him as a wife*; it would be complete even if *to him* were not added.

**110.** (30:27) *If I have found favor before you, I would have taken an omen; for God has blessed me at your coming in*; it does not seem consistent, because here it should have been said: "if I had found favor before you, I would have taken an omen";

but as it is, *if I have found*, the order is: "if I have found favor before you, permit me to take an omen"; for he said *I would have taken an omen* as if to say: "Oh that I had taken an omen!" That is, "that I might hold you in my house for a good omen."

**111.** (30:33) *And my justice will answer for me on the morrow*; that is, it will cause me to be heard.

**112.** (31:2) *And Jacob saw Laban's face, and behold it was not toward him as yesterday and the day before*; a most familiar expression in the Scriptures: *yesterday and the day before*, he put for past time.

**113.** (31:10) *And I saw with my eyes in sleep*; although the eyes of the body are shut in sleep.

**114.** (31:13) *I am the God who appeared to you in the place of God*, this is an expression. Or is *God in the place of God* to be taken in the way that *the Lord rained from the Lord*[^6]—the Son from the Father?

**115.** (31:31) *But Jacob answering said to Laban:* '*for I said, lest perhaps you take your daughters from me, and all that is mine*.'

**116.** (31:33) *Now Laban entered and searched in the house of Leah*; it is to be noted how a wife's house is spoken of, since they were overtaken by Laban on the journey; unless perhaps the custom of the Scriptures says "house" for a chamber or tent, just as it also speaks of the maidservants' houses.

**117.** (31:37) Jacob says to Laban: *for you have searched all the vessels of my house*; now he speaks of one house, whose parts were the houses of his wives and concubines; so that it may be understood that "houses" were so called when they were chambers or tents, which they also call "pavilions."

**118.** (31:42) *Unless the God of my father Abraham and the fear of Isaac had been with me*; he calls his grandfather his father, just as Isaac his father had said to him.

**119.** (32:4-5) *Now Jacob sent messengers before him to Esau his brother into the land of Seir in the region of Edom, and charged them, saying: "thus you shall say to my lord Esau: 'thus says your servant Jacob, I have dwelt with Laban and stayed until now, and I have come to have oxen and asses and sheep and menservants and maidservants, and I have sent to announce to my lord Esau'"*; he did not say: "I have sent to announce to you."

**120.** (32:18) *If Esau asks you, saying: "whose are you? And where are you going? And whose are these that go before you?" And you shall say: "your servant Jacob's"*; it would be complete and entire even if it did not have *and*.

**121.** (32:19) *He sent gifts to my lord Esau, and behold he himself is behind us*; but it should have been said in the customary manner of speech: "I have sent gifts to my lord, to you," or: "he has sent gifts to his lord, to you."

**122.** (32:23) *Now he rose up that same night, and took his two wives and his two maidservants*; here they are distinguished from the wives, whom Scripture had earlier called *wives*.

**123.** (32:23) *And he took his two wives and two maidservants*; it is to be noted how, by the custom of the Scriptures, it says: *he took*; for he did not then marry them, nor then receive them from his father-in-law.

**124.** (33:13) Whereas the Latin manuscripts have: *and the sheep and oxen are giving birth*, the Greeks have: *are giving birth upon me*; which is said with this meaning, so that it may be understood: "upon my concern" or "my care"; in the way we are accustomed to speak when we say that something is "upon our head" of which we bear the greatest care.

**125.** (34:7) *And the sons of Jacob came from the field, and when they had heard it, the men were stung, and it was very grievous to them, that he had done a disgraceful thing in Israel, in that he had lain with Jacob's daughter, and it shall not be so*;

such an expression is rarely found in the Scriptures—that the writer should have inserted the words of speakers without introducing the persons speaking; for it does not say "and they said," but with this verb left unsaid it spoke their words. For who else could have said *and it shall not be so* except those who, gravely indignant, were plotting vengeance?

**126.** (34:8) It is to be noted that Hamor, speaking to Jacob and his sons about Dinah, says *your daughter*; he does not say: "your daughter, the sister of these men."

**127.** (34:15) *In this we will be like you, and we will dwell among you*; that is: "among you."

**128.** (34:19) *For he was attached to Jacob's daughter*; that is: "he loved her."

**129.** (34:26) *And they slew his son Shechem with the edge of the sword*; as if it said: "with the sword."

**130.** (34:28-29) The things that the sons of Jacob took from the city of the Shechemites, Salem, when they had stormed it, are enumerated thus: *their sheep, and their oxen, and their asses, whatever was in the city, and whatever was in the field they took; and all their bodies and all their household goods and their wives they took captive, and they plundered whatever was in the city, and whatever was in the houses*.

Among all these things, what it said by *and their bodies* is not well enough understood; for it is not to be supposed that they carried off the bodies of the slain; but it is to be understood of things that are possessed bodily;

so that the execution of it would be what follows: *and the household goods*, and the rest, as in law "delivery of bodies" is spoken of; although the Greeks, by a most customary expression, call "slaves" σωμάτια, yet since it does not say σωμάτια,

but σώματα is said here, it is not to be rashly affirmed that slaves are meant; yet it can be that this is rather the case.

**131.** (36:40) When Scripture had mentioned the nation of Edom, that is, of the Idumeans, and those who had reigned in it, it afterward says: *these are the names of the chiefs of Esau, according to their places in their regions, and in their nations*; calling the families of one nation "nations," on account of the multiplied generations, since even he himself was called Edom; whence also the nation that he propagated was named, of which he was indeed the father.

**132.** (37:21) *But when Reuben heard it, he delivered him out of their hands, and said: 'let us not strike him in the soul.'* He did not, then, deliver him and afterward say this; rather, by saying this he delivered him. He first said, then, *he delivered him*, and afterward, by a brief recapitulation, conveyed how he delivered him.

**133.** (37:21) *Let us not strike him in the soul*; in this place by the name of *soul* it signifies the life of the animate body, by the cause for the effect. According to this it can be seen that it was also said to the devil concerning Job: *do not touch his soul*[^7]; that is: do not kill him. For according to that signification by which the nature of the soul is meant, this is said by the Lord: *do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^8].

**134.** (37:27) As to what Judah says: *but let not our hands be upon him*, this is what we are accustomed to say: "let us not lay hands on him."

**135.** (37:27) *Because he is our brother and our flesh*; not something else, but this same thing is said twice, as if explaining what *our brother* is, that is *our flesh*, namely that they had been begotten of the same flesh.

**136.** (37:31) *They killed a kid of the goats*; Scripture is accustomed to speak thus, as in the *Psalm*: *like lambs of the sheep*[^9], as though there could be a kid except of goats, or lambs except of sheep.

**137.** (38:13) *And it was announced to his daughter-in-law Tamar, saying* (plural); but it should have been said: "they announced, saying."

**138.** (38:14) *And having laid aside the garments of her widowhood from herself*; what would be lacking if there were not added: *from herself*?

**139.** (38:26) *And he did not go on to know her any more*; in place of being joined with her.

**140.** (39:4) *And Joseph found favor in the sight of his lord*; this expression is unknown to no one in the Scriptures.

**141.** (39:6) *And he did not know anything of the things that were around him, nothing*; this is an expression commonly used among the Greeks; among us it is not correct, but we say: "he did not know anything."

**142.** (39:6) *Except the bread that he himself ate*; by "bread" he certainly wished to be understood all things pertaining to his food; whence in the Lord's Prayer bread alone is understood as put for daily food[^10].

**143.** (39:7) *And his lord's wife cast her eyes upon Joseph*; this is an expression commonly used even among us, in place of: "she loved him."

**144.** (39:7) *And she said: 'sleep with me'*; this too is a customary expression, in place of: "lie with me."

**145.** (39:22) *And the keeper of the prison gave the prison through the hand of Joseph*; in place of: "into the hands of Joseph"; by which it signifies: "into his power."

**146.** (40:8) *And they said: 'we have seen a dream, and there is no one to interpret it'*; although each of them had seen his own dream, they did not say: "we have seen dreams," but: *we have seen a dream*.

**147.** (40:12) *The three baskets are three days*; he did not say: "the three baskets signify three days." And this expression is much to be noted, where things that signify are called by the name of the things they signify; hence it is that the Apostle says: *and the rock was Christ*[^11]; he does not say: "the rock signified Christ."

**148.** (40:13) *And you shall give the cup to Pharaoh into his hand*; though it would be complete even if there were not added: *into his hand*.

**149.** (40:19) *And Pharaoh will take away your head from you*; it would be complete even if it did not have: *from you*.

**150.** (40:19) *And the birds of heaven will eat your flesh from you*; similar to the expression above.

**151.** (41:1) *And it came to pass after two years of days*; what would be lacking if it did not have: *of days*?

**152.** (41:7) *Now Pharaoh rose up, and it was a dream*; the Scriptures love to speak thus of dreams: when one has awakened, then, the truth of waking persons being restored to the man, that appears as a dream which, while it was being seen, was as if truth.

**153.** (41:9-10) *I remember my sin today. Pharaoh was angry with his servants, and put us in prison*; he was speaking to him as though of another.

**154.** (41:11) *And we both saw a dream in one night, I and he*; as though it would not suffice that he had said *both*. Then here too he says *we saw a dream*, not: "we saw dreams"; as though both had seen one.

**155.** (41:13) *And it came to pass, as he interpreted to us, so it also happened. And it came to pass*; thus Scripture is accustomed to put it; for it would be complete: "and as he interpreted to us, so it also happened."

**156.** (41:19) *Such as I never saw, more wretched ones in all the land of Egypt*; but it could be said in our manner: "than which I never saw more wretched ones," or "such as I never saw," or "I never saw such ones."

**157.** (41:21) *And rising up I slept; rising up* he says, in place of: "awaking."

**158.** (41:25) *How great things God does, he has shown to Pharaoh*; though he was speaking to him, he says it as though of another.

**159.** (41:30) *And the famine will consume the land*; in place of: "the men who are in the land."

**160.** (41:33-34) *Now therefore look for a prudent and understanding man, and set him over the land of Egypt; and let Pharaoh do this and appoint chiefs of the regions over the land*; as though the one to whom he says *provide a prudent man* were one person, and the one of whom he says *let Pharaoh appoint* another.

**161.** (41:35) *And let the grain be gathered under Pharaoh's hand*, that is: "under his power."

**162.** (41:40) *Yet I will go before the throne of you*; thus the words stand in the Greek which Pharaoh said to Joseph. But *I will go before of you* is a customary expression among the Greeks, whereas among us it is said: *I will go before you*;

but that phrase *I will go before you the throne* is reported not to be customary even among the Greeks, but rather: *I will go before you in the throne*, that is, in the honor of the seat; which is understood: "I will go before you in the kingdom," for this is also what he says to him afterward.

**163.** (41:44) *I am Pharaoh; without you no one will lift up his hand over all the land of Egypt*; as if he said: "I am king, but you are the chief or overseer of Egypt." For *Pharaoh* is not the name of a man, but of the royal power.

**164.** (42:1-2) *Now Jacob seeing that there was selling in Egypt, said to his sons: 'why are you slothful? Behold, I have heard that there is selling in Egypt'*; it is to be noted that what he says he heard, Scripture had said he saw.

**165.** (42:2) *Buy us a little food, that we may live, and not die*; one of these would suffice, either: *that we may live*, or: *that we may not die*.

**166.** (42:11) *We are peaceable; your servants are not spies*; as though they spoke of others: *your servants are not spies*, when they could say: "we are not"; but out of a manner of honor they especially spoke thus.

**167.** (42:13) *We are twelve, your servants, brothers in the land of Canaan*; whereas they say afterward that one of them is not—namely, supposing Joseph himself not to exist, that is, to have perished. By this expression that too was said: *these are the sons of Jacob who were born to him in Mesopotamia*[^12]; although Benjamin was not born there. And as for their saying *we are in the land of Canaan*, when at the time they were speaking they were in Egypt, *we are* was said for "we dwell"; for they had come thence and would return there at once, abiding there.

**168.** (42:14) *This is what I said to you, saying, that you are spies*; what would be lacking even if it did not add: *saying*?

**169.** (42:19) *But you, go and bring the purchase of your grain: bring*, in place of: "carry"; for since the beasts on which it is carried are led, this too was called "leading."

**170.** (42:22) *Did I not speak to you, saying: 'do not harm the boy,' and you did not hear me?* It is to be noted that "hearing" is not said only of that which God hears.

**171.** (42:23) *But they did not know that Joseph heard. He hears*, in place of "he understands"; for one who does not understand the language certainly hears the words even with his ear. That expression is repeated when they recount to their father what had happened to them in Egypt, and what they had said to Joseph.

**172.** (42:32) *We are twelve brothers, the sons of our father; one is not, and the little one is with our father today in the land of Canaan*. There are many kinds of expression in these few words,

and that which I mentioned a little before: *we are twelve*, when they themselves say *one is not*; and: *we are sons of our father*, as though they could be sons of someone not their father. *And the little one is with our father today in the land of Canaan*—nor did they say "is," or anything of this kind.

Then it is to be noted, which seems especially necessary on account of the narratives of the evangelists, how, when the things that were said are recounted as having been said, they are not repeated in altogether the same way, while yet in the diversity of words nothing of the truth of the meaning is lost.

For as to their saying that it had been said to them by Joseph, *and trade in the land*, this is not found to have been said by him. Yet they said it without lying, because from the words he had said they recognized it in his will; for words are not necessary except to express the will and convey it, as far as we can, to the knowledge of the hearers.

**173.** (42:35) *And the bundle of each one's silver was in their sack*; it did not say: "in his sack," or: "in their sacks," but as though all had one sack.

**174.** (42:36) *All these things are come upon me*; that is: they burden me with misery.

**175.** (43:3) *But Judah said to him, saying*; it could be complete even if it did not have: *saying*.

**176.** (43:7) *Asking the man asked us*; such an expression is constant in the Scriptures: *asking the man asked us*; or: "by asking, he asked," and any like it.

**177.** (43:16) *For the men will eat loaves with me at midday*; was it only loaves? But the expression has embraced the rest from that which is preeminent. *For the men will eat loaves with me at midday*; in saying *at midday* it signifies the meal that is taken in the middle of the day, for this is midday.

**178.** (43:18) *That he may take us as slaves and our asses*; "slaves" is certainly not to be supplied; for where the Latin manuscripts have *slaves*, παῖδας is read in the Greek, which can in no way be asses. Therefore only *our asses* is to be supplied with *may take*.

**179.** (43:21) *We opened our sacks, and this silver of each one in his sack*; it did not add: "was found," or: "appeared," or: "was," or anything of this kind.

**180.** (43:23) *Gracious to you, do not fear*; in these words, in which it is said *gracious to you*, there are two words that are to be supplied, both "be" and "God." For the full form is: "may God be gracious to you," which is entirely most customary in the Greek.

**181.** (43:28) *Your servant our father is safe, he still lives*; here it is shown more expressly that by the name "servant" (or "boy") a slave is customarily signified; for in that old man this could not be a name of age.

**182.** (43:32) *For the Egyptians could not eat loaves with the Hebrews; for it is an abomination to the Egyptians*. That expression is frequent, so that by *loaves* all foods are understood.

**183.** (43:34) *Now Benjamin's portion was made greater than the portions of them all, fivefold compared to theirs*; since it had already said *than the portions of them all*, it could have left unsaid: *compared to theirs*.

**184.** (44:6-7) *And finding them, he spoke according to these words*; he could have said: "he spoke these words to them." Or is it perhaps not a manner of speech, but does it bear also on the meaning?

For it is one thing to say the very words, another to speak according to them, so that whatever other words may be said, the same meaning is held that was contained in those words; this is "according to them," even if not the very words.

But because there follows, as they answered: *why does my lord speak according to these words?*—who certainly ought to have said in the customary manner: "why does my lord speak these words?"—it is established to be a manner of speech.

**185.** (44:7) *Far be it from your servants to do according to this word*; they could have said: "far be it from us"; but it is a customary mark of honor in the Scriptures to speak thus, as though of others: *servants* is said for "slaves."

**186.** (44:9) *And we too will be slaves to our lord*. Here too the Greeks have the word, that is "boys," which Scripture so constantly puts for "slaves" that it is hard to find it not calling slaves by this name.

**187.** (44:34) *But how shall I go up to my father, when the boy is not with us? That I may not see the evils that will find my father!* The custom of speaking rather required that it be said: "that I may see the evils that will find my father"; that is: "how shall I go up that I may see?" And so by a novel manner of speaking he said what he said, putting *how* as if he had said: "not." For this is the customary order of these words: "I will not go up to my father, when the boy is not with us, that I may not see the evils that will find my father."

**188.** (45:2-3) When Joseph wept, as he was being recognized by his brothers, Scripture says: *and all the Egyptians heard, and it was heard in the house of Pharaoh*. Then follows what it was recounting: *and Joseph said to his brothers*; thus Scripture first said what happened afterward; for this came about by the swiftness of report, so that it became known to all the Egyptians; and afterward the narrative returned by a brief recapitulation to what was being said.

**189.** (45:16) *And the report was spread abroad in the house of Pharaoh, saying: "Joseph's brothers have come"; saying*, it put for "of those saying," "for the report of those saying was spread abroad: *Joseph's brothers have come*."

**190.** (46:2) *But he answered: 'what is it: saying?'*. The order is: "but he answered, saying: what is it?".

**191.** (46:4) God says to Jacob: *and I will go down with you into Egypt, and I will bring you up at the end*; thus the Greeks have it, which the Latin have as: *and I will bring you up at the end*.

**192.** (46:28) *But he sent Judah before him to Joseph, to meet him near the city of Heroes*; I do not know whether the name "of Heroes" is easily found in the Scriptures.

**193.** (46:31-32) In the words of Joseph, in which he says to his brothers: *going up I will announce to Pharaoh and say to him: "my brothers and my father's house, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me; the men are shepherds* (*for the men were keepers of cattle*)*, and they have brought their beasts and sheep and all that is theirs,"*

what is interposed—*for the men were keepers of cattle*—the writer interposed in his own person, and returns to the words of Joseph by adding: *and they have brought their beasts and all that is theirs*, so that the order in Joseph's words is: "*the men are shepherds, and they have brought their beasts and sheep and all that is theirs*."

**194.** (47:8) *And Pharaoh said to Jacob: 'how many are the years of the days of your life?'*, "are" is supplied.

**195.** (47:9) *Few and evil have been the days of the years of my life. Few*, is put for "few in number," for the days of one person's life cannot be shorter in the span of hours than those of others. But Jacob said this by comparison with the life of his forefathers; for no one now lives the hundred and thirty years that he was then living.

**196.** (47:12) *Grain according to the body*, that is: "according to the number of bodies"; for by *body* it signifies the number of bodies, and by the number of bodies the number of men.

**197.** (47:13) *But the famine had grown very severe, and the land of Egypt failed*; it put *land* for the men who were in the land.

**198.** (47:15) *And all the Egyptians came to Joseph, saying: 'give us loaves'*; by *loaves* it signifies grain, by an expression that puts the effect for the cause.

**199.** (47:20) *And the land became Pharaoh's*; it did not say: "of Pharaoh." Scripture loves to speak thus; as in the *Psalm* it is said: *and I kept your law; this became mine, because I sought out your justifications*[^13]; of the law of God it said: *this became mine*, that is: "for my benefit."

**200.** (47:22) *Only the land of the priests Joseph did not possess*; as if it had said: "except only the land of the priests, Joseph possessed all the land."

**201.** (47:26) *And Joseph set it for them as an ordinance, even to this present day, in the land of Egypt, that they should render fifths to Pharaoh*; from this it is understood that the name "Pharaoh" was one of royal power, in that it said: *even to this present day*. For the Egyptians could not render to him who then was, down to the day on which these things were being written, when he was no longer living.

**202.** (47:28) *And the days of Jacob were of the years of his life*; Scripture often says *days of years*, when it could say "years" in a single word.

**203.** (48:1) As to what is written: *it was announced to Joseph, that your father is troubled*; some manuscripts have *vexed*, some *in turmoil*, and one thing one and another another, as the Latin were able to translate what is written in Greek: ἐνοχλεῖται.

And *troubled* seems to be said more fittingly, because this is customarily said of those who, with the affliction of the body and death drawing near, are tossed about. And from this too a crowd is called ὄχλος, for a crowd is a disordered multitude—not like a people, which is called δῆμος, nor like the commons, which is called λαός, but like ὄχλος, which is called a crowd.

**204.** (48:16) Jacob, blessing his grandsons the sons of Joseph, says among other things: *and in these shall my name be invoked, and the name of my fathers*; whence it is to be noted that not only "hearing" but also "invocation" is sometimes said of what is not God's but men's.

**205.** (48:18) *For this is the firstborn*; it lacks "is," according to the Greek manuscripts.

**206.** (49:24) In the blessing of Joseph, Jacob says among other things: *thence who strengthened Israel*; it would be remarkable if "is" is not to be supplied, so that it be complete: "thence is he who strengthened Israel."

**207.** (50:2-3) As to what is written: *Joseph said to his servants the buriers, that they should bury his father*; the Latin language did not find how to render ἐνταφιαστάς: for they do not themselves bury, that is, commit to the earth the bodies of the dead, which in Greek is not ἐνταφιάσαι, but: θάψαι.

They, therefore, do what is rendered to bodies to be interred, whether by embalming or drying or wrapping and binding, in which work the care of the Egyptians especially excels. So when it says *they also buried*, we ought to understand "they tended." And when it says *forty days of burial*, they are to be taken as days of the tending itself. For he was not buried except where he had charged himself to be buried.

**208.** (50:4) *Speak in the ears of Pharaoh*: this is an expression customary in the Scriptures.

**209.** (50:6) *And Pharaoh said to Joseph: 'go down and bury your father'*; even if Pharaoh said through those powerful men through whom Joseph had sent word what they were to carry to Joseph, he certainly did not say it except to Joseph himself.

Hence there is that passage in the Gospel, where one of the evangelists says that a centurion came to the Lord and said to him: *my servant lies at home a paralytic*[^14]; but another, recounting the whole more carefully, mentions that he sent friends to the Lord[^15] to say this to him, in which friends he himself certainly came, whose will was present in them; whence it is said: *he who receives you receives me; and he who receives me receives him who sent me*[^16].

**210.** (50:10) *They mourned him with a great and strong mourning*: *they mourned a mourning*, not "they mourned with a mourning"; this is an expression not unknown in the Latin language, as it is said: "he served a servitude," "he soldiered a soldiery," and the like.

**211.** (50:15) *And by a requital he will requite us all the evils that we showed him*. From this expression the Apostle too says: *Alexander the coppersmith showed me many evils*[^17]: for *we showed*, or *he showed*, is said in place of "we did," or "he did."

**212.** (50:17) *Receive the iniquity of the servants of the God of your father*; it is a novel expression: *receive the iniquity*, for: "forgive," or: "remit," or: "forget"; but I think *receive* is said in such a way as if it were said: "receive it with an even mind," that is: "do not take it amiss."

**213.** (50:18) *And coming to him they said*; they did not come again, but what had been said is said again; Scripture is accustomed to do this.
