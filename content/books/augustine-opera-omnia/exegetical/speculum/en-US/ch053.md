# From the First Letter to Timothy

**387. 387.** [1 Tim 1]. The goal of the commandment is love from a pure heart, a good conscience, and unfeigned faith. Some have strayed from these and turned to empty talk, wanting to be teachers of the law but understanding neither what they say nor the things about which they make assertions. We know that the law is good if anyone uses it lawfully, knowing this: the law is laid down not for a righteous person, but for the unjust and insubordinate, the ungodly and sinners, the wicked and defiled, killers of fathers and killers of mothers, murderers, fornicators, men who lie with males, kidnappers, liars, and perjurers, and whatever else opposes sound teaching according to the Gospel of the blessed God's glory, which was entrusted to me [1:5–11].

**388. 388.** [1 Tim 2]. *And a little later:* Therefore, first of all I appeal for entreaties, prayers, petitions, and thanksgivings to be made for all people: for kings and all those in high positions, so that we may lead a quiet and tranquil life in all piety and chastity [2:1–2].  
*Again, a little later:* Therefore, I want men to pray in every place, lifting pure hands without anger or dispute. Likewise women should adorn themselves in orderly clothing, with modesty and sobriety, not with braided hair, gold, pearls, or expensive dress, but, as befits women professing piety, through good works. A woman should learn in silence with all subjection. I do not permit a woman to teach or exercise dominion over a man, but to remain in silence [2:8–12].

**389. 389.** [1 Tim 3]. *And several verses later:* Therefore, a bishop must be irreproachable, the husband of one wife, sober, prudent, orderly, chaste, hospitable, and a teacher; not given to wine or blows, but moderate; not quarrelsome or greedy, but presiding well over his own household, with children subject to him in all chastity. For if someone does not know how to preside over his own household, how will he care for God's Church? He must not be a new convert, lest, lifted up in pride, he fall into the devil's judgment. He must also have a good reputation among outsiders, so that he may not fall into reproach and the devil's snare. Deacons likewise must be chaste, not double-tongued, not devoted to much wine, not pursuing shameful gain, holding the mystery of faith in a pure conscience. These too should first be tested; then, being free of accusation, let them minister. Women likewise must be chaste, not slanderers, sober, and faithful in everything. Deacons should be husbands of one wife, presiding well over their children and their own households. For those who minister well acquire a good standing for themselves and great confidence in the faith that is in Christ Jesus [3:2–13].

**390. 390.** [1 Tim 4]. *And twelve verses later:* The Spirit expressly says that in the last times some will depart from faith, attending to spirits of error and the teachings of demons, through the hypocrisy of liars whose consciences are seared. They forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving by believers and those who have come to know the truth. For everything God created is good, and nothing received with thanksgiving is to be rejected, for it is sanctified through God's word and prayer. Setting these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, nourished by the words of faith and the good teaching you have followed. Avoid foolish old wives' tales. Train yourself in piety. For bodily training is useful for a little, but piety is useful for everything, having the promise of the present life and the future one. The saying is faithful and worthy of every acceptance. For this we labor and are cursed, because we have hoped in the living God, who is the savior of all people, especially believers. Command and teach these things. Let no one despise your youth, but be an example to believers in speech, conduct, love, faith, and chastity. Until I come, attend to reading, exhortation, and teaching. Do not neglect the grace within you, given you through prophecy with the laying on of the presbytery's hands. Meditate on these things and remain in them, so that your progress may be evident to everyone. Attend to yourself and to teaching; persevere in them, for by doing this you will save both yourself and those who hear you [4:1–16].

**391. 391.** [1 Tim 5]. Do not rebuke an older man, but entreat him as a father; younger men as brothers; older women as mothers; younger women as sisters, in all chastity. Honor widows who are truly widows. If a widow has children or grandchildren, they should first learn to govern their own household and make a return to their parents, for this is acceptable before God. But the one who is truly a widow and left alone hopes in God and perseveres in entreaties and prayers night and day. The one living in luxury is dead while alive. Command this too, so that they may be irreproachable. If anyone does not care for his own, especially members of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever. A widow should be chosen who is at least sixty years old, has been the wife of one husband, and has a reputation for good works: if she has raised children, received guests, washed the saints' feet, assisted those suffering tribulation, and pursued every good work. But refuse younger widows. For when they have grown self-indulgent in Christ, they want to marry, incurring condemnation because they have invalidated their first pledge of faith. At the same time they also learn to be idle, going around houses; and not only idle but talkative, saying what they ought not. Therefore, I want younger women to marry, bear children, manage households, and give the adversary no opportunity for abuse. For some have already turned back after Satan. If a believer has widows, let him provide for them, so that the Church may not be burdened and may provide sufficiently for those who are truly widows. Presbyters who preside well should be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in word and teaching. For Scripture says: You shall not muzzle an ox threshing grain[^1]; and: The worker deserves his wages[^2]. Do not accept an accusation against a presbyter except on the testimony of two or three witnesses. Reprove sinners before everyone, so that the others too may have fear. I testify before God, Christ Jesus, and the chosen angels that you must observe these things without prejudice, doing nothing by favoring one side. Do not lay hands on anyone hastily or share in others' sins. Keep yourself chaste. No longer drink only water, but use a little wine because of your stomach and frequent illnesses. Some people's sins are manifest, preceding them to judgment; others follow afterward. Likewise good deeds are manifest, and those that are otherwise cannot remain hidden [5:1–25].

**392. 392.** [1 Tim 6]. All slaves under the yoke should consider their masters worthy of every honor, so that the Lord's name and the teaching may not be blasphemed. Those with believing masters must not despise them because they are brothers, but serve them all the more because those sharing in the benefit are faithful and beloved. Teach and exhort these things. If anyone teaches otherwise and does not assent to the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching according to piety, he is proud, knowing nothing, but sick with questions and battles over words. From these arise envies, contentions, blasphemies, evil suspicions, and conflicts among people corrupted in mind and deprived of truth, who think piety is a means of gain. But piety with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world; undoubtedly we can take nothing away either. Having food and coverings, we are content with these. Those who want to become rich fall into temptations, the devil's snare, and many useless and harmful desires, which plunge people into ruin and destruction. For greed is the root of all evils. Some, seeking it, have wandered from the faith and entangled themselves in many sorrows. But you, O man of God, flee these things. Pursue righteousness, piety, faith, love, patience, and gentleness. Fight the good contest of faith; take hold of eternal life, to which you were called and made the good confession before many witnesses. I command you before God, who gives life to everything, and Christ Jesus, who bore witness to the good confession under Pontius Pilate, to keep the commandment spotless and irreproachable until the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ [6:1–14].  
*And a few verses later:* Command this age's rich not to be proud-minded or to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in God, who provides us abundantly with everything for enjoyment; to do good, become rich in good works, give readily, share, and store up for themselves a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of true life. O Timothy, guard the deposit, avoiding profane novelties of speech and the oppositions of falsely named knowledge, which some professed and fell away concerning the faith. Grace be with you. Amen [6:17–21].

[^1]: Cf. Deut 25:4
[^2]: Cf. Luke 10:7
