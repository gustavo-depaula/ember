# From the Letter to the Philippians

**376. 376.** [Phil 1]. For I will be put to shame in nothing, but with all confidence, as always, so now too Christ will be magnified in my body, whether through life or through death. For to me living is Christ, and dying is gain. But if living in the flesh means fruitful work, I do not know what to choose. For I am pressed between the two: I desire to be released and to be with Christ, which is far better; but remaining in the flesh is necessary for your sake [1:20–24].  
*And six verses later:* Only conduct yourselves worthily of Christ's Gospel, so that whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear about you that you stand in one spirit, united in soul, laboring together for the Gospel's faith. In nothing be terrified by adversaries: this is a cause of destruction for them but of salvation for you, and this is from God. For it has been granted you on Christ's behalf not only to believe in him but also to suffer for him, having the same contest that you saw in me and now hear of me [1:27–30].

**377. 377.** [Phil 2]. Therefore, if there is any consolation in Christ, any comfort of love, any fellowship of the spirit, any affection and compassion, fulfill my joy by having the same mind, the same love, united in soul and thought. Do nothing through contention or empty glory, but in humility regard one another as superior to yourselves. Each should consider not his own concerns but those of others. For have within you the disposition that was also in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in God's form, did not regard being equal to God as robbery, but emptied himself, taking a slave's form, becoming in human likeness, and being found in appearance as a man. He humbled himself, becoming obedient to death, even death on a cross. Therefore, God also exalted him and gave him the name above every name, so that at Jesus's name every knee should bend, of those in heaven, on earth, and below the earth, and every tongue confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father. Therefore, my dearest ones, as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence but much more now in my absence, work out your salvation with fear and trembling. For God is the one who works in you both willing and accomplishing for good will. Do everything without murmuring or hesitation, so that you may be blameless and innocent children of God, irreproachable in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation. Among them you shine like lights in the world, holding fast the word of life for my glory on Christ's day, because I did not run in vain or labor in vain. But even if I am offered upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I rejoice and share in the joy of you all. Likewise you too should rejoice and share my joy. I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I too may be encouraged by knowing your circumstances. For I have no one so united in soul with me who will be concerned for you with sincere affection. For everyone seeks his own concerns, not those of Jesus Christ. But recognize his proven character, for he served with me in the Gospel as a son with his father. Therefore, I hope to send him as soon as I see how things stand with me. I am confident in the Lord that I myself too will come to you soon. But I thought it necessary to send you Epaphroditus, my brother, coworker, and fellow soldier, your apostle and the servant of my need, because he longed for all of you and was distressed because you heard that he was sick. Indeed, he was sick to the point of death, but God had mercy on him, and not only on him but also on me, lest I have sorrow upon sorrow. Therefore, I sent him more quickly, so that on seeing him you may rejoice again and I may be free from sadness. Welcome him, then, with all joy in the Lord, and hold such people in honor, because for Christ's work he came close to death, surrendering his life to supply what was lacking in your service to me [2:1–30].

**378. 378.** [Phil 3]. *And a little later:* Brothers, I do not consider myself to have attained it. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and stretching myself toward what lies ahead, I pursue the goal, the prize of God's heavenly calling in Christ Jesus. Therefore, all of us who are perfect should think this way, and if you think differently in anything, God has revealed this to you too. Nevertheless, let us have the same mind with regard to what we have reached and remain in the same rule. Be imitators of me, brothers, and observe those who walk as you have our example. For many walk as enemies of Christ's Cross; I often told you of them, and now tell you even with tears. Their end is destruction, their god their belly, and their glory in their shame; they set their minds on earthly things. But our citizenship is in heaven [3:13–20].

**379. 379.** [Phil 4]. *And thirteen verses later:* Rejoice in the Lord always; again I say, rejoice. Let your moderation be known to everyone, for the Lord is near. Be anxious about nothing, but in every prayer and entreaty, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known before God. May God's peace, surpassing all understanding, guard your hearts and thoughts in Christ Jesus. For the rest, brothers, whatever is true, whatever modest, whatever just, whatever holy, whatever lovable, whatever of good repute, if there is any virtue or any praise, think on these things. What you learned, received, heard, and saw in me, do these things, and the God of peace will be with you. I rejoiced greatly in the Lord because at last your concern for me has flourished again, as indeed you were concerned, but you were occupied. I do not say this because of want, for I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I am. I know both how to be brought low and how to abound; everywhere and in everything I have been trained both to be satisfied and to hunger, both to abound and to suffer want. I can do everything in the one who strengthens me. Nevertheless, you did well to share in my tribulation. You Philippians yourselves know that at the Gospel's beginning, when I departed from Macedonia, no church shared with me in the account of giving and receiving except you alone. Even at Thessalonica you sent once and again for my needs. It is not that I seek a gift, but I seek abundant fruit to your account. I have everything and abound. I am filled, having received from Epaphroditus what you sent: a sweet fragrance, an acceptable sacrifice pleasing to the Lord [4:4–18].
