# From the Letter of James

**414. 414.** [Jas 1]. Consider it all joy, my brothers, when you fall into various trials, knowing that the testing of your faith produces patience. Patience has a perfect work, so that you may be perfect and complete, lacking in nothing. If any of you lacks wisdom, he should ask God, who gives abundantly to everyone without reproaching, and it will be given him. But he should ask in faith, wavering in nothing. For the one who wavers is like a sea wave moved and tossed by the wind. That man should not think he will receive anything from the Lord: a man divided in soul, unstable in all his ways. Let the lowly brother glory in his exaltation, but the rich in his humiliation, because he will pass away like the grass's flower. For the sun rose with its heat and dried the grass; its flower fell, and the beauty of its appearance perished[^1]. So the rich man too will wither in his journeys. Blessed is the man who endures trial, because after being tested he will receive the crown of life God promised to those who love him [1:2–12].  
*And a little later:* Therefore, do not be mistaken, dearest brothers. Every excellent gift and every perfect present comes from above, descending from the Father of lights [1:16–17].  
*And a few verses later:* You know, my beloved brothers: let everyone be quick to hear, slow to speak[^2], and slow to anger. For a man's anger does not accomplish God's righteousness. Therefore, casting away every impurity and abundance of malice, receive with gentleness the implanted word that can save your souls. Be doers of the word, not merely hearers, deceiving yourselves. For if anyone hears the word and does not do it, he will be compared to a man observing his natural face in a mirror: he looked at himself, went away, and immediately forgot what he was like. But the one who looks intently into the law of perfect freedom and remains in it, becoming not a forgetful hearer but a doer of work, will be blessed in his deed. If anyone thinks himself religious while not restraining his tongue but deceiving his heart, his religion is empty. Pure and immaculate religion before God the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their tribulation and keep oneself unstained by this age [1:19–27].

**415. 415.** [Jas 2]. My brothers, do not hold faith in our Lord Jesus Christ of glory with partiality toward persons [2:1].  
*And some verses later:* Listen, my dearest brothers: has not God chosen this world's poor to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom God promised to those who love him? But you have dishonored the poor person. Do not the rich oppress you through their power and drag you into court? Do they not blaspheme the good name invoked over you? If you fulfill the royal law according to the Scriptures, *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^3], you do well. But if you show partiality, you commit sin, convicted by the law as transgressors [2:5–9].  
*And a few verses later:* For judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy. But mercy rises above judgment [2:13].

**416. 416.** [Jas 3]. *And somewhat later:* Do not many of you become teachers, my brothers, knowing that you take on a greater judgment. For we all stumble in many things. If anyone does not stumble in speech, he is a perfect man, able also to direct the whole body with a bridle [3:1–2].  
*And in another place:* But no human being can tame the tongue, a restless evil full of deadly poison. With it we bless God the Father, and with it we curse people made according to God's likeness. From the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to be so [3:8–10].  
*And a few verses later:* Who among you is wise and disciplined? Let him show his work from good conduct in wisdom's gentleness. But if you have bitter jealousy and contentions in your hearts, do not boast and lie against the truth. This is not wisdom descending from above, but earthly, natural, and demonic. For where there is jealousy and contention, there is instability and every wicked work. But wisdom from above is first chaste, then peaceful, moderate, open to persuasion, assenting to good things, full of mercy and good fruits, judging without pretense. The fruit of righteousness is sown in peace for those who make peace [3:13–18].

**417. 417.** [Jas 4]. Where do wars and quarrels among you come from? Is it not from your desires that wage war in your members? [4:1].  
*Again, a few verses later:* You ask and do not receive because you ask wrongly, so as to spend it on your desires. Adulterers, do you not know that friendship with this world is hostile to God? Therefore, anyone wanting to be a friend of this age becomes God's enemy [4:3–4].  
*And a little later:* Therefore, he says: *God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble*[^4]. Therefore, be subject to God; resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, sinners, and purify your hearts, you who are divided in soul. Be wretched, mourn, and weep. Let your laughter turn to mourning and your joy to sorrow. Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you. Do not slander one another, brothers [4:6–11].  
*And a few verses later:* There is one lawgiver and judge, who can destroy and deliver. But who are you to judge your neighbor? Look now, you who say: Today or tomorrow we will go to that city, spend a year there, trade, and make a profit—you do not know what tomorrow will bring. For what is your life? It is a vapor appearing for a little while, then disappearing. Instead you should say: If the Lord wills; and: If we live, we will do this or that. But now you exult in your arrogance; all such exultation is evil. Therefore, to the one who knows how to do good and does not do it, it is sin [4:12–17].

**418. 418.** [Jas 5]. Come now, you rich, weep and wail over the miseries that will come upon you. Your riches have rotted, and moths have eaten your clothes. Your gold and silver have rusted, and their rust will testify against you and eat your flesh like fire. You have stored up wrath for yourselves in the last days. See, the wages of the workers who harvested your fields cry out, those whom you defrauded; their cry has entered the ears of the Lord of hosts. You feasted on earth and nourished your hearts in extravagance. On the day of slaughter you brought forward and killed the righteous man, and he did not resist you. Therefore, brothers, be patient until the Lord's coming [5:1–7].  
*And a little later:* You too must be patient and strengthen your hearts, because the Lord's coming will draw near. Do not groan against one another, brothers, so that you may not be judged [5:8–9].  
*And in another place:* Above all, my brothers, do not swear, either by heaven or earth or any other oath. Let your speech be: Yes, yes; no, no, so that you may not fall under judgment. Is anyone among you sad? Let him pray. Is he of a tranquil mind? Let him sing psalms. Is anyone among you sick? Let him summon the Church's presbyters, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the Lord's name. The prayer of faith will save the sick person, and the Lord will raise him up; if he is in sins, they will be forgiven him. Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be saved. For the righteous person's continual entreaty has great power [5:12–16].  
*And several verses later:* My brothers, if anyone among you strays from the truth and someone brings him back, he ought to know that the one who brings a sinner back from the error of his way will save his soul from death and cover a multitude of sins [5:19–20].

[^1]: Cf. Isa 40:6–7
[^2]: Cf. Prov 10:19; 17:27
[^3]: Lev 19:18
[^4]: Prov 3:34
