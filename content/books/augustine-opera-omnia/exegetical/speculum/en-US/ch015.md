*These things we have gathered from the book of Psalms, in which let each one inspect his own life, if he aspires to make progress. Here I admonish the reader that the things I have set down from the Psalms he should read consecutively, as though one psalm, passing over my own words, which I have interposed for this purpose, so that, if he wishes, he may inspect where what I have set down is written—that is, at which verse or in what place of the same psalm. For if, leaving these aside, he inspect by continuous reading the words of the Psalms alone, he will be much more pleasantly, and therefore more usefully, moved by the divine utterances. Now from the books of Solomon we will gather what seems necessary for this work: and first from* Proverbs*; which book, if it be well understood, will avail almost entirely for forming devout conduct. But the things that are obscure I pass over: let readers and lovers of spiritual letters have them, that they may be exercised by them; we have determined in this work to set down those things which, easily understood, bear upon the conduct of life. Although I may seem to many to have passed over the plainest things; and some things here are rather closed off, where they are thought to be clear. For what seems so clear, and what is done so ineptly, if you would take it in its proper sense, as that which is written there:* Keep away from another's water, and do not drink from another's fountain[^1]*? Or what avails it for correcting conduct, unless a deeper meaning be sought there, that which is said:* Poverty humbles a man, but the hands of the strong make him rich[^2]*? Since neither to be free of poverty, nor to be strong of hand, is in the power of good men, but has befallen many of the worst as well; and this proverb, ill understood, might provoke the poor and strong to think they ought to grow rich by robbery. And who would not laugh, if he thought it set down in its proper sense, what is read there:* For children are not born to the malignant[^3]*? All such things, then, we will leave aside, along with those in which something is urged at somewhat greater length, when what is urged and enjoined is not obscure, and that rather is to be set down in this* Mirror*, where those may inspect themselves who have already been persuaded to live well and laudably, but who, in order to do this, ask what they should desire and observe. These things, therefore, we believe should be set down from the* Proverbs *of Solomon.*

[^1]: Prov 9:18 (LXX)
[^2]: Prov 10:4
[^3]: Prov 24:20
