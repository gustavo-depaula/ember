# From Ecclesiasticus

**238. 238.** [Sir 1]. The fear of the Lord is the religiousness of knowledge. Religiousness shall keep and justify the heart, it shall give joy and gladness. It shall be well with him who fears the Lord, and in the days of his end he shall be blessed. The fullness of wisdom is to fear God, and fullness comes from her fruits [1:17-20].  
*And after two verses:* The crown of wisdom is the fear of the Lord [1:22].  
*And after four verses:* The root of wisdom is to fear God: for her branches are long-lived. In the treasures of wisdom is understanding, and the religiousness of knowledge: but wisdom is an abomination to sinners. The fear of the Lord drives out sin. For he who is without fear cannot be justified. For the wrath of his high spirit is his ruin. The wise man will hold his peace till the right time, and afterward joy shall be rendered. The good understanding will hide his words for a time, and the lips of many shall declare his understanding [1:25-30].  
If you desire wisdom, keep justice; and God will give her to you. For the fear of the Lord is wisdom and discipline; and what is well pleasing to him is faith and meekness, and he will fill up your treasures. Be not incredulous to the fear of the Lord, and do not approach him with a double heart. Be not a hypocrite in the sight of men, and let not your lips be a stumbling block to you. Watch over them, lest perhaps you fall, and bring dishonor upon your soul; and God reveal your hidden things, and cast you down in the midst of the assembly. Because you approached the Lord wickedly, and your heart is full of deceit and guile [1:33-40].

**239. 239.** [Sir 2]. Son, when you come to the service of God, stand in fear and justice, and prepare your soul for temptation; and humble your heart, and endure. Incline your ear, and receive the words of understanding, and do not hasten in the time of darkness. Wait for God's sustaining; join yourself to God, and endure, that your life may increase at the last. Take all that shall be brought upon you; and in your sorrow endure, and in your humiliation have patience. For gold and silver are tried in the fire; but acceptable men in the furnace of humiliation. Believe God, and he will recover you; and direct your way, and hope in him. Keep his fear, and grow old in it [2:1-6].  
You who fear the Lord, wait for his mercy; and turn not away from him, lest you fall. You who fear the Lord, believe him, and your reward shall not be made void. You who fear the Lord, hope in him, and mercy shall come to you for your delight. You who fear God, love him, and your hearts shall be enlightened. Consider, my children, the generations of men, and know: who ever hoped in the Lord and was confounded? who remained in his commandments and was forsaken? and who called upon him and was despised by him? For God is compassionate and merciful, and will forgive sins in the day of tribulation to all who seek him in truth [2:7-13].  
Woe to the double heart, and to wicked lips, and to hands that do evil, and to the sinner who walks on the earth two ways. Woe to those who are dissolute of heart, who do not believe God; therefore they shall not be protected by him. Woe to those who have lost endurance, who have forsaken the right ways, and turned aside into crooked ways. And what will you do when God begins to examine? Those who fear the Lord will not be incredulous to his word; and those who love him will keep his way. Those who fear the Lord will seek out what is well pleasing to him; and those who love him will be filled with his law. Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts, and will sanctify their souls in his sight. Those who fear the Lord keep his commandments, and will have patience until his visitation, saying: If we do not penance, we shall fall into the hands of God, and not into the hands of men. For according to his greatness, so also is his mercy with him [2:14-23].

**240. 240.** [Sir 3]. The children of wisdom are the assembly of the just; and their nation is obedience and love. Hear, beloved children, the judgment of your father, and so do that you may be saved [3:1-2].  
*And after two verses:* He who loves God will pray for his sins, and will refrain himself from them; and in the prayer of days he shall be heard. And as one who lays up treasure, so is he who honors his mother. He who honors his father shall have joy in his own children. He who honors his father shall live a longer life; and he who obeys his father shall be a comfort to his mother. He who fears God honors his parents; and will serve those who gave him birth as his masters. In deed, and in word, and in all patience, honor your father, that a blessing may come upon you from the Lord.  
*And after three verses:* Do not glory in the dishonor of your father [3:12].  
*And after three verses:* Son, support the old age of your father, and do not grieve him in his life. And if his understanding fails, have patience with him, and do not despise him in your strength [3:14-15].  
*And after four verses:* How ill-famed is he who forsakes his father! and he is cursed by God who angers his mother. Son, do your works in meekness, and you shall be loved above the glory of men. The greater you are, humble yourself in all things; and you shall find grace before God: for great is the power of God alone, and he is honored by the humble. Seek not the things that are too high for you, and search not into things above your strength: but the things that God has commanded you, think on them always; and be not curious in many of his works. For it is not necessary for you to see with your eyes those things that are hidden [3:18-23].  
*And after three verses:* For their own suspicion has led many astray, and held their minds in vanity. A hard heart shall fare ill at the last; and he who loves danger shall perish in it. A heart that goes two ways shall not have success; and the perverse of heart shall be scandalized in them. A wicked heart shall be laden with sorrows; and the sinner will add sin to sin [3:26-29].  
*And after two verses:* A wise heart is understood by wisdom; and a good ear will hear wisdom with all desire. A wise and understanding heart will abstain from sins, and shall have success in works of justice. Water quenches a flaming fire; and almsgiving resists sins: and God is the beholder of him who renders kindness, he remembers him afterward; and in the time of his fall he shall find a sure support [3:31-34].

**241. 241.** [Sir 4]. Son, do not defraud the poor of alms, and do not turn away your eyes from the poor man. Do not despise the hungry soul, and do not provoke the poor in his need. Do not afflict the heart of the needy, and do not put off giving to him who is in distress. Do not reject the petition of one in tribulation, and do not turn away your face from the needy. Do not turn away your eyes from the poor man because of anger, and do not leave those who seek you cause to curse you behind your back. For the prayer of him who curses you in the bitterness of his soul shall be heard. And he who made him will hear him. Make yourself affable to the assembly of the poor, and humble your soul to the elder, and bow your head to the great man. Bow down your ear to the poor without sadness, and pay your debt, and answer him peaceably in meekness. Deliver him who suffers wrong out of the hand of the proud; and bear it not heavily in your soul. In judging, be merciful, like a father to the orphans, and as a husband to their mother; and you shall be like an obedient son of the Most High, and he will have mercy on you more than a mother [4:1-11].  
Wisdom breathes life into her children, and receives those who seek her, and will go before them in the way of justice. And he who loves her loves life; and those who watch for her shall embrace her sweetness. Those who hold her fast shall inherit life, and where she enters, God will bless. Those who serve her shall be obedient to the Holy One; and those who love her, God loves. He who listens to her shall judge nations; and he who looks upon her shall remain secure. If he believes her, he shall inherit her, and his offspring shall be in confirmation. For in temptation she walks with him, and chooses him among the first. She will bring fear and dread and trial upon him, and will torment him in the tribulation of her teaching; until she tries him in his thoughts, and trusts his soul. And she will strengthen him, and lead him by a straight way to herself, and will make him glad; and will reveal her secrets to him, and will store up upon him knowledge and the understanding of justice. But if he goes astray, she will forsake him, and deliver him into the hands of his enemy [4:12-22].  
Son, observe the time, and avoid evil. For your soul be not ashamed to speak the truth. For there is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that brings glory and grace. Do not accept a person against your own face, nor a lie against your own soul. Do not be in awe of your neighbor in his fall, nor keep back a word in the time of salvation. Do not hide your wisdom in its beauty. For by the tongue wisdom and understanding are discerned, and knowledge and doctrine in words of truth, and steadfastness in works of justice. Do not contradict the truth in any way, and be ashamed of the lie of your own ignorance. Be not ashamed to confess your sins, and do not subject yourself to any man for sin. Do not resist the face of the mighty, and do not strive against the stream of the river. Strive for justice for your soul, and even unto death contend for justice; and God will fight your enemies for you. Do not be hasty in your tongue, and useless and slack in your works. Do not be like a lion in your house, overthrowing your household, and oppressing those who are subject to you. Let not your hand be stretched out to take, and shut when you should give [4:23-36].

**242. 242.** [Sir 5]. Do not set your heart on unjust possessions, and do not say: I have enough for my life. For it shall profit nothing in the time of vengeance and darkness. Do not follow the desire of your heart, and do not say: How have I been able? or who will subject me for my deeds

? For God, the avenger, will surely avenge. Do not say: I have sinned, and what sad thing has befallen me? For the Most High is a patient rewarder. Be not without fear concerning sin forgiven; and do not add sin upon sin; and do not say: The mercy of God is great; he will have mercy on the multitude of my sins. For mercy and wrath quickly draw near from him, and his wrath looks upon sinners. Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day. For his wrath shall come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you. Be not anxious for unjust riches: for they shall not profit you in the day of darkness and vengeance [5:1-10].  
Do not winnow yourself with every wind, and do not go into every way. For so is every sinner proved by a double tongue. Be steadfast in the way of God, and in the truth of your understanding and knowledge; and let the word of peace and justice follow you. Be meek to hear the word of God, that you may understand; and with wisdom give a true answer. If you have understanding, answer your neighbor: but if not, let your hand be upon your mouth; lest you be caught in an unwise word, and be confounded. Honor and glory are in the speech of the wise: but the tongue of the imprudent is his own undoing. Do not be called a whisperer in your life, and be not caught and confounded in your tongue. For upon the thief is confusion and repentance, and a most evil mark upon the double-tongued: but to the whisperer, hatred, and enmity, and reproach. Justify the small and the great alike [5:11-18].

**243. 243.** [Sir 6]. Do not become an enemy to your neighbor instead of a friend: for the evil man shall inherit reproach and shame, as shall every envious and double-tongued sinner. Do not exalt yourself in the thought of your soul, like a bull; lest perhaps your strength be dashed down through folly [6:1-2].  
*And after seven verses:* Let many be at peace with you; and let one of a thousand be your counselor. If you would acquire a friend, acquire him by testing; and do not lightly trust yourself to him. For there is a friend according to his own occasion, and he will not abide in the day of tribulation; and there is a friend who turns to enmity: and there is a friend who will lay bare hatred and strife and reproaches. And there is a friend who is a companion at table, and he will not abide in the day of need. A friend, if he remains steadfast, shall be to you as your equal, and shall deal confidently with your household. If he humbles himself before you, and hides himself from your face, you shall have a good friendship of one mind. Separate yourself from your enemies; and be on guard with your friends. A faithful friend is a strong protection: and he who has found him has found a treasure. To a faithful friend there is no comparison, and no weight of gold and silver is worthy of the goodness of his fidelity. A faithful friend is the medicine of life and immortality; and those who fear the Lord shall find him. He who fears God shall likewise have good friendship; for his friend shall be like himself. Son, from your youth receive instruction, and even to your gray hairs you shall find wisdom. Come to her as one who plows and sows, and wait for her good fruits. For in working at her you shall labor a little, and shall soon eat of her produce. How very harsh is wisdom to the unlearned, and the senseless man does not abide in her. She shall be to them like a touchstone for trial, and they shall not delay to cast her away [6:6-22].  
*And after two verses:* But to those by whom she is known, she remains even to the sight of God. Hear, my son, and receive the counsel of understanding, and do not reject my counsel. Put your foot into her fetters, and your neck into her chain. Bow down your shoulder, and bear her, and do not be grieved with her bonds. Come to her with all your soul, and keep her ways with all your strength. Search her out, and she shall be made known to you; and when you have laid hold of her, do not let her go: for in the end you shall find rest in her, and she shall be turned to your delight; and her fetters shall be a protection of strength to you, and supports of virtue, and her chain a robe of glory [6:23-30].  
*And after four verses:* Son, if you attend to me, you shall learn; and if you apply your soul, you shall be wise. And if you incline your ear, you shall receive instruction; and if you love to hear, you shall be wise. Stand in the multitude of prudent elders, and join yourself from your heart to their wisdom, that you may hear every discourse of God, and that the proverbs of praise may not escape you. And if you see a man of understanding, watch for him early, and let your foot wear out the steps of his doors. Have your thoughts upon the precepts of God, and be most assiduous in his commandments; and he himself will give you a heart, and the desire of wisdom shall be given to you [6:33-37].

**244. 244.** [Sir 7]. Do no evil, and it shall not lay hold of you. Depart from the unjust, and evils shall depart from you. Do not sow evils in the furrows of injustice, and you shall not reap them sevenfold. Do not seek from man rulership, nor from the king a seat of honor. Do not justify yourself before God; for he is the knower of the heart: and before the king do not wish to seem wise. Do not seek to become a judge, unless you have strength enough to break through iniquities; lest perhaps you fear the face of the mighty, and lay a stumbling block in your own integrity. Do not sin against the multitude of the city, nor cast yourself into the crowd: nor bind yourself with double sins; for not even in one shall you be guiltless. Do not be faint-hearted in your soul. Do not despise to pray and to give alms. Do not say: God will look upon the multitude of my gifts, and when I offer to the Most High God, he will accept my offerings. Do not mock a man in the bitterness of his soul: for there is one who humbles and exalts, even God. Do not plow up a lie against your brother, nor do the like to a friend. Do not wish to tell any lie at all: for the habit of it is not good. Do not be wordy in the multitude of the elders [7:1-15].  
*And after twelve verses:* Do not wrong the servant who labors in truth, nor the hired man who gives his life. Let a sensible servant be dear to you as your own soul: do not defraud him of liberty, nor leave him needy [7:22-23].  
*And after two verses:* Do you have children? instruct them, and bend them from their childhood. Do you have cattle? look after them. Do you have daughters? keep their bodies, and do not show a cheerful face toward them. Give your daughter in marriage, and you shall have done a great work; and give her to a sensible man. If you have a wife after your own soul, do not cast her off; and do not trust yourself wholeheartedly to one who is hateful. Honor your father, and do not forget the groanings of your mother. Remember that but for them you would not have been born; and repay them as they also have done for you. With all your soul fear God, and reverence his priests. With all your strength love God who made you; and do not forsake his ministers. Honor God with all your soul; and honor the priests [7:25-33].  
*And after six verses:* Stretch out your hand to the poor, that your blessing may be perfected. Let a gift be given in the sight of every living one, and do not withhold kindness even from the dead. Do not fail to comfort those who weep, and walk with those who mourn. Do not be slow to visit the sick: for by these things you shall be confirmed in love. In all your works remember your last end, and you shall never sin [7:36-40].

**245. 245.** [Sir 8]. Do not quarrel with a powerful man; lest perhaps you fall into his hands. Do not contend with a rich man, lest perhaps he bring a suit against you. For gold has destroyed many; and reached even to the heart of kings, and turned them. Do not quarrel with a man of tongue, and do not heap wood upon his fire [8:1-4].  
*And after two verses:* Do not despise a man who turns away from sin, nor reproach him: remember that we are all under correction. Do not despise a man in his old age: for we too grow old. Do not rejoice over your dead enemy, knowing that we all die, and do not wish to come into joy. Do not despise the discourse of wise elders, and be conversant with their proverbs. For from them you shall learn the doctrine of understanding, and how to serve the great without complaint. Let not the discourse of the elders escape you; for they too learned from their parents. For from them you shall learn understanding, and an answer in the time of need [8:6-12].  
*And after sixteen verses:* Do not take counsel with the foolish: for they cannot love anything but what pleases them. Do not take counsel before a stranger: for you do not know what it will bring forth [8:20-21].

**246. 246.** [Sir 9]. *And after two verses:* Do not be jealous of the wife of your bosom [9:1].  
*And after seven verses:* Do not desire a virgin, lest perhaps you be scandalized in her beauty. Do not give your soul to harlots in any way, lest you destroy yourself and your inheritance [9:5-6].  
*And after two verses:* Turn away your face from an adorned woman, and do not gaze upon another's beauty [9:8].  
*And after twelve verses:* Do not forsake an old friend: for the new will not be like him. New wine, a new friend; let it grow old, and you shall drink it with pleasure. Do not be jealous of the glory and riches of a sinner: for you do not know what his ruin shall be. Let not the wrong done by the unjust please you [9:14-17].  
*And after two verses:* Keep far from the man who has power to kill, and you shall not suspect the fear of death. And if you do come to him, commit no fault; lest perhaps he take away your life. And conduct your affairs with the wise and prudent. Let just men be your guests; and let your glory be in the fear of God [9:18-22].

**247. 247.** [Sir 10]. *And after fifty-three verses:* The offspring of men that fears God shall be honored: but that offspring of men shall be dishonored which transgresses the commandments of the Lord. In the midst of their brothers their ruler is in honor; and those who fear the Lord shall be in his eyes. The glory of the rich, of the honored, and of the poor, is the fear of God. Do not despise a just man who is poor, and do not magnify a sinful man who is rich. Great is the judge, and mighty in honor, and there is none greater than he who fears God. The free serve a sensible servant: a prudent and well-disciplined man will not murmur when reproved [10:23-28].  
*And after five verses:* Son, in meekness keep your soul, and give it honor according to its desert [10:31].

**248. 248.** [Sir 11]. *And after six verses:* The wisdom of the humble shall lift up his head, and shall make him sit in the midst of the great. Do not praise a man for his beauty, nor despise a man for his appearance. The bee is small among flying things, but her fruit has the chief place of sweetness. Do not ever glory in your clothing, nor be lifted up in the day of your honor [11:1-4].  
*And after six verses:* Before you inquire, do not blame anyone; and when you have inquired, reprove justly. Before you hear, do not answer a word; and do not interrupt in the midst of the elders. Do not contend about a matter that does not trouble you; and do not stand in the judgment of sinners. Son, let not your affairs be in many things; and if you are rich, you shall not be free from offense. For if you pursue, you shall not overtake; and you shall not escape, if you run ahead [11:7-10].  
*And after twenty verses:* Do not abide in the works of sinners: but trust in God, and stay in your place [11:22].  
*And after some verses:* Before death praise no man whatever [11:30].  
*And after one verse:* Do not bring every man into your house: for many are the snares of the deceitful. For as the bowels of the young belch out, and as the partridge is led into the snare, and as the roe into the trap, so is the heart of the proud; and like one who watches, seeing his neighbor's fall. For turning good into evil he lies in wait, and on the elect he will lay a blemish. From one spark a fire is increased, and by one deceitful man blood is increased. For a sinful man lies in wait for blood. Beware of the pestilent man, for he contrives evils: lest perhaps he bring upon you a derision forever. Admit a stranger to you, and he shall overthrow you in a turmoil, and shall estrange you from your own [11:31-36].

**249. 249.** [Sir 12]. If you do a kindness, know to whom you do it, and there shall be much thanks for your good deeds. Do good to the just man, and you shall find great recompense; and if not from him, surely from God. There is no good for those who are constant in evils, and who give no alms; for the Most High also hates sinners, and has had mercy on the penitent. Give to the merciful, and do not receive a sinner. Do good to the humble, and do not give to the wicked. Forbid bread to be given him, lest thereby he be mightier than you: for you shall find double evils in all the good you do to him; for the Most High also hates sinners, and will take vengeance on the wicked [12:1-4].  
*And after four verses:* Never trust your enemy: for as a brass pot rusts, so does his malice. And though he go humbly with downcast looks, set your mind to it, and beware of him. Do not set him beside you, nor let him sit at your right hand; lest, turning, he stand in your place: lest perhaps, turning into your place, he seek your seat, and at the last you acknowledge my words, and be pricked by my sayings. Who will pity an enchanter struck by a serpent, or any who come near to wild beasts? So is it with him who keeps company with an unjust man, and is wrapped up in his sins: for one hour he will abide with you; but if you decline, he will not bear it. With his lips an enemy speaks sweetly, but in his heart he lies in wait to overthrow you into a pit. With his eyes an enemy weeps; and if he find the time, he shall not be satisfied with blood. If evils come upon you, you shall find him there first. With his eyes an enemy weeps, and as if helping you he will dig under your feet. He will shake his head, and clap his hand; and whispering much, he will change his countenance [12:10-19].

**250. 250.** [Sir 13]. *And after sixty-one verses:* Good is the substance that has no sin on the conscience: and most wicked is the poverty in the mouth of the wicked man [13:30].  
*And after three verses:* Blessed is the man who has not slipped by a word out of his mouth, and has not been pricked with the sorrow of sin. Happy is he who has had no sadness of his mind, and has not fallen away from his hope. Substance is without reason to a covetous and grasping man; and to an envious man what good is gold? [14:1-3].

**251. 251.** [Sir 14]. *And after fourteen verses:* Son, if you have, do good to yourself, and offer to God worthy offerings. Remember that death does not delay, and the testament of hell, because it has been shown to you: for the testament of this world shall surely die. Before death do good to your friend, and according to your means stretch out your hand and give to the poor [14:11-13].  
*And after sixteen verses:* Blessed is the man who shall abide in wisdom, and who shall meditate on his own justice, and in his mind shall think on the all-seeing eye of God: who searches out her ways in his heart, and understands her secrets [14:22-23].

**252. 252.** [Sir 15]. *And after twenty-five verses:* Foolish men shall not lay hold of her; but sensible men shall meet her. Foolish men shall not see her; for she is far from pride and deceit. Lying men shall not be mindful of her; but truthful men are found in her, and shall have success even to the sight of God. Praise is not seemly in the mouth of a sinner; for wisdom comes from God. For the praise of wisdom shall stand by the wisdom of God; and shall abound in a faithful mouth, and the Ruler shall give it to him. Do not say: It is through God that she is wanting; for what he hates you should not do. Do not say: He has led me astray: for he has no need of wicked men. God hates every abomination of error, and it shall not be lovely to those who fear him. God made man from the beginning, and left him in the hand of his own counsel. He added his commandments and precepts: if you will keep them, they shall preserve you, and they shall make you always keep the faith that pleases him. He has set water and fire before you; stretch out your hand to whichever you will. Before man is life and death, good and evil; whichever pleases him shall be given to him. For the wisdom of God is great, and he is strong in power, seeing all men without ceasing. The eyes of God are upon those who fear him, and he knows every work of man. He has commanded no man to do wickedly; and he has given no man license to sin. For he does not desire a multitude of unfaithful and unprofitable children [15:7-22].

**253. 253.** [Sir 16]. And do not delight in wicked children, if they multiply: do not take pleasure in them, if the fear of God is not with them. Do not trust their life, and do not look to their labors. For better is one who fears God, than a thousand wicked children: and it is better to die without children, than to leave wicked children [16:1-4].  
*And after twenty-one verses:* Do not say: I shall be hidden from God, and who from on high will remember me? [16:16].

**254. 254.** [Sir 17]. *And a little later:* Their covenants are not hidden by their iniquity, and all their iniquities are in the sight of God. The almsgiving of a man is as a seal with him, and he will keep the kindness of a man as the apple of his eye: and afterward he shall rise up, and shall render them their reward, to every one upon their heads, and shall turn them down into the inner parts of the earth. But to the penitent he has given the way of justice, and has strengthened those who were failing to endure, and has appointed for them the lot of truth. Turn to God, and forsake your sins: pray before his face, and lessen your offenses. Return to God, and turn away from your injustice, and greatly hate the abomination [17:17-23].  
*And after nine verses:* How great is the mercy of God, and his forgiveness toward those who turn to him! For all things cannot be in men; since the son of man is not immortal [17:28-29].

**255. 255.** [Sir 18]. *And after thirty verses:* May he have mercy who receives the teaching of mercy, and those who hasten in his judgments. Son, in your good deeds make no complaint, and in any gift do not give the sadness of an evil word. Shall not the dew cool the heat? So a word is better than a gift. Behold, is not a word better than a good gift? but both are with a justified man. A fool will upbraid bitterly, and the gift of an undisciplined man makes the eyes to waste away. Before judgment prepare justice for yourself, and learn before you speak. Before sickness apply medicine, and before judgment examine yourself; and you shall find forgiveness in the sight of God. Before sickness humble yourself, and in the time of infirmity show your manner of life. Do not be hindered from praying always, and do not be afraid to be justified even unto death; for the reward of God abides forever. Before prayer prepare your soul, and do not be like a man who tempts God [18:14-23].  
*And after six verses:* A wise man will fear in everything, and in the days of sins will beware of sloth [18:28].  
*And after five verses:* Do not go after your lusts, and turn away from your own will. If you grant your soul her desires, she will make you a joy to your enemies. Do not take pleasure in crowds, nor delight in immoderate things [18:30-32].

**256. 256.** [Sir 19]. *And after five verses:* And he who despises small things shall fall little by little. Wine and women make wise men fall away, and shall rebuke the sensible [19:1-2].  
*And after four verses:* He who is light of heart to believe shall be lessened; and he who sins against his own soul shall be held in low esteem [19:4].  
*And after two verses:* And he who hates much talking quenches malice [19:5].  
*And after eight verses:* Have you heard a word against your neighbor? let it die within you, trusting that it will not burst you. From the face of a word the fool is in travail, like the groaning of a woman in childbirth. As an arrow that sticks in the flesh of the thigh, so is a word in the heart of a fool. Reprove a friend, lest perhaps he did not understand, and say: I did not do it; or if he did it, that he do it no more. Reprove your neighbor, lest perhaps he did not say it; and if he said it, that he say it no more. Reprove a friend; for often an offense is committed: and do not believe every word. There is one who slips with his tongue, but not from his heart. For who is there who has not offended with his tongue? Reprove your neighbor before you threaten him; and give place to the fear of the Most High: for all wisdom is the fear of God, and in it is the fear of God [19:10-18].  
*And after five verses:* Better is the man who lacks wisdom, and who lacks understanding in fear, than he who abounds in understanding, and transgresses the law of the Most High [19:21].

**257. 257.** [Sir 20]. *And after sixteen verses:* How much better it is to reprove than to be angry! [20:1].  
*And after twelve verses:* And he who unjustly assumes power to himself shall be hated [20:8].  
*And after eight verses:* A wise man by his words makes himself beloved [20:20-22].  
*And after thirteen verses:* The slip of a false tongue is like one who falls on the pavement: so does the downfall of the wicked come hastily. A graceless man is like a vain fable, and shall be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined. A parable shall be rejected from the mouth of a fool: for he does not speak it in its season [20:24-29].  
*And after two verses:* There is one who loses his soul through shame, and who through an imprudent person will lose it; and by accepting the person of another he will destroy himself. There is one who through shame makes promises to a friend, and gains him for an enemy without cause. A lie is a foul reproach in a man, and shall be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined. A thief is better than a man who is constantly lying: but both shall inherit destruction. The manners of lying men are without honor, and their confusion is with them without ceasing. A wise man by his words advances himself [20:24-29].  
*And after four verses:* Presents and gifts blind the eyes of judges, and like a muzzle in the mouth they turn away their reproofs. Hidden wisdom, and an unseen treasure, what profit is there in either? Better is the man who hides his folly, than he who hides his wisdom [20:31-33].

**258. 258.** [Sir 21]. Son, you have sinned; do not add to it again: but pray for your past sins, that they may be forgiven you. Flee from sins as from the face of a serpent; and if you come near them, they will lay hold of you. The teeth thereof are the teeth of a lion, slaying the souls of men: all iniquity is like a two-edged sword; for its wound there is no healing [21:1-4].  
*And after four verses:* He who hates correction is in the path of the sinner; and he who fears God will turn to his own heart [21:7].  
*And after five verses:* The way of sinners is paved with stones; and at their end are hell and darkness and punishments. He who keeps justice shall control his understanding. The fullness of the fear of God is wisdom and understanding [21:11-13].  
*And after seven verses:* A wise word, whatever the knowing man hears, he will praise, and add to himself: the wanton hears it, and it displeases him, and he casts it behind his back. The talk of a fool is like a burden on the road: but in the lips of the sensible grace shall be found. The mouth of the prudent is sought in the assembly, and they shall consider his words in their hearts [21:18-20].  
*And after two verses:* Instruction is to a fool as fetters on the feet, and like bonds on the right hand. A fool lifts up his voice in laughter: but a wise man will scarcely laugh quietly. Instruction is to the prudent as an ornament of gold, and like a bracelet on the right arm [21:22-24].  
*And after eight verses:* In the mouth of fools is their heart, and in the heart of the wise is their mouth. While the wicked man curses the devil, he curses his own soul. The whisperer shall defile his own soul, and shall be hated in all things; and he who abides with him shall be hateful: the silent and sensible man shall be honored [21:29-31].

**259. 259.** [Sir 22]. The sluggard is pelted with a lump of dung, and everyone will speak of his contemptibleness[^1].  
*And after four verses:* A sensible daughter is an inheritance to her husband; for she who brings shame becomes a disgrace to him who begot her. She who is brazen brings shame upon her father and her husband, and she will not be thought little of by the wicked; but she will be dishonored by both. Music in mourning is an ill-timed tale; but stripes and instruction are wisdom in every season. He who teaches a fool is like one who glues a potsherd together. He who tells a word to one who does not heed is like one who rouses a sleeper from a deep sleep. He who relates to a stupid man speaks with one who is asleep, and at the end he says: Who is this? Weep over the dead, for the light has failed him; and weep over the fool, for understanding has failed him. Weep but little over the dead, for he is at rest; but the worthless life of the worthless man is worse than the death of the fool. Mourning for the dead is seven days; but for the fool and the wicked, it is all the days of their life[^2].  
*And after twenty-one verses:* Whoever throws a stone at birds will scare them away; and so he who reproaches his friend will break up the friendship. Even if you have drawn a sword on a friend, do not despair; for there is a way back. If you have opened a grim mouth against a friend, do not be afraid; for there can be a reconciliation; except for reproach, and insult, and pride, and the disclosure of a secret, and a treacherous blow: in all these a friend will flee away[^3].  
*And after ten verses:* Who will set a guard over my mouth, and a sure seal upon my lips, that I may not fall by them, and that my tongue may not destroy me?[^4].

**260. 260.** [Sir 23]. O Lord, Father and ruler of my life, do not forsake me, and do not let me fall by them. Who will set scourges over my thoughts, and the discipline of wisdom over my heart, so that they may not spare me in my errors, and that my faults may not go unseen; lest my errors increase, and my faults be multiplied, and my sins abound, and I fall before my adversaries, and my enemy rejoice over me? O Lord, Father and God of my life, do not leave me to their devices. Do not give me haughtiness of eyes, and turn away all evil desire from me. Take from me the cravings of the belly, and let no lust of the flesh lay hold of me; and do not hand me over to a shameless and senseless soul. Hear, my children, the discipline of the mouth; and he who keeps it will not be caught by his lips, nor be brought to ruin by wicked deeds. The sinner is caught in his own vanity, and the proud man and the slanderer will be brought down by them. Do not accustom your mouth to swearing; for there are many falls in it. And let the naming of God not be continually in your mouth, nor mingle the names of the saints with your speech; for you will not be guiltless in these things. For just as a slave constantly questioned will not be without bruises, so everyone who swears and invokes the name will not be cleansed of sin. A man who swears much will be filled with iniquity, and the scourge will not depart from his house. And if he plays false, his fault will be upon him; and if he overlooks it, he sins doubly. And if he swears in vain, he will not be justified; for his house will be filled with retribution[^5].  
*And after four verses:* Do not accustom your mouth to coarse speech; for in it is the word of sin. Remember your father and your mother, for you sit in the midst of great men: lest God forget you in their sight, and you, made a fool by your habits, suffer reproach, and wish you had never been born, and curse the day of your birth. A man accustomed to abusive words will never be corrected all his days. Two kinds of men multiply sins, and a third brings on wrath and ruin. A hot soul, like a burning fire, will not be quenched until it has swallowed something; and a man who is wicked in the appetites of his flesh will not cease until he has kindled a fire[^6].  
*And after two verses:* Every man who transgresses against his own bed, despising his own soul, and saying: Who sees me? Darkness surrounds me, and the walls cover me, and no one looks at me; whom should I fear? The Most High will not remember my sins—and he does not understand that the eye of God sees all things, and that such a man's fear drives out the fear of God from himself, and that the eyes of men fear him. And he does not know that the eyes of the Lord are far brighter than the sun, watching over all the ways of men, and the depth of the abyss, and beholding the hearts of men in their hidden parts[^7].

**261. 261.** [Sir 25]. *And a little later:* The things you did not gather in your youth, how will you find them in your old age? How fitting is judgment to gray hairs, and for elders to know counsel! How fitting is wisdom to the aged, and understanding and counsel to those held in honor! Much experience is the crown of old men, and the fear of God is their glory[^8].  
*And after ten verses:* The fear of God is set above all things. Blessed is he to whom it is given to have the fear of God. To whom shall he be likened who fears him?[^9].  
*And after twenty verses:* Do not look upon a woman's beauty, and do not desire a woman for her beauty[^10].

**262. 262.** [Sir 26]. *And after twenty-eight verses:* A drunken woman is a great wrath; and her insolence and her shamelessness will not be hidden. A woman's unchastity is known by the haughtiness of her eyes and by her eyelids. Keep strict watch over a daughter who does not turn from you, lest, finding an opportunity, she misuse herself. Beware of every shamelessness of the eyes; and do not be surprised if she slights you[^11].  
*And after four verses:* The grace of a diligent woman will delight her husband, and her good sense will put fat on his bones. A sensible and silent woman is a gift of God: there is no exchange for a well-instructed soul. Grace upon grace is a holy and modest woman. And no weight is worthy of a self-controlled soul[^12].  
*And after twelve verses:* And him who passes over from justice to sin, God has prepared for the sword[^13].

**263. 263.** [Sir 27]. *And after four verses:* And he who seeks to grow rich turns away his eye[^14].  
*And after three verses:* Sin will be ground out. If you do not hold yourself steadfast in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overturned. As when a sieve is shaken, the refuse remains, so does a man's filth in his thinking. The furnace tests the potter's vessels, and the trial of tribulation tests just men. As the cultivation of a tree shows its fruit, so does a word show the thought of a man's heart. Do not praise a man before he has spoken; for this is the test of men. If you pursue justice, you will lay hold of it, and put it on like a robe of honor; and you will dwell with it, and it will protect you forever, and in the day of recognition you will find a firm foundation. Birds flock to their own kind, and truth returns to those who practice it. The lion lies in wait for its prey always; so do sins for those who work iniquity. A holy man abides in wisdom like the sun; but the fool changes like the moon. In the midst of senseless men keep a word for its time; but in the midst of thoughtful men be constant. The talk of sinners is hateful, and their laughter is in the wantonness of sin. Talk full of swearing makes the hair stand on end, and its irreverence makes one stop the ears. The shedding of blood is in the quarreling of the proud, and their cursing is grievous to hear. He who lays bare the secrets of a friend will lose his trust, and will not find a friend to his soul. Love your neighbor, and join yourself to him in good faith; but if you lay bare his secrets, do not pursue after him. For as a man who has destroyed his friend, so is he who destroys the friendship of his neighbor. And as one who lets a bird go from his hand, so have you let your neighbor go, and you will not catch him again. Do not pursue him, for he is far off. He has escaped like a gazelle from the snare; for his soul has been wounded; and you will be able to bind him no more. And for one cursed there is reconciliation; but to lay bare a friend's secrets is the despair of an unhappy soul. He who winks with his eyes plots evil things, and no one will cast him off. In your sight he will make his speech sweet, and will marvel at your words; but at the last he will twist his mouth, and in your words he will find occasion for offense. I have hated many things, but none like him; and the Lord will hate him[^15].  
*And after two verses:* He who digs a pit will fall into it; and he who sets a stone in his neighbor's way will stumble on it; and he who lays a snare for another will perish in it. To him who works wicked counsel, it will roll back upon himself; and he will not know whence it comes upon him. Mockery and reproach are for the proud, and vengeance will lie in wait for him like a lion. They will perish in the snare who delight in the fall of the just[^16].

**264. 264.** [Sir 28]. *And after three verses:* He who wishes to be avenged will find vengeance from God, and he will surely keep account of his sins. Forgive your neighbor who has wronged you, and then, when you pray, your sins will be loosed. One man cherishes anger against another, and does he seek healing from God? He has no mercy on a man like himself, and does he beg pardon for his own sins? He, being but flesh, nurses anger, and does he ask God for atonement? Who will entreat pardon for his sins? Remember your last days, and cease from enmity; for corruption and death are at hand among the commandments of God. Remember the fear of God, and be not angry with your neighbor. Remember the covenant of the Most High, and overlook your neighbor's ignorance. Refrain from strife, and you will lessen your sins. For an angry man kindles strife; and a sinful man troubles friends, and sows enmity in the midst of those who are at peace[^17].  
*And after four verses:* A hasty contention kindles a fire, and a hasty quarrel sheds blood, and a tongue that bears witness brings death. If you blow on a spark, it will burn like fire; and if you spit on it, it will be put out: and both come forth from the mouth. The whisperer and the double-tongued is accursed; for he has troubled many who were at peace. The third tongue has shaken many, and scattered them from nation to nation. It has destroyed the fortified cities of the rich, and overthrown the houses of great men. It has cut down the strength of peoples, and broken up strong nations. The third tongue has cast out brave women, and deprived them of the fruits of their labors. He who heeds it will have no rest, nor will he have a friend in whom to find rest[^18].  
*And after twenty-three verses:* Make a balance and weights for your words, and a proper bridle for your mouth; and take heed lest you slip with your tongue, and fall before enemies lying in wait for you, and your fall be incurable, unto death[^19].

**265. 265.** [Sir 29]. He who shows mercy lends to his neighbor; and he who is strong of hand keeps the commandments. Lend to your neighbor in the time of his need; and again, repay your neighbor in his own time. Keep your word, and deal faithfully with him; and at all times you will find what you need. Many have thought a loan to be a windfall, and have caused trouble to those who helped them[^20].  
*And after thirteen verses:* Yet be patient with one who is lowly in spirit, and do not keep him waiting for your alms. For the sake of the commandment help the poor man, and because of his need do not send him away empty. Lose your money for the sake of a brother and a friend, and do not hide it under a stone, to be lost. Lay up your treasure according to the precepts of the Most High; and it will profit you more than gold. Shut up alms in the heart of the poor; and it will pray for you against all evil: better than the shield of a mighty man, and better than a spear, it will fight against your enemy. A good man gives surety for his neighbor; and he who has lost his sense of shame abandons him. Do not forget the kindness of the one who gives surety; for he has given his own life for you. The sinner and the unclean flee from one who gives surety. The sinner counts a surety's goods as his own, and the man without gratitude abandons the one who set him free. A man stands surety for his neighbor, and when he has lost his self-respect, he is forsaken by him. Wicked suretyship has destroyed many who were prospering, and tossed them about like the waves of the sea. It has driven mighty men into exile, and they have wandered among foreign nations. The sinner who transgresses the commandments of the Lord will fall into a wicked suretyship[^21].  
*And after one verse:* Help your neighbor according to your means, and take heed to yourself that you do not fall[^22].

**266. 266.** [Sir 30]. *And after sixteen verses:* He who loves his son chastises him often, that he may rejoice in him at the last. He who instructs his son will have joy in him[^23].  
*And after ten verses:* An untamed horse turns out stubborn, and a son left to himself turns out headstrong. Pamper a son, and he will make you tremble; play with him, and he will grieve you. Do not laugh with him, lest you suffer, and at the last your teeth be set on edge. Do not give him liberty in his youth, and do not overlook his follies. Bend his neck in his youth, and beat his sides while he is a child; lest he grow stubborn, and disobey you, and your soul be grieved. Instruct your son, and labor over him, lest you be put to shame by his disgrace. Better is a poor man who is healthy and strong in body, than a rich man who is feeble and afflicted with disease. Health of soul in the holiness of justice is better than all gold and silver; and a sound body, than immense wealth. There is no wealth above the wealth of bodily health; and no delight above joy of heart. Better is death than a bitter life, and eternal rest than continual sickness. Good things hidden in a closed mouth are like dishes of food set around a grave[^24].  
*And after five verses:* Do not give your soul to sorrow, and do not afflict yourself in your own counsel. Gladness of heart is the life of a man, and an unfailing treasure of holiness; and the joy of a man is length of days. Have mercy on your own soul by pleasing God, and restrain yourself; and gather up your heart in his holiness, and drive sorrow far from you. For sorrow has killed many, and there is no profit in it. Envy and anger shorten one's days, and anxiety brings on old age before its time[^25].

**267. 267.** [Sir 31]. *And after ten verses:* He who loves gold will not be justified; and he who pursues gain will be filled by it. Many have been brought to ruin for the sake of gold, and their destruction lay before their eyes. It is a stumbling block to those who sacrifice to it: woe to those who run after it, and every imprudent man will perish by it. Blessed is the rich man who is found without blemish, and who has not gone after gold, nor put his hope in money and treasures. Who is he, that we may praise him? for he has done wonderful things in his life. Who has been tested by it, and been found perfect? It will be to him for everlasting glory. Who could transgress and did not transgress; and could do evil and did not do it: therefore his goods are made secure in the Lord, and the assembly of the saints will declare his almsgiving[^26].  
*And after ten verses:* Use as a thrifty man the things that are set before you, lest, if you eat much, you be hated. Be the first to leave off, for discipline's sake, and do not be excessive, lest you give offense. And if you sit in the midst of many, do not reach out your hand before them, nor be the first to ask for drink. How sufficient a little wine is for a well-bred man![^27].  
*And after sixteen verses:* Do not provoke those who are devoted to wine; for wine has destroyed many[^28].

**268. 268.** [Sir 32]. *And after twenty-five verses:* Speak, you who are older, for it is fitting for you; but with sound knowledge, and do not hinder the music. Where there is listening, do not pour out talk; and do not unseasonably display your wisdom[^29].  
*And after five verses:* Young man, speak in your own cause scarcely when it is necessary. If you are asked twice, let your answer be brief. In many things act as if you knew nothing, and listen in silence, and at the same time inquire. In the midst of great men do not presume to speak; and where there are elders, do not talk much. Lightning goes before the hailstorm, and grace goes before modesty, and for your reverence good favor will come to you. But hasten home before others, and there withdraw, and there amuse yourself, and indulge your fancies, yet not in sins or proud words. And above all these things bless God, who made you, and gives you to drink your fill of all his good things. He who fears God will receive his instruction; and those who watch for him will find his blessing. He who seeks the law will be filled with it; and the hypocrite will be tripped up by it. Those who fear the Lord will find just judgment, and will kindle justice like a light. A sinful man will shun reproof, and will find an excuse to suit his own will. A man of counsel will not neglect understanding[^30].  
*And after four verses:* My son, do nothing without counsel; and after you have done it you will not regret it[^31].  
*And after six verses:* He who trusts in God will attend to the commandments; and he who has confidence in him will not be brought low[^32].

**269. 269.** [Sir 33]. No evils will meet the man who fears the Lord; but in temptation God will preserve him, and deliver him from evils. A wise man does not hate the commandments and the precepts of justice; and he will not be dashed about like a ship in a storm. A man of sense trusts in the law of God, and the law is faithful to him. He who clears up a question will prepare a word[^33].

**270. 270.** [Sir 34]. *And a little later:* Vain hope and falsehood are for the senseless man; and dreams lift up the imprudent. As one who grasps at a shadow, and chases the wind, so is he who heeds lying visions. This is like that: the vision of dreams is the likeness of a man before a man's face. What can be made clean by the unclean? and what truth can be told by the liar? Divination of error, and lying omens, and the dreams of evildoers, are vanity. And like a woman in labor, your heart suffers fantasies, unless a visitation is sent from the Most High: do not give your heart to them. For dreams have led many astray, and those who hoped in them have fallen. Without falsehood the law will be fulfilled, and wisdom will be perfected in a faithful mouth[^34].  
*And after five verses:* I have seen many things in my travels, and many customs of words. Sometimes I have been in peril of death because of these things, and I have been delivered by the grace of God. The spirit of those who fear God is sought out, and in his regard they will be blessed. For their hope is in him who saves them; and the eyes of God are upon those who love him. He who fears the Lord will tremble at nothing, and will not be afraid; for he himself is his hope. Blessed is the soul of one who fears the Lord. To whom does he look? and who is his strength? The eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear him: he is a protector of power, a support of strength, a shelter from the heat and a shade from the noonday; a guard against stumbling and a help against falling; he lifts up the soul and gives light to the eyes; he gives health, and life, and blessing. The offering of one who sacrifices from what is unjustly gained is defiled, and the mockeries of the unjust are not well pleasing. The Lord is for those alone who wait for him in the way of truth and justice. The Most High does not approve the gifts of the wicked, nor does he regard the offerings of the wicked, nor will he be appeased for their sins by the multitude of their sacrifices. He who offers a sacrifice from the substance of the poor is like one who slays a son before his father's eyes. The bread of the needy is the life of the poor; he who defrauds him of it is a man of blood. He who takes away the bread earned by sweat is like one who kills his neighbor. He who sheds blood, and he who defrauds the laborer of his wages, are brothers. When one builds and another tears down, what do they gain but toil? When one prays and another curses, whose voice will God hear? He who washes after touching a dead body, and touches it again, what does his washing profit him? So is a man who fasts for his sins, and then does the same things again: what does he gain by humbling himself? who will hear his prayer?[^35].

**271. 271.** [Sir 35]. He who keeps the law multiplies offerings. It is a saving sacrifice to heed the commandments, and to depart from all iniquity; and it is an atoning offering to turn away from injustice, and a supplication for sins to draw back from injustice[^36].  
*And after a few verses:* You shall not appear before God empty. For all these things are done because of the commandments of the Lord. The offering of the just man makes the altar rich, and is a sweet odor in the sight of the Most High. The sacrifice of the just is accepted, and the Lord will not forget the remembrance of it. Give glory to God with a good heart, and do not diminish the firstfruits of your hands. In every gift make your face cheerful, and sanctify your tithes with gladness. Give to the Most High according to what he has given you, and with a generous eye do what your hands have found; for the Lord is a rewarder, and he will repay you sevenfold. Do not offer corrupt gifts; for he will not accept them. Do not look to an unjust sacrifice; for the Lord is judge, and with him there is no respect of persons. The Lord will not show partiality against a poor man, and he will hear the prayer of one who is wronged. He will not despise the prayers of the orphan, nor of the widow, when she pours out her tale of groaning. Does not the tear run down the cheek, and the cry rise up from the cheek because of what makes it fall?[^37].  
*And after four verses:* The prayer of one who humbles himself will pierce the clouds[^38].

**272. 272.** [Sir 37]. *And after a while:* Be constant with a holy man, whomever you know to keep the fear of God, whose soul is according to your own soul[^39].  
*And after five verses:* And in all these things entreat the Most High, that he may direct your way in truth[^40].  
*And after twenty-one verses:* My son, test your soul in your life; and if it is wicked, do not give it power. For not all things are good for all, and not every kind pleases every soul. Do not be greedy in any feasting, and do not pour yourself out upon every food[^41].

**273. 273.** [Sir 38]. *And after four verses:* Honor the physician for the need you have of him; for the Most High created him. For healing is from God, and he will receive a gift from the king. The skill of the physician will lift up his head, and he will be praised in the sight of great men. The Most High created medicines out of the earth, and a prudent man will not despise them. Was not the bitter water made sweet by wood? The power of these things has come to the knowledge of men, and the Most High has given knowledge to men, that he may be honored in his wonders. By these he heals and soothes their pain[^42].  
*And after four verses:* My son, in your sickness do not despair; but pray to the Lord, and he will heal you. Turn away from sin, and order your hands aright, and cleanse your heart from every fault[^43].  
*And after one verse:* And make your offering rich, and give place to the physician; for the Lord created him: and let him not depart from you, for his works are necessary. For there is a time when you fall into their hands; and they will entreat the Lord, that he may direct their care and their healing toward the patient's recovery[^44].  
*And after one verse:* My son, shed tears over the dead, and begin to lament as one who has suffered grievously. And according to custom cover his body, and do not neglect his burial. For fear of slander, mourn his death bitterly for one day, and be comforted, for your sorrow; and make your mourning according to what he deserved, for a day or two, to avoid reproach. For sorrow hastens death, and overpowers strength, and grief of heart bows down the neck[^45].  
*And after one verse:* Do not give your heart over to sorrow[^46].  
*And after nine verses:* The wisdom of the scribe comes in his time of leisure, and he who is occupied with little will gain wisdom[^47].

**274. 274.** [Sir 39]. *And a little later:* Say with one voice: Hearken to me, you holy fruits of the waters, and bring forth fruit like the rose planted by the stream. Give a sweet fragrance like Lebanon. Blossom like the lily, give forth a fragrance, and put out leaves for grace, and join in the song, and bless the Lord in his works. Ascribe greatness to his name, and acknowledge him with the voice of your lips, with songs of the lips and with harps; and thus you shall say in your acknowledgment: All the works of the Lord are exceedingly good. At his word the water stood like a heap, and at the word of his mouth like reservoirs of water. For at his command good favor is shown, and there is no failing in his salvation. The works of all flesh are before him, and nothing is hidden from his eyes. From age to age he looks down; and nothing is wonderful in his sight. There is no saying: What is this? or what is that? for all things will be sought out in their time[^48].

**275. 275.** [Sir 40]. *And a little later:* Grace is like a paradise in blessings, and mercy abides forever[^49].  
*And after twelve verses:* Brothers are a help in the time of tribulation; but mercy will deliver more than they[^50].

**276. 276.** [Sir 41]. *And a little later:* Hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure—what use is there in either? Better is a man who hides his folly, than a man who hides his wisdom[^51].

**277. 277.** [Sir 43]. *And somewhat later:* When you bless the Lord, exalt him as much as you can; for he is greater than all praise. When you exalt him, gather up your strength[^52].

**278. 278.** [Sir 51]. *And after much else:* Draw near to me, you who are untaught, and gather yourselves into the house of instruction. Why are you still slow in these things? and what do you still say about them? Your souls thirst exceedingly. I opened my mouth and spoke: Buy wisdom for yourselves without silver, and bend your neck to her yoke, and let your soul receive instruction. For she is near at hand to find. See with your own eyes that I have labored but little, and have found much rest for myself. Take up instruction at the cost of much silver, and possess abundant gold in her. Let your soul rejoice in his mercy, and you will not be put to shame in his praise. Do your work before the time, and he will give you your reward in his own time[^53].
