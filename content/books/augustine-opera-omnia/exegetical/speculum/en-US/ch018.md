# From Ecclesiastes

**140. 140.** [Eccl 2]. *Take only a little.* And I saw that wisdom excelled folly as much as light differs from darkness. The eyes of the wise are in his head: the fool walks in darkness [2:13-14].

**141. 141.** [Eccl 4]. *And after a little:* Guard your foot when you enter the house of God. For obedience is much better than the sacrifices of fools, who do not know what evil they do [4:17].

**142. 142.** [Eccl 5]. Do not speak anything rashly, nor let your heart be swift to utter a word before God. For God is in heaven, and you upon earth; therefore let your words be few. Many cares follow dreams, and in many words folly is found. If you have vowed anything to God, do not delay to pay it; for an unfaithful and foolish promise displeases him: but whatever you have vowed, pay it: and it is much better not to vow, than after a vow not to pay what was promised. Do not give your mouth to make your flesh to sin, nor say before the angel: There is no providence; lest perhaps God, angry at your word, scatter all the works of your hands. Where there are many dreams, there are very many vanities and innumerable words: but do you fear God [5:1-6].  
*And after six verses:* The covetous man shall not be satisfied with money; and he who loves riches shall reap no fruit from them [5:9].

**143. 143.** [Eccl 7]. *And after a little:* It is better to go to the house of mourning, than to the house of feasting: for in the former all men are admonished of the end of all, and the living thinks of what is to come. Anger is better than laughter: because by the sadness of the countenance the mind of the offender is corrected. The heart of the wise is where there is sadness; and the heart of fools where there is gladness. It is better to be reproved by a wise man, than to be deceived by the flattery of fools: for as the crackling of burning thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of a fool [7:3-7].  
*And after four verses:* The patient man is better than the arrogant. Do not be swift to anger; for anger rests in the bosom of a fool. Do not say: What do you think is the cause that the former times were better than now? for such a question is foolish [7:9-11].  
*And after three verses:* But learning and wisdom have this advantage, that they give life to their possessor [7:13].

**144. 144.** [Eccl 8]. *And somewhat later:* I observe the mouth of the king, and the precepts of the oath of God. Do not hasten to depart from his face, nor continue in an evil work [8:2-3].  
*And a little later:* Because a sinner, *he says,* does evil a hundred times, and is borne with through patience, I have known that it shall be well with those who fear God, who reverence his face. Let it not be well with the wicked, nor let his days be prolonged; but let them pass as a shadow, who do not fear the face of God [8:12-13].

**145. 145.** [Eccl 9]. *And after a little:* I said that wisdom is better than strength [9:16].  
*And after four verses:* Wisdom is better than weapons of war: and he who sins in one thing shall lose many good things [9:18].

**146. 146.** [Eccl 10]. *And after two verses:* Wisdom and a little glory are more precious than folly for a time [10:1].  
*And after thirteen verses:* He who digs a pit shall fall into it; and he who breaks down a hedge, a serpent shall bite him. He who removes stones shall be hurt by them; and he who splits wood shall be wounded by it. If the iron be blunt, and this not as before, but be dulled; it is sharpened with much labor, and after diligence wisdom follows. If a serpent bite in silence, he who slanders secretly is no better than it. The words of a wise man's mouth are grace, and the lips of a fool shall throw him down headlong [10:10-12].

**147. 147.** [Eccl 11]. *And a little later:* Rejoice therefore, young man, in your youth; and let your heart be in good in the days of your youth, and walk in the ways of your heart, and in the sight of your eyes; and know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment. Remove anger from your heart, put away malice from your flesh. For youth and pleasure are vain things [11:9-10].

**148. 148.** [Eccl 12]. Remember your Creator in the days of your youth [12:1].  
*And after thirty-three verses:* Fear God, and keep his commandments [12:13].

*There remains that book of Solomon whose title is* The Song of Songs*. But what can we transfer from it into this work, since the whole of it commends the holy loves of Christ and the Church in figurative speech, and proclaims them with prophetic loftiness? Except that in it, although it is most difficult to understand, we can nevertheless easily perceive how greatly that divine and divinely inspired love is to be sought after, and how highly to be valued: since it is not said there once only, but is repeated again a second and a third time, now in one place, now in another.*
