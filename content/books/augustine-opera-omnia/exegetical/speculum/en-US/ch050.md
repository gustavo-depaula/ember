# From the First Letter to the Thessalonians

**380. 380.** [1 Thess 2]. You yourselves know, brothers, that our coming to you was not fruitless. After suffering much and being insulted at Philippi, as you know, we had confidence in our God to speak God's Gospel among you with much diligence. For our exhortation comes neither from error nor impurity nor deceit. Instead, just as we were approved by God to be entrusted with the Gospel, so we speak, seeking to please not people but God, who tests our hearts. For we never used flattering speech, as you know, or a pretext for greed—God is witness—or sought glory from people, either from you or from others. Although we could have been a burden to you as Christ's Apostles, we became little ones among you, as a nursing mother cherishes her children. Longing for you in this way, we eagerly wished to share with you not only God's Gospel but also our own souls, because you had become very dear to us. For you remember, brothers, our labor and toil: working night and day so as not to burden any of you, we preached God's Gospel among you. You and God are witnesses of how holy, just, and blameless we were toward you who believed. You know how, like a father with his children, we entreated and consoled each of you, testifying that you should walk worthily of God, who called you into his kingdom and glory. Therefore, we too give thanks to God without interruption, because when you received from us the word of God's message, you received it not as a human word but as it truly is, God's word, who works in you who have believed. For you became imitators, brothers, of God's churches in Judea in Christ Jesus, because you too suffered the same things from your fellow countrymen as they did from the Jews [2:1–14].

**381. 381.** [1 Thess 4]. *And in another place:* For the rest, then, brothers, we ask and appeal to you in the Lord Jesus: just as you received from us how you ought to walk and please God, so walk, so that you may abound still more. For you know what precepts we gave you through the Lord Jesus. This is God's will, your sanctification: that you abstain from fornication; that each of you know how to possess his own vessel in sanctification and honor, not in the passion of desire like the gentiles who do not know God; and that no one overstep or defraud his brother in business, because God is the avenger of all these things, as we previously told and testified to you. For God called us not to impurity but to sanctification. Therefore, anyone who rejects these things rejects not a human being but God, who also gave his Holy Spirit within you. Concerning brotherly love, we have no need to write to you, for you yourselves learned from God to love one another. Indeed, you practice it toward all the brothers in the whole of Macedonia. But we ask you, brothers, to abound still more, and to strive to be quiet, attend to your own business, and work with your hands as we commanded you, so that you may walk honorably toward outsiders and desire nothing from anyone. We do not want you to be unaware, brothers, about those who have fallen asleep, so that you may not be saddened like the others who have no hope [4:1–13].

**382. 382.** [1 Thess 5]. *And some verses later:* Therefore, let us not sleep like the others, but keep watch and be sober. For those who sleep sleep at night, and those who are drunk are drunk at night. But we who belong to the day should be sober, clothed in the breastplate of faith and love, and with the hope of salvation as a helmet. For God has appointed us not to wrath but to the obtaining of salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ, who died for us so that whether we watch or sleep, we may live together with him. Therefore, console one another and build each other up, as you already do. We ask you, brothers, to recognize those who labor among you, preside over you in the Lord, and admonish you, and to hold them abundantly in love because of their work. Have peace with them. We ask you, brothers: correct the disorderly, console the fainthearted, support the weak, and be patient toward everyone. See that no one repays anyone evil for evil, but always pursue what is good toward one another and toward everyone. Rejoice always; pray without interruption; give thanks in everything. For this is God's will in Christ Jesus for all of you. Do not extinguish the spirit. Do not despise prophecies. But test everything; hold on to what is good. Abstain from every evil appearance [5:6–22].
