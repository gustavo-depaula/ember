# From the Book of Wisdom

**234. 234.** [Wis 1]. Love justice, you who judge the earth. Think of the Lord in goodness, and seek him in simplicity of heart. For he is found by those who do not tempt him; and he appears to those who have faith in him. For perverse thoughts separate from God: but his power, when it is tried, reproves the foolish. For wisdom will not enter into a malicious soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sins. For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee from the deceitful; and will withdraw himself from thoughts that are without understanding; and he shall be driven out by iniquity when it comes upon him. For the spirit of wisdom is kind, and will not free the evil-speaker from his lips; for God is witness of his reins, and the true searcher of his heart, and the hearer of his tongue. For the spirit of the Lord has filled the whole world; and that which contains all things has knowledge of the voice. Therefore he who speaks unjust things cannot be hidden, nor shall the chastising judgment pass him by. For inquiry shall be made into the thoughts of the wicked: and the hearing of his words shall come to the Lord, for the chastising of his iniquities. For the ear of jealousy hears all things, and the tumult of murmurings shall not be hidden. Keep yourselves, then, from murmuring, which profits nothing; and refrain your tongue from detraction: for an obscure word shall not go for nothing. But the mouth that lies slays the soul [1:1-11].

**235. 235.** [Wis 3]. *And a little later:* Those who trust in him shall understand the truth; and the faithful in love shall rest in him: for grace and peace are for his elect. But the wicked shall have chastisement according to what they have thought, who neglected justice, and departed from the Lord. For he who casts away wisdom and discipline is unhappy [3:9-11].  
*And after six verses:* For blessed is the barren and undefiled woman, who has not known a bed in sin; she shall have fruit in the visitation of souls: and the eunuch who has not wrought iniquity with his hands, nor thought wicked things against the Lord [3:13-14].

**236. 236.** [Wis 6]. *And after a little:* Hear, then, you kings, and understand; learn, you judges of the ends of the earth. Give ear, you who rule the multitude, and please yourselves in crowds of nations. For power is given you by the Lord, and strength by the Most High, who will examine your works, and search out your thoughts. For when you were ministers of his kingdom, you did not judge rightly, nor keep the law of justice, nor walk according to the will of God. Horribly and swiftly will he appear to you; for a most severe judgment shall be for those who govern. For to the little one mercy is granted; but the mighty shall be mightily tormented. For God will not except any man's person, nor will he stand in awe of any man's greatness; for he made the little and the great, and he has equal care for all. But to the stronger a stronger torment is at hand. To you, then, O wicked kings, are these my words, that you may learn wisdom, and not fall away. For those who have kept just things justly shall be justified; and those who have learned these things shall find what to answer. Covet, then, my words, love them, and you shall have discipline. For wisdom is bright, and never fades; and is easily seen by those who love her; and is found by those who seek her. She prevents those who covet her, so that she first shows herself to them. He who watches for her at daybreak shall not labor: for he shall find her sitting at his doors. For to think about her is perfect understanding; and he who watches for her shall quickly be secure. For she goes about seeking those who are worthy of her, and shows herself to them cheerfully in the ways, and meets them with all providence. For the beginning of her is the most true desire of discipline. The care, then, of discipline is love; and love is the keeping of her laws: and the keeping of her laws is the confirmation of incorruption; and incorruption brings near to God. The desire, then, of wisdom leads to the everlasting kingdom. If then you delight in thrones and scepters, O kings of the people, love wisdom, that you may reign forever [6:2-22].

**237. 237.** [Wis 8]. *And a little later:* And if anyone loves justice, her labors have great virtues. For she teaches sobriety and wisdom, and justice and virtue, than which nothing is more useful in the life of men [8:7].

*Now from the book that is called Ecclesiasticus the things that seem fitting must be set down. About which let me be regarded as having said here the same things I premised about the book of Proverbs; although in this Ecclesiasticus I have found more things necessary for this work.*
