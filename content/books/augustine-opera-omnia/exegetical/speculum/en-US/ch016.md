# From Proverbs

**109. 109.** [Prov 1]. The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge. Fools despise wisdom and instruction. Hear, my son, the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother [1:7-8].  
*And after fourteen verses, when he was speaking of murderers:* These very ones, *he says,* lie in wait against their own blood, and contrive frauds against their own souls. So are the paths of every covetous man: they seize the souls of those who possess them [1:18-19].  
*Likewise after four verses:* How long, you little ones, will you love childishness, and fools desire the things that are harmful to them, and the imprudent hate knowledge? [1:22].  
*And after eleven verses:* Then they will call upon me, and I will not hear; they will rise early, and will not find me: because they have hated discipline, and have not received the fear of the Lord, nor consented to my counsel, but have slighted all my reproof. They shall therefore eat the fruits of their own way, and shall be filled with their own counsels. The turning away of the little ones shall slay them, and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them. But he who hears me shall rest without terror, and shall enjoy abundance, the fear of evils being taken away [1:28-33].

**110. 110.** [Prov 2]. *And after four verses:* For if you call upon wisdom, and incline your heart to prudence; if you seek it like money, and dig for it like treasure: then you shall understand the fear of the Lord, and shall find the knowledge of God; for the Lord gives wisdom, and out of his mouth come knowledge and prudence. He will guard the salvation of the upright, and protect those who walk in simplicity: keeping the paths of justice, and guarding the ways of the saints. Then you shall understand justice, and judgment, and equity, and every good path. If wisdom enter your heart, and knowledge please your soul; counsel shall keep you, and prudence shall preserve you; that you may be rescued from the evil way, and from the man who speaks perverse things. Those who forsake the right path, and walk by dark ways: who rejoice when they have done evil, and exult in the worst things: whose ways are perverse, and their steps infamous. That you may be rescued from the strange woman, and from the foreigner who softens her words, and has forsaken the guide of her youth, and forgotten the covenant of her God [2:3-18].

**111. 111.** [Prov 3]. *And after thirteen verses:* Let mercy and truth not forsake you: bind them about your throat, and write them on the tablets of your heart; and you shall find grace and good discipline before God and men. Have confidence in the Lord with all your heart, and lean not on your own prudence. In all your ways think on him, and he will direct your steps. Do not be wise in your own eyes. Fear God, and depart from evil [3:3-7].  
*And after one verse:* Honor the Lord with your substance, and with the firstfruits of all your produce [3:9].  
*And after two verses:* The discipline of the Lord, my son, do not cast away; nor faint when you are reproved by him. For whom the Lord loves he reproves; and as a father in his son he takes delight. Blessed is the man who finds wisdom, and who abounds in prudence. The getting of it is better than the trade in silver, and its fruit better than the finest gold: it is more precious than all riches; and all things that are desired are not to be compared with it [3:11-15].  
*And after ten verses:* My son, let not these things flow away from your eyes: keep the law and counsel [3:21].  
*And after nine verses:* Do not forbid him who is able to do good; if you can, do good yourself also. Do not say to your friend: Go, and come back, and tomorrow I will give you; when you can give at once. Do not contrive evil against your friend; when he has confidence in you. Do not contend against a man without cause [3:27-30].  
*And after seven verses:* The scorners he will himself mock, and to the meek he will give grace [3:34].

**112. 112.** [Prov 4]. *And after forty-three verses:* With all watchfulness keep your heart; for from it life proceeds. Remove from yourself the perverse mouth, and let detracting lips be far from you. Let your eyes look straight ahead; and let your eyelids go before your steps. Make straight the path for your feet, and all your ways shall be established. Turn not aside to the right or to the left. Turn your foot away from evil. For the ways that are on the right hand the Lord knows; but perverse are those on the left. He himself makes your courses straight, and will bring your journeys forth in peace [4:23-27].

**113. 113.** [Prov 5]. My son, attend to my wisdom, and incline your ear to my prudence; that you may keep your thoughts, and your lips preserve discipline. For the lips of a harlot are a dripping honeycomb, and her throat smoother than oil. But her latter end is bitter as wormwood, and sharp as a two-edged sword. Her feet go down into death, and her steps penetrate to hell [5:1-5].

**114. 114.** [Prov 6]. *And after ninety-seven verses*: Let not your heart covet her beauty, lest you be caught by her glances. For the price of a harlot is scarcely one loaf of bread: but a woman captures the precious soul of a man. Can a man hide fire in his bosom, and his garments not burn? or walk upon coals, and his soles not be scorched? So he who goes in to his neighbor's wife shall not be clean when he has touched her. The fault is not great when one has stolen: for he steals to fill his hungry soul; and being caught he shall restore sevenfold, and shall hand over all the substance of his house. But he who is an adulterer, through want of heart shall destroy his own soul [6:25-32].

**115. 115.** [Prov 7]. *And after four verses:* My son, keep my words, and lay up my precepts with you. Keep my commandments, and live, and my law as the apple of your eye [7:1-2].

**116. 116.** [Prov 8]. *And after fifty-seven verses*: Understand, you little ones, shrewdness; and you senseless, take notice [8:5].  
*And after two verses:* My throat shall meditate truth, and my lips shall detest the wicked [8:7].  
*And after three verses:* Receive my discipline and not money; love instruction rather than gold. For wisdom is better than all the most precious things; and nothing that is desirable can be compared to it [8:10-11].  
*And after two verses:* The fear of the Lord hates evil; it detests arrogance, and pride, and the perverse way, and the double-tongued mouth [8:13].  
*And after six verses:* I love those who love me; and those who watch early for me shall find me. With me are riches and glory, lofty wealth and justice. My fruit is better than gold and precious stone, and my produce than choice silver. I walk in the ways of justice, in the midst of the paths of judgment; that I may enrich those who love me, and fill their treasures [8:17-21].  
*And after twenty-three verses:* Blessed is the man who hears me, who watches daily at my doors, and waits at the posts of my gateway. He who finds me shall find life, and shall draw salvation from the Lord. But he who sins against me shall harm his own soul. All who hate me love death [8:34-36].

**117. 117.** [Prov 9]. *And after nine verses:* Forsake childishness, and live, and walk by the ways of prudence. He who instructs a scorner does himself an injury; and he who reproves the wicked begets a blemish for himself. Do not reprove a scorner, lest he hate you: reprove a wise man, and he will love you. Give occasion to a wise man, and wisdom shall be added to him. Teach a just man, and he will make haste to receive. The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and the knowledge of the holy is prudence [9:6-10].

**118. 118.** [Prov 10]. *And after eighteen verses:* Treasures of iniquity shall profit nothing; but justice shall deliver from death [10:2].  
*And after four verses:* He who gathers in the harvest is a wise son: but he who snores in summer is a son of confusion [10:5].  
*And after four verses:* The wise of heart shall receive precepts; the fool is struck down by his lips. He who walks in simplicity walks confidently: but he who corrupts his ways shall be found out. He who winks with the eye shall cause sorrow; the fool shall be beaten with his lips. The mouth of the just is a vein of life; and the mouth of the wicked covers iniquity. Hatred stirs up quarrels; and love covers all offenses. In the lips of the wise wisdom is found; and a rod on the back of him who lacks heart. The wise lay up wisdom: but the mouth of the fool is next to confusion [10:8-14].  
*And after four verses:* The way of life for him who keeps discipline: but he who forsakes reproofs goes astray. Lying lips hide hatred. He who utters insult is foolish. In much talking sin shall not be wanting: but he who moderates his lips is most prudent. The tongue of the just is choice silver: the heart of the wicked is worth nothing. The lips of the just instruct many: but those who are untaught shall die in the want of heart. The blessing of the Lord makes rich, nor shall affliction be joined to it. The fool works wickedness as if in sport: but wisdom is prudence to a man. What the wicked man fears shall come upon him: the desire of the just shall be granted them [10:17-24].  
*And after two verses:* As vinegar to the teeth, and smoke to the eyes; so is the slothful to those who sent him. The fear of the Lord shall add days; and the years of the wicked shall be shortened. The expectation of the just is gladness: but the hope of the wicked shall perish. The strength of the simple is the way of the Lord; and dread to those who work evil. The just man shall not be moved forever: but the wicked shall not dwell in the land. The mouth of the just shall bring forth wisdom: the tongue of the perverse shall perish. The lips of the just consider what is pleasing, and the mouth of the wicked perverse things [10:26-32].

**119. 119.** [Prov 11]. A deceitful balance is an abomination before God; and a just weight is his will. Where pride is, there also will be insult: but where humility is, there also is wisdom. The simplicity of the just shall guide them; and the supplanting of the perverse shall destroy them. Riches shall not profit in the day of vengeance: but justice shall deliver from death. The justice of the simple shall make straight his way; and the wicked shall fall in his own wickedness. The justice of the upright shall deliver them; but the unjust shall be caught in their own snares. When the wicked man is dead, there shall be no more hope, and the expectation of the anxious shall perish [11:1-7].  
*And after five verses:* He who despises his friend is wanting in heart: but a prudent man will hold his peace. He who walks fraudulently reveals secrets: but he who is faithful of mind conceals what is committed to him. Where there is no governor, the people shall fall: but there is safety where there is much counsel. He shall be afflicted with evil who goes surety for a stranger: but he who guards against snares shall be secure [11:12-15].  
*And after two verses:* The merciful man does good to his own soul: but he who is cruel casts off even his kindred [11:17].  
*And after three verses:* A perverse heart is abominable to the Lord; and his will is in those who walk in simplicity. Hand in hand, the evil man shall not be innocent: but the seed of the just shall be saved [11:20-21].  
*And after two verses:* The desire of the just is all good: the expectation of the wicked is fury. Some divide their own goods, and grow richer: others seize what is not theirs, and are always in want. The soul that blesses shall be made fat: and he who inebriates shall himself also be inebriated. He who hides up grain is cursed among the peoples: but a blessing is upon the head of those who sell. He shall rise well at dawn who seeks good things: but he who is a searcher of evils shall be oppressed by them. He who trusts in his riches shall fall: but the just shall spring up like a green leaf [11:23-28].  
*And after four verses:* If the just man receives in the land, how much more the wicked and the sinner? [11:31].

**120. 120.** [Prov 12]. He who loves discipline loves knowledge: but he who hates reproofs is senseless. He who is good shall draw grace from the Lord: but he who trusts in his own thoughts acts wickedly. A man shall not be strengthened by wickedness, and the root of the just shall not be moved [12:1-3].  
*And after some verses:* The desire of the wicked is a fortress of the worst: but the root of the just shall flourish. By the sins of the lips ruin draws near to the evil man: but the just shall escape out of distress. From the fruit of his own mouth shall each one be filled with good things, and according to the works of his hands shall it be repaid to him. The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he who is wise listens to counsel [12:12-15].  
*And after four verses:* There is one who promises, and is pricked as with the sword of conscience: but the tongue of the wise is health. The lip of truth shall be firm forever: but he who is a hasty witness frames a lying tongue. Deceit is in the heart of those who plot evils: but joy shall follow those who enter into counsels of peace. Whatever shall befall the just shall not grieve him: but the wicked shall be filled with evil. Lying lips are an abomination to the Lord: but those who act faithfully please him. A cunning man conceals knowledge: and the heart of the senseless will provoke folly [12:18-23].  
*And after two verses:* Grief in the heart of a man shall humble him, and with a good word he shall be made glad. He who neglects loss for the sake of a friend is just: but the way of the wicked shall deceive them. The fraudulent man shall not find gain, and the substance of a man shall be worth its weight in gold. And in the path of justice is life: but the byway leads to death [12:25-28].

**121. 121.** [Prov 13]. A wise son is the discipline of his father: but he who is a scorner does not listen when he is reproved. From the fruit of his own mouth a man shall be filled with good things: but the soul of transgressors is unjust. He who keeps his mouth keeps his soul: but he who is heedless in speaking shall feel evils. The slothful man wills and does not will: but the soul of those who labor shall be made fat. The just man shall detest a lying word: but the wicked man confounds and is confounded. Justice keeps the way of the innocent: but wickedness overthrows the sinner. There is one who is as it were rich, when he has nothing; and there is one who is as it were poor, when he is in great riches. The ransom of a man's soul is his riches: but he who is poor does not endure reproof. The light of the just gives gladness: the lamp of the wicked shall be put out. Among the proud there are always quarrels: but those who do all things with counsel are ruled by wisdom. Substance gotten in haste shall be diminished: but what is gathered little by little with the hand shall be multiplied. Hope that is deferred afflicts the soul: a tree of life is the desire that comes. He who detracts from anything binds himself for the future: but he who fears the commandment shall dwell in peace. The law of the wise is a fountain of life, that he may decline from the ruin of death. Good doctrine shall give grace: in the way of scorners there is a pit [13:1-15].  
*And after some verses:* He who spares the rod hates his son; but he who loves him chastens him diligently. The just man eats and fills his soul: but the belly of the wicked is insatiable [13:24-25].

**122. 122.** [Prov 14]. *And after two verses:* He who walks by the straight path, and fears God, is despised by him who walks the way of infamy. In the mouth of a fool is the rod of pride: the lips of the wise keep them [14:2-3].  
*And after two verses:* A faithful witness will not lie: a deceitful witness utters falsehood. The scorner seeks wisdom, and finds it not: the learning of the wise is easy. Go against a foolish man, and you shall not know the lips of imprudence. The wisdom of the shrewd is to understand his own way; and the imprudence of fools goes astray. The fool shall mock sin: but among the just shall grace abide. The heart that knows the bitterness of its own soul, in its joy a stranger shall not share. The house of the wicked shall be wiped out: but the tents of the just shall flourish. There is a way which seems just to a man; but its latter end leads to death. Laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning takes hold of the end of joy [14:5-13].  
*And after three verses:* The wise man fears, and declines evil: the fool leaps over, and trusts. The impatient man shall work folly; and the cunning man is hateful [14:16-17].  
*And after four verses:* He who despises his neighbor sins: but he who has mercy on the poor shall be blessed. They go astray who work evil: mercy and truth prepare good things. In every work there shall be abundance: but where there are many words, there is frequent want [14:21-23].  
*And after four verses:* In the fear of the Lord is the confidence of strength; and there shall be hope for his children. The fear of the Lord is a fountain of life, that he may decline from the ruin of death [14:26-27].  
*And after two verses:* He who is patient is governed by much prudence: but he who is impatient exalts his own folly. The health of the heart is the life of the flesh: envy is the rotting of the bones. He who calumniates the needy reproaches his maker: but he honors him who has mercy on the poor [14:29-31].

**123. 123.** [Prov 15]: *And after seven verses:* A soft answer breaks anger: a harsh word stirs up fury. The tongue of the wise adorns knowledge: the mouth of fools bubbles out folly [15:1-2].  
*And after seventeen verses:* The pestilent man does not love him who reproves him, nor does he go to the wise [15:12].  
*And after five verses:* Better is a little with the fear of the Lord, than great and insatiable treasures. Better is it to be called to herbs with love, than to a fatted calf with hatred. An angry man provokes quarrels: but he who is patient calms those stirred up. The way of the slothful is like a hedge of thorns: the way of the just is without stumbling block. A wise son makes his father glad: but a foolish man despises his mother [15:16-20].  
*And after two verses:* Thoughts are scattered where there is no counsel: but where there are many counselors, they are confirmed. A man is glad in the sentence of his mouth; and a word in season is best of all. The path of life is above the learned man, that he may decline from the lowest hell [15:22-24].  
*And after four verses:* He who follows after greed troubles his own house: but he who hates gifts shall live. The mind of the just shall meditate on obedience: the mouth of the wicked overflows with evils. The Lord is far from the wicked; and he will hear the prayers of the just. The light of the eyes gladdens the soul: a good name fattens the bones. The ear that hears the reproofs of life shall abide in the midst of the wise. He who casts away discipline despises his own soul: but he who yields to reproofs is master of his heart. The fear of the Lord is the discipline of wisdom; and humility goes before glory [15:27-33].

**124. 124.** [Prov 16]. It is man's part to prepare the mind, and God's to govern the tongue. All the ways of men are open to his eyes: the Lord weighs the spirits. Reveal your works to the Lord, and your thoughts shall be directed. The Lord has made all things for his own sake; the wicked also for the evil day. Every arrogant man is an abomination to the Lord: though hand be in hand, he shall not be innocent. By mercy and truth iniquity is redeemed, and in the fear of the Lord one declines from evil. When the ways of a man please the Lord, he will turn even his enemies to peace. Better is a little with justice, than great revenues with iniquity. The heart of man disposes his way; but it is the Lord's to direct his steps. Divination is on the lips of the king; in judgment his mouth shall not err. Weight and balance are the judgments of the Lord, and all the weights in the bag are his work. They who act wickedly are abominable to the king; for the throne is established by justice. Just lips are the will of kings: he who speaks right things shall be directed. The wrath of a king is a messenger of death; and a wise man will appease it. In the cheerfulness of the king's countenance is life; and his clemency is like the latter rain. Possess wisdom, for it is better than gold: and get prudence, for it is more precious than silver. The path of the just declines evils; he who guards his own soul keeps his way. Pride goes before destruction; and the spirit is exalted before a fall. It is better to be humbled with the meek, than to divide spoils with the proud. He who is learned in word shall find good things; and he who hopes in the Lord is blessed. He who is wise of heart shall be called prudent; and he who is sweet of speech shall attain greater things [16:1-21].  
*And after seventeen verses:* The patient man is better than the strong; and he who rules his own soul, than he who storms cities. Lots are cast into the lap; but they are disposed by the Lord [16:32-33].

**125. 125.** [Prov 17]. Better is a dry morsel with joy, than a house full of victims with strife. A wise servant shall rule over foolish sons, and shall divide the inheritance among the brothers. As silver is tried by fire, and gold in the furnace; so the Lord tries hearts. The evil man obeys an unjust tongue, and the deceitful man hearkens to lying lips. He who despises the poor reproaches his maker; and he who rejoices at another's ruin shall not be unpunished. The crown of old men is their children's children; and the glory of children is their fathers. Composed words do not become a fool, nor a lying lip a prince. The expectation of one who waits is a most pleasing jewel; wherever he turns, he shall understand prudently. He who conceals a fault seeks friendships: he who repeats it in another word separates allies. Reproof profits more with a prudent man, than a hundred stripes with a fool. The evil man always seeks quarrels: but a cruel angel shall be sent against him. It is better to meet a she-bear robbed of her cubs, than a fool trusting in his own folly. He who renders evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house. He who lets out water is the head of quarrels; and before he suffers insult, he abandons judgment. He who justifies the wicked, and he who condemns the just, both are abominable before the Lord [17:1-15].  
*And after sixteen verses:* The wicked man takes gifts out of the bosom, to pervert the paths of judgment. In the face of the prudent wisdom shines: the eyes of fools are at the ends of the earth [17:23-24].  
*And after three verses:* He who moderates his words is learned and prudent, and a man of precious spirit is well taught [17:27].

**126. 126.** [Prov 18]. *And after two verses:* He who wishes to depart from a friend seeks occasions; at all times he shall be reproachable [18:1].  
*And after six verses:* To accept the person of the wicked is not good, so as to turn aside from the truth of judgment [18:5].  
*And after six verses:* He who is soft and slack in his work is brother to him who lays waste his own works. The name of the Lord is a most strong tower: to it the just man runs, and shall be exalted [18:9-10].  
*And after two verses:* Before he is broken, the heart of man is exalted; and before he is glorified, he is humbled [18:12].  
*And after seven verses:* The just man is the first accuser of himself; his friend comes, and shall search him out. The lot represses contradictions, and decides even among the mighty. A brother who is helped by his brother is like a strong city, and judgments are like the bars of cities [18:17-19].  
*Likewise after two verses:* Death and life are in the hand of the tongue; those who love it shall eat its fruits. He who finds a good woman finds a good thing; and shall draw delight from the Lord. The poor man shall speak with entreaties; and the rich shall speak roughly. A friendly man is for fellowship, and shall be more a friend than a brother [18:21-24].

**127. 127.** [Prov 19]. Better is a poor man who walks in his simplicity, than a rich man who twists his lips, and is a fool. Where there is no knowledge of the soul, there is no good; and he who is hasty stumbles with his feet. The folly of a man supplants his steps, and against God he rages in his heart. Riches add many friends: but from a poor man even those he had are separated. A false witness shall not be unpunished; and he who speaks lies shall not escape. Many court the person of the mighty, and are friends of him who gives gifts. The brothers of a poor man hate him; moreover, his friends too have departed far from him. He who follows only after words shall have nothing: but he who is master of his mind loves his own soul [19:1-8].  
*And after nine verses:* Slothfulness brings on a deep sleep, and the slack soul shall go hungry. He who keeps the commandment keeps his own soul; but he who neglects his ways shall be put to death. He who has mercy on the poor lends to the Lord, and he will repay him his recompense. Instruct your son, do not despair [19:15-18].  
*And after six verses:* A needy man is merciful; and better is a poor man than a lying man. The fear of the Lord is unto life, and he shall abide in plenty without the visitation of the worst [19:22-23].  
*And after two verses:* When the pestilent man is scourged, the fool shall be wiser: but if you reprove a wise man, he shall understand discipline. He who afflicts his father, and flees from his mother, is disgraceful and unhappy. Cease not, my son, to hear instruction, nor be ignorant of the words of knowledge. An unjust witness mocks judgment; and the mouth of the wicked devours iniquity. Judgments are prepared for scorners, and hammers striking on the bodies of fools [19:25-29].

**128. 128.** [Prov 20]. Wine is a luxurious thing, and drunkenness tumultuous: whoever delights in these shall not be wise [20:1].  
*And after some verses:* Weight and weight, measure and measure, both are abominable before God. By his pursuits a child is known, whether his works be clean and right [20:10-11].  
*And after two verses:* Do not love sleep, lest want oppress you: open your eyes, and be filled with bread [20:13].  
*And after seventeen verses:* Weight and weight is an abomination before God; a deceitful balance is not good [20:23].

**129. 129.** [Prov 21]. *And after twenty-five verses:* He who stops his ear at the cry of the poor, shall himself also cry, and shall not be heard. A hidden gift extinguishes anger, and a present in the bosom the greatest indignation. It is joy to the just to do judgment; and dread to those who work iniquity. The man who strays from the way of doctrine shall be reckoned in the company of the giants [21:13-16].  
*And after nine verses:* He who keeps his mouth and his tongue keeps his soul from straits. The proud and arrogant man is called unlearned, who works pride in anger. Desires kill the slothful man; for his hands have refused to work anything. All the day he covets and desires: but the just man shall give, and shall not cease. The sacrifices of the wicked are abominable; because they are offered out of wickedness. A lying witness shall perish: a man who speaks well shall tell of victory [21:23-28].

**130. 130.** [Prov 22]. *And after five verses*: A good name is better than many riches [22:1].  
*And after fifteen verses:* He who is inclined to mercy shall be blessed: for of his own bread he has given to the poor. Cast out the scorner, and quarreling will go out with him, and lawsuits and insults shall cease [22:9-10].  
*And after four verses:* The slothful man says: There is a lion outside, I shall be slain in the midst of the streets [22:13].  
*And after three verses:* He who calumniates a poor man to increase his own riches, shall himself give to one richer, and shall be in want [22:16].  
*And after eight verses:* Do no violence to a poor man, because he is poor, nor crush the needy in the gate; for the Lord will judge his cause, and will pierce those who have pierced his soul. Do not be a friend to an angry man; nor walk with a furious man: lest perhaps you learn his ways, and take a stumbling block for your soul. Do not be with those who pledge their hands, and who offer themselves as sureties for debts [22:22-26].

**131. 131.** [Prov 23]. *And after a little:* Do not withhold discipline from a child: for if you strike him with the rod, he shall not die. You strike him with the rod, and shall deliver his soul from hell [23:13-14].  
*And after eight verses:* Do not be at the banquets of drinkers, nor at the revels of those who bring meats to eat [23:20].  
*And after twenty verses:* Do not gaze on the wine when it grows golden, when its color shines in the glass. It goes in pleasantly; but in the end it will bite like a serpent, and like a basilisk it will spread its venom [23:31-32].

**132. 132.** [Prov 24]. *And after seven verses:* Do not envy, *he says,* evil men, nor desire to be with them; for their mind meditates robberies, and their lips speak frauds [24:1].  
*Likewise after sixteen verses:* Rescue those who are led to death, and cease not to deliver those who are dragged to destruction. If you say: My strength is not sufficient: he who beholds the heart, he understands; and nothing escapes the keeper of your soul, and he will render to a man according to his works [24:11-12].  
*Likewise after nine verses:* When your enemy falls, do not rejoice, and at his ruin let not your heart exult: lest perhaps the Lord see, and it displease him, and he turn away his anger from him. Do not contend with the worst men, nor envy the wicked [24:17-19].  
*And after two verses:* Fear the Lord, my son, and the king; and do not mingle with detractors: for their destruction shall rise up suddenly; and who knows the ruin of both? These things also for the wise: To know a person in judgment is not good. He who says to the wicked: You are just; the peoples shall curse him, and the tribes shall detest him. Those who reprove shall be praised, and upon them shall come a blessing. He shall kiss the lips who answers right words [24:21-26].  
*And after two verses:* Do not be a witness without cause against your neighbor, nor cajole anyone with your lips. Nor say: As he has done to me, so will I do to him: I will render to each one according to his work. I passed through the field of the slothful man, and through the vineyard of the foolish man; and behold, nettles had filled it all, thorns had covered its surface: and the wall of stones was destroyed. And when I had seen it, I laid it up in my heart, and by the example I learned discipline [24:28-32].

**133. 133.** [Prov 25]. *And after twenty verses:* What your eyes have seen, do not bring forth hastily in a quarrel; lest afterward you be unable to make amends, when you have dishonored your friend. Treat your cause with your friend; and do not reveal a secret to a stranger; lest perhaps he insult you when he has heard it, and cease not to reproach you. He who speaks a word in its season is like golden apples on beds of silver. He who reproves a wise man and an obedient ear is like a golden earring and a shining pearl [25:8-12].  
*And after seventeen verses:* He who sings songs to a wicked heart is like vinegar on niter. And if your enemy is hungry, feed him; and if he is thirsty, give him water to drink: for you shall heap coals upon his head, and the Lord will repay you [25:20-22].  
*And after ten verses:* As a city that is open and without a circuit of walls, so is the man who cannot restrain his spirit in speaking [25:28].

**134. 134.** [Prov 26]. *And after two verses:* As a bird flying off elsewhere, and a sparrow going wherever it pleases; so a curse uttered without cause shall come upon someone. A whip for the horse, and a bit for the ass, and a rod for the back of the imprudent. Do not answer a fool according to his folly; lest you be made like him. Answer a fool according to his folly; lest he seem wise to himself [26:2-5].  
*And after ten verses:* As a dog that returns to his vomit, so is the imprudent man who repeats his folly. Have you seen a man who seems wise to himself? a fool shall have more hope than he. The slothful man says: There is a lion in the way, and a lioness in the paths. As a door turns on its hinge, so does the slothful man on his bed. The slothful man hides his hands under his armpit, and is wearied if he should turn them to his mouth. The slothful man seems wiser to himself than seven men who speak sentences. As one who takes hold of a dog by the ears, so is he who, impatient, passes by and meddles in another's quarrel. As a harmful man who hurls lances and arrows unto death; so is the man who fraudulently harms his friend, and when he is caught says: I did it in jest. When the wood fails, the fire is put out; and when the whisperer is removed, quarrels die down. As coals to embers, and wood to fire, so an angry man stirs up quarrels. The words of a whisperer seem simple, and they reach to the inmost parts of the belly [26:11-22].  
*And after three verses:* An enemy is known by his lips, while in his heart he plots deceits. When he lowers his voice, do not trust him; for there are seven wickednesses in his heart. He who covers hatred fraudulently, his malice shall be revealed in the assembly. He who digs a pit shall fall into it; and he who rolls a stone, it returns upon him. A deceitful tongue loves not the truth; and a slippery mouth works ruin [26:24-28].

**135. 135.** [Prov 27]. Do not boast for tomorrow, not knowing what the coming day may bring forth. Let another praise you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips [27:1-2].  
*And after four verses:* Open reproof is better than hidden love. Better are the wounds of one who loves, than the deceitful kisses of one who hates [27:5-6].  
*And after three verses:* Do not forsake your friend, and your father's friend [27:10].  
*And after some verses:* Hell and perdition are not filled: in like manner the eyes of men are insatiable. As silver is tried in the melting-pot, and gold in the furnace; so is a man tried by the mouth of one who praises him. Though you should bray a fool in the mortar, as with a pestle striking down on grain, his folly shall not be taken from him [27:20-22].

**136. 136.** [Prov 28]. *And after some verses:* A poor man who calumniates the poor is like a violent shower in which famine is prepared. Those who forsake the law praise the wicked: those who keep it are inflamed against him. Evil men do not consider judgment: but those who seek the Lord take notice of all things. Better is a poor man who walks in his simplicity, than a rich man in crooked ways [28:3-6].  
*And after two verses:* He who heaps up riches by usury and interest, gathers them for one who is generous to the poor. He who turns away his ear from hearing the law, his prayer shall be detestable. He who deceives the just in an evil way shall fall in his own destruction, and the simple shall possess his goods [28:8-10].  
*And after four verses:* He who hides his crimes shall not be directed: but he who confesses and forsakes them shall obtain mercy. Blessed is the man who is always fearful: but he who is of a hard mind shall fall into evil [28:13-14].  
*Likewise after twelve verses:* A faithful man shall be much praised: but he who hastens to grow rich shall not be innocent. He who in judgment respects a person does not do well; even for a morsel of bread that man forsakes the truth. The man who hastens to grow rich and envies others, does not know that want shall come upon him. He who reproves a man shall afterward find favor with him, more than he who deceives by the flatteries of the tongue. He who takes away anything from his father and mother, and says: This is no sin, is partaker with a murderer. He who boasts and puffs himself up stirs up quarrels: but he who hopes in the Lord shall be healed. He who trusts in his own heart is a fool: but he who walks wisely, he shall be saved. He who gives to the poor shall not want: he who despises the suppliant shall suffer penury [28:20-27].

**137. 137.** [Prov 29]. *And after ten verses:* The man who speaks with flattering and feigned words to his friend spreads a net for his steps [29:5].*  
And after twenty-five verses:* A servant cannot be taught by words; because he understands what you say, and disdains to answer. Have you seen a man swift to speak? more is to be hoped from folly than from his correction [29:19-20].  
*And after four verses:* Humility follows the proud, and glory shall uphold the humble of spirit. He who shares with a thief hates his own soul; he hears one putting him on oath, and does not disclose it. He who fears man shall quickly fall: but he who hopes in the Lord shall be lifted up. Many seek the face of the prince, but the judgment of each one comes forth from the Lord. The just abominate the wicked man; and the wicked abominate those who are in the right way [29:23-27].

**138. 138.** [Prov 30]. *And after fourteen verses:* Two things I have asked of you, do not deny them to me before I die: Remove far from me vanity and lying words. Give me neither beggary nor riches; grant me only what is necessary for my sustenance: lest perhaps, being filled, I be enticed to deny, and say: Who is the Lord? or, compelled by want, I steal, and forswear the name of my God. Do not accuse a servant to his master, lest perhaps he curse you, and you fall [30:7-10].
