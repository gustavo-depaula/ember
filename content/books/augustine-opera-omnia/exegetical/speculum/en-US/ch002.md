# These Are from the Book of the Law, Which Is Called Exodus

**1. 1.** [Ex 20]. You shall not have strange gods before me. You shall not make for yourself a graven thing, nor any likeness that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, nor of those things that are in the waters under the earth: you shall not adore them, nor worship them [20:3-5].  
*Likewise:* You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain: for the Lord will not hold him guiltless who has taken the name of the Lord his God in vain [20:7].  
*And a little later:* Honor your father and your mother, that you may be long-lived upon the land which the Lord your God will give you. You shall not kill. You shall not commit adultery. You shall not steal. You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor. You shall not covet your neighbor's house, nor desire his wife; not his manservant, not his maidservant, not his ox, not his ass, nor any of the things that are his [20:12-17].  
*Likewise after the Decalogue, in other places, these precepts of living are found in the same book:* You shall not make gods of silver with me, nor shall you make gods of gold for yourselves [20:23].

**2. 2.** [Ex 21]. *And somewhat later:* He who strikes a man, willing to kill him, let him die the death. But he who did not lie in wait, and into whose hands God delivered him, I will appoint you a place to which he may flee. If a man kill his neighbor on purpose and by lying in wait, you shall take him away from my altar, that he may die. He who strikes his father or mother, let him die the death. He who steals a man and sells him, being convicted of the crime, let him die the death. He who curses his father or mother, let him die the death. If men quarrel, and one strike his neighbor with a stone or with his fist, and he does not die but lies in bed; if he rise again and walk abroad upon his staff, he who struck him shall be innocent: yet so that he make restitution for his work and his expenses on the physicians. He who strikes his manservant or maidservant with a rod, and they die under his hands, shall be guilty of the crime: but if they survive a day or two, he shall not be subject to the penalty, because it is his own money. If men quarrel, and one strike a pregnant woman, and she miscarry indeed but herself live; he shall be subject to such damages as the woman's husband shall demand and the arbiters shall award: but if her death follow thereupon, he shall render life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, burning for burning, wound for wound, bruise for bruise. If anyone strike the eye of his manservant or maidservant and leave them but one eye, he shall let them go free for the eye he has put out. Also if he knock out a tooth of his manservant or maidservant, he shall in like manner let them go free. If an ox gore a man or a woman, and they die, it shall be stoned, and its flesh shall not be eaten; and the owner of the ox shall be innocent. But if the ox was wont to gore yesterday and the day before, and they warned its owner, and he did not shut it up, and it kill a man and a woman; the ox shall be stoned, and they shall kill its owner. But if a price be laid upon him, he shall give for his life whatever shall be demanded. If he gore a son or a daughter, he shall be subject to a like sentence. If it attack a manservant or a maidservant, he shall give thirty shekels of silver to the master; but the ox shall be stoned. If anyone open a cistern and dig it and not cover it, and an ox or an ass fall into it; the owner of the cistern shall pay the price of the beasts: but what is dead shall be his own. If one man's ox wound another's ox, and it die; they shall sell the live ox and divide the price; and the carcass of the dead one they shall part between them. But if he knew that the ox was wont to gore yesterday and the day before, and its owner did not keep it; he shall render ox for ox, and shall receive the whole carcass [21:12-36].

**3. 3.** [Ex 22]. If anyone steal an ox or a sheep, and kill or sell it; he shall restore five oxen for one ox, and four sheep for one sheep. If a thief be found breaking into a house, or undermining it, and being wounded die; the one who struck him shall not be guilty of blood. But if he did this after sunrise, he has committed murder, and he himself shall die. If he have not the means to make restitution for the theft, he shall be sold. But if what he stole be found with him alive, whether ox, or ass, or sheep; he shall restore double. If anyone damage a field or a vineyard, and let his beast loose to feed upon what is another's: whatever best he has in his own field or vineyard, he shall restore according to the estimate of the damage. If a fire break out and catch in thorns, and lay hold of stacks of grain, or standing crops in the fields; he who kindled the fire shall make good the damage. If anyone entrust money or a vessel to a friend for safekeeping, and they be taken away by theft from the one who received them; if the thief be found, he shall restore double: if he be hidden, the master of the house shall be brought to the gods, and shall swear that he did not put forth his hand to his neighbor's goods, to commit fraud, as well in the ox as in the ass, and the sheep and the garment, and whatever can cause loss; and the cause of both shall come before the gods; and if they so judge, he shall restore double to his neighbor. If anyone entrust to his neighbor an ass, an ox, or a sheep, and any beast for safekeeping, and it die, or be hurt, or be taken by enemies, and no one saw it; there shall be an oath between them, that he did not put forth his hand to his neighbor's goods; and the owner shall accept the oath, and the other shall not be compelled to make restitution. But if it were taken away by theft, he shall make good the damage to the owner. If it were eaten by a beast, let him bring to him what was slain, and he shall not make restitution. He who borrows from his neighbor any of these things, and it be hurt or die, the owner not being present; shall be compelled to make restitution. But if the owner was present, he shall not make restitution, especially if it had come hired for the wages of its work. If anyone seduce a virgin not yet betrothed, and lie with her; he shall endow her, and shall have her to wife. If the virgin's father will not give her, he shall pay money according to the measure of the dowry which virgins are wont to receive. You shall not suffer wizards to live. He who lies with a beast, let him die the death. He who sacrifices to gods shall be slain, save to the Lord alone. You shall not grieve the stranger, nor afflict him: for you yourselves were strangers in the land of Egypt. You shall not hurt the widow or the orphan. If you hurt them, they will cry out to me, and I will hear their cry, and my fury shall be roused up, and I will strike you with the sword, and your wives shall be widows, and your children orphans. If you lend money to my poor people who dwell with you, you shall not press him as an extortioner, nor oppress him with usury. If you take your neighbor's garment in pledge, restore it to him before sunset; for it is the only covering wherewith the clothing of his flesh is covered, nor has he any other to sleep in: if he cry out to me, I will hear him, because I am merciful. You shall not speak ill of the gods, nor curse the ruler of your people. You shall not delay to offer your tithes and your firstfruits. You shall give me the firstborn of your sons [22:1-29].

**4. 4.** [Ex 23]. *And a little later:* You shall not receive the voice of a lie, nor join your hand to bear false witness for a wicked man. You shall not follow the crowd to do evil, nor in judgment yield to the opinion of the many, so as to stray from the truth. Neither shall you pity the poor man in judgment. If you meet your enemy's ox, or his ass going astray, bring it back to him. If you see the ass of one who hates you lying under his burden, you shall not pass by, but shall lift it up with him. You shall not turn aside in the judgment of the poor. You shall flee from lying. The innocent and the just you shall not put to death; for I abhor the wicked. Take no gifts, which blind even the prudent, and pervert the words of the just. You shall not be troublesome to the sojourner: for you know the souls of strangers, since you yourselves also were strangers in the land of Egypt [23:1-9].  
*And after certain things are interposed, when he was speaking of the foreigners:* You shall not adore their gods, nor worship them. You shall not do their works, but shall destroy them, and break their statues: and you shall serve the Lord your God [23:24-25].

**5. 5.** [Ex 34]. *And after many things in the same book concerning the gods of the Gentiles:* But destroy their altars, break their statues, and cut down their groves. Do not adore a strange god; the Lord, whose name is Jealous, he is a jealous God. Make no covenant with the men of those regions; lest, when they have committed fornication with their gods and adored their idols, someone call you to eat of what is sacrificed. Neither shall you take a wife of their daughters for your sons; lest, after they themselves have committed fornication, they make your sons also commit fornication with their gods. You shall not make for yourself molten gods [34:13-17].  
*And a little later:* You shall offer the firstfruits of the fruits of your land in the house of the Lord your God [34:26].

*These I thought should be gathered from the book of the Law, which is called Exodus. Now let us inspect in the same manner in the following book, Leviticus.*
