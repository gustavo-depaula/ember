# From the First Letter of Peter

**406. 406.** [1 Pet 1]. Though now for a little while you may need to be saddened by various trials, so that the testing of your faith may be much more precious than gold tested by fire [1:6–7].  
*And in another place:* Therefore, girding the loins of your mind, soberly place your hope fully in the grace offered to you at Jesus Christ's revelation. As children of obedience, do not conform to the former desires of your ignorance, but according to the Holy One who called you, be holy yourselves in all your conduct, because it is written: *You shall be holy, because I am holy*[^1]. And if you invoke as Father the one who judges impartially according to each person's work, conduct yourselves in fear during the time of your sojourn [1:13–17].  
*And some verses later:* Purifying your souls in the obedience of love, in brotherly affection, love one another more earnestly from a simple heart, being born anew [1:22–23].

**407. 407.** [1 Pet 2]. *And a few verses later:* Therefore, laying aside all malice, all deceit, pretenses, envies, and all slanders, as newly born infants, rational and without deceit, desire milk [2:1–2].  
*And a few verses later*: Beloved, I appeal to you as strangers and pilgrims to abstain from fleshly desires that wage war against the soul. Keep your conduct good among the gentiles, so that in the matter in which they slander you as evildoers, they may observe your good works and glorify God on the day of visitation. Be subject to every human ordinance for God's sake, whether to the king as preeminent or to governors as sent by him to punish evildoers and praise the good. For God's will is this: that by doing good you silence the ignorance of imprudent people, as free people, not using freedom as a cloak for malice, but as God's slaves. Honor everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God, and honor the king. Slaves, be subject to your masters with all fear, not only the good and moderate but also the harsh. For it is a grace if, from consciousness of God, someone endures sorrows while suffering unjustly. For what glory is there if you are punished for sinning and endure being struck? But if you suffer while doing good and endure patiently, this is a grace before God. For to this you were called, because Christ too suffered for us, leaving you an example so that you might follow his footsteps. He committed no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth. When cursed, he did not curse; when suffering, he did not threaten, but handed himself over to the one judging him unjustly. He himself bore our sins in his body upon the wood, so that dead to sins, we might live to righteousness. By his bruise you have been healed. For you were like wandering sheep, but now have turned to the shepherd and bishop of your souls [2:11–25].

**408. 408.** [1 Pet 3]. *And in another place:* Likewise wives should be subject to their own husbands, so that even if some do not believe the word, they may be gained without a word through their wives' conduct, observing your chaste conduct in fear. Their adornment should not be outward, of hairstyles, the wearing of gold, or the putting on of clothing, but the hidden person of the heart in the incorruptibility of a quiet and moderate spirit, which is rich in God's sight. For in this way holy women who hoped in God once adorned themselves, subject to their own husbands, as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord[^2]. You are her daughters if you do good and fear no disturbance. Husbands likewise should dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honor to the female as the weaker vessel and as coheirs of life's grace, so that your prayers may not be hindered. In faith, all should be united in soul, sympathetic, lovers of the brotherhood, merciful, moderate, and humble, not repaying evil for evil[^3] or curse for curse, but blessing instead, because you were called to this, to possess a blessing as your inheritance. *For anyone who wants to love life and see good days should restrain his tongue* *from evil and his lips from speaking deceit. He should turn away from evil and do good, seek peace and pursue it. For the Lord's eyes are upon the righteous, and his ears attend their prayers; but the Lord's face is against evildoers*[^4]*.* And who can harm you if you are zealous for good? But even if you suffer anything for righteousness, you are blessed. *Do not fear their terror,* *so that you may not be disturbed*[^5]. Sanctify Christ the Lord in your hearts, always ready to make a defense to everyone demanding an account of the hope within you, but with moderation and fear, having a good conscience. Thus, in the matter in which they slander you, those maligning your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame. For it is better, if God's will so wishes, to suffer while doing good than while doing evil [3:1–17].

**409. 409.** [1 Pet 4]. *And some verses later:* Therefore, since Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves with the same thought, because the one who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sins, to live the remaining time in the flesh no longer by human desires but by God's will. For the past time is sufficient for accomplishing the gentiles' will for those who walked in extravagances, desires, indulgence in wine, revelries, drinking parties, drunkenness, and unlawful idol worship [4:1–3].  
*And a few* *verses later:* Therefore, be prudent and keep watch in prayers. Above all, have continual mutual love among yourselves, because love covers a multitude of sins[^6]. Be hospitable to one another without murmuring. As each has received grace, administer it to one another as good stewards of God's manifold grace. If anyone speaks, let it be as God's words; if anyone ministers, let it be as from the power God supplies, so that God may be honored in everything through Jesus Christ the Lord, to whom belong glory and dominion forever and ever. Beloved, do not be astonished at the burning trial that comes upon you for testing; do not be frightened as though something new were happening to you. Instead, rejoice in sharing Christ's sufferings, so that at the revelation of his glory you may also rejoice exultantly. If you are reproached in Christ's name, you will be blessed, because what belongs to God's honor, glory, and power, and his Spirit, rests upon you. But let none of you suffer as a murderer, thief, abusive person, or one who covets others' possessions. If he suffers as a Christian, however, let him not be ashamed but glorify God in that name, because it is time for judgment to begin from God's house. If it begins with us, what will be the end of those who do not believe God's Gospel? And if the righteous person will scarcely be saved, where will the ungodly and the sinner appear?[^7] Therefore, those who suffer according to God's will commend their souls to the faithful Creator through good deeds [4:7–19].

**410. 410.** [1 Pet 5]. Therefore, I appeal to the elders among you as a fellow elder, a witness of Christ's sufferings, and a sharer in the glory to be revealed in the future: tend God's flock among you, exercising care not under compulsion but willingly according to God, not for shameful gain but voluntarily, not lording it over those allotted to you, but becoming examples to the flock from your soul. When the chief shepherd appears, you will receive the unfading crown of glory. Likewise, younger people, be subject to the elders. All of you should show humility toward one another, because God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble[^8]. Therefore, humble yourselves under God's powerful hand, so that he may exalt you at the time of visitation. Cast all your anxiety upon him, because he cares for you. Be sober and keep watch, because your adversary the devil goes around like a roaring lion seeking someone to devour. Resist him, firm in faith, knowing that the same suffering comes upon your brotherhood throughout the world. The God of all grace, who called us into his eternal glory in Christ Jesus, will himself perfect, confirm, and establish you after you have suffered a little. To him be glory and dominion forever and ever. Amen [5:1–11].
