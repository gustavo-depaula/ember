# From the First Letter to the Corinthians

**344. 344.** [1 Cor 1]. I appeal to you, brothers, through the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, that you all say the same thing and that there be no schisms among you. Instead, be made complete in the same understanding and the same judgment. For it has been made known to me about you, my brothers, by Chloe's people, that there are disputes among you. What I mean is that each of you says: I belong to Paul; I to Apollos; I to Cephas; I to Christ. Has Christ been divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in Paul's name? [1:10–13].*  
And a little later:* But from him you have your being in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption, so that, as it is written: Let the one who glories glory in the Lord[^1] [1:30–31].

**345. 345.** [1 Cor 3]. *And in another place:* For when jealousy and strife exist among you, are you not fleshly and walking in a merely human way? For when one says: I belong to Paul, and another: I to Apollos, are you not merely human? [3:3–4].  
*And a little later:* Do you not know that you are God's temple and God's Spirit dwells in you? If anyone violates God's temple, God will destroy him. For God's temple is holy, and you are that temple. Let no one deceive himself. If anyone among you seems to be wise in this age, let him become foolish so that he may be wise. For this world's wisdom is foolishness before God [3:16–19].

**346. 346.** [1 Cor 4]. *And a few verses later:* Therefore, do not judge before the time, until the Lord comes. He will illuminate what is hidden in darkness and reveal the counsels of hearts; then each will have praise from God. Brothers, I have applied these things to myself and Apollos for your sake, so that through us you may learn not to go beyond what is written, lest one be inflated on behalf of someone against another. For who distinguishes you? What do you have that you did not receive? And if you received it, why do you glory as though you had not received it? [4:5–7].  
*And a little later:* Until this hour we hunger and thirst, lack clothing, are struck with fists, have no settled dwelling, and labor with our own hands. We are cursed, and we bless; we suffer persecution, and we endure; we are blasphemed, and we entreat. We have become like this world's refuse, everyone's offscouring, up to the present. I write this not to shame you, but to admonish you as my dearest children [4:11–14].

**347. 347.** [1 Cor 5]. *Again, a little later:* It is widely reported that there is fornication among you, and such fornication as is not found even among the gentiles: someone has his father's wife. Yet you are inflated with pride and have not rather mourned, so that the one who did this deed might be removed from your midst. For my part, absent in body but present in spirit, I have already judged as though present the man who acted in this way: when you and my spirit are gathered in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, with the power of the Lord Jesus, such a man is to be handed over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ. Your boasting is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven corrupts the whole mass? Clean out the old leaven so that you may be a new batch, as you are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed. Therefore, let us feast not with the old leaven or with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. I wrote to you in my letter not to associate with fornicators. I did not mean this world's fornicators, or the greedy, robbers, or idol worshipers; otherwise, you would have had to leave this world. But now I have written to you not to associate with anyone called a brother who is a fornicator, greedy, an idol worshiper, abusive, a drunkard, or a robber—not even to eat with such a person. For what have I to do with judging outsiders? Do you not judge those inside? God will judge those outside. Remove the evil one from among yourselves [5:1–13].

**348. 348.** [1 Cor 6]. Does any of you, having a dispute with another, dare to seek judgment before the unjust rather than before the saints? Or do you not know that the saints will judge this world? If the world will be judged by you, are you unworthy to judge the smallest matters? Do you not know that we will judge angels? How much more, then, worldly matters? Therefore, if you have worldly lawsuits, appoint those held in contempt in the Church to judge them. I say this to your shame. Is there really no wise person among you who can judge between his brothers? Instead, brother contends with brother in court, and before unbelievers at that? It is already an utter fault among you that you have lawsuits with one another. Why do you not rather accept injury? Why do you not rather suffer fraud? But you inflict injury and commit fraud, and do so against brothers. Or do you not know that the unjust will not possess God's kingdom? Do not be mistaken: neither fornicators nor idol worshipers, neither adulterers nor effeminate men nor men who lie with males, neither thieves nor the greedy, neither drunkards nor the abusive nor robbers will possess God's kingdom [6:1–10].  
*And a little later:* Do you not know that your bodies are Christ's members? Shall I then take Christ's members and make them a prostitute's members? Far from it. Or do you not know that anyone who clings to a prostitute becomes one body? For he says: The two will be in one flesh[^2]. But anyone who clings to the Lord is one spirit. Flee fornication. Every sin a person commits is outside the body, but the fornicator sins against his own body. Or do you not know that your members are a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and that you are not your own? For you were bought at a great price. Glorify and carry the Lord in your body [6:15–20].

**349. 349.** [1 Cor 7]. Concerning what you wrote to me: it is good for a man not to touch a woman. But because of fornications, let each man have his own wife and each woman her own husband. A husband should render the debt to his wife, and likewise a wife to her husband. The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband does; likewise the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife does. Do not deprive one another, except perhaps by agreement for a time, so that you may devote yourselves to prayer; then come together again, lest Satan tempt you because of your lack of self-control. I say this as a concession, not as a command. I wish everyone were like myself, but each has his own gift from God, one this way, another that way. To the unmarried and widows I say that it is good for them to remain as I am. But if they do not exercise self-control, let them marry, for it is better to marry than to burn. To those joined in marriage I command—not I, but the Lord—that a wife not separate from her husband. If she does separate, she should remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband. And a husband must not divorce his wife. To the others I say—I, not the Lord—if a brother has an unbelieving wife who agrees to live with him, he must not divorce her. If a woman has an unbelieving husband who agrees to live with her, she must not divorce her husband. For the unbelieving husband is sanctified in the believing wife, and the unbelieving wife is sanctified in the believing husband. Otherwise, your children would be unclean, but now they are holy. But if the unbeliever leaves, let him leave. A brother or sister is not subject to slavery in such cases; God has called us in peace. For how do you know, wife, whether you will save your husband? Or how do you know, husband, whether you will save your wife? Only as the Lord has allotted to each. Let each walk as God has called him, as I teach in all the churches. Was someone called while circumcised? He must not draw forward a foreskin. Was someone called while uncircumcised? He must not be circumcised. Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of God's commandments matters. Let each remain in the calling in which he was called. Were you called as a slave? Do not let it trouble you; but even if you can become free, make use of it all the more. For the slave called in the Lord is the Lord's freedman; likewise the free man who was called is Christ's slave. You were bought at a price; do not become slaves of human beings. Brothers, let each remain before God in the condition in which he was called. Concerning virgins, I have no command from the Lord, but I give counsel as one who has received mercy from the Lord to be faithful. Therefore, I think this is good because of the pressing necessity: it is good for a man to remain as he is. Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek release. Are you released from a wife? Do not seek a wife. But if you marry, you have not sinned; and if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. Nevertheless, such people will have tribulation in the flesh, and I would spare you. Therefore, I say this, brothers: time is short. What remains is that those with wives should be as though they had none; those who weep as though not weeping; those who rejoice as though not rejoicing; and those who use this world as though not using it. For this world's form is passing away. I want you to be free from anxiety. The unmarried man is concerned with the Lord's things, how to please God. But the married man is concerned with this world's things, how to please his wife, and he is divided. The unmarried woman and the virgin think about the Lord's things, so as to be holy in both body and spirit. But the married woman thinks about the world's things, how to please her husband. I say this for your benefit, not to put a snare upon you, but for what is honorable and offers the opportunity to entreat the Lord without hindrance. But if anyone thinks he appears to be acting dishonorably toward his virgin because she is beyond the flower of youth, and it ought to be so, let him do as he wishes; he does not sin if she marries. But the one who has resolved firmly in his heart, having no necessity but having authority over his own will, and has judged in his heart to keep his virgin, does well. Thus the one who gives his virgin in marriage does well, and the one who does not does better. A woman is bound by law for as long as her husband lives; but if her husband falls asleep, she is released. Let her marry whom she wishes, only in the Lord. But she will be happier if she remains as she is, according to my counsel. And I think that I too have God's Spirit [7:1–40].

**350. 350.** [1 Cor 8]. Concerning what is sacrificed to idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge inflates, but love builds up. If anyone thinks he knows something, he has not yet come to know as he ought. But if anyone loves God, that person is known by him. Concerning foods sacrificed to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world [8:1–4].  
*And a little later:* But not everyone has knowledge. Some, still conscious of the idol, eat as though it were food offered to an idol; and their conscience, being weak, is defiled. Food does not commend us to God. If we do not eat, we will not be worse off; if we eat, we will not be better off. But take care lest this liberty of yours become an obstacle to the weak. For if someone sees a person who has knowledge reclining in an idol's temple, will not his conscience, being weak, be built up toward eating food offered to idols? Through your knowledge the weak brother for whom Christ died will perish. Thus, by sinning against your brothers and striking their weak conscience, you sin against Christ. Therefore, if food leads my brother into sin, I will never eat meat, lest I lead my brother into sin [8:7–13].

**351. 351.** [1 Cor 9]. *And a little later:* Do we not have the right to eat and drink? Do we not have the right to take a sister, a woman, along with us, as do the other Apostles, the Lord's brothers, and Cephas? Or are Barnabas and I alone without the right to do this? Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard without eating its fruit? Who tends a flock without feeding on its milk? Do I say these things according to human reasoning? Does not the Law say these things too? For it is written in Moses's Law: You shall not bind the mouth of an ox threshing grain[^3]. Is God concerned about oxen? Or does he say it entirely for us? It was indeed written for us, because the plowman ought to plow in hope, and the thresher in hope of receiving the fruit. If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your material things? If others share in this authority over you, should we not do so all the more? Yet we have not used this authority; instead, we endure everything so as to place no obstacle before Christ's Gospel. Do you not know that those working in the sanctuary eat what comes from the sanctuary, and those serving the altar share with the altar? In the same way the Lord ordained that those proclaiming the Gospel should live from the Gospel. But I have used none of these things, and I have not written this so that they may be done for me. For it is better for me to die than for anyone to empty my glory. If I preach the Gospel, I have no glory, for necessity is laid upon me: woe to me if I do not preach the Gospel. If I do this willingly, I have a reward; if unwillingly, a stewardship has been entrusted to me. What, then, is my reward? That in preaching I offer Christ's Gospel free of charge, so as not to make full use of my authority in the Gospel. For though I was free from everyone, I made myself everyone's slave to gain more people. To the Jews I became as a Jew, to gain Jews; to those under the Law, as though under the Law, although I myself was not under the Law but under grace, to gain those under the Law. To those without the Law, as though without the Law, although I was not without God's Law but within Christ's Law, to gain those without the Law. To the weak I became as weak, to gain the weak. I became everything to everyone, to gain everyone. I do everything for the Gospel's sake, to become a sharer in it. Do you not know that those running in a stadium all run, but one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may obtain it. Everyone contending in a contest abstains from everything; they do so to receive a corruptible crown, but we an incorruptible one. Therefore, I run in this way, not aimlessly; I fight in this way, not as though beating the air. Instead, I discipline my body and bring it into slavery, lest after preaching to others I myself become rejected [9:4–27].

**352. 352.** [1 Cor 10]. For I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud [10:1].  
*And a very few* *verses later:* But God was not well pleased with most of them, for they were struck down in the desert. These things happened as a figure of us, so that we should not desire evil things as they did. Do not become idolaters, as some of them did, as it is written: The people sat down to eat and drink and rose up to play[^4]. Nor let us commit fornication, as some of them did, and twenty-three thousand fell in one day[^5]. Nor let us tempt Christ, as some of them did, and they perished by serpents. Nor should you murmur, as some of them did, and they perished by the destroyer. All these things befell them as a figure, and were written for our correction, upon whom the ends of the ages have come. Therefore, whoever thinks he stands should take care lest he fall. Let no temptation take hold of you except what is human. God is faithful and does not allow you to be tempted beyond your strength, but with the temptation will also provide an outcome that enables you to endure it. Therefore, my dearest ones, flee the worship of idols [10:5–14].  
*And a little later:* But what the gentiles sacrifice they sacrifice to demons, not to God. I do not want you to become companions of demons. You cannot drink the Lord's cup and the cup of demons. You cannot share in the Lord's table and the table of demons. Or are we provoking the Lord to jealousy? Are we stronger than he? Everything is permitted me, but not everything is beneficial. Everything is permitted me, but not everything builds up. No one should seek his own advantage, but another's. Eat everything sold in the market without questioning on account of conscience[^6] [10:20–25].  
*And a little later:* But if anyone says: This has been sacrificed to idols, do not eat it, for the sake of the one who pointed it out and for conscience's sake. I mean not your conscience but the other's. For why is my freedom judged by another conscience? If I partake with thanksgiving, why am I blasphemed for what I give thanks for? Therefore, whether you eat, drink, or do anything else, do everything for God's glory. Give no offense to Jews, gentiles, or God's Church, just as I too please everyone in everything, seeking not my own advantage but that of the many, so that they may be saved [10:28–33].

**353. 353.** [1 Cor 11]. Be imitators of me, as I too am of Christ [11:1].  
*And a little later:* Every man praying or prophesying with his head covered dishonors his head. Every woman praying or prophesying with her head uncovered dishonors her head [11:4–5].  
*And somewhat later:* In giving this command, however, I do not praise you, because you come together not for the better but for the worse. First, when you assemble in church, I hear that there are divisions among you, and I partly believe it. For there must also be heresies, so that those approved may become manifest among you. Therefore, when you assemble together, it is no longer possible to eat the Lord's Supper. For each takes his own supper beforehand to eat, and one is hungry while another is drunk. Do you not have houses for eating and drinking? Or do you despise God's Church and shame those who have nothing? What shall I say to you? Shall I praise you? In this I do not praise you [11:17–22].  
*And some verses later:* Therefore, anyone who eats the bread or drinks the Lord's cup unworthily will be guilty of the Lord's Body and Blood. A person should examine himself and thus eat of that bread and drink of that cup. For the one who eats and drinks unworthily eats and drinks judgment upon himself, failing to discern the Lord's Body. Therefore, many among you are sick and weak, and many have fallen asleep. But if we discerned ourselves, we would certainly not be judged. When we are judged, however, we are corrected by the Lord so that we may not be condemned with this world. Therefore, my brothers, when you come together to eat, wait for one another. If anyone is hungry, let him eat at home, so that you may not come together for judgment. I will arrange the remaining matters when I come [11:27–34].

**354. 354.** [1 Cor 12]. *And in another place:* But God has arranged the body, giving more abundant honor to the part that lacked it, so that there may be no schism in the body, but the members may have the same concern for one another. If one member suffers anything, all the members suffer together; if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice together. You are Christ's body, and members from a member. God has appointed certain people in the Church: first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then mighty works, then the grace of healings, governance, assistance, kinds of tongues, and interpretations of speech. Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all workers of mighty deeds? Do all have the grace of healings? Do all speak in tongues? Do all interpret? But strive for the greater gifts. And I show you a still more excellent way [12:24–31].

**355. 355.** [1 Cor 13]. If I speak in the tongues of human beings and angels but have no charity, I have become like sounding bronze or a clanging cymbal. If I have prophecy and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so as to move mountains, but have no charity, I am nothing. If I distribute all my possessions to feed the poor, and if I surrender my body to burn, but have no charity, it profits me nothing. Charity is patient and kind. Charity does not envy, does not act wrongly, is not inflated, is not ambitious, does not seek its own, is not provoked, does not think evil, does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices with the truth. It bears everything, believes everything, hopes for everything, and endures everything. Charity will never fail [13:1–8].  
*And some verses later:* Now these three remain: faith, hope, and charity; but the greater among these is charity [13:13].

**356. 356.** [1 Cor 14]. Pursue charity [14:1].  
*And a little later:* Since you are eager for spirits, seek to abound for the building up of the Church [14:12].  
*Again, a little later:* Brothers, do not become children in your understanding; be little children in malice, but mature in understanding [14:20].  
*And in another place:* What, then, brothers? When you gather, does each of you have a psalm, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, an interpretation? Let everything be done for building up [14:26].  
*And a very few* *verses later:* Women should be silent in the churches, for they are not permitted to speak but to be subject, as the Law too says[^7]. If they wish to learn anything, they should ask their husbands at home. For it is shameful for a woman to speak in the Church. Did God's word originate with you, or has it reached you alone? [14:34–36].

**357. 357.** [1 Cor 15]. *And a little later:* Do not be deceived: bad conversations corrupt good conduct. Awake righteously and do not sin, for some have ignorance of God; I speak to bring you to shame [15:33–34].  
*And a little later:* Therefore, my beloved brothers, be firm and immovable, always abounding in the Lord's work, knowing that your labor is not empty in the Lord [15:58].

**358. 358.** [1 Cor 16]. Concerning the collections being made for the saints, do as I directed in the churches of Galatia. On the first day of the week, each of you should put something aside at home, storing up whatever he has decided upon, so that collections need not be made when I come. When I am present, I will send those you approve with letters to carry your gift to Jerusalem. If it is fitting for me to go too, they will go with me. I will come to you when I have passed through Macedonia, for I am going to pass through Macedonia. Perhaps I will remain with you, or even spend the winter, so that you may send me on my way wherever I go. For I do not want to see you now merely in passing; I hope to stay with you for some time, if the Lord permits. But I will remain at Ephesus until Pentecost, for a great and evident door has opened to me, and there are many adversaries. If Timothy comes, see that he is among you without fear [16:1–10].  
*And a little later:* Keep watch, stand in faith, act courageously, and be strengthened. Let all your affairs be conducted in love [16:13–14].
