# From the Letter to Titus

**397. 397.** [Titus 1]. I left you in Crete for this reason: to correct what is lacking and appoint presbyters in the cities, as I directed you. A man should be free of accusation, the husband of one wife, with believing children not accused of extravagance or insubordinate. For a bishop must be free of accusation as God's steward: not proud, irascible, given to wine or blows, or eager for shameful gain, but hospitable, kind, sober, just, holy, self-controlled, and embracing the faithful word according to the teaching, so that he may be able to exhort in sound teaching and reprove opponents [1:5–9].  
*And a few* *verses later:* For this reason rebuke them sharply, so that they may be sound in faith, not attending to Jewish fables and commandments of people who turn away from truth. Everything is clean for the clean; but for the defiled and unbelieving nothing is clean, and both their mind and conscience are defiled. They profess to know God, but deny him by their deeds, being abominable, unbelieving, and unfit for every good work [1:13–16].

**398. 398.** [Titus 2]. But you, speak what befits sound teaching: that older men be sober, chaste, prudent, and sound in faith, love, and patience. Older women likewise should be holy in their manner, not accusers or enslaved to much wine, teaching well, so that they may teach the young women prudence: to love their husbands and children, to be prudent, chaste, attentive to their homes, kind, and subject to their own husbands, so that God's word may not be blasphemed. Likewise exhort young men to be sober. In everything present yourself as an example of good works, with integrity and dignity in teaching. Let your speech be sound and irreproachable, so that the opponent may feel shame, having nothing evil to say about us. Slaves should be subject to their masters, pleasing them in everything, not contradicting or defrauding them but showing good faith in everything, so that in everything they may adorn the teaching of God our savior. For the grace of God the savior has appeared to all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly desires and to live soberly, justly, and piously in this age, awaiting the blessed hope and the coming of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ. He gave himself for us to redeem us from every iniquity and cleanse for himself an acceptable people devoted to good works. Speak these things, exhort, and reprove with all authority. Let no one despise you [2:1–15].

**399. 399.** [Titus 3]. Remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities, to obey orders, to be ready for every good work, to blaspheme no one, to be moderate rather than quarrelsome, and to show all gentleness toward all people [3:1–2].  
*And a few verses later:* The saying is faithful, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who believe God may take care to lead in good works. These things are good and useful for people. But avoid foolish questions, genealogies, contentions, and battles about the law, for they are useless and empty. Avoid a heretical person after a first and second correction, knowing that such a person is subverted and sins, condemned by his own judgment [3:8–11].
