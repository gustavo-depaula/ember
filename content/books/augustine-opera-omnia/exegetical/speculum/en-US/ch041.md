# From the Gospel According to Luke: **305. 305.** [Luke 2]. Glory to God in the highest places, and peace on earth to people of good will [2:14].

**306. 306.** [Luke 3]. *And somewhat later:* The crowds questioned him, saying: What, then, shall we do? He answered them: Whoever has two tunics should give to someone who has none, and whoever has food should do likewise. Tax collectors also came to be baptized and said to him: Teacher, what shall we do? He told them: Collect nothing beyond what has been prescribed for you. Soldiers too questioned him, saying: And what shall we do? He told them: Do not extort from anyone or make false accusations, and be content with your pay [3:10–14].

**307. 307.** [Luke 6]. *And somewhat later:* Blessed are the poor, because God's kingdom is yours. Blessed are you who are hungry now, because you will be satisfied. Blessed are you who weep now, because you will laugh. You will be blessed when people hate you, exclude you, reproach you, and cast out your name as evil because of the Son of man. Rejoice on that day and exult, for see, your reward in heaven is great; their fathers treated the prophets in the same way. Nevertheless, woe to you who are rich, because you have your consolation. Woe to you who are satisfied, because you will be hungry. Woe to you who laugh now, because you will mourn and weep. Woe when everyone speaks well of you; their fathers treated the prophets in this way. But to you who listen, I say: Love your enemies, and do good to those who hate you. Bless those who curse you. Pray for those who slander you. To someone who strikes you on one cheek, offer the other as well; and from someone who takes your outer garment, do not withhold your tunic either. Give to everyone who asks you, and do not demand back your belongings from someone who takes them. Treat people in the same way you want them to treat you. If you love those who love you, what credit is that to you? Even sinners love those who love them. If you do good to those who do good to you, what credit is that to you? Sinners too do this. If you lend to those from whom you hope to receive repayment, what credit is that to you? Even sinners lend to sinners to receive the equivalent back. Nevertheless, love your enemies; do good, and lend, hoping from this, and your reward will be great, and you will be children of the Most High, because he himself is kind toward the ungrateful and evil. Therefore, be merciful, as your Father too is merciful. Do not judge, and you will not be judged. Do not condemn, and you will not be condemned. Forgive, and you will be forgiven. Give, and you will be given to. They will pour into your lap a good measure, packed down, shaken together, and overflowing. For the same measure with which you measure will be measured back to you. He also told them a comparison: Can a blind person lead a blind person? Do not both fall into a pit? A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone will be perfected if he is like his teacher. Why do you see the speck in your brother's eye but fail to consider the beam in your own eye? How can you tell your brother, 'Brother, let me take the speck out of your eye,' when you yourself do not see the beam in your own eye? Hypocrite, first take the beam out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother's eye. For no good tree produces bad fruit, nor does a bad tree produce good fruit. Every tree is recognized by its own fruit. People do not gather figs from thorns, nor harvest grapes from a bramble. A good man brings forth good from the good treasure of his heart; an evil man brings forth evil from his evil treasure. The mouth speaks from the heart's abundance. Why do you call me 'Lord, Lord,' and fail to do what I say? I will show you what everyone who comes to me, hears my words, and does them is like. He is like a man building a house who dug deep and laid its foundation upon rock. When a flood came, the river struck that man's house and could not shake it, for it was founded on rock. But someone who hears and does not act is like a man building his house on the ground without a foundation. The river struck it, and it immediately fell, and great was the collapse of that house [6:20–49].

**308. 308.** [Luke 7]. *And somewhat later:* To what, then, shall I compare the people of this generation? What are they like? They are like children sitting in the marketplace, speaking to one another and saying: We played flutes for you, and you did not dance; we lamented, and you did not weep. For John the Baptist came neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and you say: He has a demon. The Son of man came eating and drinking, and you say: Look, a gluttonous man and a wine drinker, a friend of tax collectors and sinners. And wisdom has been justified by all her children [7:31–35].

**309. 309.** [Luke 8]. *Again, somewhat later:* This is the parable: the seed is God's word. Those beside the road are the ones who hear; then the devil comes and takes the word from their hearts, so that they may not believe and be saved. Those on the rock are the ones who, when they hear, welcome the word joyfully. These have no roots, because they believe for a time and withdraw in a time of temptation. What fell among thorns represents those who have heard and, as they go along, are choked by life's worries, riches, and pleasures, and bear no fruit. What fell on good ground represents those who hear the word with a good and excellent heart, retain it, and bear fruit with patience. No one lights a lamp and covers it with a vessel or puts it under a bed; instead, he puts it on a lampstand, so that those entering may see the light. For there is nothing concealed that will not be revealed, or hidden that will not be known and come out into the open. Therefore, consider how you have heard. For to the one who has, more will be given; and from anyone who does not have, even what he thinks he has will be taken away [8:11–18].

**310. 310.** [Luke 9]. *And somewhat later:* Calling together the twelve Apostles, he gave them power and authority over all demons and to cure diseases. He sent them to preach God's kingdom and heal the sick. He told them: Take nothing for the journey: no staff, bag, bread, or money, and do not have two tunics. Whatever house you enter, stay there and do not leave it. As for anyone who does not welcome you, as you leave that city, shake even the dust from your feet as a testimony against them [9:1–5].  
*Again, somewhat later:* He said to everyone: Anyone who wants to come after me must renounce himself, carry his cross daily, and follow me. For anyone who wants to preserve his life will lose it. But anyone who loses his life on my account will save it. What does a man gain if he gains the whole world but loses himself and suffers his own ruin? For anyone who is ashamed of me and my words, the Son of man will be ashamed of him when he comes in his majesty and that of his Father and the holy angels [9:23–26].  
*And in another place:* A thought entered their minds as to which of them was greater. But Jesus, seeing the thoughts of their hearts, took a child, placed him beside himself, and told them: Anyone who welcomes this child in my name welcomes me, and anyone who welcomes me welcomes the one who sent me. For the one who is lesser among you all is greater [9:46–48].  
*And somewhat later:* He told another: Follow me. But he said: Lord, first allow me to go and bury my father. Jesus said: Leave the dead to bury their own dead; but you go and announce God's kingdom. Another said: I will follow you, Lord, but first allow me to take leave of those at home. Jesus told him: No one who puts his hand to a plow and looks behind him is fit for God's kingdom [9:59–62].

**311. 311.** [Luke 10]. *And a little later:* See, I send you like lambs among wolves. Carry no purse, bag, or sandals, and greet no one along the road. Whatever house you enter, first say: Peace to this house. If a son of peace is there, your peace will rest upon that house; otherwise, it will return to you. Stay in that same house, eating and drinking what they have, for the worker deserves his wages. Do not move from house to house. Whatever city you enter where they welcome you, eat whatever is set before you. Heal its sick and tell them: God's kingdom has drawn near to you. Whatever city you enter where they do not welcome you, go out into its streets and say: Even the dust of your city that has clung to us we wipe off against you. Nevertheless, know this: God's kingdom has drawn near. I tell you that on that day Sodom will be treated more leniently than that city [10:3–12].  
*And a little later:* Nevertheless, do not rejoice because the spirits are subject to you; rejoice instead because your names are written in heaven [10:20].  
*And a little later:* See, an expert in the law stood up to test him, saying: Teacher, by doing what shall I possess eternal life? He told him: What is written in the Law? How do you read it? He answered: You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, your whole soul, all your strength, and your whole mind, and your neighbor as yourself [^1] [10:25–27].  
*And a little later:* Which of these three seems to you to have been a neighbor to the man who fell among robbers? He said: The one who showed him mercy. Jesus told him: Go and do likewise yourself [10:36–37].

*And a little later:* Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled over many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the best portion, which will not be taken from her [10:41–42].

**312. 312.** [Luke 11]. *Again, a little later:* When you pray, *he says,* say: Father, may your name be sanctified. May your kingdom come. Give us our daily bread today. And forgive us our sins, since we ourselves forgive everyone indebted to us. And do not lead us into temptation. He told them: Which of you will have a friend and go to him at midnight and say: Friend, lend me three loaves, because a friend of mine has arrived from a journey and I have nothing to set before him; and the other will answer from inside: Do not bother me; the door is already shut, and my children are in bed with me; I cannot get up and give you anything? If the first keeps on knocking, I tell you, even if the other does not get up and give to him because he is his friend, nevertheless he will get up because of his persistence and give him as many as he needs. And I tell you: Ask, and it will be given to you; search, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, the one who searches finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened. Now if any of you asks his father for bread, will he give him a stone? Or for a fish, will he give him a snake instead of a fish? Or if he asks for an egg, will he offer him a scorpion? Therefore, if you, though evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father from heaven give a good spirit to those who ask him? [11:2–13].  
*And a little later:* As he said these things, a woman in the crowd raised her voice and told him: Blessed is the womb that carried you, and the breasts you nursed at. But he said: Rather, blessed are those who hear God's word and observe it [11:27–28].  
*And a little later:* While he was speaking, a Pharisee asked him to dine with him. He entered and reclined at table. The Pharisee began reflecting inwardly and asking why he had not washed before dinner. The Lord told him: Now you Pharisees clean the outside of the cup and dish, but your inside is full of robbery and iniquity. Fools, did not the one who made the outside make the inside too? Nevertheless, give what remains as alms, and see, everything is clean for you. But woe to you Pharisees, because you tithe mint, rue, and every vegetable, and pass over justice and God's love. You ought to have done these without leaving the others undone. Woe to you Pharisees, because you love the foremost seats in synagogues and greetings in the marketplace. Woe to you, because you are like tombs that are not visible, and people walk over them without knowing. One of the experts in the law answered him: Teacher, in saying these things, you insult us too. But he said: Woe to you experts in the law as well, because you burden people with burdens that cannot be carried, while you yourselves do not touch the loads with even one finger [11:37–46].

**313. 313.** [Luke 12]. *And in another place:* Beware of the Pharisees' leaven, which is hypocrisy [12:1].  
*And a little later:* I tell you, my friends: do not be terrified of those who kill the body and after that have nothing further they can do. I will show you whom to fear. Fear the one who, after killing, has the power to send into hell. Yes, I tell you, fear him. Are not five sparrows sold for two pennies? Yet not one of them is forgotten before God. Even the hairs of your head are all numbered. Therefore, do not fear; you are worth more than many sparrows. I tell you: anyone who acknowledges me before people, the Son of man will also acknowledge him before God's angels; but anyone who denies me before people will be denied before God's angels. Everyone who speaks a word against the Son of man will be forgiven, but anyone who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven. When they bring you into synagogues and before magistrates and authorities, do not be anxious about how or what to answer, or what to say. For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say. Someone in the crowd told him: Teacher, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me. He told him: Man, who appointed me a judge or divider over you? And he told them: Take care and beware of every kind of greed, because a person's life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions. He told them a comparison, saying: The land of a certain rich man produced abundant crops, and he thought to himself: What shall I do, since I have nowhere to gather my crops? He said: I will do this: I will pull down my barns and build larger ones, and gather all my produce and goods there. Then I will say to my soul: Soul, you have many goods stored up for very many years; rest, eat, drink, and feast. But God told him: Fool, this very night they demand your soul from you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be? So it is with everyone who stores up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God. He told his disciples: Therefore, I tell you, do not be anxious about your life, what to eat, or about your body, what to wear. Life is more than food, and the body more than clothing. Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap; they have neither storeroom nor barn, and God feeds them. How much more are you worth than they? Which of you, by worrying, can add one cubit to his height? If, then, you cannot do even the smallest thing, why are you anxious about the rest? Consider how the lilies grow: they neither labor nor spin. Yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not clothed like one of these. If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is in the field today and is thrown into an oven tomorrow, how much more you, with your little faith? Do not seek what you will eat or drink, and do not be lifted on high. For the world's nations seek all these things, but your Father knows you need them. Nevertheless, seek God's kingdom, and all these things will be added to you. Do not fear, little flock, because your Father has been pleased to give you the kingdom. Sell what you possess and give alms. Make for yourselves purses that do not wear out, an unfailing treasure in heaven, where no thief approaches and no moth destroys. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be too [12:4–34].  
*And a little later:* You too must be ready, because the Son of man will come at an hour you do not expect. Peter told him: Lord, are you telling this parable to us or to everyone as well? The Lord said: Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent steward whom his master will place over his household to give the ration of grain at the proper time? Blessed is that slave whom the master finds acting in this way when he comes. Truly I tell you, he will place him over everything he possesses. But if that slave says in his heart: My master is taking a long time to come, and begins beating the male and female slaves, eating, drinking, and getting drunk, that slave's master will come on a day he does not expect and at an hour he does not know. He will cut him apart and assign his portion with unbelievers. That slave who knew his master's will but did not prepare or act according to his will will receive many blows; but the one who did not know and did things deserving blows will receive few. From everyone who has been given much, much will be required; and from anyone entrusted with much, they will ask more [12:40–48].  
*Again, a little later:* Hypocrites, you know how to assess the appearance of earth and sky; how is it that you do not assess this time? And why do you not judge for yourselves what is right? [12:56–57].

**314. 314.** [Luke 13]. *And somewhat later:* Strive to enter through the narrow gate, because many, I tell you, seek to enter and will be unable [13:24].

**315. 315.** [Luke 14]. *And in another place:* Noticing how the guests chose the foremost places, he told them a parable, saying: When you have been invited to a wedding, do not recline in the foremost place, lest someone more honored than you have been invited by the host, and the man who invited you both come and tell you: Give this man your place. Then you would begin occupying the last place with embarrassment. Instead, when invited, go and recline in the last place, so that when your host comes he may tell you: Friend, come up higher. Then you will have honor before those reclining at table with you. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and anyone who humbles himself will be exalted. He also told his host: When you give a dinner or supper, do not invite your friends, brothers, relatives, or rich neighbors, lest they in turn invite you back and you receive repayment. Instead, when you give a feast, invite the poor, the disabled, the lame, and the blind. You will be blessed because they have no means of repaying you, for you will be repaid at the recompense of the righteous [14:7–14].  
*And a little later:* If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father and mother, wife, children, brothers, sisters, and even his own life, he cannot be my disciple. And anyone who does not carry his cross and come after me cannot be my disciple. For which of you, wanting to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the necessary expense to see whether he has enough to finish it? Otherwise, after he has laid a foundation and cannot finish, everyone who sees may begin mocking him and saying: This man began to build and could not complete it. Or what king about to wage war against another king does not first sit down and consider whether with ten thousand men he can confront someone coming against him with twenty thousand? Otherwise, while the other is still far away, he sends a delegation and asks for terms of peace. In the same way, then, any of you who does not renounce everything he possesses cannot be my disciple [14:26–33].

**316. 316.** [Luke 16]. *And somewhat later:* And I tell you: Make friends for yourselves from the mammon of iniquity, so that when you fail, they may welcome you into eternal dwellings. Anyone faithful in the smallest matter is faithful in a greater one too; anyone unjust in a small matter is unjust in a greater one too. Therefore, if you have not been faithful with unjust mammon, who will entrust you with what is true? And if you have not been faithful with what belongs to another, who will give you what is yours? No one can serve two masters, for he will either hate one and love the other, or cling to one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon. The Pharisees, who were greedy, heard all this and mocked him. He told them: You are the ones who justify yourselves before people, but God knows your hearts; for what is exalted among people is an abomination before God [16:9–15].  
*And a few verses later:* Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery [16:18].

**317. 317.** [Luke 17]. *And a little later:* It is impossible for occasions of sin not to come, but woe to the person through whom they come! It would be better for him if a millstone were placed around his neck and he were thrown into the sea than for him to lead one of these little ones into sin. Take care of yourselves: if your brother sins, rebuke him; if he repents, forgive him. And if he sins against you seven times in a day and turns to you seven times in a day, saying, 'I repent,' forgive him [17:1–4].

**318. 318.** [Luke 18]. *And in another place:* To some who trusted in themselves as righteous and scorned everyone else, he told this parable: Two men went up to the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector. Standing, the Pharisee prayed these things to himself: Lord, I thank you that I am not like other people—robbers, unjust men, adulterers—or even like this tax collector. I fast twice a week; I give a tenth of everything I possess. The tax collector stood far away and would not even raise his eyes toward heaven, but struck his breast, saying: God, be merciful to me, a sinner. I tell you, this man went down to his house justified in comparison with the other. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and anyone who humbles himself will be exalted. They were also bringing infants to him so that he might touch them. When the disciples saw this, they rebuked them. But Jesus called them to him and said: Allow the children to come to me, and do not forbid them, for God's kingdom belongs to such as these. I tell you truly: anyone who does not receive God's kingdom like a child will not enter it. A certain ruler questioned him, saying: Good teacher, by doing what shall I possess eternal life? Jesus said: Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone. Do you know the commandments? You shall not kill; you shall not commit adultery; you shall not steal; you shall not bear false witness; honor your father and mother[^2]. He said: I have kept all these from my youth. On hearing this, Jesus told him: You still lack one thing: sell everything you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come and follow me. Hearing this, the man grew sad, because he was very rich. Seeing that he had become sad, Jesus said: How difficult it will be for those who have wealth to enter God's kingdom! For it is easier for a camel to pass through a needle's eye than for a rich man to enter God's kingdom. Those listening said: Who, then, can be saved? He told them: What is impossible with human beings is possible with God. Peter said: See, we have left everything and followed you. He told them: I tell you truly: there is no one who has left house, parents, brothers, wife, or children for the sake of God's kingdom who will not receive much more in this time, and eternal life in the age to come [18:9–30].

**319. 319.** [Luke 19]. *And elsewhere:* Standing, Zacchaeus told the Lord: See, Lord, I give half my goods to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I repay it fourfold. Jesus told him: Today salvation has come to this house, because he too is a son of Abraham. For the Son of man came to seek and save what had been lost [19:8–10].

**320. 320.** [Luke 20]. *And somewhat later:* Give back, then, to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God [20:25].  
*And in another place:* With all the people listening, he told his disciples: Beware of the scribes, who want to walk about in long robes and love greetings in the marketplace, the foremost seats in synagogues, and the foremost places at feasts. They devour widows' houses while making a show of long prayer; they will receive a greater condemnation [20:45–47].

**321. 321.** [Luke 21]. Looking up, he saw rich people putting their gifts into the treasury. He also saw a poor little widow putting in two small coins, and said: I tell you truly that this poor widow has put in more than everyone else. For all these people contributed to God's offerings from their surplus, but she, from her want, put in her entire livelihood, all she had [21:1–4].  
*Again, in another place:* Settle it, then, in your hearts not to rehearse beforehand how to answer. For I will give you speech and wisdom that all your adversaries will be unable to resist or contradict. You will be handed over by parents, brothers, relatives, and friends, and they will put some of you to death. You will be hated by everyone because of my name, but not a hair of your head will perish. In your patience you will possess your souls [21:14–19].  
*And somewhat later:* Take care of yourselves, lest your hearts become weighed down by overindulgence, drunkenness, and this life's cares, and that day come upon you suddenly. For it will come like a snare upon everyone dwelling on the face of the entire earth. Therefore, keep watch, praying at every time, so that you may be considered worthy to escape all these things that are going to happen and to stand before the Son of man [21:34–36].

**322. 322.** [Luke 22]. *Again, somewhat later:* A dispute arose among them about which of them seemed to be greater. He told them: The kings of the nations lord it over them, and those who hold power over them are called benefactors. But you must not be so. Instead, the greater among you should become like the younger, and the leader like one who serves. For who is greater, the one reclining at table or the one serving? Is it not the one reclining? Yet I am among you like one who serves [22:24–27].  
*And in another place:* Pray that you may not enter into temptation [22:40].

[^1]: Cf. Deut 6:5; Lev 19:18
[^2]: Cf. Exod 20:13
