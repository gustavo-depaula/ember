# From the Book of the Acts of the Apostles

**329. 329.** [Acts 15]. *James, then, speaking, says:* Therefore, *he says,* I judge that those turning to the Lord from among the gentiles should not be troubled, but that we should write to them to abstain from the pollutions of idols, fornication, strangled animals, and blood [15:19–20].  
*And a little later:* For it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to place no further burden upon you beyond these necessary things: that you abstain from sacrifices offered to idols, blood, strangled meat, and fornication. If you keep yourselves from these things, you will do well. Farewell [15:28–29].

**330. 330.** [Acts 20]. *And much later, when the Apostle Paul was addressing the leaders of the Church whom he had gathered*, *he said:* Attend, *he says,* to yourselves and to the whole flock in which the Holy Spirit has placed you as bishops to govern God's Church, which he acquired with his own blood [20:28].  
*And a little later, the same man to the same people:* I coveted no one's silver, gold, or clothing. You yourselves know that these hands provided for my needs and for those with me. In everything I have shown you that by laboring in this way we ought to support the weak and remember the word of the Lord Jesus, who himself said: It is more blessed to give than to receive [20:33–35].

**331. 331.** [Acts 21]. *And in another place, when James and those with him were speaking to the Apostle Paul, they told him:* You see, brother, how many thousands among the Jews have believed, and all are zealous for the Law. They have also heard about you that you teach the Jews living among the gentiles to abandon Moses, saying that they should not circumcise their sons or follow the customary way of life. What, then, is to be done? The crowd must certainly assemble, for they will hear that you have arrived. Therefore, do what we tell you. We have four men under a vow. Take them with you, sanctify yourself with them, and pay their expenses so that they may shave their heads[^1]. Then everyone will know that what they heard about you is false, and that you yourself walk in observance of the Law. As for the gentiles who have believed, we have written, judging that they should abstain from food sacrificed to idols, blood, strangled meat, and fornication [21:20–25].

*We have found these passages from the book of the Acts of the Apostles suitable and sufficient to include in this work of ours. There we see that the Apostles did not wish to impose any burdens of the Old Law upon believers from among the gentiles, so far as abstinence from bodily pleasure was concerned, except that they should keep themselves from these three things: what was sacrificed to idols, blood, and fornication. Hence some think that only three sins are deadly: idolatry, murder, and fornication; under the last, of course, adultery and all intercourse with anyone other than one's wife are understood. As though any other sins besides these three that separate people from God's kingdom were not deadly, or as though it were said vainly and falsely:* Neither thieves nor the greedy, neither drunkards nor the abusive nor robbers will possess God's kingdom[^2].

*But we ought now to restrain our discussion, come to the apostolic letters, and see what in them too may fittingly be adapted to this work.*

[^1]: Cf. Num 6:18
[^2]: 1 Cor 6:10
