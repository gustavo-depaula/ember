# From the Second Letter to the Corinthians

**359. 359.** [2 Cor 1]. For this is our glory: the testimony of our conscience that we have conducted ourselves in the world in God's simplicity and sincerity, not in fleshly wisdom but in God's grace [1:12].

**360. 360.** [2 Cor 2]. *And a little later:* If anyone has saddened me, he has saddened not me, but to some extent—to avoid burdening him—all of you. For such a person, this rebuke inflicted by the majority is sufficient. On the contrary, you should rather forgive and console him, lest such a person be swallowed up by excessive sadness. Therefore, I appeal to you to confirm your love for him. For I wrote for this reason: to know the proof of you, whether you are obedient in everything. Whatever you have forgiven anyone, I forgive too. For what I have forgiven, if I have forgiven anything, I have done for your sake in Christ's person, so that we may not be outwitted by Satan, for we are not ignorant of his thoughts [2:5–11].

**361. 361.** [2 Cor 4]. *And in another place:* Therefore, having this ministry according to the mercy we have received, we do not lose heart. Instead, we renounce shameful hidden things, neither walking in cunning nor adulterating God's word, but by manifesting the truth commending ourselves to every human conscience before God [4:1–2].  
*And a few verses later:* For we preach not ourselves but Jesus Christ as Lord, and ourselves as your slaves through Jesus [4:5].  
*And a little later:* We have this treasure in earthen vessels, so that the surpassing power may be God's and not from us. We suffer tribulation in everything but are not crushed; we are at a loss but not abandoned; we suffer persecution but are not forsaken; we are cast down but do not perish. We always carry Jesus's death in our body, so that Jesus's life too may be revealed in our bodies. For we who live are always being handed over to death because of Jesus, so that Jesus's life too may be revealed in our mortal flesh. Thus death works in us, but life in you. Having the same spirit of faith, as it is written: I believed, therefore I spoke (cf. Ps 116:10; 115:1 Vg), we too believe, and therefore we speak [4:7–13].  
*And six verses later:* Therefore, we do not lose heart. Even though our outer man is being corrupted, our inner man is nevertheless renewed day by day. For the present momentary and light burden of our tribulation produces in us an eternal weight of glory immeasurably exalted, as we contemplate not the things seen but the things unseen. For the things seen are temporal, but the things unseen are eternal [4:16–18].

**362. 362.** [2 Cor 6]. *And a little later:* Working together, we urge you not to receive God's grace in vain. For he says: In an acceptable time I heard you, and on a day of salvation I helped you[^1]. See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation. We give no offense to anyone, so that our ministry may not be blamed. Instead, in everything let us present ourselves as God's servants: in great patience, tribulations, necessities, anguishes, beatings, imprisonments, riots, labors, vigils, fasts, chastity, knowledge, forbearance, gentleness, the Holy Spirit, unfeigned love, the word of truth, and God's power; through the weapons of righteousness on the right and on the left; through glory and dishonor, ill repute and good repute; as deceivers and yet truthful, as unknown and yet known; as dying, and see, we live; as punished and not killed; as sorrowful yet always rejoicing; as needy yet enriching many; as having nothing and possessing everything [6:1–10].  
*And a few verses later:* Be enlarged yourselves as well. Do not bear a yoke with unbelievers. For what sharing has righteousness with iniquity? Or what fellowship has light with darkness? What agreement has Christ with Belial? Or what portion has a believer with an unbeliever? What accord has God's temple with idols? For you are the living God's temple, as the Lord says: I will dwell in them and walk among them; I will be their God, and they will be my people[^2]. Therefore, come out from their midst and be separated[^3], says the Lord, and do not touch what is unclean. I will welcome you and be a father to you, and you will be sons and daughters to me, says the Lord Almighty[^4] [6:13–18].

**363. 363.** [2 Cor 7]. Therefore, having these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit, bringing sanctification to completion in God's fear [7:1].  
*And a little later:* For even if I saddened you in my letter, I do not regret it. Even if I did regret it, seeing that the letter saddened you for a time, now I rejoice—not because you were saddened, but because you were saddened into repentance. For you were saddened according to God, so that you might suffer no loss from us. Sadness according to God produces repentance unto lasting salvation, but the world's sadness produces death. For see how much diligence this very thing, your being saddened according to God, produces in you! [7:8–11].

**364. 364.** [2 Cor 8]. *And a few verses later:* We make known to you, brothers, God's grace given in the churches of Macedonia: in a great trial of tribulation, the abundance of their joy and their profound poverty overflowed into the riches of their generosity. For according to their ability—I bear witness to them—and beyond their ability, they gave willingly, begging us with great insistence for the favor of sharing in the ministry to the saints. They did not act as we had expected, but gave themselves first to the Lord and then to us through God's will, so that we asked Titus to complete this grace among you as he had begun. But just as you abound in everything—in faith, speech, knowledge, all diligence, and your love toward us—see that you abound in this grace too. I do not say this as a command, but through others' diligence I also test the good character of your love. For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ: though rich, he became poor for your sake, so that through his poverty you might be rich. And in this matter I give counsel. For this is beneficial for you, who began not only to act but also to wish to do so last year. Now bring the deed to completion as well, so that just as your will was ready, so too there may be a completion from what you have. For if the will is ready, it is accepted according to what it has, not according to what it does not have. This is not so that others may have relief while you have tribulation, but for equality: at the present time your abundance should supply their need, so that their abundance too may supply your need, and equality may result. As it is written: The one with much had no surplus, and the one with little had no lack[^5]. Thanks be to God, who placed the same diligence for you in Titus's heart. He accepted our encouragement, but being more eager, he set out for you of his own will. We sent with him the brother whose praise in the Gospel is known through all the churches. Moreover, he was appointed by the churches as our traveling companion in this grace administered by us for the Lord's glory and our resolved willingness. We avoid this: that anyone should blame us in this abundance administered by us. For we provide for good things not only before God but also before people [8:1–21].

**365. 365.** [2 Cor 9]. *And nine* *verses later:* It is indeed superfluous for me to write to you about the ministry to the saints. For I know your readiness, about which I glory on your behalf before the Macedonians: Achaia has been ready since last year, and your zeal has stirred very many people. But I have sent the brothers so that our boasting about you may not be emptied in this matter, and so that you may be ready, as I said. Otherwise, if Macedonians come with me and find you unprepared, we—to say nothing of you—would be ashamed in this confidence. Therefore, I thought it necessary to ask the brothers to go ahead to you and prepare this previously promised blessing, so that it might be ready as a blessing, not as something wrung out by greed. I say this: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows in blessings will also reap from blessings[^6]. Each should give as he has resolved in his heart, not from sadness or necessity, for God loves a cheerful giver[^7]. God can make every grace abound in you, so that always having every sufficiency in everything, you may abound in every good work. As it is written: He scattered abroad and gave to the poor; his righteousness remains forever[^8]. The one who supplies seed to the sower will also provide bread for eating, multiply your seed, and increase the growth of the fruits of your righteousness, so that enriched in everything, you may abound in all generosity, which through us produces thanksgiving to God. For the ministry of this service not only supplies what the saints lack but also abounds through many thanksgivings in the Lord. Through the proof of this ministry they glorify God for the obedience of your confession and the generosity of your sharing with them and everyone, while they themselves pray for you, longing for you because of God's surpassing grace in you. I give thanks to God for his indescribable gift [9:1–15].

**366. 366.** [2 Cor 10]. *And in another place:* Let the one who glories glory in the Lord[^9]. For the approved person is not the one who commends himself, but the one whom God commends [10:17–18].

**367. 367.** [2 Cor 11]. *And somewhat later:* Are they Christ's servants? So am I. I speak as one less wise: I am more so. In very many labors, more abundantly in prisons, in beatings beyond measure, often in deaths. Five times I received from the Jews forty blows less one. Three times I was beaten with rods, once stoned, three times shipwrecked; I spent a night and a day in the sea's depths. Often on journeys, in dangers from rivers, dangers from robbers, dangers from my own people, dangers from gentiles, dangers in the city, dangers in the wilderness, dangers at sea, dangers among false brothers; in labor and hardship, in many vigils, in hunger and thirst, in many fasts, in cold and nakedness. Besides the things from outside, there is my daily pressure: concern for all the churches. Who is weakened, and I am not weakened? Who is led into sin, and I do not burn? If I must glory, I will glory in the things of my weakness [11:23–30].

**368. 368.** [2 Cor 12]. *And a little later:* Therefore, I will gladly glory in my weaknesses, so that Christ's power may dwell in me. Thus I take pleasure in weaknesses, insults, necessities, persecutions, and many anguishes for Christ. For when I am weak, then I am powerful [12:9–10].  
*And eleven* *verses later:* See, I am ready to come to you a third time, and I will not burden you. For I seek not your possessions but you. Children ought not to store up for parents, but parents for children. I will very gladly spend and be spent myself for your souls, even if, loving you more, I am loved less [12:14–15].  
*And several verses later:* For I fear that when I come I may find you not as I wish, and you may find me as you do not wish. Perhaps there may be strife, jealousy, animosity, dissensions, slanders, whisperings, arrogance, and riots among you. Perhaps when I come again, God may humble me among you, and I may mourn many of those who sinned previously and have not repented of the impurity and shamelessness they practiced [12:20–21].

**369. 369.** [2 Cor 13]. *And in another place:* Let us pray to God that you do no evil, not so that we may appear approved, but so that you may do what is right [13:7].  
*And six verses later:* For the rest, brothers, rejoice, be perfected, be encouraged, have the same mind, and have peace; and the God of love and peace will be with you [13:11].
