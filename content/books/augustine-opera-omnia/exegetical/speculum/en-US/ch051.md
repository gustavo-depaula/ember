# From the Second Letter to the Thessalonians

**383. 383.** [2 Thess 1]. We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers, as is fitting, because your faith grows exceedingly, and the love of each and every one of you toward one another abounds. Thus we ourselves glory in you among God's churches for your patience and faith amid all your persecutions and the tribulations you endure as a demonstration of God's just judgment, so that you may be considered worthy of God's kingdom, for which you also suffer. For it is indeed just before God to repay tribulation to those who trouble you, and rest with us to you who are troubled, at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven with the angels of his power and with flaming fire. He will exact vengeance upon those who do not know God and who do not obey the Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ. They will pay eternal penalties of destruction away from the Lord's face and the glory of his power, when he comes to be glorified in his saints and to become marvelous among all who have believed, because our testimony was believed among you, on that day. To this end we also always pray for you, that our God may deem you worthy of his calling and fulfill every good intention and work of faith in power, so that the name of our Lord Jesus Christ may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ [1:3–12].

**384. 384.** [2 Thess 3]. *And in another place:* We command you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, to withdraw from every brother who walks disorderly and not according to the tradition they received from us. For you yourselves know how you ought to imitate us: we were not disorderly among you, nor did we eat anyone's bread for nothing. Instead, with labor and toil we worked night and day so as not to burden any of you. This was not because we lacked the right, but to give you ourselves as an example for imitating us. For even when we were with you, we gave you this command: if anyone is unwilling to work, neither should he eat. For we have heard that some among you walk disorderly, doing no work but meddling. Such people we command and entreat in the Lord Jesus Christ to work quietly and eat their own bread. But you, brothers, do not grow weary in doing good. If anyone does not obey our word through this letter, mark that person and do not associate with him, so that he may be ashamed. Yet do not regard him as an enemy, but correct him as a brother [3:6–15].
