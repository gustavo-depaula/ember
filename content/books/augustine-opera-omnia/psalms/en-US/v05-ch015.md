# Exposition of Psalm 109

**1.** As far as the Lord grants, who set us as ministers of his word and sacrament to serve you in the fatness of his mercy, we have taken up this psalm, which we sang just now, brief in the number of words, great in the weight of meanings, with him helping who made you attentive, that he may also make us fit, as we can, to consider and treat it. Let your soul live, and watch toward God. For God set a time for his promises, and a time for fulfilling what he promised. The time of the promises was in the time of the Prophets up to John the Baptist: but from him and thereafter to the end, it is the time of fulfilling what was promised. Faithful is God, who made himself our debtor; not by receiving anything from us, but by promising us so great things. The promise was too little; he willed himself to be bound even by writing, as it were making with us a bond of his promises; so that, when he began to pay what he promised, we might consider in the writing of the promises the order of the things to be paid. So the time of prophecy was, as we have often already said, the foretelling of the promises. He promised eternal salvation, and a blessed life with the angels without end, and an unfading inheritance, everlasting glory, the sweetness of his countenance, the house of his sanctification in the heavens, from the resurrection from the dead no fear thereafter of dying. This is his promise as it were final, to which all our intention runs, to which when we have come, let us seek nothing further, demand nothing further. But by what order one comes to that which will be at the end, neither did he keep silent of this by promising and foretelling. For he promised to men divinity, to mortals immortality, to sinners justification, to the abject glorification. Whatever he promised, he promised to the unworthy, that the reward might be promised not as it were to works, but that grace might be given freely from his name. Because even this very thing, that he lives justly, insofar as a man can live justly, is not of human merit, but of divine benefit. For no one lives justly, except being justified, that is, made just: but a man is made just by him who can never be unjust. For as a lamp is not kindled by itself, so neither does the human soul furnish light to itself; but it cries to God: *You will light my lamp, O Lord*[^1].

**2.** Since, then, the kingdom of heaven was promised to sinners, not those remaining in sin, but freed from sin, and serving righteousness; and that they may be able to do this very thing, they are helped by grace, as we said, and justified by him who is always just: it seemed incredible that God bears so great a care for men, and even today those who despair of divine grace, and are unwilling to convert themselves from their worst morals to God, that they may be justified by him, and, through his indulgence all their sins being blotted out, may begin to live justly in him who never lived unjustly, have this ruin of their thought against themselves, that they say God does not care for human affairs, nor from this can suppose that the maker and ruler of this world can think how each mortal lives on the earth. So a man supposes himself not to be reckoned by God, who was made by God. If we could address such a man: if he first admitted us to his ears, then to his heart; if he did not repel by resisting one who seeks him, and suffered himself, lost, to be found; we could say to him: O man, how will God not reckon you, made, who cared beforehand that you should be made? Why do you suppose you are not numbered in the order of created things? Do not believe the seducer: your hairs are numbered by your creator. This finally the Lord too says in the Gospel to the disciples, lest they fear death, lest they suppose that anything of theirs would perish in death. They in death feared for their soul, he gave them security even about their hairs. Does the soul, then, perish, whose hair does not perish? Yet, brothers, because it seemed incredible to men what God promised, that from this mortality, corruption, abjection, weakness, dust and ashes men would be equal to the angels of God; he not only made a writing with men, that they might believe; but even set a mediator of his faith, not any prince, or any angel or archangel, but his only Son: so that by what way he would lead us through to that end which he promised, through that same Son of his he might both show and provide. For it was too little for God if he made his Son the shower of the way; he made him himself the way, that you might go through him governing you, walking through himself.

**3.** He promised, then, that we would come to him, that is, to that ineffable immortality, and equality with his angels. How far away were we? how far above was he, how far below were we? how far in the highest was he, how far in the lowest did we lie in despair? We were sick, without expectation of health: a physician was sent, whom the sick one did not recognize. For if they had recognized him, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. But this too availed for the medicine of the sick one, that the sick one killed the physician: he came to visit, he was killed to heal. He insinuated himself to believers as God and man; God by whom we were made, man by whom we were re-created. One thing in him appeared; another lay hidden; and what lay hidden was much more excellent than that which appeared: but what was more excellent could not be seen. The sick one was cured by that which could be seen; that he might afterward become capable of the vision of that which by being hidden was deferred, not by being denied taken away. So the only Son of God, about to come to men, to take up man, and through that which he took to be man, to die, to rise, to ascend into heaven, to sit at the right hand of the Father, and to fulfill among the nations what he promised, and after the fulfillment of his promises among the nations to fulfill even this, that he come, and demand what he advanced, separate the vessels of wrath from the vessels of mercy, render to the impious what he threatened, to the just what he pledged: all this, then, had to be prophesied, had to be foretold, his coming had to be commended; that, coming suddenly, he might not be dreaded, but, believed, be awaited. From these promises is this psalm, prophesying certainly and openly our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ; so that we can in no way doubt that Christ is announced in this psalm: since indeed we are Christians, and already believe the Gospel. For when our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ himself asked the Jews whose son they said the Christ was, and they had answered, *David's;* he immediately rejoined to those answering, and said: *How then does David in the spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies under your feet? If, then,* he says, *in the spirit he calls him Lord, how is he his son?*[^2] From this verse this psalm begins.

**4.** [v. 1.] *The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet.* This question, then, set before the Jews by the Lord, we ought to treat at the very entrance of the Psalm. For if what the Jews answered is demanded of us, whether we too confess it, or deny it; far be it that we deny. If it is said to us, Is Christ the son of David, or not? if we say no, we contradict the Gospel; for, as Matthew writes, the Gospel begins thus: *The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David*[^3]. The Evangelist says that he writes the book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David. Rightly, then, the Jews, asked by Christ whose son they believed the Christ to be, answered: *David's.* The Gospel agrees with their answer. This not only the supposition of the Jews has, but also the faith of Christians. Still I see other supports. The Apostle says: *Who was made for him from the seed of David according to the flesh*[^4]: he says too to Timothy: *Remember that Christ Jesus rose from the dead from the seed of David, according to my gospel.* And of this gospel what does he say? *In which I labor even to chains, as a malefactor; but the word of God is not bound*[^5]. The Apostle labored, then, even to chains for his gospel, that is, for the evangelical dispensation, which he preached to the peoples, which he expended on the peoples: *he who in the morning had seized, in the evening divided the food*[^6]. He labored, then, even to chains for the gospel. What gospel? *That Christ Jesus rose from the dead, from the seed of David.* For this gospel the Apostle labored: and yet about this Christ asked; and to the Jews answering what the Apostle preached, he rejoined a voice as it were of contradiction, and said: *How then does David in the spirit call him Lord?* and he interposed the testimony from this psalm: *The Lord said to my Lord. If, then, in the spirit he calls him Lord, how is he his son?* The Jews under this question fell silent; what to answer thereafter they did not find: nor yet did they seek him as Lord, because they did not recognize him to be that son of David either. But we, brothers, let us believe, and say: for with the heart one believes unto righteousness; but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation: let us believe, I say, and say both son of David, and Lord of David. Let us not blush at the son of David, lest we find the Lord of David angry.

**5.** For by this name those blind men, most rightly calling him as he passed by, deserved to be enlightened. For Jesus was passing by, and they, having heard the sound of the passing crowd, recognizing already by the ear what they could not yet by the eyes, cried out with a great voice, and said: *Have mercy on us, son of David*[^7]. But the crowds rebuked them, that they should be silent: and they nonetheless, by desire of the light, conquering the contradiction of the crowds, persevered in crying out; they held him passing by; and from the one who touched them they deserved to be enlightened. For they said to him as he passed: *Have mercy on us, son of David.* He stood, and, the cry of those contradicting being conquered by them: *What,* he says, *do you wish that I do for you?* And they: *Lord, that we may see*[^8]. He touched and opened their eyes; they saw present the one they had perceived passing by. The Lord did, then, something transitory: but there is another thing that stands. One thing, I say, is the transitory of the Lord, another the stable of the Lord. The transitory of the Lord: the birth of the Virgin, the incarnation of the Word, the gradation of ages, the showing of miracles, the enduring of sufferings, death, resurrection, ascension into heaven: all this was transitory. For Christ is not still being born, or still dying, or still rising, or still ascending into heaven. Do you not see that these deeds ran through times, exhibited through times to the wayfarers something transitory, lest they remain on the way, but reach the homeland? Finally, those blind men too were sitting by the way, there they perceived him passing by, and by crying out held him. On the way, then, of this age the Lord worked this transitory thing of his, and this transitory thing pertains to the son of David. Therefore those said to the passing Lord: *Have mercy on us, son of David.* As if they said: We recognize the son of David in the one passing by, we learn the son of David made in passing. Let us too, then, recognize, and confess the son of David, that we may deserve to be enlightened. For we perceive the son of David passing by, and are enlightened by the Lord of David.

**6.** Behold, then, since our Master asked the Jews, and they for this reason did not answer, because they were unwilling to be disciples; behold, if he asked us, what would we answer? The Jews failed in this question, let the Christians make progress; let them not be disturbed, but instructed. For the Lord does not ask wishing to learn, but asks as it were a teacher. Let the wretched Jews say: You, tell us. They preferred to be burst by puffed-up taciturnity, than to be taught by humble confession. Let our Master, then, tell us, and let us see what we may answer the one asking: *What do you think of Christ? whose son is he?* Let us altogether answer what the Jews did, but not remain where the Jews did. Let us recall the Gospel which we believe: *The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David.* Let not the fact that we are asked cause us to forget that Christ is the son of David, which the Apostle commends to memory; Come, Christian, *remember that Christ Jesus rose from the dead, from the seed of David.* Therefore let us be asked and answer: *What do you think of Christ? whose son is he?* Let Christian mouths say in concord: *David's.* Let the Teacher too rejoin, and recall to us: *How then does David in the spirit call him Lord? The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies under your feet.* How would we say it, unless we learned it from you? Now, then, because we have learned it, we say: In the beginning you were the Word, and the Word was with God, and God was the Word; all things were made through you: behold the Lord of David. But because of our weakness, because, despairing flesh, we lay, the Word became flesh, that you might dwell among us: behold the son of David. Certainly, when you were in the form of God, you did not think it robbery to be equal to God; therefore the Lord of David: but you emptied yourself, taking the form of a servant; thence the son of David. Finally, even in your very question, saying, *How is he his son?* you did not deny him to be his son, but inquired the manner in which it might be. *He calls him,* you say, *David, Lord;* how is he his son? I do not deny, but say how. Those, then, from the Scriptures which they read, and did not understand, if in that question they recalled the manner, would they not answer: How do you ask? *Behold, a virgin will conceive in the womb, and bear a son, and they will call his name Emmanuel, which is interpreted, God with us*[^9]. A virgin will conceive in the womb, a virgin of the seed of David will bear a son, that he may be the son of David: for Joseph and Mary were of the house and lineage of David. So that virgin bore, that he might be the son of David. But the one she bore, *they will call his name Emmanuel, God with us*: behold, you have the Lord of David too.

**7.** Something perhaps about this matter, how Christ is both son of David, and Lord of David, this psalm too will intimate to us. Let us hear, then, and treat it; let us knock with piety, let us wring it out with love. David himself, then, says it: for it is not lawful to contradict the Lord: *David,* he says, *in the spirit calls him Lord.* David himself, then, what does he say of Christ? For: *A Psalm for David himself*: and this is the whole title, simple, without the figure of a question, without any knot of difficulty. What, then, does David say? *The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet.* What is, *a footstool for your feet;* this is, *under your feet*: for the footstool of the feet is under the feet. *He said,* he says, *the Lord, to my Lord.* David heard this, heard it in the spirit: where we, when he heard, did not hear; but we believed him who heard, speaking and writing what he heard. He heard, then, surely, he heard in a certain secret chamber of truth, in a certain sanctuary of mysteries: where the Prophets heard in secret what they preached openly; there David heard, who with great confidence says: *The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet.* We know that Christ sits at the right hand of the Father after the resurrection from the dead, and the ascension into the heavens. It is now done; we did not see it, but believed: in the Books we read, preached we heard, by faith we hold. Whence even by the very fact that Christ was the son of David, he was made the Lord of David. For that which was born of the seed of David was so honored that it was even the Lord of David. So you wonder at this, as if such things did not happen even in human affairs. For if it should happen that someone's son becomes king, his father being a private man, will he not be lord of his father? More wonderful is what can happen, that not only a private man's son, made king, is lord of his father; but a layman's son, made bishop, is father of his father. Therefore even by the very fact that Christ took flesh, that he died in the flesh, that he rose again in the same flesh, that he ascended into heaven in the same and sits at the right hand of the Father, and in that same flesh so honored, so glorified, so changed into a heavenly habit, he is both son of David, and Lord of David. For according to this dispensation of the passing of Christ, that too is said by the Apostle: *Wherefore God exalted him from the dead, and gave him the name which is above every name; that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth*[^10]. *He gave him,* he says, *the name which is above every name*: to Christ according to man, to Christ according to the flesh dead, rising, ascending, *he gave the name which is above every name; that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth.* Where will David be, that he is not his Lord? Be he in heaven, be he on earth, be he under the earth; his Lord will be the one who is Lord of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth. Let David too, then, rejoice with us, honored by the nativity of his son, freed from his dominion; and rejoicing let him say, let him be heard by those rejoicing: *The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet.*

**8.** *Sit,* not only on high, but also in secret: excelling that you may have dominion, hidden that you may be believed. For what would be the reward of faith, unless that which we believe were hidden? But the reward of faith is to see what we believed before we saw. But as Scripture sounds: *The just lives by faith*[^11]. There would not, then, be the righteousness of faith, unless that which, preached, we should believe, were hidden, and by believing we should arrive at seeing. For *how great is the multitude of your sweetness, O Lord, which you have hidden for those who fear you!* Have you hidden it, then, and have they remained? Far from it: *But you have perfected it for those who hope in you*[^12]. A wonderful mystery, then, of Christ sitting at the right hand of God: it is hidden that it may be believed, withdrawn that it may be hoped for. *For by hope we were made safe. But hope that is seen is not hope: for what one sees, why does he hope for it?* These are the words of the Apostle. You recognize them indeed, but I commend them for the sake of the untaught. What, then, does the Apostle say? *By hope,* he says, *we were made safe. But hope that is seen is not hope: for what one sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, with patience we wait for it*[^13]. Because, then, *that which is seen is not hope; You hid* the sweetness *for those who fear you.* Because *that which we do not see, we hope for,* and *through patience wait for; You perfected it for those who hope in you.* Finally, dearest ones, attentively receive what I am about to say. Since our righteousness is from faith, and by faith our hearts are cleansed, that we may be able to see what we have believed: for both are set down; both: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^14]; and: *By faith cleansing their hearts*[^15]. Because, then, this is the righteousness of faith, to believe what you do not see, and by the very merit of faith to arrive at the vision in its own time; the Lord in the Gospel, when he promised the Holy Spirit, said this: *He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment*[^16]. Of what sin? of what righteousness? of what judgment? He himself goes on and expounds; he does not admit human conjectures: *Of sin indeed,* he says, *because they did not believe in me.* How many other sins of the Jews! And yet as if this were the only one, he so said: *Of sin indeed, because they did not believe in me.* This is that sin, of which elsewhere he says: *If I had not come, they would not have sin*[^17]. What is this: *If I had not come, they would not have sin?* Did you, then, come to the just, and make them sinners? But, other sins excepted, which could be remitted through faith, he named this one sin, which, if it were not admitted, all would be relaxed. *Of sin indeed,* he says, *because they did not believe in me*: and elsewhere: *If I had not come, they would not have sin.* For by the very fact that he came and they did not believe in him, they fell into sin: which if they did not fall into, all the other sins could be forgiven through the indulgence of grace obtained through faith. Therefore *of sin, because they did not believe in me: of righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me*[^18]. This is righteousness, because you go to the Father, and they will no longer see you: for this righteousness is from faith. *For the just lives by faith*; and then he lives by faith, if he does not see what he believes. Because, then, it pertains to righteousness to live by faith, and no one lives by faith except by not seeing what he believes; that he might make righteousness itself in men, that is, that they might believe what they do not see: *Of righteousness,* he says, *because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me.* This, he says, will be your righteousness, that you believe in him whom you do not see, and, cleansed by faith, may afterward, on the day of resurrection, see him in whom you believed.

**9.** Christ sits, then, at the right hand of God, the Son is at the right hand of the Father in secret: let us believe. For he says two things here, because both God said: *Sit at my right hand;* and he added, *until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet,* that is, under your feet. You do not see Christ sitting at the right hand of the Father: at least you can see how his enemies are set as a footstool for his feet. When this is fulfilled openly, believe that the other is in secret. What enemies are set as a footstool for his feet? Those to whom, meditating empty things, it is said: *Why did the nations rage, and the peoples meditate empty things? The kings of the earth stood up, and the princes gathered together into one, against the Lord and against his Christ;* and they said: *Let us break their bonds, and cast off from us their yoke*: let them not have dominion over us, let them not subject us to themselves. *He who dwells in the heavens will mock them*[^19]: you were an enemy; you will be under his feet, either adopted, or conquered. Seek, then, what place you have under the feet of the Lord your God; for you must have one, either of grace, or of punishment. He sits, then, at the right hand of God, until his enemies are set under his feet. This is done, this is being carried out: even if it is accomplished little by little, it is accomplished unceasingly. For though the nations rage, and the peoples meditate empty things; though the kings of the earth stand up, and the princes gather together into one, against the Lord and against his Christ: will they, by raging, by meditating empty things, by gathering against Christ into one, bring it about that this be not fulfilled: *I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth?*[^20] It will be wholly fulfilled, both with them raging, and with them meditating empty things: *I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth.* For they meditate empty things: but that there be fulfilled, *I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth;* not some empty-speaker, but *the Lord said to me*[^21]. So in this psalm too we can say: *He said,* not anyone whatever, not those who rage and meditate empty things; but *The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet.* Let them rage, let them meditate empty things, let them make a din; will it not be fulfilled? *Their memory perished with a din.* Certainly another psalm too speaks, but not another spirit: *Their memory perished with a din, and the Lord abides forever*[^22]. He, then, who, their memory perishing with a din, abides forever, he himself *said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand.* For he sits at the right hand of the Father, until he set all his enemies as a footstool for his feet.

**10.** [v. 2.] And what follows? *The Lord will send forth the rod of your power out of Zion.* It appears, brothers, most evidently it appears that the Prophet does not speak of that kingdom of Christ by which he always reigns with the Father, Lord of the things which were created through him: for when does the Word, God with God, not reign in the beginning? For it is said: *But to the King of the ages, invisible, incorruptible, the only God, honor and glory, for ages of ages*[^23]. *To the King of the ages honor and glory, for ages of ages*: to what *King of the ages?* *Invisible, incorruptible.* For in that Christ too is with the Father invisible and incorruptible, because he is his Word, and his Power, and his Wisdom, and God with God, through whom all things were made; he is the King of the ages: but yet that dispensational and transitory thing, by which he called us through the medium of his flesh into eternity, his kingdom begins from the Christians; but of his kingdom there will be no end. His enemies, then, are set as a footstool for his feet, as he sits at the right hand of the Father; they are set, as was said; this is done, this is being carried out wholly to the end. Let no one say that what has begun cannot be fulfilled. Why do you despair of the end of what has begun? The Almighty began, the Almighty promised that he would fulfill what he began. But whence did he begin? *The Lord will send forth the rod of your power out of Zion.* Which Zion is itself Jerusalem. Hear the Lord himself: *Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead on the third day*[^24]. Hence he sits at the right hand of God the Father, by rising again where he was. Then, with him sitting at the right hand of the Father, what is done? that his enemies be set as a footstool for his feet, what is done? Hear him teaching and expounding: *And there will be preached in his name repentance and remission of sins through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^25]; because: *The Lord will send forth the rod of your power out of Zion. The rod of your power,* that is, the kingdom of your might; because: *You will rule them with a rod of iron*[^26]: *the Lord will send forth out of Zion*; because: *Beginning from Jerusalem.*

**11.** When, then, he has sent forth out of Zion the rod of his power, what will happen? *And have dominion in the midst of your enemies.* First, *have dominion in the midst of your enemies*: among the raging nations. For will he, after the saints have received their honor, and the impious their damnation, have dominion in the midst of his enemies? And what wonder if then he will have dominion, the just reigning with him forever, and the impious burning in eternal punishments? what wonder if then? Now in the midst of your enemies, now in this passing of the ages, in this propagation and succession of human mortality, now while the torrent of times glides by, for this the rod of your power is directed out of Zion, that you may have dominion in the midst of your enemies. Have dominion, have dominion in the midst of the pagans, the Jews, the heretics, the false brothers. Have dominion, have dominion, son of David, Lord of David, have dominion in the midst of the pagans, the Jews, the heretics, the false brothers. *Have dominion in the midst of your enemies.* We do not rightly understand this verse, if we do not now see it being done. Sit, then, at the right hand of God, hide yourself that you may be believed, until the times of the nations are fulfilled. For so it is written: *Whom heaven had to receive, until the times of the nations are fulfilled*[^27]. For that you might rise, you died; that you might ascend, you rose; that you might sit at the right hand of the Father, you ascended: therefore that you might sit at the right hand of the Father, you died. For from death is resurrection, from resurrection ascension, from ascension the sitting at the right hand: all this, then, began from death. The excellence of this brightness has the beginning of humility. With you, therefore, sitting at the right hand of the Father, the times of the nations are fulfilled, all the enemies are set as a footstool for your feet, and that this may be reached, you will first have dominion in the midst of your enemies: for to this end *the Lord will send forth the rod of your power out of Zion.* But that you might die, and through your death the bond of sins be blotted out, and repentance and remission of offenses be preached through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem, the blindness of the Jews brought about. For the enlightening of some, the blindness of others served: *For blindness in part has come upon Israel, that the fullness of the nations might enter, and so all Israel be saved*[^28]. *Blindness in part has come upon Israel* killed you; killed, you rose again, by your blood you washed away the sins of the nations, sitting at the right hand of the Father you gathered from every quarter those suffering and fleeing to you. *Blindness has come upon Israel in part,* it has come *that the fullness of the nations might enter, and so all Israel be saved,* and all your enemies be a footstool for your feet. But this now; afterward, what?

**12.** [v. 3.] *With you is the beginning in the day of your power.* What is this day of his power? When the beginning is with him, or what beginning, or how the beginning is with him; since he himself too is the beginning? May the Lord help, that it be disturbed neither for me to say, nor for you to hear. For I see what has already been done, and with you I see it with the eyes of faith: with the eyes of the flesh too I see what is now being done, and again with the eyes of faith with you I hope for what is to be. What, then, has been done? what is being done? what is to be? Christ suffered, died, rose on the third day, ascended into heaven, as we know, on the fortieth day, sits at the right hand of the Father: this has now been done, this we did not see, but believe. What is now being done? He has dominion in the midst of his enemies, the rod of his power being sent forth out of Zion; this is being done, this is being carried out. The servants then saw the form of a servant present; and the servants now believe it absent. This we believe of the form of a servant, which we can grasp, while we are still servants. For this is that milk of the little ones, which he tempered, passing the bread through the flesh. For that bread of the angels, in the beginning was the Word: yet that man might eat the bread of the angels, the Creator of the angels became man. So the incarnate Word became receivable for us: which we would not be able to receive, if the Son, equal to God, did not empty himself, *taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man*[^29]. So that we might in some way grasp him who could not be grasped by mortals, the immortal became mortal; that, his death accomplished, he might make us immortal, and give something to be gazed upon, something to be believed, something afterward to be seen. He gave the form of a servant to be gazed upon by those present, not only to be seen by the eyes, but even to be handled by the hands. With the same form he ascended into heaven; he bade us believe what he granted them to see. But we too have what we may see: for they saw the rod sent forth out of Zion, we see him have dominion in the midst of his enemies. All this, brothers, pertains to the dispensation of the servile form, which is tolerably grasped by servants, and loved by those who will be free. For the unchangeable Truth which is the Word of God, God with God, through whom all things were made, *abiding in himself makes all things new*[^30]. That we may see this, great and perfect cleanness of heart is necessary, which is made through faith. For the form of a servant being shown, it was deferred to the showing of the form of God. For he himself, speaking in the form of a servant to servants, says: *He who loves me, keeps my commandments: and he who loves me will be loved by my Father; and I will love him, and show myself to him*[^31]. To those seeing him he promised that he would show himself. What did they see? what did he promise? They saw the form of a servant, he promised the form of God. *I will show,* he says, *myself to him.* This is the brightness to which the kingdom is led which is now gathered in the passing of this age: for it is led to a certain ineffable vision, which the impious will not deserve. But the form of a servant, when it was here, was seen by the impious: those who believed saw it, those who killed him saw it too. Lest you suppose it something great to see that form, friends saw it, enemies saw it; and some seeing killed him, some not seeing believed. This form of a servant, then, which here in lowliness both the pious and the impious saw, both the pious and the impious will see in the judgment too. For when the Lord was borne into heaven before the eyes of his disciples, an angelic voice sounded to those gazing on him: *Men of Galilee, why do you stand gazing into heaven? This Jesus will so come, as you saw him going into heaven*[^32]. *So,* then, *so,* in the same form: because of the impious it was said: *They will see him whom they pierced*[^33]. They will see going to judge the one they mocked being judged. This form of a servant, then, will be conspicuous in the judgment both to the just and the unjust, both to the pious and the impious, both to the faithful and the unbelieving. What, then, will the impious not see? For those of whom it was said, *They will see him whom they pierced;* of them again it was said: *Let the impious be taken away, that he not see the brightness of the Lord*[^34]. What is this, brothers? Let us distinguish, let us examine. The impious is roused, to see something; the impious is taken away, that he not see something. What he is going to see, we have already shown; that form of which it was said: *So he will come.* What, then, will he not see? *And I will show myself to him.* What is it, *myself?* Not the form of a servant. What is it, *myself?* The form of God, in which I did not think it robbery to be equal to God. What is it, *myself? Most beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we will be: we know that when it appears, we will be like him; for we will see him as he is*[^35]. This brightness of God is the ineffable light, the fountain of light without changeableness, truth without failing, wisdom abiding in itself, making all things new: this is the substance of God. And so the impious will be taken away, that he not see this honor of the Lord. *For blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^36].

**13.** It seems to me, then, brothers, as far as the Lord deigns to impart to our capacity, that it is of that very time: if indeed it should be called time; for at a certain time we are to come to the no-time: thence it seems to me said (which I say without prejudice, if anyone can understand something better, more readily, more probably): thence it seems to me said: *With you is the beginning in the day of your power.* Finally this, as I think, the following verse explains sufficiently. For because here too his power was named, by which he subjected the nations to his yoke, by which he laid the peoples low, not by iron, but by wood, even though in the flesh, even though in lowliness, even though as yet as far as the servile form allows, yet his great power is grasped; because what is weak of God is stronger than men: because, then, his power was named here too, which he commended by saying: *The Lord will send forth the rod of your power out of Zion, and have dominion in the midst of your enemies*: for how great is his power, having dominion in the midst of his enemies making a din, availing nothing against him, daily saying to themselves: *When will he die, and his name perish?*[^37] while his glory grows among the peoples, while the nations are subjected to his name, while *the sinner sees and is angry, gnashes his teeth and wastes away*[^38]: because, then, this too is his power, the Prophet, wishing to commend in another way his power, as Christ is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God in the perpetual light of unchangeable truth; for which vision we are kept, for which vision we are deferred, for which vision we are cleansed by faith, from which vision the impious is taken away, lest he see the honor of the Lord; wishing, then, to show this: *With you,* he says, *is the beginning in the day of your power.* What is it, *With you is the beginning?* Suppose any beginning. If Christ himself; it would rather be said: You are the beginning, than *With you is the beginning.* For he answered those asking, *Who are you,* and said: *The Beginning, who also speak to you*[^39]: since the Father too is a beginning, of whom is the only-begotten Son, in which beginning was the Word, because the Word was with God. What, then, if both the Father is a beginning, and the Son a beginning, are there two beginnings? Far from it. For just as the Father is God and the Son is God, but the Father and the Son are not two gods, but one God: so the Father is a beginning and the Son a beginning, but the Father and the Son are not two, but one beginning. *With you is the beginning.* Then you will be seen how the beginning is with you. For neither here is the beginning not with you. For did you not say: *Behold, you will each go to your own, and leave me alone; but I am not alone, because the Father is with me?*[^40] So here too *with you is the beginning.* For you said elsewhere too: *But the Father, abiding in me, does his works*[^41]. *With you is the beginning;* nor was the Father ever separated from you. But when it will be seen that the beginning is with you, then it will be manifest to all now made like you; for they will see you as you are: for here Philip saw you, and sought the Father. Then, then, will be seen what is now believed: then *with you is the beginning,* the saints seeing, the just seeing; the impious being taken away from the midst, that they not see the honor of the Lord.

**14.** Now, then, let us believe, brothers, what we shall then see. For Philip too was rebuked because he sought to see the Father, when in the Son himself he did not recognize the Father: *So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me? Philip, he who has seen me, has seen the Father too*[^42]. But *he who has seen me,* not he who has seen in me the form of a servant. *He*, then, *who has seen me,* such as I hid myself from those who fear me, such as I perfect myself to be seen by those who hope in me, *has seen the Father too.* But because this vision will be afterward, now in its place what shall we have? Let us see what he says to Philip, to whom he had said: *He who has seen me, has seen the Father.* As if Philip silently answered him: And how shall I see you, if you are seen otherwise than in the form of a servant? or how shall I see the Father, a weak mortal man, dust and ashes? turning to him, deferring the vision, commanding faith, he who had said, *He who has seen me, has seen the Father;* because it was too much for Philip, and far from him to see: *Do you not believe,* he says, *that I am in the Father, and the Father in me?*[^43] What you cannot yet see, believe, that you may deserve to see. When, then, it has come about that we see, then it will appear: *With you is the beginning in the day of your power. Of your power*: not of the power of your weakness, because there is the power. *Of your power*: men now have in faith, in hope, in love, in good works their own powers; but *they will go from powers unto power*[^44]. *With you*, then, *is the beginning*: you will be seen with the Father, in the Father, so that the Father is *with you the beginning in the day of your power,* that power of yours which the impious will not see. For this weakness of yours too is stronger than men: for indeed *in the day of your power is the beginning with you.*

**15.** Of what power do you speak, expound. Because here too, as was said, his power was named, when the rod of his power is sent forth out of Zion, that he may have dominion in the midst of his enemies. Of what power do you speak? *In the splendor of the saints. In the splendor,* he says, *of the saints.* He speaks of that power, when the saints will be in splendor: not when, still bearing earthly flesh and groaning in a mortal and corruptible body, which weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind thinking of many things; when the very thoughts of one another are not seen, this is not it: *In the splendor of the saints.* But what is it, *In the splendor of the saints? Until the Lord comes, and lights up the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart; and then there will be praise for each one from God*[^45]. This will be: *In the splendor of the saints;* because *then the just will shine in the kingdom of their Father, like the sun.* For hear what this is: *In the splendor of the saints. There will come,* he says, *the harvest, there will come the end of the age: the householder will send his angels, and they will gather out of his kingdom all scandals, and cast them into the furnace of burning fire. Then the just will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^46]. In what kingdom? See whether a certain vision is reserved, of which it was said to us: *With you is the beginning.* In what kingdom? Certainly in eternal life. For to those set on the right he is going to say this: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^47]. Then, the impious being damned, the just separated and praised, what follows what he had said, *Receive the kingdom? Then the impious will go into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^48]. What he had said *kingdom,* this he said *eternal life,* into which the impious will not go. See whether eternal life is a certain vision: *But this is eternal life, that they know you the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^49]; because *with you is the beginning in the day of your power.* Therefore: *With you is the beginning in the day of your power, in the splendor of the saints.*

**16.** But this is deferred, this will be given afterward: what now? *From the womb before the daystar I begot you.* What here: If God has a Son, does he have a womb too? Like the bodies of flesh, he does not have one: because neither a bosom; yet it was said: *He who is in the bosom of the Father, he has declared him*[^50]. But what is the bosom, that is the womb; and both bosom and womb are set for what is secret. What is it, *From the womb?* From the secret, from the hidden; from myself, from my substance; this is, *From the womb:* because: *His generation who will declare?*[^51] Let us take, then, the Father saying to the Son: *From the womb before the daystar I begot you.* What is it, then, *before the daystar?* The daystar is set for the stars, as if Scripture from a part signifying the whole, and by the eminent star all the stars. But how were those stars made? *That they may be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and for years*[^52]. If, then, the stars were set both for signs and for seasons, and the daystar was named for the stars; what is before the daystar, this is before the stars; and what is before the stars, this is before the seasons; if, then, before the seasons, from eternity: do not ask when; eternity has no when. "When" and "at some time" are words of times. From the Father he was not born in time, through whom the times were made. It was said, then, as it had to be said, figuratively, prophetically, so that both the womb is set for the secret substance, and the daystar for the times. Or do you wish that we look at David himself, who called his Lord his son? For to say this, he heard it from his Lord; from him he heard it by whom he could not be deceived: and he already called him his Lord, because: *The Lord said,* he says, *to my Lord: Sit at my right hand.* And he himself speaks, his as it were discourse is woven. If, then, he himself speaks, perhaps he himself could say: *From the womb before the daystar I begot you*: from the virginal womb, *from the womb before the daystar I begot you.* For if that virgin, drawing her line from the flesh of David, Christ being born of that womb, as it were begotten from the womb by David. *From the womb,* which no male approached: *from the womb* indeed, properly *from the womb,* because alone from a sole womb. Therefore: *From the womb,* says that one, who had called him his Lord: *From the womb before the daystar I begot you.* And this very *before the daystar* was signally said, and properly said, and so fulfilled. For the Lord was born by night from the womb of the virgin Mary: the testimonies of the shepherds indicate it, who were keeping watch over their flock. *From the womb before the daystar I begot you.* O you, my Lord, sitting at the right hand of my Lord, whence my son, except because *From the womb before the daystar I begot you?*

**17.** [v. 4.] And for what were you born? *The Lord swore, and it will not repent him: You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.* For to this end were you born from the womb before the daystar, that you might be a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. If we understand born from the womb, of the virgin; before the daystar, by night, as the Gospels attest; without doubt thence from the womb before the daystar, that he might be a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. For according to that by which he was born of the Father, God with God, coeternal with the begetter, he is not a priest; but a priest because of the flesh assumed, because of the victim which he would offer for us, received from us. *The Lord swore,* then. What is it, *The Lord swore?* Does the Lord, then, swear, who forbids man to swear? Or does he perhaps for this reason rather forbid man to swear, lest he slip into perjury, and therefore God rather swears, because he cannot be perjured? For man, who by the habit of swearing can slip in tongue into perjury, is well forbidden to swear: for he will be the further from perjury, the further he is from swearing. For man, who swears, can swear false and true: but he who does not swear. Why, then, should the Lord not swear, when the oath of the Lord is the confirmation of the promise? Let him by all means swear. What, then, do you do, when you swear? You call God to witness: this is to swear, to call God to witness; and therefore it is troublesome, lest you bring God as witness to some falsehood. If, then, you by swearing call God to witness, why should not God too by swearing call himself to witness? *As I live, says the Lord,* is an oath of God. So he swore concerning the seed of Abraham: *As I live, says the Lord; because you have heard my voice, and have not spared your only son for my sake, blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply your seed like the stars of heaven, and like the sand which is on the lip of the sea, and in your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^53]. And the seed of Abraham which is Christ, that seed of Abraham, taking flesh from the seed of Abraham, will be a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. Concerning the priesthood, then, according to the order of Melchizedek, *the Lord swore, and it will not repent him.* What of the priesthood according to the order of Aaron? Does it repent God, as a man, or does he, unwilling, fall into something so as to do it, or imprudent slip, so that afterward it repents him of his slip? He knows what he does, he knows how far anything proceeds; whence it may be changed into something else, is in the power of the ruler. But by repentance a change of things is signified. For just as you, when something repents you, grieve over a thing you have done: so when God changes anything into something else, beyond the hope of men, that is, beyond what men hope, he is said to repent; even so far that it even repents him of our punishment, if we have repented of our bad life. *The Lord swore,* then; *he swore,* he confirmed: *it will not repent him,* he will not change it. What? *You are a priest forever.* Therefore *forever,* because *it will not repent him.* But a priest, according to what? Will there be those sacrifices, the victims offered by the Patriarchs, the altars of blood and the tabernacle, and those sacraments of the first Old Testament? Far from it. Now those are taken away, the temple now overthrown, that priesthood removed, their victim and sacrifice perishing: these the Jews do not even have. They see that the priesthood according to the order of Aaron has now perished, and they do not recognize the priesthood according to the order of Melchizedek. *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.* I speak to the faithful. If the catechumens do not understand something, let them put away laziness, let them hasten to knowledge. There is no need, then, to bring forth the mysteries; let the Scriptures intimate to you what is the priesthood according to the order of Melchizedek.

**18.** [v. 5.] *The Lord at your right hand.* The Lord had said: *Sit at my right hand;* now the Lord is at his right hand, as if they had changed seats. Or is it perhaps rather thus: *The Lord swore, and it will not repent him: You are a priest forever,* said to Christ? *You are a priest forever, the Lord swore.* What Lord? He who *said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand,* he himself *swore. You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek;* and to the very Lord who swore, the discourse is directed: *The Lord at your right hand.* O Lord, who swore and said: *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek;* that very priest forever, the Lord is at your right hand; that very one, I say, priest forever, of whom you swore: *the Lord* is *at your right hand*: because to that same my Lord you said: *Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet.* That Lord, then, who is at your right hand, of whom you swore, and to whom you swore, saying: *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. He shattered kings in the day of his wrath.* He certainly, Christ, the *Lord at your right hand,* to whom you swore, and it will not repent you: what does the priest forever do? what does he do, who is at the right hand of God, and intercedes for us, as a priest entering into the inner parts or into the holy of holies, into the secrets of the heavens, he alone having no sin, and therefore easily cleansing from sins? He, then, *at your right hand shattered kings in the day of his wrath.* What kings, do you ask? Has it slipped your mind: *The kings of the earth stood up, and the princes gathered together into one, against the Lord and against his Christ?*[^54] These kings his glory shattered, and by the weight of his name made the kings weak, that they could not accomplish what they wished. For they tried much to blot the Christian name out of the earth, and could not; because *he who strikes against that stone will be shattered*[^55]. They struck, then, against the stone of stumbling, and therefore the kings were shattered, when they say: Who is Christ? Some Jew, some Galilean, so killed, so dead. The stone is before your feet, lying as it were cheaply and humbly: therefore by scorning you strike against it, by striking you fall, by falling you are shattered. If, then, so great is the wrath of the hidden one, what will be the judgment of the manifest one? You have heard of the wrath of the hidden one, of which the psalm is inscribed: *For the hidden things of the son*: the ninth psalm, if I remember well, is inscribed: *For the hidden things of the son;* and there is shown the hidden judgment of the hidden wrath. With God angry they live who strike against that stone; they are shattered. And what avails it that they are shattered? Hear also concerning the future judgment: *Because he who strikes against that stone,* he says, *will be shattered; but on whomever that stone falls, it will grind him to powder*[^56]. When, then, he is struck against, he lies as it were humble; then he shatters: but when he will grind to powder, he will come from above. See how by these two words, "will shatter" and "will grind to powder," one strikes against him and the other he comes upon, two times are distributed, of the humility and the brightness of Christ, of the hidden punishment and the future judgment. He will not grind to powder, coming, the one he does not shatter, lying. Lying I say, as appearing contemptible. For he is at the right hand of God, and mightily from on high cried out: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^57] But yet, although from heaven, he would not say, *Why do you persecute me?* whom no one was touching, unless he so sat in heaven at the right hand of the Father, that he also as it were lay on earth in us. *The Lord at your right hand, shattered kings in the day of his wrath.*

**19.** [v. 6.] *He will judge among the nations.* But now: *For the hidden things;* for there will be a manifest judgment too: *He will judge among the nations.* For now it happens: *Their memory perished with a din.* In that very psalm it is, *For the hidden things: Their memory perished with a din, and the Lord abides forever; he has prepared his seat in judgment, and he himself will judge the world in equity.* There it was said: *You rebuked the nations, and the impious perished; you blotted out their name forever*[^58]: this is done in secret. *In the day*, then, *of his wrath he shattered kings. He will judge among the nations.* How? Hear what follows: *He will fill up the ruins.* Now he so judges among the nations that he fills up the ruins: for when at the end he will have judged, he will damn the ruins. *He will fill up the ruins*: what ruins? Whoever has feared from his name, will fall: when he has fallen, what was will be overthrown, that what was not may be built. *He will judge among the nations, he will fill up the ruins.* Whoever you are who are obstinate against Christ, you have erected on high a tower that will fall. It is good that you cast yourself down, become humble, fall prostrate at the feet of the one sitting at the right hand of the Father, that there be made in you a ruin to be built up. For if you remain in the bad height, then you will be cast down, when you will not be built up. For of such Scripture says in another place: *Destroy them, and you will not build them up*[^59]. Without doubt he would not say of some: *Destroy them, and you will not build them up;* unless he destroyed some so as to build them up. Which is done at this time, when Christ so judges among the nations that he fills up the ruins. *He will shatter many heads upon the earth.* Here, *upon the earth,* in this life, *he will shatter many heads.* Of the proud he makes the humble: and I dare to say, my brothers, it is useful, with head shattered, to walk humbly here, than with head erect to fall into the judgment of eternal death. He will shatter many heads by making ruins, but by filling up he will build.

**20.** [v. 7.] *From the torrent in the way he will drink; therefore he will lift up his head.* Let us see him too drinking from the torrent in the way. First, what is the torrent? The flowing-by of human mortality. For as a torrent is gathered from the rain-waters, overflows, makes a din, runs, and by running runs down, that is, finishes its course; so is all this course of mortality. Men are born; they live, they die; and, others dying, others are born, and again, those dying, others arise: they succeed, they come, they depart, nor will they remain. What is held here? what does not run? what does not, as if gathered from rain, go into the deep? For as a river, suddenly gathered from rain, from the drops of showers, goes into the sea, and does not appear; nor did it appear, before it was gathered from rain: so this human race is gathered from hidden things, and flows forth; by death it again goes into the hidden; in the middle it makes this din, and passes. From this torrent he drank; he did not disdain to drink from this torrent. For to drink from this torrent was for him to be born and to die. This the torrent has, nativity and death: this Christ took up; he was born, he died: so *from the torrent in the way* he drank. For *he exulted as a giant to run the way*[^60]. *From the torrent*, then, *in the way* he drank; because *he did not stand in the way of sinners*[^61]. Because, then, *from the torrent in the way* he drank; *therefore* he lifted up his *head*: that is, because he was humbled, "and was made subject unto death, even the death of the cross; therefore God exalted him from the dead, and gave him the name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth, and every tongue confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father[^62].
