# Exposition of Psalm 38

**1.** [v. 1] The title of this psalm, which we just now sang and have undertaken to treat, is: *To the end, for Jeduthun, a Canticle of David himself*[^1]. The voices, then, of a certain man, who is called Jeduthun, are to be awaited and heard: and if each one of us can be Jeduthun, in what he sings he finds himself, and hears himself. For you may see who was called Jeduthun according to the ancient birth of men: but let us hear what this name is interpreted, and in the very interpretation of the name let us seek the understanding of truth. As, then, inquiring, we have been able to find in those names which have been interpreted for us from the Hebrew tongue into Latin by students of the divine letters, Jeduthun is interpreted, "Leaping over them." Who, then, is this one leaping over? or whom did he leap over? because "Leaping over" is not set down bare; but, "Leaping over them." For does he sing by leaping over, or leap over by singing? Whether he sings by leaping over, or leaps over by singing, yet a Canticle of one leaping over we sang a little before: whether we too are leaping over, let God see, to whom we sang. But if anyone sang leaping over, let him rejoice that he is what he sang: but if anyone sang still cleaving to the earth, let him desire to be what he sang. For certain ones cleaving to the ground, bent toward the earth, thinking of the things that are lowest, placing their hope in passing things, this one leaped over who is called "Leaping over them." For whom did he leap over, but those remaining?

**2.** You know that certain psalms are entitled "A Canticle of degrees": and there indeed, in the Greek tongue, it is evident enough what *anabathmoi* means. For anabathmoi are degrees, but of those ascending, not descending. The Latin, because it could not say it properly, said it generally: and since it called them degrees, it left it ambiguous whether they were of those ascending or descending. But because there are no speeches nor discourses whose voices are not heard, the preceding tongue expounds the following; and what was ambiguous in one is made certain in another. As, then, there one ascending sings, so here one leaping over. But this ascent and this leaping over is not with feet, not with ladders, not with wings; and yet, if you attend to the inner man, both with feet, and ladders, and wings. For if not with feet, how does the inner man say, *Let not the foot of pride come to me*[^2]? If not with ladders, what are those which Jacob saw, where there were angels ascending and descending[^3]? If not with wings, who is it that says, *Who will give me wings like a dove, and I will fly, and rest?*[^4] But in bodily things feet are one thing, ladders another, wings another. But within, both feet, and ladders, and wings are the affections of a good will. With these let us walk, with these let us ascend, with these let us fly. When, then, anyone hears this man leaping over, and chooses to imitate him, let him not seek by lightness of body to leap over ditches, or to fly over by leaping some rather lofty things: but, as far as it pertains to bodies, I say: for he leaps over ditches too. *Things burned with fire and dug out, which shall perish at the rebuke of your countenance*[^5]. For what are the things *burned with fire and dug out, which shall perish at the rebuke* of the Lord, but sins? Burned with fire are those which evil-burning cupidity makes: and dug out are those which evil-lying timidity makes. For hence are all sins, either by coveting, or by fearing. Let this man, then, leap over all things by which he could be held on the earth: let him raise his ladders, let him put forth his wings, let him see whether anyone here recognizes himself: rather indeed, in the grace of the Lord many recognize themselves, who perhaps, now holding the world cheap, and all the things that delight in the world, choose to live rightly, while here they live in certain spiritual joys. And whence will these be to those still walking upon the earth, except from the divine words, from the word of God, from some parable of the Scriptures searched out and investigated, from the sweetness of the finding, which the labor of the inquiry preceded? There are certain holy and good delights in books. For they are not in gold and silver, in feasts and luxury, in hunting and fishing, in play and jest, in theatrical trifles, in the affecting and grasping of ruinous honors; for there are no true joys in all these, and in these books there are none: rather indeed let that soul leaping over those lowest things, and delighted in these, say—because it says the truth and says it secure—*The unjust have told me of their delights, but not as your law, O Lord*[^6]. Let this Jeduthun still come, let him leap over those who delight in lowest things; and let him be delighted in these, and rejoice in the word of the Lord, in the delight of the law of the Most High. But what do we say? And from here too must one leap over to something else? Or does he have thus far where to leap over who desires to leap over? Let us rather hear his voices. For now this one leaping over seems to me to have dwelt in the words of God, and there to have learned these things, which we are about to hear.

**3.** [v. 2] *I said: I will keep my ways, that I may not transgress in my tongue*[^7]. You may believe that, by reading, discoursing, preaching, admonishing, rebuking, exhorting, when he was engaged in work, when he was exercised in certain human difficulties, a man acting among men, although now leaping over those who do not delight in these things (because it is difficult that anyone not slip and sin with the tongue, and *he who has not sinned with the tongue,* as it is written, *he is a perfect man*[^8]) had perhaps said something to be repented of, and there had slipped from his mouth things which he would wish to recall, and could not. For not in vain is the tongue in a wet place, except because it slips easily. Seeing, then, how difficult it was, that a man should have the necessity of speaking, and in speaking should not say something which he would wish not to have said; and being affected with weariness from these sins, he sought to avoid such things. He suffers this difficulty, this one leaping over. Let him not judge me who is not yet leaping over: let him leap over, and experience what I speak; for then he will be both a witness and a son of truth. When, therefore, these things had happened to him, he had resolved not to speak, lest he say something which he would repent of having spoken. This his first words indicate: *I said: I will keep my ways, that I may not transgress in my tongue.* Keep, then, your ways, O Jeduthun, and do not transgress in your tongue: weigh what you are about to say, examine it, consult the inner truth, and so bring it forth to the outer hearer. You seek these things very often in the perturbation of affairs, in the occupation of minds, while the very weakness of the soul, which the body that is corrupted weighs down, both wishes to hear and to speak, to hear within, to speak without, sometimes, perturbed by the zeal of speaking, fails by the negligence of recognizing: and in these things says something which perhaps ought not to be said. Against these things silence is the better remedy. For the sinner stands, a sinner by a certain proper mark, someone proud and envious; he hears the one speaking, leaping over, he catches the words, he sets snares; it is difficult that he should not find something not said as it ought to have been said: nor by hearing does he pardon, but by envying he slanders. Against these this Jeduthun, leaping over them, had chosen to be silent; whence he sang thus: *I said: I will keep my ways, that I may not transgress in my tongue.* As long as I am caught by the slanderous, or am hunted, even if I am not caught: *I will keep my ways, that I may not transgress in my tongue.* Although I have leaped over earthly delights, although the flitting affections of temporal things do not catch me, although I now despise these lowest things, and rise to better ones; yet in those very better ones the delight of understanding before God suffices me: what need have I to speak things to be caught at, and to give an opening to slanderers? *I said,* then: *I will keep my ways, that I may not transgress in my tongue. I set a guard to my mouth.* Why this? On account of the godly, on account of the studious, on account of the faithful and the holy? Far be it. They hear in such a way that what they approve they praise: but what they disapprove among many things they perhaps praise, they give pardon rather than prepare slander. On account of whom, then, do you wish to keep your ways, that you may not transgress in your tongue, and set a guard to your mouth? Hear: *While the sinner stands against me.* He does not stand toward me, but *stands against me.* What shall I finally say, whereby I may satisfy? I speak of spiritual things to a carnal man, seeing and hearing without, deaf and blind within. For the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God[^9]. But unless he were found animal, when would he slander? Blessed is he who tells the word to a hearing ear, not to the ear of a sinner standing against him. For such, many stood around and raged around, when he was led like a sheep to be slain, and like a lamb before its shearer, without voice, so he opened not his mouth[^10]. For what would you say to the swollen, the turbulent, the slanderous, the litigious, the wordy? What would you say, holy and godly, and leaping over them in religion: when, even to those gladly hearing, desiring to learn, gaping for the food of truth, eagerly receiving, the Lord too says, *I have yet many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^11]? And the Apostle: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal*: yet not to be despaired of, but to be nourished. For it follows: *As to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not yet able.* Therefore say even now: *But neither indeed are you yet able*[^12]. Do not, then, hasten to hear what you do not grasp, but grow that you may grasp. So we address a little one, to be nourished in the bosom of mother Church with godly milk, and to be made fit for the food of the Lord's table. But what such thing should I say to a sinner standing against me, thinking or feigning himself fit for the things he does not grasp; so that when I have said it to him, and he has not grasped it, he should think not that he did not grasp it, but that I failed? Therefore on account of this sinner standing against me, *I set a guard to my mouth.*

**4.** [v. 3] And what followed? *I became deaf, and was humbled, and was silent from good things*[^13]. For this one leaping over suffers difficulty on a certain step, which he has now leaped over; and he seeks to leap over from there too, to avoid this difficulty. I feared to sin, so that I would not speak, that I might impose silence on myself; for this I had said: *I will keep my ways, that I may not transgress in my tongue*: and while I fear to speak lest I sin, *I became deaf, and was humbled, and was silent from good things.* While I too much fear lest I speak some evil things, I kept silent about all good things: *I became deaf, and was humbled, and was silent from good things.* For whence did I speak good things, except because I heard? *For to my hearing you will give exultation and gladness*[^14]. And the friend of the bridegroom stands, and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the voice, not his own, but the bridegroom's[^15]. That he may say true things, he hears what he is to say. For he who speaks a lie, speaks of his own[^16]. Therefore this man suffered something sad and troublesome; and by this his confession he admonishes that what he suffered is to be guarded against, not imitated. For by fearing too much, as I said, lest he speak some things not good, he resolved to speak nothing even good: and because he resolved to be silent, he began not to hear. For you stand, if you are leaping over, you await to hear from God what you are to say to men: between rich God, and the needy man seeking what he may hear, you run between, leaping over, you who can both hear from here, and speak this way: if you choose not to speak this way, you will not deserve to hear from there: you despise the poor, you are despised by the rich. Have you forgotten that you are a servant, whom the Lord set over his household to give rations to his fellow servants? Why, then, do you seek to receive, what you are slothful to dispense? Deservedly, then, because what you had received you were unwilling to speak, you are hindered from receiving what you desired to receive. For you wished something, you had something: give what you have, that you may deserve to receive what you do not have. So, when I had, as it were, set a guard to my mouth, and had enjoined silence on myself, because I saw speech everywhere dangerous; there came to pass in me, he says, what I did not wish; *I became deaf, and was humbled*: I did not humble myself; but, *was humbled. I became deaf, and was humbled, and was silent from good things.* I began not to speak good things, while I fear lest I speak some evil things: and I reproved my own counsel. For *I was silent from good things. And my grief was renewed.* For from a certain grief which the slanderers and reprovers had inflicted on me, I had, as it were, rested in silence, and that grief which had been made by slanderers had ceased: but when I was silent from good things, my grief was renewed. I began to grieve more that I had kept silent about the things I ought to speak, than I had grieved that I had spoken the things I ought not to speak. *My grief was renewed.*

**5.** [vv. 4-5] *And in my meditation a fire shall burn*[^17]. My heart began to be restless. I saw the senseless, and I pined away, I did not rebuke; and as I thus kept silent, the zeal of your house consumed me. For I looked to my Lord saying, *Wicked and slothful servant, you should have given my money to the bankers, and I, coming, would have exacted it with interest*[^18]. And what follows, may God turn away from his dispensers: *Let him be cast into the outer darkness,* bound hand and foot, a servant, not an overthrower bent on destroying, but slothful in dispensing[^19]. What ought those to expect who have consumed with luxury, if those are condemned who have kept with sloth? *In my meditation a fire shall burn.* And set in this fluctuation of speaking and being silent, between those who are ready to slander, and those who long to be instructed, between the abundant and the needy, made a reproach to those who abounded, and a contempt to the proud, looking to those blessed who hunger and thirst for justice; laboring in both, afflicted in both; in peril lest he cast pearls before swine, in peril lest he not dispense rations to his fellow servants: in this heat he sought another, better place than this dispensation, in which a man so labors and is imperiled; and sighing toward a certain end, where he would not suffer these things, toward that end, I say, in which the Lord is to say to the good dispenser, *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^20]: *I spoke,* he says, *with my tongue.* Amid these heats, amid these perils, amid these difficulties, because the law of the Lord so delights, that yet, on account of the abundance of scandals, the love of many grows cold: amid these heats, then: *I spoke,* he says, *with my tongue.* To whom? Not to the hearer whom I wish to instruct, but to the hearer-of-prayers by whom I wish to be instructed. *I spoke with my tongue,* to him from whom I hear within, if I hear any good thing, if I hear any true thing. What did you speak? *Make known to me, O Lord, my end*[^21], he says. For I have leaped over certain things, and have come to certain things; and those things to which I came are better than those from which I leaped over: but there still remains what is to be leaped over. For we shall not remain here, where we suffer temptations, where scandals, where hearers and slanderers. *Make known to me my end*: the end which I lack, not the course which is at hand for me.

**6.** He means that end which the Apostle, running, beheld, and confessed about his own imperfection, beholding one thing in himself, seeking another elsewhere. For he says, *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect, brothers, I do not reckon myself to have apprehended.* And lest you should say: If the Apostle has not apprehended, have I apprehended? if the Apostle is not perfect, am I perfect? see what he does, attend to what he says. What, then, do you do, O Apostle? Have you not yet apprehended, are you not yet perfect? What do you do? to what action do you exhort me? what do you set before me to imitate and follow? *But one thing,* he says, *forgetting the things that are behind, stretched out toward the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the prize of the supernal calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^22]: according to intention, not yet according to arrival, not yet according to apprehension. Let us not slip back to where we have already leaped over, nor remain in the things to which we have already come. Let us run, let us be intent, we are on the way: and be not so secure about the things you have passed, as solicitous for those to which you have not yet come. *Forgetting,* he says, *the things that are behind, stretched out toward the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the prize of the supernal calling of God in Christ Jesus.* For he himself is the end. But one thing, this is that one thing: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^23]. But one thing, which is also called one in another psalm: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek. Forgetting the things that are behind, stretched out toward the things that are before: One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life.* For what? *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*[^24]. For there I shall rejoice over a companion, I shall not fear an adversary: for there a fellow contemplator with me will be a friend, not a slanderer an enemy. This this Jeduthun desired, that it be made known to him while he was here, so that he might know what he lacked; and that he might not so much rejoice over those things to which he had come, as desire those to which he had not yet come; and, certain things being now leaped over, that he might not remain on the way, but be carried by desire into the supernal regions; until he who had leaped over certain things might leap over all, and from a certain besprinkling of the Lord's drops coming from the cloud of the Scriptures, might come like a stag to the fountain of life, and in that light see light, and be hidden in the countenance of God from the disturbance of men: where he would say: It is well, I want nothing further, I love all here, I fear no one here. A good desire, a holy desire. You who already have this, rejoice with us; and pray that we may have it perseveringly, lest amid scandals we fail. For we too ask this same thing for you. For it is not that we are worthy to pray for you, and you unworthy to pray for us. The Apostle commended himself to his hearers to whom he preached the word of God. Pray, then, for us, brothers, that we may both well see what is to be seen, and well say what is to be said. But this desire, I know, is in few: nor do they best understand me, except those who have tasted whence I speak. Yet we speak to all, both to those having such desire, and to those not yet having it: to those having it, that they may sigh with us in it; to those not having it, that they may shake off sloth, leap over lowest things, come to the sweetness of the Lord's law, not remain in the delights of the unjust. For many tell many things, and many praise many things, the unjust unjust things. And truly those unjust things too have a delight, but not as your law, O Lord. Let those, then, say with us who believe that we too say these things. For this business is within, it can be shown by no words. But let him who does this believe that it is in another too: let him not think that he alone has received what is of God. Let Jeduthun, then, say in these things: *Make known to me, O Lord, my end.*

**7.** *And the number of my days, which is*[^25]. I seek the number of my days which is. So I can say, so I can understand, a number without number, just as years without years can be spoken of. For where there are years, there is, of course, as it were a number; and yet: You are the very same, and your years shall not fail[^26]. Make known to me the number of my days, but that which is. What then? Is this number in which you are, not? Plainly, if I attend well, it is not: if I cleave to it, it as it were is; if I leap over it, it is not: if, shaking myself from these things, I contemplate the supernal, if I compare the passing things with those that remain, I see what is true: but what seems to be more than it is? Shall I say that these are my days? These, I say, shall I say are days; and shall I rashly give so great a word to this course of falling things? So, then, I myself, failing, almost am not, that there has slipped from me who said, *I am who am*[^27]. Is there, then, any number of days? Truly there is, and it is without end. But in these days I shall say there is something, if I hold the day about which you ask me whether it is: that you may at least ask me, hold that whence you ask me. Do you hold this day? If you held yesterday, you hold today too. But yesterday, you say, I do not hold, because it now is not: but this one in which I am, and which is with me, I hold. So, then, has it slipped from you how much of it has passed since the first light? Did not this day begin from the first hour? Give me its first hour? give me also its second; for perhaps it too has flown away. The third, you say, I will give you: for perhaps in it we now are. Surely, then, these days exist, and there is a third day: and if you will give the third, you will give not a day, but an hour. Yet not even this do I grant you, if you have somehow leaped over these things with me. Give me at least the third hour, give me the very one in which you are. For if something of it has already passed, and something of it still remains; neither what has passed can you give me, because it now is not; nor what remains, because it not yet is. What will you give me of this hour, which is now being transacted? What will you give me of that to which I may commit this word, that I may say, It is? When you say the very "It is," surely it is one syllable, and is one moment, and the syllable has three letters: in the very stroke you do not reach the second letter of this word, unless the first is finished; the third will not sound, unless the second too has passed. What will you give me of this one syllable? And do you hold days, who do not hold one syllable? With moments flying away all things are snatched off, a torrent of things flows; of which torrent he who is on the way drank for us, who has now exalted his head[^28]. These days, then, are not: they almost go before they come; and when they have come, they cannot stand: they join themselves, they follow each other, and do not hold themselves. Nothing is recalled from the past: what is to come is awaited as about to pass; it is not yet had, while it has not come; it is not held, when it has come. *The number,* then, *of my days which is*: not this one which is not, and which more difficultly and dangerously disturbs me, both is and is not; nor can we say that to be which does not stand, nor that not to be which comes and passes. I seek that simple "Is": I seek the true "Is": I seek the genuine "Is": that "Is" which is in that Jerusalem the bride of my Lord, where there will be no death, there will be no failing, there will be no passing day, but an abiding one, which is neither preceded by yesterday, nor driven on by tomorrow. This, I say, *the number of my days which is, make known to me.*

**8.** *That I may know what is lacking to me*[^29]. For this is lacking to me, laboring here; and as long as it is lacking to me, I do not call myself perfect: as long as I do not receive this, I say, *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect; but I follow toward the prize of the supernal calling of God*[^30]: this I shall receive as the reward of my course. A certain dwelling will be the end of the running; and in that dwelling a homeland without pilgrimage, without sedition, without temptation. Therefore: *Make known to me* this *number of my days which is, that I may know what is lacking to me;* because I am not yet there: lest I be proud of that in which I now am, so that I be found in him not having my own justice. For in comparison with that which is, attending to these things which are not so, and seeing more to be lacking to me than present to me, I shall be more humble from what is lacking, than more lifted up from what is present. For those who think they have something while they live here, by being proud do not receive what is lacking; because they think great what is present: for he who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, seduces himself[^31]. Nor are these great for this reason: for inflation and swelling imitate greatness, but do not have health.

**9.** [v. 6] Now, then, this one leaping over, doing something hidden in the heart, which no one knows except him who likewise does it; as though, his end being made known to him, obtaining what he asked, the number of his days being made known to him, not that which passes, but that which is, attends to these things which he leaped over, and compares them with the higher knowledge: and as if you should say to him: Why did you desire the number of your days which is? for what do you say of these days? attending to that other, he says these things: *Behold, you have set my days old*[^32]. For these grow old, I want new ones, new ones never growing old; so that I may say, The old things have passed away, behold they have become new: now in hope, then in reality. For, renewed by faith and hope, how many old things do we still do? For we are not so clothed with Christ that we now bear nothing from Adam. See the Adam growing old, and Christ being renewed in us: *And though,* he says, *our outer man is corrupted, yet the inner is renewed from day to day*[^33]. Therefore, attending to sin, to mortality, to the times flying past, to groaning and labor and sweat, to ages succeeding, not remaining, passing without our perceiving it from infancy to old age, attending to these things, let us see here the old man, the old day, the old song, the Old Testament: but turned to the inner man, to the things that are to be renewed, in place of those that will be changed, let us find the new man, the new day, the new song, the New Testament; and so let us love this newness, that we may not there fear oldness. Now, then, in this course we pass from the old to the new: that very passage is transacted when the outer things are corrupted, and the inner renewed; until even this very thing which is outwardly corrupted, pays its debt to nature, comes to death, and this too is renewed in the resurrection. Then truly all things will become new, the rest which are now in hope. You do something, then, now, by stripping yourself of old things, and running into new ones. Running, then, into new things, and stretched out toward the things that are before: *Make known to me, O Lord, my end, and the number of my days which is, that I may know what is lacking to me.* Behold, he still drags Adam, and so hastens to Christ. *Behold,* he says, *you have set my days old.* Old days from Adam, those old days you have set: they grow old daily; and so grow old, that sometime they are even consumed. *And my substance is as nothing before you.* Before you, O Lord, my substance is as nothing, before you who see this: and I, when I see this, see it before you, before men I do not see it. For what shall I say? with what words shall I show that what I am is nothing in comparison with that which is? But it is said within, it is somehow felt within. *Before you,* O Lord, where your eyes are, not where the eyes of men are: what, where your eyes are? *My substance is as nothing.*

**10.** *Yet all things are vanity, every man living. Yet*: for what was he saying? Behold now I have leaped over all mortal things, and despised the lowest, trampled earthly things, ascended to the delight of the law of the Lord, fluctuated in the dispensation of the numbers of the Lord's days, desired even that end, of which there is no end; I desired the number of my days which is, because the number of these days is not; behold, I am now such, so great things have I leaped over, I so gape after the things that stand: *Yet,* such as I am here, as long as I am here, as long as I am in this world, as long as I carry mortal flesh, as long as human life on earth is a temptation, as long as amid scandals I sigh, as long as I fear lest I fall who stand, as long as both my evils and my goods are uncertain to me, *all things are vanity, every man living. Every one,* I say, both cleaving and leaping over, and Jeduthun himself still pertains to all vanity: because all things are vanity, and vanity of vanities: what abundance is there for a man in all his labor, with which he himself labors under the sun[^34]? Is Jeduthun too still under the sun? He has something under the sun, he has something beyond the sun. Under the sun he has to wake, to sleep, to eat, to drink, to hunger, to thirst, to flourish, to be wearied, to become a boy, to become young, to grow old, to have uncertain the things he desires and fears: all these things under the sun Jeduthun too has, even he who leaps over them. Whence, then, leaping over? From that desire: *Make known to me, O Lord, my end.* For this desire is beyond the sun, it is not under the sun. Under the sun are all visible things: whatever is not visible, is not under the sun. Not visible is faith, not visible is hope, not visible is love, not visible is kindness, not visible, finally, is that chaste fear remaining forever and ever. Having sweetness and consolation in all these things, Jeduthun, and conversing beyond the sun, because his conversation is in the heavens[^35], from these things which he still has under the sun, groans; and he despises these and grieves, in those he burns which he desires. He spoke of those, let him speak also of these. You heard the things to be desired, hear the things to be despised. *Yet all things are vanity, every man living.*

**11.** [v. 7] *Although in an image a man walks*[^36]. In what image, except of him who said, *Let us make man to our image and likeness*[^37]? *Although in an image a man walks.* For therefore *although,* because this image is something great. And this *although,* there followed *yet*: so that what you heard, *although,* is beyond the sun; but this which follows, *yet,* is under the sun; and that pertains to truth, this to vanity. *Although,* then, *in an image a man walks: yet he is disturbed in vain.* Hear his disturbance, and see whether it is not vain: that you may trample it, leap over it, and dwell in the heights, where this vanity is not. What vanity? *He treasures up, and does not know for whom he will gather these things.* O insane vanity! Blessed is he whose hope is the Lord, and who has not looked back to vanities and lying follies[^38]. I seem to you to be raving, greedy one, when I say these things: these words seem to you old wives' tales. For you, forsooth, a man of great counsel and great prudence, devise daily kinds of acquiring money, from business, from agriculture, perhaps also from eloquence, from the consulting of law, from military service, you add also from usury. A man of sense, you let pass nothing at all, whence coin upon coin, and in secret it may be more diligently scraped together. You plunder a man, you beware a plunderer; what you do you fear to suffer, and in that which you suffer you do not correct yourself. But you do not suffer: for you are a prudent man, you keep well, you not only gather well: you have where to put it, to whom to commit it, how nothing may perish of what you have gathered. I question your heart, I examine your prudence: Behold, you have gathered, behold, you have so kept it, that you can lose nothing of the things you kept; tell me for whom you keep it? I do not deal with you, I do not recall, I do not exaggerate whatever other evil the greed of your vanity has: this one thing I set forth, this I examine, which the occasion of the reading of this psalm gives me. Plainly you gather, you treasure up: I do not say: Lest perhaps while you gather, you be gathered: I do not say: Lest perhaps when you wish to be a plunderer, you be plunder: this I will say more openly; for perhaps, blind with greed, you have not heard or understood: I do not say, I say, Lest perhaps when you wish to be the plunderer of a lesser, you be the plunder of a greater. For you do not perceive that you are in the sea, nor discern the smaller fish devoured by the larger. I do not say these things, I do not say the difficulties and perils in the very acquiring of money, how great things they suffer who gather it, in how many things they are imperiled, in almost everything they see death: I pass over all these. Plainly you gather with none contradicting, you keep with none taking away: shake out your heart, and so great a prudence by which you deride me, by which you think me foolish in speaking these things; and tell me: you treasure up; for whom will you gather these things? I see what you wish to say, as though what you wish to say has not occurred to him: you are about to say, For my children I keep it. This is the voice of piety, the excuse of iniquity: For my children, you say, I keep it. You keep it even for your children. Did Jeduthun, then, not know this? He knew it plainly, but he reckoned it among the old days, and therefore despised it; because he was hastening to the new days.

**12.** For behold, I examine you with your children: you keep, about to pass away, for those about to pass away, rather indeed passing away for those passing away. For I said you were about to pass away, as though you now remained. This very today: from when we began to speak even to this moment, you perceive that we have grown old. For you do not discern even the growth of your hairs; and now while you stand, while you are here, while you do something, while you speak, your hairs grow in you: for they did not suddenly grow, that you should seek a barber. The age, then, is transacted, flying away, both in those who understand, and those who do not perceive, and those badly occupied with other things. You pass, and you keep for your passing son. First I ask this of you: Do you know that the one for whom you keep it is going to possess it? or, if he is not yet born, do you know he is going to be born? You keep for your children, it is uncertain whether they are to be or to possess; nor do you lay up the treasure where it is to be laid up. For your Lord would not give such counsel to his servant, that he should lose his peculium. You are a servant with a peculium, of a certain great head of a household. What you love and what you have, he himself gave you, and he does not wish you to lose what he gave you, who will give even himself to you. But neither, I say, does he wish you to lose this which he gave you for a time. It is much, it overflows, it surpasses the strength of your need, it is now surely reckoned superfluous: nor do I wish you to lose this, says your Lord. And what do I do? Move it elsewhere; where you have put it is not a safe place. Surely you wish to serve greed: see lest perhaps my counsel agree even with greed itself. For you wish to have what you have, and not to lose it: I show you a place where you may put it. Do not treasure up on the earth, not knowing for whom you gather these things, and afterward how he is to consume it who will possess it, who will hold it. For perhaps, himself possessed, he will possess; and what he has from you he will not hold. Perhaps while you keep for him, before he comes you will lose it. To your solicitude I give counsel: *Treasure up for yourselves treasure in heaven*[^39]. If here on earth you wished to keep riches, you would seek a granary: perhaps you would not entrust it to your house, on account of your domestics: you would commit it to the bankers' street: for there a fall is difficult, a thief does not easily draw near, all things are well kept. Why do you think these things, except because you have nothing better where to keep it? What if I will give you something better? I will tell you: Do not commit it to this less suitable one, but there is a certain suitable one, commit it to him: he has great granaries, where riches cannot perish; he is great, rich above all the rich. Now perhaps you are about to say: And when do I dare to commit to such a one? What if he himself exhorts you? Recognize him, he is not only a head of a household, but is also your Lord. I do not wish, he says, my servant, that you lose your peculium, recognize where to put it: why do you put it there where you can lose it; where, even if you do not lose it, you cannot remain perpetually? There is another place, to which I will transfer you. Let what you have go before you; do not fear to lose it; I was the giver, I will be the keeper. This your Lord says to you: question your faith, see whether you are willing to believe him. You are about to say: I count as lost what I do not see, here I want to see it. While you wish to see it here, neither here will you see it, and there you will have nothing. You have I know not what treasures hidden in the earth; when you go forth, you do not carry them with you. You came to hear the sermon, to gather inner riches, you think of outer ones: have you brought them here with you? Behold, not even now do you see them. You believe you have at home what you know you put there: do you know you have not lost it? How many have returned to their homes, and not found what they had put there? At this perhaps the hearts of the covetous have trembled; and because I said many often returned to their home, and did not find what they had put there, each one said in his heart: Far be it, Bishop; wish good, pray for us; far be it that it happen, far be it that it be so; I trust in God, that what I put I find safe. You trust in God, and do not trust God himself? I trust in Christ, that what I put will be safe, no one will draw near, no one will take it away. You wish to be secure by trusting in Christ, that you lose nothing of your house: you will rather be secure by trusting Christ, so as to put it where he gave counsel. Or are you secure about your servant, and solicitous about your Lord? secure about your house, and solicitous about heaven? But I, you say, how do I put it in heaven? I have given you counsel: where I say, put it: how it may reach heaven, I do not wish you to know. Put it in the hands of the poor, give to the needy: what is it to you how it reaches there? Shall I not bring through what I receive? Or have you forgotten: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^40]? If some friend of yours had certain pools or cisterns, and various receptacles of buildings for keeping some liquid, of wine or oil, and you sought where to hide or keep your fruits, and he said to you: I keep them for you: and he had to those receptacles certain hidden channels and passages, so that through these might go secretly what was poured openly: and he said to you: What you have, pour here: but you saw that this was not the place where you thought to put it, and you feared to pour it: would not he, who knew certain hidden contrivances of his places, say to you: Pour securely, from here it reaches there; you do not see by what way, but believe me, who built it? For he built, through whom all things were made, dwellings for us all: there he wishes what we have to go before, lest we lose it here on earth. But when you keep it on earth, tell me for whom you will gather it? You have children: count one more, and give something to Christ too. *He treasures up, and does not know for whom he will gather these things: he is disturbed in vain.*

**13.** [v. 8] *And now*: when, says this Jeduthun, looking back at a certain vanity, looking up at a certain truth, set in the middle, having something below him, something above him (below him he has what he leaped over, above him he has whither he stretches himself): *And now,* he says, when I have leaped over certain things, when I have trampled many things, when I am no longer held by temporal things; I am not yet perfect, I have not yet received. For in hope we have been saved: but hope that is seen is not hope. For what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, by patience we await it[^41]. Therefore: *And now what is my expectation? is it not the Lord?*[^42] He himself is my expectation, who gave all these things which I despise: he himself will give me also himself, who is above all things, and through whom all things were made, and by whom I was made among all things, he himself is my expectation, the Lord. You see Jeduthun, brothers, you see how he waits. Let no one, then, call himself perfect here: he deceives himself, he beguiles himself, he seduces himself, he cannot have perfection here. And what does it profit, that he loses humility? *And now what is my expectation? is it not the Lord?* When he has come, he is no longer awaited; then will be that perfection: but now, however much Jeduthun has leaped over, he still waits. *And my substance is before you always.* Now advancing, now tending to him, and beginning somewhat to be: *my substance is before you always.* But this substance is also before men. You have gold, you have silver, slaves, estates, trees, cattle, servants; these can be seen even by men: there is a certain substance always before you. *And my substance is before you always.*

**14.** [v. 9] *From all my iniquities deliver me*[^43]. Many things I have leaped over, many things indeed I have leaped over: but *if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^44]. Many things I have leaped over; but still I beat my breast, and say, *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^45]. You, then, my expectation, my end: *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto justice to everyone who believes*[^46]. *From all,* not only from those, lest I roll back into the things I have leaped over: but from all altogether, on account of which I now say, beating my breast, *Forgive us our debts. From all my iniquities deliver me*: being thus wise, and holding what the Apostle says, *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind.* For when he said he was not yet perfect, there immediately following he said, *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind.* What is, *As many as are perfect*? Just before you had said, *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect.* Follow the order of the sayings. *But one thing, forgetting the things that are behind, stretched out toward the things that are before, according to my intention I follow, toward the prize of the supernal calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^47]. Therefore not yet perfect, because he follows toward the prize of the supernal calling of God, which he has not yet found, to which he has not yet come. But if he is not perfect, because he has not come there, which of us is perfect? Yet he follows and says, *As many of us as are perfect, let us be of this mind.* Are you not perfect, O Apostle, and we perfect? But has it slipped from you that he just now said he was not perfect? For he does not say: As many as are perfect, be of this mind; but: *As many,* he says, *as are perfect, let us be of this mind*: when a little before he had said, *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect.* Therefore you cannot here be perfect in any other way, except by knowing that you cannot be perfect here. This, then, will be your perfection, that you have so leaped over certain things, that you hasten to certain things; that you have so leaped over certain things, that there remains another to which, all things being transacted, you must leap over. This is the safe faith. For whoever thinks he has now arrived, sets himself on high, that he may fall.

**15.** Because, then, I am thus minded, because I call myself both imperfect and perfect: imperfect indeed, because I have not yet received something which I wish; but perfect, because I know this very thing that is lacking to me: because, then, I am thus minded, because I despise human things, because I do not wish to rejoice in perishing things, because I am derided by the greedy man boasting himself that he is prudent, and deriding me as though I were foolish, because I act thus, because I take this way: *You have made me,* he says, *a reproach to the foolish*[^48]. You willed me to live among them, to preach the truth among them, who love vanity; and I cannot but be derided by them: because we have been made a spectacle to this world, and to angels, and to men; angels praising, men reproaching: rather, both angels praising and blaming, and men praising and blaming. On the right and on the left we have arms, in which we war, through glory and ignobility, through infamy and good fame, as seducers and truthful[^49]. These among angels, these among men: because among the angels too there are holy angels, whom by living well we please; and there are prevaricating angels, whom by living well we displease: and among men there are holy men, to whom our life is pleasing; there are most worthless men, who deride our good life. And these are arms, those on the right, those on the left; yet both are arms: with both arms, both right and left, I use, both with those praising and those blaming, both with those bestowing honor and those inflicting ignominy: with both these arms I struggle with the devil, with both I strike him: with prosperous things, if I am not corrupted; with adverse, if I am not broken.

**16.** [vv. 10-11] *You have made me,* then, *a reproach to the foolish. I became deaf, and did not open my mouth*[^50]. But against the foolish *I became deaf, and did not open my mouth.* For to whom should I say what is transacted in me? For I would hear what the Lord God speaks in me, since he will speak peace to his people: but *there is no peace for the ungodly,* says the Lord[^51]. *I became deaf, and did not open my mouth. Because you are he who made me.* Did you therefore not open your mouth, because it is God who made you? It is wonderful. For did God not make your mouth for speaking? He who planted the ear, does he not hear? he who formed the eye, does he not see[^52]? God gave you a mouth for speaking: and do you say, *I became deaf, and did not open my mouth: because you are he who made me*? Or does *Because you are he who made me* pertain to the following verse? *Because you are he who made me, remove from me your scourges.* Because you are he who made me, do not destroy me: only strike me that I may advance, not that I may fail: only beat me that I may be brought forth, not that I may be broken in pieces. *Because you are he who made me, remove from me your scourges.*

**17.** [v. 12] *From the strength of your hand I have failed in rebukes*[^53]: that is, when you rebuked me, I failed. And what is your rebuking, except what follows? *For iniquity you have chastened a man, and you have made my soul to pine away like a spider.* It is much that this Jeduthun understands: if anyone understands with him, if anyone leaps over with him. For he says that in the rebukes of God he has failed, and wishes the scourges to be removed from him, since he himself is he who made him. He who made, let him himself remake; and he who created, let him himself recreate. But yet that he so failed, that he wished himself both to be recreated and reformed, do we think, brothers, it was done without cause? *For iniquity,* he says, *you have chastened a man.* The whole of what I failed, that I am weak, that I cry from the depth, this whole is for iniquity: and in this you have instructed, not condemned: *For iniquity you have chastened a man.* Hear this more plainly from another psalm: *It is good for me that you have humbled me, that I may learn your justifications*[^54]. And I was humbled, and it is good for me: and it is a punishment, and it is grace. What does he keep after the punishment, who through grace displays the punishment? For he himself is he of whom it was said, *I was humbled, and he saved me*[^55]: and, *It is good for me that you have humbled me, that I may learn your justifications. For iniquity you have chastened a man.* And that which is written, *You who form grief in a precept*[^56], could be said to God only by one leaping over; because only by one leaping over could it be seen. *You form,* he says, *grief in a precept,* you make for me a precept out of grief. You shape my very grief: you do not leave it shapeless, but shape it: and my grief, shaped, inflicted by you, will be a precept to me, that I may be freed by you. For "you form," it was said, you form grief, you shape grief, you mould grief, you do not feign grief: as a craftsman forms, whence the potter too is named from forming. Therefore: *For iniquity you have chastened a man.* I see myself in evils, I see myself in punishment, and with you I do not see iniquity. If, then, I am in punishment and with you there is no iniquity, does it not remain that for iniquity you have instructed a man?

**18.** And how have you *chastened*? Tell the very instruction, O Jeduthun, how were you instructed? *And you have made my soul to pine away like a spider.* This is the instruction. What is more wasting than a spider? I mean the animal itself. Although, what is more wasting than the very webs of spiders? Attend to the animal itself, how wasting it is. Set your finger upon it lightly, it is ruin: nothing at all is more wasting. Such you have made my soul, he says, by instructing me for iniquity. When the instruction made me weak, a certain strength was a vice. I see that some have flown ahead, and understood: but the slower are not to be deserted by the swifter, that they may together take the way of the discourse. This I said, understand this: If the instruction of the just God made this weakness, a certain strength was a vice. By a certain strength a man displeased, that he might be instructed by weakness: because by a certain pride he displeased, that he might be instructed by humility. All the proud say they are strong. Therefore many conquered, coming from the east and the west, so as to recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven: why did they conquer? Because they were unwilling to be strong. What is, they were unwilling to be strong? They feared to presume on themselves: they did not establish their own justice, that they might be subjected to the justice of God. Finally, when the Lord said this: *Many shall come from the east and the west, and shall recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven; but the sons of the kingdom,* that is, the Jews, ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish their own, *shall go into the outer darkness;* recall that faith of a certain Centurion, one from the people of the Gentiles, so weak in himself, so not strong, that he said, *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof.* He was not worthy to receive Christ into his house, and had already received him in his heart. For that master of humility, the Son of man, had already found in his breast where to recline his head. To this word of the Centurion the Lord, looking back, said to those following him, *Amen I say to you, I have not found so great faith in Israel*[^57]. This weak one he found, the Israelites strong he found, so that between the two he said, *The healthy do not need a physician, but those who are ill*[^58]. On account of this, then, that is, on account of this humility: *Many shall come from the east and the west, and shall recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven; but the sons of the kingdom shall go into the outer darkness.* Behold, you are mortal, behold, you carry rotting flesh, and like one of the princes you shall fall: like men you shall die, and shall fall like the devil. What does the medicine of mortality profit you? The devil is proud, as an angel not having mortal flesh: but you who have received mortal flesh, and not even this profits you, that you be humbled by so great weakness, like one of the princes you shall fall. This, then, is the first grace of the benefit of God, to reduce us to the confession of weakness, that whatever good we can do, whatever power we have, we may be in him: so that he who glories, may glory in the Lord. *When I am weak,* he says, *then am I strong*[^59]. *For iniquity you have chastened a man: and you have made my soul to pine away like a spider.*

**19.** *Yet in vain is every man living disturbed*[^60]. He returns to that which a little before he recalled: although here he advances, *in vain is every man living disturbed*: living, of course, in uncertainty. For who is secure even about his own good? *He is disturbed in vain.* Let him cast his care upon the Lord, upon him let him cast whatever he is solicitous about, let him himself nourish, let him himself guard. For what in this earth is certain, except death? Consider all things altogether, whether good or evil of this life, whether in justice itself, or in iniquity itself: what here is certain, except death? You have advanced: what you are today, you know; what you will be tomorrow, you do not know. You are a sinner: what you are today, you know; what you will be tomorrow, you do not know. You hope for money; it is uncertain whether it will come. You hope for a wife; it is uncertain whether you will receive her, or what kind you will receive. You hope for children; it is uncertain whether they will be born: they are born; it is uncertain whether they will live: they live; it is uncertain whether they will advance or fail. Wherever you turn, all things uncertain: death alone certain. You are poor; it is uncertain whether you will grow rich: unlearned; it is uncertain whether you will be instructed: feeble; it is uncertain whether you will recover. You are born; it is certain that you will die: and in this very thing, that death itself is certain, the day of death is uncertain. Amid these uncertain things, where death alone is certain, whose hour too is uncertain, and which alone is much guarded against, which in no way is avoided, *every man living is disturbed in vain.*

**20.** [v. 13] Therefore, amid these things now leaping over, now engaged in certain higher things, despising these lowest ones, set amid these: *Hear,* he says, *my prayer*[^61]. Over what do I rejoice, over what do I groan? Over the things transacted I rejoice, for the things that remain I groan. *Hear my prayer and my supplication: with your ears perceive my tears.* For is it that, because I have leaped over so great things, because I have transcended so great things, I now do not weep? Do I not much rather weep? For he who adds knowledge, adds grief[^62]. The more I desire what is absent, do I not the more groan until it comes, the more weep until it comes? Is it not the more, the more scandals increase, the more iniquity abounds, the more the love of many grows cold? I say, *Who will give water to my head, and to my eyes a fountain of tears?*[^63] *Hear my prayer and my supplication: with your ears perceive my tears. Be not silent toward me.* Let me not grow deaf forever. *Be not silent toward me*: let me hear you. For secretly God speaks, to many he speaks in the heart: and there is a great sound there in the great silence of the heart, when with a great voice he says, *I am your salvation. Say,* he says, *to my soul: I am your salvation*[^64]. By this voice, by which God says to the soul, *I am your salvation,* he desires not to be silent toward him. *Be not silent toward me.*

**21.** *Because I am a sojourner with you*[^65]. But with whom a sojourner? When I was with the devil, I was a sojourner, but I had a bad landlord: but now I am indeed with you, but still a sojourner. What is, *a sojourner*? Whence I am about to migrate, not where I am perpetually to remain. Where I am perpetually to remain, let it be called my house: whence I am about to migrate, I am a sojourner: but yet I am a sojourner with my God, with whom, a house being received, I am to remain. But what is the house, whither I must migrate from this sojourning? Recognize that house, of which the Apostle says, *We have a dwelling from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens*[^66]. If this house is eternal in the heavens, when we have come to it, we shall not be sojourners. For how will you be a sojourner in an eternal house? But here, where the master of the house is to say: Migrate, and you do not know when he is to say it, be ready. But by desiring the eternal house you will be ready. And do not be angry at him, because when he wills he says: Migrate. For he did not make a bond with you, and bind himself by a certain agreement, and you came as a renter of the house at a fixed rent for a fixed time: when your Lord wills, you are to migrate. For therefore you remain for free. *Because I am a sojourner with you, and a pilgrim.* Therefore there the homeland, there the house: *a sojourner with you, and a pilgrim.* And here too is understood, *with you.* For many are pilgrims with the devil: but those who have now believed and are faithful, are indeed pilgrims, because they have not yet come to that homeland and house, but yet they are with God. *For as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord: and we are eager, whether remaining here, or pilgrims, to be pleasing to him*[^67]. *And a pilgrim and a sojourner: like all my fathers.* If, then, like all my fathers, shall I say that I am not to migrate, when they have migrated? Am I to remain on another condition than they remained?

**22.** [v. 14] What, then, remains for me to ask, since hence without doubt I am to migrate? *Forgive me, that I may be refreshed before I go*[^68]. See, see, Jeduthun, what knots you have to be forgiven you, which forgiven, you wish to be refreshed before you go. For you have certain heats, from which you wish to be refreshed, and you say, *Let me be refreshed;* and you say, *Forgive me.* What will he forgive you, except perhaps that scruple where you say, and whence you say, *Forgive us our debts*[^69]? *Forgive me before I go, and I shall be no more.* Make me free from sins before I go, lest I go with sins. Forgive me, that I may rest in my conscience, that it may be unburdened of the heat of solicitude: by which solicitude I take care for my sin. *Forgive me, that I may be refreshed,* before all things, *before I go, and I shall be no more.* For if you do not forgive me, that I may be refreshed, I shall go and shall not be. *Before I go*: whither if I go, I shall now not be. *Forgive me, that I may be refreshed.* A question has arisen, how he will now not be. Behold, does he not now go to rest? Which God turn away from Jeduthun! For Jeduthun will plainly go, he will go to rest. But suppose someone unjust, not Jeduthun, not leaping over; here treasuring up, brooding over his hoard, unjust, proud, boastful, lifted up, a despiser of the poor lying before his door: will he too not be? What is it, then, *I shall be no more*? For if that rich man was not, who is it who was burning? who is it who desired a drop of water to be dripped onto his tongue from the finger of Lazarus? who is it who said, *Father Abraham, send Lazarus*[^70]? Surely there was one who spoke, and there was one who burned: and who rises again at the end, and who is condemned with the devil in eternal fire. What is it, then, *I shall be no more,* unless this Jeduthun look back at what it is to be, and not to be? For he saw that end, with what heart he could, with what edge of mind he was able, which he had desired to be shown to him, saying, *Make known to me, O Lord, my end.* He saw the number of his days which is: he attended that, below all the things that are, in comparison with that, to be, is not to be; and he said that he was not. For those things remain; these are mutable, mortal, fragile: and the grief itself eternal, full of corruption, for this is not ended, that it may be ended without end. He looked back, then, to that blessed region, the blessed homeland, the blessed house, where the saints are partakers of everlasting life and unchangeable truth: and he feared to go outside, where there is no being; desiring to be there, where there is the highest being. On account of this comparison, then, set between the two, still fearing, he says, *Forgive me, that I may be refreshed before I go, and I shall be no more.* For if you do not forgive me my sins, I shall go forever from you. And from whom shall I go forever? From him who said, *I am who am*: from him who said, *Say to the children of Israel: He who is has sent me to you*[^71]. From him, then, who truly is, he who goes to the contrary, goes to not being.

**23.** And so, my brothers, even if I have been burdensome to the labor of your body, bear it; because I too have labored: and truly I say, that this labor you make for yourselves. For if I perceived that you were wearied of what is said, I would quickly be silent.
