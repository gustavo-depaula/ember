# Exposition of Psalm 48

**1.** How much the word of God avails us for the correction of our life, and for the hoping of his rewards and the dreading of his punishments, let each one measure in himself; and let him set his conscience before his eyes without deceit, nor flatter himself in so great peril; since the Lord our God himself, you see, flatters no one: and if he consoles us by promising his good things, and confirming our hope; yet to those who live badly, and despise his word, he in no way spares. Let each one question himself while there is time, and see where he is, and either persevere in good, or be changed from evil. For as he says in this psalm, not any man, or any angel, but *the God of gods, the Lord, has spoken*[^1]. But by speaking what did he do? *He has called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting.* He who called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting, is our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. The Word made flesh, that he might dwell among us. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, is the God of gods; because through him all things were made, and without him was made nothing. The Word of God, if he is God, surely is the God of gods: but whether he is God, the Gospel answers: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^2]. And if all things were made through him, as it consequently says, even the gods, if any were made, were made through him. For there is one God not made, and truly God he alone. But he alone God, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God.

**2.** [v. 2] *Hear these things, all you nations*[^3]. Not, then, you only who are here. For how great is our voice, that we should so cry, that all nations may hear? For our Lord Jesus Christ cried through the apostles, cried by so many tongues which he sent; and we see this psalm, which before was not recited except in one nation, in the synagogue of the Jews, recited throughout the whole world, through all the churches, and that to be fulfilled which was here said: *Hear these things, all you nations.* To this alone I wish to make your minds attentive, lest on account of bodily labor you fail to raise the mind, the length of this psalm terrifying you. If it can, it will be finished today: if not, something will remain for us for the morrow; yet you, always be attentive. For you will hear, if the Lord wills, only so much as may not burden you, but relieve you. *Hear these things, all you nations*: whence you too are. *Give ear, all you who inhabit the world.* This seems again to have repeated, as though it were too little that he said: *Hear.* What I say, he says, *hear, give ear,* that is, do not hear in passing. What is, *give ear*? What the Lord said: *He who has ears to hear, let him hear*: when all who were in his sight surely had ears; which ears did he seek but those of the heart, when he said: *He who has ears to hear, let him hear*[^4]? These too this psalm strikes. *Give ear, all you who inhabit the world.* Perhaps here too there is some distinction. We ought not indeed to constrain ourselves, but it is no harm to unfold this sentence too. Perhaps there is some difference between what he said, *all nations,* and what he said, *all you who inhabit the world.* For perhaps he wished us to understand more significantly what he said, *you inhabit,* so that by nations we understand all the unjust, but by the inhabitants of the world all the just. For he inhabits who is not held: for he who is held, is inhabited, does not inhabit. As he possesses whatever he has, who is master of his own things; but he is master, who is not entangled by greed: but he who is held by greed, is possessed, not possessor. For we have a certain word marked by inhabiting in the Scripture of God, where it says: *I have chosen to be an outcast in the house of the Lord, rather than to dwell in the tabernacles of sinners*[^5]. For what, if you are an outcast in the house of the Lord, do you not dwell there? He did not wish to signify dwelling, except in those who rule, and hold, and are masters, and govern: but those who are despised, do not as it were dwell, but are subjected. So he said: I wish to be subject in the house of God, rather than to reign in the tabernacles of sinners. And so, if there is some distinction between *all nations,* and *the inhabitants of the world,* as there is a distinction between *hear,* and *give ear,* this indeed seems to have repeated; but yet it is something different which he plainly wished to signify; because these words were to be heard not only by sinners and the ungodly, but also by the just. Now all hear commingled: but when it has come to rendering account, those who heard without cause will be separated from those who gave ear. Let the sinners hear, then: *Hear these things, all you nations.* Let the just too hear, who did not hear without cause, and rule the earth rather than are ruled by the earth: *Give ear, all you who inhabit the world.*

**3.** [v. 3] And again he says: *Both you earth-born and you sons of men*[^6]. What he says, *earth-born,* he referred to sinners; what he says, *sons of men,* to the faithful and just. You see, then, that this distinction is kept. Who, then, are the *earth-born*? The sons of the earth. Who are the sons of the earth? Those who seek earthly inheritances. Who are the *sons of men*? Those who pertain to the Son of man. We have sometimes already distinguished this for your Holiness, and we found that Adam was a man, was not a son of man; but Christ was a son of man, and was God. For whoever pertain to Adam, are *earth-born*: whoever pertain to Christ, are *sons of men.* Yet let all hear, I withdraw my discourse from none. He is earth-born, let him hear on account of judgment; he is a son of man, let him hear on account of the kingdom. *Together in one rich and poor.* Again these are repeated. What he says *rich,* pertains to the earth-born; what he says, *poor,* to the sons of men. Understand the rich as the proud, the poor as the humble. Let one have many resources of money; if he is not lifted up in them, he is poor: let another have nothing, and covet and be inflated; among the rich and reprobate God reckons him. Both the rich and the poor God questions in the heart, not in the chest and the house. Are they not poor who receive the command of the Apostle saying to Timothy: *Charge the rich of this world not to be high-minded*? How did he make those who were rich poor? He took from them the reason for which riches are sought. For no one wishes to be rich, except to be inflated among those among whom he lives, and to seem superior to them. But when he said, *not to be high-minded,* he made them equal to those who do not have; so that perhaps a beggar with a few coins is more lifted up, than that rich man who hears the Apostle saying: *Charge the rich of this world not to be high-minded.* Whence *not high-minded*? If they do what follows: *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy*[^7]. He did not say, who furnishes them; but, *who furnishes us.* Did not Paul himself have riches? He had them plainly. What riches? Of which Scripture says in another place: *To the faithful man the whole world is of riches*[^8]. Hear him too confessing: *As having nothing, and possessing all things*[^9]. He who wishes, then, to be rich, let him not cling to a part, and he will possess the whole: let him cling to him who created the whole. *Together in one rich and poor.* He says in another psalm: *The poor shall eat, and shall be satisfied.* How did he commend the poor? *The poor shall eat, and shall be satisfied.* What do they eat? What the faithful know. How shall they be satisfied? By imitating the passion of their Lord, and not without cause receiving their price. *The poor shall eat, and shall be satisfied, and they shall praise the Lord who seek him.* What of the rich? They too eat. But how do they eat? *All the rich of the earth have eaten and adored*[^10]. He did not say: They have eaten, and have been satisfied; but: *They have eaten, and have adored.* They adore God indeed, but they are unwilling to show brotherly humanity. Those eat, and adore; these eat, and are satisfied: yet all eat. It is required of the one eating what he eats; let him not be forbidden to eat by the dispenser, but warned to fear the exactor. Let the sinners and the just hear these things, then, the nations and those who inhabit the world, *the earth-born and the sons of men, together in one rich and poor*: not divided, not separated. The time of harvest will do this, the hand of the winnower will be able to do this. Now together in one let the rich and poor hear, together in one let the goats and lambs be fed, until he comes who separates some to the right, others to the left. Together in one let them hear the teacher, lest, separated from one another, they hear the judge.

**4.** [v. 4] And what is it that they are now to hear? *My mouth shall speak wisdom, and the meditation of my heart, understanding*[^11]. And these too are repeated rather, lest perhaps, when he said, *my mouth,* you should understand him to speak to you, who had wisdom in his lips. For many have it in the lips and do not have it in the heart, of whom Scripture says: *This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me*[^12]. What, then, does he say who speaks to you? who, when he said: *My mouth shall speak wisdom;* that you may understand that what is poured forth from the mouth flows from the vein of the heart, added: *And the meditation of my heart, understanding.*

**5.** [v. 5] *I will incline my ear to a parable, I will open my proposition on the psaltery*[^13]. Who is this whose meditation of heart speaks understanding, so that it is not only on the surface of the lips, but possesses the inner parts of the man? Who is this who hears, and so speaks? For many speak what they do not hear. Who are they who speak what they do not hear? Those who do not do what they say: such as the Lord says the Pharisees to sit upon the seat of Moses. He wished to speak to you in the seat of Moses, through those who speak these things, and do not do them; and the Lord wished to give you security. Do not fear: *What they say,* he says, *do, but what they do, do not do; for they say, and do not do*[^14]. They do not hear what they say. But those who do, and so say, hear what they say: and therefore they say fruitfully, because they hear. He, then, who is a sayer and not a hearer, profits another, does not profit himself. This one, then, who wished to be both a hearer and a sayer, who speaks to you, before he said: *I will open my proposition on the psaltery,* which is now to speak through the body, for so the soul uses the body, as the lyre-player uses the psaltery, said: *I will incline my ear to a parable.* Before I speak to you, he says, through the body, before the psaltery sounds, first I *will incline my ear to a parable,* that is, I will hear what I say to you. And why, *to a parable*? Because *we see now through a mirror in an enigma*[^15], as the Apostle says. *As long as we are in this body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord*[^16]. Because that vision of ours is not yet face to face, where there are no longer parables, where there are no longer enigmas and likenesses. Whatever we now understand, we behold through enigmas. An enigma is an obscure parable which is understood with difficulty. However much a man cultivates his heart, and takes refuge in understanding the inner things, as long as we see through the corruptibility of this flesh, we see in part. But when incorruption is taken on in the resurrection of the dead, when the Son of man shall appear about to judge the living and the dead, then shall be seen the Son of man, who was first judged, now judging, discerning the bad from the good, setting the bad at the left, the good at the right. Both the good and the bad shall see him, but to the bad he will say: *Go into the eternal fire*: but to the good he will say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom. The bad shall depart into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^17]; and there will be that vision face to face, of which they are not worthy. Attend to what I say. For the Son of man, as here, when he was still to be judged, both the bad saw, and the good; for the apostles who followed saw him, the Jews who crucified saw him; so when he shall come to judge, both the good shall see him and the bad: the good, to receive the reward, because they followed; the bad, to receive the punishment, because they crucified. Will those only, then, be damned who crucified? I dare to say, only those. Therefore we, say the sinners of this time, are secure. If God does not question the mind, you are secure. What is it that I said? Let your Charity understand, lest they say this in the judgment of God, that they did not understand. The Jews, because they saw Christ, crucified him; you, because you do not see Christ, resist his word. You who resist the word, would you not crucify the flesh if you saw it? The Jew despised him hanging on the wood, you despise him sitting in heaven. Therefore both kinds saw him, while he was here: both kinds will see him too, when he comes. For the Son of man will come to judge; because the Son of man came to be judged. Therefore, because the Father was not incarnate, the Father did not suffer, and through the Son of man he judges, as he himself said in the Gospel: *The Father judges no one, but all judgment he has given to the Son*: and following a little after he says: *And he has given him power to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*[^18]. For according to what he is the Son of God, the Word always with the Father; and because always with the Father, with the Father he always judges: but according to what he is the Son of man, he was both judged, and will judge. But as he was seen by those who believed, and by those who crucified, when he was judged; so he will be seen, when he begins to be the judge, both by those whom he will damn, and by those whom he will crown. But that vision of the divinity, which he promised to those who love him, when he said: *He who loves me, will be loved by my Father: and he who loves me, keeps my commandments, and I will love him, and will show myself to him*[^19], the ungodly will not now see. This showing is in a way familiar, he reserves it for his own, he does not show it to the ungodly. What is this vision? What kind is Christ? Equal to the Father. What kind is Christ? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^20]. For this vision we sigh now, and groan as long as we are pilgrims; for this vision we shall be rendered in the end, this vision we now see in an enigma. If, then, we see in an enigma, let us incline our ear to a parable, and so open our proposition on the psaltery: let us hear what we say, let us do what we enjoin.

**6.** [v. 6] And what did he say? *Why shall I fear in the evil day? The iniquity of my heel shall surround me*[^21]. He began rather obscurely: *Why shall I fear,* he says, *in the evil day? The iniquity of my heel shall surround me.* He ought rather to fear, then, if the iniquity of his heel will surround him. For let not a man fear, he says, what he does not have in his power to avoid. For example: he who fears death, what will he do that he not die? Let him tell me by what way he may escape what he owes as a son of Adam, who is born of Adam. But let him consider that he was born of Adam, and has followed Christ, and that he must pay what Adam owes, and obtain what Christ promised. He who fears death, then, has no way to escape: but he who fears the damnation which the ungodly will hear: *Go into the eternal fire,* has a way to escape. Let him not, then, fear? Why should he fear? Will the iniquity of his heel surround him? If, then, he avoids the iniquity of his heel, and walks through the ways of God, he will not come to the evil day: the evil day, the last day, will not be evil to him. For the last day will be evil to some, will be good to some. Will it be evil to those to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*? But it will be evil to those to whom it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire*[^22]. But if the iniquity of his heel will surround him, why, then, does he fear in the evil day? Let them now, while they live, provide for themselves, let them take iniquity from their heel: let them walk that way, let them walk through the way of which he himself said: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^23]: and let them not fear in the evil day, because he gives them security, who was made the way. *Why shall I fear in the evil day? The iniquity of my heel shall surround me.* Therefore let them avoid the iniquity of their heel. In the heel each one slips. Let your Charity attend. What was said by God to the serpent? *She shall watch your head, and you shall watch her heel*[^24]. The devil watches your heel, when you slip, that he may cast you down. He watches your heel, do you watch his head. What is his head? The beginning of an evil suggestion. When he begins to suggest evil, then repel it before delight arises, and consent follows; and then you will avoid his head, and he will not lay hold of your heel. But why did he say this to Eve? Because through the flesh a man slips. Eve is our inner flesh. *He who loves,* he says, *his wife, loves himself.* What is, *himself*? It follows, and says: *For no one ever hated his own flesh*[^25]. Because, then, just as the devil supplanted that man Adam through Eve, so the devil wishes to supplant us through the flesh; it was commanded to Eve to watch the head of the devil, because the devil watches her heel. If, then, the iniquity of the heel will surround us, why do we fear in the evil day, since, converted to Christ, we have it in our power not to do iniquity; and there will be nothing to surround us, and we shall rejoice in the last day, not lament?

**7.** [v. 7] But who are they whom the iniquity of the heel will surround? *Those who trust in their own power, and glory in the abundance of their riches*[^26]. Therefore I will avoid these things, and the iniquity of my heel will not surround me. What is it to avoid these things? Let us not trust in our own power, let us not glory in the abundance of our riches; but let us glory in him who promised us, the humble, the heights, and threatened the lifted-up damnation; and the iniquity of the heel will not surround us. *Those who trust in their own power, and glory in the abundance of their riches.*

**8.** [v. 8] There are those who presume on their friends: some presume on their power, others on riches. This is the presumption of the human race, not presuming on God. He spoke of power, he spoke of riches, he speaks of friends: *A brother does not redeem; shall a man redeem?*[^27] Do you await that a man redeem you from the wrath to come? If a brother does not redeem you, shall a man redeem you? Who is the brother, who, if he does not redeem, no man will redeem? He who after the resurrection said: *Go, tell my brothers*[^28]. He wished to be our brother: and when we say to God, Our Father, this is manifested in us. For he who says to God, Our Father; to Christ says, Brother. Therefore he who has God as Father and Christ as brother, let him not fear in the evil day. For the iniquity of his heel will not surround him; because he does not presume on his own power, nor glory in the abundance of his riches, nor boast himself of his powerful friends. In him, then, let him presume who died for him, that he might not die forever; who was humbled for him, that he might be exalted; who sought the ungodly, that he might be sought by the faithful. Therefore if he himself does not redeem, shall a man redeem? Will some man redeem, if the Son of man does not redeem? If Christ does not redeem, will Adam redeem? *A brother does not redeem; shall a man redeem?*

**9.** [v. 9] *He shall not give to God his propitiation, and the price of the redemption of his soul*[^29]. He trusts in his own power, and glories in the abundance of his riches, who *shall not give to God his propitiation;* that is, the placation by which he may bend God for his sins: *nor the price of the redemption of his soul,* who presumes on his own power, and on his friends, and on his riches. But who are they who give the price of the redemption of their soul? Those to whom the Lord says: *Make for yourselves friends of the mammon of iniquity, that they too may receive you into the eternal dwellings*[^30]. Those give the price of the redemption of their soul, who do not cease to give alms. To this degree those whom the Apostle warns through Timothy, he was unwilling that they be proud, lest they glory in the abundance of their riches: finally, what they possessed, he was unwilling should grow old with them, but that something be made of it, that there might be a price of the redemption of their soul. For he says: *Charge the rich of this world not to be high-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy.* And as though they said: What, then, are we to do with our riches? *Let them be rich,* he says, *in good works, let them give easily, let them share*: and they will not lose it. Whence do we know? Hear what follows: *Let them treasure up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold on true life*[^31]. So they will give the price of the redemption of their soul. And our Lord too warns this: *Make for yourselves purses that do not grow old, a treasure that does not fail in the heavens, where no thief draws near, nor moth corrupts*[^32]. God was unwilling that you lose your riches, but he gave you counsel to change their place. Let your Charity understand: now if your friend entered into your house, and found that you had put your grain in a damp place, who perhaps knew the nature of the corruption of grain which you did not know, he would give you such counsel, saying: Brother, you lose what you gathered with great labor; you have put it in a damp place, in a few days these things rot. And what shall I do, brother? Lift it into the upper parts. You would hear a friend suggesting that you lift the grain from the lower to the upper parts; and do you not hear Christ warning that you lift your treasure from earth to heaven, where this is not repaid you which you keep, but you keep earth, receive heaven, keep mortal things, receive everlasting? You lend to Christ; let him receive on earth a little, that he may repay you in heaven much. But truly those whom the iniquity of their heel will surround, because they trust in their own power, and glory in the abundance of their riches, and presume on human friends able to furnish nothing, *shall not give to God their propitiation, and the price of the redemption of their soul.*

**10.** [v. 10] And what did he say of such a man? *And he has labored forever, and shall live unto the end*[^33]. His labor will be without end, his life will have an end. Why did he say this, *shall live unto the end*? For these men set life as nothing except daily delights. To such a degree many of our needy and poor, little firm, and not beholding what God promises them for these labors, when they see the rich at daily feasts, in the splendor and gleam of gold and silver, what do they say? These alone are they, these truly live. It is said; let it now not be said, and we admonish, and it has to be said, or let it be said by fewer, than would be said if we did not admonish. For neither do we presume so to say these things that it not be said, but that it be said by fewer; for it will be said even to the end of the world. It is too little that he says he lives; he adds, and says, he thunders: You think he alone lives. Let him live; his life will be finished: since he does not give the price of the redemption of his soul, his life will be finished, his labor will not be finished. *He has labored forever, and shall live unto the end.* How *shall he live unto the end*? As that man lived who was clothed in purple and fine linen, and feasted splendidly every day; and the proud and swollen one despised the one lying full of sores before his gate, whose sores the dogs licked, and who desired the crumbs which fell from his table. What did those riches profit him? Both changed their lots: the one was taken from the gate of the rich man into the bosom of Abraham; the other was sent from the splendid feasts into the fire: the one rested, the other burned; the one was satisfied, the other thirsted: the one had labored unto the end, was living forever; the other had lived unto the end, was laboring forever. And what did it profit the rich man, who sought, set in torments among the dead, that a drop of water be dripped onto his tongue from the finger of Lazarus, saying: *Because I am tormented in this flame*, and it was not granted him? So he desired the drop from the finger, as the other the crumbs from the rich man's table: but the labor of the one was finished, and the life of the other was finished; the labor of this one forever, the life of the other forever. We do not have life here, who perhaps here labor on earth: and we shall not be so afterward; for our life will be Christ forever: but those who wish to have life here, will labor forever, and will live unto the end.

**11.** [v. 11] *Because he shall not see destruction, when he shall have seen the wise dying*[^34]. This one who has labored forever, and shall live unto the end, *shall not see destruction, when he shall have seen the wise dying*: what is this? He will not understand what destruction is, when he shall have seen the wise dying. For he says to himself: This man, because he was wise, and dwelt with wisdom, and worshiped God with godliness, did he not die? Let me, then, do well for myself, while I live: for if those who are wise otherwise could do anything, they would not die. He sees him die, and does not see what death is. *He shall not see destruction, when he shall have seen the wise dying.* As the Jews saw Christ hanging on the cross, and despised him, saying: This man, if he were the Son of God, would come down from the cross: not seeing what destruction is. If they saw what destruction is, if they saw! He was dying temporally, that he might live again forever; they were living temporally, that they might die forever. But because they saw him dying, they did not see destruction, that is, they did not understand what the true destruction was. What do they say too in Wisdom? *Let us condemn him with a most shameful death, for there will be regard had from his words; for if he is truly the son of God, he will free him from the hands of his adversaries*[^35]: he will not permit his son to die, if he is truly his son. But when they saw him on the cross insulting them, and not coming down from the cross, they said: Truly he was a man. It was said: and surely he who could rise from the tomb could come down from the cross; but he taught us to bear those who insult, taught us to be patient against the tongues of men, to drink now the cup of bitterness, and afterward to receive everlasting salvation. Drink, sick man, the bitter cup, that you may be well, you whose inner parts are not sound: do not tremble, for, that you might not tremble, the physician drank first; that is, the Lord first drank the bitterness of the passion. He drank who had no sin, who had nothing to be healed in him. Drink until the bitterness of this world pass, and there come the world where there is no scandal, no anger, no rot, no bitterness, no fever, no deceit, no enmities, no old age, no death, no contention. Labor here, about to come to the end; labor, lest, when you are unwilling to labor here, you come to the end of life, and never come to the end of labors. *Because he shall not see destruction, when he shall have seen the wise dying.*

**12.** *Together the imprudent and the foolish shall perish*[^36]. Who is *imprudent*? He who does not provide for himself for the future. Who is *foolish*? He who does not understand in what evil he is. But do you understand in what evil you now are, and provide that you be in good things afterward. By understanding in what evil you are, you will not be foolish: by providing for yourself for the future, you will not be imprudent. Who is it that provides for himself? That servant to whom his lord gave what he should dispense, and afterward said to him: *You cannot deal with me, render an account of your stewardship.* And he: *What shall I do? to dig I am not able, to beg I am ashamed.* But even from his lord's property he made for himself friends, who might receive him, when he was cast out of the stewardship. And he committed fraud against his lord, that he might procure for himself friends who would receive him: do not you fear lest you commit fraud; the Lord himself exhorts you to do it, he himself says to you: *Make for yourself friends of the mammon of iniquity*[^37]. Perhaps the things you have acquired, you acquired from iniquity; or perhaps this itself is iniquity, that you have and another does not have, you abound and another is in need. Of this mammon of iniquity, of these riches which the iniquitous call riches, make for yourself friends and you will be prudent: you procure for yourself, you are not defrauded. For now you seem to lose. Will you lose by putting it in the strongbox? For boys, brothers, when they find coins to buy themselves I know not what, put them in a strongbox, and do not open it except afterward: do they, because they do not see what they gather, therefore lose it? Do not fear: boys put it in a strongbox, and are secure; you put it in the hand of Christ, and fear! Be prudent, and provide for yourself for the future in heaven. Be prudent, then, imitate the ant, as Scripture says; store up in summer, lest you go hungry in winter: winter is the last day, the day of tribulation; winter is the day of scandals and bitterness: gather what may be there for you for the future; but if you do not, together imprudent and foolish you will perish.

**13.** But that rich man died, and such a funeral was made for him. Behold to what men have turned: they do not attend how bad a life he had while he lived, but what pomp when he died. O happy he whom so many bewail! But this man so lived that few bewail. For all ought to bewail one living so badly. But there is pomp of the funeral, he is received in a precious tomb, wrapped in precious garments, buried with ointments and spices. Then what a memorial he has! how marbled! Is there living in that memorial? He there is dead. Men, thinking these things good, have wandered from God, nor sought the true things, and were deceived by the false: see what follows. He who did not give the price of the redemption of his soul, who did not understand destruction, because he saw the wise dying, was made imprudent and foolish, so that he should perish together. And how shall they perish who *shall leave their riches to strangers*? *Together the imprudent and the foolish shall perish.*

**14.** Attend, brothers: *And they shall leave their riches to strangers*[^38]. As though he set them in a curse, who, when they have died, strangers will possess their things. Therefore happy are those who leave sons in possession of their goods, to whom their own succeed. He had sons, he did not die. What of his sons? They too keep what their parents left them: it is too little that they keep it; they even increase it. For whom do they too keep it? For their sons, and they for their sons, and the third for their sons. What for Christ? what for their soul? All for the sons. Among the sons whom they have on earth, let them reckon one brother whom they have in heaven: to whom they ought to give the whole, or let them divide with him. But yet someone says to me: Behold, those whom Scripture said were cursed, whom it said would perish and leave their riches to strangers; but he is blessed who leaves them to his own. I examine this sense, because I incline my ear to a parable; and I see that not in vain does Scripture so speak. For I see many iniquitous men die, to whom their sons are successors; nor could Scripture so speak, as to separate from misery those whose life it reproves: and what, do you think, do I understand, brothers, except that all such leave their riches to strangers? How are sons strangers? The sons of the iniquitous are strangers; for we find a certain stranger made a neighbor, because he profited him. If any of your own profits you nothing, he is a stranger. Where do we find a certain stranger made a neighbor, because he profited? In the Gospel. A certain man wounded by robbers lay there, and the Lord had said to a certain man: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself. And he had answered: And who is my neighbor? And the Lord narrated: A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers, who wounded him, and left him half-alive on the way*: the neighbors passed by; for he was a Jew, he was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho: *a priest passed, and went by; a Levite passed, and he too went by; a certain Samaritan passed,* some Samaritan, he was a stranger; *he himself drew near to him, inspected his misery, and out of mercy cured his wounds, lifted him onto the beast, and led him to the inn, commended him to the innkeeper.* These things were said in a mystery, and now seem rather lengthy to examine: yet on account of what I proposed, brothers, the Lord says: *Which of those was a neighbor to that wounded man? He answered: I believe, he who showed mercy to him. Go,* he says, *and you too do likewise*[^39]. To whom you show mercy, he is your neighbor. If, then, the stranger Samaritan, by showing mercy and coming to aid, was made a neighbor; whoever cannot come to your aid in tribulation, has been made a stranger to you. Now let us attend to those rich men who lived badly, who acted proudly, who died, and left, I do not say to strangers, but to their sons they left riches, and their sons follow the way of their parents: as those proud, so these too; as those rapacious, so these too; as those greedy, so these too: they are strangers to them. For that you may know they are strangers, the successors of his riches would come to the aid of that rich man who was burning in the flame. But perhaps he did not have those who would succeed him, and strangers possessed his riches? We find in that same Gospel that he had: for he says: *I have five brothers*[^40]. His brothers could not come to the aid of him burning in the flame. What would the rich man say to you? I have five brothers: I made not one brother a friend for myself, who lay before the gate: those brothers cannot come to my aid, who possess my riches; they have been made strangers to me. You see that all who live badly leave their riches to strangers.

**15.** [v. 12] But surely those very strangers, who are called their own, furnish them something? Hear what they furnish them, attend how they are mocked: *Together the imprudent and the foolish shall perish; and they shall leave their riches to strangers.* Why did he say *to strangers*? Because they can profit them in nothing. And yet in what they seem to profit themselves: *And their tombs are their houses forever*[^41]. Now because these tombs are constructed, the tombs are houses. For very often you hear a rich man saying: I have a marbled house which I am to leave, and I do not think for myself of an eternal house where I shall always be. When he thinks to make for himself a marbled or carved memorial, he as it were thinks of an eternal house; as though that rich man remained there. If he remained there, he would not burn among the dead. Where the spirit of one acting badly remains, not where the mortal body is set, is to be thought of: but *their houses are their tombs forever. Their tabernacles from generation to generation. The tabernacles,* in which they remained temporally, the *houses,* in which as it were forever they shall remain, that is, the tombs. The tabernacles, then, they leave to their own, where they remained while they lived; they pass over as it were to eternal houses, to the tombs. What do *their tabernacles from generation to generation* profit them? Now generation and generation, suppose, are sons, there will be grandsons and great-grandsons: what do they do, what do their tabernacles profit? What? Hear: *They shall call upon their names in their lands.* What is this? They will carry bread and wine to the tombs, and there call upon the names of the dead. Think how much the name of that rich man was called upon afterward, when men got drunk in his memory, nor did one drop come down upon his burning tongue? Men serve their own belly, not the spirits of their own. To the spirits of the dead comes only what they themselves did while living: but if while living they did nothing for themselves, nothing comes to the dead. But what do those do? Only *they shall call upon their names in their lands.*

**16.** [v. 13] *And man, when he was in honor, did not understand: he has been compared to the senseless beasts, and has been made like them*[^42]. How was it insulted to the men who did not understand what they should do with their riches while they lived, and thought they would be blessed if they had a marbled memorial, as it were an eternal house, and if their own, to whom they had left their substance, called upon their names in their lands. But they ought rather to have prepared for themselves an eternal house in good works, to have prepared for themselves an immortal life, to have sent before themselves their expenses, to have followed their works, to have attended to the needy companion, to have given to him with whom they were walking, not to have despised Christ full of sores before the gate, who said: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^43]. Because, then, man set in honor did not understand. What is, set in honor? Made to the image and likeness of God, man set before the beasts. For God did not make man as he made the beast; but God made man whom the beasts should serve: by his strength, and not by his understanding? But he *did not understand*: and he who was made to the image of God, *has been compared to the senseless beasts, and has been made like them.* Whence elsewhere it is said: *Do not be like the horse and mule, in which there is no understanding*[^44].

**17.** [v. 14] *This way of theirs is a scandal to them*[^45]. Let it be a scandal to them, not to you. But when will it be a scandal to you too? If you think that such men are blessed. If you understand that they are not blessed, their way will be a scandal to them; not to Christ, not to his body, not to his members. *And afterward they will bless in their own mouth.* What is, *afterward they will bless in their own mouth*? When they have been made such that they seek nothing but temporal goods, they become hypocrites; and when they bless God, they bless with the lips, not with the heart. Made such Christians, when eternal life is praised to them, and it is said to them that they ought to be despisers of riches in the name of Christ, they twist the mouth in their heart: and if they do not dare to in the face, lest they blush, or lest they be rebuked by men, they do it in the heart, they despise; and there remains to them in the mouth a blessing, and in the heart a curse. *And afterward they will bless in their own mouth.* It is long for us to finish the psalm: let it suffice your Charity what you have heard meanwhile today; tomorrow, what shall please the Lord you will hear.
