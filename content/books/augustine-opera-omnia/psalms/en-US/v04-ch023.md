# Exposition of Psalm 93

**1.** As we heard most attentively, when this psalm was read; so let us attentively hear, when the Lord reveals the mysteries which here he deigned to overshadow. For to this end certain sacraments of the Scriptures are closed, not that they may be denied, but that they may be opened to those who knock[^1]. If, then, with pious affection and the sincere love of the heart you knock, he will open who sees from where you knock. It is known to us all (and would that we be not of their number), that many murmur against the patience of God, and grieve that iniquitous and impious men either live on this earth, or even can do much; and—what is more—often the evil can do much against the good, and often the evil press the good; the evil exult, the good labor; the evil are proud, the good are humbled. Attending to such things in the human race (for they abound), the impatient and weak of soul are perverted, as though the good were in vain; because God turns away or seems to turn away his eyes from the good works of the pious and faithful, and to increase the evil in the things they love. The weak, then, supposing that they live well in vain, are either invited to imitate the malice of those whom they behold as it were flourishing: or, if through the weakness either of their person or of their soul, they fear to do evil, lest something evil befall them according to the laws of the world; not because they love righteousness, but, to speak more openly, fearing to be condemned among men by men, they abstain indeed from evil deeds, but they do not abstain from evil thoughts. And among their iniquitous thoughts, that impiety especially holds the head of iniquity, by which God seems to them to neglect and not care for human affairs; and either to hold the good and the evil equally; or even—what is more pernicious to think—to harass the good and favor the evil. He who thinks such things, even if he does no evil to anyone, does very much to himself, and is impious against himself; and by his iniquity he does not harm God, but kills himself. Nor do they harm men, because those who think such things are timid; but yet their murders, their adulteries, their frauds and robberies God sees, and punishes in their thoughts. For what they wish, he attends to, whose eye is not repelled by the flesh, so as not to see the will. Such men, if they find occasions, do not become evil, but are manifested: not that you may feel that what was born has been made manifest, but that you may understand what was lying hidden enclosed. In these few years, and nearly yesterday, men have seen these things, and even those who understand slowly have proved them. For there was here a certain house most powerful for a time, of which God had made a scourge for the human race, and the human race was chastised by it; if it recognize the scourge of the Father, and fear the sentence of the judge. When, then, that same house was great here, many groaned under it, murmured, blamed, detested, blasphemed. How men straiten themselves, and many are given over by that divine judgment to the desires of their heart[^2]? Suddenly those who were murmuring about that house became of that very house; and men suffered from them such things, as they themselves a little before were complaining that they suffered from such men. He, then, is good, who even when he can do evil, does not; of whom it is written: *He who could transgress, and has not transgressed; and do evils, and has not done them. Who is this, and we will praise him? For he did marvelous things in his life*[^3]. Scripture was speaking of the powerful innocent. For the wolf too wishes to harm as much as the lion: they harm differently, but they do not desire differently. For the lion not only despises the barking dog; but even puts it to flight, and comes to the sheepfold, and, with the dogs falling silent, seizes what it can: the wolf does not dare amid the barking of the dogs. Does it therefore return more innocent, because, terrified, it could not take from the dogs? Therefore God teaches innocence, that each one be innocent not from fear of punishment, but from love of righteousness. For then the innocent man is free and is truly innocent. But he who is made innocent from fear, is not innocent, although he does not harm whom he wishes to harm. For he does not harm another by an evil deed, but himself very much by an evil desire. For how he harms himself, hear the Scripture: *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^4]. And truly men err much who suppose that their injustice harms others, and does not harm themselves. The iniquity of anyone proceeds to others, that it may harm the body, that it may injure the family property, that it may invade the farm, that it may carry off the slave, that it may take away the gold, or silver, or whatever else he possesses. To this end is that iniquity brought forth to another. Therefore does your iniquity harm another's body, and not harm your own soul?

**2.** Against this simple and truthful teaching, by which it is intimated to good men that they should love righteousness itself, and from it wish to please God; that they should understand their soul to be flooded by him with a certain intelligible light, that they may do just works, and prefer that light of wisdom to all the things that are loved in the world: against this teaching such are the murmurs of men; and if they do not proceed into the voice, they are gnawed in the heart. What, then, do they say? Shall I truly please God by righteousness? or do those righteous ones please him, under whose rule the evil flourish? They commit such great evils, and no evil befalls them. Or if perhaps some evil befalls them; what do they say to you, when you have begun to say to them: Behold how great evils he did, how it was repaid to him? what sort of end did I have? They begin to think of the righteous to whom evils befell, and set them against us, and say: If something evil befell that man, because he was iniquitous; why did it befall that other, who lived so justly? who gave so great alms, who worked so many good things in the Church, why did he find such a lot? why did he have such an end, as that man who committed many iniquities? But they say these things to this end, that they may show that they do not do evil for this reason, because they cannot, or because they do not dare. For what the heart wishes, the tongue testifies: and indeed, even if the tongue should fall silent and itself be pressed down by fear, God would see within what a man thought, even if it lay hidden from another man. The silent thoughts of men, then, of this kind, or even those bursting forth into words or deeds, this psalm cures, if they wish to be cured. Let them attend, then, and be cured. And would that in this whole multitude which is now between these walls, and hears through us the word of the Lord, there be no such wounds to be cured; would that there be none. Yet we do not do a superfluous thing in speaking, if there are no wounds there. Let hearts be instructed to heal others, when they have begun to hear such things. For I believe that each Christian, when he hears someone saying such things, if he is a good faithful man, and believes God well, and his hope is in the world to come, is not in this earth, not in this life, and not in vain hears that he should have his heart on high; he mocks and grieves at those murmuring such things, and says to himself: God knows what he does, we cannot know his counsel, why he spares the evil for a time, or why the good labor for a time; yet it suffices me to know this, that both the good man labors for a time, and the evil man flourishes for a time. He, then, who is such, is secure; and patiently bears all the felicities of the evil, and patiently bears the labors of the good, endures, until this world is ended, until iniquity passes. Such a one is now blessed, and God has instructed him out of his law, and has made him mild from the malignant days, until a pit be dug for the sinner. But he who is not yet such, let him hear through us what pleases the Lord. But let him himself say more in his heart, who sees better the wound that he is to cure.

**3.** The psalm has this title, that is, this inscription: *A Psalm of David himself, the fourth of the sabbath.* This psalm is to teach patience in the labors of the righteous: against the felicities of the iniquitous it teaches patience, it builds up patience. This it has from beginning to end. Why, then, does it have such a title, *on the fourth of the sabbath?* The first of the sabbath is the Lord's day; the second of the sabbath, the second weekday, which the worldly call the day of the Moon; the third of the sabbath, the third weekday, which they call the day of Mars. The fourth, then, of the sabbaths, the fourth weekday, which is called the day of Mercury by the pagans, and by many Christians: but we would not wish it; and would that they correct it, and not say so. For they have their own tongue which they may use. For not in all nations are these things said: many nations, one and another, name them in one way and another. Better, then, from a Christian mouth proceeds the ecclesiastical manner of speaking. Yet if perhaps custom has drawn anyone, so that there comes out of the mouth what he disapproves in the heart, let him understand that all those from whose names the heavenly bodies were called were men, and that these heavenly bodies did not begin to be in heaven from the time those men began: they were there before; but through certain mortal benefits of mortals, those men, for their own time, because they could do much and were eminent in this world, when they were dear to men, not on account of eternal life, but on account of temporal advantage, divine honors were conferred on them. For the ancients of the world, deceived, and wishing to deceive, in flattery of those who had bestowed something on them according to the love of the world, pointed out the stars in heaven, saying that this star was that man's, and that one another's: but men who had not before looked, that they might see that those stars were there too before they were born, deceived, believed; and the opinion of vanity was conceived. This opinion of error the devil confirmed, Christ overturned. We, then, according to what we speak, by *the fourth of the sabbaths* understand the fourth day from the Lord's day. Let Your Charity therefore attend to what this title means: here is a great mystery, and truly hidden. For most things of the Psalm itself sound manifestly, and manifestly move, and are quickly understood; but this title, it must be confessed, has not a little obscurity: but the Lord will be present, will clear the cloud, and you will see the Psalm, and from the brow of the Psalm you will recognize it. For on the brow this psalm has: *A Psalm of David himself, the fourth of the sabbath.* On the threshold is the title, on the doorposts it is fixed. Men wish to recognize the title, and so to enter the house. Let us, then, recall the holy Scripture in Genesis, what was done on the first day; we find light: on the second day what was done; we find the firmament, which God called heaven: on the third day what was done; we find the appearance of land and sea, and the separation, that every gathering of waters might be called sea, and the dry land be called earth. On the fourth day, God made luminaries in heaven[^5]; the sun for the power of the day, the moon and stars for the power of the night[^6]: this he made on the fourth day. What, then, does it mean that the Psalm received its title from the fourth day; in which psalm patience is taught against the felicities of the evil, and the labors of the good? You have the apostle Paul saying to the holy faithful strengthened in Christ: *Do all things without murmuring and disputing; that you may be blameless and sincere, unspotted sons of God in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom you shine like luminaries in the world, holding the word of life*[^7]. The likeness of luminaries was given to the saints, that they be without murmuring in a crooked and perverse nation.

**4.** But lest anyone therefore suppose that the luminaries of heaven are to be worshiped and adored, because from them some likeness was drawn to the signification of the saints; let us first explain this in the name of Christ, that it does not follow that the sun, or moon, or stars, or heaven seem to you to be adored, because some likeness was drawn from them, by which the saints might be signified; because there are many things from which a likeness is drawn to signify the saints, which are not adored. For if whatever there is whence a likeness is drawn to the saints, you think is to be adored; adore the mountains and hills, because it was said: *The mountains exulted like rams, and the hills like lambs of the flock*[^8]. You speak of the saints, I speak of Christ himself. Adore the lion, because it was said: *The lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered*[^9]: adore the rock, because it was said: *But the rock was Christ*[^10]. But if you do not adore in Christ these earthly things, although some likeness was given from them; from whatever creature a likeness has been drawn to signify the saints, do you understand the likeness of the creature, and adore the maker of the creature. Our Lord Jesus Christ was called the sun[^11]: is it this sun which even the tiniest animals see with us? No, but the one of whom it was said: *It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^12]. For this light enlightens not man only, but also the beasts and cattle and all animals: but that which enlightens every man, enlightens him in the heart, where alone he has understanding.

**5.** Let Your Charity therefore understand to whom the Apostle said: *In a crooked and perverse nation,* that is, among the iniquitous, *among whom you shine like luminaries in the world, holding the word of life*[^13]: he in a manner admonished us both to understand this psalm, and to foreknow its title. For such saints, in whom is the word of life, from the conversation which they have in heaven, despise all the iniquitous things that are done on earth: and as the luminaries in heaven proceed through day and through night, complete their journeys, have their fixed courses, and so great evils are committed, nor do the stars fixed in heaven deviate from above, going through the heavenly tracts which their Creator appointed and constituted for them; so ought the saints, but if their hearts are fixed in heaven, if not in vain they hear and answer that they have their heart on high, if they imitate him who says: *But our conversation is in the heavens*[^14]. Since, then, they are in the things above, and think of the things above, as it was said: *Where your treasure is, there is your heart too*[^15]; from those very thoughts of the things above they are made patient; and whatever is committed on earth, they so do not care, until they complete their journeys, as the luminaries of heaven do not care, except how they perform the days and nights, although they see so great evils done upon the earth. But it is perhaps easy for the righteous to bear the iniquities of the evil, which are not done against themselves: but as they bear those done against others, so let them bear also those done against themselves. For they ought not to bear and endure because they are done against others: and if they are done against themselves, they ought not to lose endurance. For he who has lost endurance has fallen from heaven: but he who has his heart fixed in heaven, his earth labors on earth. How great things men even invent about the luminaries themselves, and they bear it patiently? In the same way the righteous ought patiently to bear all the false accusations even against themselves. This very thing which I said a while ago, that that star is Mercury's, and that star is Saturn's, and that star is Jupiter's, are reproaches made to the stars. What? when they hear so great reproaches, are they moved, or do they not perform their courses? So too the man who in a perverse and crooked nation has the word of God, is as a luminary shining in heaven. How many who seem to themselves to honor the sun, lie about it? Those who say: Christ is the sun, lie about the sun. The sun knows that its Lord is Christ and its Creator. And if it could be indignant, it is more bitterly indignant against the one falsely honoring it, than against the one reproaching it. For to a good servant an injury to the Lord is a greater reproach. How many false things do certain men say about the luminaries themselves? And they bear it, endure it, and are not moved. Why? Because they are in heaven. But what is heaven? Let us not pass over this either: how many lies men tell, when they see the moon darkened, and say: The sorcerers bring it down? whereas at fixed times it has its eclipse according to the disposition of God. Yet she who is in heaven does not care for these words of men. But what is, In heaven? She is in the firmament of heaven. Whose heart, then, is in the firmament of the book of God, does not care for these things.

**6.** For heaven, that is, the firmament, is understood by a figure as the Book of the Law. Therefore in a certain place it is said: *He stretches out heaven like a skin*[^16]. If it is stretched out like a skin, it is as it were a book stretched out, that it may be read. But the time being past, it is not read. For the Law is read for this reason, because we have not yet come to that Wisdom which fills the hearts and minds of those beholding it: and there will be no need that anything there be read to us. For in that which is read to us, syllables sound and pass: that light of truth does not pass, but, fixed, remaining, inebriates the hearts of those who see; as it was said: *They shall be inebriated from the abundance of your house, and you shall give them to drink of the torrent of your delights; for with you, O Lord, is the fountain of life.* And see the fountain itself: *In your light,* he says, *we shall see light*[^17]. Now, then, reading is necessary, as long as *in part we know, and in part we prophesy,* as the Apostle says: *but when that which is perfect comes, that which is in part will be done away*[^18]. For not in that city Jerusalem, where the angels live, from which we are now pilgrims, and our pilgrimage groans; but it groans, if we know that we are pilgrims; for he hates the homeland exceedingly, who thinks it is well with him when he is a pilgrim: is the Gospel perhaps read in that city where the angels are, or the Apostle? They are fed by the Word of God: which Word of God, that it might sound to us for a time, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us[^19]. But yet that very Law, which is written, is a firmament for us: if our heart be there, it is not torn away by the iniquities of men. It was said, then: *He stretches out heaven like a skin.* But when the times of the necessity of books pass, what was said? *Heaven will be folded up like a book*[^20]. He, then, who has his heart on high, his very heart is a luminary: it shines in heaven, nor is it overcome by darkness. For below are darknesses: but darkness is iniquity; not unchangeable darkness. We recalled this yesterday too. But those who are darkness today, if they wish, will be light tomorrow: those who entered here darkness, if they wish, can now be light. For the Apostle openly, lest anyone should suppose the iniquities to be natural, which cannot be changed: *For you were,* he says, *once darkness; but now light in the Lord: walk as children of light*[^21]. *Light,* he says, *in the Lord*: not in yourselves. The heart, then, in the book: if the heart is in the book, the heart is in the firmament of heaven. If the heart is there, let it shine thence, and it will not be moved by the iniquities set beneath: not because it is there in heaven by the flesh, but because it is there by its conversation, according to what was said: *But our conversation is in the heavens.* You cannot conceive of that city, because you do not yet see it. Do you wish to conceive of heaven? Conceive of the Book of God. Hear the psalm: *And in his law he shall meditate day and night.* And there he is called *blessed who has not gone in the counsel of the impious, and in the way of sinners has not stood, and in the seat of pestilence has not sat; but in the law of the Lord was his will.* See the luminary in heaven: *And in his law he shall meditate day and night*[^22]. Does he wish to bear all things patiently? Let him not descend from heaven, and let him meditate in his law day and night. Therefore his heart is in heaven: if his heart is in heaven, all the iniquities that are done on earth for a time, all the felicities of evil men, all the labors of the righteous, to one meditating day and night on the law of God, are nothing; and he patiently endures all things, and will be blessed, instructed by God. And how in the firmament of heaven? Because the law is a firmament. *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and from your law teach him: that you may make him mild from the malignant days, until a pit be dug for the sinner*[^23]. Attend, then, to the luminaries, how they proceed, and set, and return, perform their courses, distinguish day and night, roll the years and the times; and so great evils are done on earth, while they have their quiet in heaven. What, then, is it that God teaches us? Now let us attend to the Psalm.

**7.** *The God of vengeances, the Lord, the God of vengeances has acted with confidence.* Do you suppose he does not take vengeance? He takes vengeance, the *God of vengeances.* What is: *The God of vengeances?* The God of retributions. In this surely you murmur, that vengeance is not taken on the evil. Do not murmur, lest you be among those on whom vengeance is taken. That man commits theft, and lives: you murmur against God, because he who stole from you does not die. If you no longer commit theft, see: for if you no longer do it, see whether you ever did it. If you are now day, recall your night: if you are now fixed in heaven, recall your earth. You find that perhaps you were once a thief; and perhaps someone else was vexed, because you too, committing theft, lived, and did not die: but as when you were doing it, you therefore lived that afterward you might not do it; do not, because you have crossed over, wish to overturn the bridge of God's mercy. Do you not know that many will cross over there, where you too crossed over? Would you now be one who murmured, if there were heard against you the one who murmured first about you? And yet even now you desire the vengeance of God upon the evil, that the thief may die, and you murmur against God, because the thief does not die. Weigh in the scale of equity the thief and the blasphemer: you now say that you are not a thief; but by murmuring against God, you are a blasphemer. He catches a man's sleep, that he may steal something; and you say that God sleeps, and does not see man. Therefore do you wish him to correct his hand, first correct your own tongue: do you wish him to correct his heart against man, you correct your heart against God; lest perhaps, when you desire the vengeance of God, if it comes, it find you first. For he will come, he will come and will judge those persevering in their wickedness, ungrateful for the prepayment of his mercy, ungrateful for his patience, storing up for themselves wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works[^24]: because the *God of vengeances, the Lord, the God of vengeances,* therefore *has acted with confidence.* For he spared no one, when he spoke here: he himself was the Lord in the weakness of the flesh, but in the power of the word. He did not accept the persons of the princes of the Jews. How great things he says against them? and, as it was said, truly in confidence: because it is written in the Psalms of him: *On account of the misery of the needy and the groaning of the poor, now I will arise, says the Lord*[^25]. Who are the poor? who are the needy? Those who have no hope except in him alone, in whom alone hope is not deceived. Attend, brothers, who are the poor and needy. Not at all are the poor who have nothing seem to be called by Scripture, when the poor are praised. For you find a poor man, who, when he suffers some injury, attends to nothing but his patron, in whose house perhaps he dwells, of whom he is a lodger, of whom he is a tenant, of whom he is a client; and therefore he asserts that he suffers unworthily, because he belongs to that man: his heart is in a man, his hope is in a man, ash in ash. But there are others who are opulent, and are supported by human honors according to the time; and yet they place their hope neither in their money, nor in their lands, nor in their household, nor in the splendor of transitory dignity; but they place their whole hope in him to whom there is no successor, who cannot die, who cannot be deceived and cannot deceive: such men, even if they seem to have many things according to the world, yet govern them well for the refreshment of the needy; they are numbered among the poor of the Lord. For they see that they live perilously in this life, they feel that they are pilgrims: they so abide in the opulence of their riches, as a traveler in an inn, about to pass on, not to possess. Therefore what does the Lord say? *On account of the misery of the needy and the groaning of the poor, now I will arise, says the Lord: I will set in salvation.* Our salvation is our Savior: in him he willed to place the hope of all the needy and poor. And what does he say? *I will act with confidence in him.* What is: *I will act with confidence?* He will not fear, he will not spare the vices and concupiscences of men. Truly a faithful physician, equipped with the medicinal iron of the word, he cut all the wounds. Therefore he who was so foretold and foreannounced, was also so found. He was speaking on the mountain, where he said: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.* There *blessed* were called *those who suffer persecution for righteousness*: in that very sermon he said: *For theirs is the kingdom of heaven.* And that he might make them luminaries, that is, patiently enduring all these iniquitous things which pass: *You shall be blessed,* he says, *when they have persecuted you, and said every evil against you: rejoice and exult, because your reward is great in heaven*[^26]. Then in the progress of the sermon, when he began to teach, although a crowd surrounded him, he said to his disciples such things as would strike the face of the Pharisees and Jews, who as it were held the primacy of expounding all the Scriptures; who seemed righteous to themselves, or thought themselves seen so, and to whose primacy the obedience of the people seemed to defer: he did not spare, and said: *When you pray, you shall not be as the hypocrites, who love to stand praying in the synagogues and corners of the streets, that they may be seen by men*[^27]; and other such things. He touched all, he feared no one. And when he had ended that sermon, the scripture of the Gospel concluded of him thus: *It came to pass,* it says, *when Jesus had finished these words, the crowds marveled at his teaching. For he was teaching them as one having authority; not as their scribes and Pharisees*[^28]. How great things, then, did he of whom it was said: *He was teaching them as one having authority,* how great things did he say: *Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites*[^29]; how great things against them openly to their face? He feared no one. Why? Because he is the God of vengeances. Therefore he did not spare in word, that there might afterward be those whom he would spare in judgment: because if they were unwilling to receive the medicine of the word, they were surely to incur and find the sentence of the judge. Why? Because he said: *The God of vengeances, the Lord, the God of vengeances has acted with confidence:* that is, he spared no one in word. He who in word did not spare, about to suffer, will he spare in sentence, about to judge? he who feared no one in humility, will he fear anyone in glory? From the fact that he already acted with confidence, conceive how he is about to act at the end of the world. Do not, then, murmur against God, who as it were spares the evil; but be good, whom perhaps for a time in the scourge he does not spare, and at the end he may spare in judgment. *The God of vengeances, the Lord, the God of vengeances has acted with confidence.*

**8.** [v. 2.] And because he acted with confidence, they did not bear his confidence: and because he had come humble, and was clothed with mortal flesh, and had come to die; not to do what sinners do, but to suffer what sinners suffer; because for this he had come, when he had acted with confidence, and they could not bear his confidence in word, what did they do? They seized him, scourged him, mocked him, buffeted him, smeared him with spittle, crowned him with thorns, raised him on the cross, finally killed him. But what follows for having acted with confidence? *Be exalted, you who judge the earth.* Do you suppose that, because they seized him humble, they will seize him exalted? Do you suppose that, because they judged him mortal, they will not be judged by the immortal? What, then, does he say? *Be exalted,* you who acted with confidence, and whose confidence of word the iniquitous did not bear, and supposed they had done something, because, seizing you, they crucified you; who ought to seize you by faith, seized you by persecution: you, then, who acted with confidence among the iniquitous, and feared no one, and because you suffered: *Be exalted;* that is, rise again, go into heaven. Let the Church too suffer patiently what the head of the Church suffered patiently. *Be exalted, you who judge the earth; render retribution to the proud.* He is about to render, brothers. For what is it that was said: *Be exalted, you who judge the earth; render retribution to the proud?* It is the prophecy of one foretelling, not the audacity of one commanding. For not because the Prophet said: *Be exalted, you who judge the earth,* did Christ obey the Prophet, that he should rise and go into heaven; but because Christ was to do this, the Prophet foretold it: not therefore did Christ do it, because the Prophet had foretold; but therefore the Prophet had foretold, because he was to do it. He sees Christ humble in the spirit, he sees him humble; fearing no one, sparing no one in word, and says: *He acted with confidence.* He sees him, how confidently he acted, he sees him seized, he sees him crucified, he sees him humbled, he sees him rising and going into heaven, and about to come thence to the judgment of those among whose hands he suffered all evils: *Be exalted,* he says, *you who judge the earth; render retribution to the proud.* To the proud he will render, not to the humble. Who are the proud? To whom it is little that they do evil, and they wish to defend their sins. For of those who crucified Christ miracles were afterward done, when of that very number of the Jews they believed, and the blood of Christ was granted to them. They bore impious and bloody hands from the blood of Christ: he himself, whose blood they had shed, washed them. They were joined to his body, that is, the Church, who persecuted his mortal body, which they had seen. They shed their own price, that they might drink their own price. For afterward many were converted[^30]. When many miracles were being done by the Apostles, several thousands of men believed in one day: and they were found so near at hand, that they sold all that they had, and laid the price of their goods at the feet of the Apostles; and to each was distributed as there was need; and they had one soul and one heart toward God[^31]; of those very crucifiers of the Lord. But why was it not rendered to them? Because: *Render retribution to the proud,* was said; but they were unwilling to be proud. For when they saw many miracles done through the name of Christ, whom they supposed they had killed; moved by the miracles, they heard from Peter in whose name those things were done: for the servants were unwilling to arrogate to themselves the power of their Lord, so as to say that what he did in them was done by themselves. Therefore the servants gave honor to their Lord: they said that the things they marveled at were done in the name of him whom they had crucified. And they became humble, were pricked in heart, were disturbed, confessing their sin; and they sought counsel, saying: *What then shall we do?* They do not despair of salvation, but seek medicine. Then Peter said to them: *Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^32]. Those who did penance were humble: therefore it was not rendered to them. Because—see what this psalm says: *Be exalted, you who judge the earth; render retribution to the proud.* From that number, then, those were excepted; in them prevailed that voice of the Lord hanging on the cross, and saying: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^33]. *Be exalted, you who judge the earth; render retribution to the proud.* Therefore is he about to render retribution? He is about to render, but to the proud.

**9.** [vv. 3-4.] But when? when will he render? Meanwhile the evil triumph, and the evil exult, the evil blaspheme, do all evils. Does it move you? With piety inquire, do not with pride reprove. Does it move you? The Psalm too has compassion on you, it inquires with you, not because it does not know; but it inquires with you what it knows, that in it you may find what you did not know. As he who wishes to console someone, unless he grieves with him, does not lift him up: first he grieves with him, and so refreshes him with a consoling word. But if he enters to him laughing at his mourning, he does not do what was just now read, the Apostle saying: *Rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep*[^34]. Therefore, that he may rejoice with you, first you weep with him; you are saddened with him, that you may refresh him: so too the Psalm and the Spirit of God, surely knowing all things, inquires with you, as it were says your words. *How long, O Lord, shall sinners, how long shall sinners glory; shall they answer, and speak iniquity; shall all who work injustice speak?* What shall they speak, except against God, who say: What does it profit us that we live so? What will you say? Does God truly care for these things which we do? For because they live, they suppose that God does not know what they do. See what evil befalls them! For if the watchman knew, he would hold them; and therefore they avoid the eyes of the watchman, lest they be at once held: for the eyes of God no one can avoid; because he sees not only in the chamber, but even the inmost things of your heart. They themselves too think that nothing can lie hidden from God; and because they do it, and know what they have done, and see themselves living with God knowing, they who would not live with the watchman knowing, say to themselves: These things please God; and truly, if our deeds displeased him, as they displease the judges, as they displease the kings, as they displease the emperors, and as they displease the registrars, could we, as we avoid their eyes, avoid the eyes of God? Therefore these things please God. Therefore in another psalm it is said to the sinner: *These things you have done, and I kept silent; you suspected the iniquity, that I would be like you.* What is, *I would be like you?* That, as your evil deed pleases you, so you should suppose it pleases me too. And he threatens for the future: *I will reprove you*[^35]. Therefore he who said, *I kept silent,* does not keep silent. When he said: *These things you have done, and I kept silent; you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you;* he did not keep silent. For when we speak, he does not keep silent; when the reader reads, he does not keep silent; when the Psalm sings these things, he does not keep silent: and all these are voices of God made throughout the whole earth. How, then, does he keep silent, how does he not keep silent? He does not keep silent in word, he keeps silent in vengeance. What, then, is: *These things you have done, and I kept silent?* These things you have done, and I did not take vengeance. *You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you.* Of that very silence of vengeance—that is, of the cessation of vengeance—he says in another place: *I have kept silent; shall I keep silent forever*[^36]? *How long, O Lord, shall sinners, how long shall sinners glory; shall they answer, and speak iniquity; shall all who work injustice speak?* And he says all the works. *They answer, and speak iniquity.* What is, *they answer?* Against the righteous man they have something to answer. Some righteous man comes, and says: Do not do iniquity. Why? Lest you die. Behold, I have done iniquity; why do I not die? That man did righteousness, and died: why did he die? I have done iniquity; why has God not taken me away? Behold, that man did righteousness; and why did he so take vengeance on him? why does he so labor? they answer; that is, *they answer,* because they have what they may say; because they are spared, from the patience of God they find the argument of their answer. He spares for another reason, they answer for another reason, because they live. For why he spares, the Apostle says, expounds the counsel of the patience of God: *Do you suppose,* he says, *you who do such things, that you will escape the judgment of God? Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and longsuffering, not knowing that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But you,* that is, that man who answers and says: If I displeased God, God would not spare me: see what he does to himself, hear the Apostle: *But you, according to your hardness of heart and your impenitent heart, store up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works*[^37]. He, then, increases longsuffering, and you increase iniquity. His treasure will be in everlasting mercy for those who have not despised mercy; but your treasure will be found in wrath, and what you lay down daily a little at a time, afterward you will find a mass: little by little you lay it down, but you will find a heap. Do not attend to your small daily sins; from the tiniest drops rivers are filled.

**10.** [vv. 5-6.] But what do those do, who answer and speak iniquity, because they do it and are spared? *Your people, O Lord, they have humbled*: that is, all who live justly, against whom all the evil wish to be proud. *Your people, O Lord, they have humbled, and your inheritance they have vexed: the widow and the orphans they have killed, and the proselyte they have slain*: that is, the foreigner, the newcomer, the immigrant; he calls him a proselyte. These individual things are manifest, nor is there need to linger on them.

**11.** [v. 7.] *And they said: The Lord will not see.* He does not attend to these things, he neglects these things, he cares for other matters, he does not understand. For these are the two voices of the evil: one which I have already said: *These things you have done, and I kept silent: you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you.* What is, *that I would be like you?* You suppose that I see your deeds, and they please me, because I do not take vengeance. The other is the voice of the iniquitous: Because God neither attends to these things, nor adverts to know how I live, God does not care for me. Does God then reckon me among any number? or does God truly count me? or does he count men themselves? Unhappy man! he cared that you should be; does he not care that you should live well? Theirs, then, is this voice. *And they said: The Lord will not see, nor will the God of Jacob understand.*

**12.** [v. 8.] *Understand now, you who are senseless among the people; and you fools, at some time be wise.* He instructs his people, whose feet can be moved, when a man sees the felicities of the iniquitous, now living well in the number of the saints of God, that is, in the number of the children of the Church: he sees that the evil flourish, and do iniquity, and he envies them, and is led to imitate their deeds; because he sees as it were that it profits him nothing to live well, humble, hoping for a reward here. For if he hopes for that reward to come, he does not lose it; because the time has not yet come that he should receive it. You work in the vineyard, do your work, and you will receive your reward. You would not demand of the head of the household before you worked, and do you demand of God before you work? And this endurance pertains to your work, and this pertains to the reward: you wish to do less in the vineyard, who do not wish to endure; because that very endurance pertains to that very working, that you may find the reward. But if you are crafty, see lest you not only not receive the reward, but even find punishment; because you wished to be a crafty workman. And indeed the crafty workman, that he may begin not to work well, attends to the eyes of the head of the household, looks to him who hired him to the vineyard, that when he turns his eyes away, he may cease, and not work well; but when he turns his eyes back, he may work well. But God who hired you does not turn his eyes away; it is not lawful for you to work craftily: over you always are the eyes of the head of the household; seek where you may deceive him, and cease if you can. Therefore if perhaps any of you were thinking something, when you saw the evil flourish, and your thoughts made your feet waver in the way of God; this psalm speaks to you: but if none of you is such, through you it speaks to others, saying: *Understand now;* because they said: *The Lord will not see, nor will the God of Jacob understand. Understand,* he says, *now, you who are senseless among the people; and you fools, at some time be wise.*

**13.** [vv. 9-10.] *He who planted the ear, shall he not hear?* Has he nothing whereby to hear, who made for you that whereby you hear? *He who planted the ear, shall he not hear? or he who formed the eye, does he not consider? He who instructs the nations, shall he not reprove?* Attend to this greatly, my brothers: *He who instructs the nations, shall he not reprove?* This is how God acts, he instructs the nations: therefore he sent his word throughout the whole earth to men, sent it through angels, through patriarchs, through prophets, through servants, through so many heralds going before the judge. He sent also his very Word, sent also his very Son; he sent the servants of his Son, and in those very servants his Son. Throughout the whole earth the word of God is preached everywhere. Where is it not said to men: Leave your former iniquities, turn yourselves to the right paths? Therefore he spares, that you may correct yourselves; therefore yesterday he did not take vengeance, that today you may live well. He instructs the nations; shall he not, then, reprove? will he not hear those whom he instructs? will he not judge those to whom he sent forth and pre-sowed the word? In a school if you were, would you receive, and not render? Surely when you receive from the master, you are instructed: the master commits to you what he bestows; and will he not demand when you render? or when you have begun to render, will you be without fear of blows? Now, then, we receive; afterward we are set before the master, that we may render all our past, that is, that we may render an account of all these things which are now dispensed to us. Hear the Apostle saying: *We shall all stand before the tribunal of Christ*[^38], *that there each one may receive according to what he did through the body, whether good or evil*[^39]. *He who instructs the nations, shall he not reprove; he who teaches man knowledge?* Does he himself not know who made you to know; *he who teaches man knowledge?*

**14.** [v. 11.] *The Lord knows the thoughts of men, that they are vain.* For even if you do not know the thoughts of God, that they are just; he *knows the thoughts of men, that they are vain.* And men have known the thoughts of God: but to those to whom he is now made a friend, he discloses his counsel. And you too, my brothers, do not despise yourselves: if with faith you draw near to the Lord, you hear the thoughts of God; them you now learn, this is said to you, and to this you are instructed, why God now spares the evil, lest you murmur against God, *who teaches man knowledge. The Lord knows the thoughts of men, that they are vain.* Leave, then, the thoughts of men, which are vain; that you may comprehend the thoughts of God, which are wise. But who is he who comprehends the thoughts of God? He who is set in the firmament of heaven. We have already sung this, we have already said and expounded this.

**15.** [vv. 12-13.] *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and from your law you teach him: that you may make him mild from the malignant days, until a pit be dug for the sinner.* Behold you have the counsel of God, why he spares the evil: a pit is dug for the sinner. You already wish to bury him: still a pit is being dug for him; do not hasten to bury him. What is, *until a pit be dug for the sinner?* Or whom does he set as the sinner? one man? No. What, then? Every kind of sinful men, but of the proud; for he premised: *Render retribution to the proud.* For that Publican too was a sinner, who cast his eyes down to the earth, and struck his breast, saying: *God, be merciful to me a sinner*: but because he was not proud, but God will render retribution to the proud; not for him, but for such men a pit is dug, until he render retribution to the proud. Therefore what he says: *Until a pit be dug for the sinner,* understand the proud. Who is the proud? He who does not, by confession of sins, do penance, that he may be able to be healed through humility. Who is the proud? He who wishes to arrogate to himself those very few goods which he seems to have, and detracts from the mercy of God. Who is the proud? He who, even if he attributes to God the good things he does, yet insults those who do not do them, and exalts himself above them. For that Pharisee too: *I give you thanks,* he said: he did not say: I do it. For the things he was doing, he gave thanks to God: he felt, then, both that he did well, and that he did it from him. Whence, then, was he disapproved? Because he insulted the Publican. That you may be perfected, attend. First confession of sins ought to go before, whether of a man or a woman, wholesome penance which may avail to correct a man, not to mock God: but when after penance he has begun to live well, he still has something to think of, lest he attribute to himself what he does well, but give thanks to him, by whose grace it was done that he should live well; because he called him, he enlightened him. Is this man, then, already perfect? No; something is still lacking to him. What is lacking to him? That he be not proud over those who do not yet live as he himself lives. He who is such, let him be secure; that retribution is not rendered to him of which it was said: *Render retribution to the proud:* he is not among those for whom a pit is dug. For see that man who said: *I give you thanks, that I am not as the rest of men, unjust, robbers, adulterers, as is also this Publican*: how much he exalted himself, when he says: *That I am not as this Publican too?* But that other, with face cast down, struck his breast, saying: *God, be merciful to me a sinner.* That one was proud in good deeds, that other humble in evil deeds. See, brothers, humility in evil deeds pleased God more than pride in good deeds: so does God hate the proud. And therefore he so concluded: *Amen I say to you, the Publican went down justified rather than the Pharisee.* And he says why: *Because everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled; and everyone who humbles himself shall be exalted*[^40]. My brothers, from this alone we learn that Christ taught us humility, because God became man. This is the humility which displeases the pagans; whence they insult us: What kind of God do you worship, who was born? what kind of God do you worship, who was crucified? The humility of Christ displeases the proud: but if it pleases you, a Christian, imitate it. If you imitate it, you will not labor; because he himself said: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart*[^41]. This, then, is the Christian discipline: no one does anything well, except by his grace. What a man does ill, is the man's own: what he does well, he does from the benefit of God. When he has begun to do well, let him not attribute it to himself: when he has not attributed it to himself, let him give thanks to him from whom he has received it. But when he does well, let him not insult him who does not do it, or exalt himself over him: for the grace of God is not so ended in him, that it may not reach another.

**16.** *That you may make him mild from the malignant days, until a pit be dug for the sinner.* Be mild, then, whoever you are a Christian, from the malignant days. For malignant are the days in which the sinners seem to flourish and the righteous to labor; but the labor of the righteous is the scourge of the Father, and the felicity of the sinners is their pit. For because God makes you mild from the malignant days, until a pit be dug for the sinner; do not suppose that now in some place angels stand with mattocks, and dig that great pit which can hold every kind of the iniquitous; and because you see that there are many iniquitous, and say to yourselves carnally: Truly, so great a multitude of the iniquitous, so great a crowd of sinners, what pit can hold them? when is so great a one dug that may hold them all, when is it emptied out? therefore God spares. It is not this: the digging of the sinners is their very felicity; for into it they will fall as into a pit. Attend, brothers, that it is a great thing that felicity is called a pit: *Until a pit be dug for the sinner.* For God spares him whom he knows to be a sinner and impious, by his hidden justice; and this very thing, that God spares him, by impunity makes him lifted up. He thinks himself high, and falls: in that very thing he falls, by which he thinks himself high. This he thinks to go sublimely, and God calls it a pit. The pit tends to the depths, not to heaven: but the proud sinners as it were go to heaven, and are sunk into the earth. On the contrary, the humble as it were press themselves down to the earth, and ascend into heaven. Be mild, then, whoever you are faithful, if you are instructed out of the law of God, that your heart may be in the firmament of heaven: because God made the luminaries on the fourth day, which is called *the fourth of the sabbath,* whence this psalm received its title. As you see the luminaries with all patience direct their courses, and not care what men say about them; so you too do not care whatever the flesh may do to you. For every man is flesh and blood. For you are not cheap in comparison with another flesh, by which you seem to be pressed; because for you he assumed flesh, and for you he shed blood, who is to bring both you and that man to his examination: and if he prepaid so great things, when you were impious, what does he keep for the faithful? Hence be mild. How are you made mild? When you say: Because God wills this, therefore the evil flourish; he wishes to spare the evil, he leads to repentance those whom he spares, but they are not corrected: he knows how he may judge concerning them. But a man is harsh, when he wishes to contradict either the goodness of the Lord, or his patience, or his power, or the justice of the judge. The proud man is raised up against God, God sinks him; and in that very thing he is sunk, by which he is raised up against God. For in another psalm he says thus: *You cast them down, while they were being lifted up*[^42]. He did not say: You cast them down, because they were lifted up; or: You cast them down, after they were lifted up; that the time of their being lifted up should be one thing, the time of their being cast down another: but in that very thing by which they were being lifted up, there they were being cast down. For as much as the heart of man is proud, so much it withdraws from God; and if it withdraws from God, it goes into the depths. On the contrary, a humble heart brings God down from heaven, that he may be made near. Surely God is high, above all the heavens is God, he transcends all the angels: how much have you to be raised, that you may reach to that lofty one? I do not wish you to break yourself by stretching yourself; I give you another counsel, lest in that stretching perhaps you crack through pride: surely God is high; do you humble yourself, and he will descend to you.

**17.** [v. 14.] We have heard why he spares the evil; this itself is their pit. God says to you: How a pit is dug for them, and why a pit is dug for them, is not yours to know; but from my law learn that you ought to be patient, *until a pit be dug for the sinner.* And what of me, you say, who labor, and labor among those very sinners? It is answered you what follows: *For the Lord will not cast off his people.* He exercises, he does not cast off. For how does Scripture say in another place? *Because whom God loves, he rebukes; and he scourges every son whom he receives*[^43]. He receives the scourged one, and do you say that he casts him off? We see men do that in their sons: sometimes, despairing now of their sons, they dismiss them to live as they wish; those who have hope, they scourge; those whom they have seen to be altogether without hope and untamed, they dismiss to do what they wish. Now him whom he dismisses to do what he wishes, he does not wish to admit to his inheritance: but he scourges the son for whom he keeps that inheritance. But when God scourges his son, let him run under the hand of the scourging Father; because he who scourges, instructs for the inheritance: from the inheritance he does not cast off his son whom he chastises; but therefore he scourges, that he may receive him. Let him not be of so vain and childish a sense, as to say: My father loves my brother more, to whom he permits to do whatever he wishes; I, if I move against the command of my father, find scourges. Do you rejoice under the scourges; because for you the inheritance is kept: *For the Lord will not cast off his people.* For a time he amends, he does not condemn forever: but those he spares for a time, he will condemn forever. Choose for yourself: do you wish temporal labor, or everlasting punishment? temporal happiness, or everlasting life? What does God threaten? Everlasting punishment. What does God promise? Everlasting rest. In that he scourges the good, it is temporal: in that he spares the evil, it is temporal. *For the Lord will not cast off his people, and his inheritance he will not forsake.*

**18.** [v. 15.] *Until righteousness,* he says, *be turned into judgment: and all who have it are right of heart.* Attend now, and have righteousness; because judgment you cannot yet have. First you must have righteousness; but that very righteousness of yours will be turned into judgment. Here the Apostles had righteousness, and bore the iniquitous. But what is said to them? *You shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^44]. Therefore their righteousness will be turned into judgment. For now whoever is righteous here, is for this end, that he may suffer evils and endure them: let him suffer the time of suffering, and the day of judging comes. But what do I say of the servants of God? The Lord himself, who is the judge of all the living and the dead, first willed to be judged, and so to judge. *Until righteousness be turned into judgment: and all who have it are right of heart.* Those who now have righteousness, do not yet judge. For first is to have righteousness, and afterward to judge: first one suffers the evil, and afterward judges the evil. Now let there be righteousness; afterward it will be turned into judgment. And so long he suffers evil men, as long as God wills, as long as the Church of God bears them, that it may be instructed through their malice. Yet God will not cast off his people, *until righteousness be turned into judgment: and all who have it are right of heart.* Who are *right of heart?* Those who wish what God wishes. He spares the sinners, you wish that he should now destroy the sinners. You are of a distorted heart and of a perverse will, when you wish one thing, God wishes another. But God wishes to spare the evil, you do not wish them to be spared: God is patient with sinners, you do not wish to endure sinners. But, as I had begun to say, you wish one thing, God another: turn your heart, and direct it to God; because the Lord too had compassion on the weak. He saw in his body, that is, in his Church, weak ones, who would first try to follow their own will; but when they saw the will of God to be another, they would direct themselves and their heart to taking up and following the will of God. Do not wish to twist the will of God to your will, but correct yours to the will of God. The will of God is as it were a rule: behold, suppose, you have twisted the rule; whence have you to be corrected? But it remains whole: for the rule is unchangeable. As long as the rule is whole, you have whither to turn yourself and correct your distortion, you have whence to correct what in you is twisted. But what do men wish? It is little that they have their will distorted; they wish even to make the will of God distorted according to their heart, that God may do what they themselves wish, when they themselves ought to do what God wishes.

**19.** But how did the Lord embrace out of two wills one made in the man he bore? Prefiguring in his body, that is, in his Church, certain future ones who would wish to do their own will, but would afterward follow God's; because he showed that certain weak ones belong to him, and prefigured them in himself. For therefore he sweated blood in his whole body[^45], because in his body, that is, in his Church, he showed the blood of the martyrs. Through the whole body the blood went out: so his Church has martyrs; through his whole body the blood was shed. Prefiguring, then, certain weak ones in himself, or in his body; from the person of the weak, having compassion on them, he said: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me.* He showed the human will: if he remained in that very will, it would now seem to show a distorted heart. But if he had compassion on you, and frees you in himself; imitate what follows, saying: *But not what I will, but what you will, Father*[^46]. If the human will should begin to creep upon you: O, if God would kill this enemy of mine, that he might not persecute me! O, if it could happen that I should not suffer such great things from him! now if you persevere, and this pleases you, and you see that God does not wish this, you are distorted in heart, you have not the righteousness which will be turned into judgment: *for they who have it are all right of heart.* And who are *right of heart?* Those who are found such as Job was found, who said: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^47]. Behold a right heart. Again in a grave wound, what did he say to his wife, whom the devil therefore had left and not killed, that he himself might have her as a helper, not the husband as a consoler? For he remembered that through that Eve Adam was deceived[^48], and thought this Eve necessary to himself. She drew near to Job, as it were Eve; but Adam was better a conqueror in the dung-heap than Adam conquered in paradise: for what did he answer that woman? See a heart prepared, see a right heart. Was he not suffering persecutions, and grave ones? And all Christians suffer; and if men do not rage, the devil rages: and if Christians have been made emperors, has the devil been made a Christian? Let Your Holiness attend, then, to what is a right heart. She drew near to him, and said: *Say something against God, and die.* He enumerated all the troubles either of himself or of his own: *Say something,* she said, *against God, and die.* And he, now recognizing Eve, wishing to return whence he had slipped, with his heart fixed in God as a luminary in the firmament, dwelling in heart in the book of God: *You have spoken,* he said, *as one of the foolish women; if we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure evil things*[^49]? Because the heart was fixed in God, therefore it was right: for because God is right, when you fix your heart in him, let him be a form to you, that your heart may be right. Fix, then, your heart in him, and it will be a right heart. But the human will was creeping; I know not what was suckling your mind from the weakness of the flesh: do not yet despair. The Lord presignified you in his weakness, not himself: for the Lord did not fear to suffer, being about to rise on the third day. If he suffered altogether as a man, and did not come to suffer as God, and if he knew that he would rise after three days, in no way would he dread, about to die, what the apostle Paul did not dread, about to rise at the end of the world. For he says: *I am constrained by two: having a desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ, for that is much the better; but to remain in the flesh is necessary on account of you*[^50]. For it was weariness to him to remain in the flesh, from two things he suffered an ardor; to be dissolved and to be with Christ, he said was much the better. So much, when his very passion drew near, how he exulted! how he gloried! *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; for the rest there remains for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge*[^51]. This man rejoices, about to be crowned, and sad is that one about to crown: so Paul rejoices, and the Christ our Lord says: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass.* But he took up sadness as he took up flesh. For do not suppose that we say this, that the Lord was not sad. For if we said this, that he was not sad, since the Gospel says: *My soul is sorrowful even unto death*[^52]: then also when the Gospel says: Jesus slept[^53], Jesus did not sleep; and when the Gospel says: Jesus ate[^54], Jesus did not eat: there creeps in the little worm of rottenness, and leaves nothing sound, so that it is said that even the body was not real, and he did not have real flesh. Whatever, then, is written of him, brothers, was done, is true. Was he, then, sad? Altogether sad, but taking up sadness by his will, as taking up flesh by his will; as by his will real flesh, so by his will real sadness. So, then, by his will he showed in himself, that if perhaps human weakness should creep upon you, and you should begin to wish something other than God wishes, you may see the distortion of your heart outside the rule, and fix it to the rule, and your heart may be directed to God, which in the man had begun to be distorted. So, then, the Lord, showing you, said: *My soul is sorrowful even unto death;* and said: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me.* But straightway do what he therefore did that he might teach you: *But not what I will, but what you will, Father.* For if you do this, you will have righteousness: if you have righteousness, the heart is right; if the heart is right, that righteousness which now endures, will be turned into judgment, and afterward, with your Lord judging, you will not only not dread the evils, but will even glory in the crown. Then you will see how the patience of God profited, either for their punishment, or for your crown: now you do not see it; believe what you do not yet see, lest you blush when you see. *Until righteousness be turned into judgment: and they who have it are all right of heart.*

**20.** [v. 16.] *Who will rise up for me against the malignant? or who will stand with me against those who work iniquity?* Many persuade many evils, the serpent does not cease to whisper, that you may do iniquity: wherever you turn, perhaps if you have made progress, you seek to live well with someone, and scarcely find one; many evil ones surround you, because the grains are few, the chaff much. This threshing-floor has its grains, but they still labor. Separated, then, from the chaff, the whole heap will be large: the grains are few, but in comparison with the chaff; but many in themselves. Therefore when the evil rage on every side, and say: Why do you live so? are you the only Christian? Why do you not do what others too do? why do you not watch the games, as others too? why do you not apply remedies and amulets? why do you not consult astrologers and soothsayers, as others too? and you sign yourself, and say, I am a Christian, to repel those I-know-not-whom: but the adversary presses, urges; and—what is worse—by the example of Christians he chokes Christians. There is sweat, there is heat; the Christian soul is troubled: yet it is to conquer; but not of itself? Therefore see what it says. For it answers: What does it profit me that I now make remedies for myself, and gain a few days? I go hence from this world, and go to my Lord, and he will send me into the fire; because I preferred a few days to the future life, he will send me into hell. What hells? Of the eternal judgment of God. Truly, unless you suppose that God cares how men live? And this perhaps a friend says to you not in the street, but in the house your wife, or perhaps the husband to a good and holy faithful wife, the deceiver of her. If the woman to the husband, she is an Eve to him; if the man to the wife, he is a devil to her: either she herself is an Eve to you, or you are a serpent to her. Sometimes a father wishes to turn his thought even to his son: he finds him too evil, most wicked; he is in a sweat, he fluctuates, he seeks how he may conquer, he is nearly swallowed up, he nearly consents, but let God be present. Hear, then, the Psalm: *Who will rise up for me,* he says, *against the malignant?* They are so many; wherever I look, they meet me. Who will meet the prince of iniquity, the devil, and his angels, and the men seduced by him?

**21.** [v. 17.] *Unless the Lord,* he says, *had helped me, my soul had almost dwelt in hell.* I had nearly fallen into that pit which is prepared for sinners: this is, *my soul had almost dwelt in hell.* Because it was already wavering, already nearly consenting, it looked to the Lord. As, for example, he was insulted, that he might do iniquity. For sometimes the evil gather themselves, and insult the good; especially if those are more, and have caught that one, as sometimes there is much chaff around one grain (then they will not be together when the heap has been winnowed): that one is caught among many iniquitous, he is insulted, he is surrounded; they wish to set themselves over him, they harass him as if righteous, and insult him as if about righteousness itself: Great, they say, the apostle; you have flown into heaven, like Elijah. Men do these things, so that sometimes, attending to the human tongue, one blushes to be good among the evil. Let him therefore resist the evil; but not of his own strength, lest he become proud, when he wishes to escape the proud, and increase the number of the proud. But what does he say? *Who will rise up for me against the malignant? or who will stand with me against those who work iniquity? Unless the Lord had helped me, my soul had almost dwelt in hell.*

**22.** [vv. 18-19.] *If I said: My foot is moved; your mercy, O Lord, was helping me.* See how God loves confession. Your foot is moved, and you do not say: My foot is moved; but you say that you stand, when you are already falling. Rather, if you have already begun to be moved, if you have already begun to waver, confess the movement, lest you bewail the fall; that he may help you, that your soul be not in hell. God wishes confession, he wishes humility. You are moved, as a man; he helps you, as God: but yet say: *My foot is moved.* Why are you already moved, and say: I stand? *If I said: My foot is moved; your mercy, O Lord, was helping me.* As Peter presumed, not in his own strength. The Lord seemed to walk upon the sea, treading the heads of all the proud in this world. His very journey of treading the heads of the proud, he signified by walking upon the swollen waves. The Church too treads them: for it is itself Peter. Yet Peter did not dare to walk upon the waters by himself: but what did he say? *Lord, if it is you, bid me come to you upon the waters.* He in his own power, Peter at his bidding. *Bid me,* he says, *come to you.* He answered: *Come.* For the Church too treads the heads of the proud; but because it is the Church and has human weakness, that it might be fulfilled: *If I said: My foot is moved,* Peter wavered in the sea, and cried out: *Lord, I perish.* What, then, is set here: *If I said: My foot is moved;* is there set: *Lord, I perish.* And what is set here: *Your mercy, O Lord, was helping me;* is there set: *Jesus stretched out his hand, saying: O of little faith, why did you doubt*[^55]? It is marvelous how God proves men: our very perils make the deliverer sweeter to us. For see what follows. Because he said: *If I said: My foot is moved; your mercy, O Lord, was helping me*: the Lord surely became sweet to him, rescuing him from the perils; and therefore, expounding that very sweetness of the Lord, he cries out, saying: *O Lord, according to the multitude of my sorrows in my heart, your exhortations have made glad my soul.* Many sorrows, but many consolations: bitter wounds, but sweet medicines.

**23.** [v. 20.] *Does the seat of iniquity cleave to you, you who form sorrow in the precept?* This he said: No iniquitous one sits with you, nor will you have a seat of iniquity. And whence he may understand this, he gives the reason: *You who form,* he says, *sorrow in the precept.* For from this I understand that the seat of iniquity does not cleave to you, because you did not spare us either. You have this in the Epistle of the apostle Peter, and to this end he set a testimony from Scripture: *It is the time,* he says, *of the beginning of judgment from the house of the Lord;* that is, it is the time that now those be judged who belong to the house of the Lord. If the sons are scourged, what ought the most wicked servants to hope? And therefore he added: *But if the beginning be from us, what end of those who do not believe the Gospel of God?* Then he adds that testimony: *And if the righteous shall scarcely be saved, where shall the sinner and the impious appear*[^56]? How, then, will the iniquitous be with you, when you do not spare even your faithful, that you may exercise and instruct them? But because for this he does not spare, that he may instruct; therefore he said: *You who form sorrow in the precept.* For *you form* is: You make forms, you shape; whence too the potters are so called, and a clay vessel (vas fictile) is so called: not that fiction which is a lie; but that which is shaped that it may be, and have some form, as he said a while ago: *He who formed (finxit) the eye, shall he not see*[^57]? Is *he formed the eye* a lie? But it is understood: He molded the eye, he made the eye. Is he not a potter, when he makes the fragile, the weak, and the earthly? Hear the Apostle saying: *We have this treasure in earthen vessels*[^58]. But perhaps another made these vessels for us? Hear him saying: *O man, who are you to answer back to God? Does the thing formed say to him who formed it: Why have you made me thus? Has not the potter of the clay power, from the same lump, to make one vessel unto honor, another unto dishonor*[^59]? See also the Lord Christ himself, that he showed himself a potter. For because he had made man from clay[^60], with clay he anointed the one for whom in the womb he had made less of eyes[^61]. Therefore what he said: *Does the seat of iniquity cleave to you, you who form sorrow in the precept?* let us say thus: Does the seat of iniquity cleave to you, you who shape sorrow in the precept? You shape, he says, sorrow in the precept, that is, of sorrow you make a precept for us, that the very sorrow may be a precept for us. How is sorrow a precept for us? When he scourges you who died for you, and does not promise you blessedness in this life, and cannot deceive, and here does not give what you seek. What will he give? where will he give it? how much will he give, who here does not give, who here instructs, who forms sorrow in the precept? Here is your labor, and rest is promised to you. You attend to your having labor here; but attend to what kind of rest he promises. Can you conceive of it? If you could conceive of it, you would see that you labor nothing in compensation. Hear him who beheld that in part, who said: *Now I know in part*[^62]: what does the Apostle say? *For that which is for the present temporal and light of our tribulation, beyond an incredible measure, and unto an incredible measure, works for us an eternal weight of glory.* What is, *works for us an eternal weight of glory?* for whom does it work? *For those not regarding the things which are seen, but the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal*[^63]. Do not be slothful in a brief labor, and you will rejoice unceasingly. God is to give you eternal life: consider with how great a labor it is to be bought.

**24.** Attend, brothers; it is for sale. What I have is for sale, God says to you; buy it. What has he for sale? I have rest for sale; buy it with labor. Attend, that we may be in the name of Christ strong Christians: now it is little that remains in the Psalm, let us not be wearied. For how will he be able to be strong in doing, who fails in hearing? The Lord will be present, that we may explain to you what remains. Attend: God in a manner set the kingdom of heaven for sale. You say to him: What is it worth? Its price is labor: as if he said: Its price is gold, it would not suffice to say this alone, but you would ask how much gold. For both a solidus is gold, and a half-ounce, and a pound, and something of the kind. Therefore he said the price, lest you should labor seeking how long until you find it. The price of this thing is labor: how much is the labor? Now ask you how much is to be labored. It is not yet said to you how great that labor will be, or how much labor is exacted of you: this God says to you: I show how great that rest is; do you judge with how great a labor it is to be bought. Let God, then, say how great that rest will be. *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^64]. This is the everlasting rest: without end will be that rest, without end will be this joy, without end will be that gladness, without end will be incorruption; you will have eternal life; rest which has no end. Of how great a labor is the rest worthy which has no end? If you wish to compare truly, and to judge truly, eternal rest is rightly bought with eternal labor. This is true: but do not fear, God is merciful. For if you had eternal labor, you would never arrive at eternal rest. Always laboring, when were you to arrive at that which can indeed be bought with everlasting labor, because it is everlasting rest? Make the price equal: surely eternal rest is worthy to be purchased with eternal labor. But if you labored always, you would never arrive at rest. Therefore, that at some time you may arrive at what you buy, it is not to be labored forever: not because it is not worth so much, but that what is bought may be possessed. It is indeed worthy to be bought with perpetual labor; but it is necessary that it be bought with temporal labor. Surely the labor ought to have been so great, that is, everlasting, for everlasting rest. What are a thousand thousand years in labor worth? A thousand thousand years have an end: What I will give you, God says, will have no end. What kind of mercy of God? Nor does he say: Labor a thousand thousand years; he does not say, even labor a thousand years; he does not say, labor five hundred years: as long as you live labor, in a few years: from there now there will be rest, and it will have no end. And still hear what follows: *O Lord, according to the multitude of my sorrows in my heart, your exhortations have made glad my soul.* And you labor a few years, and in those very labors consolation is not lacking, daily joys are not lacking. But do not rejoice in the world: rejoice in Christ, rejoice in his word, rejoice in his law. To those very joys pertains what we speak, and what you hear. How great, then, are these consolations in so great labors? It is true, then, what the Apostle said: *For that which is for the present temporal and light of our tribulation, beyond an incredible measure, and unto an incredible measure, works for us an eternal weight of glory*[^65]. Behold how great a price we give, as it were one carob-seed to receive everlasting treasures: a carob-seed of labor for incredible rest, according to what was said: *Beyond an incredible measure, and unto an incredible measure, it works an eternal weight of glory.* You rejoice for a time; do not trust it: you are sad for a time; do not despair. Let not happiness corrupt you, nor adversity break you: lest perhaps you say in your soul: It cannot be that God admits to himself the iniquitous, he who amends the righteous themselves, that he may save them; who therefore amends them, that he may instruct them. *If the righteous shall scarcely be saved; where shall the sinner and the impious appear?* *Does the seat of iniquity cleave to you?* that is: Does the seat of the impious cleave to you; *you who form sorrow in the precept,* you who willed thus to exercise and instruct even these sons, willed thus to give them precepts, that they should not be without fear, lest they love something else, and forget you, their true good? God is good: if God ceased, and did not mingle bitternesses with the felicities of the world, we would forget him.

**25.** But where the anguishes of troubles make the waves of the soul, let that faith which was sleeping there be roused. For it was tranquil, when Christ slept in the sea: while he slept the tempest arose, and they began to be in peril. Therefore in a Christian heart there will be both tranquility and peace; but as long as our faith keeps watch: but if our faith sleeps, we are in peril. For this is what the sleeping Christ signifies, that certain ones forget their faith, and are in peril. But as that ship, when it was tossed, Christ was roused by those tossing and saying: *Lord, we perish;* he rose, commanded the tempests, commanded the waves, the peril ceased, tranquility was made[^66]: so when evil concupiscences, evil persuasions trouble you, they are waves, they will be made tranquil. Now you despair, and suppose that you do not belong to the Lord: let your faith awaken, rouse Christ in your heart; with faith rising, you now recognize where you are: and if perhaps the waves of concupiscence tempt you, you look to what God has promised; and the sweetness of the promises will make you a despiser of the sweetnesses of the world: and if perhaps many threats of powerful evil men urge you, and these too expel you from righteousness; you attend to what God threatens: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^67], and you do not forsake righteousness: fearing, then, the everlasting fire, you despise temporal sorrows; and for the sake of what God promised, you despise temporal happiness. He promised rest; suffer trouble: he threatens eternal fire; despise temporal sorrows: and with Christ awaking let your heart be made tranquil, that you may also arrive at the harbor. For he would not fail to prepare the harbor, who prepared the ship. *Does the seat of iniquity cleave to you, you who form sorrow in the precept?* Out of evil men he exercises us, and out of their persecutions he instructs us. Out of the malice of the evil the good man is scourged, and out of the servant the son is amended: so sorrow is formed in the precept. What God permits them to be able to do, this the evil men do, whom for a time he spares.

**26.** [v. 21.] For what follows? *They will lie in wait for the soul of the righteous.* Why will they lie in wait? Because they find no true charge to bring against him. For what did they lie in wait for in the Lord? They concocted false charges[^68], because they could not find true ones. *And they will condemn innocent blood.* Why all this is done, he will declare in what follows.

**27.** [v. 22.] *And the Lord,* he says, *was made for me a refuge.* You would not seek such a refuge, if you were not in peril: but for this you were put in peril, that you might seek it; because he forms sorrow in the precept. He makes for me, out of the malice of the evil, tribulation; pricked by tribulation I began to seek the refuge which in that worldly felicity I had ceased to seek. For who easily remembers God, who is always happy, and rejoices in present hope? Let the hope of the world withdraw, and let the hope of God draw near; that you may be able to say: *And the Lord was made for me a refuge*: to this end let me grieve, that the Lord may be made for me a refuge. *And my God for the help of my hope.* For now the Lord is hope; for as long as we are here, we are in hope, not yet in reality. But lest we fail in hope, the promiser is present, lifting us up, and tempering the very evils which we suffer. For not in vain was it said: *God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you can bear; but will make with the temptation also a way of escape, that you may be able to endure*[^69]; so he sends into the furnace of tribulation, that the vessel may be baked, not broken. *And the Lord was made for me a refuge, and my God for the help of my hope.* Why, then, what seemed to you as it were unjust, that he spares the evil? See how now the Psalm is corrected, and you too be corrected with the Psalm: for the Psalm had your voices. What are the voices? *How long, O Lord, shall sinners, how long shall sinners glory?* The Psalm had your voices: now, then, do you have the voices of the Psalm. What are the voices of the Psalm? *And the Lord was made for me a refuge, and my God for the help of my hope.*

**28.** [v. 23.] *And the Lord will render to them according to their works; and according to their malice the Lord our God will destroy them.* Not in vain he says, *according to their malice.* It is bestowed on me from them; and yet it is called their malice, not their benefits. For surely out of the evil he exercises us, scourges us. To what thing does he scourge us? Surely to the kingdom of heaven. For *he scourges every son whom he receives: and what son is there to whom his father does not give discipline*[^70]? Which when God does, he instructs us for the everlasting inheritance; and this he often bestows on us out of evil men, in whom he exercises and perfects our love, which he wishes to be stretched even to enemies. For the love of a Christian is not perfect, except when he fulfills what Christ commanded: *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute you*[^71]. Hence the devil himself is conquered, hence the crown of victory is received. Behold how great things God bestows on us out of evil men: nor yet according to that which out of them he bestows on us, but according to their malice will he repay them. For see how great things he bestowed on us out of that most monstrous crime of Judas the betrayer. For Judas indeed betrayed to the passion the Son of God, and through the passion of the Son of God all the nations were redeemed to salvation: nor yet for the salvation of the nations was a reward rendered to Judas, but for his malice due punishment was repaid. For if the betrayal of Christ, and not the mind of the betrayer, is to be considered, Judas did what God the Father did, of whom it is written, that *he did not spare his own Son, but for us all delivered him up*[^72]. Judas did what the Lord Christ himself did, of whom it is written: *Who gave himself for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for an odor of sweetness*: and again: *As Christ,* he says, *loved the Church, and gave himself for it*[^73]. And yet we give thanks to God the Father, who did not spare his only Son, but for us delivered him up; we give thanks to the Son himself, who gave himself for us, and in this fulfilled the will of the Father: and we detest Judas, out of whose deed God bestowed so great a benefit on us; and we rightly say: The Lord rendered to him according to his iniquity, and according to his malice destroyed him. For he did not betray Christ for us, but for the silver for which he sold him: although the betrayal of Christ is our reception, and the selling of Christ is our redemption. So too those who persecuted the martyrs, by persecuting on earth, sent them into heaven; and, knowingly indeed, they inflicted the loss of the present life, but, unknowingly, they conferred the gain of the future life: yet whoever persevered in the unjust hatred of the righteous, the Lord will render to them according to their iniquities, and according to their malice will destroy them. For as the goodness of the righteous harms the evil, so the iniquity of the impious profits the good. For the Lord too says: *I came, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind*[^74]: and the Apostle: *To some indeed,* he says, *we are the odor of life unto life, but to others the odor of death unto death*[^75]. But the malice of the iniquitous is the left-hand arms of the righteous; as the same apostle says: *Through the arms of righteousness on the right and the left,* that is, *through glory and ignobility*[^76]: and so thereafter he follows out the rest, showing the right-hand arms, the glory of God, the good fame, the truth by which they were known that they were alive, that they were not put to death, that they rejoiced, that they enriched many, that they possessed all things; but the left-hand, that they were held as ignoble and of evil fame, that they were thought seducers, that they were unknown, were killed, were coerced, were saddened, seemed to be in want and to have nothing. And what wonder if the soldiers of Christ assail the devil both with right-hand and left-hand arms? But as peace is to men of good will[^77], even when they are to others the odor of death unto death; so destruction is to men of evil will, even when to the righteous the left-hand arms are unto salvation. And so he will render to them not according to our profit, which is made out of them; but according to their iniquity, by which, loving it, they hate their own souls: and not according to the benefit which out of them he bestows on us, does he honor them, he who uses even the evil well; but *according to their malice the Lord our God will destroy them.*

**29.** Let the righteous, then, endure the unrighteous; let the temporal labor of the righteous endure the temporal impunity of the unrighteous: but *the righteous lives by faith*[^78]. For there is no other righteousness of man in this life, than to live by faith, which works through love[^79]. But if he lives by faith, let him believe both that there will be for him rest after the present labor, and for those everlasting torments after the present exultation. And if faith works through love, let him love even his enemies, and, as far as is in him, wish to profit them: for so he will act that they not harm him, when they wish this. And when perhaps they have received power as it were of harming and ruling; let him have his heart on high, where no one harms him, taught and instructed out of the law of God, that he may be made mild from the malignant days, until a pit be dug for the sinner. For if his will is in the law of the Lord, and in his law he will meditate day and night[^80], whose conversation is in the heavens[^81]; he shines from the firmament upon the earth: whence this psalm received its title from *the fourth of the sabbath,* when the luminaries were made[^82]; that he may do all things without murmuring, having the word of life in a crooked and perverse nation[^83]. For as the night does not extinguish the stars in heaven; so iniquity does not conquer the minds of the faithful clinging to the firmament of the Scripture of God. And this very thing, that our earthly goods are sometimes given into the power of the evil, pertains not only to our instruction, that the Lord may be made for us a refuge, and God the help of our hope; but also profits the very pit of the sinner, of whom in another psalm it is said: *He shall be bowed down and fall, when he shall have dominion over the poor*[^84].

**30.** Perhaps the length of the sermon has been burdensome to you: although in this eagerness of your zeal this does not appear. But even if it is so, pardon it: first, because bidden I did it; for the Lord our God bade me through those brothers in whom he dwells. For God does not bid, except from his seat. Then, because you were so eager for us, we confess, we too were eager for you. May our God, then, console this labor, that this our sweat may be for you unto the advancement of salvation, not unto the testimony of accusation. This I say, brothers, that from what you have heard, you may make progress, and ruminate it with yourselves: do not permit yourselves to forget it, not only by reconsidering and conversing about these things, but also by so living. For a good life which is lived from the precepts of God is as it were a stylus, writing in the heart what is heard. If it were written in wax, it would easily be blotted out: write it in your hearts, in your conduct, and it will never be blotted out.
