# Exposition of Psalm 50

**1.** Neither must the multitude of this throng be defrauded of its abundance, nor its weakness be burdened. We ask for silence and quiet, that our voice, after yesterday's labor, may be able to last with some strength. It is to be believed that your Charity has come together more abundantly today for no other reason, than that you may pray for those whom an alien and perverse affection makes absent. For we speak not of pagans, nor of Jews, but of Christians; nor of these still catechumens, but of many even baptized, from whose laver you are in no way distant, and yet from their heart you are unlike. For how many of our brothers we today consider and bewail going into vanities and lying follies, neglecting that to which they were called! who, if perhaps in the circus itself for some cause they take fright, at once sign themselves, and stand there carrying on their forehead that from which they would withdraw if they carried it in the heart. The mercy of God is to be besought, that he grant understanding to condemn these things, and affection to flee them, and mercy to pardon them. Opportunely, then, the psalm of penitence was sung today. Let us speak even with the absent: our voice to them will be your memory. Do not neglect the wounded and languishing, but, that you may more easily heal them, you ought to remain healthy. Correct by rebuking, console by addressing, furnish an example by living well, he will be present to them who was present to you too. For now that you have passed beyond these perils, the fountain of God's mercy is not cut off. By the way you came, they will come; by the way you passed, they will pass. It is troublesome indeed, and exceedingly perilous, rather pernicious, and for certain destructive, that they sin knowingly. For he runs to these vanities in one way who despises the voice of Christ, in another he who knows what he flees. But that even of such men one ought not to despair, this psalm shows.

**2.** [vv. 1-2] For its title is inscribed: *A psalm of David himself, when Nathan the prophet came to him, when he had gone in to Bathsheba*[^1]. Bathsheba was a woman, another man's wife. We say it indeed with grief and trembling, but yet God did not wish that to be kept silent which he wished to be written. I will say, then, not what I wish, but what I am compelled; I will say, not exhorting to imitation, but instructing to fear. Captivated by the beauty of this woman, another man's wife, King David and prophet, of whose seed according to the flesh the Lord was to come, committed adultery with her. This is not read in this psalm, but appears in its title; and in the book of Kings it is read more fully. Both Scriptures are canonical, to both, without any doubt, faith is to be given by Christians. It was committed and written down. Her husband too he took care to have killed in war: by homicide he increased the adultery: and after this deed Nathan the prophet was sent to him, sent by the Lord, to rebuke him for so great a deed committed.

**3.** What men should beware, we have said; but what, if they have fallen, they should imitate, let us hear. For many wish to fall with David, and do not wish to rise with David. The example of falling, then, is not set forth, but, if you have fallen, of rising again. Attend, lest you fall. Let not the lapse of the greater be a delight to the lesser, but let the fall of the greater be a trembling to the lesser. For this it was set forth, for this it was written, for this it is often read and sung in the Church: let those who have not fallen hear, lest they fall, let those who have fallen hear, that they may rise. The sin of so great a man is not kept silent, it is preached in the Church. Those who hear badly hear, and seek for themselves patronage for sinning; they attend to whence they may defend what they have prepared to commit, not to whence they may beware what they have not committed, and they say to themselves: If David, why not I too? Hence a more iniquitous soul, which, because it did it for this reason, that David did it, therefore did worse than David. I will say this very thing, if I can, more plainly. David had set himself no one to imitate as you do: he had fallen by a lapse of cupidity, not by a patronage of holiness: you set yourself as it were a saint that you may sin; you do not imitate his holiness, but imitate his ruin. You love in David what David hated in himself: you prepare yourself for sinning, you arrange to sin: you inspect the book of God that you may sin; you hear the Scriptures of God for this, that you may do what displeases God: this David did not do; he was rebuked through the prophet, he did not slip into the prophet. But others, hearing wholesomely, in the fall of the strong measure their own weakness; and, desiring to avoid what God condemns, they withhold their eyes from a secure gaze: they do not fix them on the beauty of another's flesh, nor make themselves secure out of a perverse simplicity, they do not say: I looked with a good mind, I looked kindly, out of love I gazed long. For they set before themselves the fall of David, and they see that he, great, fell for this, that the small may be unwilling to see whence they may fall. For they restrain their eyes from wantonness, they do not easily join themselves, do not mingle with other women, do not lift easy eyes to other balconies, to other terraces. For from afar David saw her by whom he was captivated. The woman afar, the lust near. Elsewhere was what he saw, in himself whence he fell. This weakness of the flesh, then, is to be attended to, the words of the Apostle are to be recalled: *Let not sin, then, reign in your mortal body*[^2]. He did not say, let it not be; but, let it not reign. Sin is present, when you are delighted; it reigns, if you have consented. Carnal delight, especially when it advances even to unlawful and another's, is to be bridled, not relaxed; to be tamed by command, not set in command. Look secure, if you have nothing whereby you may be moved. But you answer: I hold firmly. Are you stronger than David?

**4.** He admonishes too by such an example, that no one ought to exalt himself in prosperous things. For many fear adverse things, do not fear prosperous things. A prosperous thing is more perilous to the mind, than an adverse thing to the body. Prosperity first corrupts, that adversity may find what it may break. My brothers, against felicity one must keep watch more keenly. Therefore see how the word of God, in our felicity, takes from us security: *Serve,* he says, *the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling*[^3]. In exultation, that we give thanks; in trembling, lest we fall. This sin David did not commit, when he was suffering the persecutor Saul. When holy David was suffering his enemy Saul, when he was harassed by his persecutions, when through diverse places he fled lest he fall into his hands, he did not covet another's woman, nor, the wife being adulterated, kill the husband. He was in the weakness of his tribulation so much the more intent on God, the more wretched he seemed. A useful thing is tribulation; more useful is the iron of the physician, than the temptation of the devil. He was made secure, the enemies conquered; he lacked oppression, the swelling grew. This example, then, avails for this, that we fear felicity. *I found tribulation,* he says, *and grief, and I called upon the name of the Lord*[^4].

**5.** But it is done: let me have said these things to those who have not committed it, that they may keep watch to guard their integrity, and when they attend to a great one having fallen, the small may fear. But if anyone, already fallen, hears these things, and holding something of evil in his conscience; let him attend to the words of this psalm: let him attend indeed to the greatness of the wound, but not despair of the majesty of the physician. Sin with despair is certain death. Let no one, then, say: If I have now done something evil, I am now to be damned: God does not pardon such evils, why do I not add sins to sins? Let me enjoy this world in pleasure, in wantonness, in wicked cupidity: now that hope of restoration is lost, let me have at least this which I see, if I cannot have what I believe. This psalm, then, as it makes cautious those who have not fallen, so it does not wish those to be in despair who have fallen. Whoever you are who have sinned, and hesitate to do penance for your sin, despairing of your salvation, hear David groaning. To you Nathan the prophet has not been sent, David himself has been sent to you. Hear him crying out, and together cry; hear him groaning, and groan together; hear him weeping, and join your tears; hear him corrected, and rejoice together. If sin could not be barred from you, let the hope of pardon not be barred. Nathan the prophet was sent to that man: attend to the humility of the king. He did not reject the words of the one commanding, he did not say: Do you dare to speak to me, a king? The lofty king heard the prophet; let his humble people hear Christ.

**6.** [v. 3] Hear, then, these things, and say with him: *Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy*[^5]. He who beseeches great mercy, confesses great misery. Let those seek your small mercy, who have sinned in ignorance: *Have mercy on me,* he says, *according to your great mercy.* Come to the aid of a grave wound according to your great medicine. Grave is what I have, but I flee to the Almighty. Of my so deadly wound I would despair, unless I found so great a physician. *Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy: and according to the multitude of your tender mercies, blot out my iniquity.* What he says, *blot out my iniquity,* this is, *have mercy on me, O God.* And what he says, *according to the multitude of your tender mercies,* this is, *according to your great mercy.* Because the mercy is great, the tender mercies are many; and of your great mercy, your tender mercies are many. You attend to the despisers that you may correct them, you attend to the ignorant that you may teach them, you attend to those confessing that you may pardon them. Did he do it in ignorance? Someone had done certain things, and had done many evils: *I obtained mercy,* he says, *because I did it ignorantly in unbelief*[^6]. This David could not say: *I did it ignorantly.* For he was not ignorant how great an evil it is to lay hands on another's wife, and how great an evil it is to kill the husband not knowing it, and not even angry. They obtain the mercy of the Lord, then, who did it in ignorance; and those who did it knowingly obtain not just any mercy, but great mercy.

**7.** [v. 4] *More and more wash me from my injustice*[^7]. What is, *More and more wash me*? Much defiled. More and more wash the sins of one knowing, you who have washed away the sins of one ignorant. Nor even so is one to despair of your mercy. *And from my sin cleanse me.* By what merit? He is a physician, offer the fee: he is God, offer a sacrifice. What will you give that you may be cleansed? See whom you call upon; you call upon a just one: he hates sins, if he is just; he avenges on sins, if he is just; you will not be able to take from the Lord God his justice. Implore mercy, but attend to justice: it is mercy to pardon the sinner, it is justice to punish sin. What then? You seek mercy, will the sin remain unpunished? Let David answer, let the fallen answer, let them answer with David, that they may merit mercy as David, and say: Not, Lord, not will my sin be unpunished: I know the justice of him whose mercy I seek: it will not be unpunished, but for this reason I do not wish you to punish me, because I myself punish my sin; for this I ask that you pardon, because I acknowledge.

**8.** [v. 5] *Because I acknowledge my iniquity, and my sin is before me always*[^8]. I have not set behind my back what I did, I do not gaze at others, forgetful of myself, I do not affect to cast the speck from the eye of my brother, when there is a beam in my eye[^9]: my sin is before me, not behind me. For it was behind me when the prophet was sent to me, and set before me a likeness about the sheep of the poor man. For Nathan the prophet said to David: *There was a certain rich man having very many sheep; but a poor neighbor of his had one little ewe, which he nourished in his bosom and from his own food: a guest came to the rich man; he took nothing from his own flock, he coveted the little ewe of the poor neighbor, he killed it for his guest: what is he worthy of?* And he, angry, brought forth the sentence: plainly then the king, not knowing where he was caught, said the rich man worthy of death, and the sheep to be repaid fourfold. Most severely and most justly. But his sin was not yet before him, what he had done was behind his back: he did not yet acknowledge his own iniquity, and therefore did not pardon another's. But the prophet, sent for this, took the sin from his back, and set it before his eyes, that he might see that sentence, so severe, brought forth against himself. To cut and heal the wound of his heart, he made an iron of his tongue[^10]. This the Lord did to the Jews, when they brought to him the adulterous woman, setting forth a snare of temptation, and falling into what they had set forth. *This woman,* they say, *was caught in adultery: Moses commanded such to be stoned; but you, what do you judge of her?* as it were trying with a two-headed trap to catch the Wisdom of God, so that if he ordered her to be killed, he would lose the fame of meekness; but if he ordered her to be released, he would incur, as a reprover of the law, slander. What, then, did he answer? He did not say: Kill her; he did not say: Release her: but he said: *He who knows himself to be without sin, let him first cast a stone at her.* A just law, which orders the adulteress to be killed: but let this just law have innocent ministers. You attend to whom you bring; attend too to who you are. *They, hearing this, one after another withdrew. There remained the adulteress and the Lord,* there remained the wounded one and the physician, there remained great misery and great mercy. Those bringing her blushed, and did not ask pardon; the one brought was confounded, and healed. *The Lord said to her: Woman, has no one condemned you? And she: No one, Lord. And he: Neither will I condemn you; go, henceforth now sin no more*[^11]. Did Christ act against his own law? For neither had the Father given the law without the Son. If heaven and earth and all things that are in them were made through him, how was the law written without the Word of God? Not, then, did God act against his own law, because not even an emperor acts against his own laws, when he grants pardon to those who confess. Moses the minister of the Law, Christ the promulgator of the Law: Moses stones as a judge, Christ pardons as a king. God had mercy on him, then, according to his great mercy, as this man here asks, as here he begs, as he cries out and grieves: which those offering the adulteress were unwilling to do; they recognized their wounds, the physician showing them, they did not seek medicine from the physician. So there are many whom it does not shame to sin, it shames to do penance. O incredible madness! At the wound itself you do not blush, at the binding of the wound do you blush? Is not the naked one fouler and more putrid? Flee, then, to the physician, do penance, say: *I acknowledge my iniquity, and my sin is before me always.*

**9.** [v. 6] *To you alone have I sinned, and done evil before you*[^12]. What is this? For before men, was not another's wife adulterated, and the husband killed? Did not all know what David had done? What is, *To you alone have I sinned, and done evil before you*? Because you alone are without sin. He is the just punisher, who has nothing in him to be punished: he is the just reprover, who has nothing in him to be reproved. *To you alone,* he says, *have I sinned, and done evil before you: that you may be justified in your words, and may overcome when you are judged.* To whom he says it, brothers, to whom he says it is difficult to discern. He speaks of course to God, and it is manifest how God the Father was not judged. What is, *To you alone have I sinned, and done evil before you: that you may be justified in your words, and may overcome when you are judged*? He sees the future judge to be judged, the just one to be judged by sinners, and overcoming in this, because there was not in him what might be judged. For he alone among men could speak the truth, the God-man: If you have found sin in me, say so. But perhaps there was what lay hidden from men, and they did not find what was indeed, but was not manifest? In another place he says: *Behold, the prince of the world comes,* a keen inspector of all sins: *behold,* he says, *the prince of this world comes,* afflicting sinners with death, the prince set over death; for by the envy of the devil death entered into the world of the earth[^13]. *Behold,* he says, *the prince of this world comes* (he said these things near his passion), *and in me he will find nothing,* nothing of sin, nothing worthy of death, nothing worthy of damnation. And as though it were said to him: Why, then, do you die? It follows, and he says: *But that all may know that I do the will of my Father, rise, let us go hence*[^14]. I suffer, he says, unworthy for the worthy, that I may make them worthy by my life, for whom I unworthily suffer their death. To him, therefore, having no sin, the prophet David says in the present: *To you alone have I sinned, and done evil before you: that you may be justified in your words, and may overcome when you are judged.* For you surpass all men, all judges, and he who thinks himself just, is unjust before you: you alone judge justly, judged unjustly, you who have power to lay down your soul, and power to take it up again. You overcome, then, when you are judged. You surpass all men, because you are more than men, and through you men were made.

**10.** [v. 7] *To you alone have I sinned, and done evil before you: that you may be justified in your words, and may overcome when you are judged. For behold I was conceived in iniquities*[^15]. As though it were said: They are overcome who did what you too, David, did: for it is no small evil and no small sin, adultery and homicide: what of those who, from the time they were born from their mother's womb, did no such thing? do you impute some sins even to them, that he may overcome all when he begins to be judged? David took on the person of the human race, and attended to the chains of all, considered the propagation of death, perceived the origin of iniquity, and said: *For behold I was conceived in iniquities.* Was David born of adultery, from Jesse a just man and his wife? What is it that he says he was conceived in iniquity, except that iniquity is drawn from Adam? Even that very chain of death is congealed together with that very iniquity. No one is born except drawing punishment, drawing the desert of punishment. The prophet says also in another place: *No one is clean in your sight, not even an infant whose life is of one day upon the earth*[^16]. For we know that by the baptism of Christ too sins are loosed, and that the baptism of Christ avails for the remission of sins. If infants are in every way innocent, why do mothers run to the Church with their sick ones? What by that baptism, what by that remission is forgiven? What did I see in the infant rather weeping than angry? What does baptism wash away? what does that grace loose? It looses the propagation of sin. For if that infant could speak to you, he would say; and if he now had the understanding which David had, he would answer you: *Why do you attend to me an infant?* You do not indeed see my misdeeds: but I was conceived in iniquity: *And in sins my mother nourished me in the womb.* Apart from this chain of carnal concupiscence Christ was born without a male, of a virgin conceiving by the Holy Spirit. He cannot be said to be conceived in iniquity; he cannot be said: In sins his mother nourished him in the womb, to whom it was said: *The Holy Spirit shall come upon you, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you*[^17]. Not, then, for this reason are men conceived in iniquity, and nourished in sins in the womb by their mothers, because to be joined to spouses is a sin; but because that which comes to be, surely comes to be of penal flesh. For the punishment of the flesh is death, and surely that very mortality is inherent. Whence the Apostle did not say the body to be about to die, but dead: *The body indeed is dead,* he says, *because of sin; but the spirit is life because of justice*[^18]. How, then, is that born without the chain of sin, which is conceived and sown of a body dead because of sin? This chaste work in a spouse does not have fault, but the origin of sin draws with it the punishment owed. For the husband is not, because he is a husband, not mortal, or mortal from anything else but sin. For the Lord too was mortal, but not from sin: he took on our punishment, and therefore loosed our guilt. Deservedly, then, in Adam all die, but in Christ all shall be made alive. *For through one man,* says the Apostle, *sin entered into this world, and through sin death, and so it passed over into all men, in whom all sinned*[^19]. The sentence is fixed: In Adam, he says, all sinned. The infant alone could be innocent, who was not born of the work of Adam.

**11.** [v. 8] *For behold you have loved truth: the uncertain and hidden things of your wisdom you have made manifest to me. You have loved truth*[^20]: that is, the unpunished sins even of those whom you pardon, you have not left. *You have loved truth*: so you have granted mercy beforehand, that you have also kept truth. You pardon the one confessing, you pardon, but the one punishing himself: so mercy and truth are kept; mercy, because the man is freed; truth, because the sin is punished. *For behold you have loved truth: the uncertain and hidden things of your wisdom you have made manifest to me.* What *hidden things*? what *uncertain things*? That God pardons even such. Nothing so hidden, nothing so uncertain. At this uncertainty the Ninevites did penance. For they said, although after the threats of the prophet, although after that voice: *In three days Nineveh shall be overthrown*: they said among themselves, that mercy was to be sought: they said, disputing thus among themselves: *Who knows, whether God turn his sentence to the better, and have mercy?* It was uncertain, when it is said: *Who knows.* Of the uncertain they did penance, they merited certain mercy: they prostrated themselves in tears, in fastings, in sackcloth and in ashes they prostrated themselves, they groaned, they wept, God spared. Did Nineveh stand, or was Nineveh overthrown? Otherwise indeed it seems to men, and otherwise it seemed to God. But I think that was fulfilled which the prophet had foretold. Look at what Nineveh was, and see that it was overthrown, overthrown in evil, built up in good: as Saul the persecutor was overthrown, Paul the preacher was built up. Who would not say that this city in which we now are was happily overthrown, if all those mad ones, their trifles forsaken, ran to the church with compunct heart, and called upon the mercy of God for their past deeds? Would we not say: Where is that Carthage? Because it is not what it was, it is overthrown: but if it is, what it was not, it is built up. So it is said to Jeremiah: *Behold, I give you to root up, to dig under, to overthrow, to destroy, and again to build, and to plant*[^21]. Hence is that voice of the Lord: *I will strike, and I will heal*[^22]. He strikes the rottenness of the misdeed, he heals the grief of the wound. Physicians do this when they cut, they strike and heal; they arm themselves to strike, they carry iron and come to cure. But because the sins of the Ninevites were great, they said: *Who knows?* This uncertainty God had laid open to his servant David. For when he had said, the prophet standing and rebuking him: *I have sinned*: at once he heard from the prophet, that is, from the Spirit of God who was in the prophet: *Your sin is forgiven you*[^23]. He made manifest to him the uncertain and hidden things of his wisdom.

**12.** [v. 9] *You will sprinkle me,* he says, *with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed*[^24]. We know hyssop to be a humble herb, but medicinal: it is said to cling by its roots to a rock. Hence in the mystery the likeness of cleansing the heart is taken. Lay hold, you too, of the root of love, your rock: be humble in your humble God, that you may be exalted in your glorified God: You shall be sprinkled with hyssop, the humility of Christ will cleanse you. Do not despise the herb, attend to the force of the medicine. I will say something too which we are wont to hear from physicians, or to experience in the sick. They say hyssop is fit for purging the lungs. In the lung pride is wont to be marked: for there is the inflation, there the panting. It was said of Saul the persecutor, as of Saul the proud, that he was going to bind the Christians breathing slaughter: he panted slaughter, panted blood, the lung not yet purged. Hear him here too humbled, because purged with hyssop: *You shall sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; you shall wash me,* that is, you shall cleanse me: *and I shall be made whiter than snow. Even if,* he says, *your sins be like scarlet, I will make them white as snow*[^25]. Of such God displays to himself a garment without spot and wrinkle. Accordingly his garment on the mountain, which shone white as snow, signified the Church cleansed of every spot of sin.

**13.** [v. 10] But where is the humility from the hyssop? Hear the following: *To my hearing you shall give exultation and gladness, and the humbled bones shall exult*[^26]. *To my hearing,* he says, *you shall give exultation and gladness*: I shall rejoice by hearing you, not by speaking against you. You have sinned, why do you defend yourself? You wish to speak: suffer, hear, yield to the divine voices, lest you be perturbed and more wounded: it was committed, let it not be defended; let it come to confession, not to defense. You apply yourself as the defender of your sin, you are conquered: you have not employed an innocent patron, your defense is not useful to you. For who are you who defend yourself? You are fit to accuse yourself. Do not say either: I did nothing; or: What great thing did I do; or: Others did it too. If, by committing sin, you say you did nothing, you will be nothing, you will receive nothing; God is ready to give pardon, you shut it against yourself; he is ready to give, do not set against him the bar of defense, but open the bosom of confession. *To my hearing you shall give exultation and gladness.* May he himself grant that I say what I feel. Happier are those who hear, than those who speak. For he who learns is humble: but he who teaches labors that he be not proud, lest the affection of badly pleasing creep in, lest he displease God who wishes to please men. Great is the trembling in the teacher, my brothers, great is our trembling in these voices of ours. Believe our heart what you cannot see: he himself knows who is gentle to us, who is favorable to us, with how great trembling under him we speak to you. But when we hear him himself within suggesting and teaching something, we are secure, secure we rejoice: for we are under a master, we seek his glory, we praise him teaching: his truth delights us within, where no one makes or hears a din: there this man said his gladness and his exultation to be. *To my hearing,* he says, *you shall give exultation and gladness.* And therefore, because humble, he hears. He who hears, who truly hears and well hears, hears humbly: for the glory is in him from whom he hears what he hears. After he said: *To my hearing you shall give exultation and gladness;* at once he showed what hearing does: *The humbled bones shall exult.* The bones are humbled, the bones of the hearer have no haughtiness, no swelling, which he who speaks scarcely overcomes in himself. Hence too that humble great one, than whom among those born of women no greater arose, he who so humbled himself that he said himself unworthy to loose the strap of the sandal of his Lord, John the Baptist, giving glory to his master and therefore his friend, said; when Christ was thought to be, and from this he could grow proud, and stretch himself out: for he himself had not said himself to be the Christ; but he could receive the error of men thinking this, willing of their own to confer this honor: but he rejected the false honor that he might find the true glory: and see the humility from hearing: he says: *He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him.* He made himself standing and hearing, not falling and speaking: *He stands,* he says, *and hears him.* You have heard the hearing; where is the exultation and gladness? At once it follows: *He stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice*[^27]. *To my hearing you shall give exultation and gladness, and the humbled bones shall exult.*

**14.** [v. 11] *Turn away your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities*[^28]. For now the humbled bones exult, now, cleansed with hyssop, I have been made humble. *Turn away your face,* not from me, but *from my sins.* For elsewhere praying he says: *Turn not away your face from me*[^29]. He who does not wish the face of God to be turned away from himself, wishes the face of God to be turned away from his sins. For the sin from which God does not turn himself away, he attends to: if he attends to it, he takes notice of it. *Turn away your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities.* He is busy about that great sin: he presumes more, he wishes all his iniquities to be blotted out; he presumes on the hand of the physician, on that great mercy which at the beginning of the psalm he called upon: *Blot out all my iniquities.* God turns away his face, and so blots out; by turning away his face he blots out the sins, by attending to them he writes them. You have heard him blotting out by turning away, hear what he does by attending. *But the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil things, to cut off their memory from the earth*[^30]: not by blotting out their sins. But what does this man ask here? *Turn away your face from my sins.* He asks well. For he himself does not turn his face away from his sins, saying: *Because I know my sin.* Deservedly you ask, and well you ask that God turn his face from your sin, if you do not turn your face from it: but if you set your sin on your back, God there sets his face. Do you turn your sin before your face, if you wish God to turn his face from it; and so secure you ask, and he hears.

**15.** [v. 12] *Create in me a clean heart, O God. Create,* he wished to say, not as though establish something new: but because the penitent prayed, who had committed something whereby, before he committed it, he was more innocent, he showed how he said: *Create. And renew a right spirit in my inward parts.* By my deed, he says, the rectitude of my spirit had grown old and crooked. For he says from another psalm: *They made my soul crooked*[^31]. And when a man makes himself prone to earthly concupiscences, he is in a way bent down; but when he is lifted up to the supernal things, his heart is made right, so that God is good to him. For how good is God to the upright of heart! Accordingly, brothers, hear. Sometimes God, on whom he has mercy in the future world, rebukes him for the sin in this world. For to David himself, to whom it had already been said through the prophet: *Your sin is forgiven*, certain things happened which God had threatened on account of that very sin. For his son Absalom waged a bloody war against him, and in many things humbled his father. He walked in grief, in the tribulation of his humbling, so subject to God, that, attributing all just things to him, he confessed that he suffered nothing undeservedly, having now a right heart to which God was not displeasing. He patiently heard a certain insulting one casting hard curses to his very face, from the opposite party one of the soldiers who were with his impious son. And when that one was casting curses at the king, one of the companions of David, angry, wished to go and strike him; but he is forbidden by David. And how is he forbidden? So that he says: *God sent him, to curse me*[^32]. Acknowledging his fault, he embraced his punishment, seeking glory not his own; praising the Lord in the good he had, praising the Lord in what he suffered, blessing the Lord at all times, his praise always in his mouth. Such are all the upright of heart: not those perverse ones, who think themselves upright, and God perverse; who, when they do something evil, rejoice; when they suffer something evil, blaspheme; moreover set in tribulation and the scourge, they say from a distorted heart: God, what have I done to you? Truly because they have done nothing to God; for they have done all things to themselves. *And renew a right spirit in my inward parts.*

**16.** [v. 13] *Do not cast me away from your face. Turn away your face from my sins: and do not cast me away from your face*[^33]. Whose face he fears, his face he calls upon. *Do not cast me away from your face: and your Holy Spirit do not take away from me.* For the Holy Spirit is in the one confessing. It now pertains to the gift of the Holy Spirit, that what you did displeases you. To the unclean spirit sins are pleasing, to the holy displeasing. Although, then, you still beg pardon, yet from the other side, because the evil which you committed displeases you, you are joined to God: for this both displeases you, which displeases him too. Now you two are to assail your fever, you and the physician. Because, then, there cannot be confession of sin and punishment of sin in a man from himself; when anyone is angry at himself and is displeasing to himself, it is not without the gift of the Holy Spirit. Nor does he say, give me your Holy Spirit, but, *do not take it away. And your Holy Spirit do not take away from me.*

**17.** [v. 14] *Render to me the exultation of your salvation. Render* the one I had, which by sinning I had lost: *Render to me the exultation of your salvation*[^34]: of your Christ, of course. For who could be healed without him? Because even before he was born of Mary, *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^35]: and so by the holy fathers the future dispensation of the flesh to be assumed was believed, as it is believed by us to have been done. The times were varied, not the faith. *Render to me the exultation of your salvation: and with a principal spirit confirm me.* Some have understood here the Trinity in God, God himself, apart from the dispensation of the flesh: since it is written: *God is a spirit*[^36]. For that which is not a body, and yet is, it seems to remain that it be a spirit. Some, therefore, understand the Trinity here named; in the *right spirit* the Son, in the *holy spirit* the Holy Spirit, in the *principal spirit* the Father. Whether, then, this be so, or whether he wished the right spirit of the man himself to be understood, saying: *Renew a right spirit in my inward parts,* which I curved and distorted by sinning, so that now the Holy Spirit himself is the principal spirit, whom he both did not wish to be taken from himself, and by whom he wished to be confirmed; it is not any heretical sentence.

**18.** [v. 15] But see what he adds: *With a principal spirit,* he says, *confirm me*[^37]. In what *confirm*? Because you have pardoned me, because I am secure that what you have granted is not imputed to me, from this made secure, and confirmed by this grace, I shall not be ungrateful. For what shall I do? *I will teach the iniquitous your ways*[^38]. I will teach the iniquitous out of an iniquitous one; that is, I who was iniquitous too, now not iniquitous, the Holy Spirit not taken from me, and confirmed with a principal spirit, *I will teach the iniquitous your ways.* What ways will you teach the iniquitous? *And the impious shall be converted to you.* If the sin of David is reckoned to impiety, let not the impious despair of themselves, because God spared an impious man; but if they are converted to him, if they learn his ways: but if the deed of David is not reckoned to impiety, but that is properly called impiety, to apostatize from God, not to worship the one God, either never to have worshiped him, or to have forsaken him whom he worshiped, it avails to the sum what he says: *And the impious shall be converted to you.* You are so full of the fat of mercy, that to those converted to you, not only any sinners, but even the impious, one ought not to despair. *And the impious shall be converted to you.* For what? That, believing in him who justifies the impious, their faith may be reckoned to justice[^39].

**19.** [v. 16] *Deliver me from bloods, O God, the God of my salvation*[^40]. The Latin interpreter expressed by a word less Latin, yet the property from the Greek. For we all know that in Latin one does not say "bloods" (sanguines), nor "bloods" (sanguina); yet because the Greek set it so in the plural number, not without cause, except that he found this in the first Hebrew tongue, the godly interpreter preferred to say something less in Latin, than less in property. Why, then, did he say in the plural: *from bloods*? By "in the many bloods," as in the origin of the flesh of sin, he wished many sins to be understood. Looking to those very sins, which come from the corruption of flesh and blood, the Apostle says: *Flesh and blood shall not possess the kingdom of God*[^41]. For surely, according to the true faith of the same apostle, this flesh shall rise again, and shall itself merit incorruption, he himself saying: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality*[^42]. Because, then, this corruption is from sin, sins are called by its name: just as the tongue is called both that piece of flesh and the member which is moved in the mouth, when we distinguish words, and the tongue what is done through the tongue, as we say one tongue Greek, another Latin: for the flesh is not different, but the sound. As, then, the speech which is done through the tongue is called tongue: so too the iniquity which is done through blood is called blood. Attending, then, to his many iniquities, and that above: *And blot out all my iniquities*; and attributing them to the corruption of flesh and blood: *Free me,* he says, *from bloods*: this is: Free me from iniquities, cleanse me from all corruption. For he desires incorruption who says: *Free me from bloods: because flesh and blood shall not possess the kingdom of God, nor corruption incorruption. Deliver me from bloods, O God, the God of my salvation.* He shows that when salvation has been made perfect in this body, there will not be in it the corruption which is understood by the name of flesh and blood: for that is the perfect health of the body. For now how is that healthy which slips, which is in need, which has a certain perpetual sickness of hunger and thirst? These then will not be: because food is for the belly, and the belly for foods. But God will destroy both this and these[^43]. The form of the body will be perfect from God, death being swallowed up in victory, no corruption remaining, no failing creeping in, changed by no ages, wearied by no labor that it need be propped by food, and refreshed by any nourishment. But we shall not be without food and drink: he himself will be our food and our drink. That food alone refreshes and does not fail. *Deliver me from bloods, O God, the God of my salvation.* For now we are already in salvation itself. Hear the Apostle: *For in hope we have been saved.* And see that he was speaking of the very salvation of the body: *In ourselves,* he says, *we groan, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body. For in hope we have been saved. But hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, by patience we await it*[^44]. He who perseveres to the end, that is that patience: he shall be saved, that is the salvation which we do not yet have, but shall have. It is not yet the reality, but a sure hope. *And my tongue shall exult your justice.*

**20.** [v. 17] *O Lord, you will open my lips, and my mouth shall announce your praise*[^45]. *Your praise,* because I was created; *your praise,* because, sinning, I was not forsaken; *your praise,* because I was admonished to confess; *your praise,* because I was cleansed that I might be secure. *You will open my lips, and my mouth shall announce your praise.*

**21.** [vv. 18-19] *Because if you had wished sacrifice, I would surely have given it*[^46]. David was in that time, when the sacrifices of animal victims were offered to God, and he saw these future times. Do we not recognize ourselves in these voices? Those sacrifices were figurative, foreannouncing one salutary sacrifice. Nor are we dismissed without a sacrifice, which we may offer to God. For hear what he says, taking care for his sin, and wishing the evil he did to be forgiven him: *If you had wished,* he says, *sacrifice, I would surely have given it. With holocausts you will not be delighted.* Shall we, then, offer nothing? shall we so come to God? And whence shall we placate him? Offer; you surely have in yourself what you may offer. Do not buy frankincense from outside, but say: *In me, O God, are your vows, which I will render of praise to you*[^47]. Do not seek from outside cattle to slay, you have in yourself what you may kill. *A sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit: a contrite and humbled heart God does not spurn.* He plainly spurns the bull, the goat, the ram: it is no longer the time that these be offered. They were offered when they signified something, when they promised something; the promised things having come, the promises were taken away. *A contrite and humbled heart God does not spurn.* You know that God is high: if you make yourself high, he will go far from you; if you humble yourself, he will draw near to you.

**22.** [v. 20] See who this is: one seemed to pray, David; see here our image and the type of the Church. *Deal kindly, O Lord, in your good will with Zion*[^48]. With this Zion deal kindly. What is Zion? The holy city. What is the holy city? Which cannot be hidden, set on a mountain. Zion in watching, because it watches for something which it hopes. For Zion is interpreted Watching, and Jerusalem Vision of peace. You recognize yourselves, then, in Zion and in Jerusalem, if certain you await a future hope, and if you have peace with God. *And let the walls of Jerusalem be built. Deal kindly, O Lord, in your good will with Zion, and let the walls of Jerusalem be built*: For let not Zion attribute to itself any merits of its own: do you with it deal kindly. *Let the walls of Jerusalem be built*: let the fortifications of our immortality be constructed, in faith, and hope, and love.

**23.** [v. 21] *Then you will accept the sacrifice of justice*[^49]: but now the sacrifice for iniquity, a troubled spirit and a humbled heart; then the sacrifice of justice, praises alone. For blessed are they who dwell in your house, forever and ever they shall praise you: for this is the sacrifice of justice. *Oblations and holocausts.* What are *holocausts*? Wholly consumed by fire. When the whole beast was set on the altar to be consumed by fire, it was called a holocaust. Let the divine fire consume us wholly, and let that fervor seize us wholly. What fervor? *And there is no one who hides himself from its heat*[^50]. What fervor? Of which the Apostle says, *Fervent in spirit*[^51]. Let not only our soul be consumed by that divine fire of wisdom, but also our body, that it may there merit immortality; so let the holocaust be lifted up, that death may be swallowed up in victory. *Oblations and holocausts. Then they shall set calves upon your altar.* Whence *calves*? What will he choose there? The innocence of the new age, or necks free from the yoke of the law?

**24.** The psalm, in the name of Christ, even if perhaps not as we wish, yet as we were able, has been finished. It remains to address you in a few words, brothers, on account of the many evils among which we live. For neither, living among human affairs, can we migrate from human affairs. We must live with tolerance among the bad: because, when we were bad, the good lived with tolerance among us. Not forgetting what we were, we shall not despair of those who now are what we were. Yet, dearly beloved, amid so great a diversity of morals and so detestable a corruption, rule your houses, rule your sons, rule your households. As it pertains to us to speak to you in the Church, so it pertains to you to act in your houses, that you may render a good account of those who are subject to you. God loves discipline. But it is a perverse and false innocence, to loosen the reins to sins. Very uselessly, very perniciously the son perceives the leniency of the father, so that afterward he perceives the severity of God: and this not alone, but with his dissolute father. For what? If he himself does not sin, and does not do what his son does, ought he therefore not to forbid his son that very wickedness? Or is it perhaps that it may seem to his son that his father too would do such things, had he not grown old? The sin which does not displease you in your son, delights you; but age has deserted you, not cupidity. Above all, my brothers, attend to your faithful sons, for whom you stood surety that they be baptized. But perhaps the bad son neglects either the warnings of the father, or his rebuke, or his severity: do you fulfill your person; God exacts his from him.
