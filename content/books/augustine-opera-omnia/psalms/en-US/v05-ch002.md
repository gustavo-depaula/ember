# Exposition of Psalm 100

**1.** [v. 1.] What this hundredth psalm has in its first verse, that we ought to seek in its whole body. *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* Let no one flatter himself toward impunity over the mercy of God; for there is judgment too: and let no one changed for the better shudder at the judgment of God; for mercy goes before. For when men judge, sometimes, overcome by mercy, they act against justice; and there seems to be mercy in them and no judgment: but sometimes, wishing to keep rigid judgment, they lose mercy. But God neither in the goodness of mercy loses the severity of judgment, nor in judging with severity lets go the goodness of mercy. See, if we distinguish these two by their times, mercy and judgment—for perhaps not without reason are they set in this very order, so that he did not say, Judgment and mercy, but *mercy and judgment*: if, then, we distinguish these two by their times, perhaps we find that now is the time of mercy, but the time to come is the time of judgment. How is the time of mercy first? Consider it first in God, that you too may imitate the Father in the measure he has granted you. For we do not arrogantly say that we ought to imitate our Father; since the Lord himself, the only Son of God, exhorts us to this, saying, *Be like your heavenly Father.* When he said, *Love your enemies, pray for those who persecute you: that you may be*, he says, *sons of your Father who is in heaven; who makes his sun rise on the good and the bad, and rains on the just and the unjust*[^1]. Behold mercy. When you see the just and the unjust gaze on the same sun, gather the same light, drink from the same springs, be fattened by the same rain, be filled with the same fruits of the earth, alike breathe this air, equally have the goods of the world; do not suppose God to be unjust, who gives these things equally to both the just and the unjust. It is the time of mercy, not yet of judgment. For unless God first spared through mercy, he would not find any to crown through judgment. It is, then, the time of mercy, when the patience of God leads sinners to repentance.

**2.** Hear the Apostle distinguishing each time, and you too distinguish them: *Do you suppose*, he says, *O man who judge those who do such things, and do the same, that you will escape the judgment of God?*[^2] Attend. For he saw himself (but to whom does he say this? For he does not say it to one man, but to such a kind of men), he saw himself committing many evils daily, and yet living, with no evil befalling him; and he supposed that either God was asleep, or did not attend to human affairs, or loved the evil deeds of men. He removes this thought from the heart—but for those who understand well. What then does he say? *Do you suppose, O man who judge those who do such things, and do them, that you will escape the judgment of God?* And as if he were saying, Why do I commit such evils daily, and no evil befalls me? he goes on to show him the time of mercy: *Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and patience and long-suffering?* And indeed, because he was despising this; but he made him anxious. *Do you not know*, he says, *that the kindness of God leads you to repentance?*[^3] Behold the time of mercy. But lest he should suppose this would always be so, how did he go on to frighten him? *But you* (now hear the time of judgment; you heard the time of mercy, because of *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*): *But you*, he says, *according to your hardness of heart and impenitent heart, are storing up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each one according to his works*[^4]. Behold: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* But he threatened concerning judgment: is the judgment of God therefore only to be feared, and not loved? It is to be feared by the bad because of the punishment, to be loved by the good because of the crown. Because, then, the Apostle frightened the bad in this testimony which I have recalled, hear where he gives hope to the good concerning judgment. He sets himself forward, and speaks, and shows in himself too the time of mercy. For unless he too had found a time of mercy, what sort of man would judgment find him? A blasphemer, a persecutor, an insulter. For so he speaks, and commends the time of mercy in which we now are: *I who was formerly*, he says, *a blasphemer and a persecutor and an insulter; but I obtained mercy.* But perhaps he alone obtained mercy? Hear how he lifts us up: *That in me*, he says, *Christ Jesus might show forth all his long-suffering, for the instruction of those who are going to believe in him unto eternal life*[^5]. What is it, *that he might show forth in me his long-suffering?* That every sinner and criminal might see that Paul received pardon, and not despair of himself. Behold, he showed himself, he lifted up others too. Where? In the time of mercy. Hear in the time of judgment what he says about the good, again speaking of himself and of others. First he obtained mercy. Why? Because he was a blasphemer and a persecutor and an insulter. The Lord came to give to Paul, not to repay. For if he had wished to repay, what would he find to repay a sinner, except punishment and torment? He did not wish to repay punishment, but he gave grace. Hear that the one to whom he gave holds the Lord even as debtor. He found him a giver in the time of mercy; he holds him a debtor in the time of judgment. See how he says this. *For I am already being sacrificed*, he said, *and the time of my release is near. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith.* This, in the time of mercy; hear about judgment. *For the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge.* He did not say, will give; but, *will render.* When he was giving, he was merciful; when he renders, he will be judge: because, *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* But by forgiving sins, he made himself debtor of the crown: there, *I obtained mercy.* Merciful, then, was the Lord first; but here *he will render to me* the crown *of righteousness.* On what ground will he render? Because *he is a just judge.* Why a just judge? Because *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith.* Being just, therefore, he can only crown these things. For he found these things to crown: but before, what had he found? *I who was formerly a blasphemer and a persecutor.* These things he forgave, those he will crown; he forgave these in the time of mercy, he will crown those in the time of judgment: because, *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* But did Paul alone deserve this? For this is what I had said, that as in that testimony he frightened, so in this he lifted up. When he had said, *The Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge: not only to me*, he says, *but to all who love his appearing and his kingdom*[^6].

**3.** Therefore, brothers, since we have the time of mercy, let us not flatter ourselves, let us not let ourselves go, let us not say: God always spares. See, I did it yesterday, God spared; I do it today too, and God spares; I will do it tomorrow too, because God spares. You attend to mercy, and do not fear judgment. If you wish to sing mercy and judgment, understand that he spares for this reason, that you may be corrected, not that you may remain in malignity. Do not store up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God. For in the time of mercy it is said in another psalm: *But to the sinner God said: Why do you declare my statutes, and take up my covenant through your mouth? But you have hated discipline, and cast my words behind you: if you saw a thief, you ran with him, and you placed your portion with adulterers; sitting, you slandered your brother, and against your mother's son you placed a stumbling block. These things you did, and I was silent.* See the time of mercy. What is it, *I was silent?* Is it: I did not rebuke? No, but: I did not judge. For how is he silent who daily cries out in the Scriptures, in the Gospel, in his preachers? I was silent from punishment, not from the word. *These things you did, and I was silent.* And because God was silent, that is, did not take vengeance, what did the sinner say to himself in his heart? Hear: *You suspected*, he says, *iniquity, that I would be like you.* That is, it is too little that you were such; you supposed me to be such too. And when he had shown him the time of mercy, he frightened him about the time of judgment: *I will rebuke you, and set you before your face*[^7]. You place yourself behind you, I will place you before you. For everyone who does not wish to see his own sins places himself behind his back, and keenly attends to the sins of others, not through diligence, but through envy; not wishing to heal, but to accuse: but he forgets himself. Whence the Lord says to such: *You see the speck in your brother's eye, and you do not see the beam in your own eye*[^8]. Because, then, mercy and judgment are sung to us, let us too, doing mercy, await judgment with confidence: and let us be in his body, and sing these things ourselves. For Christ sings this: if the head alone sings, this canticle is from the Lord, it does not pertain to us; but if the whole Christ, that is, the head and his body, then be among his members, cling to him through faith, and through hope, and through love; and in him you sing, in him you exult: because he too labors in you, thirsts in you, hungers and is afflicted in you. He still dies in you, and you have already risen in him. For if he did not die in you, he would not wish to be spared, in you, by the persecutor, when he said, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^9] Therefore, my brothers, Christ sings; but how, you know: We have constantly commended Christ to you; and I know that these things are not raw to you. The Lord Christ is the Word of God, through whom all things were made. This Word, that it might redeem us, was made flesh, and dwelt among us: the man became God over all things, the Son of God equal to the Father; for this he became man, that God-man might be mediator between men and God, and might reconcile those placed far off, and join together those separated, and call back those estranged, and lead back those who were strangers; for this he became man. He became, then, the head of the Church, and he has a body and members too. Seek his members: now they groan throughout the whole world: then they will rejoice in the end, in the crown of righteousness, of which Paul says, *which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge*[^10]. And now too, then, let us sing in hope, all gathered into one. For having put on Christ, we are Christ with our head; since indeed we are the seed of Abraham. The Apostle says this. For I said, We are Christ. The Apostle says, *Therefore you are the seed of Abraham, heirs according to the promise.* You are the seed of Abraham: let us see whether Christ is the seed of Abraham. *In your seed shall all the nations be blessed. He does not say: In seeds, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^11]. And to us he says, *Therefore you are the seed of Abraham.* It is clear that we pertain to Christ; and that, being his members and his body, we are with our head one man. Let us sing, then: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.*

**4.** [v. 2.] *I will sing psalms, and I will understand in the immaculate way, when you will come to me.* Unless you are in the immaculate way, you cannot sing psalms, nor understand. If you wish to understand, sing psalms in the immaculate way, that is, work in cheerfulness for your God. What is the immaculate way? Hear what follows: *I walked in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house.* This immaculate way began from innocence, and in that very thing it is also reached. Why do you seek many words? Be innocent, and you have accomplished righteousness. But what is it to be innocent? For a man does harm in two ways, as far as it is in him; either by making someone wretched, or by deserting the wretched: for you too do not wish to be made wretched by another, and do not wish to be deserted by another, if you should be wretched. Who is it that makes others wretched? One who inflicts violence or treachery, seizes others' goods, oppresses the poor, steals, solicits others' marriages, is slanderous, wishes to inflict on men something for them to grieve over, out of zeal for malevolence. Who is it that deserts the wretched? One who sees a needy person lacking some help, and, though he has the means to provide it, scorns, despises, estranges his heart. But if he were already altogether such as to have no need of any mercy, he would be proud if he deserted the wretched: he is still set in the tribulation of the flesh, not knowing what may befall him tomorrow, and yet he despises the tears of the wretched; he is not innocent. But who is innocent? One who, while he does no harm to another, does no harm to himself either. For one who does harm even to himself is not innocent. Someone says: See, I have not taken from anyone, nor oppressed anyone: from what is mine, from my just labor, I will treat myself well; I wish to have a feast prepared, I wish to spend as much as delights me, to drink with whom I will as much as delights me; from whom have I taken anything? whom have I oppressed? who has complained of me? He seems innocent. But if he corrupts himself, if he overturns the temple of God in himself, what do you expect, that he should show mercy to others and spare the wretched? Can one who is cruel to himself be merciful to another? The whole of righteousness, then, is reduced to the one word of innocence. *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^12]. When he loved iniquity, he supposed that he was harming others. But see whether he was harming others: *He who loves iniquity*, he says, *hates his own soul.* First, then, he harms himself who wishes to harm others; nor does he walk, because there is no room. For all malice suffers from narrows: innocence alone is broad, where one may walk. *I walked in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house.* By the midst of his house he means either the Church itself—for Christ walks in it—or his own heart; for our inner house is our heart: so that he has explained what he said above, *In the innocence of my heart.* What is the innocence of his heart? The midst of his house. Whoever has this house bad is driven out of it. For whoever is pressed in heart by a bad conscience, just as one goes out of his house because of a leak, or because of smoke, and does not let himself dwell there, so one who does not have a quiet heart cannot willingly dwell in his own heart. Such men go outside themselves by the bent of the mind, and take delight in the things that are outside, around the body; they seek quiet in trifles, in spectacles, in luxuries, in all evils. Why do they wish it to be well with them outside? Because it is not well with them within, so as to rejoice in conscience. Therefore the Lord, when he had healed the paralytic, said, *Take up your bed, and go into your house*[^13]. Let the soul do this, which is as it were loosened by paralysis: let it be braced up in the members of good work, that it may work well, let it take up its bed, govern its body; let it now go into its house, enter its conscience: it will now find it broad, where it may walk, and sing psalms, and understand.

**5.** [vv. 3, 4.] *I did not set before my eyes an evil thing.* What is it, *I did not set before my eyes an evil thing?* I did not love it. For it is commonly said, as you know, of a man who is loved by someone: He has him before his eyes; and one who is scorned is wont to complain thus: He does not have me before his eyes. What then is it, to have before the eyes? To love. What is it, not to love? Not to dwell there in heart. He said, then, *I did not set before my eyes an evil thing*: I did not love an evil thing. And he explains that evil thing: *Those who commit transgression I have hated.* Attend, my brothers. If you walk with Christ in the midst of his house, that is, if you either rest well in your own heart, or take a good road in the very Church on the immaculate way; you ought to hate as transgressors not only those who are outside, but whoever you find within. Who are the transgressors? Those who hate the law of God; those who hear it and do not do it are called transgressors. Hate those who commit transgression, drive them away from you. But you ought to hate transgressors, not men. One man a transgressor—see that he has two names, man and transgressor: God made the man, he himself made himself a transgressor; love in him what God made, persecute in him what he made for himself. For when you have persecuted his transgression, you slay what the man made, and what God made is set free. *Those who commit transgression I have hated.*

**6.** *A depraved heart has not clung to me.* What is a depraved heart? A crooked heart. What is a crooked heart? A heart that is not straight. What is a heart that is not straight? See what a straight heart is, and there you find what a heart that is not straight is. The heart of a man is called straight who does not himself refuse all that God wills. Attend. Someone prays that something or other may not happen; he prays, and he is not forbidden. Let him ask as much as he can: but if something happens against his own will, let him subject himself to the will of God, let him not resist the great will. For the Lord himself explains this thus: showing our weakness in himself, when he was about to suffer, he said, *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^14]. For he did not truly fear death, who had power to lay down his soul, and power to take it up again. And Paul the apostle, his soldier, his servant, cries out: *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; for the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge*[^15]. He exults because he is about to die; and his Lord and his Commander is sorrowful because he is about to die? Is the servant, then, better than the Lord? And where is that which the Lord himself says: *It is enough for the servant that he be as his lord; it is enough for the disciple that he be as his master?*[^16] Behold, Paul is brave at his coming death, and the Lord is sorrowful? *I desired*, he says, *to be dissolved, and to be with Christ*[^17]. He rejoices because he is dissolved, that he may be with Christ; and Christ himself is sorrowful, with whom this man rejoices that he will be? But what was that voice, except the sound of our weakness? Many who are still weak are saddened by coming death: but let them have a straight heart; let them avoid death, as far as they can; but if they cannot, let them say what the Lord himself said, not for his own sake, but for ours. For what did he say? *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me.* Behold, you have the human will expressed; now see the straight heart: *Yet not what I will, but what you will, Father*[^18]. If, then, a straight heart follows God, a depraved heart resists God. Let something adverse befall him, he cries out: God, what have I done to you? what have I committed? what have I sinned? He wants himself to seem just, God unjust. What is so depraved? It is too little that he is crooked; he even thinks the rule crooked. Correct yourself, and you find straight the one from whom you have made yourself crooked. He acts justly, you unjustly: and therefore you are perverse, because you call a man just, and God unjust. What man do you call just? Yourself. For when you say, What have I done to you, you think yourself just. But let God answer you: You speak truly, you have done nothing to me; for you have done all to yourself. For if you had done anything to me, you would have done good. For whatever is done well is done to me; because it is done from my command: but whatever evil is done is done to yourself, not done to me; because the bad man does nothing except to himself, since this I do not command. When you see such men, brothers, rebuke them, reprove them, correct them: and if you cannot rebuke or correct, do not consent; that you may be able to say, *A depraved heart has not clung to me.*

**7.** *When the malicious one turned aside from me, I did not know him.* What is it, *I did not know him?* I did not approve, I did not praise, he did not please me. For we find that in the Scriptures to know is sometimes said for what is to please us. For what is hidden from God, brothers? Does he know the just, and not know the unjust? What do you think that he does not know? I do not say, what you do; but, what do you think that he does not know? I do not say, what you think; but, what are you going to think that he has not seen beforehand? God knows all things, then: and yet in the end, that is, in the judgment after mercy, he says of some: *On that day many will come, and will say, Lord, Lord, in your name we cast out demons, in your name we did many mighty works, in your name we ate and drank; and I will say to them: Depart from me, workers of iniquity; I do not know you.*[^19] Does he not know someone? But what is it, *I do not know you?* In my rule I do not recognize you. For I know the rule of my righteousness: you do not fit it, you have turned aside from it, you are distorted. So here too he said, *I did not know him. When the malicious one turned aside from me, I did not know him.* What is it, *I did not know him?* Is it perhaps that the malicious one, when he meets the just man in a narrow lane, says to himself what is written in the Wisdom of Solomon, *He is grievous to us even to look upon*[^20], and changes his way lest he see whom he does not wish to see? But how many malicious men there are whom we see, and who see us, and who not only do not turn aside from us, but run to us, and sometimes desire to have their iniquities fulfilled through us? This often happens to us. How then do they turn aside? He turns aside from you who is unlike you. What is it, he turns aside from you? He does not follow you. What is it, he does not follow you? He does not imitate you. Therefore: *When the malicious one turned aside from me*, that is, when the malicious one was unlike me, and was unwilling to imitate my ways, was unwilling to live as a malicious man in the way I set myself before him for imitation; *I did not know him.* What is it, *I did not know him?* Not that I did not know him, but that I did not approve him.

**8.** [v. 5.] *The one who slandered his neighbor secretly, him I persecuted.* Behold a good persecutor, not of the man, but of the sin. *With the proud eye and the insatiable heart, with this one I did not eat.* What is it, *I did not eat?* I did not eat with him; for to feed is to eat. Why then do we find the Lord himself first eating with the proud? Not with those publicans and sinners, for they were humble: for they recognized their own sickness, and sought the physician. With the very proud Pharisees we find him eating. For a certain proud man had invited him: he is the one who was displeased that a sinful woman, who was notorious in the city, came to the feet of the Lord; and he said in his heart (for such was the cleanliness of the Pharisees that no sinner might touch them; if any unclean person had touched them even slightly, they shuddered, lest the unclean touch should as it were make them unclean. But when that sinful woman, who was notorious in the city, came to weep at the feet of the Lord, that man, when he saw her, said in his heart): *If this man were a prophet, he would know what kind of woman has come to his feet.* How did he know that Christ did not know, except that he suspected him of not knowing, because he did not push her away from himself? Because if he were the man, he would push her away from himself. But the Lord not only knew that woman, the sinner, but the physician also saw the incurable wounds of that proud man. For when he had heard him thinking, in order to show him to be proud, he said: *Simon, I have something to say to you: There were two debtors to a certain moneylender; one owed him fifty denarii, the other five hundred; since they had nothing to repay with, he forgave them both: which one loved him more?* And the man pronounced sentence against himself, truth wringing the confession from him: *I believe, Lord, the one to whom he forgave more. And turning to the woman, he said to Simon: Do you see this woman? I entered your house; you gave me no water for my feet: but she has washed my feet with her tears*[^21]; and the rest you know. There is no need to dwell longer on the other matters for which we brought this in as testimony. This Pharisee was proud, and the Lord was eating with him; what then is it that he says, *With the proud eye and the insatiable heart, with this one I did not eat?* What is it, *I did not eat?* I did not eat with him. How does he set before us these things which he himself did not do? He exhorts us to his imitation: we see that he feasted with the proud; how does he forbid us to feast with them? We indeed, brothers, for the sake of some rebuke, hold ourselves back even from our brothers, and do not feast with them, that they may be corrected. Let us rather feast with strangers, with pagans, than with those who cling to us, if we see them living badly, that they may blush and be corrected; as the Apostle says, *If anyone does not obey our word by the letter, note that man, and do not associate with him; and do not regard him as an enemy, but rebuke him as a brother*[^22]. We do this very often for the sake of the medicine; and yet we often eat with many strangers and with the impious.

**9.** What is this that he says, *With the proud eye and the insatiable heart, with this one I did not eat?* The pious heart has its own feasts, the proud heart has its own feasts: for it is on account of the very foods of the proud heart that he said, *with the insatiable heart.* On what is the proud heart fed? If he is proud, he is envious: it cannot be otherwise. Pride is the mother of envy: it can only beget her, and always be with her. Every proud man, then, is envious: if he is envious, he feeds on others' evils. Whence the Apostle says, *If you bite and devour one another; see that you are not consumed by one another*[^23]. You see them eating, then; do not feed with them, flee such a banquet; for they are not satisfied by rejoicing over others' evils, because they are of an insatiable heart. Beware lest you be caught in their feasts, in the snare of the devil. On such foods the Jews fed when they crucified the Lord: but because they fed as if on the Lord's punishment. (For we too feed on the Lord's cross, because we eat his body.) For they said, when they saw him hanging on the cross, insulting, because they were of an insatiable heart; they said, then, *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross; he saved others, he cannot save himself*[^24]. They were fed on the food of their cruelty, and he was fed on the food of his mercy. *Father*, he said, *forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^25]. They, then, had one feast, he another. But hear what is said about the table of the proud: *Let their table become before them a snare, and a recompense, and a stumbling block*[^26]. They were fed, they were caught. For just as birds are fed at the snare, or fish at the hook, but are caught; so were they too. The impious, then, have their feasts, and the pious too have their feasts. Hear the feasts of the pious: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied*[^27]. If, then, the pious man is fed on the food of righteousness, and the impious on the food of pride; it is no wonder if he is of an insatiable heart. He is fed on the food of iniquity: do not be fed on the food of iniquity, and the proud of eye and insatiable of heart does not feed with you.

**10.** [v. 6.] And on what were you fed? and what delighted you, where that man did not feed with you? *My eyes*, he says, *are upon the faithful of the earth, that these may sit with me.* The Lord says, *My eyes are upon the faithful of the earth, that these may sit with me*: that is, that they may sit with me. How would they sit? *You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^28]. The faithful of the earth judge, to whom it is said, *Do you not know that we shall judge angels?*[^29] *My eyes are upon the faithful of the earth, that these may sit with me. He who walked in the immaculate way, this one ministered to me. To me*, he says, not to himself. For many minister the Gospel, but minister to themselves; because they seek their own things, not what is Jesus Christ's. What is it to minister to Christ? To seek the things that are Christ's. And indeed, when the bad announce the Gospel, others are saved, while they themselves are punished. For it is said, *Do what they say; do not do what they do*[^30]. Do not fear, then, when you hear the Gospel from a bad man. Woe to him who ministers to himself, that is, who seeks his own things there: you, receive Christ's. *He who walked in the immaculate way, this one ministered to me.*

**11.** [v. 7.] *He who works pride has not dwelt in the midst of my house.* Refer this to that house, that is, to the heart. He who works pride did not dwell in my heart: no such man dwelt in my heart; for he sprang back from there. No one dwelt in my heart but the meek and the quiet; the proud did not dwell there. For the unjust does not dwell in the heart of the just. Let the just man be removed from you by I know not how many thousands of miles and dwellings; you dwell together, if you have one heart. *He who works pride has not dwelt in the midst of my house. He who speaks unjust things has not prospered in the sight of my eyes.* This is the immaculate way, where we understand when the Lord comes to us.

**12.** [v. 8.] *In the mornings I slew all the sinners of the earth.* This is obscure; deign to attend: now is the end of the psalm. *In the mornings I slew all the sinners of the earth.* Why? *That I might destroy out of the city of the Lord all who work iniquity.* There are, then, in the city of the Lord those who work iniquity, and they are as it were spared for now. Why? Because it is the time of mercy: but the time of judgment will come too; for so the psalm began: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* He has already enumerated above that none clung to him but the good. To the bad he did not cling, nor was he delighted by the feasts of the iniquity of those who ministered to themselves, not to the Lord, that is, who sought their own things. And as if it were said to him: And why have you put up with such men in your city for so long a time? It is the time of mercy, he says. What is the time of mercy? The judgment is not yet revealed: it is night; the day will appear, the judgment will appear. Hear the Apostle: *Therefore do not judge anything before the time.* What is it, *before the time?* Before the day. Hear that he said before the day: *Until the Lord comes, and lights up the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart; and then there will be praise for each one from God*[^31]. For now, as long as I do not see your heart and you do not see mine, it is night. You have asked something of a man; you did not receive it, you think yourself scorned: and perhaps you are not scorned; for you do not see the heart: and you quickly blaspheme; in the night, pardon must be given to one who errs. Some man loves you, and you suppose that he hates you; or he hates you, and you suppose that he loves you: but whichever it be, it is night. Do not fear, presume on Christ, in him have your day: there is no reason for you to think any evil of him, for we are secure, and certain that he cannot be deceived; he loves us. But of one another we are not yet certain. For God knows our love for one another: but as for us, even if we love one another, who sees with what intention these things are done by us? Why does no one see the heart? Because it is night. In this night temptations abound. As if of this very night the psalm said, *You set the darkness, and night was made; in it all the beasts of the forest will pass by: the young lions roaring to seize, and to seek from God their food*[^32]. In the night the young lions seek their food. Who are the young lions? Of the princes and powers of this air, the demons and angels of the devil. How do they seek their food? When they tempt. But because they do not approach unless God has given them power; therefore it is said, *Seeking from God their food.* Job asked to be tempted. What sort of food? A rich one, a fat one, the just man of God, to whom God himself bore witness, saying, *A man without complaint, a true worshiper of God he was*[^33]. He asked for him to be tempted, seeking food from God: and he received him to be tempted, but not to be crushed; to be purified, not to be overthrown; or perhaps not even to be purified, but to be proved. Yet those too who are tempted are sometimes handed over, by their own hidden desert, into the hands of the tempter, because they have perhaps been handed over to their own desires. For the devil harms no one unless he has received power from God. But when? In the night. What is it, in the night? In this present time. But when the night has passed, and the day has come, the bad are sent with him into eternal fire, but the just into eternal life. There will be no tempter there, because there are no young lions there; because the night is over. Therefore the Lord said to his disciples, *This night Satan asked to sift you like wheat; and I prayed for you, Peter, that your faith might not fail*[^34]. What is it, *to sift like wheat?* Just as wheat is not eaten by a man unless it has first been ground, that he may make bread; so the devil eats no one unless he has first overthrown him through tribulation. He grinds, that he may eat: but when you are afflicted, if you remain a grain, do not be disturbed; nothing happens to you. As the oxen tread out the grain, do they enter only into the wheat? They are sent together with the sledge onto the threshing floor. But is the wheat to fear? Far from it. Only the chaff is cut up; the wheat is stripped of what is superfluous, and let the winnowing come, and it will find a pure mass: the one it finds a grain it puts into its barn; and the heap of chaff it will burn with inextinguishable fire.

**13.** Why did I say this? Because we have the day in hope. The day ought to be for us in Christ. For as long as we are among temptations, it is night. In this night God spares sinners, so as not to take them away; he scourges them with temptations, that they may be corrected; he puts up with them in his city. Do we suppose he will always put up with them? If it is always mercy, there is no judgment: but if *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord;* now he spares, then he will judge. But when will he judge? When the night has passed. Therefore he said, *In the mornings I slew all the sinners of the earth.* What is it, *In the mornings?* When the day has now come, the night being over. *In the mornings I slew all the sinners of the earth.* Why does he spare them until morning? Because it was night. What is it, it was night? Because it was the time of sparing: he spared, while the hearts of men were hidden. You see someone living badly, you put up with him: for you do not know what sort he will be, because it is night; whether the one who lives badly today may live well tomorrow; and whether the one who lives well today may be bad tomorrow. For it is night, and God puts up with all, because he is long-suffering. He puts up with them, that sinners may be converted to him. But those who have not corrected themselves in this time of mercy will be slain. And why will they be slain? That they may be scattered out of the city of the Lord, out of the fellowship of Jerusalem, out of the fellowship of the saints, out of the fellowship of the Church. But when will they be slain? *In the mornings.* What is it, *In the mornings?* When the night is over. Why does he now spare? Because it is the time of mercy. Why does he not always spare? Because *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* Brothers, let no one flatter himself: all who work iniquity will be slain; Christ will slay them in the mornings, and will destroy them out of his city. But now, while it is the time of mercy, let them hear him. Everywhere he cries out through the Law, through the Prophets, through the Psalms, through the Epistles, through the Gospels: see that he is not silent, that he spares, that he dispenses mercy; but beware, because judgment is to come.
