# Exposition of Psalm 52

**1.** [v. 1] We undertake to treat this psalm with you, as far as the Lord suggests. The brother bids us to be willing, and prays that we may be able. If perhaps in my haste I leave anything out, it will be filled up in you by the One who deigns to give to us also what we have been able to say. Its title is: *Unto the end, for Maeleth, the understanding of David himself. For Maeleth*, as we find in the Hebrew interpretations of names, seems to mean: For one in travail, or in grief. Who travails and grieves in this age, the faithful recognize, because they are of that company. Christ travails here, Christ grieves here; the head is above, the members below. For one not travailing and grieving would not say: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^1] By his persecution he was travailing, and once converted he made him travail. For he too was afterward enlightened, and grafted into those very members he was persecuting, and, pregnant with the same love, said: *My little children, with whom I am again in travail, until Christ be formed in you*[^2]. For the members of Christ, then, for his body which is the Church, for that one man, that is, for that very unity whose head is above, this psalm is sung. And this man groans, and travails, and grieves. Why, or among whom, except because he has received and learned from his head, who says: *Iniquity will abound, and the love of many will grow cold*[^3]? But if *iniquity will abound, and the love of many will grow cold*, who will remain to travail? There follows: *He who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved*[^4]. Whence would perseverance be a great thing, unless one had to persevere amid troubles and temptations and disturbances and scandals? For no one is bidden to bear with good things. But because it is said for this man, and because it is sung for this man, let us see what it is. For this man, men are here rebuked, among whom there is groaning, among whom there is grief; and the consolation of the one grieving and travailing is set forth and expressed at the end of the psalm. Who, then, are they among whom we travail and groan, if we are in the body of Christ; if we live under that head, if we are reckoned among his members—who are they? Hear.

**2.** *The fool has said in his heart: There is no God.*[^5] Such is the kind of men among whom the body of Christ grieves and groans. If this is the kind of men, we do not travail over many: as far as seems to occur to our thoughts, they are very few; and it is difficult that we should fall in with a man who says *in his heart: There is no God*; yet they are so few only because, fearing to say this among the many, they say it in their heart, since they do not dare say it with their mouth. It is not, then, much that we are bidden to bear with; it is scarcely found: the men are a rare kind who say *in their heart: There is no God.* Or is it found, examined under another understanding, to be in many, what we thought was in few and rare and almost in none? Let those come forward into the open who live wickedly; let us inspect the deeds of the shameful, the criminal, and the wicked men, of whom there is a great throng; who daily cherish their sins, who, their deeds turned into habit, have lost even shame: so great a multitude of men is this, that the body of Christ set among them scarcely dares to rebuke what it is not forced to commit, and thinks it a great thing for itself to preserve the integrity of innocence, not to do what, through habit, it now either dares not blame, or, if it has dared, the rebuke and outcry of those who live wickedly more easily bursts out than the free voice of those who live well. And such men are these, that they say *in their heart: There is no God.* I convict such men. Whence do I convict them? They suppose their deeds to please God. So it does not say: Some say; but: *The fool has said in his heart: There is no God*—they who so far believe God to exist that they suppose that what they do pleases that same God. And yet, if you understand prudently that *the fool has said in his heart: There is no God*, if you take note, if you understand, if you examine it: he who thinks evil deeds please God does not think him to be God. For if he is God, he is just; if he is just, injustice displeases him, iniquity displeases him. But you, when you think iniquity pleases him, deny God. For if he is God to whom iniquity displeases, but to you he does not seem to be God to whom iniquity displeases, and yet there is no God except one to whom iniquity displeases, then when you say in your heart, "God favors my iniquities," you say nothing other than: *There is no God.*

**3.** [v. 2] Let us turn ourselves also to that understanding which presents itself concerning Christ our Lord himself, concerning our head himself. For when he appeared on earth in the form of a servant, those who crucified him said: *There is no God.* Because he was the Son of God, he was surely God. But those who were corrupt and made abominable, what did they say? *There is no God*: let us kill him, *there is no God.* You have their voice in the book of Wisdom; but first see them corrupt, that they might say *in their heart: There is no God.* For when the verse had gone before: *The fool has said in his heart: There is no God*, as though the causes were being sought whence the fool could say this, it added: *They are corrupt, and have become abominable in their iniquities.*[^6] Hear them corrupt. For they said among themselves, thinking not rightly: corruption began from evil faith, thence one goes into base morals, thence into the bitterest iniquities; these are the steps. For what did they say among themselves, thinking not rightly? *Our life is brief and full of weariness.*[^7] From this evil faith follows that which the Apostle too said: *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.*[^8] But there luxury itself is described more fully: *Let us crown ourselves with roses before they wither; let us everywhere leave the signs of our gladness.*[^9] After that luxury described more fully, what follows? *Let us kill the poor righteous man*[^10]: this, then, is to say: *There is no God.* A little while ago they seemed to speak gentle things: *Let us crown ourselves with roses before they wither.* What is more delicate, what is gentler? Would you expect crosses and swords from this gentleness? Do not be surprised; gentle too are the roots of thorns; if anyone handles them, he is not pricked: but that by which you are pricked is born thence. *Corrupt*, then, these men *and made abominable in their iniquities. The fool has said in his heart: There is no God. If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^11]. Behold, this is to say openly: *There is no God.*

**4.** But how does the body of Christ groan among them? The Apostles who lived then, and the disciples of Christ, groaned among them: what do these men pertain to us? how do we travail among these men? There are still those who say: Christ is not God. This the remaining pagans say; this the very Jews say, who, as a testimony of their own confusion, have been scattered everywhere; this many heretics also say. For the Arians too said: *There is no God*; the Eunomians said: *There is no God.* To this add, brethren, that even those of whom I spoke a little before, who live wickedly, say nothing other than: *There is no God.* For when we say to them that Christ is coming as judge to judgment, which the Scriptures say, which do not deceive—they, rather lending their ear to the serpent who suggests: *You shall not die the death*[^12], which he had said in paradise against the truth of God who appointed and said: *You shall die the death*[^13]—do evil in such a way as to say to themselves: Christ will come, and will give indulgence to all. So then he is a liar who said that he will separate the unrighteous to the left, will separate the righteous to the right; that to the righteous he will say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world*; to the unrighteous he will say: *Go into the eternal fire that is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^14]. How, then, will he give indulgence to all? How will he condemn no one? Then he lies. This is to say: *There is no God.* See, then, that you yourself do not perhaps lie. For you are a man, he is God: for God is truthful, but every man a liar. But what of these men, O body of Christ? Separate yourself for the present in heart and life; neither imitate them, nor grow accustomed to them, nor consent, nor approve; rather, refute them. For why do you attend to those who say these things? *They are corrupt, and have become abominable in their iniquities: there is none that does good.*[^15]

**5.** [v. 3] *The Lord looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see if there were one understanding or seeking God.*[^16] What is this: *They are corrupt, and have become abominable*, all these who say: *There is no God*? And what? was this hidden from God, that they had become such? or would their inner thought be opened to us, unless it were told by him? If, then, he had known, if he knew, what is this that is said, that he *looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see if there were one understanding or seeking God*? For these are the words of one seeking, not of one knowing: *God looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see if there were one understanding or seeking God.* And as if he found by inspecting and looking down from heaven what he sought, he pronounces the sentence: *They have all turned aside, together they have become useless: there is none that does good, there is not even one.*[^17] Two rather difficult questions arise. For if God looks down from heaven to see whether there is one understanding or seeking God, the thought creeps in upon the imprudent that God does not know all things. This is one question: what is the other? If there is none that does good, not even one, who is it that travails among the wicked? That question, then, is solved thus, that very often Scripture so speaks that what the creature does by God's gift, God is said to do: so, for example, when you have mercy on a poor man, because you do it by God's gift, God has mercy; when you know who you are, because you do it by his shining upon you, if you are the one who says: *You will light my lamp, O Lord my God, you will lighten my darkness*[^18]—what you have come to know by his giving and doing it, he knows. For whence is it: *The Lord your God tests you that he may know whether you love him*[^19]? What is *that he may know*? That by his gift he may make you know. So here too: *God looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see if there were one understanding or seeking God.* Let him be present himself, and grant us that what our heart caused to conceive, he may cause also to bring forth. The Apostle says: *But we have not received the spirit of this world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we may know the things that have been given us by God*[^20]. By this Spirit, then, by which we know the things given us by God, we distinguish between ourselves and those to whom these things have not been given, and from ourselves we come to know them. For if we understand that we could not have any good thing except by his giving and bestowing it, from whom are all good things, we see at the same time that they can have no such thing to whom God has not given these things. This discernment is ours from the Spirit of God; and by that whereby we see this, God sees, because it is God who makes us see. For hence too was that said: *For the Spirit searches all things, even the deep things of God*[^21]: not because he searches who knows all things, but because the Spirit has been given to you, who also makes you search; and what you do by his gift, he is said to do, because without him you would not do it. So God is said to do, when you do. By the gift of the Spirit of the sons, to whom the Spirit of God has been given, they look down *upon the sons of men, to* see *if there is one understanding or seeking God*: but because they do this by the gift of God and the Spirit of God, God is said to do this, as it were to look down and see. But why *from heaven*, if men do this? Because the Apostle says: *But our citizenship is in heaven*[^22]. For whence do you do this so as to see, whence do you look down so as to understand? is it not with the heart? If you do this with the heart, Christian, see whether you have your heart above. If you have your heart above, you look down *from heaven* to earth. And because you do this by God's gift, *God looks down from heaven upon the sons of men.* That question, then, is, according to our small measure, thus solved.

**6.** [v. 4] What is it that, looking down, we recognize? What is it that God, looking down, recognizes? what, because he gives it here, does he recognize? Hear what: that *they have all turned aside, together they have become useless: there is none that does good, there is not even one.*[^23] What, then, is that other question, except the one I mentioned a little before? If *there is none that does good, there is not even one*, no one remains to groan among the wicked. Wait, says the Lord; do not be quick to pass sentence: I have given men to do good, but from me, he says, not from themselves; for of themselves they are evil: they are sons of men when they do evil; when they do good, my sons. For this is what God does: out of sons of men he makes sons of God, because out of the Son of God he made a son of man. See what that participation is: participation in divinity has been promised to us; he who promised lies, if he was not first made a partaker of mortality. For the Son of God was made a partaker of mortality, so that mortal man might become a partaker of divinity. He who promised you that his good was to be shared with you, first shared with you your evil: he who promised you divinity, showed in you his love. Therefore take away the fact that men are sons of God, and there remains the fact that they are sons of men: *There is none that does good, there is not even one.*

**7.** [v. 5] *Will they not know, all who work iniquity, who devour my people as food of bread? Will they not know?*[^24] Will it not be shown to them? Speak, threaten, say it on behalf of the one travailing and grieving. For your people is devoured as food of bread. There is, then, here a people of God who is devoured. Surely *there is none that does good, there is not even one.* The answer is given from that rule. But this people who is devoured, this people who suffers the wicked, this people who groans and travails among the wicked, has already from sons of men been made sons of God: therefore it is devoured. *For you have confounded the counsel of the poor man, since the Lord is his hope.*[^25] For very often, in order that the people of God may be devoured, this very thing is despised in it, that it is the people of God. I will plunder, he says, and despoil; if he is a Christian, what will he do to me? He who speaks on behalf of the one travailing speaks for him, and threatens the devourers, saying: *Will they not know, all who work iniquity?* For even the one who saw a thief, and ran with him, and set his portion with adulterers, sitting, spoke against his brother and set a stumbling block against his mother's son, *said in his heart: There is no God.* Therefore this is said to him: *These things you have done, and I was silent: you supposed iniquity, that I would be like you*: that is, I would not be God, if I were like you. But what follows? *I will rebuke you, and set you before your own face.*[^26] So here too: *I will rebuke you, and set you before your own face.* You do not wish now to know so as to be displeased with yourself; you will know so as to lament. For God is not going to fail to show the unrighteous their iniquity. If he is not going to show it, who will they be who are going to say: *What has pride profited us, and what has the boasting of riches brought us?*[^27] Then, therefore, they will know who now refuse to know. *Will they not know, all who work iniquity, who devour my people as food of bread?* What did he add, *as food of bread*? As though they eat my people as bread. For the other things we eat, we can eat now these, now those; not always this vegetable, not always this meat, not always these fruits: but always bread. What is it, then, *they devour my people as food of bread*? Without intermission, without ceasing they devour, *who devour my people as food of bread.*

**8.** [vv. 6-7] *They have not called upon God.*[^28] He consoles the one groaning, and chiefly by the reminder, lest, by imitating the wicked who often prosper, it should delight one to become wicked. What was promised you is kept for you: their hope is present, yours is future; but theirs is fleeting, yours is sure; theirs is false, yours is true. For they *have not called upon God.* Do not such men ask God daily? They do not ask God. Attend, if I can say this with God himself helping. God wishes to be worshiped freely, wishes to be loved freely, that is, to be loved chastely; not to be loved for this reason, that he gives something besides himself, but because he gives himself. He, then, who calls upon God to become rich, does not call upon God; for he calls upon the thing he wishes to come to him. For what is it to call upon (*invocare*), except to call into oneself (*vocare in se*)? To call into oneself, then, this is meant by to call upon. For when you say, "God, give me riches," you do not wish God himself to come to you, but you wish riches to come to you. What you wish to come to you, that you call upon. But if you were calling upon God, he himself would come to you, he himself would be your riches. But as it is, you wish to have a full chest and an empty conscience: God does not fill the chest, but the breast. What do outward riches profit you if an inner poverty presses you? Therefore those who, for the sake of worldly advantages, who, for earthly goods, who, for the present life and earthly happiness, call upon God, do not call upon God.

**9.** Therefore what follows about them? *There they feared with fear, where there was no fear.*[^29] For is it fear, if someone should lose riches? There is no fear there, and yet there is fear. But if someone should lose wisdom, that is truly fear; and there there is no fear. Hear, distinguish, understand such men: a purse is entrusted to someone; he is unwilling to return it, he reckons it as his own, he thinks it cannot be reclaimed, he will now hold it as his own, he refuses to return it. Let him see what he fears to lose, and what he is unwilling to have: money and faith come into doubt; whichever is more precious, there the heavier loss must be feared. But you, in order to hold the gold, lose faith: you are struck with the heavier loss, and you have rejoiced over the gain: there you feared *with fear, where there was no fear.* Return the money: I say too little: Return; lose the money, lest you lose faith. You feared to return the money, and you were willing to lose faith! The martyrs did not carry off another's property; but even their own they despised, lest they lose faith: and it was a small thing to lose money, when they were proscribed; they lost even their soul, when they suffered: they lost their soul, that they might find it unto eternal life. Therefore there they feared where one ought to fear. But those who said of Christ, *There is no God, there they feared where there was no fear.* For they said: *If we let him go, the Romans will come and take from us both our place and our kingdom*[^30]. O folly and imprudence, saying *in its heart: There is no God!* You feared to lose the land, you lost heaven: you feared lest the Romans come and take from you place and kingdom; surely they would not take God from you? What, then, remains? What, except that you confess that you wished to hold, and by holding lost wickedly? For you lost both place and nation by killing Christ. For you preferred to kill Christ rather than lose your place; and you lost place and nation and Christ. By fearing, they killed Christ: but why this? *Because God scattered the bones of those who please men.*[^31] Wishing to please men, they feared to lose their place. But Christ himself, of whom they said: *There is no God*, preferred to displease such men as they were; he preferred to displease the sons of men, not the sons of God. Thence their bones were scattered; his bones no one broke. *They were confounded, because God despised them.* Truly, brethren, as far as concerns them, great confusion came upon them. In the place where they crucified the Lord—whom they crucified for this reason, lest they lose both place and kingdom—there the Jews are not. *God*, then, *despised them*: and yet by despising them he admonished them to be converted. Now let them recognize Christ, and say: He is God; whom they said: *There is no God.* Let them return to the paternal inheritance, to the inheritance of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; let them possess eternal life with them, even if they have lost temporal life. Why this? Because out of sons of men they have been made sons of God. For as long as they remain and are unwilling, *there is none that does good, there is not even one: they were confounded, because God despised them.* And as though turned to them, he says: *Who will give out of Zion the salvation of Israel?*[^32] O fools! You rebuke, you insult, you buffet, you besmear with spittle, you crown with thorns, you raise upon the cross—whom? *Who will give out of Zion the salvation of Israel?* Is it not he himself whom you said: *There is no God? When God turns away the captivity of his people.* For none turns away the captivity of his people except he who willed to be captive among your hands. But who will understand this? *Jacob will exult, and Israel will rejoice.* The true Jacob and the true Israel, that younger one whom the elder served, will himself exult, because he himself will recognize him.
