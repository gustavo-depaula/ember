# Exposition of Psalm 34

**1.** [vv. 1-2] Let your Charity know that this psalm has been imposed on me to treat by my brothers and fellow bishops at their bidding. They wished that we should all hear something from it. For from him we all hear, from whom we alike learn, and in whose school we are fellow disciples. Its title does not delay us; for it is brief, and not difficult to understand, especially for those nourished in the Church of God. For it has: *Of David himself*[^1]. A psalm, then, of David himself: David is interpreted "strong in hand," or "desirable." A psalm, then, of him who is strong in hand and desirable, who conquered our death, who promised us life: in this he is strong in hand, because he conquered our death; in this he is desirable, because he promised eternal life. For what is stronger than this hand, which touched the bier, and the dead man rose? What is stronger than this hand, which conquered the world, armed not with iron but pierced with wood? And what is more desirable than he whom the martyrs, not seeing, wished to die, that they might deserve to come to him? A psalm, then, to him: to him let our heart, to him let our tongue sing worthily—if indeed he himself will deign to grant what it may sing. No one sings worthily to him, except one who has received from him what he can sing. In short, this which we now sing was spoken by his Spirit through his prophet, and in those words where we recognize both ourselves and him. Nor do we do wrong in saying both ourselves and him: since, when he was in heaven, he cried out thus, *Why do you persecute me?*[^2]—when no one was touching him, and we were toiling on earth. Therefore let us hear his voice, now that of the body, now that of the head. For this psalm is one calling upon God against enemies in the tribulations of this world: and indeed it is Christ himself, his head being then in tribulation, his body now in tribulation; yet through tribulations giving eternal life to all his members, by promising which he became desirable.

**2.** *Judge, O Lord, those who harm me; fight against those who fight against me*[^3]. If God is for us, who is against us[^4]? And from where does God grant us this? *Take hold,* he says, *of arms and shield, and rise up to my aid*[^5]. It is a great spectacle, to see God armed for you. And what is his shield? What are his arms? *O Lord,* says this man in another place, who here too speaks, *you have crowned us as with a shield of your good will*[^6]. But his arms, by which he not only fortifies us but also strikes the enemies, we too will be, if we have advanced well. For as we, that we may be armed, have it from him, so he himself is armed with us. But he is armed with those whom he made; we are armed with those things which we have received from him who made us. The Apostle in a certain place calls these our arms the shield of faith, and the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God[^7]. He has armed us with such arms as you have heard, praiseworthy and unconquered, insuperable and splendid; spiritual indeed and invisible, because we fight against invisible enemies too. If you see your enemy, let your arms be seen. We are armed with the faith of those things which we do not see, and we lay low enemies whom we do not see. Yet, dearly beloved, do not suppose that these arms are such that what is a shield is always a shield, or what is a helmet is always a helmet, or what is a breastplate is always a breastplate. For with these bodily arms it is so—although even what is made of iron can be changed, so that from a sword an axe may be made—yet we see the Apostle himself say in one place "the breastplate of faith," and in another place "the shield of faith." Therefore faith itself can be both a breastplate and a shield: it is a shield, because it receives and repels the darts of the enemies; it is a breastplate, because it does not allow your inward parts to be pierced. These are our arms; but what are God's? We read in a certain place, *Deliver my soul from the ungodly, your sword from the enemies of your hand*[^8]. What he said above, *from the ungodly,* this in the following verse he says, *from the enemies of your hand;* and what he said above, *my soul,* this in the following verse he says, *your sword,* that is, your blade. Therefore he called his soul the sword of God: *Deliver,* he says, *my soul from the ungodly;* that is, *from the enemies of your hand deliver your sword.* For you take hold of my soul, and you war down my enemies. And what is our soul, however splendid, however drawn out, however sharp, however anointed, however brandished with the light of wisdom and the flashing of brilliance? What is our soul itself, or what can it do, unless God holds it and fights with it? For any blade, however excellently made, if it has no warrior, lies idle. But we had said, concerning our arms, that they are not to be taken as something fixed, so that what is one thing cannot be another: so too in the arms of God we find it. Behold, he called the soul of the just man the sword of God; again he says that the soul of the just man is the seat of God; the soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom. Therefore whatever he wishes, he does with our soul. When it is in his hand, let him use it as he wishes.

**3.** Let him rise up, then (for so he was invoked), let him take hold of arms, let him rise up to our aid. From where he is to rise up is said to him in another place by the same voice: *Rise up, why do you sleep, O Lord?*[^9] And when he is said to sleep, we sleep; and when he is said to rise up, we are awakened. For the Lord too was sleeping in the boat; and therefore the boat was tossed, because Jesus was sleeping. For if Jesus were keeping watch there, the boat would not be tossed. Your boat is your heart: Jesus in the boat, faith in the heart. If you remember your faith, your heart is not tossed; if you have forgotten your faith, Christ sleeps: watch out for shipwreck. Yet do what remains, that if he has slept he may be awakened; say to him, "Lord, rise up, we perish," that he may rebuke the winds, and there may be a calm in your heart. For all temptations will withdraw, or at least will avail nothing, when Christ, that is, your faith, has kept watch in your heart. What, then, is *Rise up*? Become known, appear, be perceived. *Rise up,* then, *to my aid.*

**4.** [v. 3] *Pour out the sword, and shut up the way against those who persecute me*[^10]. Who are they who persecute you? Perhaps your neighbor, or the one you have injured, or to whom you have done wrong, or who wishes to take away your goods, or against whom you preach the truth, or whose sin you rebuke, or whom by living well you injure though he lives badly. These indeed are also our enemies, and they persecute us; but we are taught to know other enemies, against whom we struggle invisibly, of whom the Apostle warns us, saying, *Our struggle is not against flesh and blood,* that is, against men; not against those whom you see, but against those whom you do not see; *against principalities and powers and the rulers of this world of darkness*[^11]. For when he said *rulers of the world* (for he was speaking of the devil and his angels), it was to be guarded against that men should understand it badly, and suppose that the world is ruled by the devil and his demons. But because this fabric which we see is called the world, and the world is spoken of in sinners, and in those who love the world, of whom it is said, *And the world did not know him*[^12]; and of whom it is said, *The whole world is set in wickedness*[^13]; the Apostle explained of what world they were the rulers: *of this darkness,* he says. "Rulers of the world," I say, "rulers of this darkness." Again he compels us to understand what he said, *this darkness.* Of what darkness are the devil and his angels the rulers? Of all unbelievers, of all the wicked, of whom it is said, *The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*[^14]. Then, since many from their very number came to believe, what does the same apostle say? *For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^15]. Do you not wish to be ruled by the devil? Pass over to the light. And from where will you pass over to the light, unless he pour out the sword, and deliver you from your enemies, and from those who persecute you? How does he pour out the sword? (Since we have already heard what his sword is: for it is the soul of the just man.) Let the just abound, and the sword is poured out, and the way is shut up against the enemies. For concerning that very pouring out of the sword the Apostle, warning us to live justly, in what follows says, *That the adversary may stand in awe, having nothing evil to say of us*[^16]. The way is shut up against him, because he cannot find anything to say against the saints.

**5.** And from where will the just come? Or what do the enemies who persecute us say? Those invisible enemies, what do they say? Do these say nothing? It is suggested above all to the human heart by the enemies who assail us invisibly that God is no helper to us: so that, seeking other helps, we may be found weak, and be captured by those very enemies. This, then, is suggested. Against these voices above all we ought to keep watch, which are shown in another psalm: *Many rise up against me; many say to my soul: There is no salvation for him in his God*[^17]. Against these voices what is said here? *Say to my soul: I am your salvation*[^18]. When you have said to my soul, *I am your salvation,* it will live justly, so that I seek no one for aid besides you.

**6.** [v. 4] And what follows? *Let those be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul*[^19]: for they seek it for this, to destroy it. Would that they sought it well! For in another psalm he reproaches this in men, that there was no one who sought his soul: *Flight has failed me, and there is no one who seeks my soul*[^20]. Who is it that says, *There is no one who seeks my soul*? Is it perhaps he of whom so long before it was foretold, *They pierced my hands and my feet, they numbered all my bones; but they themselves looked and gazed upon me; they divided my garments among them, and over my garment they cast lots*[^21]? Now all these things were being done before their eyes, and there was no one who sought his soul. Let us call upon him, then, brothers, that he may say to our soul, *I am your salvation;* and may open its ears, that it may hear him saying, *I am your salvation.* For he says it, but some grow deaf; whence they rather hear the persecuting enemies when set in tribulation. If anything is lacking, if the soul is in straits, in want of temporal things, it often seeks help from demons, wishes to consult those possessed by demons, seeks soothsayers: the persecuting invisible enemies have approached it, entered, assailed, captured, conquered it by saying, *There is no salvation for him in his God.* It has grown deaf against the voice saying, *I am your salvation. Say to my soul: I am your salvation,* that *those may be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul,* to whom you say, *I am your salvation.* May I hear him saying to me, *I am your salvation.* I will seek no other salvation besides the Lord my God. Salvation is suggested to me from a creature; it is from him: and if I lift up my eyes to the mountains, whence help may come to me; yet not from the mountains, but my help is from the Lord who made heaven and earth[^22]. In these very temporal straits God comes to aid through a man; he himself is your salvation. Through an angel God comes to aid; he himself is your salvation. All things are subject to him, and for this temporal life indeed he comes to aid, some from here, some from there: but eternal life he gives only from himself. Behold, to one set in straits, what you seek is not at hand, but he whom you seek is present. And seek him who can never be lacking. Let the things he gave be taken away; is he taken away who gave? Let the things he had given be repaid; are those riches themselves, when they have been repaid, and not he who took them away to test, and repaid to console? For he consoles when these things are not lacking to us. He consoles as it were on the way, but only if we understand the way: because this whole life, and all the things you use in this life, ought to be to you as an inn to a traveler, not as a house to a dweller. Remember that you have completed something, that something remains: that you have turned aside for refreshment, not for failing.

**7.** There are those who say: God is good, great, supreme, invisible, eternal, incorruptible; he will give us eternal life, and that incorruption which he promised in the resurrection; but these worldly and temporal things belong to the demons, and to those powers of this darkness. Saying these things, when they are entangled by love of these things, they abandon God, as one to whom these things do not belong; and they seek, by abominable sacrifices and by I know not what remedies, or by I know not what unlawful persuasion of men, to provide for themselves what is temporal, such as money, a wife, children, and whatever things either console human life as it passes or hinder it as it walks. Against this opinion, with divine providence keeping watch, in order that God might show that all these things belong to him and are in his power—not only the eternal things which he promised for the future, but also the temporal things which he gives on earth to whom he wills, and when he wills opportunely, knowing to whom to give and to whom not to give, like a physician with medicines, knowing the disease of the sick man better than the sick man himself—in order, then, that God might show this, he distributed the times of the Old and New Testament. In the Old Testament are promises of earthly things; in the New, of the kingdom of heaven. Most of the precepts for worshiping God and living rightly are the same both there and here: but because the promise there seems one thing, here another; the command of him who orders and the obedience of him who serves is the same, but the reward, as it were, is not the same. For to them it was said: that you may receive the land of promise, that you may reign in it, that you may overcome your enemies, that you may not be subjugated by them, that all things may abound for you in this land, that you may beget children. These are earthly promises, but nonetheless figurative. Suppose that some so receive them as they are promised: and truly many so received them. For the land was given to the children of Israel, riches were given, children were given even to the barren and the aged who asked God, and who presumed on him alone, and sought no other helper even for these things. They heard in their heart the voice of the Lord, *I am your salvation.* If for eternal things, why not for temporal? God showed this in the case of that holy man Job: that even the devil himself has no power to take these things away, unless he has received it from that supreme power. He could envy the saint; could he harm him? He could accuse; could he condemn? Was he able to take anything away, was he able to injure even a nail, even a hair, unless he said to God, *Stretch out your hand*[^23]? What is *Stretch out your hand*? Give power. He received it. He tempted; the other was tempted. Yet the one tempted conquered, the tempter was conquered. For God, who had permitted the devil to take those things away, had not abandoned that servant of his within, and to overcome the devil himself he had made the soul of his servant a sword for himself. How much is this worth? I speak of a human being. Conquered in paradise, conqueror on the dunghill. There he was conquered by the devil through a woman, here he conquered the devil and the woman. *You have spoken,* he says, *like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, why should we not endure evil things?*[^24] How well he had heard, *I am your salvation!*

**8.** *Let those be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul.* Look to men. *Pray,* he says, *for your enemies*[^25]. But here it is prophecy. And what are said in the figure of wishing are unfolded with the mind of one prophesying. "Let this be done, and let that be done" is nothing else than "this and that will be." Hear, then, the prophecy thus: *Let those be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul.* What is *let those be confounded and put to shame*? They will be confounded and put to shame. For it was done: many were salutarily confounded, many, put to shame, passed over from the persecution of Christ to the fellowship of his members with devout piety: and this would not happen unless they were confounded and put to shame. Therefore he wished them well. But because there are two kinds of those who are conquered—for they are conquered in two ways; either they are conquered so as to be converted to Christ, or they are conquered so as to be damned by Christ—these two very kinds are unfolded here too, obscurely indeed, but they require an understanding reader. Of those who are converted, take what was said: *Let those be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul. Let them be turned back*[^26]. Let them not go before, but follow; let them not give counsel, but receive it. For Peter wished to go before the Lord, when the Lord was speaking of his coming passion: he wished to give him counsel as it were of salvation, counsel of salvation from a sick man to a savior. And what did he say to the Lord, who was confirming that future passion of his? *Far be it from you, Lord; be merciful to yourself; this shall not be*[^27]. He wished to go before, that the Lord might follow. And he, what? *Get behind me, Satan*[^28]. By going before you are Satan; by following you will be a disciple. This, then, to these also: *Let them be turned back, and confounded, who think evil against me.* For when they have begun to follow behind, they will no longer think evil things, but will desire good things.

**9.** [vv. 5-6] What of the others? For not all are so conquered that they are converted and believe: many remain in obstinacy, many keep the spirit of going before in their heart; and if they do not bring it forth, yet they are in travail with it, and where they find a place, they bring it forth. Of such, what follows? *Let them become like dust before the face of the wind*[^29]. Not so the ungodly, not so, but like the dust which the wind drives from the face of the earth[^30]. The wind is temptation, the dust the wicked one. When temptation comes, the dust is carried off; it neither stands nor resists. *Let them become like dust before the face of the wind: and the angel of the Lord afflicting them. Let their way become darkness and slipperiness*[^31]. A dreadful way. Who would not dread darkness alone? Who would not beware slipperiness alone? In darkness and slipperiness where do you go? Where do you set your foot? These are two great evils of human punishment: darkness, ignorance; slipperiness, lust. *Let their way become darkness and slipperiness: and the angel of the Lord pursuing them*: so that they cannot stand. For each one, in darkness and slipperiness, when he sees that if he moves his foot he slips, and there is no light before his feet, perhaps does even this, that he waits until it grows light: but there is the angel of the Lord pursuing them. These things to come he foretold for them, not as one wishing that they might happen. And although the prophet too, in the Spirit of God, says these things in the way that God does them, by a sure judgment, good, just, holy, tranquil, not disturbed by anger, not by bitter zeal, not with a mind for exercising enmities, but by the justice of punishing faults; yet it is prophecy.

**10.** [vv. 7-8] But from where come these so great evils? By what desert? Hear by what desert: *For without cause they have hidden the corruption of their snare for me*[^32]. Attend, in our very head, that the Jews did this; they hid the corruption of their snare. For whom did they hide the snare? For one who saw the hearts of those who hid it. Yet among them he was like one ignorant, as though he were deceived, while they were deceived in him, in that they thought they were deceiving him. For he, as it were, was deceived while living among them, because we were so to live among such people that we would without doubt be deceived. He saw his betrayer, and chose him rather for a necessary work. From his evil he worked a great good; and yet he was chosen among the twelve, lest that very number of twelve, so small, should be without an evil one. This is for an example of our patience, since it was necessary that we should live among the wicked; it was necessary that we should bear the wicked, whether knowingly or unknowingly: he furnished an example of patience, that you may not fail when you have begun to live among the wicked. And because that school of Christ did not fail among the twelve, how much more ought we to be firm, when there are fulfilled in the great Church the things that were foretold about the mixture of the wicked? For that school itself did not yet see paid to the seed of Abraham what had been promised, nor the very threshing-floor from which the mass that will fill the granary is to come forth. Why, then, is the chaff not worthily borne in it while it is threshed, until by the last winnowing it is purged? For this will come to the wicked, which you have heard.

**11.** But yet what is to be done? *Without cause they have hidden the corruption of their snare for me.* What is *without cause*? Those to whom I did no evil, whom I in no way harmed. *In vain they reproached my soul*[^33]. What is *in vain*? Saying false things, proving nothing. *Let the snare which they do not know come upon them*[^34]. A magnificent recompense; nothing more just. They hid a snare, that I might be ignorant of it: let the snare come upon them which they do not know. For I know their snare. But what snare is to come upon them? That which they do not know. Let us hear, lest perhaps he name it: *Let the snare which they do not know come upon them.* Did they perhaps hide one, and another is to come upon them? No: but what? Each one is bound by the cords of his own sins. From the very thing by which they wished to deceive, they are deceived. By the very thing by which they tried to harm, they will be harmed. For there follows, *And let the trap which they concealed catch them.* As if someone prepared a cup of poison for another, and forgetting, drank it; and as if someone dug a pit into which each enemy of his might fall in the dark; and he, forgetting what he had dug, walking that way, falls there first. Indeed, my brothers, so believe, so be certain: so, if there is in you a more excellent measure of prudence, see and perceive: no evil man fails first to harm himself. For suppose malice to be like fire. You wish to set something on fire: that which you bring near burns first; unless it burns, it does not set fire. It is a torch; you apply this torch to set something on fire: does not the very torch you apply burn first, that it may be able to set something on fire? Malice, then, proceeds from you, and whom does it first lay waste but you? Where it is poured forth it harms a branch; where it has its root does it not harm? And indeed I say, it can come to pass that your malice does not harm another; but that it not harm you cannot come to pass. For what harm was done to the holy man Job, of whom we spoke before? As it is said in another psalm, *Like a sharp razor you have wrought deceit*[^35]. What happens with a sharp razor? Hairs, superfluous things, are cut off. What, then, do you do to him whom you wish to harm? If the one you wish to harm, being worthless, consents with you to evil, it will not be your malice that harms him, but his own; but if within he himself lacks malice, and submits a clean heart to that voice saying, *I am your salvation,* you assail him from without, you do not conquer the inner man: yet your malice proceeds from your inner self, and renders you first empty. You are rotten within, from which this worm came forth; within it has left nothing whole. *And let the trap which they concealed catch them; and let them fall into that very snare.* Not what you perhaps supposed a little before when you heard, *Let the snare which they do not know come upon them,* that is, as it were something else from a hidden source, unavoidable. Into which, then? Into the very iniquity which they hid from me. Was this not done to the Jews? Their Lord conquered their iniquity; they were conquered by their own iniquity. He rose again for us; they died in themselves.

**12.** [v. 9] These things, then, to the wicked who wish to harm me: but to me what? *But my soul shall exult in the Lord*[^36]: as in him from whom it heard, *I am your salvation*; as not seeking other riches from outside, as not seeking to flow about with pleasures and earthly goods; but loving its true spouse freely, not wishing to receive from him what may delight, but setting him alone before itself as the one by whom it may be delighted. For what better will be given me than God? God loves me; God loves you. Behold, he has set it before you: ask what you wish. If the Emperor said to you, "Ask what you wish," what tribuneships and countships you would belch forth! How many things you would set before yourself, both to receive and to bestow on others! When God says to you, "Ask what you wish," what will you ask? Shake out your mind, stretch out your greed, extend it as far as you can, and enlarge your desire: not just anyone, but the omnipotent God said, "Ask what you wish." If you are a lover of possessions, you will desire the whole earth, that all who are born may be your tenants or your slaves. And what, when you have possessed the whole earth? You will ask for the sea, in which nevertheless you cannot live. In this greed the fish will surpass you. But perhaps you will possess the islands. Pass beyond these too, ask even for the air, though you cannot fly: extend your desire even to heaven, say that the sun, moon, and stars are yours, because he who made all things said, "Ask what you wish": yet you will find nothing dearer, you will find nothing better, than him who made all things. Ask for him who made them, and in him and from him you will have all that he made. All things are dear, because all things are beautiful: but what is more beautiful than he? They are strong: but what is stronger than he? And he wishes to give nothing more than himself. If you find something better, ask for it. If you ask for something else, you will do him wrong, and harm to yourself, by preferring to him what he made, when he who made you wishes to give you himself. In this love a certain soul said to him, *Are you yourself my portion, O Lord?* that is, you are my portion. Let those who wish choose for themselves what to possess, let them make portions for themselves of things: *You are my portion,* I have chosen you for myself. And again, *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance*[^37]. Let him possess you, that you may possess him: you will be his estate, you will be his house. He possesses to profit; he is possessed to profit. Is it that you may profit him in anything? For *I said to the Lord: You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^38]. *But my soul shall exult in the Lord. It shall be delighted in his salvation.* The salvation of God is Christ. *For my eyes have seen your salvation*[^39].

**13.** [v. 10] *All my bones shall say: Lord, who is like you?*[^40] Who could worthily say anything about these words? I think they are only to be pronounced, not expounded. Why do you seek this or that? What is like your Lord? You have him before you. *All my bones shall say: Lord, who is like you?* The unjust have told me of their delights, but not as your law, O Lord. There were persecutors who said, "Adore Saturn, adore Mercury." "I do not worship idols," he says. *Lord, who is like you?* They have eyes, and do not see; they have ears, and do not hear. *Lord, who is like you,* who made the eye for seeing, the ear for hearing? "But I do not worship idols," he says, "because a craftsman made them." Worship a tree and a mountain: and did a craftsman not make this too? And here: *Lord, who is like you?* Earthly things are shown to me; you are the creator of the earth. And from here perhaps they turn to a higher creation, and say to me: Worship the moon, worship that sun, which by its light, like a great lamp, makes day from heaven. And here too plainly I say: *Lord, who is like you?* You made the moon and the stars, you kindled the sun, you composed the heaven. There are many invisible things that are better. But perhaps here too it is said to me: Worship the angels, adore the angels. And here too I will say: *Lord, who is like you?* The angels themselves too you created. The angels are nothing except by seeing you. It is better to possess you together with them, than by adoring them to fall away from you.

**14.** *All my bones shall say: Lord, who is like you?* O body of Christ, holy Church, let all your bones say, *Lord, who is like you?* And if the flesh has yielded to persecution, let the bones say, *Lord, who is like you?* For of the just it was said, *The Lord loves all their bones; not one of them shall be broken*[^41]. How many of the just have had their bones broken in persecution? Finally, the just man lives by faith, and Christ justifies the ungodly. But how does he justify, except the one who believes and confesses? Because with the heart one believes unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation[^42]. So even that robber, although led from robbery to the judge, and from the judge to the cross, yet on the cross itself was justified: with the heart he believed, with the mouth he confessed. For to one not just and not already justified the Lord would not say, *Today you shall be with me in paradise*[^43]: and yet his bones were broken. For when it came to taking down the bodies because of the impending Sabbath, the Lord was found already lifeless, and his bones were not shattered. But those who were living, that they might be taken down, had their legs broken, so that by this pain, being dead, they could be buried. Were the bones of only the one robber who persevered ungodly on the cross broken, and not also of him who with the heart believed unto justice and with the mouth confessed unto salvation? Where, then, is that which was said, *The Lord guards all their bones; not one of these shall be broken*[^44]; except that in the body of the Lord all the just are called bones—firm of heart, strong, yielding to no persecutions and temptations so as to consent to evil? And from where could they yield to no temptations, except when the persecutors say: Behold that god, behold what a god that is; let him come, let him bind for you a charm; behold here a certain great priest on the mountain; perhaps you are poor because that god does not help you; supplicate him, and he helps; perhaps you are sick because you do not supplicate him; supplicate him, and you will recover; perhaps for this reason you have no children; supplicate him, and you will have them. But here, if one is of the bones in the body of the Lord, he repels all these voices, and says, *Lord, who is like you?* Give, if you wish to give, in this life too, what I seek: but if you do not wish, be yourself my life, whom I always seek. From here shall I go forth to you with free brow, if I have adored another, and offended you? Tomorrow perhaps I am to die; with what brow shall I see you? Great is his mercy, both that he warned us to live well, and that he hid from us the last day of our death, lest we promise ourselves anything about the future. I do it today, and I live: tomorrow I do not do it. What if it does not find you tomorrow? Say, then, among the bones of Christ, *Lord, who is like you? All my bones shall say: Lord, who is like you?*

**15.** *Delivering the needy from the hand of those stronger than he, and the needy and poor from those who plunder him*[^45]. Thus far the psalm was read today, thus far it is to be treated; lest what has been said come into distaste, while we wish to say other things too. Let these things suffice for today, then: *Delivering the needy from the hand of those stronger than he.* Who is the deliverer, but he who is strong in hand? That David will deliver the needy from the hand of those stronger than he. For the devil had been stronger to hold you, because he conquered the one to whom you consented. But what did the one strong in hand do? No one enters the house of a strong man to plunder his goods unless he first binds the strong man[^46]. By his most sacred, most magnificent power he bound the devil, by pouring out the sword to shut him up, that he might deliver the needy and the poor, who had no helper. For who is a helper to you, except the Lord to whom you say, *Lord, my helper and my redeemer*[^47]? If you wish to presume on your own strength, you will fall from where you presumed; if on another's, he wishes to dominate, not to come to your aid. That one alone, then, is to be sought, who both redeemed and made free, and gave his blood to buy them, and made his servants brothers.
