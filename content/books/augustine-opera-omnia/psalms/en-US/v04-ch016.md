# Exposition of Psalm 88

**1.** [v. 1.] This psalm, of which we have proposed to speak to Your Charity, receive, the Lord granting, concerning the hope which we have in Christ Jesus our Lord: and lift up your souls, because he who promised will so fulfill the rest, as he has fulfilled many things. For it is not our merit, but his mercy, that gives us confidence toward him. And he himself is, as I judge: *The understanding of Ethan the Israelite*; whence this psalm received its title. For you may inquire what man he was who was called Ethan; yet the interpretation of this name indicates "Strong." And no one in this world is strong, except in the hope of the promise of God: for as far as concerns our merits, we are weak; as far as concerns his mercy, we are strong. And so this man, weak in himself, strong in the mercy of God, began from there.

**2.** [v. 2.] *Your mercies, O Lord, I will sing forever: from generation to generation I will announce your truth with my mouth.* Let my members, he says, obey my Lord: I speak, but I speak your things; *I will announce your truth with my mouth.* If I do not obey, I am not a servant; if I speak from myself, I am a liar. Therefore that both I may speak from you, and I myself may speak, there are two things: one yours, one mine; your truth, my mouth. What truth, then, he may announce, what mercies he may sing, let us hear.

**3.** [v. 3.] *For you have said: Forever mercy shall be built up.* This I sing; this is your truth, to announce which my mouth serves: *For you have said: Forever mercy shall be built up.* So, you say, I build, that I not destroy: since some you destroy so as not to build, but some you destroy so as to build. For unless some were destroyed to be built up, it would not be said to Jeremiah: *Behold, I have set you for destruction, and for building up*[^1]. And surely all who worshiped idols and served stones would not be built up in Christ, unless they were destroyed in their former error. Again, unless some were so destroyed as not to be built up, it would not be said: *You shall destroy them, and not build them up*[^2]. On account of those, then, who are destroyed and built up, lest they should suppose the building by which they are built up to be temporal, as the ruin in which they are destroyed was temporal; this man, whose mouth serves the truth of God, held himself to the very truth of God. Therefore I will announce, therefore I say: *For you have said;* a secure man I say it, because you, God, have said it: because, even if I wavered in my own word, I would be confirmed by your word. *For you have said.* What have you said? *Forever mercy shall be built up. In the heavens your truth shall be prepared.* As above, so in what follows. *Your mercies, O Lord,* he says, *I will sing forever: from generation to generation I will announce your truth with my mouth.* He said mercies, he said truth; so again he joined both: *For you have said: Forever mercy shall be built up. In the heavens your truth shall be prepared.* And here he repeated mercy and truth. *For all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth*[^3]. For truth would not be shown in the fulfillment of the promises, unless mercy went before in the remission of sins. Then, since to the people of Israel coming even according to the flesh from the seed of Abraham many things were prophetically promised, and so that people was propagated, in which the promises of God might be fulfilled; but God did not close the fountain of his goodness even to the foreign nations, which he had set under the angels, making the people of Israel his portion: into these two the Apostle too distinctly distributed the mercy of the Lord and his truth. For he says that *Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers*[^4]. Behold that God did not deceive; behold that he did not cast off his people, whom he foreknew. For when the fall of the Jews was being treated, lest anyone should suppose them so disapproved that nothing of the wheat from that winnowing was put into the granary, the Apostle says: *God did not cast off his people, whom he foreknew; for I too am an Israelite*[^5]. If all that was thorns, whence do I speak the wheat to you? Therefore the truth of God was fulfilled in those who from the Israelites believed, and one wall came from the circumcision, applying itself to the cornerstone. But that stone would not make a corner[^6], unless it received another wall from the Gentiles. That wall, then, as it were properly belongs to truth, but this other to mercy. *For I say,* he says, *that Jesus Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers; but that the Gentiles should glorify God for his mercy*[^7]. Rightly: *In the heavens your truth shall be prepared.* For all those Israelites called as Apostles were made heavens, who declare the glory of God. Of these heavens it is said: *The heavens declare the glory of God, and the firmament announces the works of his hands.* And that you may know it is said of these heavens, surely of these it was said more expressly in what follows: *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard*[^8]. Ask whose, you will find nothing above except of the heavens. If, then, they are the Apostles, in all whose speeches the voice was heard, they are those of whom it was said: *Into all the earth their sound went out, and to the ends of the world their words*[^9]; because even if they were taken from here before the Church filled the world, yet their words reached even to the ends of the world: we well take it as fulfilled in these what we now read: *In the heavens your truth shall be prepared.*

**4.** [v. 4.] *I have disposed a testament for my chosen.* You have said (you understand) all this: you have said: *I have disposed a testament for my chosen.* What testament, except the new? What testament, except that by which we are renewed unto a new inheritance? What testament, except for the desire and love of whose inheritance we sing the new song? *I have disposed,* he says, *a testament for my chosen. I have sworn to David my servant.* How secure this man speaks, who understands, whose mouth serves the truth! Because you have said, I speak securely. If you make me secure because you have said; how much more secure because you have sworn? For the oath of God is the confirmation of the promise. Rightly is man forbidden to swear[^10]; lest by the habit of swearing, because man can be deceived, he slip even into perjury. God alone swears securely, because he cannot be deceived.

**5.** [v. 5.] Let us see, then, what God swore. *I have sworn,* he says, *to David my servant: even forever I will prepare your seed.* What is the seed of David, except the seed of Abraham? But what is the seed of Abraham? *And to your seed,* he says, *which is Christ*[^11]. But perhaps that Christ, the head of the Church, the savior of the body[^12], is the seed of Abraham, and therefore of David; but we are not the seed of Abraham? Rather, we are, as the Apostle says: *But if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed, heirs according to the promise*[^13]. So, then, let us take it here, brothers: *Even forever I will prepare your seed;* not only that flesh of Christ born of the Virgin Mary, but also all of us believing in Christ: for we are the members of that head. This body cannot be beheaded: if the head is forever, forever the members too glory, that that whole Christ may be forever. *Even forever I will prepare your seed; and I will build up your seat from generation to generation.* We suppose he said this, *forever,* which is, *from generation to generation;* since above too he had said: *From generation to generation I will announce your truth with my mouth.* What is, *from generation to generation?* Unto every generation. For the word would not have to be repeated as often as a generation comes and passes. Therefore the multiplication of generations is signified and commended in the repetition. Or are perhaps two generations to be understood, as you know, as you retain, already intimated to Your Charity? For there is now a generation of flesh and blood; there will be a future generation in the resurrection of the dead. Here Christ is preached; he will be preached there: but here he is preached, that he may be believed; there he will be preached, that he may be seen. *I will build up your seat from generation to generation.* Now Christ has his seat in us, his seat is built up in us: for unless he sat in us, he would not rule us; but if we were not ruled by him, we would be cast headlong by ourselves. He sits, then, in us, reigning in us: he sits also in another generation, which will be from the resurrection of the dead. Forever Christ will reign in his saints. This God promised, this God said: if it is little, this God swore. Since, then, the promise is firm not according to our merits, but according to his mercy; no one ought to preach with trembling that of which he cannot doubt. Let there come about, then, in our hearts that strength, whence Ethan received his name, Strong of heart: let us preach the truth of God, the utterance of God, the promises of God, the oath of God; and firmed in every part by these ways, let us preach, and, bearing God, be heavens.

**6.** [v. 6.] *The heavens shall confess your wonders, O Lord.* The heavens shall not confess their own merits, but *the heavens shall confess your wonders, O Lord.* For in every mercy on the lost, in the justification of the impious, what do we praise except the wonders of God? You praise because the dead have risen; praise more because the lost have been redeemed. What grace, what mercy of God! You see a man yesterday a whirlpool of drunkenness, today an ornament of sobriety: you see a man yesterday the mire of luxury, today the comeliness of temperance: you see a man yesterday a blasphemer of God, today a praiser of God: you see a man yesterday a servant of the creature, today a worshiper of the Creator. From all these despairs men are so converted: let them not look to their own merits; let them become heavens, let the heavens confess the wonders of him by whom the heavens were made. *For I shall see,* he says, *the heavens, the works of your fingers*[^14]. *The heavens shall confess your wonders, O Lord.* And that you may know which heavens shall confess, see where they shall confess: for it follows: *And your truth in the church of the saints.* Of the heavens, then, there is no doubt, that the preachers of the word of truth are understood. And where shall the heavens confess your wonders and your truth? *In the church of the saints.* Let the Church receive the dew of the heavens; let the heavens rain on the thirsty earth, and let it, receiving the rain, germinate good germinations, good works: lest for good rain it give thorns, and for the granary expect fire. *The heavens shall confess your wonders, O Lord, and your truth in the church of the saints.* The heavens, then, shall confess your wonders and your truth. The whole, whatever the heavens preach, is from you, is of you; and therefore they preach securely: for they know whom they preach, because they cannot blush at the one preached.

**7.** [v. 7.] What do the heavens preach? what shall they confess in the church of the saints? *For who in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord?* Is this what the heavens shall confess, this what the heavens shall rain? What? *For who in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord?* Hence the preachers are secure, because no one in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord. A great praise it seems to you, brothers, that the clouds are not equaled to the Creator. If it be understood without mystery, to the letter, it is no great praise, that the clouds are not equaled to the Lord. For what? are the stars which are above the clouds equaled to the Lord? What? could the sun, moon, angels, heavens even be compared to the Lord? What is it that he says as something great: *For who in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord?* We understand, brothers, these clouds, like the heavens, to be the preachers of truth; the Prophets, the Apostles, the announcers of the word of God. For that all these kinds of preachers were called clouds, we understand from that prophecy, where God, angry, said to his vineyard: *I will command my clouds not to rain rain upon it*: which vineyard he explains and designates most openly, saying: *The vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel*[^15]. Lest you understand the vineyard otherwise, and, leaving the men signified, search the earth: *The vineyard,* he says, *of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel.* Let it understand nothing else, let it understand that the house of Israel is my vineyard; let it understand that it gave me not a grape, but thorns; let it understand that it was ungrateful to the planter and cultivator, ungrateful to the one who watered. If, then, *the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel,* what did he say, being angry? *I will command my clouds not to rain rain upon it.* And indeed he did so: the Apostles were sent as clouds, to rain on the Jews; and those, repelling the word of God, because for the grape they gave thorns, the Apostles said: *To you we were sent; but because you have repelled the word of God, we go to the Gentiles*[^16]. From then the clouds began not to rain rain upon that vineyard. If, then, the clouds are preachers of truth, let us first ask why they are clouds. For the same are heavens, the same clouds: heavens on account of the brightness of truth, clouds on account of the hidden things of the flesh: for all clouds are misty, on account of mortality; and they come, and pass. On account of these very hidden things of the flesh—that is, the hidden things of the clouds—the Apostle says: *Do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, and enlightens the hidden things of darkness*[^17]. Now, then, what a man speaks, you see; what he carries in heart, you do not see: what is expressed from the cloud, you see; what is kept in the cloud, you do not see. For whose eyes penetrate the cloud? Therefore the clouds, in the flesh, are the preachers of truth. The Creator of all things himself too came in the flesh. But *who in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord? Who,* then, *in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord? and who shall be like the Lord among the sons of God?* Therefore no one among the sons of God shall be like the Son of God. He himself was called the Son of God, and we were called sons of God; but *who shall be like the Lord among the sons of God?* He the only-begotten, we many; he one, we one in him; he born, we adopted; he the Son begotten from eternity by nature, we made in time by grace; he without any sin, we through him freed from sin. *Who,* then, *in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord? or who shall be like the Lord among the sons of God?* We are called clouds on account of the flesh, and we are preachers of truth on account of the rain of the clouds; but our flesh comes one way, his flesh another. We too are called sons of God, but he is the Son of God in another way. His cloud is from the Virgin, he the Son from eternity, equal to the Father. *Who,* then, *in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord? or who shall be like the Lord among the sons of God?* Let the Lord himself say whether he finds one like him. *Whom do men say that the Son of man is?* For behold, because I am seen, because I am beheld, because I walk among you, and perhaps my presence has made me cheap; say *whom do men say that the Son of man is?* Surely because they see the Son of man, they see the cloud: let them say, or do you say *whom men say that I am.* And they answered the sayings of men: *Some say Jeremiah, others Elijah, others John the Baptist, or one of the Prophets.* Many clouds and sons of God were named. For because they were righteous and holy, surely they too were sons of God: Jeremiah, Elijah, John, sons of God; and clouds, because preachers of God. You have said which clouds men suppose me to be, and among which sons of God men reckon me: say you too *whom you say that I am.* Peter answering for all, one for the unity: *You are,* he says, *Christ the Son of the living God*[^18]. For who in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord? or who shall be like the Lord among the sons of God? *You are Christ the Son of the living God;* not as the sons of God who are not equaled to you: you came in the flesh; but not as the clouds which are not equaled to you.

**8.** [v. 8.] For who are you, to whom it is answered: *You are Christ the Son of the living God;* whom other men, not holy, not righteous, supposed to be one of the Prophets, or Elijah, or Jeremiah, or John the Baptist: who, then, are you? Hear what follows: *God, to be glorified in the counsel of the righteous. Who,* then, *in the clouds shall be equaled to the Lord? or who shall be like the Lord among the sons of God,* when he is *God, to be glorified in the counsel of the righteous?* Since they cannot be equal to him; let their counsel be to believe in him: since the clouds and sons of God cannot be equal to him; there remains the counsel for human frailty, that he who glories may glory in the Lord[^19]. *God, to be glorified in the counsel of the righteous: great and terrible above all who are round about him.* God is everywhere. Who, then, are round about him, who is everywhere? For if he has some round about him, he is understood as it were bounded on every side. Further, if it was truly said to God and of God: *Of his greatness there is no end*[^20]; who remain, who are round about him, except because he who is everywhere willed to be born through the flesh in one place, to dwell in one nation, to be crucified in one place, to rise from one place, to ascend into heaven from one place? Where he did this, round about him are the Gentiles. If he remained where he did these things, he would not be *great and terrible above all who are round about him*: but because he there so preached, that thence he sent forth the preachers of his name through all the Gentiles in the whole world; by doing miracles through his servants, he became *great and terrible above all who are round about him.*

**9.** [v. 9.] *O Lord God of hosts, who is like you? You are powerful, O Lord, and your truth is round about you.* Great is your power; you made heaven and earth, and all things that are in them: but greater is your mercy, which showed your truth round about you. For if you were preached only there, where you willed to be born, to suffer, to rise, whence to ascend; that truth of God's promise would be fulfilled, *to confirm the promises of the fathers*: but it would not be fulfilled: *that the Gentiles should glorify God for his mercy*[^21], unless that truth were spread abroad, and from that place where you willed to appear, were diffused round about. You indeed in that place thundered from your own cloud, but to water round about, into the Gentiles you sent other clouds. Truly powerful, you fulfilled what you said: *Henceforth you shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven*[^22]. *You are powerful, O Lord, and your truth is round about you.*

**10.** [v. 10.] But when your truth began to be preached round about, surely the Gentiles raged, and the peoples meditated vain things; the kings of the earth stood up, and the princes came together against the Lord and against his Christ[^23]. Surely when your truth had begun to be preached round about you, as though you came to lead a wife from the foreigners, a roaring lion met you, and was strangled by you. For this was prefigured in Samson[^24]: and at those words of mine, said without naming him, you would not have acclaimed, unless you recognized; for you heard, as you who are wont to be rained on by the clouds of God. Therefore *your truth is round about you.* But when without persecutions, when without contradictions, since it was said that he was born for a sign that will be contradicted[^25]? Since, then, that nation, where you willed to be born, to dwell, was as it were a land separated from the waves of the Gentiles, so that the dry land might appear to be watered, but the rest of the Gentiles were a sea in the bitterness of their barrenness; what do your preachers do, scattering your truth round about you, when the waves of the sea rage? what do they do? *You rule the power of the sea.* For what did the sea do by raging, except the day which we celebrate today? It killed the martyrs, it scattered the seeds of blood, the harvest of the Church sprouted up. Let the clouds, then, go forth securely; let them diffuse your truth round about you, let them not fear the raging waves: *You rule the power of the sea.* The sea is indeed moved, the sea contradicts, the sea makes a din; but God is faithful, who does not allow you to be tempted beyond what you can[^26]. Since, then, he is faithful who does not allow you to be tempted beyond what you can: *But the movement of its waves you assuage.*

**11.** [v. 11.] Finally, that the sea might be appeased, rather that the rage of the sea might be unnerved, what did you do in the sea itself? *You have humbled the proud one as one wounded.* There is a certain proud dragon in the sea, of whom another Scripture says: *I will command the dragon there to bite him*[^27]; there is a dragon of whom it is said: *This dragon which you have formed to mock him*[^28]: whose head he crushes upon the water. *You,* he says, *have humbled the proud one as one wounded.* You humbled yourself, and the proud one was humbled. For the proud one by pride held the proud: the great one was humbled, but he who believes in him was made small. While the small one is nourished by the example of the great made small, the devil lost what he held; because the proud one held none but the proud. So great an example of humility being given, men learned to condemn their own pride, to imitate the humility of God. So, then, that one too, by losing those he held, was humbled himself; not corrected, but cast down. *You have humbled the proud one as one wounded.* You were humbled, and you humbled; you were wounded, and you wounded: for your blood, which was shed to blot out the bond of sins, would surely wound him. For whence was he proud, except because he held a writ against us? This writ, this bond, you blotted out with your blood[^29]: him, then, you wounded, from whom you took away so many. For understand the devil wounded, not in his flesh, which he does not have; but in his heart being struck, where he is proud. *And in the arm of your power you have scattered your enemies.*

**12.** [vv. 12-13.] *Yours are the heavens, and yours is the earth.* From you they rain, upon yours they rain. *Yours are the heavens,* through which your truth was preached round about you: *yours is the earth,* which received the truth round about you. Finally, what was done from that rain? *The world and its fullness you have founded; the north and the seas you have created.* For nothing avails against you, against its Creator. And the world by its own malice, by the perversity of its will, can rage; yet does it exceed the measure set by the Creator, who made all things? Why, then, do I fear the north? why do I fear the seas? There is indeed in the north the devil, who said: *I will set my throne in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^30]; but you humbled the proud one as one wounded. Therefore what you have done in them avails more for your dominion, than their will for their malice. *The north and the seas you have created.*

**13.** [v. 13.] *Tabor and Hermon shall exult in your name.* These are understood as mountains, but they signify something. *Tabor and Hermon shall exult in your name.* Tabor is said to be interpreted "coming light." But whence comes the light of which it was said: *You are the light of the world*[^31], except from him of whom it was said: *It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^32]? That light, then, which is the light of the world, comes from that light which is not kindled from elsewhere, so that it should be feared lest it be extinguished. Therefore light comes from him, that lamp which is not set under a bushel, but upon a lampstand, the coming light, Tabor. But Hermon is interpreted "his anathema." Rightly came light, and his anathema was made. Whose, except of the devil, the wounded, the proud? Therefore that we might be enlightened, it was given by you; that he should be an anathema to us who held us in his error and pride, it is from you for us. Therefore *Tabor and Hermon shall exult in your name*: not in their own merits, but *in your name.* For these will say: *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory;* on account of the raging sea: *lest perhaps the Gentiles say: Where is their God*[^33]?

**14.** [v. 14.] *Your arm is with power.* Let no one arrogate anything to himself. *Your arm is with power*: by you we were made, by you we were defended. *Your arm is with power; let your hand be strengthened, and your right hand be exalted.*

**15.** [v. 15.] *Righteousness and judgment are the preparation of your seat.* Your righteousness and your judgment will appear at the end: now these things are hidden. And of your judgment it is said in another psalm: *For the hidden things of the son*[^34]. There will be a manifestation of your judgment and your righteousness. Some will be set at the right, and some at the left[^35]: and the unbelieving will be terrified, when they see what now, mocking, they do not believe; the righteous will rejoice, when they see what now, not seeing, they believe. *Righteousness and judgment are the preparation of your seat*: surely on the day of judgment. Now, then, what? *Mercy and truth go before your face.* The preparation of your seat, your righteousness and your judgment that is to come, I would fear, unless your mercy and truth went before you: why should I fear at the end your judgments, when, your mercy going before, you blot out my sins, and by showing your truth you fulfill your promises? *Mercy and truth go before your face.* For all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth[^36].

**16.** [vv. 16-17.] In all these things shall we not exult? or shall we grasp what we exult over? or will words suffice for our gladness? or will the tongue be fit to explain our joy? If, then, no words suffice; *Blessed is the people that knows joyful praise.* O blessed people! do you suppose, do you understand joyful praise? In no way are you blessed, unless you understand joyful praise. What is it, to understand joyful praise? To know whence you rejoice over what you cannot explain in words. For your joy is not of yourself, that he who glories may glory in the Lord[^37]. Do not, then, exult in your own pride, but in the grace of God. See that grace itself is so great, that the tongue is not fit to explain it, and you have understood joyful praise.

**17.** Finally, if you have understood joyful praise in grace, hear the commendation of that grace itself. *Blessed* surely *is the people that knows joyful praise.* What joyful praise? See whether it is not of grace, see whether it is not of God, and not at all of yourself. *O Lord, in the light of your countenance they shall walk.* That Tabor: coming light, unless it walk in the light of your countenance, by the wind of pride the lamp is extinguished. *O Lord, in the light of your countenance they shall walk; and in your name they shall exult all the day.* That Tabor and Hermon *shall exult in your name*: if they wish *all the day,* in your name; but if they exult in their own name, they will not exult all the day. For they will not persevere in joy, when they rejoice over themselves, and by pride fall. Therefore, that they may exult all the day, *in your name they shall exult; and in your righteousness they shall be exalted.* Not in their own righteousness, but *in yours*: lest they be those having a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge. For certain ones are noted by the Apostle, that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge; *being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own,* and not exulting in your light, *they were not subject to the righteousness of God.* But whence? Because *they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge*[^38]. But the people knowing joyful praise (for they were not according to knowledge; but a blessed people, not ignorant, but knowing joyful praise), whence ought they to give joyful praise, whence ought they to exult, except in your name, walking in the light of your countenance? And it will indeed deserve to be exalted, but in your righteousness: let it take away from the midst its own righteousness, and be humbled; the righteousness of God will come, and it will be exalted. *And in your righteousness they shall be exalted.*

**18.** [v. 18.] *For you are the glory of their strength, and in your good pleasure our horn shall be exalted*: because it pleased you, not because we are worthy.

**19.** [v. 19.] *For the acceptance is the Lord's.* For I, as a heap of sand, was pushed, and was about to fall; and I would have fallen, unless the Lord had accepted me. *For the acceptance is the Lord's; and of the Holy One of Israel our king.* He himself the acceptance, he himself enlightens you: in his light you are safe, in his light you walk, by his righteousness you are exalted. He himself accepted you, your weakness he himself guards; he himself makes you strong from himself, not from yourself.

**20.** [v. 20.] *Then you spoke in vision to your sons, and you said. You spoke in your vision,* you revealed this to your Prophets. Therefore you spoke to them in vision, that is, in revelation: whence the Prophets were called Seers[^39]. They saw something within which they would say outside; and in secret they heard what they preached openly. *Then you spoke in vision to your sons, and you said: I have set help upon the powerful one.* You understand whom the powerful one. *I have exalted a chosen one out of my people.* You understand whom the chosen one; whom you now rejoice to be exalted.

**21.** [v. 21.] *I have found David my servant*: that David of the seed of David. *With my holy oil I have anointed him*: for of him it is said: *God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows*[^40].

**22.** [v. 22.] *For my hand shall help him, and my arm shall strengthen him*: according to that the acceptance of the man was made, according to that the flesh was assumed in the womb of the Virgin[^41], according to that by him who in the form of God is equal to the Father, the form of a servant was assumed, and he was made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross[^42].

**23.** [v. 23.] *The enemy shall not profit against him.* The enemy indeed rages, but shall not profit against him: he is wont indeed to harm, but he shall not harm. What is it, then, that he will afflict? He will exercise, not harm: he will profit by raging, because those against whom he rages will be crowned by conquering. For what is conquered, if nothing rages against us? or where is God our helper, if we do not fight? The enemy, then, will do what is his own; but *the enemy shall not profit against him, and the son of iniquity shall not add to harm him.*

**24.** [v. 24.] *And I will cut down his enemies before his face.* They are cut down from their conspiracy, and by believing they are cut down. For little by little they believe; as the head of the calf was ground small, they will come into the drink of the people of God. For Moses ground small the head of the calf, scattered it in the water, and gave it to the children of Israel to drink[^43]. All the unbelieving are ground small, they believe little by little; and are drunk by the people of God, are transferred into the body of Christ. *And I will cut down his enemies before his face; and those who hate him I will put to flight*: that they may not harm. But of those put to flight perhaps some say: *Whither shall I go from your spirit? and from your face whither shall I flee*[^44]? And seeing that they have nowhere to flee from the Almighty, being converted they flee to the Almighty. *And those who hate him I will put to flight.*

**25.** [v. 25.] *And my truth and my mercy is with him.* All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth[^45]. Remember, as far as you can, how often these two are commended to us, that we may render them to God. For as he himself showed us mercy, to blot out our sins; and truth, to fulfill his promises: so we too, walking in his way, ought to render to him mercy and truth; mercy, that we have mercy on the wretched; truth, that we do not judge unjustly. Let not truth take mercy from you, nor mercy hinder truth: for if through mercy you judge against truth, or, as by a rigid truth, you have forgotten mercy; you will not walk in the way of God, where mercy and truth have met each other[^46]. *And in my name his horn shall be exalted.* What need is there to linger? You are Christians, acknowledge Christ.

**26.** [v. 26.] *And I will set his hand in the sea*: that is, he shall have dominion over the Gentiles. *And in the rivers his right hand.* The rivers run into the sea; covetous men slip down into the bitterness of this world: yet all these kinds will be subjected to Christ.

**27.** [vv. 27-28.] *He shall call upon me, You are my Father, my God and the upholder of my salvation. And I will set him the firstborn, high above the kings of the earth.* Our martyrs, whose birthdays we celebrate, shed their blood for these things believed and not yet seen: how much stronger ought we to be, seeing what they believed? For high above the kings of the earth those men had not yet seen Christ; still the princes came together against the Lord and against his Christ: not yet was fulfilled what follows in that very psalm: *And now, you kings, understand; be instructed, all you who judge the earth*[^47]. Now, then, Christ has been made high above the kings of the earth.

**28.** [v. 29.] *Forever I will keep for him my mercy, and my testament faithful to him.* On account of him, the testament is faithful; in him the testament was mediated; he himself the mediator of the testament, he himself the sealer of the testament, he himself the surety of the testament, he himself the witness of the testament, he himself the inheritance of the testament, he himself the coheir of the testament.

**29.** [v. 30.] *And I will set his seed forever and ever.* Not only in this world, but *forever and ever;* whither his seed will surely pass over, which is his inheritance, the seed of Abraham which is Christ. But if you are Christ's, then you are the seed of Abraham: and if you are to receive the inheritance forever, he will set *his seed forever and ever. And his throne as the days of heaven.* The thrones of earthly kings are as the days of the earth. Some are days of heaven, some are days of the earth. The days of heaven are those years of which it is said: *But you are the same, and your years shall not fail*[^48]. The days of the earth are pressed by those succeeding, the preceding ones are shut out, nor do those who succeed remain; but they come that they may go, and almost before they come they go. Such are the days of the earth. But the days of heaven, and those years not failing, have neither beginning nor end; nor is any day there straitened between yesterday and tomorrow: no one there awaits the future, no one there loses the past; but the days of heaven are always present, where his throne will be forever. What remains, if it please you, let us keep; since the Psalm is long, and we are still to do something with you in the name of Christ. Refresh, then, your strength; I do not say of your soul, for I see that in soul you are untiring: but on account of the servants of the soul, that your bodies may endure in the ministry, refresh yourselves a little, and refreshed return to the foods.
