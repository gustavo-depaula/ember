# Exposition of Psalm 112

**1.** [vv. 1-3.] You know, brothers, and have most often heard the Lord say in the Gospel: *Let the children come to me; for of such is the kingdom of heaven;* and again: *If anyone does not receive the kingdom of God like a child, he will not enter into it*[^1]: and in many other places our Lord, by the singular example of humility, accuses the pride of the old man, to renew his life humbly, by the likeness of childhood. Wherefore, dearest ones, when you hear it sung in the Psalms, *Praise the Lord, you children,* do not suppose that this exhortation does not pertain to you, because, having now passed beyond the childhood of the body, you either grow green in youthful beauty, or grow gray in the honor of old age: for to all of you the Apostle says: *Do not become children in mind; but in malice be little ones, that in mind you may be perfect*[^2]. By what malice most of all, except pride? For it, presuming on a vain greatness, does not allow a man to walk through the narrow way, and to enter through the strait gate: but a child easily enters through the narrow; and therefore no one, except as a child, enters into the kingdom of heaven. And what is worse than the malice of pride, which is unwilling to have even God as its superior? For it is written: *The beginning of the pride of man is to apostatize from God*[^3]. This, raising itself with swollen necks against the divine precepts, and resisting the sweet yoke of the Lord, cast down, break, crush, and consume, and *praise the Lord, you children, praise the name of the Lord.* For it being laid low and extinguished, praise is perfected out of the mouth of infants and sucklings; it being suppressed and blotted out, he who glories, may glory in the Lord. They do not sing these things who suppose themselves great; they do not sing these things who, knowing God, did not glorify him as God, or give thanks: they praise themselves, not God; for they are not children. They wish rather their own name to be proclaimed, and do not praise the name of the Lord. And so they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened; and saying themselves to be wise, they became fools: they wished their own name to be spread abroad most lengthily and most widely, themselves soon to pass into narrows. It befits God, it befits the Lord, to be proclaimed both always and everywhere. Always, then, let him be proclaimed: *Let the name of the Lord be blessed, from now and unto the age.* Everywhere let him be proclaimed: *From the rising of the sun to its setting, praise the name of the Lord.*

**2.** Let some one of the holy children praising the name of the Lord ask of me, and say to me: Behold, *unto the age,* I take as unto eternity; but why *from now,* and not before this and before all ages *let the name of the Lord be blessed?* I will answer the little one, who does not ask contumaciously: To you it is said, lords and children, to you it is said: *Praise the name of the Lord: let the name of the Lord be blessed;* let the name of the Lord be blessed by you, *from now* certainly, from when it is said to you. For you begin to praise, but praise without end. *From now*, then, *and unto the age,* praise without end. Do not say: We begin indeed to praise the Lord, because we are children; but when we have grown and become great, we will praise ourselves. Not so, children, not so: therefore the Lord says through Isaiah: *I am he; and until you grow old, I am he*[^4]. He is always to be praised who is. *Praise, you children, from now,* praise, you old men, *and unto the age.* For your old age will indeed grow white with the gray hairs of wisdom, but will not wither with the oldness of the flesh. Or because in this place childhood seems rather to signify humility, to which is contrary the vain and false greatness of pride; and therefore none but children praise the Lord, because the proud do not know how to praise him: let your old age be childlike, and your childhood old; that is: that neither your wisdom be with pride, nor your humility without wisdom, that you may praise the Lord *from now and unto the age.* But wherever the Church of Christ is spread abroad in the holy little ones: *Praise the name of the Lord*: for this is: *From the rising of the sun to its setting, praise the name of the Lord.*

**3.** [v. 4.] *The Lord is high above all the nations.* The nations are men: what wonder if the Lord is high above men? They see with their eyes those whom they worship shining high above them in the sky, the sun and moon and stars, who serve the creation, deserting the Creator. But not only is *the Lord high above all the nations;* but even *above the heavens is his glory.* The heavens look up at him above themselves; and the humble have him with them, set in the flesh below the sky, who for his sake do not worship the sky.

**4.** [vv. 5, 6.] *For who is like the Lord our God, who dwells on high, and looks upon the lowly?* Anyone might suppose that he dwells in the high heavens, whence he looks upon the lowly earthly things; but *he looks upon the lowly in heaven and on earth.* On what high places, then, does he dwell, whence he looks upon the lowly in heaven and on earth? Or, on what high places does he dwell, does he look upon those very lowly ones? For he so exalts the humble that he does not make them proud. And so even in others whom he exalts he dwells, and makes them a heaven for himself, that is, his seat; and yet, looking upon them, not proud, but always subject, even in heaven itself he looks upon the lowly, in whom, exalted, he dwells. For the Spirit speaks thus through Isaiah: *Thus says the Most High dwelling on high, whose name is forever, the Lord most high having his rest in the saints*[^5]. He expounded what he said, *dwelling on high;* for, expressing it more fully, he said, *having his rest in the saints.* But who are the saints, except the humble, who as children praise the Lord? And so he adds: *And giving magnanimity to the pusillanimous, and giving life to those who are in lowliness of heart.* In which saints he has his rest, to these pusillanimous he gives magnanimity. By giving magnanimity he certainly makes them lofty, in whom, having his rest, he dwells on high. But because he gives magnanimity to the pusillanimous, the same high places in which he dwells, he looks upon as lowly. But *the lowly,* he says, *he looks upon in heaven and on earth.*

**5.** And it has roused us also to ask whether the Lord our God looks upon the same lowly ones in heaven that he does on earth; or others in heaven, others on earth. For if the same, I see how I may understand this according to the Apostle who says: *For walking in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh: for the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but powerful for God*[^6]. Whence powerful, then, except because spiritual? Since, then, the Apostle both walks in the flesh, and wars spiritually, it is no wonder if his lowliness is looked upon both in heaven because of the liberty of the spirit, and on earth because of the servitude of the body. For he himself says in another place: *For our citizenship is in heaven*[^7]: he likewise says that it is *best* for him *to be dissolved and be with Christ, but to remain in the flesh is necessary,* he says, *for your sake*[^8]. Accordingly, whoever understands both the citizenship of the Apostle in heaven, and his dwelling in the flesh on earth; it is necessary that he understand at once how the Lord our God, dwelling in the lofty saints, yet looks upon the same saints, humble before him, both in heaven, because they taste the things that are above, who have risen in hope with Christ; and on earth, because they are not yet loosed from the bond of the flesh, that with all their life they may be with Christ. But if the Lord our God looks upon some lowly ones in heaven, and others on earth; I believe that in heaven he already looks upon those he has called, and dwells in them; but on earth he looks upon those he calls, that he may dwell in them. For he possesses those thinking on heavenly things, those he rouses who dream earthly things.

**6.** But because we hardly obtain that even those can be called humble who have not yet joined their pious necks to the sweet yoke of the Lord, since the lowly in this place, that we may understand the holy, the divine letters admonish through the whole text of the Psalm; there is another understanding too, which let your Charity consider with me. I think that now there are signified by the heavens those who will sit upon twelve thrones, and judge with the Lord; but by the name of earth the rest of the multitude of the blessed, who will be set at the right hand, that, praised together through the works of mercy, they may be received by those into the eternal dwellings, whom they made friends for themselves of the mammon of iniquity in the mortality of this life. For to them the Apostle says: *If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal things?*[^9] Which can also be said in these words: If we have sown heavenly things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your earthly things? In heaven, then, he looks upon those who sow heavenly things, but on earth those who render earthly things; yet both these and those humble. For *he looks upon the lowly in heaven and on earth;* because both remember what they were through their own malice, what they were made through the grace of the Lord. For not only to those does the Vessel of election say: *For you were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^10]; and again: *By grace you were made safe through faith, and this not from yourselves, but it is the gift of God, not from works, lest perhaps anyone be exalted*[^11]: but he joins himself too consequently, saying: *For we are his formation, created in good works*[^12]. He says too separately of himself and of those who are looked upon in heaven: *For we too were,* he says, *by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^13]. And again: *For we too were,* he says, *once foolish and unbelieving, erring, serving various desires and pleasures, acting in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another: but when the kindness and humanity of our Savior God shone forth, not from works of righteousness which we did, but according to his mercy he made us safe through the washing of regeneration*[^14]. Behold the lowly who are looked upon in heaven. For they are spiritual, and judge all things; but yet humble, lest, cast down, they be judged. What of himself singularly? are not such the things he commemorates? *Who am not,* he says, *fit to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God*[^15]: *but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief*[^16].

**7.** [vv. 7-9.] Finally, after those verses in which the Spirit says in the Psalm: *Who is like the Lord our God, who dwells on high, and looks upon the lowly in heaven and on earth*: wishing to teach us why in heaven the lowly, when the spiritual are already great and worthy of judicial seats, are signified by such an expression; he immediately subjoined: *Who raises the needy from the earth, and from the dung exalts the poor; that he may set him with princes, with the princes of his people.* Let not the heads of the lofty, then, disdain to be lowly, under the right hand of the Lord. For although the faithful steward of the Lord's money be set with the princes of the people of God, although he will sit on twelve seats, and judge the angels too; yet the needy one is raised from the earth, and the poor one exalted from the dung. Or perhaps he was not exalted from the dung, who was serving various desires and pleasures? But perhaps when he said these things he was not now needy, not poor. Why, then, does he still groan, weighed down, desiring to put on over himself the dwelling which is from heaven? Why, lest he perhaps exalt himself, is he buffeted, and subjected to an angel of Satan, a goad of his flesh? He is indeed lofty with the Lord dwelling in him, and having that Spirit who searches all things, even the depths of God: and so he is in heaven; but even in heaven the Lord looks upon the lowly.

**8.** What then, brothers, if we have now heard of the lowly who are in heaven, exalted from the dung, that they might be set with the princes of the people; have we heard nothing consequently of the lowly whom the Lord looks upon on earth? For those friends who will judge with the Lord are fewer, but those whom they receive into the eternal dwellings are more. For although the whole mass of grain, in comparison with the separated chaff, seems to have few; yet, considered in itself, it is abundant. *For many are the children of the deserted one, more than of her who has a husband*[^17]: many are the children of her who through grace conceived in old age, more than of her who from her early age was married by the bond of the Law. And I indeed say, conceived in old age; looking at Sarah our mother in one Isaac, through all the nations the mother of the faithful. But what person is in Isaiah, see: as if altogether not of a mother, nor of one who bore anyone. And yet what is said to her? *They will say,* he says, *in your ears, your children whom you had lost: The place is too narrow for us in this place, make us therefore now too a place in which we may dwell. But you will say in your heart: Who begot these for me, when I know that I am without children and a widow? Who, then, raised these for me? for I was left alone; but where were these for me?*[^18] From that part, then, the Church says these things, by which it seems to bear nothing in these crowds which did not leave all things to follow the Lord, and sit upon twelve thrones. But how many in the same crowd, making for themselves friends of the mammon of iniquity, through works of mercy will stand at the right hand? Not only, then, does he raise from the dung the one he may set with the princes of his people; but also: *He makes the barren one to dwell in a house, the joyful mother of children*: he who dwells on high, and looks upon the lowly in heaven and on earth, the seed of Abraham like the stars of heaven, the sublime holiness set in the seats above, and like the sand on the shore of the sea, the merciful and innumerable multitude separated from the waves of the left hand and from the impious bitterness.
