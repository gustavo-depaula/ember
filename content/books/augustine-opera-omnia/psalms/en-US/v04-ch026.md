# Exposition of Psalm 96

**1.** God offers great spectacles to the Christian heart, and ones than which truly nothing more delightful can be found; if only there be present the palate of faith, to which the honey of God may taste sweet. We believe that in all of you, who have believed in our Savior with your whole heart, his Spirit is present, which delights you when the prophecies are read, brought forth so many years before from the mouth of the saints, and after so many years fulfilled in the faith of the nations. For the holy Prophets themselves then plucked a great delight, when they saw these things in the spirit, not yet fulfilled, but still to come. There was for them a great delight: but yet they too, on account of the love by which they were kindled toward us, whom they did not yet see, and in the spirit travailed; they wished, if it could be, to live with us in this time, and to see fulfilled what in the spirit they prophesied. Hence the Lord said to his disciples, now beginning to see these things: *Many righteous men and prophets wished to see what you see, and did not see; and to hear what you hear, and did not hear*[^1]. For although they saw these things in the spirit, yet they were shaped for them as it were future: but to the Apostles they were now rendered present. Whence that Simeon, a certain righteous old man, greatly exulted seeing the infant Jesus, and recognizing in the little one the great one, and in the small flesh knowing the Creator of heaven and earth. He greatly exulted, then, because he had received an answer that he would not depart from this life, before he saw the salvation of God. He recognized him, then, was made glad, exulted with joy; and said this: *Lord, now you dismiss your servant in peace; for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^2]. Great, then, is this delight; and love makes it. We have been delighted, when this psalm was sung: and certain things there were understood by all; but certain things, as we judge, either by few, or surely not by all. Together, then, let us consider it in this sermon, in which we serve you; and let us see with how great a condescension God willed to make us glad, presenting what he promised, and exhibiting to us the truth of his promises.

**2.** [v. 1.] The Psalm is inscribed: *Of David himself, when his land was restored.* Let us refer the whole to Christ, if we wish to hold the way of right understanding: let us not withdraw from the cornerstone[^3], lest our understanding make a ruin: in him let what was wavering with unstable motion be made solid; on him let what was hanging through uncertain things lean. Whatever doubt a man has in his soul when the Scriptures of God are heard, let him not withdraw from Christ; when Christ has been revealed to him in those words, let him understand that he has understood: but before he comes to the understanding of Christ, let him not presume that he has understood. *For Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^4]. What is it, then, and how is it taken in Christ: *When his land was restored?* For how David is understood as Christ, is easy to recognize. For Christ is from Mary, and from the seed of David; and because he was to be from his seed, therefore by his name in a figure he was prophesied. Therefore David is Christ: because both the interpretation of David is "strong of hand"; and who is so strong of hand, as he who from the cross conquered the world? For after his resurrection and ascension, the Holy Spirit being received, the Apostles speaking in various tongues[^5], the multitude of those very ones who had crucified him was moved, sought counsel of salvation; received it, believed; pardon was given, the guilt of the blood of Christ was forgiven, the drink of the blood of Christ was imparted; they were made the faithful of him whose persecutors they had been; they believed in him whom they crucified, and before whom, insulting, they shook the head; him very one they wished to have as head. So, then, *his land was restored,* which the title of the Psalm has. For his land is Judea: and all Judea had perished, when they crucified their Lord, not knowing, frantic, raging against the physician, and rejecting salvation in madness. As though, then, all Judea had perished: how all? The Apostles themselves too trembled. Peter who with bold love followed, with timid trembling thrice denied. Rising, the Lord Jesus Christ himself found on the way certain men speaking with each other about him, such that they said to him asking whence they were conversing: *Are you the only stranger in Jerusalem, and have you not known the things that have happened in it in these days? To whom he said: What? And they said: Of Jesus of Nazareth, who was a man, a prophet mighty in deed and word, before God and all the people; and how the chief priests and our rulers delivered him up to the condemnation of death, and crucified him. But we hoped that it was he who was to redeem Israel*[^6]. Now they had lost the hope of Christ. For they did not say: We hope that he is to redeem; but: *We hoped that he was to redeem Israel.* He himself was with them, and the hope of him was not in them. He showed himself to them, was manifested also to the rest of the disciples, was seen, handled, and found by those to whom he now seemed to have perished: the faith of those who had fallen was recalled; *his land was restored.* Then, having spent forty days with them, he ascended into heaven[^7]; and, as I recalled a little before, the Holy Spirit being sent, he made his disciples, ignorant men, speak in the tongues of all nations. Then those for whom not in vain he had said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^8], being moved, as we said, sought salvation, received counsel that they should believe in him; on one day three thousand believed, and again five thousand[^9]; the Church of Christ began to grow hot throughout Judea, where the reproach of Christ had grown hot; and *his land was restored.* But because he himself had said: *I have other sheep that are not of this fold; I must bring them too, that there may be one flock and one shepherd*[^10]; even to the Gentiles, to whom the Prophets had not been sent, the Apostles were sent. Those were sought who had not sought; those were found who awaited nothing: those who held not God as promiser, found him as redeemer. For the Jews already held God as promiser, because there the Prophets had preached Christ, there had promised Christ; but him whom they had heard promised, present they did not recognize: but to those nothing had been promised; yet in the Prophets even of their faith it had been spoken. It was not said to them, but it was said of them. He was sent to them too: and you heard, by the dispensation of God; for that very reading was just now read to you in the Acts of the Apostles, how the centurion Cornelius believed. For the centurion Cornelius was not of the nation of the Jews. He prayed, fasted, gave alms. God did not desert him though set among the Gentiles; and an angel was sent to him, to announce to him that his alms and prayers were accepted with God: he believed, having called Peter to him. Could the angel not teach him? He sent him to Peter, that faith might be made in him rather through a man; because the Lord had deigned to visit men[^11]: nor did he disdain to teach through a man, who deigned to be a man. So, then, *his land was restored,* one wall coming from the Jews, the other wall coming from the Gentiles; to which two walls coming from different sides, he himself was the cornerstone, where both were joined[^12].

**3.** Again how do we take: *When his land was restored?* When his flesh was raised again. For another understanding, yet not withdrawing from Christ, can thus occur to us: the land restored, the flesh raised again. For after his resurrection were done all these things which are sung in the Psalm. Let us now hear of the restoration of the land the Psalm full of joy. Let the Lord our God himself rouse in us an expectation and delight worthy of so great a thing; let him himself govern our discourse fit for your hearts, that whatever here our heart exults in such spectacles, it may bring through to the tongue, and thence to your ears, then to your heart, thence to your deeds.

**4.** *The Lord has reigned.* He who stood before the judge, he who received the blows, he who was scourged, he who was spat upon, he who was crowned with thorns, he who was struck with fists, he who was hung on the wood, he who, hanging on the wood, was insulted, he who died on the cross; he who was pierced with a lance, he who was buried, he himself rose again. *The Lord has reigned.* Let the kingdoms rage as much as they can; what will they do to the King of kingdoms, the Lord of all kings, the Creator of all the ages? Or is he therefore despised, because he appeared so submissive, and so humble? It is mercy, not impotence: for he appeared humble, that we might grasp him. But now let us see: *The Lord has reigned; let the earth exult, let the many islands be glad.* It is indeed so, because the word of God was preached not on the continent alone, but also in the islands which are set in the midst of the sea: and they too are full of Christians, full of servants of God. For the sea does not separate him who made the sea. Whither ships can come, can the words of God not come? The islands are filled. Yet the islands too can be rightly taken in a figure, as all the Churches. Why islands? Because they are barked at on every side by the waves of all temptations. But as an island, with the waves clashing around on every side, can be beaten, cannot be broken, and rather itself breaks the waves coming, than is broken by them: so too the Churches of God sprouting up throughout the whole earth have suffered the persecutions of the unbelieving raging on every side, and behold the islands stand, and now the sea is appeased. *Let the many islands be glad.*

**5.** [v. 2.] *Cloud and gloom are round about him; righteousness and judgment are the direction of his seat.* For whom are *cloud and gloom round about him?* For whom are *righteousness and judgment the direction of his seat?* Cloud and gloom for the impious, who did not understand him; righteousness and judgment for the faithful, who believed in him: for those through pride did not see, those through humility deserved to be directed. Hear the cloud and the mist, and hear the righteousness and the judgment. The Lord himself says: *For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind*[^13]. What is, *those who see may become blind?* Those who seem to themselves to see, who think themselves wise, who do not judge medicine necessary for themselves, let them become blind, let them not understand. But those who *do not see, may see*: those who confess their blindness, may deserve to be enlightened. Let there be, then, *round about him cloud and gloom,* for those who did not know him; but for those confessing and humbling themselves, *righteousness and judgment the direction of his seat.* He calls his seat those very ones who believed in him: for of them he made for himself a seat, because in them sits Wisdom; for the Son of God is the Wisdom of God[^14]. But we have heard from another Scripture a great document of this understanding. *The soul of the righteous is the seat of wisdom*[^15]. Therefore, because these were made righteous, who believed in him; justified by faith, they were made his seat: he sits in them, judging through them, and directing them. Why? Because he found them meek, like gentle beasts, not kicking back, not shaking their proud neck against his yoke, not refusing his scourge: they were made his good, gentle beasts; and they deserved what was said in another psalm: *He will direct the meek in judgment, he will teach the gentle his ways*[^16]. Therefore for those *cloud and gloom,* because they are not upright; but for the meek, *righteousness and judgment the direction of his seat.*

**6.** [v. 3.] *Fire shall go before him, and shall inflame round about his enemies.* Of what fire does he speak, brothers: *Fire shall go before him, and shall inflame round about his enemies?* I do not think it is said of that fire, into which the impious are to be sent by that last sentence of the judgment, to whom, separated at the left, as we remember it read in the Gospel, he is to say: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^17]: I do not think it is said of that fire. Whence do I not think this? Because he speaks of a certain fire which will go before him, before he comes to the judgment. For it was said that the fire goes before and inflames round about, that is, throughout the whole earth, his enemies. That fire will be after his coming; but this fire shall go before him. What, then, is this fire? We can take it as the punishment of the evil, we can take it as the salvation of the redeemed. As the punishment of the evil, how? Because when Christ was preached, the nations were angered, and stirred up persecution: which anger was a fire, consuming more those who persecuted, than those whom they persecuted. For when we see two, one angry, the other patiently bearing, which of them burns, it is yours to judge. You can behold such a spectacle in the human race. Set before your eyes an unrighteous man, moved in soul, fierce in face, with flaming eyes, with sparkling words, borne to the killing of a man, to plundering, to injuries, to insults, not containing himself, not holding himself; the other receiving patiently the words, the blows, whatever the other may wish to inflict, and to the one striking his cheek, preparing the other too: when you see on this side furies, on that gentleness; on this side anger, on that patience; on this side flames, on that endurance; will you doubt to pronounce which of them burns and suffers punishment? he whose body is vexed, or he whose soul is laid waste? Therefore the prophet Isaiah too said: *And now the fire shall devour the adversaries*[^18]. What is: *And now?* Before that great day of judgment comes, already by their own fury burn those who afterward by the punishment of that everlasting fire are to burn. Unless perhaps you suppose, my brothers, that the injustice which proceeds from a man to harm another man, harms him to whom it proceeds, and does not harm him from whom it proceeds. How can it be? Sometimes a burning torch is applied to wood that is damp and green, and does not set it on fire, yet itself burns; so too your enemy. If perhaps there arises an unrighteous man who contrives snares against you, or prepares some trouble, he is unrighteous: you, if you are green wood, that is, if vigorous and green with spiritual sap, you resist the flames of enmities, by praying for him who persecutes you; he burns, you remain whole; and his injustice harms him, harms you nothing. Unless perhaps you suppose that it harms you, if he does something to your body, and your soul, patient and incorrupt, arrives to be crowned with God, following the example of its Lord, who chose to suffer from the Jews, and who, being able not to die, died, because he too, being able not to be born, was born. For you were born by condition, he by will; you die by condition, he by mercy. As, then, the Jews harmed him nothing; so neither any persecuting enemy harms you, if you have chosen to be a member of that head.

**7.** Behold we have understood the fire going before him, that is, to be understood at this time as a certain punishment of the unbelieving and the iniquitous: let us understand the fire too, if we can, as the salvation of the redeemed; for so we had proposed. The same Lord says: *I came to send fire upon the earth*[^19]. Fire so, as the sword: for in a certain place too he says that he came not to send peace upon the earth, but a sword[^20]. The sword for separation, the fire for burning: but both wholesome, because the sword of his word too wholesomely separated us from the bad habit. For he brought a sword, and separated each of the faithful either from his father who had not believed in Christ, or from his mother likewise unbelieving; or certainly, if he was born of Christian parents, at least from his earlier progeny. For there is none of us who did not have either a grandfather, or great-grandfather, or some ancient origin among the Gentiles, and in that unbelief execrable to God: we have been separated from what we were; but the sword intervened separating, not killing. In this way too the fire: *I came to send fire upon the earth.* Men burned, believing in him, they received the flame of love; therefore the Holy Spirit too, when he was sent to the Apostles, so appeared: *There appeared to them,* it says, *divided tongues as it were of fire, which also sat upon each one of them*[^21]. Inflamed with this fire they began to go through the world, and to inflame, and to set on fire round about his enemies. What enemies of his? Those who, having forsaken God by whom they were made, adored the idols which they had made. They themselves were set on fire, if they were evil, unto consumption; if they were good, unto restoration: either he himself was set on fire, who refused to believe, by that fire, having heard the word of God made worse, burned up and consumed by his own envy; or if he was converted and believed, not even so was nothing burned up in him. For the hay had burned, that the gold might be purified. The gold is faith, the hay is carnal concupiscence. *All flesh is hay,* Isaiah says, *and all the honor of the flesh as the flower of hay*[^22]. Whatever, then, is in a carnal man desiring vain and worldly things, is hay. How many perhaps, even our brothers, went to the theater? they were led by the hay. Is not this fire to be desired for them, that the hay may burn, and the gold be purified? For whatever of faith is in them, is pressed down by the hay. It is good, then, for them to burn with a holy fire, that, the hay being consumed, the precious thing which Christ redeemed may shine. Therefore: *Fire shall go before him, and shall inflame round about his enemies.* There are those who burned wholesomely, today they are his faithful; they were his enemies, and now already they are his faithful: you seek enemies, there are none; they have been consumed, they have burned; love consumed in them what persecuted Christ, and purified in them what believed in Christ: *And shall inflame round about his enemies.*

**8.** [v. 4.] *His lightnings appeared to the world.* A great exultation. Do we not see? is it not manifest? His lightnings appeared to the whole earth; the enemies were inflamed, the enemies were set on fire. Whatever contradicted burned up, and *his lightnings appeared to the world.* How did they appear? That they might now believe. Whence the lightnings? From the clouds. What are the clouds of God? The preachers of truth. But you see a cloud, in the sky misty, dark, and it has within something hidden, I know not what. If it flashes from the cloud, the splendor leaps out: from that very thing which you despised, thence leaps out what you may dread. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, sent his Apostles, his preachers, as clouds: they were seen as men and despised; as the clouds are seen, and despised, before there leaps out of them what you may wonder at. For those at first were men bearing flesh, weak; then ignorant, unlearned, ignoble: but there was in them what would flash, there was in them what would gleam. Peter the fisherman drew near, prayed, and the dead man rose[^23]. The human form was a cloud, the splendor of the miracle was a flashing. So in words, so in deeds, when they say wonderful things, and do wonderful things: *His lightnings appeared to the world. The earth saw and was moved.* If it is not true? if all the earth, now Christian, does not cry, Amen, moved by the lightnings bursting from those clouds? *It saw, and the earth was moved.*

**9.** [v. 5.] *The mountains flowed like wax before the face of the Lord.* Who are the mountains? The proud. Every height lifting itself up against God trembled at the deeds of Christ and the Christians, fell down, and—when I say what was said: It flowed—a better word cannot be found. *The mountains flowed like wax before the face of the Lord.* Where is the height of the powers? where the hardness of the unbelieving? *The mountains flowed like wax before the face of the Lord.* The Lord was to them a fire, and they before his face flowed like wax; so long hard, until that fire was applied. Every height was leveled; now it does not dare to blaspheme Christ: and the pagan does not believe in him, yet he does not blaspheme him; even if he has not yet been made a living stone, yet the hard mountain has been conquered. *The mountains flowed like wax before the face of the Lord, before the face of the Lord of all the earth*: not of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles, as the Apostle says[^24]; for he is not the God of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles. The Lord, then, of all the earth, the Lord Jesus Christ, born in Judea, but not born for Judea only: because both before he was born he made all things; and he who made all things, remade all things. *Before the face of the Lord of all the earth.*

**10.** [v. 6.] *The heavens announced his righteousness, and all the peoples saw his glory.* What heavens announced? *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^25]. Who are the heavens? Those who were made his seat. For as God sits in the heavens, so he sits in the Apostles, so he sits in the preachers of the Gospel. And you too, if you will, will be a heaven. Do you wish to be a heaven? Purge the earth from your heart. If you have no earthly concupiscences, and do not in vain answer that you have your heart on high, you will be a heaven. *If you have risen with Christ* (the Apostle speaks to the faithful), *seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth*[^26]. Have you begun to savor the things that are above, and not the things that are upon the earth? have you not been made a heaven? You bear flesh, and in heart you are now a heaven: for your conversation will be in the heavens[^27]. Such, you too announce Christ: for what faithful man is silent of Christ? Let Your Charity attend: do you suppose that we alone standing here announce Christ, and you do not announce him? Whence come to us those wishing to be Christians, whom we never saw, whom we do not know, to whom we never preached? Or did they perhaps believe with no one announcing? The Apostle says: *How shall they believe him whom they have not heard, how shall they hear without a preacher*[^28]? Therefore the whole Church preaches Christ, and the heavens announce his righteousness; because all the faithful who have care to gain for God those who have not yet believed, and do this from love, are heavens. God thunders from them the terror of his judgment; and he who was unbelieving trembles, and is afraid, and believes. Show to men what Christ was able to do throughout the whole earth, by speaking to them, and bringing them to love Christ. For how many today brought their friends, either to the pantomime, or to the flute-player? Why, except by loving him? And do you love Christ. For he offered so great spectacles, in which no one can say he found anything of reproof, he who conquered the world. For sometimes even the one whom anyone loves in the theater is conquered in it. But no one is conquered in Christ: there is nothing to blush at. Seize, bring, draw whom you can: be secure, you bring them to him who does not displease those who see him; and ask him to enlighten them, and that they may watch well. *The heavens announced his righteousness, and all the peoples saw his glory.*

**11.** [v. 7.] *Let all be confounded who adore graven things.* Has it not been done? Have they not been confounded? Are they not daily confounded? For graven things are idols made by hand. Why are now all confounded who adore graven things? Because all the peoples saw his glory. Now all the peoples confess the glory of Christ: let those blush who adore stones. Because those stones were dead, we have found a living stone: rather those stones never lived, so that they may not even be called dead; but our stone is living, and always lived with the Father, and for us, having died, lived again, and now lives, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^29]. This his glory the peoples have known; they leave the temples, they run to the churches. *Let all be confounded who adore graven things.* Do they still seek to adore graven things? They were unwilling to forsake the idols; they were forsaken by the idols. *Let all be confounded who adore graven things, who glory in their idols.* But there arises I-know-not-what disputant who seemed learned to himself, and says: I do not worship that stone, nor that image which is without sense; for your Prophet could not know that they have eyes and do not see[^30], and do I not know that that image has neither a soul, nor sees with eyes, nor hears with ears: I do not worship that; but I adore what I see, and serve him whom I do not see. Who is this? A certain divine power, he says, invisible, which presides over that image. By rendering an account of their idols in this way, they seem to themselves eloquent, because they do not worship idols, and they worship demons. For indeed, brothers, as the Apostle says: *What the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, and not to God. I do not wish you,* he says, *to be associates of demons*; for *we know that an idol is nothing*[^31]. The Apostle himself said this: *We know that an idol is nothing, but what the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, and not to God*: he said: *I do not wish you to be associates of demons.* Let them not, then, excuse themselves from this, that they are not as it were devoted to senseless idols; they are rather devoted to demons, which is more perilous. For if they only worshiped idols, as those did not help them, so they would harm them nothing; but if you adore and serve demons, they will be your lords. And who will be your lords? Those who envy you: they must needs envy your freedom, they always wish to possess you, always to make you such as they can drag with them. For there is implanted in these evil spirits a certain malevolence and ruinousness of harming: they rejoice at the evil of men; and they feed on our deceit, if they have deceived us. And what do they seek? Not those over whom they may have dominion forever, but those with whom they may be condemned forever; as a malevolent robber is wont to name an innocent man. If he be burned alive, does he burn less, if two burn? does he die less, if two die? His punishment is not lessened, but his malevolence is fed. Let him die with me, not saying that he will die less, but he has the solace of another's evil. Such is the devil; he wishes to seduce those who may be punished with him: and because he cannot deceive the judge God (for he does not name an innocent man before him), wishing to have true charges to bring, he persuades to sins. Behold what kind of lords those make for themselves who worship idols and demons: *For what the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, and not to God: I do not wish you to be associates of demons.*

**12.** But what kind of God do we have? Hear what follows. For when he had said: *Let all be confounded who adore graven things, who glory in their idols*: lest they should arise as it were rendering an account of their idols, and say: We worship not stones, but divine powers; What divine powers do you worship? tell me, do you worship demons, or good spirits, such as the angels are? For there are holy angels, and there are malignant spirits. I say that in your temples none but malignant spirits are worshiped: those who proudly exact a sacrifice for themselves, and wish to be worshiped as gods, are malignant, are proud. Such too are men who are not good, who seek their own glory, and despise the glory of God. But attend to holy men, who are like the angels. When you find some holy man, a servant of God, if you wish to worship and adore him as God, he forbids you: he does not wish to arrogate to himself the honor of God, he does not wish to be for you in place of God, but to be with you under God. This the holy apostles Paul and Barnabas did. They were preaching the word of God in Lycaonia. When they had done wonderful things in Lycaonia, the citizens of that region brought victims, and wished to sacrifice to them, calling Barnabas Jupiter, and Paul Mercury: they were not delighted. Or did they perhaps refuse to be sacrificed to, because they execrated being compared to demons? No, but because they shuddered that divine honor should be displayed to men. Their words indicate it, we do not conjecture. For the reading of that same book follows, and says how they were moved: *Then Paul and Barnabas tore their garments, and said: Men, brothers, what are you doing? We too are men, subject to suffering, like you*[^32]. Attend. As, then, good men forbid those who wished to worship them as gods, and wish rather that one God be worshiped, one God adored, sacrifice offered to one God, not to themselves: so too all the holy angels seek the glory of him whom they love; to his worship, to his adoration, to his contemplation they are eager to snatch and inflame all whom they love; they announce him to them, not themselves, since they are angels: and because they are soldiers, they know not how to seek glory except for their Emperor; but if they sought their own glory, they would be condemned as tyrants. Such the devil and the demons became, that is, his angels: he arrogated to himself divine honor and to all the demons; and he filled the temples of the pagans, and persuaded to idols, and persuaded that those sacrifices be offered to him. Was it not better that they should worship the holy angels, than that they should worship demons? They answer: We do not worship evil demons; the angels whom you mention, them we too worship, the powers of the great God and the ministries of the great God. Would that you were willing to worship them: easily from them you would learn not to worship them. Hear the angel teaching. He was teaching a certain disciple of Christ, and showed him many miracles in the Apocalypse of John: but he, a certain miracle of the vision being shown to him, was afraid, and cast himself at the feet of the angel; and that angel who sought nothing but the glory of his Lord: *Arise, what are you doing?* he said, *adore him; for I too am your fellow-servant, and of your brothers*[^33]. What then, my brothers? Let no one say: I fear lest the angel be angry with me, if I do not worship him as my god. Then he is angry with you, when you wish to worship him: for he is good, and loves God. As the demons are angry, if they are not worshiped; so the angels are indignant, if they are worshiped as God. But lest perhaps a weak heart, a trembling heart, say to itself: Therefore if the demons are angry because they are not worshiped, I fear to offend the demons. What will even their prince the devil do to you? If he could do anything, none of us would remain. Are not daily so great things said against him by the mouth of Christians, and the harvest of Christians grows? When you are angry with your most wicked servant, you put this name on him: Satan, devil; this you say to him. Perhaps in this you err, because you say this to a man, and by immoderate anger you are carried to revile the image of God: and yet you choose to say to him this which you greatly detest. If he could, would he not avenge himself? But he is not permitted: and he does only as much as he is permitted. For he wished to tempt Job too, and he sought only power[^34]; and he would do nothing, unless he received power. Why, then, do you not securely adore God, with whom unwilling no one harms you, and with whom permitting you are amended, not overthrown? For if it pleases the Lord your God to permit that some man harm you, or some spirit harm you; he will amend you, that you may cry to him: *Chastening the Lord has chastened me, but has not given me over to death*[^35]. Therefore: *Let all be confounded who adore graven things, who glory in their idols. Adore him, all his angels.* Let the pagans learn to adore God. They wish to adore angels; let them imitate the angels, and adore him who is adored by the angels. *Adore him, all his angels.* Let that angel adore who was sent to Cornelius; for adoring him, he sent Cornelius to Peter[^36]: let him adore Christ the Lord of Peter, he himself the fellow-servant of Peter. *Adore him, all his angels.*

**13.** [v. 8.] *Zion heard, and was made glad.* What did Zion hear? That all his angels adore him. What did Zion hear? behold what it heard: *The heavens announced his righteousness, and all the peoples saw his glory: let all be confounded who adore graven things, who glory in their idols.* For the Church was not yet among the Gentiles; in Judea those of the Jews had believed, and the Jews themselves who had believed supposed that they alone belonged to Christ: the Apostles were sent to the Gentiles, it was preached to Cornelius; Cornelius believed, was baptized, and those too who were with Cornelius were baptized. But that they might be baptized, what was done, you know: the Reader indeed did not come this far, but yet some remember; and let those who do not remember, hear from me briefly. An angel was sent to Cornelius, the angel sent Cornelius to Peter, Peter came to Cornelius. And because Cornelius was of the Gentiles, both he and those who were with him were not circumcised: lest, then, they should hesitate to hand over the Gospel to the uncircumcised; before Cornelius himself and those who were with him were baptized, the Holy Spirit came, and filled them, and they began to speak in tongues. But on no one had the Holy Spirit fallen, except one who had been baptized: but on these he fell before baptism. For Peter could hesitate whether he should baptize the uncircumcised: the Holy Spirit came, they began to speak in tongues; the invisible gift was given, and took away the doubt about the visible Sacrament; all were baptized. And you have it there written: *But the Apostles, and the brethren who were in Judea, heard that the Gentiles too had received the word of God, and they blessed God*[^37]. This is what he here recalls: *Zion heard, and was made glad; and the daughters of Judea exulted.* What did Zion hear, and was made glad? *That the Gentiles received the word of God.* One wall had come, but the corner was not yet. Zion itself, the Church which was in Judea, is here properly named. *Zion heard, and was made glad; and the daughters of Judea exulted.* So it is written: *The Apostles, and the brethren who were in Judea, heard.* See whether the daughters of Judea did not exult. What did they hear? *That the Gentiles too received the word of God.* Where did this psalm say that? *The heavens announced his righteousness, and all the peoples saw his glory.* And because the Gentiles believed, by whom the idols were worshiped; he went on to say: *Let all be confounded who adore graven things, who glory in their idols. Zion heard, and was made glad; and the daughters of Judea exulted.* Afterward certain of the circumcision wished to find fault with Peter, and said to him: *Why did you go in to the uncircumcised Gentiles, and eat with them*[^38]? But he rendered them an account, how, when he was praying, there was shown to him a dish hanging by four lines. That dish which had all animals signified all the Gentiles. But it hung by four lines because there are four parts of the world, whence the future peoples were: and therefore four Gospels preach Christ, that his grace may be understood to pertain to all four parts of the world. Since, then, such a vision had been shown to Peter, he indicated, opened to them all, how Cornelius believed, that before the gentile man was baptized, the Holy Spirit came upon him. When they had heard these things, they were silent, and magnified God, saying: *Then to the Gentiles too God has given repentance unto life*[^39]. Behold: *Zion heard, and was made glad; and the daughters of Judea exulted, on account of your judgments, O Lord.* What judgments? Because God is not an accepter of persons. For Peter himself, when he had seen Cornelius the centurion, and those who were with him, filled with the Holy Spirit, cried out, and said: *In truth I perceive that God is not an accepter of persons*[^40]. Therefore: *The daughters of Judea exulted, on account of your judgments, O Lord.* What is, *on account of your judgments?* Because *in every nation, and in every people, whoever will serve him is accepted by him*[^41]. Because he is not the God of the Jews only, but also of the Gentiles[^42].

**14.** [v. 9.] See whether it is not this whence the daughters of Judea exulted. *And the daughters of Judea exulted, on account of your judgments, O Lord. For you are the Lord Most High over all the earth.* Not over Judea alone, not over Jerusalem alone, not over Zion alone, but *over all the earth.* For this whole earth the judgments of God prevailed, to call together peoples from every side: with whom those do not communicate, who have cut themselves off; they neither hear it foretold, nor see it fulfilled: *For you are the Lord Most High over all the earth. You are exceedingly exalted above all gods.* What is, *exceedingly?* For it is said of Christ. What is, then, *exceedingly,* except that you may be understood equal to the Father? What is, *above all gods?* who are they? Idols have no sense, have no life: demons have sense, have life; but they are evil. What great thing is it that Christ is exalted above idols? He is exalted above the demons; but not even this is very great: the demons indeed are the gods of the Gentiles, but he is exceedingly exalted above all gods. And men too were called gods: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High*: likewise it is written: *God stood in the synagogue of gods; and in the midst distinguishing gods*[^43]. Above all is our Lord Jesus Christ exalted: not only above idols, not only above demons; but above all righteous men. And this is little; above all the angels too: for whence is it: *Adore him, all his angels? You are exceedingly exalted above all gods.*

**15.** [v. 10.] What, then, do we all do who have assembled to him, to him who is exceedingly exalted above all gods? He gave us a brief precept. *You who love the Lord, hate the malignant.* Christ is not worthy that with him you should love avarice. You love him; you ought to hate what he hates. A man is your enemy, this is what you are; you were created by one Creator, in one condition: and yet if your son speaks with your enemy, and comes to the house of your enemy, and has frequent conversations with him, you wish to disinherit him; because he speaks with your enemy. And how? Because you seem to yourself to have a just voice: You are a friend of my enemy, and you seek something of my property! Therefore attend. You love Christ, avarice is the enemy of Christ; why do you speak with it? I do not say, you speak with it; why do you serve it? For Christ commands many things, and you do not do them; it commands, and you do them. Christ commands you to clothe the poor, and you do not do it; avarice commands you to commit fraud, and this you rather do. If these things are so, if you are such, do not promise yourself too much the inheritance of Christ. But you say: I love Christ. *You who love the Lord, hate the malignant.* Hence it appears that you love what is good, if you are found to hate what is evil: *You who love the Lord, hate the malignant.*

**16.** But when we have begun to hate the malignant, persecutions will follow. We hate the malignant; some persecutor says to us: Commit fraud; he says to us: Adore the idol; he says to us: Put incense to the demons: but we have heard: *You who love the Lord, hate the malignant.* We have heard indeed, but if we do not do it, he rages. How long does he rage? what will he take away? Answer; why are you a Christian? on account of the eternal inheritance, or on account of earthly happiness? Question your faith, set your soul on the rack of conscience, torture yourself with the fear of judgment, answer in whom you have believed, why you have believed. You say to me: I have believed in Christ. What did Christ promise you, except what he showed in himself? What did he show in himself? He died, and rose again, ascended into heaven. Do you wish to follow? Imitate the passion, await the promise. What, then, will the raging one take from you, when you have begun to hate the malignant, because you love the Lord? what will he take away? Your patrimony: surely not heaven? Finally, whatever God gave you, let him take it away (he does not indeed take it away, if God does not will; but if God wills, he takes away what God gave, lest God himself take himself away from you): God no one will take from you, you take him from yourself, if you flee him.

**17.** Perhaps you answer: I do not care about my patrimony; *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away;* I can say: *As it pleased the Lord, so has it been done*[^44]; but I fear lest he kill me. This is the whole thing. Hear, then, the Psalm consoling: *The Lord guards the souls of his servants.* Because, then, he had said above: *You who love the Lord, hate the malignant;* lest you therefore fear to hate the malignant, lest the malignant kill you, he subjoined straightway: *The Lord guards the souls of his servants.* Hear him guarding the souls of his servants, and saying: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^45]. He kills the body too, who can do the most against you: what has he done to you? What also to your Lord God. Why do you love to have what Christ has, if you fear to suffer what Christ suffered? He came to bear your temporal, weak, death-bound life. Surely fear to die, if you can not die. What by nature you cannot avoid, why do you not undertake for faith? Let the threatening adversary take from you that life, God gives you another life: because he himself too gave you this one, and if he does not will, not even this is taken away; but if he wills that it be taken from you, he has what to exchange with you; do not fear to be despoiled for him. Do you not wish to be stripped of a tattered garment? He is to give you a robe of glory. What robe do you say to me? *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality*[^46]. This very flesh of yours will not perish. Up to death the enemy can rage: power beyond that he has not, neither over the soul, nor over the very flesh; because even if he scatters the flesh, he does not impede the resurrection. About their soul men were afraid, and what did the Lord say to them? *But the very hairs of your head are all numbered*[^47]. Do you fear to lose your soul, who do not lose a hair? To God all things are numbered. He will restore all things, who created all things. They were not, and they were created: they were, and will they not be restored? Believe, then, with the whole heart, my brothers, and *you who love the Lord, hate the malignant.* Be strong, not only in loving God, but also in hating the malignant. Let no one terrify you; more powerful is he who called you, he is almighty; he is stronger than every strong one, higher than every lofty one. The Son of God died for us; be secure that you will receive his life, you who have as a pledge his death. For whom did he die? was it for the righteous? Question Paul. *For Christ died for the impious*[^48]. You were impious, and he died for you, you were justified, and will he desert you? He who justified the impious, will he forsake the pious? *You who love the Lord, hate the malignant.* Let no one fear: *The Lord guards the souls of his servants, from the hand of the sinner he will rescue them.*

**18.** [v. 11.] But perhaps you are about to say: I lose this light. *Light has arisen for the righteous.* What light do you fear to lose? do you fear to be in darkness? Do not fear to lose the light, rather fear lest, while you guard against losing this light, you lose that true light. For that which you fear to lose, we see to whom it was given, with whom it is common to you. Do the good alone see this sun, since he makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust[^49]? This light the iniquitous see with you, the robbers see with you, the shameless see with you, the beasts, the flies, the worms see with you. What kind of light does he keep for the righteous, who gives this one even to these? Rightly the martyrs saw this light in faith. For those who despised this one, saw a certain light which they desired, who spurned this one. *Light has arisen for the righteous, and for the upright in heart gladness.* Do not suppose that they were truly in misery, when they walked in a chain. The prison was broad for the faithful, the chains were light for those confessing. They had joys on the rack, who preached Christ amid torments. *Light has arisen for the righteous.* What light has arisen for the righteous? That which does not arise for the unrighteous; not this light which he makes rise upon the good and the evil. There is another light which arises for the righteous; of which light, not arisen for them, the unrighteous will say at the end: *Therefore we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shone for us, and the sun has not arisen for us*[^50]. Behold, by loving this sun, they lay in the darkness of the heart. What did it profit to see this one with the eyes, and not to see that one with the mind? Tobit was blind, and was teaching his son the way of God. You know this, that Tobit was admonishing his son, and saying: *Son, do almsgiving, because almsgiving does not permit to go into darkness*[^51]: and he was speaking who was in darkness. You see that there is another light which arises for the righteous, and for the upright in heart gladness? He had not eyes, and said to his son: *Do almsgiving; almsgiving does not permit to go into darkness*: nor did he fear lest his son say to him in his heart: But you did not do almsgiving: why do you, blind, speak to me? Behold, your almsgiving led you to darkness, and how do you say to me: *Almsgiving does not permit to go into darkness?* Why did he say these things with confidence, except because he saw another light? The son held the father's hand, that he might walk: but the father taught the son the way, that he might live. There is, then, another light, which arises for the righteous: *Light has arisen for the righteous, and for the upright in heart gladness.* Do you wish to know it? Be upright in heart. What is it, Be upright in heart? Do not be of a distorted heart toward God, resisting his will, and wishing to bend him to you, and not to direct yourself to him, and you will feel that gladness which all who are upright in heart know. *Light has arisen for the righteous, and for the upright in heart gladness.*

**19.** [v. 12.] *Be glad, you righteous.* Now perhaps the faithful, hearing: *Be glad,* meditate on banquets, prepare cups, await the time of roses; because it was said: *Be glad, you righteous.* See what follows: *in the Lord. Be glad, you righteous, in the Lord.* Do you await the time of spring, that you may be glad: you have the Lord as gladness, the Lord is always with you, he has no season; you have him by night, you have him by day. Be upright in heart, and there is always for you from him gladness. For the gladness which is according to the world is not true gladness. Hear the prophet Isaiah: *There is no rejoicing for the impious, says the Lord*[^52]. What the impious call rejoicing is not rejoicing. What kind of rejoicing did he know who disapproved this rejoicing? Let us believe him, brothers. He was a man, but he knew both rejoicings. Surely he knew the rejoicings of the cup, because he was a man, he knew the rejoicing of the table, he knew the rejoicing of the bed, he knew those worldly and luxurious rejoicings. He who knew those, said, presuming: *There is no rejoicing for the impious, says the Lord.* But it is not a man who says it; *the Lord says it*: from the truth of the Lord: *There is no rejoicing for the impious.* For they seem to themselves to rejoice: *But there is no rejoicing for the impious, he says,* not a man, but *the Lord.* Whence he, seeing that very rejoicing, says: *And the day of men I have not desired, you know*[^53]. You who show me another day, who teach me another light, who flood me with another gladness, who intimate to me something else within, have made me not desire the day of men. Isaiah surely saw men in drinking, in luxury, in theaters, and spectacles, the whole world reveling in various trifles; and yet he cried out: *There is no rejoicing for the impious, says the Lord.* If this is not rejoicing, what kind of rejoicing did he see, in comparison with which this was not rejoicing? As if you knew the sun, and to someone praising a lamp said: This is not light. Why is it not light? He holds it as something great, he rejoices, he exults; and you say: This is not light. Or if anyone marveled at an ape, you would say: This is not beauty. And if perhaps he were busy about the composition of the limbs in that beast, and marveled at all those congruities; you who knew another beauty, would deny this one, and say: It is not. Why? Because you know another. But you say: That which Isaiah saw, I do not see. Believe, and you will see. For perhaps you have not that by which you may see; for there is an eye by which that beauty is seen. For as there is the eye of the flesh, by which this light is seen; so there is the eye of the heart, by which that gladness is seen: perhaps that eye is wounded, is soiled, is troubled by anger, by avarice, by cupidity, by senseless lust; your eye is troubled, it cannot see that light. Believe, before you see: you will be healed, and you will see. *Light has arisen for the righteous, and for the upright in heart gladness.*

**20.** *Be glad,* he says, *you righteous, in the Lord: and confess to the memory of his holiness.* Now glad in the Lord, now rejoicing in the Lord, confess to him; because unless he willed it, we would not rejoice in him. For the Lord himself says: *These things I have spoken to you, that in me you may have peace, but in the world pressure*[^54]. If you are Christians, hope for pressures in this world; do not hope for more tranquil and better times. Brothers, you deceive yourselves; what the Gospel does not promise you, do not promise yourselves. What the Gospel says, you know: we speak to Christians; we ought not to be transgressors of the faith. The Gospel says this, that in the last times many evils, many scandals, many pressures, many iniquities will abound: but he who perseveres unto the end, he shall be saved. *The love of many,* he says, *will grow cold*[^55]. He, then, who perseveringly is fervent in spirit, according to the Apostle, who says: *Fervent in spirit*[^56], his love will not grow cold; because that very love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^57]. Let no one, then, promise himself what the Gospel does not promise. Behold gladder times will come, and I will do this, and buy that. It is good for you to attend to him who is not deceived, nor deceived anyone, who promised you not gladness here, but in himself; and that when these things have passed, you may hope that with him you will reign forever; lest, when you wish to reign here, you neither have gladness here, nor find it there.
