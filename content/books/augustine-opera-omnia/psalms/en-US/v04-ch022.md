# Exposition of Psalm 92

**1.** The title of this psalm, when it was pronounced, we heard; and what it means, from the Scripture of God—that is, from the book of Genesis—it is not difficult to know: for in the title we are as it were admonished at the threshold what we should seek within. It is inscribed thus: *The praise of a song to David himself, on the day before the sabbath, when the earth was founded.* Recalling, then, through all the days what God did, when he created and ordered all things from the first day to the sixth day (for the seventh he sanctified, because in it he rested after all the works which he made very good), we find that on the sixth day he made (which day is here recalled, because he says, *before the sabbath*) all the animals on the earth: then on that same day he made man to his own image and likeness. But not without cause are those days so ordered, except because the ages too were so to run their course, before we rest in God. But then we rest, if we do good works. To this end it is written of God: *God rested on the seventh day*[^1], when he had made all the works very good. For he was not wearied, that he should rest, nor does he now not work; since the Lord Christ openly says: *My Father works until now*[^2]. For he says this to the Jews, who thought carnally of God, nor understood that God works with quiet, and always works, and is always quiet. Therefore we too, whom God then willed to figure in himself, after all good works shall have rest. And indeed our good works, brothers, which we work here in the world before our rest, are as it were with labor; and that rest is in hope, not yet held in reality: and unless it were in hope, we would fail in labor; but the laborious and good works pass away. For what is so good as to hold out bread to the hungry? and what we heard just now, when the Gospel was read, what is so good as that which it admonished in general: *Everyone who has two tunics, let him give to him who has none; and he who has food, let him feed the hungry*[^3]? To clothe the naked is a good work: will this work always be good? It has a little labor, but it has solace in the hope of future rest. But how much labor does it have to strip the clothed one? A good work does not labor much: an evil work has labor. For he who clothes the naked, if he has whence to do it, does not labor: if he has not whence to do it: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^4]. But he who wishes to strip the clothed, how much he labors, who reckons? And yet these things too are to pass, when we have come to that rest, where no one hungers who may be fed, no one is naked who may be clothed. Therefore, because these good works too are to pass, this sixth day too, when good works are perfected very well, has its evening. But in the sabbath we do not find an evening, because our rest will have no end: for evening is set for an end. As, then, on the sixth day God made man to his own image; so we find that in the sixth age the Lord Jesus Christ came, that man might be reformed to the image of God. For the first time, as the first day, from Adam to Noah: the second time, as the second day, from Noah to Abraham: the third time, as the third day, from Abraham to David: the fourth time, as the fourth day, from David to the migration to Babylon: the fifth time, as the fifth day, from the migration to Babylon to the preaching of John. The sixth day is now enacted from the preaching of John to the end, and after the end of the sixth day we arrive at the rest. Now, then, the sixth day is enacted. If the sixth day is enacted, see what the title has: *On the day before the sabbath, when the earth was founded.* Now let us hear the Psalm itself; let us ask it how the earth was founded, lest perhaps then the earth was made: nor do we read so in Genesis. When, then, was the earth founded? When, except when there happens what was just now read in the Apostle: *If indeed you stand in faith,* he says, *stable and immovable*[^5]? When all who believe throughout the whole earth are immovable in faith, the earth is founded: then man is made to the image of God. Which that sixth day of Genesis signifies. But how did God do that? how was the earth founded? Christ came, to found the earth. *For foundation no one can lay besides that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^6]. Of him, then, the Psalm sings:

**2.** [v. 1.] *The Lord has reigned, he has put on comeliness; the Lord has put on strength, and has girded himself.* We see that he put on two things, comeliness and strength. But to what end? That he might found the earth. For it follows: *For he confirmed the world, which shall not be moved.* Whence did he confirm it? Because he put on comeliness. He would not confirm it, if he put on comeliness alone, not also strength. Why, then, comeliness? why strength? For he said both: *The Lord has reigned, he has put on comeliness; the Lord has put on strength; and has girded himself.* You know, brothers, that when our Lord had come in the flesh, to those to whom he was preaching the Gospel of the kingdom, to some he was pleasing, to others displeasing. For the tongues of the Jews were divided against themselves: *Some said: For he is good; others said: No, but he leads the crowds astray*[^7]. Some, then, spoke well; others disparaged, tore at, bit, reviled. To those, then, to whom he was pleasing, *he put on comeliness*: to those to whom he was displeasing, *he put on strength.* Imitate, then, you too your Lord, that you may be able to be his garment: be with comeliness toward those to whom your good works are pleasing; be strong against the disparagers. Hear the apostle Paul imitating his Lord, how he too had comeliness, had strength. *We are the good odor of Christ,* he says, *in every place, both in those who are saved, and in those who perish.* For those to whom the good is pleasing, are saved; those who disparage the good, perish. What was his own, he had a good odor; rather, he was a good odor: but woe to the wretched who die even from a good odor. For he did not say: To those we are a good odor, to those we are an evil odor; but: *We are the good odor of Christ,* he says, *in every place, both in those who are saved, and in those who perish.* He subjoined straightway: *To some indeed we are the odor of life unto life, to others the odor of death unto death*[^8]. To those to whom he was the odor of life unto life, he had put on comeliness: to those to whom he was the odor of death unto death, he had put on strength. But if you rejoice then, when men praise you, and your good works are pleasing to them; but when they have blamed you, you fail in good works, and as if you had lost the fruit of the good, because you find reprovers: you have not stood immovable; you do not belong to *the world which shall not be moved. The Lord has put on strength, and has girded himself.* Of that very comeliness and strength the apostle Paul has another place: *Through the arms of righteousness on the right and the left.* See where comeliness, where strength: *Through glory and ignobility.* In glory comely, in ignobility strong. Among some he was proclaimed glorious; among others, ignoble, he was despised. He brought comeliness to those to whom he was pleasing, strength against those to whom he was displeasing. And so he enumerates all things to the end, where he says: *As having nothing, and possessing all things*[^9]. When he possesses all things, he is comely; when he has nothing, he is strong. It is no wonder, then, if it follows: *For he confirmed the world, which shall not be moved.* For how shall the world not be moved? When all the faithful believe in Christ, and are ready for both; to rejoice with those who praise, to be strong against those who blame; not to soften at the tongues of those who praise, nor to be broken at the tongues of those who blame.

**3.** Perhaps let us inquire also about this word, why he said, *he has girded himself.* Girding signifies works: for then each one girds himself, when he is about to work. But why did he not say: He was girt; but, *he has girded himself?* For he says in another psalm: *Gird your sword upon your thigh, O most mighty; the peoples shall fall under you*[^10]. Nor there did he say: Be girt, nor, gird yourself about, but, *gird on*: for you gird on when you join something to your side by girding; therefore: *Gird on your sword.* The sword of the Lord, whence he subdued the world, by killing wickedness, is the Spirit of God in the truth of the word of God. Why is the sword said to be girded on around the thigh? We have said one thing out of another concerning girding from another psalm; but yet, because it was recalled, it is not to be passed over. What is the girding-on of the sword around the thigh? By the thigh he signifies the flesh. For the Lord would not otherwise subdue the world, unless the sword of truth came into the flesh. Here, then, why *has he girded himself?* He who girds himself sets before himself something with which he girds himself: whence it was said: *He girded himself with a linen cloth, and washed the feet of his disciples.* For because he was then humble, when he was girded with a linen cloth, he washed the feet of his disciples. But all strength is in humility; because all pride is fragile: therefore, when he spoke of strength, he added, *he has girded himself;* that you may recall God girded, humble, when he washed the feet of the disciples. And Peter shuddered at his Lord, his master (I said less by saying his master, when I had said his Lord) bending down to his feet, and washing his feet, he was afraid, and said: *Lord, you shall not wash my feet.* And he: *What I do, you do not know now; but you shall know afterward.* And he: *You shall not wash my feet forever.* And he: *If I do not wash you, you shall have no part with me.* Peter, who had first been afraid of the Lord washing his feet, was the more afraid: *You shall have no part with me;* and he believed that the Lord did not do this without cause, except that that was perhaps some great mystery, and said: *Lord, not only the feet, but also the head, and the whole.* And he: *He who has once been washed, has no need to wash again, but is wholly clean.* Therefore it did not pertain to a sacrament as it were of cleansing, that he washed their feet, but to an example of humility: for he had said this: *For what I do, you do not know; but you shall know afterward.* Let us see whether they knew afterward, let us see whether he opened to them what he was doing, that we may see the Lord girded with strength; because in humility is all strength. When he had washed their feet, he again reclined, and said to them: *You call me Master, and you say truly; for I am: you call me Lord, and you say truly; for I am: if, then, I your Master and Lord have washed your feet, how ought you to do to one another*[^11]? If, then, strength is in humility, do not fear the proud. The humble are as it were a rock: the rock is seen below, but it is solid. What of the proud? As it were smoke: even though they are high, they vanish. Therefore we ought to refer to the humility of the Lord the fact that he girded himself, according to the recollection of the Gospel, that he girded himself, to wash the feet of his disciples.

**4.** There is another thing which we can understand in this word. We said that he who girds himself sets before himself what he joins to himself, to gird himself. Since, then, those who disparage us sometimes do it to us absent, as it were behind the back; sometimes face to face, as they did to the Lord hanging on the cross: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^12]: you have no need of strength when someone disparages you absent, because you do not hear, nor feel it; but if he says it to your face, it is needful that you be strong. What is, Be strong? That you bear it: lest perhaps you suppose yourself strong, when you have heard it, because you strike with your fist, conquered by the reproach. This is not strength, if, reproached, you strike, since you have been conquered by anger: and it is very foolish to call a conquered man strong, since Scripture says: *Better is he who conquers anger, than he who takes a city*[^13]. He called the conqueror of anger better than the captor of a city. You have, then, a great adversary in yourself. When, the reproach being heard, anger begins to rise, that you may render evil for evil, recall the words of the Apostle: *Not rendering evil for evil, nor curse for curse*[^14]. These words being recalled you break the anger, you hold strength; and because he disparaged you to your face, not behind your back, you are girded with that.

**5.** Now let us hear the rest: the Psalm is brief: *For he confirmed the world, which shall not be moved.* You see, brothers, many believe in Christ, the crowd is great: and yet in this great crowd, you heard just now when the Gospel was read, that the Lord will come bearing the fan openly in his hand, and will cleanse his threshing-floor; the wheat he will store in the granary, the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire[^15]. There are, then, throughout the whole earth both good and evil: the good are grains, the evil chaff. Into the threshing-floor the threshing-sledge enters: it cuts the chaff, it cleanses the wheat. What, then, is the world *which shall not be moved?* Which surely he would not say, unless there were also a world which shall be moved. There is a world which shall not be moved, there is a world which shall be moved. Because both the good who are stable in faith are the world; lest anyone say: They are in a corner: and the evil, who do not stand in faith when they have felt some tribulation, are throughout the whole earth. There is, then, a movable world, there is an immovable world: of which the Apostle speaks. See the movable world. I ask you, of whom did the Apostle speak: *Of whom are Hymenaeus and Philetus, who have erred concerning the truth, saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and they subvert the faith of some?* Did these belong to the world which shall not be moved? but they were chaff: *And they subvert,* he says, *the faith of some.* He did not say: Of all; and even if he said of all, we ought to understand all those belonging to the city of Babylon, which is to be condemned with the devil: yet he said *the faith of some.* And as if it were said to him: And who can resist them? he subjoined straightway: *But the firm foundation of God stands.* Behold you have the world which shall not be moved. *Having this seal.* What seal does the firm foundation have? *The Lord knows who are his.* This is the world which shall not be moved: *The Lord knows who are his.* And what seal does it have? *And let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity*[^16]. Let him now depart from iniquity: for from the iniquitous he cannot depart, because the chaff is mingled with the wheat until it is winnowed. What do we say, brothers? And in the threshing-floor itself, it is a marvelous thing about the wheat: it departs from the chaff, when it is stripped; and it does not depart from the threshing-floor, when it is threshed. But when will it be altogether separated? When the winnower comes. Now, then, the threshing-floor is in the world: it is necessary that, if you make progress, you live among the iniquitous. From the iniquitous you cannot depart; from iniquity depart. *Let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity;* and he will be in the world which shall not be moved.

**6.** [v. 2.] *Your seat is prepared, O God, from then.* *From then,* what is it? Thenceforth. As if he said: What is the seat of God? where does God sit? In his saints. Do you wish to be the seat of God? Prepare a place in your heart where he may sit. What is the seat of God, except where God dwells? Where does God dwell, except in his temple? What is his temple? Is it built with walls? Far be it. Is this world perhaps his temple, because it is very great, and a thing worthy to hold God? It does not hold him by whom it was made. And where is he held? In a quiet soul, in a just soul: that bears him. A great thing, brothers: surely God is great; to the strong he is heavy, to the weak he is light. Whom did I call strong? The proud, who as it were have presumption of their own strength. For that weakness in humility is the greater strength. Hear the Apostle saying: *When I am weak, then am I strong*[^17]. This is what I commended, that the Lord girded himself with strength, when he taught humility. Therefore this itself is the seat of God, which is openly said in another place in the prophet: *Upon whom shall my Spirit rest?* that is, where shall the Spirit of God rest, except in the seat of God? Hear how he describes that seat. Perhaps you were about to hear of a marbled house, the ample spaces of courts, in great height and brightness of roofs. Hear what God prepares for himself: *Upon whom shall my Spirit rest? Upon the humble and quiet, and him who trembles at my words*[^18]. Behold, you are humble and quiet, and God dwells in you. God is high; he does not dwell in you, if you wish to be high. Surely you wish to be high, that he may dwell in you: be humble and trembling at his words, and there he dwells. He does not fear a trembling house, because he himself makes it firm. *Your seat is prepared, O God, from then. From then,* that is, thenceforth; that is, it signifies as it were a certain time. From then, from when? Perhaps from the day *before the sabbath.* *From then,* because the title of the Psalm prescribes for us from when. For on the sixth day—that is, in the sixth time of this world—the Lord came in the flesh. From then, then, surely from then according to the man, from then from the womb. For what does another psalm say? *In the splendor of the saints, from the womb. In the splendor of the saints,* that is, that the saints may be enlightened, that they may see God in the flesh; and the heart may be cleansed, whence he may be seen in his divinity. *In the splendor of the saints from the womb.* But what follows there? Lest perhaps you should suppose Christ to begin to be from the womb: *Before the morning-star I begot you*[^19]. When he had said: *In the splendor of the saints from the womb,* he subjoined straightway, lest you should think Christ began to be from the time he was born, as Adam began, as Abraham, as David: *Before the morning-star I begot you*: before all that is enlightened. For by the morning-star he signifies either all the stars, and by the stars the times, because God made the stars for signs of times[^20], that you may find Christ to have been born before the times: and surely he who was born before the times cannot be seen to be born from time; because the times too are a creature of God. And surely if all things were made through him, the times too were made through him. Or certainly before every spirit that is enlightened, he said of Wisdom: *Before the morning-star I begot you.* Let Your Charity attend. As, then, when he had said: *From the womb,* as if guarding our faith, lest from there we should suppose Christ began from the time he was born from the womb of the Virgin; he subjoined straightway: *Before the morning-star I begot you*: so here too, when he had said: *From then,* that is, from a certain time, from the day before the sabbath, from that sixth age of the world, when the Lord Christ came, and was born in the flesh, because he deigned, made man for our sake, being God not only before Abraham, but before heaven and earth, who said: *Before Abraham, I am*[^21]: not only before Abraham, but before Adam; not only before Adam, but before all the angels, before heaven and earth, because all things were made through him: he subjoined, lest you, attending to the day of the Lord's nativity from the time he was born, should suppose he began to be from then: *Your seat is prepared, O God.* But what God? *From the age you are*: from eternity he said; so the Greek has: sometimes "age" is set, sometimes "eternal" is set. Therefore, O you who seem born from then, you are from eternity. But let not human nativity be thought of, but let the divine eternity be thought of. Therefore he began from the time he was born, he grew: you heard the Gospel. He chose disciples, filled them, the disciples began to preach. Perhaps this is what he consequently says.

**7.** [vv. 3-4.] *The rivers have lifted up their voices.* What are these rivers which lifted up their voices? We did not hear: neither when the Lord was born, did we hear the rivers speak, nor when he was baptized, nor when he suffered; we did not hear the rivers speak. Read the Gospel, you do not find that the rivers spoke. It is little that they spoke, *they lifted up their voices*: not only did they speak, but strongly, greatly, loftily. What are these rivers which spoke? We said that we do not read it in the Gospel; yet let us seek it there. For if we do not find it there, where shall we find it? I will be able to invent for you: and at once I shall be not a sure steward, but an inept fabler. Let us seek in the Gospel, let us together seek what are the rivers which lifted up their voices. *Jesus stood, and cried out,* it was said in the Gospel. What did he cry out? Behold now the head itself of the rivers cries out; the fountain of life itself whence the rivers were to run, lifted up his voice first. And what did Jesus stand and cry out? *He who believes in me, as the Scripture says, rivers of living water shall flow from his belly.* The Evangelist follows straightway: *But this he said of the Spirit, whom those who were to believe in him were to receive. But the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^22]. But when Jesus was glorified in the resurrection and ascension into the heavens, as you know, brothers; ten days being fulfilled there on account of a certain sacrament, he sent his Holy Spirit, he filled the disciples[^23]. That very Spirit is a great river, whence many rivers were filled. Of that river the psalm says in another place: *The rush of the river makes glad the city of God*[^24]. Therefore there were made rivers running from the belly of the disciples, when they received the Holy Spirit: those rivers, the Holy Spirit being received. Whence did the rivers lift up their voices? why did they lift them up? Because first they feared. Peter was not yet a river, when at the questioning of a maidservant he thrice denied Christ: *I do not know the man*[^25]. Here, fearing, he lies; he does not yet lift up his voice, he is not yet a river. But when they were filled with the Holy Spirit, the Jews summoned them, and commanded them not to speak at all, nor teach in the name of Jesus. But Peter and John said to them: *If it is just before God to obey you rather than God, judge: for we cannot but speak the things we have seen and heard*[^26]. *The rivers lifted up their voice, from the voices of many waters.* To that very lifting up of the voice pertains what is there written: *But Peter stood with the eleven, and with lifted-up voice said to them: Men of Judea*[^27]; and the rest by which he announces Jesus without fear, with great confidence. *For the rivers lifted up their voice, from the voices of many waters.* For also when the Apostles were dismissed from the council of the Jews, they came to their own, and reported how great things the priests and elders had said to them: and they, hearing, lifted up their voice with one accord, all to the Lord, and said: *Lord, you are he who made heaven and earth, and the sea, and all things that are in them*[^28]; and the rest which the rivers lifting up their voice could say. *Marvelous are the surgings of the sea.* For when those disciples had lifted up their voices, many believed, and many received the Holy Spirit, and many rivers began to cry out from a few. Therefore it follows: *From the voices of many waters marvelous are the surgings of the sea*: that is, of this world. When Christ began to be preached with such great voices, the sea began to grow angry, the persecutions began to grow frequent. When, then, *the rivers had lifted up their voice, from the voices of many waters marvelous are the surgings of the sea.* The surgings are the liftings-up; because when the sea grows angry, the waves are lifted up. Let the waves be lifted up as much as they wish, let the sea rage as much as it wishes; *marvelous* indeed *are the surgings of the sea,* marvelous the threats, marvelous the persecutions, but see what follows: *Marvelous in the heights is the Lord.* Let the sea, then, restrain itself, and at some time be made tranquil, let peace be given to the Christians. The sea was troubled, the little ship was tossed. The little ship is the Church, the sea is the world. The Lord came, walked upon the sea, and pressed down the waves[^29]. How did the Lord walk upon the sea? Upon the heads of those great foaming waves. The powers and kings believed, were subjugated to Christ. Therefore let us not be terrified, because *marvelous are the surgings of the sea: Marvelous in the heights is the Lord.*

**8.** [v. 5.] *Your testimonies have been made exceedingly believable.* More than the surgings of the sea were marvelous, and the Lord marvelous in the heights. *Your testimonies have been made exceedingly believable. Your testimonies,* because he had said this before: *These things I say to you, that in me you may have peace, but in the world pressure*[^30]. Therefore, because the world is to make pressure for you, I say to you. They began to suffer, and they confirmed in themselves what the Lord had foretold them, and were made more strong. For when they saw the sufferings fulfilled in themselves, they hoped that the crowns too would be fulfilled in themselves: and therefore *marvelous are the surgings of the sea; marvelous in the heights is the Lord. That in me,* he says, *you may have peace, but in the world pressure.* What, then, do we do? The sea rages, the waves are lifted up, and rage foaming; we suffer pressures: do we not perhaps fail? Far be it. *Marvelous in the heights is the Lord.* So also there, when he said, *that in me you may have peace, but in the world pressure;* as if they should say: Do you suppose the world will not press us, and extinguish us? he subjoined straightway: *But rejoice, because I have conquered the world.* If, then, he says, *I have conquered the world,* cling to him who conquered the world, who conquered the sea. Rejoice in him, because the Lord is marvelous in the heights, and *your testimonies have been made exceedingly believable.* And what was made of all these things? *Holiness befits your house, O Lord.* Your house, your whole house: not here, or here, or there; but your whole house, throughout the whole earth? Why throughout the whole earth? Because he corrected the earth, which shall not be moved. The house of the Lord will be strong; it will be throughout the whole earth: many will fall, but that house stands; many will be troubled, but that house will not be moved. *Holiness befits your house, O Lord.* For a short time? Far be it. *In length of days.*
