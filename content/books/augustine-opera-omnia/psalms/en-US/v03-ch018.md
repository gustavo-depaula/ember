# Exposition of Psalm 67

**1.** [v. 1] The title of this psalm does not seem to call for laborious discussion: for it appears simple and easy. For it runs thus: *Unto the end, for David himself, a Psalm of a canticle.*[^1] But in many psalms we have already admonished what is: *Unto the end*: since *the end of the Law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^2]; an end which perfects, not which consumes or destroys. But yet if anyone tries to inquire what is meant by: *A Psalm of a canticle;* why not either *a Psalm,* or *a Canticle,* but both; or what is the difference between a Psalm of a canticle, and a Canticle of a psalm, since the titles of certain psalms are inscribed thus too; he will perhaps find something, which we leave to the more acute and more leisurely. Some before us distinguished between a Canticle and a Psalm; that, since a Canticle is uttered by the mouth, but a Psalm is sung with a visible instrument applied, that is, the psaltery, by the Canticle there might seem to be signified the understanding of the mind, but by the Psalm the works of the body. As in this very sixty-seventh psalm, which we have now undertaken to treat, that which is said: *Sing to God, play to his name*[^3]: some so distinguished it, that: *Sing to God,* would seem to be said, because the things which the mind does in itself are known to God, are not seen by men; but good works, since they are to be seen by men, that they may glorify our Father who is in heaven, are rightly said: *Play to his name,* that is, to the spreading abroad of his name, that it may be praised. This distinction somewhere, as far as I recall, I myself followed too. But I remember that we have also read: *Play to God*; because such things as we do visibly and well, please not only men, but also God. But not all the things that please God can please men too, because they cannot see them. Whence it is a wonder if, as both are read, both *Sing to God,* and *Play to God;* so somewhere it can be read: *Sing to his name.* But if this too is found said in the holy Scriptures, this distinction has been worked out in vain. It also moves me that they are by a general name called rather Psalms than Canticles, so that the Lord said: *The things that are written in the Law, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me*[^4]; and the book itself is called of Psalms, not of Canticles: *As it is written,* he says, *in the book of Psalms*[^5]; when rather, according to this distinction, they ought to have seemed to be called Canticles; since a Canticle can exist even without a Psalm: but a Psalm cannot exist without a Canticle. For there can be thoughts of the mind, of which there are no bodily works: but there is no good work, of which there is not a thought in the mind. And through this in both cases there are Canticles; not in both cases Psalms: and yet, as I said, they are generally called Psalms, not Canticles; and a book of Psalms, not of Canticles. And if the senses of the sayings are understood and examined, where the title is only of a *Psalm,* and where only of a *Canticle,* and where it is inscribed not *a Psalm of a Canticle,* as in this one, but *a Canticle of a Psalm*; I do not know whether that distinction can be demonstrated. Accordingly, as we had begun, leaving these things to those who can, and to whom there is leisure to distinguish such things, and to define them by a sure rationale of differences; let us, as far as we are able with the Lord's help, consider and treat the text of this psalm.

**2.** [v. 2] *Let God arise, and let his enemies be scattered.*[^6] It is already done: Christ rose again, who is over all things God blessed forever, and his enemies were scattered among all the nations, the Jews; in that very place where they exercised their enmities, conquered, and thence scattered through all places: and now they hate, but they fear; and in that very fear they do what follows: *And let those who hate him flee from his face.* For the flight of the mind is fear. For by carnal flight, whither do they flee from the face of him who shows everywhere the effect of his presence? *Whither shall I go,* says that one, *from your spirit, and whither shall I flee from your face*[^7]? In mind, then, not in body they flee; namely by fearing, not by hiding; nor from that face which they do not see, but from that which they are compelled to see. For his face is called his presence through his Church. Whence to those who were his enemies he said: *Henceforth you will see the Son of man coming in the clouds*[^8]. As he came in his Church, spreading it throughout the whole world, in which his enemies were scattered. But he came in such clouds as those of which he says: *I will command my clouds not to rain rain upon it*[^9]. *Let*, then, *those who hate him flee from his face*: let them fear from the presence of his saints and faithful, of whom he says: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^10].

**3.** [v. 3] *As smoke fails, let them fail.*[^11] For they lifted themselves up from the fires of their hatreds into the swelling of pride; and setting their mouth in heaven, and crying: *Crucify, crucify*[^12], they mocked him captive, derided him hanging: and whence as victors they swelled, soon, conquered, they vanished. *As wax flows from the face of fire, so let the sinners perish from the face of God.* Although perhaps in this place he signified those whose hardness is dissolved into the tears of repentance: yet that too can be understood, that he threatens the future judgment; because, when in this age, by lifting themselves up like smoke, that is, by being proud, they have failed, there will come to them in the end the final damnation, that they perish from his face forever, when he has been presented in his brightness, like fire, to the punishment of the ungodly and the light of the just.

**4.** [v. 4] Finally there follows: *And let the just be glad, and exult in the sight of God, and be delighted in gladness.*[^13] For then they will hear: *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom*[^14]. *Let them be glad,* then, who have labored, *and exult in the sight of God.* For there will be of this exultation no empty boasting, as it were before men; but in the sight of him who without error inspects what he gave. *Let them be delighted in gladness*: no longer exulting with trembling, as in this age, as long as human life upon earth is a temptation.

**5.** [vv. 5-6] Then he turns himself to those very ones to whom he gave so great a hope, and addresses and exhorts them while they live here: *Sing to God, play to his name.*[^15] Already here in the exposition of the title we said beforehand what seemed to be. He sings to God who lives to God; he plays to his name who works for his glory. So by singing, so by playing, that is, by so living, by so working: *Make a way for him*, he says, *who ascends over the setting.* Make a way for Christ; that through the beautiful feet of those who evangelize, the hearts of believers may be passable for him. For he it is who ascends over the setting: either because the new life of one converting himself to him does not receive him, unless the old life of one renouncing this age has set; or because he ascends over the setting, when by rising again he conquered the fall of the body. *For the Lord is his name.* Which if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory.

**6.** *Exult in his sight.*[^16] O you to whom it was said: *Sing to God, play to his name, make a way for him who ascends over the setting,* also *exult in his sight*: as it were sad, but always rejoicing. For while you make a way for him, while you prepare the way by which he may come, and possess the nations, you are going to suffer much sadness in the sight of men: but you, not only do not fail, but even exult; not in the sight of men, but in the sight of God. Rejoicing in hope, enduring in tribulation. *Exult in his sight.* For those who trouble you in the sight of men: *They will be troubled at his face, the father of orphans and the judge of widows.*[^17] For they think those desolate, from whom very often, by the sword of the word of God, both parents are separated from children, and husbands from wives: but the forsaken and widowed have the consolation *of the father of orphans and the judge of widows;* they have the consolation of him to whom they say: *For my father and my mother forsook me; but the Lord took me up*[^18]: and who have hoped in the Lord, persisting in prayers night and day; at whose face those will be troubled, when they see that they profit nothing, since the whole world has gone after him.

**7.** [v. 7] For of these orphans and widows, that is, of those forsaken by the fellowship of worldly hope, the Lord builds for himself a temple: of which he consequently says: *The Lord, in his holy place.*[^19] For what his place is he opened, when he said: *God who makes those of one kind dwell in a house*: of one mind, feeling one thing: this is the holy place of the Lord. For when he had said: *The Lord in his holy place*: as though we asked in what place, since he is everywhere whole, and no place of bodily spaces contains him, at once he added; lest we seek him apart from ourselves; but rather, dwelling *of one kind in a house,* let us merit that he too deign to dwell in us. This is the holy place of the Lord, which most men seek, that they may have where, praying, they may be heard. Let them therefore be themselves what they seek; and the things they say in their hearts, that is, in such couches of theirs, let them be pricked, dwelling *of one kind in a house,* that they may be dwelt in by the Lord of the great house, and be heard within themselves. For there is a great house, in which there are not only golden and silver vessels, but also wooden and earthen; and some indeed unto honor, others unto dishonor: but if any cleanse themselves from the vessels of dishonor, they will be *of one kind in a house,* and the holy place of the Lord. For as in a man's great house, the lord of it does not rest in just any place of it, but in some more secret and more honored place: so God does not dwell in all who are in his house (for he does not dwell in the vessels of dishonor); but his holy place are those whom *he makes dwell of one kind,* or *of one custom, in a house.* For what in Greek are called *tropoi*, can be interpreted in Latin both "kinds" and "customs." Nor does the Greek have: *Who makes to dwell;* but only, *makes to dwell. The Lord*, then, *in his holy place.* What is this place? God himself makes it for himself. For God *makes those of one custom dwell in a house*: this is his holy place.

**8.** But that by his grace he builds for himself this place, not by their preceding merits from which he builds it, see what follows: *Who leads out those in fetters in strength.*[^20] For he looses the heavy chains of sins, by which they were hindered from walking in the way of the precepts: but he leads them out in strength, which before his grace they did not have. *Likewise the embittering ones who dwell in tombs*: that is, in every way dead, occupied in dead works. For these embitter by resisting righteousness: for those in fetters perhaps wish to walk, and cannot; and they pray to God that they may be able, and say to him: *From my necessities lead me out*[^21]. By whom heard, they give thanks, saying: *You have burst my bonds*[^22]. But these embittering ones who dwell in tombs are of that kind which Scripture signifies in another place, saying: *From a dead man, as from one who is not, confession perishes*[^23]. Whence is that: *The sinner, when he comes into the depth of evils, despises*[^24]. For it is one thing to desire, another to assail righteousness; one thing to wish to be freed from evil, another to defend one's own evils rather than confess them: yet both kinds the grace of Christ *leads out in strength.* By what strength, except that they may strive against sin even unto blood? For from each kind there are made fit those of whom his holy place is constructed; the former being loosed, the latter being raised up. For he, by commanding, loosed the chains of the woman whom Satan had bound for eighteen years; and by crying out he overcame the death of Lazarus. He who did these things in bodies, can do more wonderful things in morals, and make *those of one kind dwell in a house: leading out those in fetters in strength; likewise the embittering ones who dwell in tombs.*

**9.** [vv. 8-10] *O God, when you went out before your people.*[^25] His going out is understood, when he appears in his works. But he appears not to all, but to those who have known how to behold his works. For I do not now mean those works which are conspicuous to all, the heaven, and the earth, and the sea, and all that is in them; but the works by which *he leads out those in fetters in strength, likewise the embittering ones who dwell in tombs, and makes them dwell of one custom in a house.* So he goes out before his people, that is, before those who understand this grace of his. Finally there follows: *When you passed through the desert, the earth was moved.* The Gentiles were a desert, who did not know God: it was a desert, where no law had been given by God himself; where no prophets had dwelt, and foretold that the Lord was to come. *When*, then, *you passed through the desert,* when you were preached among the Gentiles, *the earth was moved,* earthly men were stirred to faith. But whence was it moved? *For the heavens dropped down from the face of God.* Perhaps here someone recalls that time, when in the desert God passed before his people, before the sons of Israel, by day in a column of cloud, by night in the brightness of fire; and thinks this to be what *the heavens dropped down from the face of God,* since manna rained for his people; and also this to be what follows: *Mount Sinai, from the face of the God of Israel, segregating a voluntary rain, O God, for your inheritance,* because on Mount Sinai God spoke to Moses, when he gave the Law: so that the manna is the voluntary rain which God segregated for his inheritance, that is, for his people: because he so fed them alone, not the other nations also: so that what he then says: *And it was weakened,* may be understood that very inheritance was weakened; since, murmuring, fastidious, they rejected the manna, desiring meats of flesh, and the things they had been accustomed to eat in Egypt. But in these words, if only the property of the letter, not the spiritual sense, is to be sought, it must be shown according to the bodily property, who then were the ones in fetters, and who also the ones dwelling in tombs that were led out in strength. Then if that people, namely that inheritance of God, by rejecting the manna in fastidiousness was weakened, there ought not to follow: *But you perfected it;* but: But you struck it. For God being offended by those murmurings and fastidiousnesses, a huge plague followed. Finally all those were laid low in the wilderness, nor did any of them, except two, merit to enter the land of promise. Although, even if it is said that that inheritance was perfected in their sons, we ought more freely to hold the spiritual sense. For all those things happened to them in figure; until the day should breathe, and the shadows be removed.

**10.** Let the Lord, then, open to us as we knock; and let the secrets of his mysteries be laid open, as far as he himself deigns. For that the earth might be moved to faith, when the Gospel passed into the desert of the Gentiles, *the heavens dropped down from the face of God.* These are the heavens, of which it is sung in another psalm: *The heavens declare the glory of God.* For of these it is said a little after there: *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard: into all the earth their sound has gone forth, and to the ends of the world their words*[^26]. Nor yet is so great glory to be attributed to these heavens, as though from men that grace came into the desert of the Gentiles, that the earth might be moved to faith. For the heavens did not drop down of themselves; but *from the face of God,* namely dwelling in them, and making them dwell of one custom in a house. For they are also the mountains, of which it is said: *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me.* And yet, lest he should seem to have placed his hope in men, he at once added: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^27]. For to him it is said in another place: *You who enlighten wonderfully, from the eternal mountains*[^28]: although from the eternal mountains, yet you who enlighten. So here too, *the heavens dropped down;* but, *from the face of God.* For they too were saved through faith, and this not of themselves; but it is the gift of God: not of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up. For we are his handiwork, *who makes those of one kind dwell in a house*[^29].

**11.** But what is it that follows: *Mount Sinai, from the face of the God of Israel*[^30]? Or is "dropped down" to be understood: so that what he called by the name of the heavens, this he wished to be understood also by the name of Mount Sinai; as we said that those are called mountains who are called heavens? Nor in this sense ought it to move us that he says, *mount;* not, mountains; when there they were called, heavens; not, heaven: because in another psalm too, when it had been said: *The heavens declare the glory of God;* in the manner of Scripture repeating the same sense in other words, it was subsequently said: *And the firmament announces the works of his hands*[^31]. First it said, heavens; not heaven; and yet afterward not, firmaments; but, firmament. *For God called the firmament, heaven*[^32], as is written in Genesis. So, then, heavens and heaven, mountains and mount, not something else, but this same thing: as many Churches and one Church, not something else, but this same thing. Why, then, *Mount Sinai which begets unto servitude*[^33], as the Apostle says? Or perhaps the Law itself is to be understood on Mount Sinai, which *the heavens dropped down from the face of God,* that the earth might be moved? and this is the moving of the earth, when men are troubled, because they cannot fulfill the Law? But if it is so, this is also the voluntary rain, of which he consequently says: *Segregating a voluntary rain, O God, for your inheritance;* because he did not do so to any nation, and his judgments he did not manifest to them. So God segregated this voluntary rain for his inheritance, in that he gave the Law. *And it was weakened*: either the Law itself, or the inheritance itself. The Law can be understood as weakened, in that it was not fulfilled: not that it is itself weak, but that it makes the weak by threatening punishment, and not helping by grace. For the Apostle too used that very word, where he says: *For what was impossible to the Law, in that it was weakened through the flesh*[^34]; wishing to signify that it is fulfilled through the spirit: yet he said it itself is weakened, since it cannot be fulfilled by the weak. But that the inheritance itself, that is, the people itself, was weakened, the Law being given to it, is understood without any ambiguity. *For the Law entered in, that the offense might abound*[^35]. But what follows: *But you perfected it,* is referred to the Law in this way, that according to that it was perfected, that is, fulfilled; which the Lord says in the Gospel: *I came not to loose the Law, but to fulfill it*[^36]. Whence the Apostle too, who had said the Law was weakened through the flesh, because the flesh does not fulfill what is fulfilled through the spirit, that is, through spiritual grace; he himself likewise says: *That the righteousness of the Law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit*[^37]. This, then, is: *But you perfected it;* because *the fullness of the Law is love*[^38]; and *the love of God is poured out in our hearts,* not through ourselves, but *through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^39]: this is: *But you perfected it,* if it is understood to have perfected the Law; but if the inheritance, the understanding is easier. For if for this reason the inheritance of God is said to have been weakened, that is, the people of God weakened by the Law being given, because *the Law entered in, that the offense might abound*: then what follows: *But you perfected it,* is understood from that which follows in the Apostle too: *But where the offense abounded, grace superabounded*[^40]. For, the offense abounding, their weaknesses were multiplied; and afterward they hastened: because they groaned, and called upon him, that with his help might be fulfilled what without his command was not fulfilled.

**12.** There is in these words another sense too, which seems to me more probable. For it is much more fittingly understood that grace itself is the voluntary rain, because it is given freely, with no preceding merits of works. *For if grace; then no longer of works: otherwise grace is no longer grace*[^41]. *For I am not worthy,* he says, *to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God: but by the grace of God I am what I am*[^42]. This is the voluntary rain. *For voluntarily he begot us by the word of truth*[^43]. This is the voluntary rain. Hence elsewhere it is said: *With the shield of your good will you have crowned us*[^44]. This rain, when God passed through the desert, that is, was preached among the Gentiles, *the heavens dropped down*: yet not of themselves, but *from the face of God,* since they too by the grace of God are what they are. And therefore *Mount Sinai,* because he too who labored more than all those, yet not he, but the grace of God with him, that it might drop down more abundantly among the Gentiles, that is, in the desert, where Christ had not been announced, lest he build on another's foundation; he himself, I say, was an Israelite of the race of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin: and he too, then, had been begotten unto servitude, of the earthly Jerusalem which serves with her sons, and therefore he persecuted the Church. For, as he himself reminded us, *as then he who was begotten according to the flesh persecuted him who was according to the spirit; so also now*[^45]. But he obtained mercy because he did it ignorantly in unbelief. We marvel, then, that *the heavens dropped down from the face of God*: let us marvel more that *Mount Sinai,* that is, he who first persecuted, a Hebrew of Hebrews, according to the Law a Pharisee. But why should we marvel? For not of himself, but, as follows, *from the face of the God of Israel;* of whom he himself says: *And upon the Israel of God*[^46]; of whom the Lord says: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^47]. This voluntary rain, then, with no preceding merits of good works, God segregated for his inheritance. *And it was weakened.* For he recognized that he was not anything through himself; that what he is is to be attributed not to his own powers, but to the grace of God. He recognized what was said: *In my weaknesses I will glory*[^48]. He recognized what was said: *Do not relish high things, but fear*[^49]. He recognized what was said: *But to the humble he gives grace*[^50]. *And it was weakened; but you perfected it*: because *power is made perfect in weakness*[^51]. Certain codices indeed, both Latin and Greek, do not have, *Mount Sinai;* but *from the face of the God of Sinai, from the face of the God of Israel.* That is, *the heavens dropped down from the face of God*: and as though it were asked of what God; *from the face of God,* he says, *of Sinai, from the face of the God of Israel;* that is, from the face of the God who gave the Law to the people of Israel. Why, then, did *the heavens drop down from the face of God,* from the face of this God, except because thus was fulfilled what was foretold: *He who gave the Law will give a blessing*[^52]? *The Law,* by which he may terrify the one presuming on human powers, *the blessing,* by which he may free the one hoping in God. You, then, perfected, O God, your inheritance; because it was weakened in itself, that it might be perfected by you.

**13.** [v. 11] *Your animals will dwell in it. Yours,*[^53] not their own; subject to you, not free for themselves; in need toward you, not sufficient for themselves. Finally there follows: *You prepared in your sweetness for the needy, O God. In your sweetness,* not in his ability. For he is needy, since he was weakened, that he might be perfected: he recognized himself in need, that he might be filled. This is that sweetness of which it is said elsewhere: *The Lord will give sweetness, and our earth will give its fruit*[^54]: that a good work may be done not by fear, but by love; not by dread of punishment, but by delight in righteousness. For that is the true and sound liberty. But the Lord prepared this for the needy, not for the abounding one, to whom that poverty is a reproach; of such it is said elsewhere: *A reproach to those who abound, and contempt to the proud*[^55]. For he called those proud, whom he called those who abound.

**14.** [v. 12] *The Lord will give the word*[^56]: namely, food to his animals which will dwell in it. But what will these animals do, to whom he will give the word? what except what follows? *To those evangelizing with much power.* By what power, except that strength in which he leads out those in fetters? Perhaps he also here calls power that by which those evangelizing did wondrous signs.

**15.** [v. 13] Who, then, *will give the word to those evangelizing with much power*? *The King*, he says, *of the powers of the Beloved.*[^57] The Father, then, is the king of the powers of the Son. For the Beloved, when it is not stated whose beloved, by antonomasia is understood the only Son. Or is the Son himself the king of his powers; namely of the powers serving him? because *to those evangelizing with much power the King of the powers will give the word,* of whom it was said: *The Lord of powers, he is the King of glory*[^58]. But that he did not say: King of his powers, but: *King of the powers of the Beloved,* is a most usual expression in the Scriptures, if anyone takes note: which appears most of all there, where even a proper name is expressed, so that there can be no doubt at all that it is the same one of whom it is spoken. Such as is found in many places in the Pentateuch: *And Moses did* this and that, *as the Lord commanded Moses*[^59]. It did not say, as is usual in our expressions: And Moses did as the Lord commanded him; but: *Moses did as the Lord commanded Moses;* as though there were one Moses whom he commanded, and another Moses who did, when it is the same one. Such expressions are found very rarely in the New Testament. Yet from this is that which the Apostle says: *Concerning his Son, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh, who was predestined the Son of God in power according to the Spirit of sanctification from the resurrection of the dead, Jesus Christ our Lord*[^60]: as though there were one Son of God who was made of the seed of David according to the flesh, and another Jesus Christ our Lord, when he is one and the same. But in the old books that expression is frequent: and therefore, when it occurs somewhat obscurely, it is to be understood from manifest examples of its kind; as in this place of the psalm which we are treating it was made somewhat obscurely. For if it were said, Jesus Christ, the King of the powers of Jesus Christ, it would be as open as that: *Moses did as the Lord commanded Moses*: but because it was said: *King of the powers of the Beloved,* it does not easily occur that he himself is the King of the powers, who is also the Beloved. *The King*, then, *of the powers of the Beloved,* can be understood as if it were said, the King of his own powers, because Christ is both the King of the powers, and the same Christ is the Beloved. Although this sense does not have so great necessity that something else cannot be taken: because the Father too can be understood as the King of the powers of his beloved Son, to whom the Beloved himself says: *All mine are yours, and yours mine*[^61]. But if perhaps it is asked whether God the Father of the Lord Jesus Christ can be called King too; I do not know whether anyone would dare to take away this name from him, where the Apostle says: *But to the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God*[^62]. For even if this is said of the Trinity itself, there too is God the Father. But if we do not understand carnally: *O God, give your judgment to the king, and your righteousness to the king's son*[^63]; I do not know whether anything else is said than, To your Son. The Father, then, is King too. Whence this little verse of this psalm: *The King of the powers of the Beloved,* can be understood in either way. So, when he had said: *The Lord will give the word to those evangelizing with much power;* because that very power is ruled by him, and fights for him by whom it is given, he himself, he says, *the Lord* who *will give the word to those evangelizing with much power, is the King of the powers of the Beloved.*

**16.** Then there follows: *Of the Beloved, and of the beauty of the house, to divide spoils.*[^64] The repetition pertains to commendation: although not all codices have this repetition, and the more diligent mark it beforehand with a star, which marks are called asterisks; by which they wish it to be recognized that those things are not in the translation of the Seventy, but are in the Hebrew, which are marked with such signs. But whether it be repeated, or what is set down be taken as said once, *Of the Beloved;* thus I think is to be understood what follows, *and of the beauty of the house, to divide spoils,* as if it were said, Of the Beloved also to divide spoils for the beauty of the house, that is, of the Beloved also for the dividing of spoils. For Christ made the house beautiful, that is, the Church, by dividing spoils to it; as a body is beautiful by the distribution of members. Now spoils are called the things taken from conquered enemies. What this is the Gospel admonishes us, where we read: *No one enters the house of the strong one, to plunder his vessels, unless he first bind the strong one*[^65]. Christ, then, bound the devil with spiritual chains; by overcoming death, and by ascending above the heavens from hell: he bound him by the sacrament of his incarnation, which, finding nothing in him worthy of death, yet was permitted to kill; and so, when he was bound, took away as it were spoils, his vessels. For he was working in the sons of unbelief, whose unbelief he was using for his own will. These vessels the Lord cleansing by the remission of sins, sanctifying these spoils wrested from the enemy prostrate and bound, divided them for the beauty of his house; appointing some apostles, some prophets, some pastors and teachers for the work of the ministry, for the building up of the body of Christ. *For as there is one body, and has many members; but all the members of the body, though they be many, are one body: so also Christ. Are all apostles? are all prophets? are all powers? do all have gifts of healings? do all speak with tongues? do all interpret? But all these things one and the same Spirit works, dividing to each his own as he wills*[^66]. And this is the beauty of the house, to which spoils are divided; that its lover, kindled by this beauty, may cry out: *O Lord, I have loved the beauty of your house*[^67].

**17.** [v. 14] Now in what follows, he turns to addressing the very members of which the beauty of the house is made, saying: *If you sleep among the midst of the lots, the wings of a dove silvered over, and between her shoulders in the greenness of gold.*[^68] First here the order of the words must be sought, how the sentence is concluded; which certainly hangs, when it is said: *If you sleep*: then what he says, *the wings of a dove silvered over,* whether it is to be understood in the singular number, of this dove's wing; or in the plural, these wings. But the Greek excludes the singular number, where this is altogether read set in the plural. But it is still uncertain whether, these wings; or, O you wings, so that he seems to speak to the wings themselves. Whether, then, this sentence is concluded by the words that went before, so that the order is: *The Lord will give the word to those evangelizing with much power, if you sleep among the midst of the lots,* O you *wings of a dove silvered over*: or by those which follow, so that the order is: *If you sleep among the midst of the lots, the wings of a dove silvered over will be whitened with snow in Zalmon*: that is, those very wings will be whitened, if you sleep among the midst of the lots: so that it is understood to be said to those who are divided for the beauty of the house as it were as spoils; that is: If you sleep among the midst of the lots, O you who are divided for the beauty of the house, through the manifestation of the Spirit unto profit, so that to one indeed there is given through the Spirit the word of wisdom, to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith, to another kinds of healings in the same Spirit, and the rest: if, then, you sleep among the midst of the lots, then the wings of the dove silvered over will be whitened with snow in Zalmon. It can also be thus: *If you, the wings of a dove silvered over, sleep among the midst of the lots, they will be whitened with snow in Zalmon,* so that men are understood, who through grace receive remission of sins. Whence also of the Church itself it is said in the Song of Songs: *Who is this who ascends whitened*[^69]? For the promise of God is held, who says through the prophet: *If your sins are like scarlet, I will whiten them like snow*[^70]. It can also be thus understood, that in what was said, *the wings of a dove silvered over,* "you will be" is supplied; so that this is the sense: O you who are divided as it were as spoils for the beauty of the house, if you sleep among the midst of the lots, you will be the wings of a dove silvered over; that is, you will be lifted up to higher things, yet cleaving to the joining of the Church. For I think no other dove is better understood here as silvered over, than that of which it was said: *One is my dove*[^71]. But she is silvered over, because she is instructed by the divine eloquences: for the eloquences of the Lord are called in another place *silver tried by the fire of the earth, purified sevenfold*[^72]. It is, then, some great good, to sleep among the midst of the lots: which some wished to be the two Testaments; so that to sleep among the midst of the lots is to rest in the authority of those Testaments; that is, to acquiesce in the testimonies of each Testament, so that when something is brought forward and proved from these, all contention is ended in peaceful quiet. But if it is so, what else do those evangelizing with much power seem to be admonished, except that then the Lord will give them the word that they may be able to evangelize, if they sleep among the midst of the lots? For then the word of truth is given them, if the authority of the two Testaments is not abandoned by them: that they themselves may be both the wings of the dove silvered over, by whose preaching the glory of the Church is borne to heaven.

**18.** But *between the shoulders*: it is certainly a part of the body; it is a part around the region of the heart, yet from behind, that is, from the back: which part of that silvered dove he says is in the greenness of gold, that is, in the vigor of wisdom; which vigor I think can be understood no better than as love. But why from the back, and not from the breast? Although I wonder how this word is set in another psalm, where it is said: *Between his shoulders he will overshadow you, and under his wings you will hope*[^73], since one cannot be overshadowed under the wings, except what is from the breast. And in Latin indeed *between the shoulders* can perhaps in some way be understood on either side, both before and behind; so that we take the shoulders to be those which have the head in the middle: and in the Hebrew it is perhaps ambiguous, which could be understood this way too; but what is in the Greek signifies only from behind, which is *between the shoulders.* Is the greenness of gold there, that is, wisdom and love, because there are as it were the roots of the wings; or because there is borne that light burden? For what are even the wings themselves, except the two precepts of love, on which the whole Law and the Prophets hang? What is the light burden itself, except that very love which is fulfilled in these two precepts? For whatever is difficult in a precept, is light to one who loves. Nor is it for any other reason rightly understood to have been said: *My burden is light*[^74]; except because he gives the Holy Spirit, through whom love is poured out in our hearts, that by loving we may do freely what one who does it by fearing does in a servile way: nor is he a friend of the right, when he would prefer, if it could be, that what is right were not commanded.

**19.** It can also be asked, since it was not said: If you sleep in the lots, but, *among the midst of the lots,* what this is, *among the midst of the lots.* Which indeed, if it were more exactly translated from the Greek, would be said, between the middle of the lots; which I have read in none of the translators: I believe for this reason, because what was said, *among the midst of the lots,* is of equal value. Hence, then, I will set forth what seems to me. Often this word is wont to be set down for connecting and pacifying certain things, lest they disagree among themselves: as a covenant between himself and the people God appointing, Scripture sets down this word; for in place of what is in Latin, *between me and you,* the Greek has, *between the middle of me and you.* So too, of the sign of circumcision, when God speaks to Abraham, he says: *It will be a covenant between me and you, and all your seed*[^75]: which the Greek has, *between the middle of me and you, and between the middle of your seed.* Likewise when he spoke to Noah of the bow in the clouds to be set up as a sign, he very often repeats this word: and what the Latin codices have, *between me and you,* or *between me and every living soul,* and whatever such things are said there; *between the middle of me and you,* is found in the Greek. David too and Jonathan set up a sign between themselves, lest by thinking otherwise they disagree: and what is said in Latin, *between both; between the middle of both,* the Greek has by the same word. And it was excellently done, that in this place of this psalm our translators did not say, between the lots, which is usual in Latin speech; but *among the midst of the lots,* as it were between the middle of the lots, which is rather read in the Greek, and which is wont to be said in those things, as I said, which ought to have agreement among themselves. Scripture, then, bids those to sleep among the midst of the lots, who either are the wings of the dove silvered over, or to whom from this it is granted that they may be such. Further, if these lots signify the two Testaments, what else are we admonished, except that we should not fight against the Testaments which agree among themselves; but should acquiesce by understanding, and should ourselves be the sign and proof of their concord, when we feel that the one has said nothing against the other, and show it with peaceful admiration, as it were the sleep of an ecstasy? But why the Testaments are understood by the lots, since this name is indeed Greek, but the Testament is not so called: this is the reason, that through a testament is given an inheritance, which in Greek is called *kleronomia*, and an heir, *kleronomos*. But *kleros* is called a lot in Greek, and lots, by the promise of God, are called the parts of the inheritance, which were distributed to the people. Whence the tribe of Levi was bidden to have no lot among its brothers, because it was to be supported from their tithes. For I think the Clergy (Cleri) and Clerics too were so called from this, who are ordained in the grades of ecclesiastical ministry, because Matthias was chosen by lot, whom we read to have been the first ordained by the Apostles. Accordingly, on account of the inheritance which is made by testament, as it were through that which is effected the thing which effects it, the Testaments themselves are signified by the name of lots.

**20.** Although there occurs to me here another sense too, unless I am mistaken, to be preferred, that we much more probably understand the lots as the inheritances themselves: so that, since the inheritance of the Old Testament is earthly happiness, though in a shadow significative of the future; but the inheritance of the New Testament is eternal immortality; to sleep among the midst of the lots is no longer to seek that one ardently, and still to await this one patiently. For those who for this reason serve God, or rather for this reason are unwilling to serve, while they seek in this life and in this land happiness, sleep is taken away from them, and they do not sleep. For, agitated by inflaming desires, they are driven into base deeds and crimes, and do not rest at all; desiring to acquire, fearing to lose. *But he who hears me,* says Wisdom, *will dwell in hope, and will rest without fear from all malignity*[^76]. This is, as far as it seems to me, to sleep among the midst of the lots, that is, among the midst of the inheritances; not yet in reality, but yet in hope to dwell in the heavenly inheritance, and to be at rest now from the desire of earthly happiness. But when that which we hope for has come, we shall no longer rest between two inheritances; but in the new, the true, of which the old was a shadow, we shall reign. Wherefore, even if we understand what was said: *If you sleep among the midst of the lots,* as if it were said: If you die among the midst of the lots, so that Scripture in its manner calls this death of the flesh a sleeping; this is the best death, that a man, in the restraining of desires from earthly things, in the hope of the heavenly inheritance persevering unto the end, should close the last day of this life. For those thus sleeping among the midst of the lots will be the wings of the dove silvered over; so that at the time when they rise again, they may be caught up in the clouds to meet Christ in the air, and now ever live with the Lord: or certainly, because through those who so die, the more securely, the more sublimely the Church is spread abroad, and is, as it were, lifted up by certain wings of lofty praise. For not in vain was it said: *Before death praise no man whatever*[^77]. All the saints of God, then, from the beginning of the human race, even to the time of the Apostles (because they too knew how to say, *The day of man I have not desired, you know*[^78], and: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek*[^79]), and from the time of the Apostles, from when the difference of the two Testaments was more clearly revealed, the Apostles themselves and the blessed martyrs and the other just ones, as rams and the sons of rams, even to this time have slept among the midst of the lots; despising now the happiness of an earthly kingdom, and hoping for the eternity of the kingdom of heaven, and not yet holding it. And because they slept so well, by them as it were by wings the Church now flies, and is exalted by praises, namely the dove silvered over; so that, by this fame of theirs the posterity being invited to imitate, while others too sleep thus, wings are added by which it may be sublimely preached even unto the end of the age.

**21.** [v. 15] *While the supercelestial one distinguishes kings over it, they will be whitened with snow in Zalmon*[^80]: that supercelestial one, that one *who ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things, while he distinguishes kings over it,* that is, over the same dove silvered over. For the Apostle follows, and says: *And he himself gave some indeed apostles, but some prophets, but some evangelists, but some pastors and teachers.* For what else is it to distinguish kings over it, except *for the work of the ministry, for the building up of the body of Christ*[^81]; since it itself is the body of Christ? But those are called *kings,* namely from ruling (regendo): and what more than the concupiscences of the flesh, lest sin reign in their mortal body to obey its desires, lest they present their members as weapons of iniquity to sin, but present themselves to God, as those alive from the dead, and their members as weapons of righteousness to God? For so will they be kings, first distinguished from the aliens, because they do not bear the yoke with unbelievers; then distinguished concordantly among themselves by their own gifts. *For not all are apostles, or all prophets, or all teachers, or all have gifts of healings, or all speak with tongues, or all interpret. But all these things one and the same Spirit works, dividing his own to each as he wills*[^82]: which by giving, that supercelestial one distinguishes kings over the dove silvered over. Of which Holy Spirit, when the angel, sent to his mother full of grace, was speaking, and she asked how it would be that she was announced about to bear, since she did not know a man: *The Holy Spirit,* he says, *will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you*[^83]. What is, *will overshadow you,* except, will make a shadow? Whence these kings too, while by the grace of the Spirit of the Lord Christ they are distinguished over the dove silvered over, *will be whitened with snow in Zalmon.* For Zalmon is interpreted "Shadow." For they are distinguished not by their merits or their own power. *For who distinguishes you?* he says. *And what have you that you did not receive*[^84]? That they may, then, be distinguished from the ungodly, they receive remission of sins from him who says: *If your sins are like scarlet, I will whiten them like snow*[^85]. Behold how *they will be whitened with snow in Zalmon;* in the grace of the Spirit of Christ, by which their own gifts too were distinguished to them: of which it was said, what I mentioned above, *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you,* that is, will make a shadow for you: *therefore that which is born of you holy, will be called the Son of God*[^86]. Now this shadow is understood as a protection from the heat of carnal concupiscences: whence that virgin conceived Christ not by carnally desiring, but by spiritually believing. But a shadow consists of light and a body: accordingly that which in the beginning was the Word, that true light, that it might become for us a noonday shade, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. Namely, man, like a body to light, drew near to God, and the shadow of protection covered those who believe in him. For it is not of such a shadow as that of which it is said: *All those things have passed away like a shadow*[^87]; nor of such a shadow as that of which the Apostle says: *Let no one judge you in food, or in drink, or in part of a feast day, or of a new moon, or of sabbaths, which is a shadow of things to come*[^88]; but of such as that of which it is written: *Under the shadow of your wings protect me*[^89]. While, then, that supercelestial one distinguishes kings over the dove silvered over, let them not exalt their own merits, let them not trust in their own power: for *they will be whitened with snow in Zalmon;* by grace they will be made white in the protection of the body of Christ.

**22.** [v. 16] But this mountain he consequently calls *the mountain of God, a rich mountain, a curded mountain,* or *a fat mountain.*[^90] But what else here would he call fat, than rich? For there is also a mountain called by this name, that is, *Zalmon.* But what mountain ought we to understand as *the mountain of God, the rich mountain, the curded mountain,* except the same Lord Christ, of whom another prophet too says: *In the last times the mountain of the Lord will be manifest, prepared on the top of the mountains*[^91]? He is the curded mountain, on account of the little ones to be nourished as it were with milk by grace; a rich mountain, for strengthening and enriching by the excellence of gifts: for milk itself, from which cheese is made, in a wonderful way signifies grace; for it flows from the abundance of the maternal bowels, and is poured into the little ones freely with delightful mercy. But the case is ambiguous in the Greek, whether it be nominative or accusative; since in that tongue mountain is of the neuter gender, not masculine: therefore some Latins did not translate, *the mountain of God,* but *mount of God.* But I think it is better, *in Zalmon the mountain of God,* that is, into the mountain of God which is called Zalmon: according to the understanding which above, as we could, we expounded.

**23.** [v. 17] Then, in that he said, *the mountain of God, the curded mountain, the rich mountain,* lest anyone should dare from this to compare the Lord Jesus Christ to the other saints, who themselves too were called mountains of God: for it is read: *Your righteousness is like the mountains of God*[^92]; whence the Apostle says: *That we may be the righteousness of God in him*[^93]. Of which mountains it is also said in another place: *You who enlighten wonderfully from the eternal mountains*[^94]; because eternal life was given to them, and through them the most eminent authority of the holy Scriptures was established, but with him enlightening from them to whom it is said: *You who enlighten. For I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me*: yet my help is not properly from those very mountains; but *my help is from the Lord who made heaven and earth*[^95]. For one of those mountains, much excelling, when he had said that he labored more than all those: *Yet not I,* he says, *but the grace of God with me*[^96]. Lest anyone, then, should dare to compare even the mountain beautiful in form before the sons of men to the mountains, sons of men; since there were not lacking those who called him, some John the Baptist, some Elijah, some Jeremiah, or one of the prophets; he turns to those, and says: *Why do you suppose curded mountains, the mountain,* he says, *in which it pleased God to dwell in it? Why do you suppose?* For as light to those, because to them too it was said: *You are the light of the world*[^97]; but that other was said: *The true light, which enlightens every man*[^98]: so, those mountains; but far other is the mountain prepared on the top of the mountains. So these mountains are glorious by bearing that mountain: of whom that one says: *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world*[^99]: *that he who glories may glory not in himself, but in the Lord*[^100]. *Why*, then, *do you suppose curded mountains* the *mountain in which it pleased God to dwell in it*? Not because he does not dwell in others, but because in them through him. *For in him dwells all the fullness of divinity*[^101]; not in a shadowy way, as in the temple made by king Solomon, but bodily, that is, solidly and truly. *For God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself*[^102]. Which whether we take to be said of the Father, since he himself says: *But the Father remaining in me, he himself does his works; I in the Father, and the Father in me*[^103]; or it is understood thus: *God was in Christ,* the Word was in the man: so was the Word in the flesh, that the Word, even made flesh, alone properly could be called it, that is, the man was coupled to the Word into one person of Christ. *Why*, then, *do you suppose curded mountains* the *mountain in which it pleased God to dwell in it?* Far otherwise certainly than in those mountains, of which you suppose this one too to be some one. For not because they too through the grace of adoption are sons of God, is therefore any of them the Only-begotten, to whom he would say: *Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies under your feet*[^104]. *For the Lord will dwell unto the end*: that is, those mountains not to be compared to this mountain the Lord himself will dwell in, who is the mountain prepared on the top of the mountains; that he may lead them to the end, that is, to himself, to be contemplated as God is. *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^105]. It pleased God, then, to dwell in this mountain, which is prepared on the top of the mountains; to whom he says: *You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased*[^106]. But that mountain is the Lord, who will dwell in the other mountains unto the end, on the top of whom he is prepared. *For there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^107], the mountain of mountains, as the holy of holies. Whence he says: *I in them, and you in me*[^108]. *Why*, then, *do you suppose curded mountains, the mountain in which it pleased God to dwell in it?* For those curded mountains this Lord, the curded mountain, will dwell in unto the end, that they may be something, to whom he says: *For without me you can do nothing*[^109].

**24.** [v. 18] So comes about also what follows: *The chariot of God is manifold by ten thousands;* or, *manifold by tens of thousands;* or, *manifold ten times a thousand times.*[^110] For one Greek word, as each of the Latin translators was able, has been rendered, which there was said. But in Latin it could not be sufficiently expressed: since a thousand is called *chilias* by the Greeks, but *myrias* is many tens of thousands; for one *myrias* is ten thousand. So by this name he signified a huge multitude of saints and faithful, who by bearing God become in a manner the chariot of God. This, by remaining in it and by ruling it, he leads to the end, as it were his chariot, as it were to some destined place. *For Christ the beginning; then those who are Christ's at his coming; then the end*[^111]. This is the holy Church, which are what follows: *Thousands of those rejoicing. For they are rejoicing in hope,* until they be led to the end which they now await through patience. But wonderfully, when he had said: *Thousands of those rejoicing,* he at once added: *The Lord in them.* Let us not marvel that they rejoice; *the Lord in them.* For *through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God*[^112]: but: *The Lord in them.* Therefore even if they are *as it were sad, yet always rejoicing*[^113]; nor now in the very end to which they have not yet come, but *in hope* are *rejoicing,* and *in tribulation patient*[^114], since *the Lord in them, in Sinai, in the holy place.* In the interpretations of Hebrew names we find Sinai interpreted "Commandment": and it is interpreted certain other things too; but I think this fits more aptly the present place. For rendering the reason why those thousands rejoiced, of whom the chariot of God consists, *The Lord,* he says, *in them, in Sinai, in the holy place*: that is: The Lord in them, in the commandment; which commandment is holy, as the Apostle says: *And so the Law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good*[^115]. But what would the commandment profit, unless the Lord were there, of whom it is said: *For it is God who works in you both to will and to work, for his good will*[^116]? For the commandment without the Lord's help is a letter that kills. *For the Law entered in, that the offense might abound*[^117]. But since *the fullness of the Law is love*[^118], therefore through love the Law is fulfilled, not through fear. *For the love of God is poured out in our hearts, through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^119]. Therefore these thousands rejoice. For they do the righteousness of the Law just so much as they are helped by the Spirit of grace; since *the Lord in them, in Sinai, in the holy place.*

**25.** [v. 19] Then, the expression turned to the Lord himself: *You ascended*, he says, *on high, you took captivity captive, you received gifts among men.*[^120] This the Apostle so mentions, so expounds, speaking of the Lord Christ: *But to each one of us,* he says, *is given grace according to the measure of the giving of Christ: wherefore he says: He ascended on high, he led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men. But that he ascended, what is it, except because he also descended into the lower parts of the earth? He who descended, the same is he who ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things*[^121]. To Christ, then, without doubt it was said: *You ascended on high, you took captivity captive, you received gifts among men.* Nor let it move us that the Apostle, mentioning the same testimony, does not say: *You received gifts among men;* but: *He gave gifts to men.* For he, by apostolic authority, spoke according to that whereby the Son is God with the Father. For according to this he *gave gifts to men,* sending them the Holy Spirit, who is the Spirit of the Father and the Son. But according to that whereby the same Christ is understood in his body, which is the Church, on account of which his saints and faithful are his members; whence it is said to them: *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^122]: without doubt he himself too *received gifts among men.* For Christ ascended on high, and sits at the right hand of the Father; but unless he were also here on earth, he would not have cried out from there: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^123]? Since, then, he himself says: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^124]; why do we doubt that he receives in his members, which gifts his members receive?

**26.** But what is: *You took captivity captive*? Whether because he conquered death, which held captive those over whom it reigned? or did he call men themselves the captivity, who were held captive under the devil? The mystery of which thing the title of that psalm too contains: *When the house was being built after the captivity*[^125]: that is, the Church after the gentility. Calling, then, those very men who were held captive the captivity, as when "the soldiery" is said and those who serve as soldiers are understood, he says the same captivity was made captive by Christ. For why should not the captivity be happy, if men can be captured even unto good? Whence it was said to Peter: *From now on you will be capturing men*[^126]. Made captive, then, because captured, and captured because subjugated; sent under that gentle yoke, freed from the sin whose slaves they were, and made slaves of the righteousness of which they were free. Whence he himself too is in those who *gave gifts to men,* and *received gifts among men.* And therefore in this captivity, in this servitude, in this chariot, under this yoke there are not thousands of those lamenting, but *thousands of those rejoicing. The Lord* namely *in them, in Sinai, in the holy place.* To which sense agrees also the other interpretation, by which Sinai is interpreted "Measure." For the Apostle, speaking of these gifts of spiritual gladness, that which I mentioned above: *But to each one of us,* he says, *grace is given according to the measure of the giving of Christ.* And then there follows what here too follows: *Wherefore he says: He ascended on high, he led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men*[^127]: which here is, *you received gifts among men.* What is more concordant than this and that truth? what more manifest?

**27.** But what does he add next? *For those who do not believe, to dwell;* or, as some codices have: *For those not believing, to dwell.*[^128] For what else are those not believing, than those who do not believe? Of whom this is said is not easy of understanding. For as though the reason of the earlier words were rendered, when it had been said: *You took captivity captive, you received gifts among men;* so it was added: *For those who do not believe, to dwell,* that is, those not believing, that they might dwell. What is this? of whom does he say this? Or does that captivity, before it passed over into the good captivity, show whence it had been an evil captivity? For by not believing they were possessed by the enemy, *who works in the sons of unbelief; among whom you too,* he says, *were once, when you lived in them*[^129]. By the gifts of his grace, then, he who received gifts among men made captive this captivity. For they were those not believing, that they might dwell. For faith freed them from there, so that now, believing, they may dwell in the house of God, themselves too made the house of God, and the chariot of God of thousands rejoicing.

**28.** [vv. 20-21] Whence he who sang these things, foreseeing them in the Spirit, himself too filled with gladness, burst forth a hymn, saying: *The Lord God blessed, the Lord God blessed from day unto day.*[^130] Which some codices have, *day by day.* Because the Greeks have it thus; which would be more truly expressed, *day by day.* Which expression I think signifies what was said, *from day unto day.* For day by day he does this even unto the end; he takes captivity captive, receiving gifts among men.

**29.** And since he leads that chariot to the end, it follows and says: *God will make a prosperous way for us, the God of our healings, our God, the God of saving.*[^131] Grace is much commended. For who would be saved, unless he himself healed? But lest it occur to the thought: Why, then, do we die, if by his grace we have been saved? he at once added, *and the Lord's the goings out of death*: as though he said: Why are you indignant, O human condition, that you have a going out of death? Even your Lord's going out was no other than of death. Rather, then, be consoled than indignant; for *the Lord's too the goings out of death.* For by hope we have been saved: but if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience. Patiently, then, let us bear even death itself, by the example of him who, although by no sin he was a debtor of death, and was the Lord from whom no one would take his soul, but he himself would lay it down of himself, yet his was the going out of death too.

**30.** [v. 22] *But yet God will shatter the heads of his enemies; the crown of the hair of those walking about in their offenses*[^132]: that is, of those lifting themselves up too much, being too proud in their offenses; in which they ought at least to be humble, saying: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner.* But he will shatter their heads; *because he who exalts himself will be humbled*[^133]. And through this, although the Lord's too is the going out of death; yet the same Lord, since he is God, and by will, not by necessity, died according to the flesh, *will shatter the heads of his enemies;* not only of those who, mocking him crucified and wagging their heads, said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^134]; but also of all who lift themselves up against his doctrine, and as it were deride the death of the man. For the same one of whom it was said: *He saved others, himself he cannot save*[^135], *is the God of our healings,* and is *the God of saving*: but for the sake of a lesson of humility and patience, and of blotting out by his own blood the handwriting of our sins, he willed his own too to be a going out of death, that we might not fear that death, but rather that one from which he freed us through this one. But yet, mocked and dead, he *will shatter the heads of his enemies,* of whom he says: *Raise me up, and I will requite them*[^136]: whether good for evil, while he subjects to himself the heads of believers; or just for unjust, while he punishes the heads of the proud. For in both ways the heads of the enemies are shattered and broken, when they are cast down from pride, whether corrected by humility, or snatched away into the depths of hell.

**31.** [vv. 23-24] *The Lord said: From Bashan I will turn back;* or as some codices have: *From Bashan I will turn.*[^137] For he turns us that we may be saved, of whom it was said above: *The God of our healings, and the God of saving.* For to him it is said elsewhere too: *O God of powers, turn us; and show your face, and we shall be saved*[^138]. Likewise elsewhere: *Turn us, O God of our healings*[^139]. But he said: *From Bashan I will turn.* Bashan is interpreted "Confusion." What is it, then, to turn from confusion, except because he is confounded about his sins who, for their being forgiven, prays the mercy of God? Hence it is that that publican did not even dare to lift his eyes to heaven: so considering himself, he was confounded; but he went down justified, because *the Lord said, From Bashan I will turn.* Bashan is interpreted also "Dryness"; and the Lord is rightly understood to turn from dryness, that is, from want. For those who seem to themselves abundant, when they are famished, and full, when they are most empty, are not turned. *For blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled*[^140]. From this dryness the Lord turns: for of a dry soul it is said to him: *I stretched out my hands to you; my soul is like a land without water to you*[^141]. That too is not absurdly understood, which other codices have: *From Bashan I will turn back.* For he too turns back to us who says: *Turn to me, and I will turn to you*[^142]: and not, if I am outside confusion, since my sin is always before me; and outside the dryness, in which we desire the rain of him who segregated a voluntary rain for his inheritance. For by dryness it was weakened; but he, turned back, perfected it, to whom it is said: *And being turned back you gave me life*[^143]. *The Lord said,* then: *From Bashan I will turn, I will turn into the depth of the sea.* If *I will turn,* why *into the depth of the sea*? For into himself the Lord turns, when he savingly turns, and he certainly is not the depth of the sea. Or does the Latin expression perhaps deceive, and is it set in place of, *into the depth,* that which is, deeply? For he does not turn himself; but there he turns those who lie in the depth of this age sunk by the weight of sins, where that one turned says: *From the depths I cried to you, O Lord*[^144]. But if it is not, *I will turn,* but, *I will be turned into the depth of the sea,* this is understood that our Lord said, that by his mercy he would be turned even into the depth of the sea, to free those too who were even most desperate sinners. Although in a certain Greek copy I have found, not, *into the depth,* but, *in the depths,* that is, *en bythois;* which confirms the former sense, that there too God turns to himself those crying from the depths. Which, even if he be understood to be turned there to free even such, it is not incongruous: and so he turns them, or to free them is so turned, that his foot be dipped in blood. Which the Prophet says to the Lord himself: *That your foot may be dipped in blood*[^145]: that is, that those very ones who are turned to you, or to free whom you are turned, although in the depth of the sea they were sunk by the weight of iniquity, may so advance by your grace, since *where sin abounded, grace superabounded*[^146]. That they may become your foot among your members, to announce your Gospel, and for your name, long enduring martyrdom, may strive even unto blood. For so, as I think, his foot is more fittingly understood as dipped in blood.

**32.** Finally he adds: *The tongue of your dogs from the enemies, from him*[^147]: calling those very ones who were going to strive even unto blood for the gospel faith, even dogs, as it were barking for their Lord. Not those dogs of whom the Apostle says: *Beware of dogs*[^148]: but those who *eat of the crumbs that fall from their masters' table.* For having confessed this, the Canaanite woman merited to hear: *O woman, great is your faith! be it done to you as you wish*[^149]. Praiseworthy dogs, not detestable; keeping faith with their master, and for his house barking against the enemies. For he did not say only *of dogs,* but *of your dogs;* nor were their teeth, but their tongue praised: since certainly not in vain, nor without a great sacrament, was Gideon bidden to lead only those who lapped the water of the river like dogs; and such were found, not more than three hundred, in so great a multitude. In which number is the sign of the cross, on account of the letter, which in the Greek number-signs signifies three hundred. Of such dogs it is said in another psalm too: *They will be turned toward evening, and will suffer hunger like dogs*[^150]. For certain dogs too were reproved by the prophet Isaiah, not for this reason that they were dogs, but that they did not know how to bark, and loved to slumber. Where certainly he showed, that if they were watchful and barked for their master, they would be praiseworthy dogs: as these are praised of whom it is said: *The tongue of your dogs.* Whom yet the Prophet foretold would be from the enemies, namely through that conversion of which he was speaking above. Whence also that psalm: *They will be turned,* it says, *toward evening, and will suffer hunger like dogs.* Then, as though to those asking whence this so great good to them, that they became his dogs, whose enemies they had been; it was answered, *from him.* For it is read thus: *The tongue of your dogs from the enemies, from him.* Namely by his love, by his mercy, by his grace. For when could they do this of themselves? For when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son: to this end too was the Lord's going out of death.

**33.** [v. 25] *Your steps were seen, O God.*[^151] The steps by which you came through the world, as it were in that chariot about to traverse the whole world; which also the cloud in the Gospel signifies as his saints and faithful, where he says: *Henceforth you will see the Son of man coming in the clouds*[^152]. Except that coming in which he will be the judge of the living and the dead; *Henceforth,* he says, *you will see the Son of man coming in the clouds. These your steps were seen,* that is, were made manifest, the grace of the New Testament being revealed: whence it was said: *How beautiful are the feet of those who announce peace, who announce good things!*[^153] For this grace and these steps lay hidden in the Old Testament: but when the fullness of time came, and it pleased God to reveal his Son, that he might be announced among the nations, *your steps were seen, O God; the steps of my God, of the king who is in the holy place.* In what *holy place,* except in his temple? *For the temple of God is holy,* he says, *which you are*[^154].

**34.** [v. 26] But that these steps might be seen: *The princes went before, joined to those playing, in the midst of young women playing the timbrels.*[^155] The princes are the Apostles: for they went before, that the peoples might follow. *They went before,* announcing the New Testament; *joined to those playing,* by whose good works, even visible ones, as it were instruments of praise, God would be glorified. But the same princes *in the midst of young women playing the timbrels,* namely in honorable ministry: for so the appointed ministers are in the midst of the new Churches; for this is, *of young women*: of those praising God with flesh subdued; for this is, *of those playing the timbrels,* in that timbrels are made of dried and stretched hide.

**35.** [v. 27] Therefore, lest anyone take these things carnally, and from these words bring into the mind certain choirs of luxury, it follows and says: *In the Churches bless the Lord*[^156]: as though he said: Why, when you hear "young women playing the timbrels," do you think of lascivious delights? *In the Churches bless the Lord.* For the Churches are shown to you by this mystical signification: the Churches are the young women, adorned with new grace; the Churches are the timbrel-players, with chastised flesh spiritually sounding. *In the Churches,* then, *bless the Lord God from the fountains of Israel.* For from there he first chose those whom he would make fountains. For from there the Apostles were chosen; and they first heard: *He who drinks of the water that I will give will never thirst; but it will become in him a fountain of water springing up unto eternal life*[^157].

**36.** [v. 28] *There Benjamin, the younger, in ecstasy.*[^158] There Paul, the last of the Apostles, who says: *For I too am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin*[^159]. But plainly *in ecstasy;* all being terrified at so great a miracle in his calling. For ecstasy is an excess of mind: which sometimes happens through fear; but sometimes through some revelation, by an alienation of the mind from the senses of the body, that to the spirit may be shown what is to be shown. Whence it can be understood thus too what is here set down, *in ecstasy;* because when it had been said to him the persecutor from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me,* the light of the carnal eyes being taken away, he answered the Lord whom he saw in spirit; while those who were with him heard the voice of the one answering, yet seeing no one to whom he spoke. Here ecstasy can also be understood as that of which he himself, speaking, says, that he knew a man caught up even to the third heaven; but whether in the body, or out of the body, he did not know; but caught up into paradise he heard ineffable words, which it was not lawful for a man to speak. *The princes of Judah, their leaders; the princes of Zebulun, the princes of Naphtali.* Since by "princes" he signifies the Apostles, where there is also *Benjamin the younger in ecstasy;* by which words no one doubts that Paul is signified; or by the name of princes all are signified who are excellent in the Churches and most worthy of imitation: what do these names of the Israelite tribes mean? For if only Judah were mentioned, since from this tribe the kings had been, from which also the Lord Christ according to the flesh; we would think that for this reason from this tribe the princes of the New Testament too were figured: but when he adds, *the princes of Zebulun, the princes of Naphtali;* someone perhaps may say that from these tribes the Apostles were, but from others not. Which although I do not see whence this could be proved; yet, because I find no source from which it can be refuted, and in this place I see the princes of the Churches commended, and their leaders who in the Churches bless the Lord, I not absurdly approve this sense too; but it delights me more what becomes clear from the interpretation of these names. For they are Hebrew names: of which Judah is said to be interpreted "Confession"; Zebulun, "Dwelling-place of strength"; Naphtali, "My enlargement." All which convey to us the truest princes of the Churches, worthy of leadership, worthy of imitation, worthy of honors. For the martyrs hold the highest place in the Churches, and excel by the summit of holy dignity. Now in martyrdom the first thing is confession, and for it follows the strength of bearing whatever befalls; then, after all is borne, the straits being ended, breadth follows in the reward. It can also be understood thus, that since the Apostle especially commends these three, faith, hope, love, confession be in faith, strength in hope, breadth in love. For it is a matter of faith that with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. But in the sufferings of tribulations the matter is sad, but hope is strong: *For if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^160]. But breadth the pouring out of love in the heart furnishes: for *consummate love casts out fear*[^161]; which fear has torment through the straits of the soul. *The princes*, then, *of Judah their leaders*, of those who in the Churches bless the Lord. *The princes of Zebulun, the princes of Naphtali*: the princes of confession, of strength, of breadth; the princes of faith, of hope, of love.

**37.** [v. 29] *Command, O God, your power.*[^162] For there is one Lord of ours, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we in him, whom we read to be the Power of God and the Wisdom of God. But how does God command his Christ, except while he commends him? *For God commends his love in us, since while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us*[^163]. *How has he not with him given us all things too*[^164]? *Command, O God, your power: confirm, O God, this which you have worked in us.* Command by teaching, confirm by helping.

**38.** [vv. 30-31] *From your temple in Jerusalem, kings will offer you gifts.*[^165] *From your temple in Jerusalem,* which is our free mother, because it itself too is your holy temple: from that temple, then, *kings will offer you gifts.* Whatever kings be understood; whether the kings of the earth, or the kings whom the supercelestial one distinguishes over the dove silvered over; *kings will offer you gifts.* And what gifts so pleasing, as the sacrifices of praise! But against this praise they make a din, having the name of Christian and holding diverse things. Let there be done, then, what follows: *Rebuke the beasts of the reed.* For they are beasts, since by not understanding they harm; and beasts of the reed, since they pervert the sense of the Scriptures for their own error. For by the reed the Scriptures are as fittingly signified, as speech by the tongue; according to which the tongue is said to be Hebrew, or Greek, or Latin, or any other, namely through that which effects the thing effected. But it is usual in Latin speech that writing be called "stylus," because it is done with a stylus; so therefore also "reed," because it is done with a reed. The apostle Peter says that the unlearned and unstable pervert the Scriptures to their own destruction: these are the beasts of the reed, of whom it is here said: *Rebuke the beasts of the reed.*

**39.** Of these too is what he adds: *A gathering of bulls among the cows of the peoples, that those who are proved by silver may be set apart.*[^166] Calling them bulls, on account of the pride of a hard and untamed neck; for he signifies the heretics. But *the cows of the peoples,* I think are to be understood as souls that can be seduced, because they easily follow these bulls. For they do not seduce whole peoples, among whom are the weighty and stable; whence it is written: *Among a weighty people I will praise you*[^167]; but in those peoples whatever cows they find. *For of these are those who penetrate houses, and lead captive little women laden with sins, who are led by various desires; always learning, and never coming to the knowledge of the truth*[^168]. But what the same Apostle says: *It is necessary that there be heresies too, that those who are proved may be made manifest among you*[^169]; this too follows here: *That those who are proved by silver may be set apart,* that is, those who are proved by the eloquences of the Lord. *For the eloquences of the Lord, the chaste eloquences, silver tried by the fire of the earth*[^170]. For *may be set apart* means, may appear, may stand out; which he says, *may be made manifest.* Whence in the silversmith's craft too they are called "those who set apart," who know how to bring out the form of a vessel from the confusion of the mass. For many senses of the holy Scriptures lie hidden, and are known to few more intelligent ones; nor are they asserted more conveniently and acceptably, except when the care of answering heretics compels. For then even those who neglect the studies of doctrine, their slumber being shaken off, are roused to diligence in hearing, that the adversaries may be refuted. Finally, how many senses of the holy Scriptures have been asserted concerning Christ as God against Photinus! how many concerning the man Christ against Manichaeus! how many concerning the Trinity against Sabellius! how many concerning the unity of the Trinity against the Arians, Eunomians, Macedonians! how many concerning the catholic Church spread throughout the whole world, and concerning the mingling of the evil even unto the end of the age, that they do not harm the good in the fellowship of his sacraments, against the Donatists and Luciferians and others, if there be any, who by a similar error dissent from the truth! how many against the other heretics, whom to enumerate or mention would be too long, and not necessary to the present work! Whose proved assertors, nevertheless, would either lie altogether hidden, or not stand out so, as the contradictions of the proud made them stand out, whom as bulls, that is, not subdued to the peaceful and gentle yoke of discipline, the Apostle mentions, where he said such a one should be chosen for the episcopate, *that he be able to exhort in sound doctrine, and to refute those who contradict*[^171]. For there are many not subdued; these are the bulls impatient by the lifting up of the neck of the plow and the yoking: vainspeakers, and seducers of minds; these minds this psalm signified by the name of cows. To this benefit, then, divine Providence permits the bulls to be gathered among the cows of the peoples, that *they may be set apart,* that is, that those *who are proved by silver* may stand out. For to this end heresies are allowed to be, *that those who are proved may be made manifest.* Although it can be understood thus too: *A gathering of bulls among the cows of the peoples, that* from those cows *those who are proved by silver may be set apart.* For the heretical teachers have this intention, that they may shut out from the ears of the souls which they strive to seduce, that is, separate from them, those who are proved by silver, that is, who are fit to teach the eloquences of the Lord. But whether the sense of this word be the former, or this; there follows: *Scatter the nations that wish for wars.* For they are zealous not for correction, but for contention. This, then, he prophesies, that they themselves rather be scattered who are unwilling to be corrected, who strive to scatter the flock of Christ. But he called them "nations," not on account of the generations of families, but on account of the kinds of sects, where a series of succession confirms the error.

**40.** [vv. 32-34] *Legates will come from Egypt; Ethiopia will anticipate his hands.*[^172] By the name of Egypt, or of Ethiopia, he signified the faith of all the nations, the whole from a part; calling "legates" the preachers of reconciliation. *For Christ,* he says, *we discharge a legation, as though God exhorting through us: we beseech for Christ, be reconciled to God*[^173]. Not, then, of the Israelites alone, from whom the Apostles were chosen, but also of the other nations, that there would be preachers of Christian peace, this in this way was mystically prophesied. But that he says: *Will anticipate his hands,* this he says, will anticipate his vengeance; namely by conversion to him, that sins may be forgiven, lest by remaining sinners they be punished. Which is also said in another psalm: *Let us anticipate his face in confession*[^174]. As by *hands* he signifies vengeance, so by *face* the revelation and presence, which will be in the judgment. Because, then, by Egypt and Ethiopia he signified the nations of the whole world, he at once added: *To God the kingdoms of the earth.* Not to Sabellius, not to Arius, not to Donatus, not to the other stiff-necked bulls; but, *to God the kingdoms of the earth.*

**41.** But many Latin codices, and especially Greek ones, have the verses so distinguished, that there is not in them one little verse, *To God the kingdoms of the earth;* but, *To God,* is at the end of the earlier verse, and so it is said: *Ethiopia will anticipate his hands to God,* and then there follows in another verse: *The kingdoms of the earth, sing to God, play to the Lord.* By which distinction, without doubt to be preferred by the consonance of many codices and those of worthier authority, faith seems to me to be commended, which precedes works: because without the merits of good works the ungodly is justified through faith, as the Apostle says: *To him who believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned unto righteousness*[^175]; that then that very faith may begin to work through love. For those alone are to be called good works which are done through the love of God. But it is necessary that faith precede this, so that from faith those things begin, not from those things that faith; since no one works through the love of God, unless he first believes in God. This is the faith of which it is said: *For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision avails anything, nor uncircumcision; but faith which works through love*[^176]. This is the faith of which it is said to the Church herself in the Song of Songs: *You will come, and pass over from the beginning of faith*[^177]. For she came as it were the chariot of God among thousands rejoicing, having a prosperous way, and passed over from this world to the Father: that there might be done in her what the bridegroom himself says, who passed over from this world to the Father: *I will that where I am, these too be with me*[^178]; but from the beginning of faith. Because, then, that good works may follow, faith precedes, nor are there any good works, except those that follow preceding faith: nothing else seems to be said: *Ethiopia will anticipate his hands to God,* except: Ethiopia will believe God. For so *she will anticipate his hands,* that is, his works. Whose, except Ethiopia's own? Because in the Greek this is not ambiguous: for *his* there is set most openly in the feminine gender. And through this nothing else is said, than *Ethiopia will anticipate her hands to God,* that is, by believing in God she will anticipate her works. *For I reckon,* says the Apostle, *that a man is justified through faith without works of the Law. Or is he the God of the Jews only? is he not also of the Gentiles*[^179]? So, then, Ethiopia, which seems the farthest of the nations, is justified through faith without the works of the Law. For, that she may be justified, she does not glory of the works of the Law; nor does she set her merits before faith, but by faith anticipates her works. Many codices indeed do not have, *hands,* but, *hand;* which is of equal value, since it is set in place of works. But I would prefer that the Latin translators had so rendered it: *Ethiopia will anticipate her hands,* or *her hand to God*; since this would be plainer than what is now said, *his*: and, the truth being preserved, it could be done, because in the Greek tongue that pronoun can be understood not only *his,* but also *her* or *her own*: *her own,* then, if *hand; her own,* but if *hands.*

**42.** Hence now, as it were all things having been run through by prophecy, which we now discern to be fulfilled, he exhorts to the praise of Christ, and then foretells his future coming. *Kingdoms of the earth, sing to God, play to the Lord; play to God who ascends above the heaven of heavens to the east*[^180]: or as some codices have: *Who ascends above the heaven of heaven to the east.* In these words he does not understand Christ who does not believe his resurrection and ascension. But that he added *to the east,* did he not express the very place, since it is in the parts of the east where he rose again and from where he ascended? Therefore above the heaven of heaven he sits at the right hand of the Father. This is what the Apostle says: *It is he who ascended above all the heavens*[^181]. For what of the heavens remains after the heaven of heaven? Which we can also call heavens of heavens, as he called the firmament heaven; which heaven, however, we read also as heavens, where it is written: *And let the waters that are above the heavens praise the name of the Lord*[^182]. And since he is to come from there to judge the living and the dead, attend to what follows: *Behold, he will give his voice, the voice of power.* He who *like a lamb before the shearer was without voice*[^183], *behold, he will give his voice*: not the voice of weakness, as one to be judged; but *the voice of power,* as one to judge. For not a hidden God, as before, and in the judgment of men not opening his mouth; but *God will come manifest; our God, and he will not be silent*[^184]. Why do you despair, unbelievers? why do you mock? What does the evil servant say: *My Lord delays to come*[^185]? *Behold, he will give his voice, the voice of power.*

**43.** [v. 35] *Give glory to God: over Israel his magnificence.*[^186] Of which the Apostle says: *Over the Israel of God*[^187]. *For not all who are of Israel, these are Israelites*[^188]; since there is also an Israel according to the flesh. Whence he says: *See Israel according to the flesh*[^189]. *But not those who are sons of the flesh, these are sons of God; but the sons of the promise are reckoned in the seed*[^190]. Then, therefore, when his people will be without any mingling of the evil, like a mass cleansed by the winnowing-fan, like an Israel in whom there is no guile, then most eminent will be *over Israel his magnificence and his power in the clouds.* For he will not come alone to the judgment, but *with the elders of his people*[^191]; to whom he promised that they would sit upon thrones to judge, who will even judge angels. These are the clouds.

**44.** [v. 36] Finally, lest the clouds be understood as something else, following he added: *Wonderful is God in his saints, the God of Israel.*[^192] For then the very name will be most truly and most fully fulfilled, Israel, which is "Seeing God"; *since we shall see him as he is*[^193]. *He himself will give power and strength to his people, blessed God*: now fragile and weak. *For we have this treasure in earthen vessels*[^194]. But then, even by the most glorious change of bodies, *he himself will give power and strength to his people. For this body is sown in weakness; it will rise in power*[^195]. *He himself,* then, *will give the power* which in his own flesh he sent before, of which the Apostle says: *The power of his resurrection*[^196]. *And the strength,* by which *the enemy death will be destroyed*[^197]. Since, then, this lengthy psalm, difficult to understand, we have at last at some time, with his help, finished. *Blessed be God.* Amen.
