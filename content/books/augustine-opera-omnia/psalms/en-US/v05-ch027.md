# On the Same Psalm 118 (2)

**1.** [v. 3.] Because of what is written in this psalm: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways,* since *sin is iniquity*, as the apostle John says, a difficult question arose: How can the saints in this life both not be without sin, because that too is true: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^1]; and yet walk in the ways of the Lord, which those who work iniquity do not walk: it was solved by the apostle Paul saying: *It is now no longer I, then, who work it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^2]. For how is he without sin, in whom sin dwells? Yet he walks in the ways of the Lord, which those who work it do not walk; because now it is not he himself who works it, but the sin that dwells in him. Yet this question was so solved that a more difficult other one was born: How does a man do what he himself does not do. For he said both; both: *Not what I will, I do;* and: *It is not I who work it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^3]. Whence we ought to understand that, when the sin which dwells in us works in us, then we do not work it; when our will by no means consents to it, and holds even the members of the body, lest they obey its desires. For what does sin work when we are unwilling, except illicit desires alone? To which if the assent of the will is not applied; some affect is indeed moved, but no effect is given over to it. This the same apostle commanded, where he says: *Let not sin, then, reign in your mortal body, to obey its desires; nor present your members as weapons of iniquity to sin*[^4]. There are, then, the desires of sin, which he forbade us to obey. These desires, then, work sin; which if we obey, we too work it: but if, obeying the Apostle, we do not obey them, it is not we who work it, but the sin that dwells in us. But if we had no illicit desires, neither we nor sin would work any evil in us. But the movement of illicit desire, which by not obeying we do not work, we are therefore said to do, since it is not the vigor of an alien nature, but the languor of our own: from which languor we will be wholly safe, when we have been made immortal both in mind and body. Wherefore both, because we walk in the ways of the Lord, we do not obey the desires of sin; and, because we are not without sin, we have the desires of sin. And through this we now do not work them, by not obeying them; but the sin which dwells in us, by moving the same. *For those who work iniquity,* that is, obey the desires of sin, *have not walked in the ways* of the Lord.

**2.** But it must still be inquired what we ask to be forgiven us, when we say to God: *Forgive us our debts*[^5]: whether the things which we work, when we obey the desires of sin; or whether we wish those very desires, which we do not work, but the sin that dwells in us, to be forgiven us. As far indeed as I can understand, the whole guilt of that languor and weakness, whence illicit desires are moved, which the Apostle calls *sin*, was loosed by the sacrament of Baptism, with all the things which, obeying it, we did, said, thought; nor would this languor afterward harm us, although it were within, if we never gave any obedience to its illicit desires, whether by deed, or by speech, or by silent assent; until it too were healed, when that which we ask were fulfilled, whether saying: *Let your kingdom come*; or saying: *Free us from evil*[^6]: but since human life on earth is a temptation; even if we are far from crimes, yet there is not lacking where, by deed, or word, or thought, we may obey the desires of sin; when, while we are watchful against the greater, certain small things creep in upon the incautious, which, if they are gathered against us, even if singly they do not crush us by their masses, yet all overwhelm us by their heap. And because of these, even those who walk in the ways of the Lord say: *Forgive us our debts*: since to the ways of the Lord both prayer itself pertains, and confession itself, although sins do not pertain to them.

**3.** And so in the ways of the Lord, which one faith embraces, by which one believes in him who justifies the impious, who also said: *I am the way*[^7]; no one works sin, but confesses it. He deviates, then, when he sins; and therefore the sin is not attributed to the way, which is committed by one deviating: but in the way of faith they are held as not sinning, to whom sins are not imputed. Of whom the apostle Paul, commending the righteousness of faith, showed that it was written in a psalm: *Blessed are those whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered: blessed is the man to whom the Lord has not imputed sin*[^8]. This the ways of the Lord provide; and through this, since the just lives by faith, from this way of the Lord that iniquity estranges, which is unbelief. But in this way, that is, in pious faith, whoever walks, either does not work sin, or, if anything is committed by one deviating, it is not imputed because of the way, and is received as if he had not worked it. And therefore it is well understood thus too: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways;* so that he signified that iniquity which withdraws from faith, or does not draw near to faith. For just as the Lord says of the Jews: *If I had not come, they would not have sin*[^9]; and yet they were not without any sin, before Christ came in the flesh, and from when he came, they began to have sin; but he wished a certain definite sin, that is, of unbelief, to be understood, since they did not believe in him: so those who work iniquity, not any whatever, but this very one of unbelief, have not walked in his ways, because *all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth*[^10]; but both are in Christ, and outside Christ are nowhere. *For I say that Christ,* says the Apostle, *was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the Fathers; but that the Gentiles glorify God for his mercy*[^11]. In this, then, is *mercy,* because he redeemed us; in this *truth,* because he fulfilled what he promised, and will fulfill what he promises. *Those*, then, *who work iniquity,* that is, unbelief, *have not walked in his ways;* because they did not believe in Christ. Therefore let them be converted, and piously believe in him who justifies the impious, and in him find mercy by their sins being forgiven, and truth by his promises being fulfilled, that is, all the ways of the Lord: in which walking they will not work iniquity; because they will not hold unbelief, but faith, which works through love, and to which sin is not imputed.
