# Exposition of Psalm 14

**1.** [v 1.] *A psalm of David himself.* About this title there is no question. *Lord, who will sojourn in your tabernacle?* Although sometimes a tabernacle is put even for an everlasting dwelling, yet when a tabernacle is taken properly, it is a thing of war: whence soldiers are called tent-companions, as it were having tents together. This sense is helped by what was said, *who will sojourn*: for it is for a time that we fight with the devil, and then there is need of a tabernacle, in which we may refresh ourselves; which signifies especially faith in the temporal dispensation, which was carried out for us in time through the incarnation of the Lord. *And who will rest in your holy mountain?* here perhaps it now signifies that very eternal dwelling, so that we understand the mountain to be the supereminence of the love of Christ in eternal life.

**2.** [v 2.] *He who enters without stain, and works righteousness*: this he proposed, then he follows it out.

**3.** [v 3.] *He who speaks truth in his heart*: for some have truth on their lips, and do not have it in the heart: as if someone deceitfully should show a way, knowing there are robbers on it, and should say, If you go this way, you will be safe from robbers; and it should happen that truly no robbers are found there, he indeed spoke the truth, but not in his heart; for he thought otherwise, and unknowingly spoke the truth: therefore it is little to speak the truth, unless it be so also in the heart. *Who has not wrought deceit with his tongue*: deceit is wrought with the tongue, when one thing is uttered with the mouth, another covered in the breast. *Nor done evil to his neighbor*: it is known that neighbor ought to be taken as every man. *And has not received reproach against his neighbor*: that is, has not gladly or rashly believed an accuser.

**4.** [v 4.] *The malicious one has been brought to nothing in his sight.* This is perfection, that the malicious one avail nothing against a man; and that this be *in his sight*, that is, that he know most certainly that there is no malicious one, except when the soul is turned away from the eternal and unchangeable form of its Creator, to the form of the creature, which was made out of nothing. *But he glorifies those who fear the Lord*: namely the Lord himself. Now the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord: so, then, as those higher things pertain to the perfect, so those which he is now about to say pertain to beginners.

**5.** [v 5.] *He who swears to his neighbor, and does not deceive: who has not given his money at usury, and has not received bribes against the innocent.* These things are not great: but he who cannot do even these, much less can he speak truth in his heart, and not work deceit with his tongue, but, as the truth is in the heart, so utter it, and have in his mouth, *Yes yes, No no*[^1]; and not do evil to his neighbor, that is, to any man; and not receive reproach against his neighbor: which are the things of the perfect, in whose sight the malicious one is brought to nothing. Yet even these lesser things he concludes thus: *He who does these things will not be moved forever*: that is, he will arrive at those greater things, in which is great and unshaken stability. For these very tenses too are perhaps not without cause so varied, that in the earlier conclusion the past tense was put, but in this the future: for there it was said, *The malicious one has been brought to nothing in his sight*; but here, *He will not be moved forever.*

[^1]: Matt 5:37
