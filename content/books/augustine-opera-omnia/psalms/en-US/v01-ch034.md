# On the Same Psalm 29

**1.** This at least we have sung: *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*[^1]. If we know from the holy Scriptures who our enemies are, we recognize the truth of this song; but if the prudence of the flesh deceives us, so that we do not recognize against whom our struggle is, then at the very beginning of this psalm we come upon a question we cannot resolve. For whose voice do we suppose this to be, praising God and giving thanks and exulting and saying: *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*? First let us consider the Lord himself, who, according to that by which he deigned to be man, could not unfittingly fit these words to himself by the preceding prophecy: for from the fact that he was man, he was thereby also weak; and from the fact that he was weak, he was thereby also one who prays. For what we have just now heard, when the Gospel was being read—how he withdrew also into the wilderness from his disciples, who followed him there and found him; and as he withdrew *he was praying there*, and the disciples who found him said, *Men are seeking you*; but he answered, *Let us go also into the other places and villages to preach; for this is why I have come*[^2]. If you consider the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, who prays? to whom does he pray? why does he pray? Does God pray? Does he pray to an equal? And what cause has he for praying, ever blessed, ever almighty, ever unchangeable, eternal, and coeternal with the Father? Gazing therefore upon what he himself thundered through John, as through a kind of cloud of his own, saying: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God: all things were made through him, and without him was nothing made; what was made in him is life, and the life was the light of men, and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*[^3]—saying this far, we find no prayer, nor cause for praying, nor place for praying, nor disposition for praying. But since a little later it says, *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^4], you have the majesty to which you may pray, you have the humanity that may pray for you. For this was said by the Apostle, even after the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ: *He who sits*, he says, *at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us*[^5]. Why does he intercede for us? Because he deigned to be a mediator. What is it to be a mediator between God and men? Not between the Father and men, but between God and men. What is God? Father and Son and Holy Spirit. What are men? Sinners, ungodly, mortal. Between that Trinity and the weakness and iniquity of men, a man was made mediator—not iniquitous, yet weak; so that by the fact that he was not iniquitous he might join you to God, and by the fact that he was weak he might draw near to you: and thus, so that he might exist as mediator between man and God, *the Word was made flesh*, that is, the Word was made man. For men have been called by the name of flesh. Hence it is said: *And all flesh shall see the salvation of God*[^6]. All flesh, all men. It was said also by the Apostle: *Our struggle is not against flesh and blood* (that is, against men) *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of this world of darkness*[^7]: of whom we shall speak in what follows, if the Lord helps. For this distinction belongs to the exposition of the psalm which I have undertaken, in the name of the Lord, to unravel for your Holiness. Yet I have said these examples to this end, that you may know that men have been called flesh; so that what was said, *And the Word was made flesh*, you may understand to mean, and the Word was made man.

**2.** And not without reason did I say this. Let your Holiness know that there was a certain heresy, or that perhaps there are still remnants of certain men who were called Apollinarists. For some of them said that the man whom the Wisdom of God took up (and in whom he expressed his own person, not as in other men, but as it is said in the psalm, *God has anointed you, your God, with the oil of gladness above your fellows*[^8], that is, more than your fellows: lest Christ be thought anointed as other men are, as other righteous men, as the patriarchs, prophets, apostles, and martyrs, and whatever is great in the human race. Since indeed nothing greater has arisen in the human race than John the Baptist; nor among those born of women has any risen up greater. If you seek the excellence of man, it is John the Baptist. But the one whose sandal-strap John says he is not worthy to untie—what was he except more than other men? Even in his own manhood, more than other men. For according to God, and according to his divinity, and according to that by which in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God, he is, above every creature, equal to the Father: but we are treating of the man. Perhaps one of you, brothers, may think that the man taken up by the Wisdom of God was equal to other men. If among your own members there is much distance between the head and the rest of the members—surely all the members make up one body, yet there is much difference between the head and the rest of the members. For in the rest of the members you sense only by touch; you sense by touching in the rest of the members: but in the head you both see, and hear, and smell, and taste, and touch. If so great is the excellence of the head over the rest of the members, how great is the excellence of the head of the whole Church—that is, of that man whom God willed to be mediator between God and men?), therefore those heretics said that the man whom the Word took up, when the Word was made flesh, did not have a human mind, but was only a soul without human understanding. For you see of what man consists: of soul and body. But the human soul itself has something that the souls of beasts do not have. For beasts too have a soul, and they are called animals (*animalia*): for they would not be called animals except from soul (*anima*); and we see that they too are alive. But what does man have besides, whereby he was made to the image of God? That he understands and is wise, that he distinguishes good from evil: in this he was made to the image and likeness of God. He has therefore something the beasts do not have. And because he despises in himself that whereby he is better than the beasts, he destroys in himself, or wears away, and in a manner makes worn out, the image of God, so that it is said to such men: *Do not be like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*[^9]. Those heretics, then, said that our Lord Jesus Christ did not have a human mind, and that which the Greeks call *logikon*, we call rational, whereby man reasons, which the rest of the animals do not have. But what do they say? The very Word of God—this was in that man in place of a mind. These men were shut out, the Catholic faith rejected them, and they made a heresy. It was confirmed in the Catholic faith that the man whom the Wisdom of God took up had nothing less than other men, as far as pertains to the integrity of his nature; but as far as pertains to the excellence of his person, he was other than other men. For other men can be called sharers in the Word of God, having the Word of God, but none of them can be called the Word of God, as he was called when it was said, *The Word was made flesh*.

**3.** Nor were there lacking certain others, springing from the same error, who said that not only did that man, Christ the mediator between God and men, not have a mind, but not even a soul: rather they said only, There was Word and flesh, and there was no human soul there, no human mind there. This they said. But what was there? Word and flesh. And these too the Catholic Church rejected, and drives them away from the sheep, and from the simple and true faith: and it was confirmed, as I have said, that that man, the mediator, had everything that belongs to a man, except sin. For if he did many things according to the body, from which we understand that he had a body not in falsehood but in truth—for instance, how do we understand that he had a body? He walked, sat, slept, was seized, was scourged, was buffeted, was crucified, died. Take away the body, and none of these could have been done. As, then, from these signs we recognize in the Gospel that he had a true body—as he himself also said after the resurrection: *Handle me, and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^10]—as from these things, from these works, we believe and understand and know that the Lord Jesus had a body, so also from certain other natural functions we know that he had a soul. To hunger, to thirst, these belong to the soul: take away the soul, and the lifeless body cannot do these things. But if they say that these things were false, then those things too which are believed about the body will be false: but if from the fact that the functions of the body were true his body was true, then from the fact that the functions of the soul were true his soul was true.

**4.** What then? Since the Lord was made weak for your sake, O man who hear, do not compare God to yourself. For you are a creature, he your Creator. Nor compare that man to yourself, because for your sake your God is man, and the Word the Son of God: but set that man before yourself, as the mediator, and God above every creature: and so understand that he who was made man for your sake not unfittingly prays for you. If, then, he not unfittingly prays for you, he could also not unfittingly speak these words for your sake: *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*[^11]. But these words, if we do not understand the enemies, will be false, if we think of the Lord Jesus Christ himself. For how is it true, if Christ the Lord speaks, *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me*? From the person of the man, from the person of weakness, from the person of the flesh, how is it true: since his enemies did rejoice over him, when they crucified him, seized him, and scourged him, and buffeted him, saying, *Prophesy to us, O Christ*[^12]? That rejoicing of theirs as it were compels us to think false what was said, *And you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*. Then, when he hung on the cross, and they passed by or stood and watched and shook their heads and said, *Behold the Son of God; he saved others, himself he cannot save; let him come down from the cross, and we will believe in him*[^13]—in saying these things were they not rejoicing over him? Where, then, is this voice: *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*?

**5.** Perhaps this is not the voice of our Lord Jesus Christ, but of the man himself, but of the whole Church of the Christian people: because every man in Christ is one man, and the unity of Christians is one man. Perhaps the man himself—that is, the unity of Christians—says this: *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*[^14]. How is this too true of them? Were not the apostles seized, were they not struck, were they not scourged, were they not slain, were they not crucified, were they not burned alive, did they not fight against beasts—they whose memorials we celebrate? But when men were doing these things to them, were they not rejoicing over them? How, then, can the Christian people also say, *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*?

**6.** [v. 1.] We shall understand this if we first look at the title of the psalm. For it has: *To the end, a psalm of the song of the dedication of the house, of David himself*[^15]. In this title is all hope, and the whole mystery for resolving this question. The house will one day be dedicated, which is now being built. For now the house, that is, the Church, is being built; afterward it will be dedicated. At the dedication will appear the glory of the Christian people, which now lies hidden. Let the enemies now rage, let them humble, let them do not whatever they wish, but whatever they are permitted from above. For whatever we suffer from enemies is not to be attributed to the enemies and not to the Lord our God. Since indeed in his own example the mediator showed that, when from above he permits men to harm us, it is not the will to harm that is given from above, but the power. For each evil man has within himself the will to harm; but whether he is able to harm, he does not have in his power. That he wills it, he is already guilty; that he is able, by the hidden disposition of God's providence he is permitted against one for punishment, against another for proving, against another for a crown. For punishment, as the *allophyloi*, that is, the foreigners, were permitted to capture the people of Israel, because they had sinned against God. For proving, the devil was permitted against Job: Job was proved, the devil was confounded. For a crown, the persecutors were permitted against the martyrs: the martyrs were slain, the persecutors supposed themselves to have conquered; in open view they triumphed falsely, in hidden truth the martyrs were truly crowned. Therefore against whom one is permitted belongs to the hidden disposition of God's providence: but that one wills to harm belongs to the man himself; for he does not at once kill the one he wishes to kill.

**7.** And so the Lord himself, the judge of the living and the dead, standing before a human judge, offering us a lesson of humility and patience, not conquered but showing the soldier an example of how to fight, said to the judge who threatened him, swollen with pride and saying, *Do you not know that I have power to release you and to kill you?*—he took away the conceit of the man's puffing, and as though giving back the breath by which he might be deflated: *You would have*, he said, *no power over me, unless it had been given you from above*[^16]. And Job (the devil, of course, killed his sons, the devil took away all his substance), and he, what did he say? *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done; blessed be the name of the Lord*[^17]. Let the enemy not triumph as though he himself did it: I know, he said, by whom he was permitted; to the devil let the will to harm be attributed, to my Lord the power to prove. In the very wound of his body his wife approaches, abandoned, like Eve, the devil's helper, not the husband's comforter; she tempts, and says, amid many reproaches, *Speak some word against God, and die*[^18]. And that Adam on the dunghill was more cautious than Adam in paradise; for Adam in paradise consented to the woman, that he might be sent out of paradise; Adam on the dunghill rejected the woman, that he might be admitted to paradise. What, then, did that Adam on the dunghill—bringing immortality to birth within, oozing with worms without—say to the woman? *You have spoken like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure evils?*[^19] Here too he called the hand of the Lord upon himself the fact that the devil had struck him: because he attended not to who struck, but to who permitted. For indeed the devil himself called that same power, which he wished to be given to him, the hand of the Lord: for, charging a fault against the righteous man, to whom the Lord bore witness, he said to God: *Does Job worship the Lord for nothing? Have you not made a hedge about him and his house, and all his substance round about? You have blessed the works of his hands, and his possession has increased in the land: you have given him such great goods, and therefore he worships you; but put forth your hand, and touch all that he has, unless he bless you to your face*[^20]. What is *put forth your hand*, when he himself wished to put it forth? But because he could not himself put forth his own hand, he called that very power which he received from God the hand of God.

**8.** What then, brothers, since the enemies did such great things against the Christians; and they exulted, and rejoiced over them? But when will it appear that they did not rejoice? When those will be confounded, and these will exult, at the coming of the Lord our God, when he comes bearing recompenses in his hand—damnation for the ungodly, the kingdom for the righteous; fellowship with the devil for the wicked, fellowship with Christ for the faithful. When, then, he has shown this, when the righteous will stand with great constancy (I speak from the Scriptures; recall the reading from the book of Wisdom: *Then the righteous will stand with great constancy against those who afflicted them; but those will say among themselves, repenting and groaning for anguish of spirit: What did our pride profit us, and what has the boasting of riches conferred on us? For all those things have passed away like a shadow*; and what will they say of the righteous? *How are they counted among the sons of God, and their lot is among the saints?*[^21]), then there will be the dedication of the house, which is now being built amid tribulations; then rightly will that people say: *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*[^22]. This voice, then, will be true in the people of God, the people that is now afflicted, now troubled, with such great temptations, such great scandals, such great persecutions, such great pressure. These torments of the mind he does not feel in the Church who does not make progress, for he thinks there is peace: but let him begin to make progress, and then he will see in what pressure he is; because when the blade had grown and borne fruit, then appeared also the weeds: and he who adds knowledge adds sorrow. Let him make progress, and he will see where he is: let the fruit come forth, and the weeds will appear. The voice is truthful through the Apostle, and cannot be blotted out from the beginning to the end: *But all who wish*, he says, *to live godly in Christ suffer persecution. But evil men and seducers go on from bad to worse, themselves erring and leading others into error*[^23]. And whence come those words of the psalm: *Wait for the Lord, be strong, and let your heart take courage, and wait for the Lord*[^24]? It was too little to say once, *Wait for the Lord*, without repeating it: lest perhaps he should wait two days, three days, four days, and the pressure and tribulation should remain; therefore he added, *be strong*; he added, *and let your heart take courage*. And because from the beginning to the end it will be so, what the sentence has at its head, this it has at its end, *and wait for the Lord*. These things that press you pass away, and he for whom you wait will come, and will wipe away the sweat: he will dry the tear, and you will weep no more. But now let us groan amid tribulations, as Job says: *Is not human life a trial upon the earth?*[^25]

**9.** Yet, brothers, before the day of the dedication of the house comes, let us see that our head has already been dedicated: the dedication of the house has already been made in the head, as the dedication of the foundation. The head is above, the foundation below: but let us not perhaps for that reason have wrongly said that Christ is the foundation; rather he is the summit, he ascended into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father. But I think we have not erred. For the Apostle said: *For no one can lay another foundation than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ: but if anyone builds upon this foundation gold, silver, precious stones*[^26]. Those who live well, who honor and praise God, who are patient in tribulations, who long for their homeland, they build gold, silver, precious stones: but those who still love worldly things, and are entangled in earthly affairs, and are given over to certain bonds and affections of the flesh—their houses, spouses, possessions—and yet are Christians, in such a way that their heart does not depart from Christ, and they set nothing before Christ, just as in building nothing is set before the foundation; these indeed build wood, hay, straw: but what follows? *What sort each one's work is, the fire will prove*[^27]: the fire of tribulation and temptation. This fire here proved many martyrs, but it proves the whole human race at the end. Martyrs were found who had these worldly things. How many rich men and senators suffered! Yet some of them were building wood, hay, straw in their affections for things of the flesh and worldly cares: but because they had Christ as the foundation upon which they were building, the hay burned, and they themselves remained on the foundation. So the Apostle says: *If anyone's work remains, he will receive a reward*[^28]: and he will lose nothing; for what he loved, this he will find. What, then, did the fire of tribulation do to them? It proved them. *If anyone's work remains, he will receive a reward; if anyone's work burns, he will suffer loss; but he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire*[^29]. But it is one thing not to be harmed by the fire, another to be saved through the fire. And whence? Because of the foundation. Let the foundation, then, not depart from the heart. Do not lay the foundation upon the hay—that is, do not set the hay before the foundation, so that the hay has the first place in your heart and Christ the second: but if there can now be no avoiding the hay being there, let Christ at least have the first place there, and the hay the second.

**10.** Christ, then, is the foundation. As I said, our head has been dedicated, the head itself is the foundation. But the foundation is usually below, the head above. Let your Holiness understand what I say; perhaps I shall explain it in the name of Christ. There are two kinds of weight. For weight is a kind of impulse of each thing, as though striving toward its own place: this is weight. You carry a stone in your hand, you feel its weight; it presses your hand, because it seeks its own place. And do you wish to see what it seeks? Withdraw your hand, it comes to the earth, it rests in the earth: it has arrived where it was tending, it has found its own place. That weight, then, was a movement as it were spontaneous, without soul, without sense. There are others that seek their place upward. For if you pour water over oil, by its weight it tends to the bottom. For it seeks its own place, it seeks to be set in order: because water is over oil contrary to order. Until, then, it comes to its order, its movement is restless, until it holds its own place. On the contrary, pour oil under water—for example, as when a jar of oil falls into water, into the deep, into the sea, and is broken—the oil does not suffer itself to be beneath. As water poured over oil by its weight seeks its place at the bottom, so oil poured beneath water by its weight seeks its place at the top. If, then, it is so, brothers, where do fire and water tend? Fire is borne upward, it seeks its place; and water seeks its place by its weight. The stone seeks the bottom, and wood, and columns, and earth, from which these houses are built: they belong, then, to that kind of things which by their weight are borne downward. It is plain, then, that for this reason the foundation is set below, because by their weight they are borne downward; and unless there be something to uphold them, the whole falls, because the whole inclines to the earth. For things tending to the bottom, then, the foundation is set at the bottom: but the Church of God, set at the bottom, tends toward heaven. Our foundation, then, is set there, our Lord Jesus Christ sitting at the right hand of the Father. If, then, your Holiness has understood that our foundation has already been dedicated, let us hear the psalm briefly, and run through it.

**11.** [v. 2.] *I will exalt you, O Lord, for you have upheld me, and you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*[^30]. What enemies? The Jews? In the dedication of the foundation let us understand the very dedication of the house to come. For what is now said from the person of the foundation will then be said from the person of the whole house. What enemies, then? The Jews, or rather the devil and his angels, who departed in confusion after the Lord rose again? The chief of death grieved that death was conquered. *And you have not let my enemies rejoice over me*: because I could not be held among the dead.

**12.** [v. 3.] *O Lord my God, I cried to you, and you healed me*[^31]: the Lord prayed on the mountain before the passion; he healed him. Whom did he heal, who never was sick—the Word, God, the Word, divinity? No, but he bore the death of the flesh, he bore your wound, to heal you of your wound. The flesh, however, was healed. When? When it rose again. Hear the Apostle, see the true healing: *Death is swallowed up*, he says, *in victory. Where, O death, is your sting? where, O death, is your striving?*[^32] Therefore that exaltation will then be the exaltation of our voice; now it is the exaltation of Christ.

**13.** [v. 4.] *O Lord, you have brought back my soul from the realm of the dead*[^33]. There is no need for it to be expounded. *You have saved me from those who go down into the pit*. Who are they who go down into the pit? All sinners sinking into the deep: for the pit is the depth of the world. What is this depth of the world? The abundance of luxury and wickedness: those, then, who immerse themselves in lusts and earthly desires go down into the pit. Such men persecuted Christ. But what does he say? *You have saved me from those who go down into the pit*.

**14.** [v. 5.] *Sing to the Lord, you his holy ones*[^34]: because your head has risen again, hope this, you the rest of the members—what you see in the head; hope this, you members—what you have believed in the head. The proverb is ancient and true: Where the head is, there the rest of the members. Christ ascended into heaven, where we are to follow: he did not remain among the dead, he rose again, he dies no more; when we too have risen again, we shall die no more. Having these promises, then: *Sing to the Lord, you his holy ones, and give thanks to the memorial of his holiness*. What is, *give thanks to the memorial*? For you had forgotten him, but he did not forget you.

**15.** [v. 6.] *For there is wrath in his indignation, and life in his will*[^35]. Wrath in his indignation against the sinner: *On the day you eat of it, you shall die the death*[^36]. They touched, they died, they were dismissed from paradise, because *there is wrath in his indignation*: but not without hope, because *there is life in his will*. What is, *in his will*? Not by our own powers, not by our own merits: but because he willed it, he made us safe; not because we were worthy. For of what is a sinner worthy, except punishment? Yet he gave life even in place of punishment. And if he granted life to the ungodly, what does he keep in store for the faithful?

**16.** *In the evening weeping will linger*[^37]. Do not fear, because he had said to us, *Sing*; and here there is groaning: in singing, exultation; in praying, groaning. Groan over present things, sing over things to come: pray over the matter, sing over the hope. *In the evening weeping will linger*. What is, *in the evening weeping will linger*? Evening comes when the sun sets. The sun set for man—that is, that light of righteousness, the presence of God. So when Adam was driven out, what is said in Genesis? When God was walking in paradise; toward evening God was walking: by then that sinner had hidden himself within the tree, he did not wish to see the face of God, in which he had been wont to rejoice: the sun of righteousness had set for him, he did not rejoice at the presence of God. From there this whole mortal life began. *In the evening weeping will linger*. Long will you be in weeping, O human race: for you are born of Adam, and so it has come to pass; and we too are of Adam, and as many as have begotten children and shall beget them are of Adam, of whom they themselves too were created. *In the evening weeping will linger; and in the morning, rejoicing*: when the light begins to rise for the faithful, which had set for sinners. For this reason too the Lord Jesus Christ rose in the morning from the tomb, so that what he dedicated in the foundation he might promise to the house. In our Lord there was an evening, in which he was buried; and a morning, in which he rose again, on the third day: you too were buried in the evening in paradise, and you have risen again on the third day. How on the third day? If you consider the age of the world, the first day is before the Law, the second under the Law, the third under grace. What your head showed in those three days is shown in you in these three days of the world. When? In the morning we must hope, we must rejoice; but for now we must endure, and groan.

**17.** [v. 7.] *But I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever*[^38]. In what abundance did man say, *I shall not be moved forever*? We understand, brothers, the person of lowly man. Who here has abundance? No one. What is the abundance of man? Hardship, calamity. But do the rich have abundance? They are the more in want the more they have: they are laid waste by desires, scattered by cupidities, tortured by fears, wasted away by sadness: what abundance is theirs? There was abundance when man was set in paradise, when nothing was lacking to him, when he enjoyed God: but he said, *I shall not be moved forever*. How did he say, *I shall not be moved forever*? When he willingly heard, *Taste, and you shall be as gods*: when God was saying, *On the day you eat of it, you shall die the death*, and the devil, *You shall not die the death*[^39]. Believing, then, the one who urged such things, he said, *I shall not be moved forever*.

**18.** [vv. 8-9.] But because the Lord had spoken truly, that he would take away from the proud what he had given to the lowly when he created him, there follows, and it says: *O Lord, in your will you furnished strength to my beauty*: that is, because I was good and strong not from myself, but from you I was both beautiful and strong; you had furnished strength to my beauty, out of your will, by which you had made me. And to show me that it was out of your will that I was this: *You turned your face away from me, and I was troubled*[^40]. He turned his face, then, away from the one whom he sent forth out of paradise. Now set here, let him cry out and say: *To you, O Lord, I will cry, and to my God I will make supplication*[^41]. In paradise you did not cry out, but praised; you did not groan, but enjoyed: set outside, groan and cry out. He draws near to the one in tribulation, who forsook the one in pride. For *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^42]. *To you, O Lord, I will cry, and to my God I will make supplication*.

**19.** [v. 10.] Now from the person of the Lord, of our very foundation, this follows: *What profit is there in my blood, while I go down into corruption?*[^43] What, then, does he pray for? That he may rise again. For if I go down, he says, into corruption, if my flesh has been corrupted in this way—like that of other men, so as to rise again at the end—why did I shed my blood? For if I do not rise again now, I shall proclaim to no one, I shall gain no one: but that I may proclaim to someone your wonders, your praises, eternal life, let my flesh rise up, let it not go into corruption. For if it goes the way of other men, what profit is there in my blood? *Shall the dust confess to you, or proclaim your truth?* Confession is of two kinds, either of sin or of praise. When it is ill with us, let us confess our sins in tribulations; when it is well with us, let us confess praise to God in the exultation of righteousness: yet let us not be without confession.

**20.** [v. 11.] *The Lord has heard, and has had mercy on me*[^44]. How? Attend to the dedication of the house. He has heard, and has had mercy. *The Lord has become my helper*.

**21.** [v. 12.] Hear now the resurrection itself: *You have turned my mourning into joy for me, you have torn off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness*[^45]. What is the sackcloth? Mortality. Sackcloth is made from goats and kids, and goats and kids are set among the sinners. The Lord took from our number only the sackcloth, he did not take on the desert of the sackcloth. The desert of the sackcloth is sin: that sackcloth is mortality. He took on mortality for your sake, who had no desert of death. For he has the desert of death who sins: but he who did not sin had no desert of the sackcloth. In another place there is his own voice, saying: *But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself in sackcloth*[^46]. What is this, *I clothed myself in sackcloth*? This I set against my persecutors, what I have of sackcloth. So that they might think him a man, he hid himself from the eyes of his persecutors; because the persecutors were unworthy to see him clothed in sackcloth. Therefore, *you have torn off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness*.

**22.** [v. 13.] *That my glory may sing to you, and I be not pierced with sorrow*[^47]. What is in the head, this is in the body. What is, *I be not pierced with sorrow*? I shall now die no more. For he was pierced when he hung on the cross, he was struck with a lance. Our head, then, says, *I shall not be pierced*, I shall now die no more. But what do we say, with reference to the dedication of the house? Let no conscience pierce us with the goads of sins. For all will be forgiven, and then we shall be free. *That my glory may sing to you*, he says, not my lowliness. If ours, then Christ's also: because we are the body of Christ. Why? Because although Christ sits in heaven, he is going to say to certain ones: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^48]. He is both there and here: there in himself, here in us. What, then, does he say? *That my glory may sing to you, and I be not pierced with sorrow*. My lowliness groans to you, my glory will sing to you. Now at the end: *O Lord my God, I will confess to you forever*. What is, *I will confess to you forever*? I will praise you forever, since we have said that there is confession also in praises, not only in sins. Confess, then, now what you have done against God, and you will confess what God has done for you. What have you done? Sins. What has God done? When you confess your iniquity, he forgives you your sins, so that afterward, confessing his praises forever, you may not be pierced with sorrow by sin.
