# On the Same Psalm 25

**1.** When the apostle Paul was being read, your Holiness heard with us: *As the truth is in Jesus, that you put off, according to your former conduct, the old man, who is corrupted according to the desires of deception; but be renewed in the spirit of your mind, and put on the new man, who according to God is created in righteousness and the holiness of truth*[^1]. And lest anyone suppose that some substance is to be put off, as one strips off a tunic; or that something is to be received from outside, as one receives some garment, as though laying aside one tunic and taking up another; and lest this carnal understanding should not allow men to do within themselves spiritually what the Apostle was commanding: he went on, and explained what it was to put off the old man, and to put on the new. For the rest of that reading pertains to the same understanding. For he says, as to one saying: And how am I to put off the old, or how am I to put on the new? Or am I myself a third man, about to put off the old man, whom I had, and to receive the new, whom I did not have; so that three men are understood, and there is in the middle one who puts off the old man, and receives the new? lest, then, anyone, hindered by such carnal thinking, should do less than what is commanded, and, not doing it, should excuse himself by the obscurity of the reading, he says in what follows: *Wherefore, putting away lying, speak the truth.* And behold, this is to put off the old man, and to put on the new: *Wherefore, putting away lying, speak the truth, each one with his neighbor: because we are members one of another*[^2].  
**2.** But let none of you think, brothers, that you should speak truth with a Christian and lies with a pagan. Speak with your neighbor. Your neighbor is the one who was born, like you, from Adam and Eve. We are all neighbors by the condition of our earthly birth; but we are brothers in another way through that hope of a heavenly inheritance. You must regard every human being as your neighbor, even before he is a Christian. For you do not know what he is before God; you are ignorant of how God has foreknown him. Sometimes the person you mock as he worships stones is converted and worships God, perhaps more devoutly than you who mocked him a little earlier. So our neighbors are hidden among those people who are not yet in the Church, and people far from us are hidden within the Church. Therefore, since we do not know the future, let us regard everyone as our neighbor, not only by the condition of human mortality, through which we have come to this earth with the same lot, but also by hope of that inheritance, because we do not know what someone who is now nothing will become.

**3.** Therefore attend to the rest in putting on the new man and putting off the old. *Putting away*, he says, *lying, speak the truth, each one with his neighbor: because we are members one of another. Be angry; and do not sin.* If you are angry at your servant because he has sinned: lest you too sin, be angry at yourself. *Let not the sun go down upon your anger*[^3]: it is understood indeed according to time, brothers; because even if, from the very human condition and weakness of the mortality which we bear, anger creeps upon a Christian, it ought not to be held long and become a day-old thing. Cast it out of the heart before this visible light goes down, lest that invisible light desert you. But it is also well understood otherwise, that our sun is the truth, Christ the sun of righteousness, not this sun which is worshiped by the Pagans and Manichees, and is seen even by sinners; but that other one by whose truth human nature is illumined, at whom the angels rejoice, but at whose rays the weakened keenness of the human heart, even if it trembles, is yet purified for the contemplation of him through the commandments. When this sun has begun to dwell in a man through faith, let not the anger which is born in you so prevail in you that it goes down upon your anger, that is, that Christ deserts your mind; because Christ does not wish to dwell with your anger. For he as it were goes down from you, when you go down from him: because anger, when it has grown old, becomes hatred; and when it has become hatred, you are now a murderer. *For everyone who hates his brother is a murderer*[^4], as the apostle John says. Likewise he himself says, that *everyone who hates his brother remains in darkness*[^5]: and it is no wonder if that one remains in darkness, from whom the sun has gone down.

**4.** To this perhaps pertains also what you heard in the Gospel: *The ship was in danger on the lake, and Jesus was sleeping*[^6]. For we sail through a kind of lake, and wind and storms are not lacking: by the daily temptations of this world our ship is almost filled. But whence does this come about, but because Jesus is sleeping? If Jesus were not sleeping in you, you would not suffer these storms; but you would have tranquillity within, with Jesus keeping watch with you. But what is, Jesus is sleeping? Your faith, which is concerning Jesus, has fallen asleep. The storms of this lake arise, you see the evil flourish, the good labor; it is a temptation, it is a fruit. And your soul says: O God, is this your righteousness, that the evil flourish, the good labor? You say to God: Is this your righteousness? And God to you: Is this your faith? For did I promise you this? was it for this that you were made a Christian, that you might flourish in this world? You are tormented, because here the evil flourish, who will afterward be tormented with the devil? But whence do you say this? whence are you troubled by the waves of the lake and by the storm? Because Jesus is sleeping, that is, because your faith, which is concerning Jesus, is lulled to sleep in your heart. What do you do that you may be set free? Rouse Jesus, and say: *Master, we perish*[^7]. For the uncertainties of the lake move us: we perish. He will awake, that is, your faith will return to you; and with his help, you will consider in your soul, that the things now given to the evil will not endure with them: for either, living, they desert them, or they are deserted by them dying; but what is promised to you will remain forever. To them what is granted temporally is quickly taken away. For it flourished as the flower of grass. For all flesh is grass: the grass has withered, and the flower has fallen: but the word of the Lord remains forever. Set, then, your back to that which falls, and your face to that which remains. Christ awaking, that storm will no longer shake your heart, the waves will not fill your ship: because your faith commands the winds and the waves, and the danger will pass. For to this, brothers, pertains all that the Apostle says, about putting off the old man. *Be angry, and do not sin: let not the sun go down upon your anger: nor give place to the devil*[^8]: the old man, then, gave place, let the new not give it. *Let him who stole, steal no more*[^9]: the old, then, stole, let the new not steal. He is the same man, he is one man: he was Adam, let him be Christ: he was old, let him be new; and the rest which follows there.

**5.** But let us look at the psalm a little more carefully, because when anyone has advanced in the Church, he must needs endure the evil in the Church. But such a one does not recognize them; although many evil men murmur against the evil, just as one healthy man more easily bears two sick than two sick bear each other one by one. And so we give this charge, brothers: The Church of this time is a threshing floor: we have often said it, we often say it: It has both chaff and grain. Let no one seek to drive out all the chaff, except at the time of winnowing. Let no one, before the time of winnowing, desert the threshing floor, as though unwilling to endure the sinners: lest, found outside the threshing floor, he be gathered up by the birds before he enters into the barn. But how this is, brothers, attend. When the grains have begun to be threshed, they no longer touch one another among the chaff; so they as it were do not know one another, because the chaff comes between. And whoever looks at the threshing floor from afar, thinks it chaff alone: unless he looks more diligently, unless he stretches out his hand, unless by the breath of the mouth, that is, by the purging blast, he separates them, he hardly comes to the distinguishing of the grains. Therefore sometimes even the grains themselves are as it were sundered from one another, and not touching one another, so that each one, when he has advanced, thinks himself alone. This thought, brothers, tempted Elijah, so great a man; and he said to God, as the Apostle too recalls: *They have killed your prophets, they have dug down your altars, and I am left alone, and they seek my life. But what does the divine answer say to him? I have left for myself seven thousand men, who have not bent their knees before Baal*[^10]. He did not say to him: You have two or three others like you. Do not, he says, think yourself alone. There are, he says, seven thousand others, and you think yourself alone! And so we give this charge briefly, as I had begun to say. Let your fraternal Holiness attend with me, and let the mercy of God be present in our hearts, that it may be so understood by you, that it may bear fruit, and work in you. Hear briefly: Whoever is still evil, let him not think that no one is good: whoever is good, let him not think that he alone is good. Do you hold this? Behold, I repeat it, see that I say: Whoever is evil, questioning his own conscience, and reporting evil to himself, let him not think that no one is good: whoever is good, let him not think that he alone is good; and let not the good man fear in the mixture of the evil, because the time will come when he will be separated from there. For to this we have sung today: *Do not destroy my soul with the impious, and my life with men of blood.* For what is: *Do not destroy me with the impious?* Do not destroy me together with them. Why does he fear to be destroyed together? For I see it said to God, since now you endure us together, whom you endure together, do not destroy us together. And the whole psalm has this, which I wish briefly to consider with your Holiness, because it is short.

**6.** [v 1.] *Judge me, Lord*: he wishes for himself troublesome and as it were dangerous vows, that he may be judged. What is the being judged that he wishes for? He wishes to be separated from the evil. In another place he says evidently of this judgment of separation: *Judge me, Lord, and distinguish my cause from the unholy nation*[^11]. He showed what he had said: *Judge*: lest as though without judgment (because now both good and evil enter the Church) both the good and the evil should go into the eternal fire. *Judge me, Lord.* Why? *Because I have walked in my innocence, and, hoping in the Lord, I will not be moved.* What is, *hoping in the Lord?* For that man wavers among the evil who does not hope in the Lord; hence it came about that schisms were made. They trembled among the evil, while they themselves were worse, and as though unwilling to be good among the evil. O if they were grain, they would bear with the chaff on the threshing floor until the time of winnowing. But because they were chaff, the wind blew before the very winnowing, and snatched the chaff from the threshing floor, and cast it into the thorns. And the chaff indeed was cast out from there: but is what remained grain alone? Nothing flies off before the winnowing but chaff, but there remains both grain and chaff; the chaff will be winnowed when the time of winnowing comes. This one said: *I have walked in my innocence, and, hoping in the Lord, I will not be moved.* For if I have hoped in man, I shall perhaps see that man at some time living evilly, and not holding those ways which he either learned or teaches as good in the Church, but those which, at the devil's teaching, he pursued; and because my hope will be in man, my hope will waver as the man wavers, and my hope will fall as the man falls: but because I hope in the Lord, I will not be moved.

**7.** [v 2.] There follows: *Prove me, Lord, and test me; burn my reins and my heart.* What is, *burn my reins and my heart?* Burn my delights, burn my thoughts (he put heart for thoughts, reins for delights) lest I think anything evil, lest anything evil delight me. But with what will you burn my reins? With the fire of your word. With what will you burn my heart? With the heat of your spirit. Of which heat it is said elsewhere: *And there is none who can hide himself from his heat*[^12]: and of which fire the Lord says: *I came to send fire upon the earth*[^13].

**8.** [v 3.] Therefore there follows: *Because your mercy is before my eyes, and I have been well-pleased in your truth*: that is, I have not been pleasing in man, but within I have been pleasing to you, where you see; and I do not fear if I displease where men see, as the Apostle says: *But let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself alone, and not in another*[^14].

**9.** [vv 4.5.] *I have not sat*, he says, *with the council of vanity.* What is this: *I have not sat*, let your Holiness attend. As God sees, he says *I have not sat.* Sometimes you are not in the council, and yet you sit there. For example, you do not sit in the theater, but you think theatrical things, against which it was said: *Burn my reins*: you sit there in heart, although you are not there in body. But it may happen that you are detained by someone, and gathered there, or some pious office makes you sit there. How can this happen? It happens from an office of piety that a servant of God has a necessity to be in the amphitheater; he wished to set free I know not what gladiator, it might come about that he should sit, and wait until the one whom he desired to set free came out. Behold, he did not sit in the council of vanity, although he seemed to sit there in body. What is to sit? To consent with those who sit there: which if, being present, you do not do, you have not sat there: if, being absent, you do it, you have sat there. *And with those who do iniquities I will not enter in. I have hated the congregation of the malicious.* You see that it is within. *And with the impious I will not sit.*

**10.** [v 6.] *I will wash my hands among the innocent*: not with this visible water. You wash your hands, when you think piously of your works, and innocently before the eyes of God: because there is also an altar before the eyes of God, which the priest entered, who first offered himself for us. There is a heavenly altar, and no one embraces that altar but he who washes his hands among the innocent. For many touch this altar unworthily, and God tolerates that his sacraments suffer wrong for a time. But, my brothers, will the heavenly Jerusalem be as these walls are? Not as you are received with the evil within these walls of the church, will you be received with the evil into the bosom of Abraham: do not, then, fear, wash your hands. *And I will go around the altar of the Lord*: where you offer vows to the Lord, where you pour out prayers, where your conscience is pure, where you tell God who you are; and if there is perhaps something in you that displeases God, he cares for it to whom you confess. Wash, then, your hands among the innocent, and go around the altar of the Lord, that you may hear the voice of praise.

**11.** [v 7.] For this follows: *That I may hear the voice of praise, and tell of all your wondrous works.* What is: *That I may hear the voice of praise?* That I may understand, he says. For this is to hear before God, not as these sounds, which many hear, and many do not hear. How many are hearers toward us, and deaf toward God! How many having ears, do not have those ears of which Jesus says: *He who has ears to hear, let him hear*[^15]! Therefore what is to hear the voice of praise? I will say, if I can, with the help of the Lord's mercy, and your prayers. To hear the voice of praise is to understand within, that whatever evil is in you from sins is yours; whatever good is in righteous works is God's. So hear the voice of praise, that you do not praise yourself even when you are good: for by praising yourself as good, you become evil; for humility had made you good, pride makes you evil. You had been converted that you might be enlightened, and by your conversion you were made luminous, made illumined by conversion. But to what? to yourself? If, converted to yourself, you could be enlightened, you could never be darkened, because you would always be with yourself. Why were you enlightened? Because you converted yourself to something else which you were not. What is the something else which you were not? God is light. For you were not light, because you were a sinner. For the Apostle says to those whom he wishes to hear the voice of praise: *For you were once darkness, but now light*[^16]. What is: *You were once darkness*, but old men? *But now light*: not without cause are you light, you who were just now darkness, except that you have been enlightened. Do not think yourself to be the light: that is the light, which enlightens every man coming into this world; but you, through yourself, through evil will, through your turning away, had been darkened, now you shine. But he added at once, lest those be proud to whom it was said: *But now light*, and added, *in the Lord.* For so he says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord.* Therefore if apart from the Lord, then not light: but if therefore light, because in the Lord; what do you have that you have not received? But if you have received it, why do you boast as though you had not received it? For this the Apostle himself said in another place to men being proud, and wishing to attribute to themselves what is God's, and to glory in a good thing as though in their own, this he says to them: *For what do you have that you have not received? But if you have received it, why do you boast as though you had not received it?*[^17] He who gave to the humble, takes away from the proud: for he who gave can take away. For to this, brothers, if at least I have explained what I wished (but I have explained as far as I could, even if not as far as I wished), to this pertains what he says: *I will wash my hands among the innocent, and I will go around your altar, Lord, that I may hear the voice of your praise*: that is, from my own good I will presume not on myself, but on you who gave it, lest I wish to be praised concerning myself in myself, but concerning you in you. Therefore there follows: *That I may hear the voice of your praise, and tell of all your wondrous works*: not mine, but yours.

**12.** [vv 8.9.] And now see, brothers, see that lover of God presuming on God, set among the evil, asking God that he be not destroyed together with the evil, because God does not err in judging. For you, when you have seen men enter into one place, think them to be of the same merit: but God is not deceived, do not fear. You, with the wind as judge, distinguish chaff from grain: you wish the wind to blow for you, and you are not the wind, but you wish the wind to blow for you: and when with the winnowing-fan you have shaken both out, the wind takes the light, the heavy remains. You seek, then, the wind to judge the threshing floor. Does God seek another to judge with him, lest he destroy the good with the evil? Do not, then, fear; be secure, being good, even in the midst of the evil; and say what you hear: *Lord, I have loved the beauty of your house.* The house of God is the Church; it still has the evil, but the beauty of God's house is in the good, is in the saints: that very beauty of your house I have loved. *And the place of the dwelling of your glory.* What is this? This too I will say, it pertains to that somewhat obscure sense: let the Lord help me, and the intention of your heart inspired by that same Lord. What does he say, *the place of the dwelling of your glory?* He said first, *the beauty of your house*: and he explains what the beauty of God's house is: *The place*, he says, *of the dwelling of your glory.* It does not suffice to say: The place of the dwelling of God, but: The place of the dwelling of the glory of God. What is the glory of God? Of which I was speaking a little before, that he who is made good may glory not in himself, but in the Lord. For all have sinned, and lack the glory of God. In those, then, in whom the Lord so dwells that he himself is glorified even from their good things, so that they do not wish to attribute to themselves, and as it were claim as their own, what they received from him, these pertain to the beauty of God's house. Nor would the Scripture wish to distinguish them, except that there are certain ones who have indeed the gift of God, and do not wish to glory in God, but in themselves: they have indeed the gift of God, but do not pertain to the beauty of God's house. For those who pertain to the beauty of God's house, in whom the glory of God dwells, they themselves are the place of the dwelling of God's glory. But in whom does the glory of God dwell, but in those who so glory that they glory not in themselves, but in the Lord? Therefore because I have loved the beauty of your house, that is, all who are there and seek your glory; but I also have not presumed in man, and have not consented to the impious, and will not enter in, and will not sit in their congregation; because I was thus in the Church of God, what will you repay me? There follows what we may answer: *Do not destroy together with the impious my soul, and with men of blood my life.*

**13.** [vv 10-12.] *In whose hands are iniquities, their right hand is filled with bribes*: bribes are not only money, not only gold and silver, not only gifts; nor do all who receive these receive bribes. For sometimes they are received by the Church. As I say, Peter received, the Lord received, he had money-bags, what was contributed Judas carried off. But what is it to receive bribes? To praise a man for the sake of bribes, to flatter a man, to fawn by flattering, to judge against the truth for the sake of bribes. For what bribes? Not only for the sake of gold and silver and something of this kind, but even for the sake of praise he who judges badly receives a bribe, and a bribe than which nothing is more empty. For his hand was open to receive the judgment of another's tongue, and he lost the judgment of his own conscience. Therefore *in whose hands are iniquities, their right hand is filled with bribes.* You see, brothers, that they are before God, and those in whose hands are not iniquities, nor is their right hand filled with bribes, are surely before God, and can say only to God: You know: they can say only to him: *Do not destroy together with the impious my soul, and with men of blood my life;* who alone can see that they do not receive bribes. For example, it happens that two men have a case before a servant of God; no one but says his own case is just; for if he thought his case unjust, he would not seek a judge. And this one thinks he has a just case, and so does that one. They come to the judge. Before the sentence is pronounced, both say: We embrace your judgment; whatever you judge, far be it that we reject it. What do you too say? Judge whatever you wish, only judge: indeed if I resist in anything, let me be anathema. Both love the judge before he judges. But when the sentence has been pronounced, it will be against one; and neither of them knows against which it will be. That judge, then, if he wishes to please both, receives a bribe, the praise of men. But having received that bribe, see what bribe he loses. He receives what sounds, and passes: he loses what is spoken, and never passes. The word of God is always spoken, never passes: the word of man, as soon as it is spoken, passes. He holds empty things, lets go solid things. But if he gazes upon God, he is about to pronounce sentence against one, having considered God, under whom as judge he pronounces it. But that one against whom it has been pronounced, even if it can no longer be broken, because it is held by a law perhaps not ecclesiastical, but of the princes of the world, who have so deferred to the Church, that whatever has been judged in it cannot be dissolved; if, then, it cannot be broken, he no longer wishes to look at himself, and directs his blind eyes upon the judge, and detracts as much as he can. He wished, he says, to please him, he favored the rich man, or received something from him, or feared to offend him. He accuses as though bribes had been received. But if a poor man has a case against a rich man, and judgment is given for the poor man; the rich man likewise says: He received bribes. What bribes from a poor man? He saw, he says, a poor man, and lest he be reproved for acting against the poor man, he oppressed justice, and pronounced sentence against the truth. Since, then, it must needs be that this is said, see that it cannot be said by those who do not receive bribes, except before the eyes of God, who alone sees who receives and who does not receive: *But I have walked in my innocence, redeem me, and have mercy on me: my foot has stood in uprightness.* I have indeed been shaken on all sides by the scandals and temptations of those who reprove a judgment with human rashness: but *my foot has stood in uprightness.* But why *in uprightness?* Because he had said above: *And, hoping in the Lord, I will not be moved.*

**14.** What, then, does he conclude? *In the churches I will bless you, Lord.* That is, in the churches I will not bless myself, as though certain of men, but I will bless you in my works. For this is to bless God in the churches, brothers, so to live that through the conduct of each one God may be blessed. For he who blesses the Lord with the tongue, and curses with deeds, does not bless the Lord in the churches. With the tongue nearly all bless, but not all with deeds. Some bless with the voice, some with their conduct. But those in whose conduct is not found what they say, cause God to be blasphemed, so that those who do not yet enter the Church, although they love their sins, and therefore are unwilling to be Christians, yet excuse themselves through the evil, that they may flatter themselves, deceiving themselves, and say: Why do you persuade me to be a Christian? I have suffered fraud from a Christian, and I have never done it; a Christian swore falsely to me, and I never have. And when they say these things, they are hindered from salvation: so that it profits them nothing, not indeed that they are now good, but that they are moderately evil. For as it profits nothing to open the eyes, if one is in darkness: so it profits nothing to be in the light, if the eyes are closed. So too the pagan indeed (that we may speak rather of those as though living well) is in darkness with open eyes; because he does not recognize his light, the Lord: but the Christian living badly is indeed in the light which is none but God's, but with closed eyes. For by living badly he does not wish to see him, in whose name he is as a blind man set in the light, animated by no vision of the true light.
