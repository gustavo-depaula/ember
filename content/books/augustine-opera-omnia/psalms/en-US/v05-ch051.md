# On the Same Psalm 118 (26)

**1.** [v. 129.] These are the words of the psalm about which, with the Lord's help, we are going to discourse: *Wonderful are your testimonies; therefore my soul has searched them out.* Who enumerates, at least in general, the testimonies of God? Heaven and earth, his visible and invisible works, give in a way testimony of his goodness and greatness; and the very frequent and accustomed course of nature, by which the rapacity of times rolls on, in the kinds of all things, although temporal and mortal—which surely have grown cheap by familiarity—if a godly considerer attends, gives testimony to the Creator. But which of these is there that is not wonderful, if we measure each single thing not by familiarity, but by reason? But if we should as it were dare to behold all things under the gaze of a single contemplation, does there not come about in us what the prophet says: *I considered your works, and was afraid*[^1]? And yet this man was not terrified by the very wonder of things, but rather said that this was the reason why he ought to search them out, because they are wonderful. For when he had said, *Wonderful are your testimonies,* he went on and added, *therefore my soul has searched them out;* as though he was made the more curious by the difficulty of investigating. For the more hidden causes a thing has, the more wonderful it is.

**2.** If, then, such a man should meet us, who says that for this reason he searches out the testimonies of God, because they are wonderful, since the whole creation, both that which is seen and that which is not seen, is full of them; do we not check him, saying, *Seek not the things too high for you, and search not the things too strong for you; but the things which the Lord has commanded you, think on these always*[^2]? But if he should answer us and say: These very things which the Lord has commanded, and which you bid me think on, are his wonderful testimonies; for they testify that he is both Lord, because he commands, and good and great, because he commands such things: shall we dare to call a man back from searching them out, and not rather exhort him to do it diligently, and to spend on so great a thing as much labor as he can? Or shall we perhaps confess that the precepts of God are indeed testimonies of his goodness, but deny that they are wonderful? For what wonder is it, if the good Lord commands good things? Indeed this very thing is altogether to be wondered at, and why it is so is to be searched out, that, although the good God commanded good things, yet he gave that good law to those whom that same law could not give life, nor would there be any righteousness from the good law. *For if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would altogether be from the law*[^3]. Why, then, was a law given which could not give life, and from which there would be no righteousness? Surely it is to be wondered at, surely it is to be marveled at. These, then, are the wonderful testimonies of God: for this reason this man's soul searched them out, since of these it could not be said to him, *Search not the things too strong for you; but the things which the Lord has commanded you, think on these always.* For these are the very things which the Lord has commanded us; and therefore they are to be thought on always. Let us rather, then, see what this man's soul, which searched them out, has found.

**3.** [v. 130.] *The manifestation,* he says, *of your words enlightens, and gives understanding to little ones.* What is a little one, except the humble and the weak? Do not be proud, then; do not presume on your own strength, which is none; and you will understand why a good law was given by the good God, which yet cannot give life. For it was given for this, that it might make you, from a great one, a little one; that it might show you that you do not have, from your own resources, strength to do the law; and so, needy and lacking help, you might flee to grace, and cry, *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak*[^4]. This, then, by searching, this little one understood, which Paul, the least of the Apostles—that is, the little one—shows: that the law which could not give life was given for this reason, because *Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise of faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*[^5]. So, O Lord, so do, merciful Lord; command what cannot be fulfilled, nay, command what can be fulfilled only through your grace: that when men by their own strength cannot fulfill it, *every mouth may be stopped*[^6], and no one seem great to himself. Let all be little ones, and let the whole world become guilty before you: because *no flesh will be justified before you by the law; for through the law is the knowledge of sin. But now, without law, your righteousness has been manifested, having testimony from the Law and the Prophets*[^7]. These are your wonderful testimonies, which the soul of this little one searched out; and therefore he found, because he was humbled, and made a little one. For who does your commandments as they are to be done—that is, out of faith that works through love—unless that very love is poured out in his heart through the Holy Spirit?

**4.** [v. 131.] This too this little one confesses: *I opened my mouth,* he says, *and drew in the spirit, for I desired your commandments.* What did he desire, except to do the divine commandments? But the weak one had not the means whereby to do strong things, the little one to do great things: he opened his mouth, confessing that of himself he would not do it; and drew in that whereby he might do it: he opened his mouth by asking, seeking, knocking; and, thirsting, he drank the good Spirit, whereby he might do, what through himself he could not, the commandment holy and just and good. For if we, being evil, know how to give good gifts to our children, how much more does our Father from heaven give the good Spirit to those who ask him[^8]? For not those who are led by their own spirit, but as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God[^9]: not because they themselves do nothing; but, that they may not do nothing good, they are led by the Good One that they may act. For each one is made the more a good son, the more abundantly the good Spirit is given him by the Father.

**5.** [v. 132.] Finally, this man still asks. He opened his mouth, indeed, and drew in the spirit; but still he knocks at the Father, and seeks: he drinks, but the more sweetly he has felt, the more ardently he still thirsts. Hear the words of one thirsting: *Look,* he says, *upon me, and have mercy on me, according to the judgment of those who love your name;* that is, according to the judgment which you made upon those who love your name; since, that they might love you, you first loved them. For so the apostle John says: *We love God;* and as if the cause were sought which made us love, he added, *because he first loved us*[^10].

**6.** [v. 133.] See what this man too most plainly says: *Direct my steps according to your utterance, and let no iniquity have dominion over me.* Where what else does he say than: Make me right and free according to your promise? But the more the love of God reigns in anyone, the less iniquity has dominion over him. What else, then, does he ask, except that, by God's gift, he may love God? For by loving God, he loves himself, that he may be able healthfully to love his neighbor too as himself: on which precepts the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets. What, then, does he pray, except that the precepts which God imposes by commanding, he may make to be fulfilled by helping?

**7.** [v. 134.] But what is it that he says: *Redeem me from the slanders of men, and I will keep your commandments?* If those men charge him with true crimes, they do not slander: if false, what is it that he desires to be redeemed from slanders—that is, from false crimes, which cannot harm him? For a false crime, which is slander, does not make a man guilty, except before a human judge: but where God is judge, no one is harmed by a false crime; because it is imputed not to the one against whom it is brought, but rather to the one who brings it. Or is here prefigured the prayer of the Church, and of the whole Christian people, which was redeemed from the slanders of men, by which Christians were everywhere harassed? But does it therefore keep the commandments of God? Did not, amid those very slanders, when they were raging, the holy people much more gloriously keep the commandments of God in its tribulations, when it did not yield to its persecutors toward perpetrating impieties? But surely this is it: *Redeem me from the slanders of men, and I will keep your commandments:* Do you act by infusing your Spirit, lest the slanders of men overcome me by terrors, and lead me away from your commandments to their evil deeds. For if you act this with me—that is, if in this way you redeem me from their slanders, lest I dread the false charges they bring, by granting patience—amid the very slanders I will keep your commandments.

**8.** [v. 135.] *Make your face,* he says, *to shine upon your servant;* that is, manifest your presence, by coming to aid and helping. *And teach me your statutes.* Teach me, surely, that I may do them: which is read more evidently elsewhere, *Teach me to do your will*[^11]. For those who hear, although they hold in memory what they hear, are by no means to be thought to have learned, if they do not do. For it is the word of Truth: *Everyone who has heard from the Father and has learned comes to me*[^12]. He, then, who does not do—that is, does not come—has not learned.

**9.** [v. 136.] But recalling the pain of penitence for his transgression: *Outpourings of waters,* he says, *my eyes have run down, because they did not keep your law;* that is, my eyes themselves. For in some manuscripts this too is read: *Because I did not keep your law. Outpourings of waters,* then, have run down—that is, effusions of tears. And by that manner of speech by which he could say, "My feet went down the mountains," even if he did not say "by the mountains" or "into the mountains": by that manner of speech it is said, "He went down the stairs," even if it is not said "by the stairs"; or, "He went down the pool," even if it is not said "into the pool." And he says well, *they have run down,* namely by the humility of penitence. For they had gone up, when by contumacious pride they had been raised and lifted up. For they seemed to themselves to be on high, when, ignorant of the righteousness of God, they wished to establish their own: in which, wearied and confounded by the transgression of the law, they came down from that lifting up by weeping, that they might rather obtain the righteousness of God by repenting. There are manuscripts which do not have *they have run down,* but *they have passed over,* as though he said, exaggerating, that he had passed over the fountains of waters by weeping; so that we understand this by *outpourings of waters:* that is, that he wept more than waters flow from their outlets. But why, the law not having been kept, is there such weeping, except that grace may be obtained, which blots out the iniquity of the penitent, and helps the will of the believer?
