# Exposition of Psalm 2

**1.** [vv 1.2.] *Why have the nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things? The kings of the earth have stood up, and the princes have come together as one, against the Lord and against his Christ.*[^1] *Why* is said as though it were said, in vain: for they did not accomplish what they wished, that Christ should be destroyed; for this is said of the Lord's persecutors, who are also mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles.

**2.** [v 3.] *Let us break their bonds, and cast their yoke from us.* Although it can also be understood otherwise, yet it is more fittingly taken from the persons of those whom he said meditated vain things; so that this is the sense: *Let us break their bonds, and cast their yoke from us*, let us take care that the Christian religion does not bind us nor be imposed upon us.

**3.** [v 4.] *He who dwells in the heavens will laugh at them, and the Lord will mock them.* The sentence is repeated: for in place of *He who dwells in the heavens*, there is afterward placed *the Lord*; and in place of *will laugh*, there is afterward placed *will mock*. Yet none of this should be understood in a carnal way, as though God laughs with his cheeks or mocks with his nose: rather, that power is to be understood which he gives to his saints, so that, perceiving what is to come, that is, the name of Christ and his dominion that would spread to later generations and gain hold over all the nations, they understand that those men meditated vain things; for this power by which these things were foreknown is God's laughter and mockery. *He who dwells in the heavens will laugh at them*: if we take the heavens to be holy souls, then through them, foreknowing what is to come, God will deride and mock them.

**4.** [v 5.] *Then he will speak to them in his anger, and trouble them in his fury*: for, showing more plainly how he will speak to them, he said, *he will trouble them*; so that *in his anger* is the same as *in his fury*. But the anger and fury of the Lord God should not be understood as a disturbance of mind, but as the power by which he most justly takes vengeance, with the whole creation subject to his service. For above all we must perceive and hold fast what is written in Solomon: *But you, O Lord of power, judge with tranquillity, and govern us with great reverence*[^2]. The anger of God, then, is the movement that arises in the soul that knows the law of God, when it sees that same law transgressed by a sinner; for through this movement of righteous souls many things are avenged. Although the anger of God can also rightly be understood as that very darkening of mind which befalls those who transgress the law of God.

**5.** [v 6.] *But I have been established as king by him over Zion, his holy mountain, proclaiming the precept of the Lord*: these things are manifest from the person of our Lord Jesus Christ himself. Now if Zion signifies contemplation, as some interpret it, we should understand nothing other than the Church, where day by day the attention is raised to contemplate the brightness of God, as the Apostle says: *But we, beholding the glory of the Lord with unveiled face*[^3]. So this is the sense: But I have been established as king by him over his holy Church, which he calls a mountain because of its eminence and firmness. *But I have been established as king by him*: I, namely, whose bonds they meditated breaking and whose yoke they meditated casting off. *Proclaiming his precept*: who does not perceive this, when it is done day by day?

**6.** [v 7.] *The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you.*[^4] Although that day might also seem to be spoken in prophecy as the day on which Jesus Christ was born as man; yet *today*, because it signifies presence, and in eternity nothing is past, as though it had ceased to be, nor future, as though it were not yet, but only present, because whatever is eternal always is; it is understood in a divine sense according to that saying, *Today I have begotten you*, by which the sincerest and catholic faith proclaims the eternal generation of the power and wisdom of God, who is the only-begotten Son.

**7.** [v 8.] *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance.* This is now in a temporal sense, according to the man taken up, who offered himself as a sacrifice for all sacrifices, who also intercedes for us; so that what is said, *Ask of me*, refers to the whole temporal dispensation that was carried out for the human race; namely, that the nations should be joined to the Christian name, and thus be redeemed from death and possessed by God. *I will give you the nations as your inheritance*, which you may possess for their salvation, and which may bear spiritual fruit for you. *And the ends of the earth as your possession.* The same is repeated: *the ends of the earth* is put for what was said, *the nations*; but more plainly, so that we understand all the nations: and *your possession* for what was said, *your inheritance.*

**8.** [vv 9.10.] *You will rule them with a rod of iron*: with inflexible justice. *And like a potter's vessel you will shatter them*; that is, you will shatter in them earthly desires, and the muddy affairs of the old man, and whatever has been contracted and ingrained from the mire of the sinner. *And now, you kings, understand*: *and now*, that is, now renewed, now that the muddy coverings have been shattered, that is, the carnal vessels of error that pertain to the past life: *now understand*, you who are now kings, that is, now able to rule whatever is servile and bestial in you; and now able to fight, not as though beating the air, but chastising your bodies and bringing them into subjection. *Be instructed, all you who judge the earth.* This same thing is repeated: *be instructed* for what was said, *understand*; and *you who judge the earth* for what was said, *kings.* For he signifies spiritual men, who judge the earth: for whatever we judge is beneath us; and whatever is beneath the spiritual man is rightly called earth, because it has been wounded by an earthly stain.

**9.** [v 11.] *Serve the Lord in fear*: lest what was said, *kings who judge the earth*, turn into pride. *And rejoice in him with trembling*: *rejoice* was most fittingly added, lest what was said, *serve the Lord in fear*, should seem to tend toward misery. But again, lest this very thing should proceed into an outpouring of recklessness, there was added, *with trembling*; so that it might tend toward caution and a circumspect guarding of one's sanctification. It can also be taken thus: *And now, you kings, understand*, that is: And now that I have been established as king, do not be sorrowful, kings of the earth, as though your good had been taken from you; but rather understand and be instructed. For it is to your advantage to be under him from whom understanding and instruction are given to you. And this is to your advantage, that you not rule recklessly, but serve the Lord of all with fear, and rejoice in the most certain and sincere blessedness, cautious and circumspect lest you fall from it through pride.

**10.** [v 12.] *Lay hold of discipline, lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you perish from the righteous way*: this is what he says, *understand and be instructed*; for to understand and be instructed is to lay hold of discipline. Yet in saying *lay hold*, it is sufficiently signified that there is a kind of protection and fortification against all things that could harm, unless it were laid hold of with such great care. Now *lest at any time the Lord be angry* is put with hesitation; not according to the vision of the Prophet, to whom it is certain, but according to those very people who are admonished; because those to whom it is not openly revealed are accustomed to think with hesitation about the anger of God. They ought, then, to say this to themselves: Let us lay hold of discipline, lest at any time the Lord be angry, and we perish from the righteous way. Now, as to *let the Lord be angry*, how it is to be understood has been said above. *And you perish from the righteous way*: this is a great punishment, which is dreaded by those who have perceived some sweetness of righteousness; for whoever perishes from the way of righteousness will wander with great misery through the ways of iniquity.

**11.** [v 13.] *When his anger blazes up shortly, blessed are all who trust in him*: that is, when the vengeance comes that is prepared for the ungodly and for sinners, not only will it not touch those who trust in the Lord, but it will even profit toward the building up and exalting of their kingdom. For he did not say: *When his anger blazes up shortly*, secure are *all who trust in him*, as though they had from it only this, that they are not punished; but he said *blessed*, which is the sum and fullness of all good things. As for what is put, *shortly*, I think this signifies that it will be something sudden, while sinners suppose it to be remote and far off in the future.

[^1]: Ps 2:1-2
[^2]: Wis 12:18
[^3]: 2 Cor 3:18
[^4]: Ps 2:7
