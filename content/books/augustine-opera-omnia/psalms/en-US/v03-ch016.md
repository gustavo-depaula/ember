# Exposition of Psalm 65

**1.** [v. 1] This psalm is inscribed in its title: *Unto the end, a canticle of the Psalm of resurrection.*[^1] When you hear *Unto the end*, when the Psalms are pronounced, understand it of Christ; the Apostle saying: *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^2]. How, then, the resurrection is here sung, and whose resurrection, you will hear, as far as he himself deigns to grant and to open. For we know that the resurrection of Christians has already been accomplished in our head, and is to come in the members. The head of the Church is Christ, the members of Christ the Church. What went before in the head, will follow in the body. This is our hope: for this we believe, for this we endure, and persevere amid so great malignity of this age, hope consoling us, before hope becomes reality. For it will be reality when we too have risen again, and, changed into the heavenly habit, have been made equal to the angels. Who would dare to hope this, unless the truth promised it? But this hope, promised to them, the Jews held; and they gloried much of good and as it were just works, that they had received the Law, by living according to which they would both have here carnal goods, and in the resurrection of the dead hoped for such things as those in which they here rejoiced. Therefore to the Sadducees, who denied the future resurrection, the Jews could not answer when they put forth the question which the same Sadducees put to the Lord. For from this we understand that they could not solve this question, because, when the Lord solved it, they marveled. The Sadducees, then, put forth a question about a certain woman, who had seven husbands; not at once, but succeeding one another. For this was of the law for propagating the people, that one who perhaps died without children, his brother, if he had one, should take his wife, to raise up seed to his brother. That woman being set forth, then, who had seven husbands, all dead without children, and, to fulfill this office, marrying the brother's wife, they said, asking: *In the resurrection whose wife of them will she be?* Without doubt the Jews would not have been wearied, would not have failed in this question, if they did not hope for themselves in the resurrection such things as they did in this life. But the Lord, promising equality with the angels, not the human corruption of the flesh, said to them: *You err, not knowing the Scriptures, nor the power of God. For in the resurrection they will neither marry, nor take wives: for neither will they begin to die, but they will be equal to the angels of God*[^3]. He showed that there succession is necessary, where there is grief over departure: there, because there will be no departers, neither will successors be sought. For to this he added: *For they will not begin to die.* But since the Jews held, even if carnally, the hope of the future resurrection, they rejoiced that the Sadducees had been answered, with whom they had a contest about this doubtful and obscure question. The Jews, then, held the hope of the resurrection of the dead; and they hoped that they alone would rise to the blessed life, on account of the work of the Law, and on account of the justifications of the Scriptures, which the Jews alone had, and the Gentiles did not have. Christ was crucified; *blindness in part came upon Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter*[^4]: the Apostle saying this. The resurrection of the dead began to be promised to the Gentiles too, believing in Jesus Christ that he rose again. Hence this psalm is against the presumption and pride of the Jews, for the faith of the Gentiles called to the same hope of resurrection.

**2.** In a manner, my brethren, you have heard the mind of the Psalm. On this which I have said, on this which I have set forth, let all your attention be fixed; let no thought turn you away from it: it is said against the presumption of the Jews, who hoped for resurrection for themselves from the justifications of the Law, and crucified Christ who first rose again, who was going to have as members about to rise not the Jews alone, but all who would believe in him, that is, all the nations. Hence it began: *Shout joyfully to God.* Who? *All the earth.* Not, then, Judea alone. See, brethren, how the universality of the Church spread throughout the whole world is commended; and not only grieve over the Jews who envied the Gentiles this grace, but more lament the heretics. For if those are to be grieved over who have not been gathered, how much more those who, gathered, have been divided? *Shout joyfully to God, all the earth.* What is, *Shout joyfully*? Break out into a voice of joys, if you cannot of words. For one does not shout joyfully with words; but only the sound of those rejoicing is rendered, as it were the gladness of a heart in travail and giving birth, into a voice over a thing conceived, which cannot be unfolded in words. *Shout joyfully to God, all the earth*: let no one shout joyfully in a part. Let no one, I say, shout joyfully in a part: let all the earth shout joyfully, let the Catholic shout joyfully. The Catholic holds the whole: whoever holds a part, and is cut off from the whole, wishes to howl, not to shout joyfully. *Shout joyfully to God, all the earth.*

**3.** [v. 2] *But play to his name.*[^5] What did he say? While you play, let his name be blessed. But what it is to play, I said yesterday, and I believe Your Charity remembers. To play is also to take up the instrument which is called a psaltery, and by the striking and work of the hands to harmonize with the voices. If, then, you shout joyfully so that God hears, play also so that men both see and hear; but not to your name. *For beware of doing your righteousness before men, to be seen by them*[^6]. And for the sake of whose name, you will say, shall I play, so that my works are not seen by men? Attend to another place: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^7]. *Let them see your good deeds, and glorify,* not you, but God. For if you do good works for the sake of being glorified yourselves, the answer is what he himself said of certain such men: *Amen I say to you; they have received their reward.* And again: *Otherwise you will not have a reward with your Father who is in heaven*[^8]. Therefore, you will say, ought I to hide my works, lest I do them *before men*? No. But what does he say? *Let your works shine before men.* I shall remain, then, in doubt: here you tell me: *Beware of doing your righteousness before men;* here you tell me: *Let your good works shine before men;* what shall I observe? what shall I do? what shall I leave undone? A man can no more serve two masters commanding diverse things, than even one commanding diverse things. I do not, says the Lord, command diverse things. Attend to the end, sing unto the end; with what end you do it, see. If you do it that you may be glorified, this I forbade; but if you do it that God may be glorified, this I bade. Play, then, not to your name, but to the name of the Lord your God. Do you play; let him be praised: do you live well; let him be glorified. For whence have you that very living well? If you had it eternally, you would never have lived badly; if you had it from yourself, you would never have not lived well: *But play to his name.*

**4.** *Give glory to his praise.*[^9] He directs our whole intention to the praise of God; he leaves us nothing whereby we may be praised. Let us rather glory in him, and rejoice; let us cleave to him, let us be praised in him. You heard when the Apostle was read: *See your calling, brethren; that not many wise according to the flesh, not many powerful, not many noble; but God chose the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God chose the weak things of the world to confound the strong; and God chose the ignoble things of the world, and the things that are not, as though they were, that the things that are might be brought to nothing*[^10]. What did he wish to say? what did he wish to show? Our Lord God Jesus Christ descended to repair the human race, and to give his grace to all who understand that it is his grace, not their merits; and lest any person glory in the flesh, he chose the weak. For thence Nathanael too was not chosen. For why does it seem to you, that he chose Matthew, a publican sitting at the toll-booth, and did not choose Nathanael, to whom the Lord himself bore witness, saying: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^11]? It is understood, then, that this Nathanael was learned in the Law. Not because he was not going to choose the learned: but if he had chosen those first, they would think themselves chosen by the merit of their learning; so their knowledge would be praised, and the praise of the grace of Christ diminished. He bore witness to him as to a good faithful man, in whom there was no guile; but yet he did not take him up among those disciples whom he first chose, the unlearned. And whence do we understand that he was skilled in the Law? When he heard from one of those who had followed the Lord, saying: *We have found the Messiah, which is interpreted Christ*[^12], he asked whence; and it was said: *From Nazareth*: and he: *Can anything good be from Nazareth*[^13]? Without doubt, he who understood that something good can be from Nazareth was skilled in the Law, and had well examined the Prophets. I know there is in those words another way of pronouncing; but it is not approved by the more prudent: as if that man seemed to despair, when, hearing, he said: *Can anything good be from Nazareth?* that is, surely it cannot? So pronouncing it as though he despaired. But there follows there: *Come, and see*[^14]. These words, that is: *Come, and see,* can follow either pronunciation. If you say it, as though not believing: *Can anything good be from Nazareth?* the answer is: *Come, and see* what you do not believe. Again, if you say it affirming: *Something good can be from Nazareth;* the answer is: *Come, and see* how truly good is what I announce from Nazareth; and how rightly you believe, come, and prove it. Yet from this he is thought to have been learned in the Law, because he was not chosen among the disciples by him who first chose the foolish things of the world; though the Lord bore him so great witness, saying: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^15]. The Lord, then, afterward chose orators too; but they would be proud, if he had not first chosen fishermen: he chose rich men; but they would say they were chosen by the merit of their riches, unless he had first chosen the poor: he chose emperors afterward; but it is better that an emperor, when he comes to Rome, lay aside the diadem and weep at the memorial of a fisherman, than that a fisherman weep at the memorial of an emperor. *For God chose the weak things of the world, to confound the strong; and God chose the ignoble things of the world, and the things that are not, as though they were, that the things that are might be brought to nothing.* And what follows? The Apostle concluded: *That no flesh may glory before God*[^16]. See how he took glory from us, that he might give glory: he took ours, that he might give his; he took the empty, that he might give the full; he took the tottering, that he might give the solid. How much stronger and firmer, then, is our glory, because it is in God! You ought not, then, to glory in yourself; the truth forbade it: but what the Apostle says, this the truth commanded: *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^17]. *Give*, then, *glory to his praise.* Do not imitate the Jews, who wished, as it were by their own merits, to attribute to themselves their justifications, and envied the Gentiles coming to the gospel grace, that all sins might be forgiven them; as though those had not what might be forgiven them; now, as it were good laborers, awaiting a reward. And when they were still sick, they thought themselves healthy, and thence were more dangerously sick. For if they had been even more mildly sick, they would not, like frenzied men, have killed the physician. *Give glory to his praise.*

**5.** [v. 3] *Say to God: How fearful are your works!*[^18] Why fearful, and not lovable? Hear another voice of the psalm: *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling*[^19]. What does this mean? Hear the voice of the Apostle: *With fear*, he says, *and trembling work out your own salvation.* Why, *with fear and trembling*? He added the cause: *For it is God who works in you, both to will and to work, for his good will*[^20]. If, then, God works in you, by the grace of God you work well, not by your own powers. Therefore if you rejoice, fear too; lest perhaps what was given to the humble be taken from the proud. For, that you may know that this was done from the very pride of the Jews, justifying themselves as it were by the works of the Law, and therefore falling, another psalm says: *These in chariots, and these in horses,* as it were in their own certain steps and instruments for their own raising up; *but we*, he says, *will be magnified in the name of the Lord our God*[^21]: *These in chariots, and these in horses; but we will be magnified in the name of the Lord our God.* See how those were lifted up in themselves; see how these gloried in God. Therefore what followed? *Their feet were bound, and they fell; but we have risen, and are set upright*[^22]. Hear our Lord himself saying this same thing: *I*, he says, *came; that those who do not see may see; and those who see may become blind*[^23]. See in one part goodness; in the other as it were malice. But what is better than he? what more merciful? what more just? Why, then, *that those who do not see may see*? On account of goodness. Why, *and that those who see may become blind*? On account of being lifted up. And did they truly see, and were made blind? They did not see, but seemed to themselves to see. For behold, see, brethren: when the Jews themselves said: *Are we then blind? the Lord said to them: If you were blind, you would have no sin: but now because you say: We see, your sin remains in you*[^24]. You came to the physician; you say you see? The eye-salves will cease; you will always remain blind: confess yourself blind, that you may deserve to be enlightened. Attend to the Jews; attend to the Gentiles. *Those who do not see may see,* he says: therefore *I came, that those who see may become blind.* The Jews saw our Lord Jesus Christ himself in the flesh; the Gentiles did not see him: behold, those who saw, crucified him; those who did not see, believed. What, then, did you do, O Christ, against the proud? what did you do? We see, because you deigned, and we are your members; we see: you hid God; you set forth a man. Why this? *That blindness in part might come upon Israel, and the fullness of the Gentiles might enter*[^25]. For this you hid God, and set a man before their gaze. They saw, and did not see: they saw what you had taken on, and did not see what you were: they saw the form of a servant, they did not see the form of God; the form of a servant, in which the Father is greater; not the form of God, on account of which you have just now heard: *I and the Father are one*[^26]. They held what they saw, they crucified what they saw; they insulted him whom they saw, they did not recognize him who lay hidden. Hear the Apostle saying: *For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^27]. Therefore you Gentiles who have been called, attend to the branches cut off on account of severity; but yourselves grafted in on account of goodness, and made partakers of the fatness of the olive, not relishing high things; that is, not being proud. *For you do not bear the root,* he says; but the root you. Rather fear, because you see the natural branches cut off. For the Jews came through the Patriarchs; they were born of the flesh of Abraham. And what does the Apostle say? *But you say: The branches were broken, that I might be grafted in. Well; on account of unbelief they were broken. But you*, he says, *stand by faith; do not relish high things, but fear: for if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you*[^28]. Attend, then, to the branches broken, and to yourself grafted in: do not be lifted up over the broken branches; but rather say to God: *How fearful are your works!* Brethren, if we ought not to be lifted up against the Jews, long ago cut off from the root of the Patriarchs, but rather to fear, and to say to God: *How fearful are your works!* how much less ought we to be lifted up against the recent wounds of those cut off? The Jews were cut off before, the Gentiles grafted in; from that very grafting the heretics were cut off; but neither against them ought we to be lifted up, lest perhaps he deserve to be cut off, whom it delights to insult the cut off. My brethren, whatever the voice of the bishop has sounded to you, we ask you to beware: whoever you are in the Church, do not insult those who are not within; but rather pray that they too be within. *For God is able to graft them in again*[^29]. Of the Jews themselves the Apostle said this; and it came about in them. The Lord rose again, and many believed: they did not understand when they crucified; yet afterward they believed in him, and so great an offense was forgiven them. The poured-out blood of the Lord was forgiven to murderers, that I may not say killers of God; because *if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory.* Now to murderers the poured-out blood of the innocent one was forgiven; and the very blood which through madness they shed, through grace they drank. *Say*, then, *to God: How fearful are your works!* Whence fearful? *Because blindness in part came upon Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter*[^30]. O fullness of the Gentiles, say to God: *How fearful are your works!* and so rejoice as to tremble; do not be lifted up over the cut-off branches. *Say to God: How fearful are your works!*

**6.** *In the multitude of your power your enemies will lie to you.*[^31] To this end, he says, your enemies will lie to you, that your power may be great. What is this? Hear more attentively. The power of our Lord Jesus Christ appeared chiefly in the resurrection, whence this psalm received its title. And rising again he appeared to his disciples. He did not appear to his enemies, but to his disciples. Crucified, he appeared to all; rising again, to the faithful: so that even afterward whoever wished might believe, and to the one believing the resurrection might be promised. Many saints did many miracles; none of them, dead, rose again: because even those who were raised up by them, were raised up to die. Let Your Charity attend. The Lord, commending his works, said: *Believe the works, if you will not believe me*[^32]. And even the past works of the Prophets are commended; and if not the same, yet many the same, many of the same power. The Lord walked upon the sea, and bade Peter too. Was he not there himself, the Lord, when the sea divided itself, that Moses with the people of Israel might cross? The same Lord did those things. He who did these things through his own flesh, he himself did those things through the flesh of his servants. Yet this he did not do through his servants (for he himself did all things), that any of them should be dead, and rise into eternal life. Because, then, the Jews could say, when the Lord did miracles: Moses did these things too, Elijah did them, Elisha did them: they could say these things to themselves, because those men too raised the dead, and did many miracles: therefore, when a sign was sought from him; commending his own sign which was to be in him alone, he said: *This wicked and embittering generation seeks a sign; and a sign will not be given it, except the sign of the prophet Jonah. For as Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights; so will the Son of man be in the heart of the earth three days and three nights*[^33]. How was Jonah in the belly of the whale? Was it not that he might afterward be vomited out alive? Hell was to the Lord what the whale was to Jonah. This sign, proper to himself, he commended; this is the most powerful. It is more powerful to have lived again from the dead, than not to have been dead. The greatness of the Lord's power, according as he was made man, appears in the strength of the resurrection. This the Apostle commends, when he says: *Not having my own righteousness which is from the Law; but that which is through the faith of Christ, which is the righteousness from God in faith, to know him, and the power of his resurrection*[^34]. So it is commended in another place too: *And although he was crucified of weakness, yet he lives of the power of God*[^35]. Since, then, this great power of the Lord is understood in the resurrection, whence this psalm received its title; what does it mean: *In the multitude of your power your enemies will lie to you,* except that you should understand thus: Therefore your enemies will lie to you that you may be crucified; therefore you will be crucified that you may rise again? Their lie, then, will avail to commend your great power. Why are enemies wont to lie? That they may diminish the power of him about whom they lie. To you, he says, it befalls the contrary. For your power would appear less, if they did not lie to you.

**7.** Attend to the very lie of the false witnesses in the Gospel, and see that it is about the resurrection. For when it had been said to the Lord: *What sign do you show us, that you do these things?* besides what he had said of Jonah, by another likeness he said this same thing, that you may know that this proper sign was chiefly commended: *Destroy*, he says, *this temple; and in three days I will raise it up. And they said: Forty-six years was this temple built; and you in three days will raise it up?* And the evangelist, expounding what that was: *But this*, he says, *Jesus said of the temple of his body*[^36]. Behold, this power of his he said he would demonstrate to men, whence he had given the likeness of the temple, on account of his flesh which was the temple of the divinity hidden within. Whence the Jews saw the temple outside; the deity dwelling within they did not see. From these words of the Lord the false witnesses contrived a lie, which they spoke against him, from those very ones where he commended his future resurrection, speaking of the temple. For the false witnesses said against him, when it was asked of them what they had heard him say: *We heard him saying: I will destroy this temple, and after three days I will raise it up*[^37]. *After three days I will raise it up,* they had heard: *I will destroy,* they had not heard; but they had heard: *Destroy.* They changed one word, and a few letters, that they might contrive false testimony. But for whom do you change a word, O human vanity, O human weakness? For an unchangeable Word you change a word! You change your word; surely you do not change the Word of God? Whence elsewhere it is said: *And iniquity lied to itself*[^38]. Why, then, did your enemies lie to you, O Lord, to whom all the earth shouts joyfully? *In the multitude of your power your enemies will lie to you.* They will say: *I will destroy;* when you said: *Destroy.* Why did they say you said: *I will destroy;* and not say what you said: *Destroy*? That they might, as it were, defend themselves from the charge of the destroyed temple without cause. For Christ, because he willed it, died: and yet you killed him. Behold, we grant you, O liars; he himself destroyed the temple. For it was said by the Apostle: *Who loved me and handed over himself for me*[^39]. It was said of the Father: *Who did not spare his own Son; but handed him over for us all*[^40]. If, then, the Father handed over the Son, and the Son handed over himself; what did Judas do? The Father, by handing over the Son to death for us, did well: Christ, by handing over himself for us, did well: by handing over his master out of his own avarice, Judas did ill. For what was furnished to us from the suffering of Christ will not be reckoned to the malice of Judas: he will have the reward of malice; Christ the praise of grace. Plainly let him himself have destroyed the temple; let him have destroyed it who said: *I have power to lay down my soul; and I have power to take it up again: no one takes it from me; but I myself lay it down of myself, and take it up again*[^41]. Let him himself have destroyed the temple by his grace, by your malice. *In the multitude of your power your enemies will lie to you.* Behold, they lie, behold, they are believed, behold, you are oppressed, behold, you are crucified, behold, you are insulted, behold, the head is wagged: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^42]. Behold, when you will, you lay down your soul, and your side is struck with a lance, and the Sacraments flow from your side; you are taken down from the wood, you are wrapped in linen cloths, you are placed in the tomb, guards are added lest your disciples take you away; the hour of your resurrection comes, the earth is shaken, the tombs are torn open, you rise hidden, you appear manifest. Where, then, are those liars? where is the false testimony of malevolence? Did not your enemies lie to you in the multitude of your power?

**8.** Bring forward those guards of the tomb too; let them report what they saw: let them receive money, and let them too lie; let them too say, perverse, admonished by the perverse, let them say, corrupted by the Jews, who in Christ were unwilling to be whole; let them say, let them too lie. What will they say? Say it; let us see: you too will lie in the multitude of the power of the Lord. What will you say? *While we were sleeping, his disciples came, and took him away from the tomb*[^43]. O truly sleeping folly! Either you were awake; and you ought to have prevented it: or you were sleeping; and you did not know what was done. They too were added to the lie of the enemies: the number of liars was increased, that the reward of believers might be increased; because *in the multitude of your power your enemies will lie to you.* Therefore they lied; in the multitude of your power they lied: you appeared against the liars to the truthful, and you appeared to those truthful ones whom you yourself made truthful.

**9.** [v. 4] Let the Jews remain in their lies: but to you, because in the multitude of your power they lied, let what follows come to pass: *Let all the earth adore you, and play to you; let it play to your name, O Most High.*[^44] A little before, most humble; now *Most High*: most humble among the hands of lying enemies; *Most High* above the summit of praising angels. *Let all the earth adore you, and play to you; let it play to your name, O Most High.*

**10.** [v. 5] *Come, and see the works of the Lord.*[^45] O Gentiles, O last nations, leave the lying Jews, come confessing. *Come, and see the works of the Lord: terrible in counsels over the sons of men.* He too indeed was called Son of man, and truly was made the son of man: the true Son of God in the form of God; the true son of man in the form of a servant: but do not weigh this form of a servant by the condition of others like him; he is *terrible in counsels over the sons of men.* The sons of men contrived a counsel to crucify Christ; crucified, he blinded those crucifying. What, then, did you do, sons of men, by contriving sharp counsels against your Lord, in whom majesty lay hidden, and weakness appeared? You contrived counsels of destroying, he of blinding and saving; of blinding the proud, of saving the humble: but to this end of blinding the proud themselves, that, blinded, they might be humbled, humbled they might confess, having confessed they might be enlightened. *Terrible in counsels over the sons of men.* Truly terrible! Behold, blindness in part came upon Israel: behold, the Jews from whom Christ was born are outside: behold, the Gentiles who were against Judea are within in Christ. *Terrible in counsels over the sons of men.*

**11.** [v. 6] Therefore what did he do by the terror of his counsel? He turned the sea into dry land. For this follows: *Who turns the sea into dry land.*[^46] The world was a sea; bitter with saltiness, turbulent with storm, raging with the waves of persecutions, it was a sea: certainly the sea was turned into dry land; now the world thirsts for sweet water, which was full of salt. Who did this? *Who turns the sea into dry land.* Now the soul of all the Gentiles, what does it say? *My soul is like a land without water to you*[^47]. *Who turns the sea into dry land. In the river they will cross over on foot.* Those very ones who were turned into dry land, when they were before a sea, *will cross over the river on foot.* What is the river? The river is all the mortality of the age. See the river: some things come and pass, others about to pass succeed. Is it not so in the water of a river, which is born of the earth, and flows? Everyone who is born must yield to one about to be born: and all this order of falling things is a certain river. Into this river let not the greedy soul cast itself, let it not cast itself; let it stand. And how will it cross over the delights of perishing things? Let it believe in Christ, and it will cross over on foot: it crosses over with him as guide, it crosses over on foot. What is it, to cross over on foot? To cross over easily. It does not seek a horse to cross over; pride is not lifted up to cross the river: the humble one crosses over, and crosses over more safely. *In the river they will cross over on foot.*

**12.** *There we will rejoice in him.*[^48] O Jews, you glory of your works: lay aside the pride of glorying of yourselves; take the grace of rejoicing in Christ. For there we will rejoice: but not in ourselves: *there we will rejoice in him.* When will we rejoice? When we have crossed over the river on foot. Eternal life is promised, resurrection is promised; there our flesh will no longer be a river: for it is now a river, when it is mortality. See whether any age stands still. Boys wish to grow; and do not know that the span of their life is diminished by the succeeding years. For years are not added, but subtracted from those growing: as the water of a river certainly approaches, but recedes from the source. And boys wish to grow, that they may be free from the dominion of their elders; behold, they grow, it is done quickly, they reach youth: those who have passed boyhood, let them hold youth if they can; it too passes. Old age succeeds; or let old age be eternal: it is taken away by death. Therefore it is a river of flesh being born. This river of mortality, lest the concupiscence of mortal things overturn it and snatch it away, he easily crosses over who humbly, that is, on foot, crosses over, with him as guide who first crossed over, who drank of the torrent on the way even unto death, therefore he lifted up his head. Crossing over, then, the river on foot, that is, easily crossing over this flowing mortality, *there we will rejoice in him.* But now in what, except either in him, or in the hope of him? For even if we rejoice now, in hope we rejoice; but then we will rejoice in him. And now in him, but through hope; but then face to face.

**13.** [v. 7] *There we will rejoice in him.* In whom? *Who has dominion in his power forever.*[^49] For what power have we? and surely not eternal? If our power were eternal, we would not have slipped, we would not have fallen into sin, we would not have deserved penal mortality. He took on by his will that into which our merit cast us down: *Who has dominion in his power forever.* Let us become partakers of him, in whose power we shall be strong; but he in his own. We enlightened; he the enlightening light: we, turned away from him, are darkened; he cannot be turned away from himself. By his heat we are kindled; from which withdrawing we had grown cold, drawing near again we are kindled. Let us, then, say to him to keep us in his power, because in him we will rejoice *who has dominion in his power forever.*

**14.** But he does not furnish this to the believing Jews alone: because the Jews greatly lifted themselves up, presuming on their own power, afterward they came to know in whose power they were savingly strong, and some of them believed: but it is not enough for Christ; it is much that he gave, he gave a great price, what he gave ought not to avail for the Jews alone. *His eyes look upon the Gentiles.*[^50] Therefore, *His eyes look upon the Gentiles.* And what do we do? The Jews will murmur; the Jews will say: What is ours, this is theirs too; to us the Gospel, to them the Gospel; to us the grace of resurrection, and to them the grace of resurrection: it profits us nothing that we received the Law, and lived in the justifications of the Law, and held the precepts of the fathers: this will avail nothing; what is ours is theirs! Let them not quarrel, let them not contend. *Let those who embitter not be exalted in themselves.* O wretched and decaying flesh, are you not a sinner? What does your tongue cry out? Let the conscience be attended to. For all have sinned, and lack the glory of God. Recognize yourself, human weakness. You received the Law, that you might be a transgressor of the Law too; for you did not hold and fulfill what you received. There came to you from the Law, not the justification which the Law commands, but the transgression which you made. If, then, sin abounded, why do you envy the superabounding grace? Do not embitter, because *those who embitter, let them not be exalted.* He seems to have cursed, as it were: *Those who embitter, let them not be exalted*: rather let them be exalted, but not *in themselves.* Let them be humbled in themselves; let them be exalted in Christ. For he who humbles himself will be exalted; and he who exalts himself will be humbled. *Those who embitter, let them not be exalted in themselves.*

**15.** [vv. 8-9] *Bless our God, you Gentiles.*[^51] Behold, those who embitter have been rejected; the reason has been rendered to them: some were converted, some remained proud. Let not those terrify you who envy the Gentiles the gospel grace; now the seed of Abraham has come, in whom all the Gentiles are blessed. Bless him in whom you are blessed; *Bless our God, you Gentiles: and make heard the voice of his praise.* Do not praise yourselves; but praise him. What is the voice of his praise? That we are his grace, whatever good we are. *Who set my soul unto life.* Behold the voice of his praise: *Who set my soul unto life.* Therefore it was in death; it was in death in you. Hence it is that you ought not to have been exalted in yourselves. Behold, it was in death in you: where will it be in life, except in him who said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^52]? As to certain believers the Apostle says: *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^53]. Darkness, then, in you; light in the Lord: so death in you; life in the Lord. *Who set my soul unto life.* Behold, he set our soul unto life, because we believe in him; he set our soul unto life: but what need is there next, except that we persevere unto the end? And this who will give, except he of whom it was consequently said: *And did not give my feet unto being moved*[^54]? He himself set the soul unto life, he himself rules the feet, lest they totter, lest they be moved, and be given unto being moved; he himself makes us live, he himself to persevere unto the end, that we may live forever. *And did not give my feet unto being moved.*

**16.** [vv. 10-12] Why did you say this: *And did not give my feet unto being moved*? For what did you suffer, or what could you suffer, that your feet should be moved? What? hear what follows. Why did I say, *he did not give my feet unto being moved*? Because we suffered many things, by which our feet would be moved from the way, unless he himself ruled them, and did not give them unto being moved. What are these things? *Because you proved us, O God; you set us afire, as silver is set afire.*[^55] You did not set us afire as hay, but as silver: by applying fire to us, you did not turn us into ashes, but washed away the dross. *You set us afire, as silver is set afire.* And see how God rages against those whose soul he set unto life. *You led us into the trap*: not that, caught, we might die, but that, freed, we might learn by experience. *You set tribulations on our back.* For, badly upright, we were proud: badly upright, we were bent, that, bent, we might be well set upright. *You set tribulations on our back: you set men over our heads.*[^56] All these things the Church suffered in various and diverse persecutions; it suffered this individually, it suffers it even now. For there is no one who can say himself free in this life from these temptations. Therefore men too are set over our heads: we bear with those we do not wish, we suffer sometimes as superiors those whom we know to be worse. But if sins be absent, a man is rightly superior; but the more they are present, the more inferior he is. And it is good that we consider ourselves to be sinners, and so bear with those set over our heads; that we too may confess to God that we suffer worthily. For what do you suffer unworthily that he does who is just? *You set tribulations on our back: you set men over our heads.* God seems to rage, when he does these things: do not fear; because he is a Father, he never so rages as to destroy. When you live badly, if he spares, he is more angry. Altogether these tribulations are the scourges of one correcting, lest there be the sentence of one punishing. *You set tribulations on our back: you set men over our heads.*

**17.** *We passed through fire and water.*[^57] Fire and water, both are dangerous in this life. Certainly water seems to extinguish fire, and fire seems to dry up water. So are the very temptations too, with which this life abounds. Fire burns, water corrupts: both are to be feared; both the burning of tribulation, and the water of corruption. When affairs are straitened, and there is something that is called unhappiness in this world, it is as it were fire: when affairs are prosperous, and the abundance of the age flows around, it is as it were water. See that the fire does not burn you up, that the water does not corrupt you. Be firm against the fire; you must be baked: like an earthen vessel you are put into the furnace of fire, that what has been formed may be made firm. The vessel, then, now made firm by fire, does not fear water; but a vessel, if it has not been made solid by fire, like clay will be dissolved by water. Do not hasten to the water; pass through the fire to the water, that you may pass through the water too. Therefore in the Sacraments too, both in catechizing, and in exorcizing, fire is first applied. For whence do the unclean spirits very often cry out: I burn, if that is not fire? But after the fire of exorcism, one comes to Baptism; so that from fire to water, from water to refreshment. But what is in the Sacraments, this is in the temptations of this age: the strait of fear comes first, in place of fire; afterward, fear being removed, it is to be feared lest worldly happiness corrupt. But when the fire has not made you crack, and you have not been sunk in the water, but have swum out; through discipline you pass to rest, and passing through fire and water you are led out into refreshment. For of which things there are signs in the Sacrament, those are the realities in that perfection of eternal life. When we have already passed over to that refreshment, dearest brethren, we shall fear no enemy there, no tempter, no envier, no fire, no water; there will be perpetual refreshment there. It is called refreshment on account of rest. For if you say: It is heat, it is true: if you say: Refreshment, it is true. For if you take refreshment badly, it is as though we grow numb there. But we do not grow numb there, but rest: nor, because it is called heat, will we swelter there, but we shall be fervent in spirit. Attend to that very heat in another psalm: *Nor is there one who can hide himself from his heat*[^58]. What does the Apostle too say? *Fervent in spirit*[^59]. Therefore: *We passed through fire and water: And you led us out into refreshment.*

**18.** [v. 13] Attend that he was not silent, not only about refreshment, but also about that very desirable fire: *I will enter into your house in holocausts.*[^60] What is a holocaust? Wholly burnt, but by the divine fire. For a sacrifice is called a holocaust when the whole is kindled. Some are parts of sacrifices; the holocaust is another thing: when the whole burns, and the whole is consumed by the divine fire, it is called a holocaust; when a part, a sacrifice. Every holocaust indeed is a sacrifice; but not every sacrifice is a holocaust. Holocausts, then, he promises; the body of Christ speaks, the unity of Christ speaks: *I will enter into your house in holocausts.* Let your fire consume the whole of me; let nothing of mine remain to me, let the whole be yours. But this will be in the resurrection of the just, *when both this corruptible puts on incorruption, and this mortal puts on immortality; then will come to pass what is written: Death has been swallowed up in victory*[^61]. Victory is as it were a divine fire: when it swallows up even our death, it is a holocaust. There remains nothing mortal in the flesh, there remains nothing blameworthy in the spirit; the whole will be consumed from the mortal life, that it may be consummated in eternal life: those, then, will be holocausts.

**19.** [v. 14] And what will it be, *in holocausts*? *I will pay you my vows, which my lips have distinguished.*[^62] What is the distinction in the vows? This is the distinction, that you accuse yourself, praise him; that you understand yourself the creature, him the creator; yourself darkness, him the enlightener, to whom you may say: *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, you will enlighten my darkness*[^63]. For when you say, O soul, that of yourself it shines for you, you will not distinguish. If you do not distinguish, you will not pay distinguished vows. Pay distinguished vows; confess yourself changeable, him unchangeable: confess yourself without him to be nothing, but him without you to be perfect; yourself to need him, but him not to need you. Cry to him: *I said to the Lord, You are my God, because you have no need of my goods*[^64]. Now in that God takes you up into a holocaust, he does not grow, he is not increased, he is not made richer, he is not made more furnished: it is better for you, whatever he does of you for you; not for him who does it. If you distinguish these things, you pay your vows to your God, which your lips have distinguished. *I will pay you my vows, which my lips have distinguished.*

**20.** [v. 15] *And my mouth has spoken in my tribulation.*[^65] How sweet often is tribulation! how necessary! What did his mouth speak there in his tribulation? *Marrow-rich holocausts I will offer you.*[^66] What is, *marrow-rich*? Let me hold your love within; it will not be on the surface, in my marrow will be that I love you. For nothing is more inward than our marrow: the bones are more inward than the flesh, the marrow more inward than the bones themselves. Whoever, then, worships God on the surface, wishes rather to please men; but feeling something else within, he does not offer the holocausts of the marrow: but of him whose marrow he inspects, he takes the whole. *Marrow-rich holocausts I will offer you, with incense and rams.* The rams the leaders of the Church: the whole body of Christ speaks; this is what it offers to God. What is incense? Prayer. *With incense and rams*? For especially the rams pray for the flocks. *I will offer you oxen with goats.* We find oxen treading out the grain, and they too are offered to God. The Apostle said it was to be understood of the announcers of the Gospel that which is written: *You shall not muzzle the ox treading out the grain. Surely it is not about oxen that God is concerned*[^67]? Therefore those great rams, those great oxen. What of the rest, who perhaps are conscious of certain sins; who perhaps slipped on the very road, and, wounded, are healed by repentance? Will they too remain, and not pertain to the holocausts? Lest they fear, he added goats too: *Marrow-rich holocausts*, he says, *I will offer you, with incense and rams: I will offer you oxen with goats.* By that very joining the goats are saved: of themselves they cannot; joined to the oxen they are accepted. For they made friends of the mammon of iniquity, that those friends might receive them into eternal dwellings. Therefore these goats will not be at the left, because they made themselves friends of the mammon of iniquity. But which goats at the left? Those to whom it will be said: *I was hungry, and you did not give me to eat*[^68]: not those who redeemed their sins by alms.

**21.** [vv. 16-17] *Come, hear, and I will tell, all you who fear God.*[^69] Let us come, let us hear what he is going to tell. *Come, hear, and I will tell.* But who: *Come and hear*? *All you who fear God.* If you do not fear God, I will not tell. There are none to whom it may be told, where the fear of God is not. Let the fear of God open the ears, that there may be that which enters, and a way by which there enters what I am going to tell. But what is he going to tell? *What great things he has done for my soul.* Behold, he wishes to tell; but what is he going to tell? Perhaps how far the earth extends, how far the heaven is stretched out, and how many the stars are, and what are the courses of sun and moon? This creation carries out its order: those who too curiously sought it out, were ignorant of its creator. Hear this, take this, O *you who fear God, what great things he has done for my soul*: if you wish, for yours too. *What great things he has done for my soul. To him with my mouth I cried out.* And this very thing he says was done for his soul: that with his mouth he cried out to him, he says was done for his soul. Behold, brethren, we were Gentiles, even if not in ourselves, in our parents. And what does the Apostle say? *You know, when you were Gentiles, how you went up, led, to speechless images*[^70]. Let the Church now say: *What great things he has done for my soul. To him with my mouth I cried out.* As a man I cried out to a stone, I cried out to a deaf piece of wood, I spoke to deaf, dumb images: now the image of God has been turned to its Creator. I who said *to a piece of wood, You are my father; and to a stone: You begot me*[^71]; now say: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^72]. *To him with my mouth I cried out. With my mouth* now, not with another's mouth. When I cried out to stones in the vain conduct of paternal tradition, I cried out with another's mouth: when I cried out to the Lord, what he himself gave, what he himself inspired, *to him with my mouth I cried out, and I exalted under my tongue.* What is, *I cried out with my mouth, and I exalted under my tongue.* Him I publicly preached, him in secret I confessed. It is too little to exalt God with the tongue; but also under the tongue, that what you certainly speak, this you silently think. *To him with my mouth I cried out, and I exalted under my tongue.* See how in secret he wishes to be whole who offers marrow-rich holocausts. Do this, brethren, imitate this, that you may say: *Come, hear what great things he has done for my soul.* For all the things that he recounts, his grace works in our soul. See the other things that he says.

**22.** [v. 18] *If I have looked upon iniquity in my heart, let the Lord not hear me.*[^73] Consider now, brethren, how easily, how daily men, blushing before men, accuse iniquities: He did wrong, he did basely, he is a wicked man: this perhaps he says on account of men. See whether you do not look upon iniquity in your heart; lest perhaps what you reprehend in another, you think to do, and therefore cry out against him, not because he did it, but because he was found out. Return to yourself; within be a judge to yourself. Behold, in your hidden chamber, in the very inmost vein of the heart, where you alone are, and he who sees, there let iniquity displease you, that you may please God. Do not look upon it, that is, do not love it; but rather despise it, that is, hold it in contempt, and turn away from it. Whatever glad thing it has promised, to entice to sinning; whatever sad thing it has threatened, to drive to doing evil, the whole is nothing, the whole passes; it deserves to be despised, that it may be trodden underfoot; not to be looked upon, that it may be accepted. [For it suggests sometimes through thoughts, or through the words of evil speakers conversing. For evil conversations corrupt good morals: do not look upon them. But it is too little in the face, too little on the tongue: in the heart do not look upon it, that is, do not love it, do not accept it. For to set "looking upon" for "loving" is an everyday thing: first, because of God we say: He looked upon me. What is, He looked upon me? Did he not see you before? Or was he attending above, and was admonished by your prayers, to cast his eyes upon you? He saw you before too; but, He looked upon me, you say: He loved me. And to a man who sees you, and whom you ask to have mercy on you, you say: Look upon me. He sees you; and you say: Look upon me. What is: Look upon me? Love, attend, have mercy on me. Therefore he did not say: *If I have looked upon iniquity in my heart,* because no iniquity at all is suggested to the human heart. There it is suggested, the suggestion does not cease; but let there be no looking upon. For if you look upon iniquity, you look back, and you incur the sentence of the Lord saying in the Gospel: *No one putting his hand to the plow and looking back is fit for the kingdom of God*[^74]. What, then, ought I to do? What the Apostle says: *Forgetting the things behind, stretching forth to the things that are before*[^75]. For behind us all our things that have passed are unrighteous. No one comes to Christ from good: all have sinned: by believing they are justified. Perfect righteousness will not be, except in that life; yet for advancement, good morals too are inspired by him, are given by him. Do not, then, count your merits, do not. And if iniquity suggests, do not consent. For what does he say?] *If I have looked upon iniquity in my heart, let the Lord not hear me.*

**23.** [v. 19] *Therefore God has heard me.*[^76] Because I did not look upon iniquity in my heart. *And he attended to the voice of my prayer.*

**24.** [v. 20] *Blessed be my God, who has not removed my entreaty, and his mercy from me.*[^77] He holds the sense from that place, where he says: *Come, hear, and I will tell you, all you who fear God, what great things he has done for my soul*; and he said the things you heard, and at the end he so concluded: *Blessed be my God, who has not removed my entreaty, and his mercy from me.* For so this one who speaks has come to the resurrection, where now we too are in hope: nay, we too are; and this is our voice. As long, then, as we are here, let us ask this of God, that he not remove from us our entreaty, and his mercy; that is, that we pray perseveringly, and he perseveringly have mercy. For many grow faint in praying, and in the newness of their conversion pray fervently, afterward faintly, afterward coldly, afterward negligently: as though they become secure. The enemy is watchful: you sleep. The Lord himself commanded in the Gospel; *that one ought always to pray, and not to fail*[^78]. And he gives a likeness of that unjust judge, who neither feared God, nor reverenced man; whom that widow importuned daily that he might hear her: and he yielded out of weariness who was not bent by mercy; and the wicked judge said to himself: Though I neither fear God, nor reverence men; yet on account of the weariness which this widow daily causes me, I will hear her case, and avenge her. And the Lord says: If the wicked judge did this, will not your Father avenge his elect who cry to him day and night? So I say to you; he will execute their judgment quickly. Therefore let us not fail in prayer. What he is going to grant, even if he defers, he does not take away: secure of his promise, let us not fail in praying; and this is from his benefit. Therefore he said: *Blessed be my God, who has not removed my entreaty, and his mercy from me.* When you see your entreaty not removed from you, be secure, because his mercy is not removed from you.
