# Exposition of Psalm 103

**1.** The day before yesterday, as far as you deign to remember, you were abundantly refreshed: but because, even after a long sermon, you let us go most eagerly, we did not think your Holiness should be defrauded of today's due; so that this may be from the debt, that from the gain. The psalm that was read is woven almost entirely of figures of things and mysteries, and needs not only our attention, but yours too, no small attention; although all the things that were said can be taken to the letter religiously as well. For there are enumerated, even if not all, yet many works of God, which are known to all who behold them, who know how, from the things that have been made and are seen, to perceive his invisible things by the understanding. For we see a certain vast fabric of the world, made of heaven and earth, and of all that is in them; and from the greatness and beauty of this fabric, we already love, even if we do not yet see, the inestimable greatness and beauty of the fabricator himself. For he who cannot yet be beheld by the purity of our heart has not ceased to set his works before our eyes, so that, seeing what we can, we may love the one we cannot see, that by the merit of that very love we may at some time be able to see him. Yet in all the things that were said, a spiritual understanding too is to be sought, in scrutinizing which your desires will help us in the name of Christ; with which, as with invisible hands, you knock at the invisible door, that it may be opened to you invisibly, and you may enter invisibly, and be healed invisibly.

**2.** [v. 1.] Let us all, then, say: *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* Let us all address our soul: because the soul of us all, through one faith, is one soul; and all of us, whoever believe in Christ, because of the unity of his body, are one man. Let our soul bless the Lord, for his so great benefits, for his so many and great gifts of grace: which gifts we find when we are intent upon this psalm, and the cloud of carnal thinking shaken off, with our mind raised up as much as we can, and our gaze directed as much as we can, and with as pure an eye of our heart as we can, as far as the present life does not impede, as far as the desires of present things do not occupy, as far as the cupidity of the world does not blind. Raised up, then, we are about to hear his great, glad, and beautiful gifts, desirable and full of gladness and joys; which he who had conceived this psalm already saw in his mind, and, in the exultation of that very vision, belched out, saying, *Bless the Lord, O my soul.*

**3.** *O Lord my God, you have been magnified exceedingly.* See the magnificent things he is about to say; in which magnificent things surely no one is to be praised but he, the author of all magnificent things. *You have put on confession and comeliness.* O Lord my God, who *have been magnified exceedingly*, whence *have you been magnified exceedingly?* Are you not always great? are you not always magnificent? Are you not perfect, so that you should grow? do you fail, so that at some time you should be diminished? But because you are what you are, and truly are, surely you told your name to your servant Moses: *I am who I am*[^1]: surely you are great, and your greatness is eternal, and neither begins nor ends; neither does it begin from the beginning of time, nor run out to the end of time, nor suffer anything in the middle: for it is unchangeable greatness. How, then, *have you been magnified exceedingly?* Another psalm admonishes us; for it says, *Your knowledge has been made wonderful from me*[^2]. If it is rightly said, *Your knowledge has been made wonderful from me;* it is rightly said, *You have been magnified exceedingly, O Lord my God*, from me. But this too must still be inquired into. Is my God magnified from me? Then he is made great from me. The daily prayer of our salvation too teaches us something. *Hallowed be your name*[^3]: daily we ask, daily we beg that it may be done. If anyone asks us: What is it that you ask, that the name of God may be hallowed? is it not sometimes holy, that it may now be hallowed? And yet, unless we wished it to be done, we would not ask that it be done. For thanksgiving is one thing, prayer another: we give thanks for what is; we pray that there may be what is not yet. What, then, is it, *Hallowed be your name?* And we understand what was here said: *O Lord my God, you have been magnified exceedingly.* This is, *Hallowed be your name*, let your name be holy among men. For your name is always holy, but to certain unclean ones your name is not yet holy. For the Apostle says, *All things are clean to the clean, but to the unclean and unbelieving nothing is clean*[^4]. If to the unclean and unbelieving nothing is clean, I ask the cause: *But their mind and conscience*, he says, *are polluted.* If nothing is clean to them, not even God; unless perhaps you suppose that God seems clean to those who daily blaspheme him. If he is clean, let him please; if he pleases, let him be praised: but if he is blasphemed, he displeases; and if he displeases, how can the one who displeases be clean to you? What, then, do we ask: *Hallowed be your name?* That to those men who through unbelief do not yet have it, the name of God may be holy, to whom he is not yet holy who through himself, and in himself, and in his holy ones is holy. We ask for the human race, we ask for the world, for all the nations that daily sit and dispute that God is not righteous, and that God does not judge rightly; that at some time they may correct themselves, and bring a straight heart to his rightness; and, cleaving to him, set straight unto the straight, may no longer find fault, but the straight one may please the straight: because *How good is the God of Israel*, but *to the upright of heart*[^5]. So when this one who sings—this very one being we ourselves, that is, the body of Christ, the members of Christ—when he saw how many things God had bestowed on the human race, to which before either no God, or a false God, or not so great a God seemed to be, seeing him in his works; *O Lord my God*, he says, *you have been magnified exceedingly*: that is, I, who did not yet understand you, understand you to be great. Always great, even hidden; but to me great when you appeared. You have been magnified, then, from me: just as *Your knowledge has been made wonderful from me*; for it was made wonderful from me. I marvel at it, turned toward it; but it, even if I do not turn toward it, even if after my conversion I turn away, remains whole. But I, now made great in it, and from diminished made whole in it, marvel at what I did not know; not that what is now made great is something that began when I learned, but that I have been made great since I learned. Hear now too where God is seen magnified exceedingly, always great; for he is magnified exceedingly in his works toward us.

**4.** *You have put on confession and comeliness.* Before comeliness he set confession; comeliness is in beauty. You seek beauty; you seek a good thing. But why do you seek beauty, O soul? That your bridegroom may love you; for, foul, you displease him. For what is he like? *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men.* You wish, foul, to kiss the beautiful one; but you do not attend that you are full of iniquities. *But grace is poured out on your lips.* For so it is said of him: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, grace is poured out on your lips; therefore the maidens have loved you*[^6]. There is, then, a certain shapely one, a certain one beautiful *beyond the sons of men;* though a son of man, yet *beyond the sons of men.* Do you wish to please him, O human soul, O one in many? Let us hear the Church; because they had one soul and one heart toward God: the Psalm addresses her. Do you wish to please him? You cannot, as long as you are deformed: what will you do, that you may be beautiful? First let your deformity displease you, and then from that very one whom you wish to please, beautiful, you will deserve beauty. For he himself will be your re-former, who was your former. First, then, attend to what you are, lest you dare to go, foul, to the kisses of the beautiful one. And looking at what, you say, shall I see myself? He has set before you a mirror, his Scripture; it is read to you: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^7]. The mirror is set before you in this reading: see whether you are what it said; if you are not yet, groan that you may be. The mirror will report your face to you: as you will not find the mirror a flatterer, so do not flatter yourself either. That brightness shows you what you are: see what you are; and if it displeases you, seek not to be it. For if, while you are foul, you still displease yourself, you already please the beautiful one. What then? Since your foulness displeases you, you begin to him in confession; as it is said elsewhere, *Begin to the Lord in confession*[^8]. First accuse your foulness: for the foulness of the soul is from sins, from iniquities. By accusing your foulness, begin to confess; from confession you begin to be adorned: by whose adorning, except by the one beautiful in form beyond the sons of men?

**5.** But that he might also make her comely, I dare to say, he loved her even when foul. What is it, he loved her even foul? *For Christ died for the impious*[^9]. What life does he keep for you, justified, who gave his own death even to the impious? Behold, that beautiful one, and *beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*, because most just beyond the sons of men, since he was coming to the foul one, to make her beautiful (I will say this too, because I find it in the Scriptures), became foul. Do not hear me in this, lest in this word I have rashly slipped. For as I had said, "He loved even the foul one"; but I would not have said it as if fittingly, with certain ones loving him, unless a witness came forth for me; and I said what the Apostle said: do you wish to know that he loved even the foul one? *Christ died for the impious*: so too now what I said: That he might come to the foul one, he became foul, he became deformed, how shall I prove it; when divine eloquence has already foretold to me, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men?* But in the very divine eloquence I have again: *We saw him, and he had neither form nor comeliness*[^10]. *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men: We saw him, and he had neither form nor comeliness.* He did not say: "We did not see him, and therefore we did not know whether he had form or comeliness": behold, *we saw him, and he had neither form nor comeliness.* Where, then, did the one see him who said, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men?* and where did the one see him who said, *He had neither form nor comeliness?* Hear where the one saw him who said, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men. Though he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal to God*[^11]. Rightly *beyond the sons of men*, because equal to God: behold, I have received and recognized where the one saw him who said, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men.* For he answered us: Where I saw him, you ask? *In the form of God.* And whence did you see him in the form of God? how did you see him in the form of God? Because *his invisible things are perceived, understood through the things that have been made*[^12]. Good, excellent; I have received, I have recognized both whom you saw, and what kind you saw, and where you saw, and whence you saw. Whom did you see? Our bridegroom. What kind did you see? Beautiful *in form beyond the sons of men.* Where did you see? *When he was in the form of God.* Whence did you see? *They are perceived, understood through the things that have been made.* Let us see also what another prophet says of this one, but not another spirit; for they do not disagree among themselves. The one set before us the one beautiful in form beyond the sons of men; let this one too set before us what he says: *We saw him, and he had neither form nor comeliness.* The one apostle Paul joins both prophets together; one chapter of Paul bears witness to both prophets. There I have the one beautiful in form beyond the sons of men: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not think it robbery to be equal to God.* There he said, what the other too saw, that he had neither form nor comeliness: because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man; he humbled himself, made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross*[^13]. Rightly they saw him, and he had neither form nor comeliness. Rightly before the cross they wagged their heads: Is this the whole Son of God? *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^14]. But he had neither form nor comeliness. Even so, O you to whom it displeases that he had neither form nor comeliness! O you who wag your head before the cross, and do not fix your head on the head that hung on the cross! Rightly the head of the insulters nods, until that very one whom they insulted be their head. Behold, he resumes his comeliness, and a great comeliness. Behold, what you say is less than what he did. You say: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*: behold, he did not come down from the cross, but he rose from the tomb.

**6.** Therefore, O soul, you cannot be comely unless you have confessed your foulness to him who is always beautiful, and for your sake for a time not beautiful; and so for a time not beautiful in the form of a servant, that he never failed from the beauty which is in the form of God. Therefore you, O Church, have comeliness: it is said to you too in the Song of Songs, *O comely among women*[^15]. It is said of you, *Who is this who ascends made white?*[^16] What is it, *made white?* Illumined: not made white, as if painted, in the way women whiten themselves who wish to seem what they are not: not whitewashed, as a whitewashed wall; for it will be destroyed, as the Apostle says, the whitewashed wall, hypocrisy and pretense. A whitewashed wall, outside plaster, inside mud. Not, then, made white in that way; but made white, illumined, because not white through herself. *I was formerly*, he says, *a blasphemer*[^17]; and again: *For we too were once by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^18]. Grace approaches, illuminating and whitening: first you were black, but you have been made white by his grace. *For you were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord*[^19]. It is said of you, then: *Who is this who ascends made white?* Now wonderful, now scarcely to be contemplated. For it is the language of one marveling to say: *Who is this who ascends made white*, so beautiful, so luminous, so without spot or wrinkle? Is not this she who lay in the mire of iniquities? is not this she who lay in the fornication of idols? is not this she who was unclean in every cupidity and carnal desire? *Who*, then, *is this who ascends made white?* Attend to who he is who for her sake was made having neither form nor comeliness, and you understand the honor of this brightness. If you marvel at his lowliness because of her, do not now marvel at her loftiness because of him. Of how great happiness is this woman made white, that even while she was black, the beautiful one laid himself down for her, dying for the impious? Therefore the Lord our God put on confession and comeliness, he put on the Church: for the Church herself is confession and comeliness. First confession, afterward comeliness; confession of sins, comeliness of right deeds: *You have put on confession and comeliness.*

**7.** [v. 2.] *Wrapped around with light, as with a garment.* This is his garment, of which I have already spoken: *Having neither spot nor wrinkle*[^20]. It is called light; and this too I have said: *You were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord.* Not, then, in yourselves; for the darkness is in you, but the light is in the Lord. Therefore: *Wrapped around with light, as with a garment. He stretched out the heaven like a skin.* How he did this, that he should put on as a garment the light, the Church, he wishes to enumerate in certain figured sacraments: how the Church was made light, how she was made without spot or wrinkle, how she was made shining, made white, gleaming in the garment of her bridegroom, clinging to him, how she was made, let us hear. *He stretched out the heaven like a skin.* And this indeed I see. For who stretched out this heaven, which we behold with our carnal eyes, except God? And: *He stretched it out like a skin*, he reduced it to easiness, if you take it to the letter. For when you see this great fabric, because whoever of men stretches out one even small vault, does it with great effort, great labor and difficulty and long work; lest human weakness suspect labor of this kind in the works of God, he brought in a certain easiness for your capacity, that so you might somehow begin to believe that God works easily, and not suppose that he stretched out the heaven as you do the roof of your house; but that, as easy as it is for you to stretch out one skin, so easy it was for him to stretch out so great a heaven. A wonderful easiness, and yet still the Spirit speaks to you, slow. To you, I say, still slow the Spirit speaks. For God did not stretch out the heaven even as you do a skin. For if he stretched it out so, behold, a skin, either wrinkled or folded, is set before you; say that it be stretched out, by your word stretch out the skin. I cannot, you say. Then even in stretching out a skin you are very far from the easiness of God. *For he spoke, and they were made*[^21]. He said, *Let there be a firmament between water and water; and so it was made*[^22]. Yet for the sake of signifying easiness, you may take something for the meanwhile to the letter, according to your understanding.

**8.** But figuratively, if we wish to uncover something covered, and to knock at what is closed, we find that God stretched out the heaven like a skin, so that we may understand the heaven to be holy Scripture. This authority God first set in his Church; thence he began to carry out the rest: for he set the heaven, and stretched it out like a skin, and not in vain like a skin. First he stretched out this fame of the preachers like a skin: the skin signifies mortality: for which reason too those two first men, our parents, the authors of the sin of the human race, Adam and Eve, when in paradise, having scorned God's precept, at the suggestion and persuasion of the serpent they had transgressed what God had commanded, made mortal, were sent out of paradise; and that their mortality itself might be signified, they were clothed with garments of skins; for they received garments made of skins: but skins are not wont to be stripped off except from dead animals: therefore by the name of skins that mortality was figured. What then, if here divine Scripture is signified by the name of skin, how did God make heaven of skin, and *stretch out the heaven like a skin?* Because those through whom Scripture was preached to us were mortal. That Word of God indeed is always the same, always unchangeable and unfailing. Behold: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^23]. Was it, and now is not? It both is, and always will be. If, then, the Word of God is God with God, read it if you can. But what do you say? that it is above, and therefore you cannot read it? The Word of God is everywhere; it reaches mightily from end to end, and touches all things because of its purity. *It was in this world, and the world was made through it*[^24]: and when it came, it was here. For it came in the flesh, never absent in divinity. Why, then, could you not read it? *Because in the wisdom of God the world did not know God through wisdom*, being set there in the wisdom of God; for all things are there, and, that withdrawn, are nothing; set there, you could not know God through wisdom: therefore what follows was necessary: *It pleased God through the foolishness of preaching to save those who believe*[^25]. If through the foolishness of preaching those who believe were to be saved, God chose certain mortal things, he chose mortal men who would die; using a mortal tongue he produced mortal sounds, using the ministry of mortals he used mortal instruments, and in that a heaven was made for you, that in a mortal thing you might know the immortal Word, and you too might become immortal by participation in the same Word. Moses lived, and died: for God said to him, *Ascend the mountain, and die*[^26]. Jeremiah died, and so many Prophets died; and the sayings of the dead, because they were not theirs, but through them his who *stretched out the heaven like a skin*, remain even to our posterity. Behold the Apostle, released from this life, who said that to be dissolved and be with Christ was by far the best, now lives with Christ, as all those Prophets live with Christ: but through what did he dispense to us what we read? Through that which was to die, through the mouth, through the tongue, the teeth, the hands. All these things by which the Apostle worked all that we read are the offices of the body, but at the bidding of the soul which God bade: therefore the heaven was stretched out like a skin. We, under the heaven, as under the skin of the divine Scriptures, read now, while it is stretched out. For afterward *the heaven will be folded up like a book*[^27]. Not in vain, brothers, here like a skin, there like a book; something is figured for us there. As far as concerns divine Scripture, the discourse of the dead is stretched out: therefore it is stretched out like a skin; and it is stretched out the more, because they are dead. For after death the Prophets and Apostles became more known; they were not so known while they lived: Judea alone had the living Prophets, all the nations have the dead. For while they lived, the skin was not yet stretched out, the heaven was not yet stretched out, to cover the world. *He stretched out*, then, *the heaven like a skin.*

**9.** [v. 3.] *Who covers its higher parts with waters.* This too we read, and it is well understood to the letter. For when he commanded that a firmament be made between the waters and the waters, it was done, so that there are lower waters which flood the earth, and there are higher waters removed from sight, yet commended to faith. *And the waters*, he says, *that are above the heavens, let them praise the name of the Lord: for he spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created*[^28]. The sense, then, has been unfolded to the letter; for *he covers its higher parts with waters.* What of the figure? Since in figure we took the skin to be holy Scripture, and the authority of the divine word dispensed to us through mortals, at whose death the fame of the same dispensation is stretched out; according to this, how does *he cover its higher parts with waters?* The higher parts of what? Of the heaven. And what is the heaven? Holy Scripture. What are the higher parts of holy Scripture? what do we find in the holy Scriptures that is higher? Ask Paul: *I show you*, he says, *a more excellent way*[^29]. What more excellent way does he mean? *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become as sounding bronze, or a clanging cymbal*[^30]. If, then, nothing more excellent can be found in holy Scripture than love; how are the higher parts of the heaven covered with waters, if the higher parts of Scripture are the precepts of love? Hear how: *The love*, he says, *of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^31]. Now by the name of pouring out understand the waters in the love of the Holy Spirit. These are the waters of which it is said in a certain Scripture: *And in your streets let your waters run; let no stranger share with you*[^32]. For all who are strangers to the way of truth, whether pagans, or Jews, or heretics, and any bad Christians, can have many gifts, but cannot have love. This very thing, why is it a gift? Not to speak of other gifts outside, which gifts all men have, because *he makes his sun rise on the good and the bad*[^33]: they are gifts of God, and not only common to the good and the bad, but even to beasts and cattle. To be itself, to live, to see, to perceive, to hear, and to use the other endowments of the senses, are gifts of God: but see with what sort and how many they are common, and with those whom you would not wish to imitate. The very keen mind even the worst men have, the skilled industry of arts even the basest stage-players have, riches even robbers have, marriages, children, many bad men have. All beautiful gifts of God, no one denies; but see with whom they are common. Look back to the gifts of the Church herself. The gift of the Sacraments in Baptism, in the Eucharist, in the other holy Sacraments, what sort of gift is it? This gift Simon Magus too obtained. What sort of gift is prophecy? Saul too, a bad king, prophesied, and prophesied then, when he was persecuting holy David. Attend: I did not say, when he had persecuted. For perhaps, having persecuted, he did penance, and was worthy of the spirit of prophesying. Not having persecuted, nor about to persecute, but in the act of persecuting he prophesied. He sent his ministers to seize David: but David at that time was among the prophets, where holy Samuel too was; those who were sent were filled with the spirit of prophecy, and they prophesied. But perhaps they had come with a good mind, either out of the necessity of their office, or not about to do what was commanded. He sent others too: this happened in them too; and let us interpret their mind in this way. When they delayed, he himself came raging, panting for slaughter, thirsting for the blood of the innocent holy one, to whom he was even ungrateful; and he himself was filled with the spirit of prophesying, and prophesied. Let them not, then, boast who perhaps without love have had this holy gift of God, like holy baptism; but let them see what account they will have to render to God, who use holy things unholily. From these will be those who are going to say, *In your name we prophesied.* It will not be said to them, "You lie"; but it will be said: *I do not know you; depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^34]. Because *if I have all prophecy, but have not love, I am nothing*[^35]. Saul too prophesied, but he worked iniquity. And who works iniquity, except one who has not had love? For the fullness of the Law is love. Therefore *he covers its higher parts with waters*: what did he say? In all the Scriptures love holds the most excellent way, the most excellent place; only the good aspire to it, the bad do not share it with us: they can share Baptism, they can share the other Sacraments, they can share prayer, they can share these walls, and this assembly; they do not share love with us. For it is the proper fountain of the good, the proper fountain of the saints, of which it is said, *Let no stranger share with you.* Who are the strangers? All who hear: *I do not know you.* For if they are not known, surely they are strangers, to whom it is said, *I do not know you.* The excellent way of love, then, holds those who properly pertain to the kingdom of heaven. Therefore the precept of love is above the heavens, above all the books: for the books are subject to it, every tongue of the saints serves it, and every motion of God's stewards, of both soul and body. It is, then, the excellent way, and rightly it covers the higher parts of the heaven with waters; because you find nothing more eminent than love in the divine Books.

**10.** But hear still more evidently what the water is. For we said that the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us: we said also, *In the streets let your waters run.* And someone says to me: It was not there expressly said whether I ought to understand love: what if another understands something else? Only remember what the Apostle says: *The love of God is poured out in our hearts.* Whence? *Through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^36]. Hear now the Lord, the master of the Apostles: *If anyone thirsts, let him come, and drink.* Let him say still: *He who believes in me, rivers of living water will flow from his belly.* What is this? Let the Evangelist explain: *But this he said*, he says, *of the Spirit whom those who believed in him were to receive. For the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^37]. Therefore, brothers, if for this reason the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified; when he was glorified and ascending into heaven, the Holy Spirit was sent, and the Apostles were filled with love, poured out in their hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to them; because the higher parts of the heaven are covered with waters. And well, because the Lord ascended into heaven, that he might be higher than the heavens, and thence send love. For God does not so protect as if he were sustained by the one whom he protects: he lifts up the one he protects, he does not burden him. So, then, he so covers the heaven with waters that it is rather lifted up by the divine Spirit. What lifts up is above; what is lifted up is below: that one suspends, this one hangs. If, then, that one suspends, this one hangs; hear that this heaven of the Scriptures hangs from love. There are, namely, those two best-known precepts of love: *On these two precepts the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets*[^38]. *Who covers its higher parts with waters.*

**11.** *Who makes the clouds his ascent.* This too is well taken to the letter. The Lord ascended visibly into heaven. How are the clouds made the ascent of heaven? *When he had said these things, a cloud received him*[^39]. You have likewise foretold about our resurrection: *And the dead*, he says, *in Christ will rise first; then we who are alive will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet Christ in the air, and so we will always be with the Lord*[^40]. You have the clouds the ascent of heaven: I will show too the clouds the ascent of this heaven, that is, of the divine Scriptures. What is this, brothers? Would that the Lord my God deign to number me among those clouds, such as they are; let him see how nebulous a cloud I am: yet take all the preachers of the word of truth to be clouds. Whoever, then, are weak, and cannot ascend into this heaven, that is, to the understanding of the Scriptures, let them ascend by the clouds. For perhaps it is happening to you even now; if we accomplish anything, if our labor and sweat is not unfruitful, you ascend into the heaven of the divine Scriptures, that is, into the understanding of them, through our preaching. How lofty was the heaven in this psalm! For none of you saw what was in the figure: *He stretched out the heaven like a skin, who covers its higher parts with waters.* This very thing that was said, *Who makes the clouds his ascent*; behold, it is now understood, as far as the Lord has granted, by our preaching; for the clouds do not rain of their own gift. Ascend you by understanding, bear fruit in the understanding itself; lest you be such as that vineyard, of which it is said in the prophet, *I will command my clouds not to rain upon it*[^41]. For a certain vineyard was accused, that instead of the grape it had given thorns, that it had not rendered worthy thanks for the sweet rain. For he who hears good things and works evil things is rained upon with sweet rain, and brings forth thorns. We must not suppose, brothers, that the Lord spoke of some earthly and visible vineyard. For there, lest under the obscurity of the discourse an excuse for iniquity should hide, the Lord himself through the same prophet set forth to which vineyard he was speaking, and of which vineyard he was saying these things: *The vineyard*, he says, *of the Lord of Sabaoth is the house of Israel*[^42]. Why do your hearts wander, O wicked ones, through the mountains and hills of the vinedressers? I know, he says, of which vineyard I speak, I know where I sought the grape, and found thorns; without cause you suspect and opine one thing and another, unwilling to understand so as to act well. For this too is written: *He was unwilling to understand so as to act well*[^43]. Remove all your suspicions from the midst: *The vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth is the house of Israel; and the man of Judah, the beloved new shoot.* Beloved, when it was planted; condemned, when it produced thorns. So, brothers, perhaps the house of Israel was a vineyard, and are we not a vineyard? Let us hear with fear what we see was said to the Jews. See how the Apostle frightens the branches grafted in, from the broken branches, and in the broken branches commends a severity to be feared, in the grafted branches commends a goodness to be loved. Do not be unfruitful in goodness, lest, barren, you feel severity. But I am not a vine, you say to me. Where is the Lord's voice: *I am the vine, you are the branches; my Father is the husbandman*[^44]? Where is the voice of the Apostle: *Who plants a vineyard, and does not partake of its fruit?*[^45] A vineyard, then, you are, O Church, and you have God as your husbandman. No human husbandman rains upon his vineyard. Therefore, most beloved brothers, the inward parts of the Church, the pledges of the Church, the sons of the heavenly mother, hear, while there is time. God threatened that vineyard with a most dreadful threat: *I will command my clouds*, he says, *not to rain upon it.* And it was done: the Apostles came to the Jews, they rejected them; and they said to them, *To you we were sent, but because you have rejected the word of God, we go to the gentiles*[^46]. You see how, in the same Spirit of God, with him bidding within who dwells in the hearts of his own, it was commanded to the clouds of God not to rain upon the vineyard, which, when it was expected to make the grape, made thorns. Therefore he both made the clouds its ascent, and stretched out the heaven like a skin. There is no reason for you to complain: the authority of the Scriptures is stretched out over the world, the clouds are not lacking, the word of truth is preached, all things that are obscure are expounded, that your hearts may ascend by the clouds. See how you believe, see what you take up: after the preacher will come the judge, after the steward will come the exactor. *Who makes the clouds his ascent.*

**12.** *Who walks upon the wings of the winds.* This is now dangerous to take to the letter. Or what are the wings of the winds? or indeed how, as in a picture, are we going to make for ourselves winds flying and having wings? The wind, brothers, is nothing but what we feel, a certain motion and as it were wave of the air, driving what it can according to its strength. What are the wings of the winds? But also, what are the wings of God? And yet it is said, *Under the shadow of your wings they will hope*[^47]. Let us try, then, to take this too to the letter, as if properly done in this creation. Perhaps Scripture commends the swiftness of the word; of which swiftness we spoke a while ago in another psalm, where it is written, *His word runs swiftly*[^48]. Because men know nothing swifter than the winds. As that easiness was commended in the skin; for nothing is more easily stretched out by a man than a skin: so here too, hinting that God or his Word, present everywhere, deserts nothing by the swiftness of its motion, because you knew nothing swifter than the wind: *He walks*, he says, *upon the wings of the winds*, that is, his swiftness surpasses the swiftness of the winds: so that by the wings of the winds you understand the swiftness of the winds, and understand the word of God to be swifter than all winds. And so on the first face of consideration: let us knock for something more inward, and let these letters indicate something to us figuratively.

**13.** We understand the winds in figure, not absurdly, to be souls: not because the soul is wind, but because the wind is invisible, although a corporeal thing, driving bodies, yet because it flees the gaze of the human eye; but the soul is invisible, therefore we rightly understand souls to be winds. Hence it is that God is said to have breathed the spirit of life into the man formed; *and the man was made into a living soul*[^49]. Therefore winds are not absurdly taken in allegory to be souls. But see that you do not suppose, the word "allegory" being named, that I have said something of the pantomime. For certain words, since they are words, and proceed from the tongue, are common to us even with playful and not honorable things: yet these words have their own place in the Church, and their own place on the stage. For I have not said what the Apostle did not say, when of the two sons of Abraham he said, *Which things are*, he says, *in allegory*[^50]. It is called allegory when something seems to sound one thing in the words, and to signify another in the understanding. As Christ is called a lamb: is he then a beast? Christ a lion: is he then a wild animal? Christ a rock: is he then a hardness? Christ a mountain: is he then a swelling of the earth? And so many things seem to sound one thing, to signify another; and it is called allegory. For he who supposes that I spoke of allegory from the theater, let him suppose that the Lord too spoke of parable from the amphitheater. You see what a city does where spectacles abound: in the country I would speak more securely; for what allegory is, perhaps men would not have learned there, except in the Scriptures of God. Therefore, what we call allegory is a figure, an allegory is a figured sacrament. And what do we take here: *He ascended upon the wings of the winds?* We have said that the winds are well taken figuratively to be souls. What are the wings of the winds, the wings of souls, except those by which they are lifted up? The wings, then, of souls are virtues, good works, right deeds. In two wings all have their feathers; for all the precepts are in two precepts. Whoever loves God and neighbor has a winged soul, flying to the Lord with free wings, with holy love. Whoever is entangled in carnal love has birdlime on his wings. For if the soul does not have wings and feathers, whence does that one, groaning in tribulations, say, *Who will give me wings, like a dove's?* And there follows: *And I will fly, and rest*[^51]. Likewise in another place: *Where shall I go from your spirit, and where shall I flee from your face? If I ascend into heaven, you are there; if I descend into hell, you are present; if I take my wings like a dove, and fly to the ends of the sea.* As though he were saying: So I can flee from the face of your wrath, if I take wings like a dove, and fly to the ends of the sea. To fly to the ends of the sea is now to extend hope to the end of the age, like that one who says, *This is the labor before me, until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand in the last things*[^52]. And how does he come to the ends of the sea, even with wings taken? *For even there*, he says, *your hand will lead me, and your right hand will bring me through*[^53]. For even with my wings I will fall, unless you lead me. Therefore the souls that work well the precepts of God have good and free wings, bound by no birdlime, having love from a pure conscience and faith unfeigned. But however much they are endowed with the virtues of love, what is that compared to that love of God by which they were loved, even when they were entangled with birdlime? Greater, then, is the love of God toward us than ours toward him. Our love is our wings, but he *walks* even *upon the wings of the winds.*

**14.** The Apostle was already saying to certain ones: *I bend my knees for you to the Father, that he may grant you according to the inner man that Christ dwell through faith in your hearts, that, rooted and founded in love.* Now he gives them love, now he gives them wings and feathers. *That you may be able*, he says, *to comprehend what is the breadth, the length, the height, and the depth*[^54]. Perhaps it signifies the cross of the Lord. For there was the breadth, in which the hands were stretched out; the length rising from the earth, on which the body was fixed; the height, from that crossbeam upward which projects; the depth, where the cross was fixed, and there all the hope of our life. For the breadth is in good works, the length in persevering to the end, the height because of the Heart-aloft, that all our good works, in which we persevere to the end, having the breadth by which we work well, and the length by which we persevere to the end, we may do only in the hope of heavenly rewards. For that is the height, not to seek the reward here, but above; lest it be said to us, *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward*[^55]. But the depth which I mentioned, where the part of the cross was fixed, and was not seen; thence rose the things that were seen. What is it that is hidden, and not public in the Church? The sacrament of Baptism, the sacrament of the Eucharist. For our good works even the pagans see, but the Sacraments are hidden from them: but from the things they do not see, those rise which they see; just as from the depth of the cross which is fixed in the earth, the whole cross rises that appears and is discerned. And what afterward? When the Apostle had said this, he added: *To know also the surpassing knowledge of the love of Christ*; when it had already been said, *Rooted and founded in love.* For you love Christ, and therefore you work on the cross. But do you love as much as he loved you? But, loving however much you love, you fly to him, that you may know how he himself loved you; that is, that you may know the surpassingness of the love of Christ. For you love as much as you can, and you fly as much as you can: but he walks even upon the wings of the winds. *Who walks upon the wings of the winds.*

**15.** [v. 4.] *Who makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a blazing fire.* And this, although we do not see the appearance of the angels; for it is hidden from our eyes, and is in a certain great commonwealth of the emperor God, yet we know that there are angels by faith, and we read it written that they appeared to many, and we hold it, nor is it lawful for us to doubt of it. The angels are spirits; and when they are spirits, they are not angels; when they are sent, they become angels. For angel is a name of office, not of nature. Do you ask the name of this nature? it is spirit; do you ask the office? it is angel: from what it is, it is spirit; from what it does, it is angel. See that in man. The name of nature is man, of office soldier; the name of nature is man, of office herald; for a man becomes a herald, that is, he who was a man becomes a herald; not he who was a herald becomes a man. So, then, those who were already spirits created by God the creator, he makes them angels, by sending them to announce what he commands; and he makes a blazing fire his ministers. We read that fire appeared in the bush, we read also that fire was sent down from above, and fulfilled what was commanded. It ministered, then, when it fulfilled: when it was, it was in its own nature; when it did what was commanded, it fulfilled the ministry. So according to the letter in the creation.

**16.** But what is it figuratively in the Church? How do we take, *Who makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a blazing fire?* By spirits he means the spiritual. He well makes spiritual men his angels, that is, the messengers of his word. *For the spiritual man judges all things; but he himself is judged by no one*[^56]. See a spiritual man made an angel of God. *I could not*, he says, *speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal*[^57]. From a certain spiritual disposition he was sent to carnal men, as an angel from heaven to the earth. How does he say, *and his ministers a blazing fire*, except as he says, *fervent in spirit?*[^58] For one fervent in spirit, every minister of God, is a burning fire. Did not Stephen burn? With what fire did he burn? and what was that fire, when he was being stoned, and prayed for those by whom he was being stoned? When you hear: The minister of God is fire; do you suppose that he is going to set on fire? Let him set on fire, but your hay; that is, let the minister of God, preaching the word of God, burn all your carnal desires. Hear him: *So let a man regard us, as ministers of Christ, and stewards of the mysteries of God*[^59]. But how did he burn, when he said, *Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians; our heart is enlarged?*[^60] He burned and blazed with love, and went into them, to set them on fire. This fire the Lord said he would send into the earth, when he said, *I came to send fire into the earth*[^61]. As the sword, so the fire. The sword divides carnal affection, the fire consumes it. Understand the whole in the word of God, recognize the whole in the spirit of God. Begin to be fervent with love through the word you hear, and see what the fire, the minister of God, has done in you. *Who makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a blazing fire.*

**17.** [v. 5.] *He founded the earth upon its firmness; it will not be inclined unto the age of the age.* To take this of this earth, I do not know whether it has any way out, whether it is rightly said, *It will not be inclined unto the age of the age;* of which it is said, *Heaven and earth will pass away*[^62]. There is labor here, if you seek to take it to the letter. For what he says, *He founded the earth upon its firmness;* perhaps there is a certain firmness hidden from us, which holds the earth, and this he said: *He founded.* Upon what? Upon the firmness of the earth itself, which he set beneath it, that it might be held, which perhaps lies hidden from your eyes. Let these things be hidden in the creation; the Creator will not lie hidden from the obscurity of the creation: let us see what we can, from the things we see let us praise and love him. Let us turn ourselves to seek something here set in figure. *He founded the earth*, I understand the Church. *The earth is the Lord's, and its fullness*[^63]; I understand the earth to be the Church. It is the thirsting earth, it is she who says in the Psalms; for one says out of all: *My soul is as earth without water to you*[^64]. What is it, *without water?* Thirsting. So my soul thirsts for you, as earth without water: for unless it thirsts, it will not be rightly watered. To a drunken soul rain is a flood; it must thirst: *for blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness*[^65]: and let it say, *My soul is as earth without water to you;* because it says elsewhere, *My soul thirsted for the living God*[^66]. The earth, then, I understand to be the Church. What is its firmness upon which it is founded, except its foundation? or do we incongruously understand the firmness upon which the earth is founded to be the foundation where the Church is established? What is that foundation? *A foundation*, he says, *no one can lay except that which is laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^67]. There, then, we have been made firm. Rightly, because there made firm, we will not be inclined unto the age of the age; for nothing is firmer than that foundation. You were infirm, but a firm foundation bears you. In yourself you could not be firm; you will be firm always, if you do not withdraw from that firm foundation. *It will not be inclined unto the age of the age.* It is the predestined pillar and ground of truth.

**18.** [vv. 6-17.] *The deep is as a garment, his wrap: the waters will stand upon the mountains. At your rebuke they will flee, at the voice of your thunder they will fear. The mountains ascend, and the plains descend, into the place which you founded for them. You set a boundary which they will not transgress, nor will they return to cover the earth. Who send forth springs in the valleys; through the midst of the mountains the waters will pass. All the beasts of the field will drink, the wild asses will take up into their thirst. Above them the birds of heaven will dwell, from the midst of the rocks they will give their voice. Watering the mountains from your higher places: from the fruit of your works the earth will be satisfied. Bringing forth hay for the cattle, and grass for the service of men. That he may bring forth bread from the earth; and wine gladdens the heart of man. That he may make the face glad with oil; and bread strengthens the heart of man. The trees of the field will be satisfied, and the cedars of Lebanon which he planted. There the sparrows will nest; the house of the coot is their leader.* You notice the heaven stretched out, you wish to ascend by understanding, and I see it. Yet how lofty it is I think your Charity considers with me. For I wished to pronounce many verses, that you might see how loftily the sacraments of God are set; lest we disdain what is offered, lest things readily available become cheap, that, always sought, even if with difficulty, they may be found with the greater joy. Yet among the other things, brothers, which can be taken to the letter; can this too which was said, *There the sparrows will nest; the house of the coot is their leader?* Is the house of the coot the leader of the sparrows? or is the house of the coot the leader of the cedars? For this too is there: *And the cedars of Lebanon which he planted: there the sparrows will nest; the house of the coot is their leader.* And indeed in Latin idiom we cannot understand *their* as referring to the cedars; for the cedars are of feminine gender. So how is the house of the coot the leader of the sparrows? For this can in no way be understood in this creation that lies before our eyes. For we know that coots are sea or marsh birds. Let the house of the coot be the nest of the coot: how, then, is the house of the coot the leader of the sparrows? Why does the Holy Spirit mingle in visible things certain as it were absurd things, except that, from what we cannot take to the letter, he may compel us so to seek spiritually?

**19.** Therefore, if, as I said, you wish to ascend by understanding to the heaven, to the stretched-out skin, and the clouds made his ascent; this cloud which speaks to you is too weak today to expound all these things. Pardon, if not your weakness, certainly mine. I see your eagerness so great that you are always ready to hear: but there are two things whose reckoning is not to be scorned. For both the weakness of our body must be considered, and the memory of the very things that are expounded. Meanwhile think of what you have heard. What did I say? Ruminate what you have eaten. For so you will be clean animals, and fit for the feasts of God. But notice your fruit in your works. For he digests very badly who hears well, and does not work well: because the Lord our God does not fail to feed us. It is known to all that of the bread we have received, and which we dispense, we will render an account. Your Charity knows very well; for the divine Page is not silent to us, nor does God flatter us. How free we are toward you from this place, you can notice: and if perhaps I am less free, or all who speak to you in this place, if we are less free, certainly the very word of God fears no one. But whether we fear, or are free, we are compelled to announce the one who fears no one: this not men, but God has bestowed on you, that even through the swollen you may hear freely. You will have no excuse in the judgment of God, unless you have exercised yourselves in good works, and have given a fitting fruit of the things you have heard, as of rain. The fitting fruit is good works; the fitting fruit is sincere love, not only of a brother, but even of an enemy. Spurn no suppliant, and the one to whom you cannot give what he asked, do not spurn him: if you can give, give; if you cannot, show yourself affable. God crowns within the will, where he does not find the means. Let no one say: I do not have it. Love is not dispensed from a purse: because whatever we say, and whatever we have said, and whatever we may be able to say, whether we, or those after us, or those before us, have no end but love: because the end of the precept is love, from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned. Question, when you ask of God, your hearts; see how you let this verse pass: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^68]. You will not be praying, unless you say this: if you say another, he does not hear you; because the Lawyer whom he sent did not dictate it to you. It is necessary, then, both that, when we say our words in prayer, we say them according to it; and that, when we say those very words, we understand well what we say, because God willed it to be manifest. If, then, you do not pray, you will have no hope: if you pray otherwise than the Master taught, you will not be heard; or if in prayer you have lied, you will not obtain. Therefore, both we must pray, and tell the truth, and so pray as he taught. Willing or unwilling, you will daily say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* Do you wish to say it securely? Do what you say.
