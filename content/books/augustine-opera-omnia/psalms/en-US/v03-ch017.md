# Exposition of Psalm 66

**1.** [v. 2] Your Charity remembers that, in the two psalms which have already been treated, we exhorted our soul to bless the Lord, and said with godly song: *Bless the Lord, O my soul*[^1]. If, then, in those psalms we exhorted our soul to bless the Lord; in this psalm it is rightly said: *May God have mercy on us, and bless us.*[^2] Let our soul bless the Lord, and may God bless us. When God blesses us, we grow; and when we bless the Lord, we grow: both profit us. He is not increased by our blessing, nor diminished by our cursing. He who curses the Lord, is himself diminished; he who blesses the Lord, is himself increased. The blessing of the Lord is first in us, and it is consequent that we too bless the Lord. That is the rain, this the fruit. Therefore there is rendered, as it were the fruit of the farmer, to God watering and cultivating us. Let us sing these things with no barren devotion, no empty voice, but with a true heart. For most openly God the Father was called a farmer. The Apostle says: *You are God's tillage, you are God's building*[^3]. In the visible things of this world, a vine is not a building, and a building is not a vineyard: but we are the vineyard of the Lord, because he cultivates us unto fruit; we are the building of God, because he who cultivates us dwells in us. And what does the same Apostle say? *I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. Therefore neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase*[^4]. He, then, gives the increase. Are these perhaps the farmers? For he is called a farmer who plants, who waters: but the Apostle said: *I planted, Apollos watered.* We ask whence he himself did this? The Apostle answers: *Not I, but the grace of God with me*[^5]. Therefore wherever you turn yourself; whether through the angels, you will find God your farmer; whether through the prophets, he is your farmer; whether through the apostles, recognize the same as your farmer. What, then, are we? Perhaps we are the laborers of that farmer, and this too with strength imparted by him, and grace given by him. He himself, then, both cultivates, and gives the increase. But a man as farmer cultivates the vineyard only so far as to plow, to prune, to apply the other things that pertain to the diligence of farmers: he cannot rain upon his vineyard. But if perhaps he can water it, from whose can he? He himself indeed leads it into a channel, but God fills the fountain. Finally, in his vineyard he cannot give increase to the branches, he cannot form the fruits, he cannot regulate the seeds, he cannot temper the times of bearing. But God who can do all things is our farmer: we are secure. Perhaps someone says: You say that God is our farmer: but I say the Apostles are the farmers, who said: *I planted, Apollos watered.* If I say it, let no one believe; if Christ says it, woe to him who does not believe. What, then, does the Lord Christ say? *I am the vine, you the branches, my Father the farmer*[^6]. Let the earth, then, thirst, and utter the voices of its thirst; since it is written: *My soul is like a land without water to you*[^7]. Let our earth, then, ourselves, desiring the rain of God, say: *May God have mercy on us, and bless us.*

**2.** *May he make his face shine upon us.*[^8] You were going to ask perhaps what is, *bless us.* In many ways men wish to be blessed by God: one wishes to be blessed, that he may have a house full of things necessary for this life; another desires to be blessed, that he may obtain health of body without blemish; another wishes to be blessed, if perhaps he is sick, that he may recover health; another, desiring children, and perhaps saddened that they are not born, wishes to be blessed that he may have posterity. And who could count the diverse vows of men desiring to be blessed by the Lord God? But which of us would say that it is not the blessing of God, if either the tillage bear him fruit, or anyone's house abound with plenty of temporal things, or health of body itself be either kept lest it be lost, or, lost, be restored? The fruitfulness too of women, and the chaste vows of those desiring children, to whom do they pertain except to the Lord God? For he who created when it was not, he himself, by the succession of offspring, makes to remain what he founded. God does these things, God gives these things. It is too little for us to say: God does these things, God gives these things; but he alone does, he alone gives. For what if God does these things, but someone not God does these things too? He does them, and he alone does them. And these things are sought in vain either from men, or from demons: and whatever goods the enemies of God receive, they receive from him; and when they ask them from others, when they receive, not knowing it they receive from him. As, when they are punished, and think they are punished by others, not knowing it they are punished by him: so too when they are quickened, filled, saved, freed; even if not knowing it, they attribute it either to men, or to demons, or to angels, they have it only from him, in whose hand is the power of all things. To this end we have said these things, brethren, that whoever even desires these earthly things perhaps, whether for the supplements of necessity, or on account of some weakness, may desire them only from him who is the fountain of all goods, and the creator and re-creator of all things.

**3.** But there are some gifts which God gives even to his enemies, others which he reserves only for his friends. What are the gifts he gives to his enemies? Those I have enumerated. For not the good alone have houses full of necessary things; or the good alone are either safe, or recover from sickness; or the good alone have children, the good alone money, the good alone the other things suited to this temporal and passing life. The evil too have these things, and sometimes they are lacking to the good: but they are lacking to the evil too, and very often to these more than to those; sometimes they abound to those rather than to these. These temporal things God willed to be mixed; because if he gave them to the good alone, the evil too would think God to be worshiped for the sake of these; again, if he gave them to the evil alone, the weak good would fear to be converted, lest perhaps these things be lacking to them. For there is still a weak soul, less capable of the kingdom of God; God our farmer ought to nourish it. For even the tree which now sustains storms by its strength, when it was born of the earth, was an herb. That farmer, then, knows not only to prune and purge robust trees; but also to hedge the tender ones in their recent rising. Therefore, dearly beloved, as I began to say, if these things were given to the good alone, all would wish to be converted to God for the sake of receiving them: again, if they were given to the evil alone, the weak would fear, lest, when they were converted, they lose what only the evil had. They are given mixed both to the good and to the evil. Again, if they were taken away from the good alone, there would be that same fear of the weak, lest they be converted to God: again, if they were taken away from the evil alone, it would be thought the only punishment by which the evil are struck. Therefore, that he gives these things to the good, he consoles those on pilgrimage: that he gives them to the evil too, he admonishes the good to desire other things, which they do not have in common with the evil. Again, he takes them away from the good when he wills, that they may question themselves about their own powers; and find themselves out, who perhaps lay hidden from themselves, whether they can now say: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^9]. For that soul too blessed the Lord, and rendered fruits, watered by the fattening of blessing. *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away*: he withdrew the things given; but did not withdraw the giver. Every blessed soul, simple, not cleaving to earthly things, nor lying with wings entangled in birdlime, but with the brightness of virtues put forth, on the twin wings of twin love exults into the free air; and sees taken away from itself that which it was treading on, not that on which it was leaning; and says secure: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord.* He gave, and took away; he who gave, and took away what he gave, remains: let his name be blessed. To this end, then, these things are taken away from the good. But lest perhaps some weak one say: When can I be of so great virtue as holy Job? You marvel at the strength of a tree, because you are just born: this great tree you marvel at, under whose branches and shade you are refreshed, was a twig. But do you fear lest these things be taken from you, when you are such? Attend that they are taken away from the evil too. Why, then, do you defer your conversion? What you, being good, fear to lose, perhaps you, being evil, will lose. If, being good, you have lost them, the consoler who took them away is present: the chest is emptied of gold, the heart is full of faith; outside you are poor, but within you are rich: you carry with you riches which you would not lose, even if you came out naked from shipwreck. What, then, you perhaps, being evil, will lose, why does the loss not find you good, since you see even the evil suffer loss? But those are struck with a greater loss: the house is empty, the conscience emptier. Whatever evil man loses these things, he has not outside what he may hold, nor within where he may rest. He flees from here where he suffered loss, where he boasted of himself before the eyes of men by the display of riches; now he cannot boast of himself in the eyes of men: he does not return within to himself, because he has nothing. He did not imitate the ant; he did not gather for himself grains, when it was summer. What did I say, when it was summer? When there was tranquility of life, when there was prosperity of this age toward him, when he had leisure, when he was called happy by all, that was his summer. He would imitate the ant, if he heard the word of God; he would gather grains, and store them within. The temptation of tribulation had come, the winter of torpor had come upon him, the storm of fear, the cold of sadness, whether it were loss, or some peril of safety, or some bereavement of his own, or some dishonor and humbling; it was winter: the ant returns to what it gathered in summer; and within, in its secret place, where no one sees, it is refreshed by its summer labors. When it was gathering these things for itself in summer, all saw it: when it feeds on these things in winter, no one sees. What is this? See the ant of God: it rises daily, runs to the church of God, prays, hears a reading, sings a hymn, ruminates on what it heard, thinks within itself, stores within the grains gathered from the threshing floor. These very things now being said, those who hear prudently, do this, and they are seen by all to go to church, return from church, hear a sermon, hear a reading, find a book, open and read it: all these things are seen, when they are done. That is the ant wearing the path, carrying and storing in the sight of those who behold. Winter comes sometime; for to whom does it not come? loss befalls, bereavement befalls: others perhaps have mercy as on a wretched man, who do not know what the ant has within to eat, and they say: Wretched is he to whom this befell, or he to whom this befell: what do you think his mind is? how worn down he is! He measures from himself, has compassion from his own powers, and therefore is deceived; because the measure by which he measures himself, he wishes to apply to him whom he does not know. You see a man who has suffered loss, or been humbled, or struck with bereavement: what do you think? This man did some evil, that this should befall him. May my enemies have such a heart, such a mind. You do not know, O man; truly you are an enemy to yourself, who do not gather for yourself in summer what that man gathered. Now within, the ant eats the labors of summer: but you could see it gathering; you cannot see it eating. These things, brethren, as far as the Lord has given, as far as he has deigned to suggest and instill into our weakness and humility, as far as we grasp according to our small measure, we have said, why God gives all these things mixed both to the good and to the evil, and why he takes them away both from the good and from the evil. He has given to you; do not be lifted up: he has taken away from you; do not be broken. Do you fear lest he take away; he can take away from the good and from the evil: it is better, then, that you, being good, lose what is God's, but hold God. So, then, that evil man too; we exhort him: You are going to suffer loss (who is not going to suffer bereavement?); some misfortune will rush in, some calamity from across, from wherever the world is full, examples do not cease: I speak to you in summer, the grains for you to gather are not lacking: attend to the ant, O lazy one; gather in summer while you can; winter will not allow you to gather, but to eat what you have gathered. For how many suffer tribulation in such a way that they have no leisure either to read anything, or to hear anything; nor perhaps are those admitted to them who would console them. The ant remains in its cave; let it see whether it gathered anything in summer, whereby to occupy itself in winter.

**4.** But now because God blesses us, why will he bless us? what blessing does this voice ask: *That God may bless us*? The blessing which he reserves for his friends, which he gives only to the good. Do not desire as a great thing what the evil too receive: because God is good, he does those things, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust. What, then, especially to the good? what especially to the just? *May he make his face shine upon us.*[^10] The face of this sun you make shine upon the good and the evil; make your face shine upon us. The good and the evil see this light with the cattle: but blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God. *May he make his face shine upon us.* There is a twofold understanding; both are to be said. Make your face shine upon us, he says; show us his face. For God does not at some time make his face shine, as though at some time he were without light: but make it shine upon us, that what lay hidden from us may be opened to us, and what was, but was hidden from us, may be revealed upon us, that is, made to shine. Or certainly, make your image shine upon us: that he may have said this: Make your face shine upon us; you have stamped your face upon us, you have made us in your image and likeness, you have made us your coin; but your image ought not to remain in darkness; send the ray of your wisdom, let it drive away our darkness, and let your image shine in us; let us recognize ourselves your image, let us hear what is said in the Song of Songs: *Unless you know yourself, O beautiful among women*[^11]. For it is said to the Church: *Unless you know yourself.* What is this? Unless you know yourself made in the image of God. O precious soul of the Church, redeemed by the blood of the immaculate Lamb, attend how much you are worth; think what was given for you. Let us, then, say and wish: *May he make his face shine upon us.* We bear his face: as the faces of emperors are spoken of, truly there is a certain sacred face of God in his image; but the unrighteous do not recognize in themselves the image of God. That the face of God may shine upon them, what ought they to say? *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, you will enlighten my darkness*[^12]. I am in the darkness of sins; but by the ray of your wisdom let my darkness be dispelled: let your face appear, and if perhaps it appears through me somewhat disfigured, let what was formed by you be reformed by you. *May he*, then, *make his face shine upon us.*

**5.** [v. 3] *That we may know on earth your way.*[^13] *On earth,* here, in this life, *let us know your way.* What is, *your way*? That which leads to you. Let us recognize whither we go, let us recognize by what way we go; both we cannot do in the darkness. You are far from those on pilgrimage; you have stretched out a way for us by which there should be a return to you: *Let us recognize on earth your way.* What is his way, since this we wished, *that* we may know on earth your way? We are to seek it, not to learn it through ourselves. We can learn it from the Gospel: *I am the way,* the Lord says. Christ said: *I am the way.* But do you fear lest you err? He added: *And the truth.* Who errs in the truth? He errs who withdraws from the truth. Christ is the truth, Christ is the way: walk. Do you fear lest you die before you arrive? *I am the life*: *I am,* he says, *the way, and the truth, and the life*[^14]. As if to say: Why do you fear? Through me you walk, in me you walk, in me you rest. What, then, does he say: *Let us know on earth your way,* except, let us know on earth your Christ? But let the psalm itself answer: lest you think that testimony must be brought from other Scriptures which perhaps is lacking here, by repeating it he shows what he said: *That we may know on earth your way;* and as though you asked: In what earth, what way? *Among all the nations your salvation.* In what earth do you seek? Hear: *Among all the nations.* What way do you seek? Hear: *Your salvation.* Or perhaps Christ is not his salvation? And what is that which that old man Simeon said; that old man, I say, in the Gospel, kept full of years until the infancy of the Word? For that old man received in his hands the infant Word of God. He who deigned to be in the womb, would he disdain to be in the hands of an old man? He in the womb of the virgin, who in the hands of the old man; a weak infant, both within the womb, and in the hands of the old man, to give us firmness, he through whom all things were made; and if all things, then the mother too; he came humble, he came weak, but clothed with a weakness to be changed: *because although he was crucified of weakness, yet he lives of the power of God*[^15], the Apostle says. He was, then, in the hands of the old man. And what did that old man say? what did he say, rejoicing that he was now to be released from here, seeing held in his hands him by whom and in whom his salvation was held? *Now you let your servant depart, O Lord, in peace; for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^16]. Therefore, May God bless us, and have mercy on us: may he make his face shine upon us, that we may know on earth your way. In what earth? Among all the nations. What way? Your salvation.

**6.** [v. 4] What follows; because the way of God is known on earth, because among all the nations the salvation of God is known? *Let the peoples confess to you,* O God; *let the peoples confess to you,* he says, *all of them.*[^17] A heretic arises, and says: I have peoples in Africa: and another from another part: And I have peoples in Galatia. You in Africa, he in Galatia: I seek him who has them everywhere. Surely at this voice you dared to exult, since you heard: *Let the peoples confess to you, O God.* Hear the following verse, because it does not speak of a part: *Let all the peoples confess to you.* Walk in the way with all the nations, walk in the way with all the peoples, O sons of peace, sons of the one Catholic Church; walk in the way, sing as you walk. Travelers do this for solace of their labor. Sing in this way; I beseech you by that very way, sing in this way: sing a new song; let no one there sing old things: sing the love-songs of your homeland; no one old things. A new way, a new traveler, a new song. Hear the Apostle exhorting you to a new song: *If, then, anyone be in Christ a new creature, the old things have passed away; behold, they have become new*[^18]. Sing a new song in the way which you have known *on earth.* In what earth? *Among all the nations.* Therefore the new song too does not pertain to a part. He who sings in a part, sings old things: whatever he sings, he sings old things, the old man sings; he is divided, he is carnal. Certainly in so far as he is carnal, in so far he is old, and in so far as he is spiritual, in so far new. See what the Apostle says: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal.* Whence does he prove them carnal? *For when one says: I am Paul's; but another: I, Apollos': are you not,* he says, *carnal*[^19]? Therefore in spirit sing a new song in the secure way. As travelers sing, and very often sing by night. All things round about resound fearful, or rather do not resound, but are silent round about; and the more silent they are, the more fearful; yet they sing too who fear robbers. How much more securely do you sing in Christ? This way has no robber, if you, by not forsaking the way, do not fall in with a robber. Sing, I say, secure a new song in the way which you have known *on earth,* that is, *among all the nations.* See that the new song itself is not sung with you by one who wished to be in a part. *Sing,* he says, *to the Lord a new song;* and there follows: *Sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^20]. *Let the peoples confess to you, O God.* They have found your way; let them confess. That very singing is confession; confession of your sins, and of the power of God. Confess your iniquity, confess the grace of God; accuse yourself, glorify him; reprehend yourself, praise him: that he too, coming, may find you your own punisher, and show himself to you your savior. For why do you fear to confess, you who have found this way among all the nations? why do you fear to confess, and in your confession to sing a new song with all the earth, in all the earth, in catholic peace? Do you fear to confess to God, lest, having confessed, he condemn you? If, not confessing, you lie hidden, having confessed you will be condemned. You fear to confess, you who, by not confessing, cannot be hidden: you will be condemned silent, who could be freed having confessed. *Let the peoples confess to you, O God; let all the peoples confess to you.*

**7.** [v. 8] And because this confession does not lead to punishment, it follows, and says: *Let the nations be glad and exult.*[^21] If robbers, having confessed, lament before a man, let the faithful, having confessed, be glad before God. If a man judges, the torturer and fear exact confession from the robber; nay, sometimes fear suppresses confession, pain exacts it: and he who laments in torments, but fears to be killed having confessed, bears the torments as much as he can; and if he is overcome by pain, he brings forth his voice unto death. Nowhere, then, glad, nowhere exulting: before he confesses, the claw plows him; when he has confessed, the executioner leads him condemned: wretched everywhere. But *let the nations be glad and exult.* Whence? Through that very confession. Why? Because he to whom they confess is good: to this end he exacts confession, that he may free the humble; to this end he condemns the one not confessing, that he may punish the proud. Be sad, then, before you confess; having confessed, exult, now you will be healed. Your conscience had gathered corruption, the abscess had swelled, it tortured you, did not let you rest: the physician applies the fomentations of words, and sometimes he cuts; he applies the medicinal iron in the rebuke of tribulation: do you recognize the physician's hand; confess, let all the corruption go out and flow away in confession: now exult, now be glad; what remains will easily be healed. *Let the peoples confess to you, O God; let all the peoples confess to you.* And because they confess, *let the nations be glad and exult; because you judge the peoples in equity.* No one deceives you: let the one to be judged rejoice, who feared the one about to judge. For he foresaw, and anticipated his face in confession: but he, when he comes, will judge the peoples in equity. What will the cunning of the accuser avail there, where the witness is the conscience, where you will be present, and your cause, where the judge does not seek a witness? He sent you an advocate: on account of him, and through him confess; plead your cause, and he is the defender of the penitent, and the seeker of pardon for the one confessing, and the judge of the innocent. Can you truly fear your cause, where your advocate will be your judge? *Let*, then, *the nations be glad and exult; because you judge the peoples in equity.* But they will be able to fear lest they be judged badly: let them give themselves to be corrected, give themselves to be directed by him who sees those to be judged. Here let them be corrected, and let them not fear when they are judged. See what he says in another psalm: *O God, save me in your name, and judge me in your power*[^22]. What does he say? If you do not first save me in your name, I ought to fear when you judge me in your power: but if you first save me in your name, what shall I fear from the one judging in power, whose salvation went before in the name? So too in this place: *Let all the peoples confess to you.* And lest you think anything is to be feared in confession: *Let the nations*, he says, *be glad and exult.* Why, *let them be glad and exult*? *Because you judge the peoples in equity.* No one gives a bribe against us, no one corrupts you, no one deceives you. Therefore be secure. But what of your cause? No one corrupts God; it is manifest: lest perhaps for this reason he be the more to be feared, because he can in no way be corrupted. How, then, are you secure? According to that which has already been said: *O God, save me in your name, and judge me in your power.* So here too: *Let the nations be glad and exult, because you judge the peoples in equity.* And lest the unrighteous fear, he added: *And the nations on earth you will direct.* The nations were crooked, and the nations were tortuous, the nations were perverse; deservedly, on account of their crookedness and distortion and perversity, they feared the judge coming: his hand came, was stretched out mercifully over the peoples; they are directed to walk the right way: what should they fear in the coming judge, who first recognized the corrector? Let them give themselves to his hands; he himself directs the nations on the earth. But the nations being directed, walking in faith, exulting in him, doing good works; and if perhaps any water, since they sail through the sea, enters through the tiniest crevices, through little chinks, into the bilge, by bailing it out through good works, lest by entering more it make a heap, and sink the ship; by bailing daily, by fasting, by praying, by giving alms, by saying with a pure heart: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^23]: by saying these things, walk secure, and exult in the way, sing in the way. Do not fear the judge; before you were faithful, you found a savior. He sought you, ungodly, to redeem you; will he forsake you, redeemed, to destroy you? *And the nations on earth you direct.*

**8.** [vv. 6-7] He exults, rejoices, exhorts, repeats the same verses in exhortation. *Let the peoples confess to you, O God; let all the peoples confess to you: the earth has given its fruit.*[^24] What fruit? *Let all the peoples confess to you.* It was earth; it was full of thorns: the hand of the uprooter came, the call of his majesty and mercy came; the earth began to confess, now the earth gives its fruit. Would it give its fruit, unless it were first rained upon? would the earth give its fruit, unless first the mercy of God came from above? Read to me, you say, that, rained upon, the earth gave its fruit. Hear the Lord raining: *Repent; for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^25]. He rains, and that very rain is thunder; it terrifies: fear the thunderer, and receive the rainer. Behold, after that voice of God thundering and raining; after that voice let us see something from the Gospel itself. Behold, that harlot, having a bad reputation in the city, burst into another's house where she had not been invited by the host, but called by the invited one; not by tongue, but by grace. The sick woman knew she had a place there, where she knew her physician was reclining. She who was a sinner entered; she does not dare to draw near except to the feet: she weeps at the feet, waters them with tears, wipes them with her hair, anoints them with ointment. Why do you marvel? *The earth has given its fruit.* Therefore this was done, the Lord raining there through his own mouth; the things were done which we read in the Gospel: and, he raining through his clouds, the Apostles being sent, and preaching the truth, *the earth* gave its fruit more abundantly; and this crop has now filled the whole world.

**9.** [v. 8] See what is said next: *May God bless us, our God; may God bless us.*[^26] *May he bless,* as I have already said; again and again may he bless, may he multiply by blessing. Let Your Charity attend that the fruit of the earth has already gone before in Jerusalem. For there the Church began: the Holy Spirit came there, and filled the saints gathered into one; miracles were done, they spoke in the tongues of all. They were filled with the Spirit of God, those who were there were converted; fearing, and receiving the divine rain, they gave in confession so great fruit, that they brought all their goods into common, distributing to the poor; that no one might say anything was his own, but all things were common to them, and they had one soul and one heart unto God. For the blood of the Lord which they had shed was forgiven them; it was forgiven, the Lord pardoning, so that now what they had shed they learned even to drink. Great fruit there: *the earth gave its fruit,* and great fruit, and the best fruit. Should that earth alone give its fruit? *May God bless us, our God; may God bless us.* Let him still bless; for blessing is wont especially and properly to be understood in multiplication. Let us prove this in Genesis: see the works of God. God made light; and God divided between the light and the darkness: the light he called day, and the darkness he called night. It is not said: He blessed the light. For the same light returns and alternates by days and nights. He called the firmament heaven between the waters and the waters: it is not said: He blessed the heaven. He separated the sea from the dry land, and named both; the dry land, earth, and the gathering of the waters, sea: nor here is it said: God blessed. He came to those things which were to have the seed of fruitfulness, and exist from the waters. For these have the greatest abundance of multiplication; and the Lord blessed them, saying: *Increase, and multiply, and fill the waters of the sea; and let the flying things multiply over the earth.* So too, when he subjected all things to man, whom he made in his image, it is written: *And God blessed them, saying: Increase and multiply, and fill the face of the earth*[^27]. Therefore blessing avails properly for multiplication, and for filling the face of the earth. Hear in this psalm too: *May God bless us, our God; may God bless us.* And to what end does this blessing avail? *And let all the ends of the earth fear him.* Therefore, my brethren, God has so abundantly blessed us in the name of Christ, that with his sons he fills the whole face of the earth, adopted as coheirs into his kingdom of his Only-begotten. He begot an only Son, and was unwilling that he be one: he begot an only Son, I say, and was unwilling that he remain one. He made him brothers; even if not by begetting, yet by adopting he made him coheirs. He made him a partaker first of our mortality, that we might believe we could be partakers of his divinity.

**10.** Let us attend to our price. All things were foretold, all things are presented, the Gospel goes through the whole world: all the labor of the human race in this time bears witness, all the things that were foretold in the Scriptures are fulfilled. As up to this present day all things have come to pass, so the things that remain are to come to pass. Let us fear the day of judgment; the Lord is to come. He who came humble, will come exalted; he who came to be judged, will come to judge. Let us recognize the humble one, that we may not be terrified at the exalted one; let us embrace the humble one, that we may desire the exalted one. For to those who desire him, he will come propitious. They desire him who hold his faith, and do his commandments. For even if we are unwilling, he will come. Let us, then, be willing that he come, who will come even if we are unwilling. How shall we be willing that he come? By living well, by acting well. Let things past not delight us, let things present not hold us; let us not close the ear, as it were with the tail, let us not press the ear to the earth; lest we be retarded by past things from hearing, lest we be entangled by present things from meditating on the future; let us stretch ourselves to the things that are before, let us forget the things behind. And what we now labor for, what we now groan for, what we now sigh for, what we now speak of, what from however small a part we feel, and cannot grasp, we shall grasp, we shall fully enjoy in the resurrection of the just. Our youth will be renewed like the eagle's; only let us grind down our old age on the rock, Christ. Whether those things are true, brethren, which are said of the serpent, or which are said of the eagle, or whether it is rather a report of men than the truth; yet the truth is in the Scriptures, and not without cause did the Scriptures say this: let us do whatever it signifies, and let us not labor over how true it is. Be such that your youth can be renewed like the eagle's. And know that it cannot be renewed, unless your old age has been ground down on the rock: that is, unless by the help of the rock, unless by the help of Christ, you cannot be renewed. Do not, by the sweetness of past life, grow deaf against the word of God: do not be so held and hindered by present things, that you say: I have no leisure to read, I have no leisure to hear. This is to press the ear to the earth. Do not, then, be such: but be such as you find on the contrary, that is, that you forget the things behind, stretch yourself to the things before; that you grind down your old age on the rock. And if any likenesses are given you; if you find them in the Scriptures, believe; if you find them said only by report, do not greatly believe. The thing itself perhaps is so, perhaps it is not so. Do you make progress; let that likeness avail you unto salvation. Do you not wish to do it through that likeness? Do it through another, provided only you do it; and secure await the kingdom of God, lest your prayer quarrel with you. For you, Christian, when you say: *Your kingdom come*[^28], how do you say: *Your kingdom come*? Shake out your heart: see; behold: *Your kingdom come.* It cries to you: I come: do you not fear? We have often said to Your Charity: both to preach the truth is nothing, if the heart dissents from the tongue; and to hear the truth is nothing, if fruit does not follow the hearing. From this place, as it were higher, we speak to you: but how much we are, in fear, under your feet, God knows, who is propitious to the humble; because the voices of those praising do not delight us so much as the devotion of those confessing and the deeds of those who do rightly. And how we are not delighted except by your advancement, but how we are in peril in these praises, let him know who frees us from all perils, and deigns to recognize and crown us with you, saved from every temptation, in his kingdom.
