# Exposition of Psalm 11

**1.** [v 1.] That the eighth day can be understood as the day of judgment, was said in the sixth psalm: *for the eighth* can also be understood as for the eternal age; because after this time which rolls on in seven days, it will be given to the saints.

**2.** [v 2.] *Save me, Lord, for the holy one has failed*: that is, is not found; as we speak when we say, The grain has failed, or, The money has failed. *Because truths have been diminished from among the sons of men.* Truth is one, by which holy souls are illumined: but since souls are many, in them many truths can be spoken of; as from one face many images appear in mirrors.

**3.** [v 3.] *Each one has spoken vain things to his neighbor.* Neighbor must be understood as every man: because there is no one with whom evil is to be done; and the love of neighbor works no evil. *Deceitful lips, with a heart and a heart they have spoken evils.* What he says twice, *with a heart and a heart*, signifies a double heart.

**4.** [v 4.] *May the Lord destroy all deceitful lips.* *All*, he said, lest anyone think himself excepted, as the Apostle says: *Upon every soul of man who works evil, the Jew first and the Greek*[^1]. *The boastful tongue*: the proud tongue.

**5.** [v 5.] *Who have said: We will magnify our tongue, our lips are our own, who is our Lord?* The proud hypocrites are signified, placing their hope in their own speech for the deceiving of men, and not subject to God.

**6.** [v 6.] *Because of the misery of the needy and the groaning of the poor, now I will rise up, says the Lord*: for so the Lord himself in the Gospel had compassion on his people, because it had no ruler, when it could well obey. Whence it was also said in the Gospel: *The harvest is great, but the laborers are few*[^2]. But this is to be taken from the person of God the Father, who, on account of the needy and the poor, that is, those lacking in poverty and want of spiritual goods, deigned to send his Son; and thence his discourse begins, in Matthew, on the mountain, when he says: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^3]. *I will set in salvation.* He did not say what he sets; but *in salvation* is to be taken as in Christ, according to that: *For my eyes have seen your salvation*[^4]: and therefore he is understood to have set in him that which pertains to the taking away of the misery of the needy and the consoling of the groaning of the poor. *I will act with confidence in him*; according to that in the Gospel: *For he was teaching them as one having authority, not as their scribes*[^5].

**7.** [v 7.] *The words of the Lord, chaste words.* This is the person of the Prophet himself. *The words of the Lord, chaste words*: he says *chaste*, without the corruption of dissimulation. For many preach the truth not chastely, because they sell it for the price of the advantages of this world: of such the Apostle says, that they proclaimed Christ not chastely. *Silver tried by fire for the earth.* The very words of the Lord proved by tribulations for sinners. *Purified sevenfold*: through the fear of God, through piety, through knowledge, through fortitude, through counsel, through understanding, through wisdom. For there are also seven degrees of blessedness, which the Lord follows out in that same discourse which he gave on the mountain according to Matthew: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, blessed are the meek, blessed are those who mourn, blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, blessed are the merciful, blessed are the pure in heart, blessed are the peacemakers*[^6]: from which seven sentences it can be observed that that whole long discourse was spoken; for the eighth, where it was said, *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for righteousness' sake*, signifies the very fire by which the silver is sevenfold proved. When this discourse was ended, it was said, *For he was teaching them as one having authority, not as their scribes*[^7]: which pertains to what was said in this psalm, *I will act with confidence in him.*

**8.** [v 8.] *You, Lord, will keep us, and guard us from this generation and forever*: here as the needy and the poor, there as the wealthy and rich.

**9.** [v 9.] *The impious walk in a circle*: that is, in the desire of temporal things, which rolls on like a wheel by the repeated circuit of seven days; and therefore they do not arrive at the eighth, that is, at the eternal, for which this psalm is entitled. So too it is said through Solomon: *For a wise king is a winnower of the impious, and brings upon them the wheel of evils*[^8]. *According to your height you have multiplied the sons of men.* Because there is also a multiplication in temporal things, which turns away from the unity of God; whence the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that thinks on many things: but the righteous are multiplied according to the height of God, when they go from virtue to virtue.

[^1]: Rom 2:9
[^2]: Matt 9:37
[^3]: Matt 5:3
[^4]: Luke 2:30
[^5]: Matt 7:29
[^6]: Matt 5:3-9
[^7]: Matt 7:29
[^8]: Prov 20:26
