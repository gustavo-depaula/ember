# Exposition of Psalm 149

**1.** [v. 1.] Let us praise the Lord both in voice, and in understanding, and in good work; and as this psalm exhorts us, let us sing to him a new song. For so it begins: *Sing to the Lord a new song.* The old man, the old song: the new man, the new song. The Old Testament, the old song: the New Testament, the new song. In the Old Testament the promises are temporal and earthly. Whoever loves earthly things sings the old song: he who wishes to sing the new song, let him love eternal things. Love itself is new and eternal: therefore always new, because it never grows old. For if you consider well, this is ancient: how, then, is it new? Is eternal life, my brothers, just now born? Eternal life is Christ himself, and according to his divinity he was not just now born: because: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^1]. If the things which were made through him are ancient; what is he himself, through whom they were made? What, but eternal, and coeternal with the Father? But we, having fallen into sin, have come to oldness. For ours is the voice in that psalm where it is said with groaning: *I have grown old among all my enemies*[^2]. Man grows old through sin, he is made new through grace. All, therefore, who are made new in Christ, that they may begin to belong to eternal life, sing the new song.

**2.** And this song is the song of peace, this song is the song of love. Whoever separates himself from the company of the saints does not sing the new song. For he has followed the old animosity, not the new love. What is there in the new love? Peace, the bond of holy society, the spiritual joining together, the building of living stones. Where is this? Not in one place, but throughout the whole world. Hear this from another psalm: it says thus: *Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^3]. From this it is understood that he who does not sing with all the earth sings the old song, whatever words proceed from his mouth. For what do I attend to what he sounds, when I see what he thinks? And do you, you say, see what he thinks? Deeds indicate it. For the eye does not penetrate into the conscience. I attend to what he does, and there I understand what he thinks. For if anyone, for example, has caught a man in theft, in murder, in adultery, he does not see his thoughts in his heart, but in his deeds. There are certain things which lie hidden within; but there are also many which go forth into works, and become manifest even to men. Since, then, there were those who separated themselves from the joining together of the love of Christ and from the society of the holy Church, evil within among themselves, no one knew but God. Temptation came; it separated them, and laid open to men what God knew. For the fruit is not shown except in deeds. Whence it is said: *By their fruits you will know them*[^4]. For the Lord said to certain men who clothe themselves in the clothing of sheep, but within are ravening wolves[^5]; and lest perhaps human frailty be unable to discern the wolf under the sheepskin, he says: *By their fruits you will know them.* We seek the fruits of love, we find the thorns of dissension. *By their fruits you will know them.* Therefore their song is old: let us sing the new song. We have already said, brothers, all the earth sings the new song. He who does not sing the new song with all the earth, let him say what he will, let him sound Alleluia with his tongues, let him say it all day, let him say it all night; my ears are not much inclined to the voice of the one singing, but I seek the morals of the one working. For I ask, and I say: What is it that you sing? He answers: Alleluia. What is Alleluia? Praise the Lord. Come, let us praise the Lord together. If you praise the Lord and I praise the Lord; why are we in discord? Love praises the Lord, discord blasphemes the Lord.

**3.** And do you now wish to know where you should sing the new song? See how the things he is going to say in this very Psalm come about, and where they come about; whether these things come about among all nations, or in some one part of the earth: and from this you will more fully understand to whom the new song belongs. Now indeed that is manifest which I recalled from another psalm: *Sing to the Lord a new song*: and to show that in the new song there is the fruit of love and unity, he added: *Sing to the Lord, all the earth.* Let no one separate himself, let no one disjoin himself: you are wheat, bear the chaff until it is winnowed. Do you wish to be shaken out of the floor? Even if you were wheat outside the threshing-floor, the birds would find you, and gather you up[^6]. Add that by the very fact that you depart and fly, you show yourself to be chaff: and because you were light, the wind came, and you were carried off from beneath the feet of the oxen. But those who are wheat bear the threshing; they rejoice, because they are grains, they groan amid the chaff, they await the winnower, whom they acknowledge as their redeemer. *Sing to the Lord a new song: his praise in the Church of the saints.* This is the Church of the saints, the Church of the grains spread throughout the whole world; sown through the field of the Lord, which is this world, the Lord himself expounding it, when he spoke of the sower: that *a man sowed good seed in his field, and the enemy came, and oversowed weeds; and the servants said to the householder: Did you not sow good seed in your field? whence then are these weeds? He answered: An enemy man has done this*[^7]. They wished to gather the weeds, and he restrained them, saying: *Let both grow until the harvest; and at the time of the harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather first the weeds, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather my wheat into the barn*[^8]. Afterward the disciples asked him, saying: *Explain to us the parable of the weeds*[^9]. And he himself expounds all things, that no man may ascribe to his own heart what he has understood there, but to the Master of heaven who expounds it. Let no one say: He has expounded it to himself as he wished. If the Lord were to expound the parable of the prophet, when he himself was speaking through them what they said; who would dare to say that it ought not to be expounded so? Much more, when he himself has expounded what he himself proposed, who dares to contradict the manifest truth? When, then, the Lord expounded this parable, he said: *But he who sows the good seed is the Son of man*[^10]: showing surely himself. *But the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom*[^11]: this is the Church of the saints. *But the weeds are the sons of the evil one. The field is this world*[^12]. Now see, brothers, that throughout the world the good seed has been sown, and throughout the world the weeds have been sown. Is the wheat in one part, and the weeds in another part? Throughout the whole is wheat, and throughout the whole are weeds. The field of the Lord is the world, not Africa. It is not as with these lands of ours: Gaetulia bears sixtyfold or a hundredfold, and Numidia bears tenfold: not so is God's field to him. Everywhere fruit is borne to him, both a hundredfold, and sixtyfold, and thirtyfold: only see what you wish to be, if you think to belong to the Lord's fruit. Therefore the Church of the saints is the catholic Church. The Church of the saints is not the Church of the heretics. The Church of the saints is that which God foresignified before it was seen; and he displayed it, that it might be seen. The Church of the saints was before in the books, now in the nations: the Church of the saints was before only read of, now both read of and seen. When it was only read of, it was believed: now it is seen, and contradicted! *His praise in the Church of the saints.*

**4.** [v. 2.] *Let Israel rejoice in him who made him.* What is Israel? Seeing God: for so the name Israel is interpreted. He who sees God, let him rejoice in him by whom he was made. What, then, is it, brothers? because we have said that we belong to the Church of the saints, do we now already see God? How are we Israel, if we do not see? There is a certain vision of this time; there will be another vision of the future time: the vision which now is, is through faith; the vision which is to come, will be through sight. If we believe, we see; if we love, we see. What do we see? God. Where is God himself? Ask John: *God is love*[^13]. Let us bless his holy name; and let us rejoice in God, if we rejoice in love. Whoever has love, why do we send him afar, that he may see God? Let him attend to his own conscience, and there he sees God. If love does not dwell there, God does not dwell there: but if love dwells there, God dwells there. Perhaps he wishes to see him sitting in heaven; let him have love, and he dwells in him as in heaven. Therefore let us be Israel, and let us rejoice in him who made us: *Let Israel rejoice in him who made him.* Let him rejoice in him who made him, not in Arius, not in Donatus, not in Caecilian; not in Proculian, not in Augustine: *Let him rejoice in him who made him.* We, brothers, do not commend ourselves; but we commend God to you, because we commend you to God. How do we commend God to you? That you may love him for your own good, not for his good: because it is to your harm that you do not love him, not to his harm. For God will not have less divinity, if man have not love toward him. You grow from God, not he from you: and yet he so loved us first[^14], before we loved him, that he sent his only Son to die for us[^15]. He who made us was made among us. How did he himself make us? *All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^16]. How was he made among us? *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^17]. Therefore it is he in whom we ought to rejoice. Let no man claim for himself his parts; from him is the gladness which makes us happy: *Let Israel rejoice in him who made him.*

**5.** *And let the sons of Sion exult in their king.* This same Israel are the sons of the Church. For Sion was indeed one city, which fell: in its remains the saints dwelt indeed for a time; but the true Sion and the true Jerusalem (because that Sion is the Jerusalem) is eternal in the heavens, which is our mother[^18]. She herself bore us, she herself is the Church of the saints, she herself nourished us; in part a pilgrim, in great part abiding in heaven. In the part by which she abides in heaven, she is the blessedness of the Angels: in the part by which she is a pilgrim in this world, she is the hope of the just. Of the former it is said: *Glory to God in the highest*: of the latter it is said: *And on earth peace to men of good will*[^19]. Those, then, who in this life groan, and desire that homeland, let them run by love, not by the feet of the body: let them not seek ships, but wings; let them lay hold of the two wings of love. What are the two wings of love? The love of God, and of neighbor. For we are pilgrims, we sigh, we groan. A letter has come to us from our homeland; we read it to you.

**6.** *Let Israel rejoice in him who made him, and let the sons of Sion exult in their king.* What he said, *who made him*; this he said, *in their king.* What you heard, *Israel*; that they are, *the sons of Sion*: and what you heard, *in him who made him*; this is, *in their king.* The Son of God who made us was made among us: and our King rules us, because our Creator made us: and therefore we are Christians, because he is Christ. Christ is named from chrism, that is, from anointing. Now kings and priests were anointed[^20]: but he was anointed both King and Priest[^21]. As King he fought for us, as Priest he offered himself for us. When he fought for us, he was as it were conquered; but in truth he conquered. For he was crucified, and from his cross, on which he was fixed, he slew the devil: and thence he is our King. But whence is he Priest? Because he offered himself for us. Give the Priest something to offer. What could man find to give as a clean victim? What victim? What clean thing can a sinner bring forth? O unjust, O impious one! whatever you bring is unclean, and something clean must be offered for you. Seek in yourself what you may offer; you will not find it. Seek out of yourself what you may offer; he is not delighted with rams, nor with goats, nor with bulls. All things are his, even if you do not offer them. Offer him, then, a clean sacrifice. But you are a sinner, you are impious, but you have a defiled conscience. Perhaps you might be able to offer something clean, once purified; but that you may be purified, something must be offered for you. What, then, will you offer for yourself, that you may be cleansed? If you are cleansed, you will be able to offer what is clean. Let a clean priest, then, offer himself, and cleanse. This is what Christ did. He found nothing clean in men, which he might offer for men; he offered himself, a clean victim. Happy victim, true victim, immaculate offering! Therefore he did not offer that which we gave him: rather he offered that which he received from us, and he offered it clean. For he received flesh from us, this he offered. But whence did he receive it? From the womb of the Virgin Mary, that he might offer something clean for the unclean. He himself King, he himself Priest; in him let us rejoice.

**7.** [v. 3.] *Let them praise his name in the choir.* And what does the choir signify? Many know what a choir is; and because we speak in a city, nearly all know. A choir is the agreement of singers. If we sing in a choir, let us sing in harmony. In a choir of singers, whoever is discordant in voice offends the hearing, and disturbs the choir. If the voice of one singing unfittingly disturbs the harmony of those singing, how does heresy, sounding discordant, disturb the harmony of those praising? The choir of Christ is now the whole world. The choir of Christ sounds in harmony from the east to the west. Let us see whether the choir of Christ extends so far. Another psalm says: *From the rising of the sun to its setting, praise the name of the Lord*[^22]. *Let them praise his name in the choir.*

**8.** *Let them sing to him on the timbrel and the psaltery.* Why does he take up the timbrel and the psaltery? That not the voice alone may praise, but also the works. When the timbrel and the psaltery are taken up, the hands join in concert with the voice. So too you, whenever you sing Alleluia, hold out bread to the hungry, clothe the naked, take in the stranger; not the voice alone sounds, but the hand too sounds in concert, because deeds accord with words. You have taken up an instrument, and the fingers agree with the tongue. And the very mystery of the timbrel and the psaltery is not to be passed over in silence. In the timbrel a hide is stretched, in the psaltery strings are stretched: in either instrument the flesh is crucified. How well he sang on the timbrel and the psaltery who said: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^23]! The psaltery itself, like the timbrel, he who loves the new song wishes you to take up, he who teaches you when he says to you: *He who wishes to be my disciple, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me*[^24]. Let him not lay down his psaltery, let him not lay down his timbrel: let him be stretched on the wood, and dried out from the concupiscence of the flesh. The strings, the more they are stretched, the more sharply they sound. That the psaltery of the apostle Paul might sound sharply, what did he say? *Forgetting the things that are behind, stretched out to the things that are before, I follow on toward the palm of the supernal calling*[^25]. He stretched himself out; Christ touched him, and the sweetness of truth sounded. *Let them sing to him on the timbrel and the psaltery.*

**9.** [v. 4.] *For the Lord has done good among his people.* What is so beneficent as to die for the impious? what is so beneficent as by just blood to blot out the bond of the sinner? what is so beneficent as to say: It does not pertain to me what you have been; be what you have not been? *The Lord has done good among his people*, by forgiving sins, by promising eternal life. He does good by turning back the one who is turned away, by helping the one who fights, by crowning the one who conquers: *the Lord has done good among his people. And he will exalt the meek in salvation.* For the proud too are exalted, but not in salvation. The meek in salvation, the proud in death: that is, the proud exalt themselves, and the Lord humbles them; but the meek humble themselves, and God exalts them. *And he will exalt the meek in salvation.*

**10.** [v. 5.] *The saints will exult in glory.* I wish to say something; listen more attentively concerning the glory of the saints. For there is no one who does not love glory. But the glory of fools, that popular glory so called, has the enticement of deception, so that each one, moved toward the praises of vain men, wishes so to live that he may be acclaimed by men of whatever sort, in whatever way. Hence men made insane, and puffed up with conceit, empty within, swollen without, even wish to lose their goods, by giving to stage-players, actors, hunters, charioteers. How much they give! how much they spend! They pour out the strength, not only of their estate, but even of their soul. These men disdain the poor man, because the people do not cry out that the poor man should receive; but the people cry out that the hunter should receive. Therefore, where there is no acclaiming for them, they are unwilling to give: where there is acclaiming by the insane, they go mad; and all become insane, both he who is watched, and he who watches, and he who gives. This insane glory is reproved by the Lord, it is disapproved before the eyes of the Almighty. And yet, my brothers, Christ thus reproaches his own, and says: I have not received from you as much as the hunters received; and that you might give to them, you gave from what is mine: but I *was naked, and you did not clothe me.* And they: *And when did we see you naked, and did not clothe you?* And he: *When you did not do it to one of the least of mine, neither did you do it to me*[^26]. But you wish to clothe him who pleases you: in what does Christ displease you? You wish to clothe the hunter, by whose being conquered you will perhaps blush: Christ is never conquered; both the devil he conquered, and for you he conquered, and for your sake he conquered, and in you he conquered. Such a conqueror you are unwilling to clothe. Why? Because there is less acclaiming, because there is less raving. For this reason those who are delighted with such glory have nothing in their conscience. As they empty their coffers, that they may send garments; so they empty their very conscience, that they may have nothing precious there.

**11.** [v. 6.] But how the saints who exult in glory may exult, there is no need for us to say; hear the following verse of the Psalm itself: *The saints will exult in glory: they will rejoice in their beds.* Not in theaters, not in amphitheaters, not in circuses, not in trifles, not in the marketplaces; but, *in their beds.* What is, *in their beds*? In their hearts. Hear the apostle Paul exulting in his bed: *For our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience*[^27]. Again it must be feared lest anyone become self-pleasing within himself, and as it were proud, glory in his conscience. For each one ought to exult with trembling[^28]; because it is the gift of God whence he exults, not his own merit. For indeed there are many self-pleasing, and judging themselves to be just; and another page comes forth against them, saying: *Who will glory that he has a chaste heart; or who will glory that he is clean from sin?*[^29] There is, then, a certain measure of glorying in the conscience, that you may know your faith to be sincere, may know your hope to be certain, may know your love to be without pretense. But since there are perhaps still many things which can offend the eyes of God, praise God who gave you these things; then he will perfect what he gave. For this reason, when he had said: *They will rejoice in their beds*; lest they seem as it were self-pleasing, he immediately added: *The exaltations of God in their throats.* Let them so rejoice in their beds that they do not ascribe to themselves that they are good, but praise him from whom they received what they are, by whom they are called that they may attain to what they are not yet, and from whom they hope for perfection; to whom they give thanks, because he has begun: *The exaltations of God in their throats.* Now see the saints, see their glory, see throughout the whole world, see that *the exaltations of God* are *in their throats.*

**12.** *And two-edged swords in their hands.* A "framea" is what people commonly call a "spatha." For there are swords sharp on one side, and these are "machaerae." But these same "frameae" are also called "romphaeae," and also "spathae." This kind of weapon has a great mystery, that it is sharp on both sides. These *two-edged swords* are *in their hands.* By two-edged swords we understand the word of the Lord: and there is one sword; but it is called many, because there are many mouths and many tongues of the saints. The word of God, then, is a two-edged sword[^30]. Whence two-edged? It speaks of temporal things, it speaks of eternal things. In both it proves what it says, and the one it strikes it separates from the world. Is it not the very sword of which the Lord says: *I did not come to send peace upon the earth, but a sword*[^31]? Attend to how he comes to disjoin, how he comes to separate. He disjoins the saints, he disjoins the impious, he separates from you what hinders you. The son wishes to serve God, the father does not wish it: the sword comes, the word of God comes; it divides the son from the father. The daughter wishes it, the mother does not wish it; by the sword they are divided from one another. The daughter-in-law wishes it, the mother-in-law does not wish it; let the two-edged sword come, let it bring the promise of the present and the future life, the consolation of temporal things, the full enjoyment of eternal things. Behold the sword sharp on both sides, promising temporal and eternal things. In what did it deceive us? Was not the Church of God not throughout the whole world? Behold, it is. Before, it was read of, and not seen: now as it is read of, so it is beheld. Whatever was promised us temporally pertains to one edge of the sword: whatever was promised forever pertains to the other edge of the sword. You have the hope of future things, you have the consolation of present things; do not be drawn back by him who wishes to draw you back: let it be father, mother, sister, wife, let it be a friend, let him not draw you back; and the two-edged sword will be useful to you. He separates you usefully, you glue yourself together badly. Our Lord came, then, bearing a two-edged sword, promising eternal things, fulfilling temporal things. For this reason also there are said to be two Testaments. What, then, were the *two-edged swords in their hands*? The two Testaments pertain to the two-edged sword: the Old Testament promises earthly things, the New eternal things. In both the word of God was found truthful, like a two-edged sword. Why, then, in their hands, and not on their tongues? *And two-edged swords*, he says, *in their hands. In their hands* he said, that is, in their power. They received, then, the word of God in their power, that where they wished they might speak, to whom they wished they might speak; they would not fear power, they would not despise poverty. For in their hands they had the sword, which they brandished where they wished, turned, struck: and all this was in the power of those who preached. For if the word is not in the hands—lest perhaps someone say: How is the word a two-edged sword, and how in the hands? if, then, the word is not in the hands, why is it written: *The word of the Lord came in the hand of the prophet Haggai*[^32]? Did God, brothers, make his word in his fingers? What is, *it came in his hand*? It was given to him in his power to preach the word of the Lord. Finally, we can also understand these hands otherwise. For those who spoke had the word of God on the tongue; those who wrote, in their hands: *And two-edged swords in their hands.*

**13.** [v. 7.] Now, brothers, you see the saints armed; attend to the slaughters, attend to the glorious battles. For if there is a commander, there is also a soldier; if a soldier, also an enemy; if war, also victory. What did these men do, having in their hands two-edged swords? *To work vengeance among the nations.* See whether vengeance has not been worked among the nations. Daily it is done; this we too do by speaking. Attend to how the nations of Babylon have been cut down. To her is rendered double: for so it is written of her: *Render to her double, according to what she has done*[^33]. How is double rendered? The saints make war, they draw forth two-edged swords; slaughters come about, killings, separations: how is double rendered to her? When she could persecute Christians, she killed the flesh, she did not break God: now double is rendered to her; both the pagans are destroyed, and the idols are broken. How, you say, are the pagans killed? How, except when they become Christians? I seek the pagan, I do not find him; he is a Christian: therefore the pagan is dead. For if they are not killed, whence was it said to Peter: *Kill and eat*[^34]? Whence was Saul himself killed as a persecutor, and Paul raised up as a preacher? I seek Saul the persecutor, and do not find him; he is killed. Whence? By the two-edged sword. But because he was killed in himself, and made alive in Christ, therefore confidently he says: *But now it is not I who live; but Christ lives in me*[^35]. As it happens to him, so through him. For indeed when he had been made a preacher, he too received in his hands a two-edged sword, *to work vengeance among the nations.* And lest you really suppose that men are struck with iron, that blood is shed, that wounds are made in the flesh, there follows, and he expounds it: *Rebukes among the peoples.* What is a rebuke? A correction. Let a two-edged sword come forth from you, do not cease; God has given it to you according to your small measure. What kind of man are you, who worship idols? Say to your friend, if at least there has remained one to whom you may say it: What kind of man are you, who have forsaken him by whom you were made, and adore what you made? The smith is better than what the smith fashions. If you are ashamed to adore the smith, are you not ashamed of what the smith made? When he begins to be ashamed, when he begins to be pricked, you have made a wound with the sword; it has reached the heart, he is to die that he may live: *Two-edged swords in their hands, to work vengeance among the nations, rebukes among the peoples.*

**14.** [v. 8.] *To bind their kings in fetters, and their nobles in iron chains. To work upon them the judgment that is written.* We have easily expounded how by the swords they fall that they may rise, are separated that they may be gathered, are wounded that they may be healed, die that they may live: but what shall we do? how shall we expound: *To bind their kings in fetters*? The kings of the nations are to be bound in fetters; *and their nobles in chains*, he added, *iron.* Be present, that you may recognize what you know. For these verses which we have begun to expound are obscure; but that which I am going to say from them is not new. It is already known to you; now you do not have need to learn, but to be reminded. For God willed to set down certain of his verses obscurely, not that something new might be dug out of them; but that what was already usual might be renewed by the expounding of obscure things. We know kings made Christians, we know nobles of the nations made Christians. They are even today, and have been, and will be; and the two-edged swords do not cease in the hands of the saints. How, then, do we understand them bound in fetters and iron chains? Your love and learning knows (because you have been nourished in the Church, and are accustomed to hear the divine readings) that *God chose the weak things of the world, that he might confound the strong; and God chose the foolish things of the world, to confound the wise; and the things that are not, as the things that are, that the things that are might be made void.* For the Apostle says: *For consider your calling, brothers, that not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble; but God chose the foolish things of the world, and the weak things of the world, that he might confound the strong; and the base things and the contemptible God chose, and the things that are not, as the things that are, that the things that are might be made void*[^36]. God, Christ, came to profit all: but he chose to profit the emperor through the fisherman, not the fisherman through the emperor; and he chose the things that were of no account in the world. He filled them with his Spirit, gave them two-edged swords, commanded them to preach the Gospel, and to go through the whole world[^37]. The world raged, the lion raised itself up against the lamb: but the lamb was found stronger than the lion. The lion was conquered by raging, the lamb conquered by suffering. The hearts of men were turned to the fear of Christ; kings began, nobles began to be moved by miracles, to be troubled by the fulfillment of prophecy, to see the human race running together into one name. And what were they to do? Many chose lowliness, and forsaking their houses, distributing their substance to the poor, ran to perfection. For to such an imperfect one it is said by the Lord: *If you wish to be perfect, go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor; and come, follow me, and you will have treasure in heaven*[^38]. Many of the nobles did this: but these ceased to be nobles of the nations; they chose poverty in the world, nobility in Christ. But many keep that very nobility, keep royal powers, and yet are Christians. They are as it were in fetters, and as it were in iron chains. Whence is this? Lest they go on to unlawful things, they received fetters; the fetters of wisdom, the fetters of the word of God[^39].

**15.** Why, then, iron chains, and not golden chains? They are iron as long as they fear; let them love, and they will be golden. Let your love attend to what I say. You have just heard the apostle John: *Fear is not in love; but perfect love casts out fear, because fear has torment*[^40]. This is the iron chain. And yet unless a man begin through fear to worship God, he will not attain to love. The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord[^41]. He begins, then, from iron chains, and ends at a golden collar. For it is said of Wisdom: *And a golden collar around your neck*[^42]. He would not put a golden collar on you, unless he had first bound you in iron fetters. You began from fear, you are perfected unto wisdom. How many there are who for this reason are unwilling to do evil, because they fear the hells, because they fear torments? They do not yet love justice. If impunity were promised them, and it were said to them: Do what you wish in security, it will go unpunished for you; they would send forth the goings of their lusts into all the most wicked things: and especially, my brothers, especially kings and nobles, to whom it is not easily said: What have you done? For a poor man, even if he does not fear God; because he is of no strength, of no resources; lest, when he has stirred himself, he be hurried off to punishment, he ceases through the fear of man, even if not of God. But the mighty of the world, kings, nobles, unless they fear God, what will they fear? But it is preached to them, and they are struck with the two-edged sword. It is said to them that there is one who sets some at the right hand, others at the left; that he may say to those who are on the left: *Go into eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^43]. They do not yet love justice, but they fear punishment; and by fearing punishment, they have already received fetters, and are trained in iron chains. There comes to us a man mighty in the world; his wife has offended him, or perhaps he has desired another more beautiful, or another richer: he wishes to dismiss her whom he has, and does not do it. He hears from a servant of God, he hears from a prophet, he hears from an apostle, and does not do it; he hears from him in whose hands is the two-edged sword: You shall not do it, it is not lawful for you, God does not permit you to dismiss your wife, except for the cause of fornication[^44]: he hears this, he fears, and does not do it. His light feet were already going forth into a fall, they are held by fetters; he has iron chains, he fears God. It is said to him: God will damn you, if you do it; he is judge over all, he hears the groan of your wife, you will be held guilty in his sight. By this desire he is allured, by this punishment he is deterred. He was going to consent to a depraved desire, had not the iron chains held him back. What is more, if he says: I wish now to be continent, I do not now wish a wife: You cannot. What, if you wish it, and she does not? ought she to become a fornicator through your continence? If she marries another while you live, she will be an adulteress. God does not wish to make up such a loss by such a gain. Render the debt; even if you do not exact it, render it. For perfect sanctification God will reckon it to you, if you do not exact what is owed, but render what is owed to your wife. You fear, you do not do it; your chains are shaken. Hear that you are bound with iron chains: *Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek to be loosed*[^45]. It is hard, it is iron. For when the Lord says this, he shows that the chain is iron: (young men, hear: they are iron chains, do not put your feet in; when you have put them in, you will be more tightly bound with the fetters. Such fetters even the hands of the bishop make firm for you. Do not the fettered flee into the Church, and are loosed here? Men flee here wishing to dismiss their wives; here they are more tightly bound: no one looses these fetters). *What God has joined, let not man separate*[^46]. But the chains are hard. Who does not know it? The Apostles grieved at that very hardness, and said: *If such is the case with a wife, it is not expedient to marry*[^47]. If they are iron chains, there is no need for feet to be put there. And the Lord: *Not all grasp this word; but let him who can grasp it, grasp it*[^48]. *Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek to be loosed*; because you are bound with iron chains. *Are you loosed from a wife? do not seek a wife*[^49]; do not bind yourself with iron chains.

**16.** [v. 9.] *To work upon them the judgment that is written.* This is the judgment which the saints work throughout all nations. Why *written*? Because these things were written beforehand, and now are fulfilled. Behold, now they come about: once they were read of, and did not come about. And he concluded thus: *This glory is to all his saints.* Throughout the whole world, throughout all nations the saints do this, thus are they glorified: thus they exalt God in their throats, thus they rejoice in their beds, thus they exult in their glory, thus they are exalted in salvation, thus they sing the new song, thus they say Alleluia, with heart, mouth, life. Amen.
