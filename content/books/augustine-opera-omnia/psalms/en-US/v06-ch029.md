# Exposition of Psalm 148

**1.** [v. 1.] The meditation of our present life ought to be in the praise of God: because the everlasting exultation of our future life will be the praise of God; and no one can be made fit for the future life who has not first exercised himself for it. Now, then, we praise God: but we also ask of God. Our praise has gladness, our prayer groaning. For something has been promised us which we do not yet have; and because he who promised is truthful, we rejoice in hope: yet because we do not yet have it, we groan in desire. It is good for us to persevere in desire until what was promised comes, and the groaning passes, and praise alone succeeds. On account of these two times—one which is now, in the temptations and tribulations of this life, the other which will be then, in security and perpetual exultation—a celebration of two seasons has been instituted for us as well, before Easter and after Easter. That which is before Easter signifies the tribulation in which we now are: but that which we now keep after Easter signifies the blessedness in which we will afterward be. Before Easter, then, what we celebrate, this we also live: but after Easter, what we celebrate, we signify what we do not yet hold. For this reason we exercise that season in fastings and prayers; but this season, the fasts relaxed, we keep in praises. For this is the *Alleluia* which we sing; which in Latin is interpreted, as you know, "Praise the Lord." Therefore that season is before the resurrection, this season is after the resurrection of the Lord. By which season is signified the future life, which we do not yet hold; because what we signify after the resurrection of the Lord, we will hold after our own resurrection. For in our head both have been figured for us, both have been shown. The Passion of the Lord shows us the life of present necessity, because we must labor, and be troubled, and at the last die: but the resurrection and glorification of the Lord shows us the life we are to receive, when he comes to render fitting things to those who are fit, evil things to the evil, good things to the good. And indeed now all the evil can sing with us *Alleluia*: but if they persevere in their malice, they will be able to say with their lips the song of our future life; yet the life itself, which will then be in that truth which is now signified, they cannot obtain; because they were unwilling to meditate, before it came, and to hold what was to come.

**2.** Now, then, brothers, we exhort you to praise God; and this is what we all say to ourselves when we say *Alleluia.* Praise the Lord, you say to another, and he himself says it to you: when all exhort one another, all do what they exhort. But praise from your whole selves; that is, let not your tongue and voice alone praise God, but also your conscience, your life, your deeds. For we praise now in church when we are gathered; when each one departs to his own affairs, he seems to cease praising God. Let him not cease to live well, and he always praises God. You cease to praise God then, when you turn aside from justice and from what pleases him. For if you never turn aside from the good life, your tongue is silent, your life cries out; and the ears of God are toward your heart. For as our ears are toward our voices, so the ears of God are toward our thoughts. But it cannot happen that he have evil deeds who has good thoughts. For deeds proceed from thought; nor can anyone do anything, or move his members to do anything, unless first the command of thought has gone before: just as from the inner palace, whatever the Emperor commands goes out through the Roman empire, whatever you see done throughout the provinces. How great a movement comes about at one command of the Emperor sitting within? He alone moves his lips when he speaks; and the whole province is moved when what he speaks is done. So too in each man there is within an emperor, sitting in the heart: if he is good, he commands good things, and good things are done; if he is evil, he commands evil things, and evil things are done. When Christ sits there, what can he command but good things? When the devil takes possession, what can he command but evil things? But God willed it to be in your choice, for whom you prepare a place, for God or for the devil: when you have prepared it, he who takes possession will himself command. Therefore, brothers, do not attend only to the sound: when you praise God, praise with the whole; let the voice sing, let the life sing, let the deeds sing. Even if there is still groaning, tribulation, temptation; hope that all things will pass, and that the day will come on which we will praise without failing. Behold, this psalm, since it is plain, must be run through by us. For it distributes the whole creation as praising God, and as it were exhorts it to praise, as though it had found it silent.

**3.** *Praise the Lord from the heavens.* As though he had found the praise of the Lord silent in the heavens, he exhorts them to rise and praise. Never have the heavenly things been silent in the praises of their Creator, never have the earthly things ceased to praise God. But plainly there are certain things which have a spirit of praising God in that affection by which God pleases them. For no one praises except what pleases him. But there are other things which do not have a spirit of life and understanding for praising God, yet because they too are good, and are wholly ordered in their own rank, and are referred to the beauty of the universe which God created; they indeed do not of themselves, by their own voice and their own heart, praise God; but when they are considered by those who understand, God is praised through them; and when God is praised through them, in a certain way they too praise God. For example, all things which have a spirit of life, and a pure understanding for contemplating him and loving him without disgust or failing, praise God in heaven. But on earth there praise God, through the understanding that discerns good and evil, through the understanding that knows the creation and the Creator, those men who think on these things, to whom the Lord has granted a mind for discerning these things, for delighting in them and praising. Men can do this: do cattle have any such understanding? If cattle had such understanding, God would not say to us: *Do not be like the horse and the mule, who have no understanding*[^1]. When he exhorts us not to be like cattle, without understanding, he shows us that he has granted understanding to man for praising God. Do trees even have life itself, by which they might feel like cattle? For cattle, even though they do not have the inner rational sense and the understanding and discerning mind which man has for praising God, yet have a manifest life, as we all know, of seeking food, of taking what is useful, of rejecting what is harmful; senses for discerning corporeal things; sight for colors, hearing for sounds, smell for odors, taste for flavors, motions either toward pleasures or away from troubles. We understand these things, and see them before our eyes. They do not have reason for understanding; but they have the spirit of an animated body, and a manifest life: but trees do not have even this; and yet all things praise God. Why do they praise God? Because when we see these things, and consider the Creator who made them, from them the praise of God is born in us; and when God is praised by the consideration of them, all things praise God. He began, then, from heaven: all things praise, and he says: *Praise.* Why, when they praise, does he say: *Praise*? Because he was delighted that they praise, and it pleased him as it were to add his own exhortation. As if you should come to men doing some good work with joy, whether in the vineyard, or in the harvest, or in some farm labor; what they do pleases you, and you say: Do it, go on: not that they should then begin to do it when you speak; but because what you have found them doing pleases you, you add your congratulation and your exhortation. For by saying: Do it, and by exhorting those who do it, you do it as it were with them by your wish. In this exhortation, then, the Prophet, full of the Spirit, says these things.

**4.** And it is a Psalm *of Haggai and Zechariah*: so the title has it. These two prophets, at the time when the people were held captive in Babylon, were prophesying that the end of the captivity was now to come, that the city of Jerusalem, which had fallen in war, might be restored[^2]. They signified to us, then, in mystery the future life, where we will praise God after the captivity of the present life; where there will be the renewal of that great city Jerusalem, for which we sigh as pilgrims, still held captive under the burden and load of the mortal body; for which we still groan in our pilgrimage, but will exult in the homeland. But he who does not groan as a pilgrim will not rejoice as a citizen; because there is no desire in him. These holy prophets, then, offered great consolation at that time to the people held captive according to the flesh, that is, set in Babylon under foreign kings. For they showed by prophecy the future time of liberation from captivity, of the restoration of Jerusalem. But all those things were done in figure[^3]; they have their truth: they were figured in the men of old, they are shown as present in us. Now, then, what does the Apostle say? *As long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord*[^4]. We are not yet in the homeland: when shall we be in the homeland? When we will triumph, the enemy devil conquered, when *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed: then will come to pass the word which is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your striving? where, O death, is your sting?*[^5] When, then, will there be no striving of death, which now there is, and makes us groan from the failing and changeableness of things, from the frailty of human flesh? Temptations contend with us daily, delights contend daily: even if we do not consent, yet we suffer trouble, and we contend, and there is great danger that he who contends be conquered; but if by not consenting we conquer, yet we suffer trouble in resisting the delights. The enemy does not cease, and does not die, except in the resurrection of the dead. But let us presume, let us trust; Haggai and Zechariah raise us up; they sing of our future liberation. If they sang to that people, and it was fulfilled; what is sung to the Christian people, will it not be fulfilled? Be secure: only see how you act in this pilgrimage of life. Let not the love of Babylon please you, lest you forget the city of Jerusalem. Even though your body is still held in Babylon, let your heart be sent ahead to Jerusalem. Let the whole creation, then, praise the Lord; because we are going to do there what here we meditate beforehand.

**5.** *Praise the Lord from the heavens; praise him in the heights.* First he speaks of the heavens, afterward of the earth: for God is praised who made heaven and earth. The heavenly things are tranquil, they are at peace; there is always joy, no death, no sickness, no trouble; the blessed always praise God: but we are still below, yet when we think how God is praised there, let us have our heart there, and not without cause hear: Lift up your hearts. Let us lift our heart upward, lest it rot on the earth; since what the Angels do there pleases us. Now we do so in hope; afterward in reality, when we will have come there. *Praise him*, therefore, *in the heights.*

**6.** [vv. 2-5.] *Praise him, all his angels, praise him, all his hosts. Praise him, sun and moon, praise him, all you stars and light. Praise him, you heavens of heavens, and let the waters that are above the heavens praise the name of the Lord.* When does he unfold all things by counting them? But nevertheless in sum and as it were he has touched on all things, and concluded all the heavenly things as praising their Creator.

**7.** And as if it were said to him: Why do they praise him? what do they owe him, what has he bestowed on them, that they should praise him? there follows: *For he spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created.* It is no wonder if the works praise the worker, no wonder if the things made praise the maker, no wonder if the creation praises the Creator. There Christ too was named, and as it were we did not hear his name. Who is Christ? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^6]. Through what were they made? Through the Word. How does he show here that they were made through the Word? *He spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created.* No one speaks, no one commands, except by a word.

**8.** [v. 6.] *He established them for ever, and for ages of ages.* All the heavenly things, all the higher things, all the hosts and the Angels, a certain supernal city, good, holy, blessed: from which, because we are pilgrims, we are still wretched; and to which we are to return, blessed in hope; and where, when we will have returned, blessed in reality. *He established them for ever, and for ages of ages: he gave a command, and it will not pass away.* What kind of command do you think the heavenly things and the holy Angels have? what command did God give them? What, except that they praise him? Blessed are they whose business this is, to praise God. They do not plow, they do not sow, they do not grind, they do not cook: for these are works of necessity; there there is no necessity. They do not steal, they do not plunder, they do not commit adultery: for these are works of iniquity; there there is no iniquity. They do not break bread for the hungry, they do not clothe the naked, they do not take in the stranger, they do not visit the sick, they do not reconcile the quarrelsome, they do not bury the dead: for these are works of mercy; there there is no misery in which mercy might be shown. O blessed ones! Do we suppose that we too will be so? Come, let us sigh, let us groan from our sighing. And what are we, that we should be there? Mortal, cast down, abject, earth and ashes. But he who promised is almighty. If we attend to ourselves, what are we? if to him, he is God, he is almighty. Will he not make an angel out of a man, who made man out of nothing? Or does God count man as something small, for whom he willed his Only-begotten to die? Let us attend to the token of his love. Such pledges of the promise of God we have received; we hold the death of Christ, we hold the blood of Christ. Who died? The Only-begotten. For whom did he die? Would that for the good, would that for the just. But what? *For indeed Christ*, says the Apostle, *died for the impious*[^7]. He who gave his death to the impious, what does he keep for the just, except his own life? Therefore let human frailty raise itself up, let it not despair, let it not dash itself down, let it not turn away, let it not say: I will not be. He who promised is God: and he came to promise; he appeared to men, he came to take up our death, to promise his own life. He came to the region of our pilgrimage, to receive here what abounds here, reproaches, scourges, blows, spittle on the face, insults, a crown of thorns, hanging on the wood, the cross, death. These things abound in our region; for such commerce he came. What did he give here, what did he receive? He gave exhortation, he gave teaching, he gave the forgiveness of sins; he received insults, death, the cross. He brought to us good things from that region, and in our region he bore evils. Yet he promised us that we are to be there whence he came; and he says: *Father, I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^8]. So great a love went before! Because where we were, he was with us; where he himself is, we will be with him. What did God promise you, O mortal man? That you are to live forever. Do you not believe? Believe, believe. What he has done is already more than what he has promised. What has he done? He died for you. What has he promised? That you may live with him. It is more incredible that the eternal one died, than that the mortal one should live forever. Now we hold what is more incredible. If God died for man, will man not live with God? will the mortal not live forever, for whom he died who lives forever? But how did God die, and from what did God die? and can God die? He received from you that from which he might die for you. Nothing could die but flesh; nothing could die but a mortal body: he clothed himself in that wherein he might die for you, he will clothe you in that wherein you may live with him. Wherein did he clothe himself with death? In the virginity of his mother. Wherein will he clothe you with life? In equality with the Father. He chose for himself here a chaste bridal chamber, where the bridegroom might be joined to the bride. The Word was made flesh[^9], that he might become the head of the Church. For the Word itself is not a part of the Church; but that he might be the head of the Church, he assumed flesh. Something of ours is already above, which he received here, where he died, where he was crucified: some first-fruits of yours have already gone before, and do you doubt that you are to follow!

**9.** [v. 7.] Therefore let him turn to the things of earth as well, since he has now spoken the praises of the heavenly things. *Praise the Lord from the earth.* For with what did he begin above? *Praise the Lord from the heavens*; and he recounted the heavenly things: now hear the earthly things. *You dragons and all you deeps.* The deeps are the depths of the waters: all the seas, this cloudy air pertain to the deep. Where there are clouds, where winds, where storms, where rains, lightnings, thunders, hail, snow, and whatever God wishes to be done over the lands out of this moist and dark air, all this he has called by the name of earth, because it is exceedingly changeable and mortal: unless perhaps you suppose that it rains up among the stars. All these things happen here, near the earth. And sometimes men are on the summits of mountains, and see clouds below themselves, and very often rains come about: and to those who attend well there appear all these things, which are stirred up in the disturbed air, to happen here in this lowest part of the world. For this reason the devil was condemned to these dark places, that is, to this air, as to a prison, having fallen with his angels from the array of the higher Angels: for the Apostle says this of him: *According to the prince of the power of this air, who now works in the sons of unbelief*[^10]. And another apostle says: *For if God did not spare the angels that sinned, but thrust them down into the prisons of the darkness of hell, delivered them up to be kept for punishment in judgment*[^11]: calling this hell, because it is the lower part of the world. For do not think of what the devil received, but of what he lost. All these things, then, you see of what kind they are, changeable, disturbed, terrible, corruptible: yet they have their own place, they have their own order, and they too fill up the beauty of the universe in their own measure, and therefore they praise the Lord. Turning, then, to them, as it were exhorting them too; rather, exhorting us, that by the consideration of them we may praise the Lord (for so they praise God, when the things considered bring forth the praise of God), he began to say: *Praise the Lord from the earth, you dragons and all you deeps.* Dragons dwell about the water, they come forth from caves, they are borne into the air; the air is stirred up because of them: dragons are certain great living things, there are none greater upon the earth. For this reason he began thence to say: *You dragons and all you deeps.* But there are caves of hidden waters, whence springs, whence rivers come forth; and some come forth to flow over the earth, others pass secretly beneath: and all this, and all this moist nature of the waters, together with the sea and this lowest air, is called the deep or the deeps, where dragons live and praise God. What? do we suppose that dragons form choirs, and praise God? Far from it. But you, considering the dragons, attend to the artificer of dragons, the creator of dragons; and when you marvel at the dragons, and say: Great is the God who made these things, the dragons praise God by your voices: *You dragons and all you deeps.*

**10.** [v. 8.] *Fire, hail, snow, ice, the spirit of the storm, which do his word.* Why did he add here, *which do his word*? Many foolish men, unable to contemplate and discern the creation acting its motions in its own places and its own order, under the nod and command of God, it has seemed to them that God governs all the higher things, but despises the lower things, casts them aside, deserts them, so that he neither cares for these things, nor governs, nor rules; but that they are ruled by chance, however they can, by whatever way they can: and the things they sometimes say to themselves move them (but let them not say them to you, that is, do not consent by listening when they say these things; for they are blasphemous and detestable to God): If God sent rain, would he send it into the sea? What kind of providence, they say, is this? Gaetulia thirsts, and the sea is rained upon. They seem to themselves to argue sharply: to whom this must be said—Gaetulia at least thirsts; but you, you do not even thirst. For it would have been good for you to say to God: *My soul is like a land without water before you*[^12]. Which is said openly in another place: *My soul has thirsted for you, how manifoldly my flesh too for you*[^13]. And the Lord in the Gospel: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they will be satisfied*[^14]. For this man is already satisfied, who argues such things; he seems to himself learned, and is unwilling to learn, and therefore does not thirst. For if he thirsted, he would wish to learn; and he would find that everything is done on the earth by the providence of God; and he would marvel at the ordered members even of a flea. Let your love attend. Who ordered the members of the flea and the gnat, that they have their own order, have their own life, have their own motion? Consider any small creature you like, the most minute. If you consider the order of its members, and the animation of the life by which it is moved; how for its own sake it flees death, loves life, seeks pleasures, avoids troubles, puts forth diverse senses, thrives in motion fitting to itself! Who gave the gnat its sting, with which it sucks blood? How fine is the little pipe by which it sucks! Who ordered these things? who made these things? You are amazed at the least things; praise the Great One. Hold this, therefore, my brothers; let no one shake you from the faith and from sound doctrine. He who made the angel in heaven, himself made the little worm on the earth: but the angel in heaven for a heavenly dwelling, the little worm on the earth for an earthly dwelling. Did he make the angel to crawl in the mire, and the little worm in heaven? He distributed the dwellers to their seats; he gave incorruption to incorruptible seats, corruptible things to corruptible places. Attend to the whole, praise the whole. He, then, who ordered the members of the little worm, does he not govern the clouds? And why does it rain into the sea? As though there were not in the sea things that are nourished by the rain; as though he did not make fishes there, as though he did not make living things there. Attend to how the fishes run to fresh water. And why, he says, does it rain for the fish, and sometimes not for me? That you may consider yourself to be in the region of the desert, and in the pilgrimage of life; and that the present life may grow bitter to you, that you may desire the future; or that you may be scourged, and rebuked, and corrected. But how does he distribute to regions what is proper to them? Behold, since we have spoken of Gaetulia, here it rains nearly every year, and every year gives grain: grain cannot be kept here, it is quickly spoiled; because it is given every year: there, because it is given rarely, it is given in abundance, and is kept a long time. But perhaps you suppose that God deserts men there, or that they do not there both praise and glorify God according to their own pleasure? Take a Gaetulian from there, set him among these pleasant trees; he wishes to flee from here, and to return to bare Gaetulia. To all places, then, regions, and times, God has distributed and ordained their own things. And it is long to recall a more diligent consideration of all things. Who unfolds it? Yet those who have eyes see many things there: when they have seen them, the things please; when they have pleased, they are praised, not as themselves, but he who made them: so all things will praise God.

**11.** Considering these things, the spirit of the Prophet, when he had said: *Fire, hail, snow, ice, the spirit of the storm*, all of which seem to certain foolish men to be disturbed and as it were driven by chance, added: *which do his word.* Therefore let not those things seem to you to be moved by chance, which in their every motion serve the word of God. Where God wills, thither the fire, thither the cloud is borne, whether it carry rain, or snow, or hail. And why do lightnings sometimes strike the mountain, and not strike the robber? Whatever I can say according to the grasp of my mind, as far as God deigns to grant: let the greater know greater things, and understand more fully; and may God give you to be wiser than I speak, yet with moderation, without pride: I can, then, according to my small measure, say why they strike the mountain, and do not strike the robber: because perhaps he still seeks the conversion of the robber; and therefore the mountain, which does not fear, is struck, that the man who fears may be changed. Sometimes you too, when you give discipline, strike the ground, that the child may be afraid. And sometimes he strikes even the man he wishes. But you say to me: Behold, he strikes the more innocent, and lets go the more wicked. Do not marvel: from wherever it comes, death is good for the godly. But how do you know what punishments are kept in secret for that more wicked one, if he is unwilling to change himself? Would they not rather be burned by lightning, to whom it will be said at the end: *Go into eternal fire*[^15]? It is necessary that you be innocent. For what? is it evil to die by shipwreck, and good to die by fever? Whether he die from this or from that, ask what kind he is who dies; whither he is to go after death, not whence he is to go out of life. By whatever occasion, we are to go out from here: by what departures did the martyrs deserve to go out? was it by fevers, as many wish to be released by fever? Some by one stroke of the sword, others by fires, others by beasts. And beasts devoured the bodies of the martyrs, and they did not fear lest their bodies perish. For from everywhere God will bring back the bodies of his saints, he to whom our hairs are numbered[^16]. And when he willed, he delivered the three young men from the fire[^17]: did he therefore desert the Maccabees in the fire[^18]? Those he delivered openly, these he crowned secretly. God, therefore, knows what he does. Do you fear, and be good. From wherever he wills you to go out from here, let him find you ready. For you are a tenant, not the owner of the house[^19]. For the house has been rented to you; this house has been rented to you, not given; even if you are unwilling, you will move out: and you did not receive it on such a condition that the times are fixed for you. What did your Lord say? When I will, when I will say: Move; be ready. I expel you from the lodging, but I will give you a house: you are a tenant on earth, you will be the owner in heaven.

**12.** Whatever, then, happens here against our will, know that it does not happen except by the will of God, by his providence, by his order, by his nod, by his laws: and if we do not understand why each thing happens, let us grant this to his providence, that it does not happen without cause, and let us not blaspheme. For when we begin to argue about the works of God—Why this, why that; and: He ought not to have made it so, he made this badly—where is the praise of God? You have lost the Alleluia. Consider all things in such a way that you please God, and praise the artificer. For if you perhaps entered the workshop of a blacksmith, you would not dare to find fault with the bellows, the anvils, the hammers. And put an unskilled man there, who knows not what each thing is for, and he finds fault with everything. But if he has not the skill of the artificer, and yet has at least the consideration of a man, what does he say to himself? Not without cause are the bellows placed in this spot; the artificer knows why, even if I do not know. In the workshop he does not dare to disparage the smith; and he dares to find fault with God in this world! Therefore, just as *fire, hail, snow, ice, the spirit of the storm, which do his word*; so all things which seem to vain men to happen rashly in the nature of things, do nothing but his word, because they happen only at his command.

**13.** [vv. 9-12.] Then he says, that they may praise the Lord: *Mountains and all hills, fruitful trees and all cedars; beasts and all cattle, creeping things and winged fowl.* Then to men: *Kings of the earth and all peoples, princes and all judges of the earth; young men and maidens, the old with the younger, let them praise the name of the Lord.* The praise from heaven has been unfolded, the praise from earth has been unfolded.

**14.** [v. 13.] *For his name alone is exalted.* Let no man seek to exalt his own name. Do you wish to be exalted? Submit yourself to him who cannot be humbled: *His name alone is exalted.*

**15.** [v. 14.] *His confession is on earth and in heaven.* What is, *his confession is on earth and in heaven*? by which he himself confesses? No; but by which all things confess him, all things cry out: the beauty of all things is in a certain way their voice, confessing God. Heaven cries out to God: You made me, not I. The earth cries out: You founded me, not I. How do these things cry out? When they are considered, and this is found; from your consideration they cry out, by your voice they cry out. *His confession is on earth and in heaven.* Attend to heaven, it is beautiful; attend to the earth, it is beautiful: both together are very beautiful. He himself made them, he himself rules them, by his nod they are governed, he himself brings the seasons round, he himself renews the moments, by himself he renews them. All these things, then, praise him, whether at rest or in motion, whether below on the earth or above in heaven, whether in their oldness or in their renewal. When you see these things and rejoice, and are lifted up to the artificer, and behold his invisible things, understood through the things that are made[^20]: *his confession is on earth and in heaven*; that is, you confess to him concerning earthly things, you confess to him concerning heavenly things. And because he himself made all things, and there is nothing better than he, whatever he made is within him; and whatever in these things pleases you is less than he. Therefore let not what he made so please you that you withdraw from him who made it; but if you love what he made, much more love him who made it. If the things he made are beautiful, how much more beautiful is he who made them? *His confession is on earth and in heaven.*

**16.** *And he will exalt the horn of his people.* Behold what Haggai and Zechariah prophesied. Now the horn of his people is lowly in the threshings, in the tribulations, in the temptations, in the beating of breasts: when *will he exalt the horn of his people*? When the Lord himself will come, and our sun will have risen: not this one which is seen with the eyes, and rises over the good and the evil[^21]; but the one of whom it is said: *To you who fear God, the sun of justice will rise, and health is in his wings*[^22]; and of whom the proud and the impious are going to say: *The light of justice has not shone for us, and the sun has not risen for us*[^23]. That will be our summer. Now the fruits do not appear through the winter in the root; you regard the trees as it were dry through the winter. He who does not know how to see, thinks the vine dry: and perhaps next to it is one that has truly dried up; both are alike through the winter: the one lives, the other is dead; but the life of the one and the death of the other are hidden: summer comes forth, the life of the one is made clear, the death of the other is made manifest; the glory of leaves comes forth, the fruitfulness of fruits; the vine is clothed in its appearance, from what it has in the root. Therefore now, brothers, we are such as other men are: as they are born, eat, drink, are clothed, pass through this life; so too the saints. Sometimes the very fact deceives men, and they say: Behold, because he has begun to be a Christian, does his head not ache? or because he is a Christian, what has he more than I? O dry vine, you regard next to you a vine bare in the winter, but not dry. Summer will come, the Lord will come, our glory, who lay hidden in the root; and then *he will exalt the horn of his people*, after the captivity in which we live mortally. Whence the Apostle says: *Do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, and he will enlighten the hidden things of darkness; and then there will be praise to each one from God*[^24]. But you say to me: Where is my root? where is my fruit? If you believe, you know where your root is. For it is there, where your faith is, where your hope and your love are. Hear the Apostle: *For you are dead.* As if through the winter they appeared dead; hear that they live: *And your life is hidden with Christ in God.* Behold where you have your root. When, then, will you be adorned with glory? when will you be made fruitful with fruit? Hear him as he follows: *When Christ, your life, will appear, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^25]. *And he will exalt the horn of his people.*

**17.** *A hymn to all his saints.* Do you know what a hymn is? It is song with the praise of God. If you praise God, and do not sing, you do not utter a hymn: if you sing, and do not praise God, you do not utter a hymn: if you praise something else that does not pertain to the praise of God, even though you praise in singing, you do not utter a hymn. A hymn, then, has these three things: song, and praise, and of God. The praise of God, therefore, in song, is called a hymn. What, then, is, *a hymn to all his saints*? Let his saints receive a hymn, let his saints utter a hymn; because this is what they are to receive in the end, an everlasting hymn. Therefore in another place the psalm says: *The sacrifice of praise will glorify me; and there is the way by which I will show him the salvation of God*[^26]: and: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; for ages of ages they will praise you*[^27]. This is, *a hymn to all his saints.* Who are his saints? *To the sons of Israel, the people drawing near to him.* Let no one say: I am not a son of Israel. Do not suppose that the Jews are the sons of Israel, and that we are not the sons of Israel: I dare to say to you, my brothers; they are not, and we are. Hear why: because the one born according to the spirit is greater than the one born according to the flesh. Whence Israel? From Abraham; because Isaac is from Abraham, Israel from Isaac. How did Abraham please God? *And Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as justice*[^28]. Whoever has imitated Abraham is a son of Abraham: whoever has degenerated from the faith of Abraham has lost the offspring of Abraham. The Jews degenerated; they lost: we imitated; we found. Hear that they lost: what does the Lord say to them when they say: *We are sons of Abraham*[^29]? They dared to display themselves, and to lift up their neck because of the nobility of a just race; and what does the Lord say to them? *If you were sons of Abraham, you would do the works of Abraham*[^30]. If, then, they themselves lost their being sons of Abraham, we found our being sons of Abraham. For by believing we found what they by not believing lost: because *Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as justice.* And the seed of Abraham is Christ[^31], and in Christ we are; and from Israel is the people, of which people is Mary, of which Mary is Christ, in which Christ we are: therefore we are sons of Israel. And how did he add, to distinguish us? *To the sons of Israel, the people drawing near to him.* Attend to the Jews: if they draw near, they are the ones. And perhaps they draw near, someone says to me: daily they too sing the psalm, and sing the hymns of God. Do you not hear what the prophet said to them? *This people honors me with their lips; but their heart is far from me*[^32]. If their heart is far, but our heart is near; because we believe, because we hope, because we love, because we are joined to Christ, because we have been made his members: are the members far from the head? If they were far, and were divided, he would not say: *Behold, I am with you, even to the consummation of the age*[^33]. If they were separated, he would not say from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^34] If he were not in us, he would not say: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*: and when they said to him: *When did we see you hungry?* he answered: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^35]. Behold the people, behold Israel drawing near; and with him now in hope, afterward in reality.
