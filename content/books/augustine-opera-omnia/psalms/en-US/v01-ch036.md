# On the Same Psalm 30

**1.** [v. 1.] Let us search out, as far as we can, the secrets of this psalm which we have just now sung, and from there render to your ears and minds a discourse drawn out of it. Its title is: *To the end, a psalm of David himself, of ecstasy*[^1]. What *to the end* means we know, if we know Christ; for the Apostle says: *For Christ is the end of the law, unto righteousness to everyone who believes*[^2]. An end that does not consume but perfects: for we speak of an end in two ways; either that by which it comes about that what was is no more, or that by which it comes about that what had been begun is made perfect. Therefore *to the end* means unto Christ.

**2.** *A psalm of David, of ecstasy*. The word *ecstasis* is Greek; in Latin, as far as can be understood, it can be expounded in one word, if one says "stepping-out" (*excessus*): for properly a stepping-out of the mind is wont to be called ecstasy. But in a stepping-out of the mind two things are understood: either dread, or an intent reaching toward things above, such that in a manner the things below slip from the memory: in this ecstasy were all the saints, to whom the secret things of God surpassing this world were revealed. Speaking of this stepping-out of the mind, that is, ecstasy, Paul, hinting at himself, said: *For whether we have stepped out of mind to God, or are sober for you, the love of Christ compels us*[^3]. That is, if we wished to do only those things, and to contemplate only those things, which we behold in a stepping-out of the mind, we would not be with you, but would be in things above, as though you were despised: and when would you follow us with feeble step to those higher and inner things, unless again, the love of Christ compelling us (who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant), we considered ourselves to be servants, and, not ungrateful to him from whom we received higher things, did not for the sake of those who are weak despise lower things, and tempered ourselves to those who cannot with us see sublime things? This, then, he says: *Whether we have stepped out of mind to God*: for he sees what we see in a stepping-out of the mind, he alone reveals his secrets. Indeed this is said by one who says that he was caught up and carried away into the third heaven, and heard ineffable words which it is not lawful for a man to speak: but so great was that stepping-out of the mind that he said, *Whether in the body, or out of the body, I do not know, God knows*[^4]. If, then, the title of this psalm signifies this stepping-out of the mind, that is, this ecstasy, we ought surely to hope that he who composed the psalm—that is, the Prophet, or rather, through the Prophet, the Holy Spirit—will say things great and lofty.

**3.** But if this ecstasy is to be understood as dread, the context of this psalm will not be lacking to this sense of the word either; for it seems about to speak of the passion, in which there is dread. But whose dread is it—is it Christ's, because it said *to the end*, and we understand the end to be Christ? or perhaps our dread? For can we rightly understand dread in Christ as the passion drew near, who had come for its sake? When he had come to that for which he came, did he dread that he was about to die? If he were a man in such a way as not to be God, would he not rejoice more that he was about to rise again than dread that he was about to die? Yet because he deigned to take on the form of a servant, and in it to clothe us with himself; he who did not disdain to take us up into himself, did not disdain to transfigure us into himself, and to speak with our words, so that we too might speak with his words. For this wondrous exchange took place, and divine transactions were carried through, a trading of things conducted in this world by a heavenly merchant: he came to receive insults, to give honors; he came to drink down sorrow, to give salvation; he came to undergo death, to give life. About to die, then, from that which he had of ours, he dreaded not in himself but in us; for he also said this, that his soul was sorrowful even unto death, and indeed all we ourselves with him. For without him we are nothing; but in him, both Christ himself and we. Why? Because the whole Christ is head and body. The head is the Savior of the body, who has already ascended into heaven; the body is the Church, which labors on earth. And unless this body cleaved to its head by the bond of love, so as to become one out of head and body, he would not, rebuking from heaven a certain persecutor, have said: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^5] When no man was touching him already seated in heaven, how was Saul, raging on earth against the Christians, in any way striking him with injury? He did not say, Why my saints, why my servants?, but *why do you persecute me?*—that is, why my members? The head was crying out for the members, and was transfiguring the members into himself. For the tongue takes up the voice of the foot. When perhaps the foot, crushed in a crowd, hurts, the tongue cries out: You are treading on me; for it does not say, You are treading on my foot, but said that it itself was being trodden on, though no one touched it: but the foot that was trodden on was not separated from the tongue. So then, in this way too the ecstasy is not unfittingly understood as dread. For what shall I say, brothers? If there were no dread at all for those about to suffer, that would have been said to Peter himself which we heard on the birthday of the apostles, when the Lord foretold to him his coming passion: *When you were younger, you girded yourself, and went where you wished; but when you have become older, another will gird you, and lead you where you do not wish. But this*, he says, *he said, signifying by what death he would die*[^6]. If, then, Peter the apostle with such great perfection went willing where he was unwilling (unwilling he died, but willing he was crowned), what wonder if there is some dread in the passion even of the righteous, even of the saints? Dread is from human weakness, hope from the divine promise. What you dread is your own, what you hope is the gift of God in you. And the better in your dread you recognize yourself, that in your deliverance you may glorify him who made you. Let human weakness dread; in that dread the divine mercy does not fail. In short, this man, dreading, began from there: *In you, O Lord, I have hoped; let me not be confounded forever*[^7]. You see that he both dreads and hopes: you see that this dread is not without hope. Even if there is some troubling in the human heart, the divine consolation does not depart.

**4.** Here, then, Christ speaks in the Prophet; I dare to say, Christ speaks. He is about to say certain things in this psalm which seem as though they could not fit Christ, that excellence of our head, and most of all that Word which in the beginning was God with God: nor perhaps will certain words here seem to fit him in the form of a servant, which form of a servant he took on from the Virgin; and yet Christ speaks, because in the members of Christ it is Christ. And that you may know that head and body are called one Christ, he himself says, when he was speaking of marriage: *The two shall be in one flesh: therefore now they are not two, but one flesh*[^8]. But perhaps he says this of any marriage whatever? Hear the apostle Paul: *And the two*, he says, *shall be in one flesh: this is a great mystery, but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^9]. There comes to be, then, as it were out of two, a certain one person, out of head and body, out of bridegroom and bride. For the wondrous and excellent unity of this person the prophet Isaiah also commends: for Christ, speaking in him too in prophecy, says: *As to a bridegroom he has bound a miter to me, and as a bride he has adorned me with ornament*[^10]. He called himself bridegroom, himself bride: why himself bridegroom, himself bride, except because the two shall be in one flesh? If two in one flesh, why not two in one voice? Let Christ speak, then, because in Christ the Church speaks, and in the Church Christ speaks; both the body in the head, and the head in the body. Hear the Apostle expressing this very thing more evidently: *For as the body is one and has many members, but all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body, so also is Christ*[^11]. Speaking of the members of Christ, that is, of the faithful, he did not say, so also are the members of Christ; but all this that he said he called Christ. For as the body is one and has many members, but all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body; so also is Christ many members, one body. Therefore all of us together with our head are Christ, without our head being worth nothing. Why? Because we together with our head are the vine: without our head, God forbid, we are branches cut off, destined not for any work of farmers but for fire alone. So he himself in the Gospel: *I am the vine, you are the branches, my Father is the farmer*; and, *Without me*, he says, *you can do nothing*[^12]. O Lord, if without you we are nothing, in you the whole. For indeed whatever he works through us, we seem to work. He can do much, and the whole, without us; we nothing without him.

**5.** [v. 2.] In whatever ecstasy, then, he speaks—whether of dread or of a stepping-out of the mind—the things that are said are fitting. Let us say, in the body of Christ, let us all say as though one, because all are unity, let us say: *In you, O Lord, I have hoped; let me not be confounded forever*[^13]. That confounding, he says, I utterly dread, which is forever. For there is a certain useful temporal confusion, the troubling of a mind that looks back upon its sins, shuddering at the sight, blushing in the shudder, correcting itself in the blushing; whence the Apostle too says: *For what glory did you have then in those things of which you now blush?*[^14] Therefore he says that they, now faithful, blush, not over present gifts, but over past sins. Let not the Christian fear this confusion: nay rather, if he does not have this one, he will have the eternal one. What is the eternal confusion? When that will come to pass which was said: *And their iniquities will convict them to their face*[^15]. And when their iniquities convict them to their face, all the evil flock will be at the left, like kids separated from the sheep; and they will hear: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^16]. They ask why? *For I was hungry, and you gave me not to eat*[^17]. They were despising then, when to the hungry Christ they gave no food, when to the thirsty they gave no drink, when they clothed not the naked, received not the stranger, visited not the sick—then they were despising: when these things begin to be enumerated to them, they will be confounded, and this confusion will be forever. Fearing this, the one who dreads, or whose stepping-out of mind is to God, asks this: *In you, O Lord, I have hoped; let me not be confounded forever*.

**6.** *And in your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me*[^18]: for if you attend to my righteousness, you condemn me. *In your righteousness rescue me*. For there is a righteousness of God which becomes ours too, when it is given to us. And it is called the righteousness of God for this reason, lest a man think he has righteousness from himself. For so the apostle Paul says: *To one who believes in him who justifies the ungodly* (what is, who justifies the ungodly? who out of the ungodly makes him righteous) *his faith is reckoned unto righteousness*[^19]. But the Jews, because they thought they could fulfill righteousness by their own powers, stumbled at the stone of stumbling and the rock of offense, and did not acknowledge the grace of Christ. For they received the Law by which they were made guilty, not by which they were freed from guilt. In short, what does the Apostle say of them? *For I bear them witness that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*. What is it that he said: the Jews *have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*? Hear what "not according to knowledge" is: *For, being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not been subject to the righteousness of God*[^20]. If, then, they have a zeal for God not according to knowledge for this reason—that they are ignorant of the righteousness of God and wish to establish their own, as though they became righteous of themselves—therefore they did not recognize the grace of God, because they were unwilling to be saved freely. Who is it that is saved freely? In whom the Savior finds nothing to crown, but something to condemn; he finds no merits of good things, but he finds merits of punishments. If he were to act, as in truth he might, by the rule of the Law set forth, the sinner must be condemned. If he acted by this rule, whom would he free? He finds all sinners: he alone came without sin, who found us sinners. This the Apostle says: *For all have sinned, and lack the glory of God*[^21]. What is, *lack the glory of God*? That he may free, not you: because you cannot free yourself, you need a deliverer. What is it that you boast of? What is it that you presume upon, of Law and righteousness? Do you not see what struggles within you, about you, against you? Do you not hear the one fighting, and confessing, and longing for help in the fight? Do you not hear the athlete of the Lord asking of the steward of the games help for his fight? For God does not await you as you contend the way the giver of the games awaits you, if perhaps you fight in the amphitheater: he can give you a prize if you have conquered, he cannot help you when you are in peril. God does not await you so. See, then, attend to the one who says: *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man, but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin which is in my members. Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^22]. Why grace? Because it is given freely. Why is it given freely? Because your merits did not go before, but the benefits of God came before you. To him, then, be the glory who frees us. *For all have sinned, and lack the glory of God. In you*, then, *O Lord, I have hoped*, not in myself: *let me not be confounded forever*, because I hope in him who does not confound. *In your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me*: because you did not find in me my righteousness, rescue me in yours; that is, let that rescue me which justifies me, which out of the ungodly makes the godly, which out of the unrighteous the righteous, which out of the blind the seeing, which out of the falling the rising, which out of the weeping the rejoicing. This frees me, not I. *In your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me*.

**7.** [v. 3.] *Incline your ear to me*[^23]: God did this when he sent Christ himself to us. He sent to us the one who, with head inclined, wrote with his finger on the ground, when the adulterous woman was offered to him to be punished. But he had inclined himself to the earth, that is, God to man, to whom it was said: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^24]. For God does not incline his ear to us as though to bodily places, nor is he bounded by these defined bodily members. Let not human fancies imagine any of these things at all. God is truth. Truth is neither square, nor round, nor long. He is present everywhere, if the eye of the heart lies open to him. Yet God inclines his ear to us, laying his mercy upon us. What greater mercy than that he should give his Only-begotten for us, not to live with us, but to die for us? *Incline your ear to me*.

**8.** *Make haste to deliver me*[^25]: for he is heard in this, when he says, *Make haste*. For the word is set down to this end, that you may understand that all this, which seems to us long while the world rolls on, is a point. That is not long which has an end. From Adam to this present day it has been carried through, and much more, surely, has been carried through than remains to be carried through. If Adam still lived, and died today, what would it profit him to have been so long, to have lived so long? Why, then, this haste? Because times fly past: and what is slow to you is brief in the eyes of God. This haste this man had already understood in ecstasy. *Make haste to deliver me. Be to me a protecting God, and a house of refuge, that you may save me*. Be to me a house of refuge, a protecting God, a house of refuge. For sometimes I am in peril, and I wish to flee: where do I flee? to what place do I flee safe? to what mountain? to what cave? to what fortified roofs? What citadel shall I hold? by what walls shall I be encircled? Wherever I go, I follow myself. For whatever you wish you can flee, O man, except your own conscience. Enter into your house, rest in your bed, enter into your inner parts: you can have nothing more inward to which to flee from your conscience, if your sins gnaw at you. But because he said, Make haste to deliver me, and in your righteousness rescue me, so that you may forgive my sins and build up in me your righteousness: you will be to me a house of refuge, to you I flee. For from you, where shall I flee? If God is angry with you, where will you flee? Hear what he says in another psalm, fearing the anger of God: *Where shall I go from your spirit, and where shall I flee from your face? If I ascend into heaven, you are there: if I descend into hell, you are present*[^26]. Wherever I go, there I find you. And if you are angry, I find you an avenger; if you are appeased, a helper. Nothing, then, remains for me but to flee to you, not from you. To escape a human master, whatever servant you are, you flee to those places where your master is not: to escape God, flee to the Lord. For there is nowhere you may flee God. All things lie open and bare to the eyes of the Almighty. You therefore, he says, be to me a house of refuge. For if I am not saved, how do I flee? Heal me, and I flee to you. For if you do not heal me, I cannot walk; how shall I be able to flee? Where would he go, where would he flee, if he could not walk—half-alive on the road, wounded by the wounds of robbers? Whom the priest passing by passed over, the Levite passing by passed over, the Samaritan passing by pitied, that is, the Lord himself, who pitied the human race. For "Samaritan" is interpreted "keeper." And who keeps us, if he forsakes us? Rightly, when the Jews reproaching him said, *Do we not say truly that you are a Samaritan and have a demon?*, he rejected the one, embraced the other: *I*, he says, *have no demon*[^27]: he did not say, I am not a Samaritan; wishing it to be understood that he is our keeper. Pitying, then, he drew near, cared for him, brought him to the inn, fulfilled his mercy toward him: now that man can walk, can even flee. Where would he flee but to God, where he has made himself a house of refuge?

**9.** [v. 4.] *For you are my strength and my refuge, and for your name's sake you will be my guide, and you will nourish me*[^28]: not for my merit, but *for your name's sake*, that you may be glorified, not because I am worthy; *you will be my guide*, lest I stray from you; *and you will nourish me*, that I may be strong to eat the food with which you feed the angels. For here he nourished us with milk, who promised us heavenly food; and he used a mother's mercy. For as a nursing mother, the same food which the infant is not fit to take she passes through her flesh and pours out as milk (for the little one receives this, which he was going to receive at the table, but, what is passed through the flesh, suits the little one); so the Lord, to make his wisdom milk for us, came to us clothed in flesh. Therefore the body of Christ says: *And you will nourish me*.

**10.** [v. 5.] *You will bring me out of this snare, which they have hidden for me*[^29]. Now the passion is signified: *You will bring me out of this snare, which they have hidden for me*. Nor is that the only passion in which our Lord Jesus Christ suffered: the devil has stretched out his snare even to the end. And woe to him who falls into that snare: but everyone falls who does not hope in God; who does not say, *In you, O Lord, I have hoped; let me not be confounded forever: and in your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me*. The enemy's snare is stretched out and ready. He has set in the snare error and terror: error by which to entice, terror by which to break and snatch away. Shut you the door of cupidity against error; shut you the door of fear against terror, and you will be brought out of the snare. An example of this kind of fight your Emperor himself, who deigned to be tempted even for your sake, showed in himself. And first he was tempted by enticements; for the door of cupidity was tempted in him, when the devil tempted him, saying: *Tell these stones to become bread. Worship me, and I will give you these kingdoms. Cast yourself down, for it is written: He has charged his angels concerning you, and on their hands they will bear you up, lest you ever strike your foot against a stone*[^30]. All this enticement tempts cupidity. But when it found the door of cupidity shut in the one who was being tempted for us, it turned to tempting the door of fear, and prepared a passion for him. In short, the Evangelist says this: *And the temptation being finished, the devil departed from him for a time*[^31]. What is, *for a time*? As though about to return and tempt the door of fear, because he found the door of cupidity shut. The whole body of Christ, then, is tempted to the end. My brothers, when some evil or other was commanded against the Christians, this whole body was struck at once, the whole was struck: whence it was said in the psalm: *Like a heap of sand I was thrust to make me fall, and the Lord upheld me*[^32]. But when those things were finished which thrust the whole body to make it fall, the temptation began to be by parts. The body of Christ is tempted: one Church does not suffer persecution, another suffers it. It does not suffer the fury of the emperor, but it suffers the fury of an evil people. How great are the devastations from the mobs? How many evils have been heaped upon the Church by evil Christians, by those who, caught in that little net, were so multiplied that they weighed down the boats in that catch of the Lord before the passion? Therefore the pressures of temptation are not lacking. Let no one say to himself: It is not a time of temptation. He who says this to himself promises himself peace: he who promises himself peace is attacked while off guard. Let the whole body of Christ, then, say: *You will bring me out of this snare, which they have hidden for me*: because our head too was brought out of the snare which those hid, to whom it was just now being said in the Gospel that they were going to say, *This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*. And, being questioned, they pronounced sentence against themselves: *What will that householder do to the evil tenants? He will destroy the evil men miserably, and will let out his vineyard to other farmers. What, and have you not read this: The stone which the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner?*[^33] For what is, *the builders rejected*, this he says: *They cast him out of the vineyard, and killed him*. Therefore he too was rescued. Our head is above, is free. Let us cleave to him through love, that afterward we may be the better cemented to him through immortality; and let us all say: *You will bring me out of this snare, which they have hidden for me; for you are my protector*.

**11.** [v. 6.] Let us hear the voice of the Lord, which he spoke on the cross: *Into your hands I commend my spirit*[^34]. Surely, when we recognize his words in the Gospel as from this psalm, let us not doubt that he himself spoke here. You have this in the Gospel; he said, *Into your hands I commend my spirit*; *and bowing his head, he gave up the spirit*[^35]. Not without cause did he will the words of this psalm to be his own, except to admonish you that he had spoken in this psalm. Seek him here; consider how in that psalm he willed himself to be sought, for the morning offering: *They have dug my hands and feet, they have numbered all my bones; but they themselves considered and looked upon me, they divided my garments among themselves, and over my clothing they cast lots*[^36]: that he might admonish you that this was fulfilled in him, he set in his own voice the head of that psalm: *O God, my God, why have you forsaken me?*[^37] And yet he transfigured the voice of the body into himself; for the Father never forsook his Only-begotten. *You have redeemed me, O Lord, God of truth*: doing what you promised, not deceiving in your promise, O God of truth.

**12.** [v. 7.] *You have hated those who keep watch over vanity to no purpose*[^38]. Who keeps watch over vanity? He who by fearing to die dies: for by fearing to die he lies, and dies before he dies, who was lying for this reason, that he might live. You wish to lie, lest you die: and you both lie and die; and while you avoid one death which you can defer but cannot remove, you fall into two, so that you die first in soul, afterward in body. Whence this, except by keeping watch over vanity? Because the passing day is sweet to you, because the times flying past are sweet to you, from which you hold nothing, and moreover you yourself are held. *You have hated those who keep watch over vanity to no purpose. But I*, who keep no watch over vanity, *have hoped in the Lord*. You hope in money, you keep watch over vanity: you hope in honor and in some loftiness of human power, you keep watch over vanity: you hope in some powerful friend, you keep watch over vanity. In all these things, when you hope, either you expire, and leave them here; or, while you live, all things perish, and you fail in your hope. This vanity Isaiah recalls, saying: *All flesh is grass, and all its glory like the flower of grass: the grass has withered, and its flower has fallen; but the word of the Lord remains forever*[^39]. But I, not like those who hope in vanity, and who keep watch over vanity, but I have hoped in the Lord, who is not vanity.

**13.** [v. 8.] *I will exult, and be glad in your mercy*[^40]: not in my righteousness. *For you have looked upon my lowliness, you have saved my soul from its necessities: and you have not shut me up in the hands of the enemy*. What are the necessities from which we wish our soul to be saved? Who could enumerate them? who could fittingly heap them up? who could suitably commend them as things to be avoided and fled? First, in the human race there is a hard necessity: not to know another's heart, often to think ill of a faithful friend, often to think well of an unfaithful friend. O hard necessity! And what do you do to look into hearts? what eye do you bring, weak and lamentable mortality? what do you do to see today the heart of your brother? You have nothing you can do. There is another, greater necessity: you do not even see what your own heart will be like tomorrow. What now shall I say of the necessities of mortality itself? It is necessary to die, and no one wishes it. No one wishes what is necessary. No one wishes what will be, willing or unwilling. A hard necessity, to be unwilling for what cannot be avoided. For if it could be done, we would surely be unwilling to die; and we would wish to be made what the angels are, but by a certain change, not by death, as the Apostle says: *We have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. For in this we groan, longing to be clothed over with our dwelling which is from heaven; if indeed, being clothed, we be not found naked. For we who are in this dwelling groan, being burdened, in that we do not wish to be stripped, but to be clothed over, that the mortal may be swallowed up by life*[^41]. We wish to come to the kingdom of God, but through death we are unwilling: and yet necessity says to you, By this way you will come. Do you hesitate to come by this way, O man, when by this way God came to you? What are also the necessities of conquering most ancient cupidities, and the long-standing evil habits of years? To conquer habit is a hard fight, you know. You see how badly you act, how detestably, how unhappily; and yet you act: you have done it, you are doing it, you will do it today. If you are displeased with yourself when I argue thus, how are you displeased with yourself when you reflect? And yet you are going to do this. Whence are you carried off? who drags you captive? Is it that law in your members fighting against the law of your mind? Cry out, then: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^42]; and there is fulfilled in you what we said just now: *But I have hoped in the Lord: I will exult, and be glad in your mercy; for you have looked upon my lowliness, you have saved my soul from its necessities*. For whence has your soul been saved from its necessities, except because your lowliness was looked upon? Unless you were first humbled, he would not hear you, who would free you from the necessities. Humbled was he who said, *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* Are they not unhumbled, who, *being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, have not been subject to the righteousness of God*[^43]?

**14.** [v. 9.] *And you have not shut me up in the hands of the enemy*[^44]: not your neighbor, not your fellow possessor, not the one with whom you served as a soldier and whom you injured, or perhaps in your city did him a wrong; for such men as these are those for whom we ought to pray. We have another enemy, the devil, the ancient serpent. All of us who die, if we die well, are freed from his hands. For whoever die badly in their iniquities are shut up in his hands, to be condemned with him at the end. The Lord our God, then, frees us from the hand of our enemy: for he wishes to capture us through our cupidities. But our cupidities, when they are strong, and when we serve them, are called necessities. But when God frees our soul from our necessities, what will there be in us that the enemy may hold, so that we be shut up in his hands?

**15.** *You have set my feet in a spacious place*[^45]. Surely the way is narrow: to one who toils it is narrow, to those who love it is broad. The very way that is narrow becomes broad. *In a spacious place*, he says, *you have set my feet*, lest my feet, cramped, should go into themselves, and by running into themselves cast me down. So what does he say, *You have set my feet in a spacious place*? Plainly you have made righteousness easy for me, which was once difficult for me: this is, *You have set my feet in a spacious place*.

**16.** [vv. 10-11.] *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am in tribulation; my eye is troubled in anger, my soul and my belly. For my life has failed in sorrow, and my years in groanings*[^46]. Let this suffice your Charity; with the Lord's help we shall perhaps fulfill the debt, so that, the psalm being completed, we may move on.
