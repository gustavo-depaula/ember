# On the Same Psalm 118 (23)

**1.** [v. 113.] The place of this psalm of which we are going to discourse in the will of God begins thus: *The unjust I have hated, and your law I have loved.* He does not say, "The unjust I have hated, and the just I have loved"; or, "Iniquity I have hated, and your law I have loved": but when he had said, *The unjust I have hated,* he expounded why, by adding, *and your law I have loved,* to show that he did not hate in unjust men the nature by which they are men, but the iniquity by which they are enemies to the law which he loves.

**2.** [v. 114.] Then there follows: *You are my helper and my upholder. Helper,* for doing good things; *upholder,* for escaping evil things. But that he adds, *In your word I have hoped beyond hope,* he speaks as a son of the promise.

**3.** [v. 115.] But what does the verse that follows mean: *Depart from me, you malignant, and I will search out the commandments of my God?* For he does not say "I will do," but *I will search out.* That he may know them perfectly, then, and diligently, he desires the malignant to depart from him, and even drives them from himself by compelling them. For the malignant exercise us toward doing the commandments, but call us away from searching them out; not only when they persecute us, or wish to quarrel with us, but even when they show deference and honor us, and yet demand that we be occupied in helping their vicious and busy cupidities, and spend our time on them; or certainly they oppress the weak, and compel them to bring their cases to us: to whom we do not dare to say, *Man, who appointed me a judge or a divider over you*[^1]? For the Apostle appointed ecclesiastics as hearers for such cases, forbidding Christians to litigate in the forum[^2]. Not even to those who do not seize what belongs to others, but greedily demand back their own, do we say: Beware of all cupidity, setting before their eyes the man to whom it was said, *Fool, this night your soul will be taken from you; whose will be the things you have prepared*[^3]? Because even when we say it, they do not withdraw nor depart from us; but they press, urge, beg, make tumult, extort, that we may rather be occupied with the things they love than with searching out the commandments of God which we love. O with how great a weariness of turbulent crowds, and with how great a desire of the divine utterances, was it said: *Depart from me, you malignant, and I will search out the commandments of my God!* Let the obedient faithful pardon us, who for their secular cases rarely seek us out, and most easily acquiesce in our judgments; nor wear us down by litigating, but rather console us by obeying. Certainly on account of those who both deal stubbornly among themselves, and, when they oppress the good, despise our judgments, and make us lose the time to be spent on divine things—certainly, I say, on account of these let it be permitted us too to cry out in this voice of the body of Christ: *Depart from me, you malignant, and I will search out the commandments of my God.*

**4.** [v. 116.] Then, after he had, as it were, driven away from the eyes of his heart the flies rushing in, he returns to him to whom he said, *You are my helper and my upholder; in your word I have hoped;* and, continuing his prayer, *Uphold me,* he says, *according to your utterance, and I will live; and do not confound me from my expectation.* He who had already said, *My upholder,* begs to be upheld more and more, and to be brought to that for the sake of which he bears so many troublesome things; trusting that he will live there more truly than in these dreams of human affairs. For it was said of the future, *and I will live,* as though in this dead body one does not live. For the body is dead because of sin[^4]: and, awaiting the redemption of our body, in hope we have been saved, and, hoping for what we do not see, we await it with patience[^5]. But hope does not confound, if the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^6]. For the sake of him, to be received more abundantly, it is cried to the Father: *Do not confound me from my expectation.*

**5.** [v. 117.] And as if it had been answered him in silence, "Do you not wish to be confounded from your expectation? Do not cease to meditate on my statutes"; feeling that this meditation is for the most part hindered by the languors of the soul, *Help me,* he says, *and I will be saved; and I will meditate on your statutes always.*

**6.** [v. 118.] *You have despised all;* or, what seems more carefully expressed from the Greek: *You have brought to nothing all who depart from your statutes; for their thought is unjust.* Therefore, then, he cried, *Help me and I will be saved, and I will meditate on your statutes always;* because God reduces to nothing all who depart from his statutes. But why do they depart? *Because their thought is unjust,* he says. There it is approached, there it is departed. All works, whether evil or good, proceed from thought: in thought each one is innocent, in thought guilty. On account of which it is written: *A holy thought will preserve you*[^7]; and elsewhere it is read: *In the thoughts of the ungodly there will be examination*[^8]; and the Apostle says, *Their thoughts accusing or even defending them*[^9]. But where is he happy who in thought is wretched? Or how is he there not wretched who has been reduced to nothing? For iniquity is a great barrenness. Rightly it was said: *Let the unjust be confounded, doing vainly*[^10]; that is, emptily, as though reduced to nothing.

**7.** [v. 119.] There follows in the psalm: *Transgressors I have accounted,* or *reckoned,* or *esteemed all the sinners of the earth.* For our interpreters rendered in many ways the one Greek word; but that sentence is profound, and, if the Lord help, is to be penetrated more laboriously in another discussion. For what is added too, *Therefore I have loved your testimonies always,* makes it much more profound. For the Apostle says, *The law works wrath,* and giving the reason of this saying, *For where there is no law,* he says, *neither is there transgression*[^11]; thus showing that not all are transgressors. For not all have the law. But that not all have the law he says more plainly in another place: *Those who have sinned without law will perish without law*[^12]. What, then, does this mean: *I have accounted transgressors all the sinners of the earth?* But here it will suffice to have proposed the question, to be treated, if God grant it, in another sermon, lest the length of this one force it to be unfolded more narrowly than that what is unfolded may be well understood.
