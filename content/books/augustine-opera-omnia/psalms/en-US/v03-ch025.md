# Exposition of Psalm 72

**1.** [v. 1] Hear, hear, most beloved inmost parts of the body of Christ, whose hope is the Lord your God, and you do not look back upon vanities and lying follies; and you who still look back, hear, that you may not look back. This psalm has an inscription, that is, a title: *The hymns of David, son of Jesse, have failed*: *A Psalm for Asaph himself.*[^1] So many psalms we have in whose titles the name of David is written, nowhere is added *son of Jesse,* except in this one alone. Which is to be believed not done in vain, nor emptily: for everywhere God beckons to us, and the godly zeal of love calls to understanding. What is: *The hymns of David, son of Jesse, have failed*? Hymns are the praises of God with song: hymns are songs containing the praise of God. If there be praise, and it be not of God, it is not a hymn: if there be praise, and the praise of God, and it be not sung, it is not a hymn. It is needful, then, that, if it be a hymn, it have these three: both praise, and of God, and song. What is, then: *The hymns have failed*? The praises that are sung to God have failed. It seems to announce a troublesome thing, and as it were mournful. For he who sings praise not only praises, but also praises with gladness: he who sings praise not only sings, but also loves him whom he sings. In the praise of one confessing there is proclamation: in the song of one loving there is affection. *The hymns of David have failed,* then, he says: and added, *son of Jesse.* For David was king of Israel, son of Jesse, at a certain time of the Old Testament; at which time the New Testament was hidden there, like fruit in the root. For if you seek the fruit in the root, you will not find it; nor yet will you find the fruit in the branches, except that which has come forth from the root. At that time, then, to the first people coming carnally from the seed of Abraham; for the second people too, pertaining to the New Testament, pertains to the seed of Abraham, but now spiritually: to that first people, then, still carnal, where few prophets understood both what was desired by God, and when it was to be publicly preached, foretold these future times, and the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. And as Christ himself, going to be born according to the flesh, was hidden in the root in the seed of the Patriarchs, and at a certain time to be revealed as it were the fruit appearing, as it is written: *A rod flowered from the root of Jesse*[^2]: so too that very New Testament, which is in Christ, in those earlier times was hidden, known to the Prophets alone, and to very few godly ones, not from the manifestation of things present, but from the revelation of things to come. For what does it mean, brethren, that I may mention one thing, that Abraham, sending his faithful servant to betroth a wife to his only son, makes him swear to him, and in the oath says to him: *Put your hand under my thigh, and swear*[^3]? What was in the thigh of Abraham, where that man put his hand swearing? What was there, except that which was then promised him too: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^4]. By the name of the thigh the flesh is signified. From the flesh of Abraham, through Isaac and Jacob, and not to mention many things, through Mary, our Lord Jesus Christ.

**2.** But that the root was in the Patriarchs, whence shall we show? Let us question Paul. The Gentiles now believing in Christ, and as it were desiring to be proud against the Jews who crucified Christ; when from that very people too came another wall, meeting at the corner, that is, in Christ himself, the wall of the uncircumcision coming from a different direction, that is, of the Gentiles: when, then, the Gentiles lifted themselves up, he so represses them: *For if you,* he says, *cut from a natural wild olive, are grafted into them, do not glory against the branches. For if you glory: you do not bear the root, but the root you*[^5]. Therefore from the root of the Patriarchs he says certain branches were broken off on account of unbelief, and the wild olive grafted in there, that it might be a partaker of the fatness of the olive, that is, the Church coming from the Gentiles. And who grafts a wild olive into an olive? The olive is wont to be grafted into the wild olive; the wild olive into the olive we have never seen. For whoever does it, will not find berries except of the wild olive. For what is grafted, this grows, and the fruit of that thing is found: not the fruit of the root is found, but of the graft. Showing this, that God by his omnipotence brought it about, that a wild olive be grafted into the root of the olive, and give not wild berries, but the olive; the Apostle, recalling this to the omnipotence of God, says: *If you are cut from a natural wild olive, and against nature are grafted into a good olive, do not glory,* he says, *against the branches. But you say,* he says: *The branches were broken off, that I might be grafted in. Well; on account of unbelief they were broken off: but you, stand by faith*; *do not relish high things, but fear*[^6]. For what is: *Do not relish high things*? Do not be proud, because you are grafted in; but fear lest you be broken off through unbelief, as they were broken off. *On account of unbelief,* he says, *they were broken off: but you, stand by faith; do not relish high things, but fear. For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.* And there follows a good place, and necessary, and all to be heard: *You see, then,* he says, *the goodness and severity of God: toward those indeed who were broken off, severity; but toward you who are grafted in, goodness, if you remain in the goodness; otherwise,* that is, if you do not remain in the goodness, *you too will be cut off: and they, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in*[^7].

**3.** In the time of the Old Testament, then, brethren, the promises from our God to that carnal people were earthly and temporal. An earthly kingdom was promised; that land was promised, into which also, freed from Egypt, they were led: through Joshua son of Nun they were brought into the land of promise, where also the earthly Jerusalem was built, where David reigned. They received the land, freed from Egypt, crossing through the Red Sea; the windings and wanderings of the wildernesses being ended, they received the land, received the kingdom: then, after the kingdom was received, because they had received earthly things, they began, by the merit of their sins, to be assailed, stormed, taken captive; at last the very city too was overturned. Such were also those promises, not going to endure, by which yet were figured the future promises going to endure; so that that whole course of temporal promises was a figure, and a certain prophecy of things to come. And so, when that kingdom failed, where David, son of Jesse, reigned, that is, a certain man, even if a prophet, even if a saint, because he saw and foresaw Christ to come, of whose seed even according to the flesh he was going to be born; yet a man, yet not yet Christ, yet not yet our king, the Son of God, but king David, son of Jesse: because, then, that kingdom was going to fail, through which kingdom received God was then praised by the carnal ones; for they held this alone for a great thing, that they had been freed temporally from those by whom they were oppressed, and had escaped the pursuing enemies through the Red Sea, and had been led through the wilderness, and had found a homeland and a kingdom; from this alone they praised God, not yet understanding what in those figures God presignified and promised: when, then, the things failed for which the carnal people praised God, to whom that David reigned, *the hymns of David* failed; not of the Son of God, but *of the son of Jesse.* We have passed by, as the Lord willed, the perilous place of the title of the present psalm: you have received why it was said: *The hymns of David, son of Jesse, have failed.*

**4.** Whose voice is the Psalm? *Asaph's.* What is, *Asaph*? As we find in the interpretations translated from the Hebrew tongue into Greek, and from Greek to us into Latin, *Asaph* is interpreted "Synagogue." It is, then, the voice of the Synagogue. But you, when you hear "Synagogue," do not at once detest it as the killer of the Lord. That Synagogue was indeed the killer of the Lord; no one doubts: but remember that from the Synagogue were the rams, whose sons we are. Whence it is said in the psalm: *Bring to the Lord the sons of rams*[^8]. What rams from there? Peter, John, James, Andrew, Bartholomew, and the other Apostles. Hence too that one, first Saul, afterward Paul: that is, first proud, afterward humble. For Saul, whence the name of Saul was given, you know that he was a proud and unbridled king. Not as by some boasting did the Apostle change his name for himself; but from Saul he was made Paul, from proud little. For Paul is "Little." Do you wish to know what Saul is? Hear him himself, now Paul, recalling what he was through his malice, and what he now is through the grace of God; hear how he was Saul, and how he is Paul: *I who was,* he says, *before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent*[^9]. You have heard Saul; hear also Paul: *For I am,* he says, *the least of the Apostles.* What is least, except, I am Paul? And there follows: *Who am not worthy to be called an Apostle.* Why? Because I was Saul. What is, I was Saul? Let him himself say it: *Because I persecuted,* he says, *the Church of God; but by the grace of God,* he says, *I am what I am*[^10]. He took from himself all his greatness; least now in himself, great in Christ. And this Paul, what does he say? *God has not rejected his people,* the people coming from the Jews, *his people,* he says, *whom he foreknew: for I too am an Israelite of the seed of Abraham, the tribe of Benjamin*[^11]. Therefore both Paul is for us from the Synagogue; and Peter and the other Apostles from the Synagogue. And so, when you hear the voice of the Synagogue, do not attend to the merit, but mark the offspring. Therefore in this psalm the Synagogue speaks, the hymns of David, son of Jesse, having failed; that is, the temporal things having failed, by which God was wont to be praised by the carnal people. But why did those fail, except that others might be sought? That what others might be sought? Those which were not there? No; but those which were there covered by figures: not those which were not now there; but there as it were in the root they were hidden by certain secrets of mysteries. Which? These, says the Apostle himself, were our figures.

**5.** And attend now briefly to that figure of ours. The people of Israel under the dominion of Pharaoh and the Egyptians; the Christian people, before faith now predestined by God, and still serving the demons and the devil their prince: behold a people subjugated to the Egyptians, serving their own sins; for the devil cannot have dominion except through our sins. The people is freed from the Egyptians through Moses; the people is freed from the past life of sins through our Lord Jesus Christ. That people crosses through the Red Sea; this one through Baptism. In the Red Sea all the enemies of that people die; in Baptism all our sins die. Attend, brethren. After that Red Sea the homeland is not at once given, nor, as though now enemies were lacking, is there a secure triumph; but there remains the solitude of the wilderness, there remain enemies lying in wait on the way: so too after Baptism there remains the Christian life in temptations. In that wilderness there was sighing for the promised homeland; what else do Christians, now washed by Baptism, sigh for? Surely they do not now reign with Christ? It has not yet come to our land of promise; but one not going to fail, for there the hymns of David will not fail. So let all the faithful hear; let them know where they are: they are in the wilderness; they sigh for the homeland. The enemies are dead in Baptism; but they pursue from behind. What is, pursuing from behind? Before our face we have things to come; behind us things past: all our past sins are blotted out in Baptism; the things by which we are now tempted do not pursue from behind, but lie in wait on the way. Whence the Apostle, still set on the way of this wilderness: *The things behind,* he says, *forgetting, stretching forth to the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the palm of the supernal calling of God*[^12]; as though he said: To the homeland of the supernal promise of God. And there now, brethren, in the wilderness, whatever that people suffered, and whatever God bestowed on them; whatever those scourges were, whatever gifts, are significations of the things which in this solitude of this life, walking in Christ, seeking the homeland, we both receive for consolation, and suffer for testing. It is no wonder, then, that that failed which figured the future. For the people was led to the homeland of the promise; surely not going to stand forever? If this were so, it would not be a figure, but the reality. But because it was a figure, that people was led to something temporal. If it was led to something temporal, it was needful that it fail, and by its failing be compelled to seek that which would never fail.

**6.** The Synagogue, then, that is, those who worshiped God there godly, but yet on account of earthly things, on account of these present things: for there are ungodly men who seek the goods of present things from demons; but this people was for this reason better than the Gentiles, that, although they sought present and temporal goods, yet they sought them from the one God, who is the creator of all things, both spiritual and corporeal: when, then, those godly according to the flesh attended, that is, that Synagogue which was among the goods, good for a time, not spiritual, such as were there the Prophets, such as the few understanders of the heavenly, eternal kingdom; therefore that Synagogue noticed what it had received from God, and what God had promised that people, the abundance of earthly things, the homeland, peace, earthly happiness: but in all these there were figures; and, not understanding what lay hidden there in the figured things, it thought that God gave this for a great thing, nor had anything better that he could give to those loving and serving him. It attended, and saw certain sinners, ungodly, blasphemers, servants of demons, sons of the devil, living in great wickedness and pride, abounding in such earthly, temporal things, for which very things it served God: and there was born a most evil thought in the heart, which made the feet totter, and nearly slip from the way of God. And behold, this thought was in the people of the Old Testament: would that it be not in our carnal brethren, when now the happiness of the New Testament is openly preached! For what did that Synagogue then say? what did that people say? We serve God, and are rebuked, scourged; the things we love are withdrawn from us, and which we had received from God for a great thing; but those wicked, most worthless, proud, blaspheming, restless men abound in all the things for which we serve God: I think God is served in vain. This is the sense of the Psalm, of the failing and tottering people: while it considers the earthly goods for which it served God to abound to those who do not serve God, it totters, and nearly slips: and with those hymns it fails, because in such hearts the hymns failed. What is, in such hearts the hymns failed? Because they now thought such things, they did not praise God. For how would they praise God, to whom he seemed as it were perverse, giving the ungodly so great happiness, and taking it away from those serving him? God did not seem good to them: and to those to whom God did not seem good, certainly he was not praised; and by those by whom God was not praised, the hymns failed in them. But afterward this people understood what God was admonishing it to seek, when he withdrew these temporal things from his servants, and gave them to his enemies, blasphemers, ungodly: admonished, it understood that before all things that God gives, both to the good and to the evil, and sometimes takes away, both from the good and from the evil, before all things he reserves something for the good. What is, he reserves something for the good? what does he reserve for them? Himself. Now, as far as I reckon, the Psalm runs on; it is understood in the name of the Lord. Hear one recalling and repenting who had erred, by thinking God not good who gives earthly goods to the evil, and takes them away from his servants. For he understood what God reserved for his servants; and, reconsidering, and chastising himself, he burst out in this manner.

**7.** *How good is God to Israel!*[^13] But to whom? *To the upright of heart.* To the perverse, what? He seems perverse. So too in another psalm it says: *With the holy you will be holy, and with the innocent man you will be innocent, and with the perverse you will be perverse*[^14]. What is, *you will be perverse with the perverse*? The perverse man will think you perverse. Not that God is in any way perverted. Far be it: what is, is. But as the sun, to one having pure, sound, vigorous, strong eyes, appears tranquil, but into bleary eyes hurls as it were rough darts; it strengthens the one beholding it, it tortures this one, not changed, but the changed; so when you begin to be perverse, God too will seem to you perverse; you are changed, not he. It will be, then, to you a punishment, what to the good is joy. This one, recalling this: *How good,* he says, *is God to Israel, to the upright of heart!*

**8.** [v. 2] But what of you? *But my feet were almost moved.*[^15] When were the feet moved, except when the heart was not upright? Whence was the heart not upright? Hear: *A little less my steps were poured out.* What he said, *almost;* this he said, *a little less*: and what he said, *my feet were almost moved;* this he said, *my steps were poured out.* The feet were almost moved, the steps were a little less poured out. *The feet moved*: but whence, *the feet moved, and the steps poured out*? *The feet moved,* to err; *the steps poured out,* to a fall; not altogether, but *almost.* For what is it? I was now going into error, I had not gone: I was now falling, I had not fallen.

**9.** [v. 3] But why this too? *Because I was zealous,* he says, *over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners.*[^16] I attended to the sinners; I saw them have peace. What peace? Temporal, fleeting, perishable, and earthly; but yet such as I too desired from God. I saw those who did not serve God to have that which I desired so that I might serve God; and my feet were moved, and my steps were almost poured out.

**10.** [vv. 4-5] But why sinners have this, he says briefly: *Because there is no declination for their death; and a firmament in their scourge. In the labors of men they are not, and with men they will not be scourged.*[^17] Now I have understood, he says, why they have peace, and flourish on the earth; because there is no declination for their death, that is, because a sure and eternal death awaits them, which neither declines from them, nor can they decline from it. *Because there is no declination for their death; and a firmament in their scourge.* And there is a firmament in their scourge: for their scourge is not temporal, but firm unto eternity. On account of these evils, then, which will be eternal for them, what now? *In the labors of men they are not, and with men they will not be scourged.* Surely the devil himself too is not scourged with men, for whom yet eternal punishment is prepared?

**11.** [v. 6] Therefore what of these, while they are not scourged, while they do not labor with men? *Therefore,* he says, *pride has held them.*[^18] Attend to them proud, undisciplined; attend to a bull devoted to the victim, permitted to wander freely, and lay waste what it can, until the day of slaughter. Now it is good, brethren, that in the very words of the Prophet we hear this as it were bull of which I spoke. For so Scripture mentions it elsewhere too: it says they are as it were prepared for the victim, and spared for an evil liberty. *Therefore,* he said, *pride has held them.* What is, *pride has held them*? *They are clothed round about with their iniquity and impiety.* He did not say, covered; but, *clothed round about,* on every side covered with their impiety. Deservedly the wretches neither see, nor are seen, because they are clothed round about, nor are their inner parts seen. For whoever could behold the inner parts of evil men, as if happy according to the time; whoever saw their fierce consciences, whoever could inspect their souls beaten by so great disturbances of desires and fears, would see them wretched, even when they are called happy. But because *they are clothed round about with their iniquity and impiety,* they do not see; but neither are they seen. The Spirit who says these things of them knew them; and with that eye we ought to inspect such men, with which we know how to see, if the covering of impiety be taken from our eyes: let us see those men; and when they are happy, let us flee; and when they are happy, let us not imitate them: nor let us wish from our Lord God such things for ourselves for a great thing, as those merited to receive who do not serve him. He reserves something else; something else is to be desired: but what it is, hear.

**12.** [v. 7] First let these be described. *Their iniquity will come forth as from fatness.*[^19] See whether that bull was not recognized. Hear, brethren: not in any way at all is what he said to be passed by: *Their iniquity will come forth as from fatness.* For there are evil men, but evil by leanness; therefore evil, because lean, that is, slight, small, affected by a certain wasting of necessity: and they too are evil, and to be condemned; for every necessity is rather to be borne, than any iniquity to be perpetrated. Yet it is one thing to sin from necessity; another in abundance. A poor beggar commits theft; iniquity came forth from leanness: a rich man, abounding in so great things, why does he plunder another's goods? His iniquity from leanness: this one's from fatness came forth. Therefore to the lean man, when you say: Why did you do this? Humbly afflicted and cast down he answers: Necessity compelled me. Why did you not fear God? Want drove me. Say to the rich man: Why do you do these things, and do not fear God? if yet you are great enough to be able to say it. See whether he even deigns to hear; see whether even in you iniquity will not come forth from his fatness. For now to their teachers and rebukers they declare enmities; and they become enemies of those who speak the truth, now accustomed to be soothed with the words of flatterers, with a soft ear, a heart not sound. Who would say to a rich man: You did wrong to plunder another's goods? Or perhaps if anyone dared to say it, and he is such that the other cannot resist him, what does he answer? He speaks the whole in contempt of God. Why? Because he is proud. Why? Because he is fat. Why? Because he is devoted to the victim. *Their iniquity will come forth as from fatness.*

**13.** *They have passed over into the disposition of the heart.*[^20] There within they have passed over. What is, *they have passed over*? They have transgressed the way. What is, *they have passed over*? They have exceeded the bounds of the human race; they do not think themselves equal to the rest. They have passed over, I say, the bounds of the human race. When you say to such a man: This poor man is your brother; you had the same parents, Adam and Eve: do not attend to your swelling, do not attend to the pride into which you are lifted up; even if a household surrounds you, even if numerous gold and silver, even if a marbled house contains you, even if paneled roofs cover you, the sky, the roof of the world, covers both you and the poor man together; but you differ from the poor man by things not yours, applied from outside: see yourself in them, not them in you. Attend to yourself, who you are toward the poor man; yourself, not what you have. For why do you despise your brother? In the inmost parts of your mothers you were both naked. Certainly even when you have gone out of this life, and these flesh, the soul breathed out, have rotted, let the bones of the rich man and the poor man be distinguished! I speak of the equal condition, of the very lot of the human race in which all are born: and the rich man, while he is here, and the poor man, will not be here forever; and as the rich man did not come rich, so neither does the rich man depart rich: the entrance is the same for both, and the exit equal. I add that perhaps you will change places. Now everywhere the Gospel is preached: attend to a certain poor man, full of sores, who lay before the gate of the rich man, and desired to be filled with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table; attend also to that equal of yours who was clothed in purple and fine linen, and feasted splendidly every day. It happened, namely, that that needy one died, and was carried away by the angels into the bosom of Abraham: but that one died, and was buried; for perhaps no one cared for his burial: and when that rich man in hell was in torments, did he not lift up his eyes, and see in infinite joy him whom he had despised before his gate, and desire a drop of water from his finger, who had desired the crumbs falling from his table? Brethren, how great was that labor of the poor man? How long were those delights of the rich man? But what they exchanged is perpetual. Therefore, because for that one there was no declination in death, and there was a firmament in his scourge; in the labors of men he was not, and among men he was not scourged: but that one, scourged here, rested there; because he scourges every son whom he receives. But to whom do you say these things? To one feasting splendidly, and clothing himself daily in purple and fine linen. To whom do you say it? To one who passed over *into the disposition of the heart.* Deservedly he is going to say late: *Send Lazarus; let him tell my brothers*[^21]; when the fruit of repentance is not granted him. For it is not that repentance is not given; but the repentance will be everlasting and no salvation after the repentance. Therefore these *have passed over into the disposition of the heart.*

**14.** [v. 8] *They thought and spoke malignity.*[^22] But men speak malignity even with fear; but these, how? *They spoke iniquity on high.* Not only did they speak iniquity; but even clearly, all hearing, proudly: I do it, I show it; you will feel with whom you have to do, I will not let you live. You would at least think these things, not even pour them out: at least within the enclosures of thought the evil desire would be restrained, or it would rein it in within its own thought. Why? is he perhaps lean? *Their iniquity will come forth as from fatness. They spoke iniquity on high.*

**15.** [v. 9] *They set their mouth in heaven, and their tongue passed over the earth.*[^23] What is this, *passed over the earth*? That which was said: *They set their mouth in heaven.* For this is, *passed over the earth,* they pass over all earthly things. What is, to pass over all earthly things? He does not think that man can suddenly die, when he speaks: as though always going to live he threatens: his thought transcends earthly frailty; he does not know with what kind of vessel he is covered, he does not know what is written in another place about such: *His spirit will go out, and will return to its earth; in that day all his thoughts will perish*[^24]. But these, not thinking of their last day, speak pride; and set their mouth in heaven, and transcend the earth. If he thought of his last day, that is, the last day of his own judgment, a robber sent into prison, nothing would be more savage than he; and yet he could escape. Whither do you flee, that you may not die? That day will be sure. What is it, long, that you are going to live? How much is long which has an end, even if it were long? To this is added that it is not: and it is not long, and even that which is called long is uncertain. Why does he not think of this? Because he set his mouth in heaven, and his tongue passed over the earth.

**16.** [v. 10] *Therefore my people will return here.*[^25] Now *Asaph* himself returns here. For he saw these things abound to the unjust, saw them to the proud: he returns to God, and begins to seek and discuss. But when? *When full days are found in them.* What is, *full days*? *But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son*[^26]. That is the fullness of time, when he came to teach temporal things to be despised, not to hold for a great thing whatever evil men desire; to suffer whatever evil men fear. He became the way, called back to the inmost thought, admonished what should be sought from God. And see from what thought, struck back into itself, and in a manner recalling the surge of its own onrush, there is a passing over to choosing the true things. *Therefore my people will return here; and full days will be found in them.*

**17.** [v. 11] *And they said: How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High?*[^27] See through what thought they pass. Behold the unjust are happy; God does not care for human affairs. Does he truly know what we do? See what is said. We beseech, brethren; let not Christians now say: *How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High.*

**18.** [v. 12] For whence does it seem to you that God does not know, and that there is no knowledge in the Most High? He answers: *Behold, these very sinners, and abounding in the age, have obtained riches.*[^28] Both they are sinners, and they have obtained abundant riches in the age. He confessed that for this reason he was unwilling to be a sinner, that he might have riches. The carnal soul had sold its righteousness for visible and earthly things. What sort of righteousness, which is held on account of gold; as though gold were more precious than righteousness itself, or when anyone denies another's goods, he suffers a greater loss to whom he denies, than he who denies it to him! That one loses a garment, this one faith. *Behold, these very sinners, and abounding in the age, have obtained riches.* Hence, then, God does not know, and hence there is no knowledge in the Most High!

**19.** [v. 13] *And I said: Therefore in vain I have justified my heart.*[^29] When I serve God, and do not have these things; they do not serve him, and abound in them: *Therefore in vain I have justified my heart, and washed my hands among the innocent.* This I did in vain. Where is the reward of my good life? where is the prize of my service? I live well, and am in want; and the unjust man abounds. *And I washed my hands among the innocent.*

**20.** [v. 14] *And I was scourged all the day long.*[^30] From me the scourges of God do not withdraw. I serve well; and am scourged: he does not serve, and is adorned. He made for himself a great question. The soul is agitated, the soul passes over, going to pass over to despising earthly things and craving eternal things. It is the passage of the soul itself in this thought: where it tosses in a certain storm, it is going to come to the harbor. And as the sick are wont, who are more slowly ill, when health is far off, when health is near to swelter more: physicians call it a critical access, through which there is a passing over to health; greater heat there, but leading to salvation; greater burning there, but the refreshment near: so, then, here too he swelters. For they are perilous words, brethren, troublesome, and almost blasphemous: *How does God know*? This is also *almost*: he did not say: God does not know; he did not say: There is no knowledge in the Most High: but as if asking, hesitating, doubting. This is what he said a little before: *My steps were almost poured out. How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High?* He does not affirm it; but the very doubt is perilous. Through peril he passes over to health. Hear now the health: *Therefore in vain I have justified my heart, and washed my hands among the innocent: and I was scourged all the day long, and my reproof in the morning.* Reproof is rebuke: he who is reproved, is rebuked. What is, *in the morning*? It is not deferred. That of the ungodly is deferred; mine is not deferred: theirs is late, or none; mine is *in the morning. And I was scourged all the day long, and my reproof in the morning.*

**21.** [v. 15] *If I said: I will recount thus*[^31]: that is, I will teach thus. How are you going to teach? That there is no knowledge in the Most High? that God does not know? Do you wish to bring forth this sentence, that those who live justly live justly in vain, that the just man has lost his service, that God rather favors the evil, or cares for no one? do you wish to say this, to recount this? He restrains himself, authority checking him. By what authority? A man wishes at some time to burst out into that sentence; but he is called back by the Scriptures saying that one must always live well, saying that God cares for human affairs, that he distinguishes between the godly and the ungodly. Therefore this one too, wishing now to bring forth that sentence, calls himself back. And what does he say? *If I said: I will recount thus: behold, I have reprobated the generation of your sons.* I have reprobated the generation of your sons, if I will recount thus: I will reprobate the generation of the just. As also certain copies have: *Behold, to the generation of your sons to whom was I in tune?* that is, to whom of your sons was I in tune? that is, with whom did I agree, to whom was I fitted? I was out of harmony with all, if I teach thus. For he is in tune who is in concord; but he who is not in concord, is not in tune. Am I going to say something other than Abraham said, than Isaac said, than Jacob said, than the Prophets said? For they all said that God cares for human affairs: am I going to say that he does not care? Is there greater prudence in me, than in them? is there greater understanding in me than in them? The most healthful authority called back the thought from impiety.

**22.** [v. 16] And what follows? *If I said, I will recount thus: behold, I have reprobated the generation of your sons.* Therefore, lest he reprobate it, what did he do? *And I undertook to know.*[^32] He undertook to know: let God be present to him, that he may know. Meanwhile, brethren, he is called back from a great fall, when he no longer presumes himself to know, but undertook to know what he did not know. For now he wished as it were to seem to know, and to proclaim that God has no care for human affairs. For this has become the most worthless and impious teaching of the unjust. Know, brethren, that many discuss, and say that God does not care for human affairs, that all things are ruled by chance, or that our wills are subject to the stars, that each one is dealt with not according to his merits, but by the necessity of his stars: an evil teaching, an impious teaching! Into these this one was going, whose *feet were almost moved,* and whose *steps a little less poured out*; into this error he was going: but because he was not in tune with the generation of the sons of God, he undertook to know; and condemned the knowledge in which he did not agree with the just of God. And let us hear what he says; because he undertook to know, and was helped, and learned something, and indicated it to us. *And I undertook,* he says, *to know. This is a labor before me.* Truly a great labor, to know how both God cares for human affairs, and it is well with the evil, and the good labor! A great force of the question! therefore: *This is a labor before me.* As though there stands before my face a certain wall: but you have the voice of the psalm: *In my God I will pass over the wall*[^33]. *This is a labor before me.*

**23.** [v. 17] You speak truly: there is labor before you; but before God there is no labor: set yourself before God where there is no labor, and there will be no labor for you either. And he did this; for he says how long there is labor before him: *Until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand concerning the last things.*[^34] A great matter, brethren. Now for a long time I labor, he says, and before my face I see a certain as it were inextricable labor, that I may know how God is both just, and cares for human affairs, and is not unjust, in that those sinning and committing crimes have happiness on this earth; but the godly and those serving God are very often wearied in temptations and labors: it is a great difficulty to know this; but *until I enter into the sanctuary of God.* For in the sanctuary of God what is furnished you, that you may solve this question? *And I will understand,* he says, *concerning the last things;* not concerning present things. I, he says, from the sanctuary of God turn my eye to the end; I pass over present things. This whole that is called the human race, all that mass of mortality is going to come to the examination, is going to come to the balance; the works of men will be weighed there. A cloud wraps all things now; but to God the merits of individuals are known. *And I will understand,* he says, *concerning the last things*: but not from myself; for before me there is labor. Whence shall *I understand concerning the last things*? Let me enter into the sanctuary of God. There, then, he understood, and whence now these are happy.

**24.** [v. 18] *But yet on account of their deceitfulness you have set it for them.*[^35] Because they are deceitful, that is, fraudulent; because they are deceitful, they suffer deceits. What is this, because they are fraudulent, they suffer fraud? They wish to commit fraud against the human race in all their wickednesses; they too suffer fraud, in that they choose earthly goods, and leave the eternal. Therefore, brethren, in that they commit fraud, they suffer fraud. Which I said a while ago, brethren; what sort of heart has he who, that he may gain a garment, loses faith? Did he from whom he took the garment suffer fraud, or this one who is struck with so great a loss? If a garment is more precious than faith, that one is afflicted with the greater loss: but if faith incomparably exceeds the whole world, that one will seem to suffer the loss of a garment; but to this one it is said: *What does it profit a man, if he gain the whole world; but suffer the loss of his own soul*[^36]? Therefore what befell them? *On account of their deceitfulness you have set it for them: you cast them down while they were lifted up.* He did not say: You cast them down, because they were lifted up: not as though after they were lifted up, you cast them down; but in that very thing in which they were lifted up, they were cast down. For so to be lifted up is already to fall: *You cast them down while they were lifted up.*

**25.** [v. 19] *How have they been made into desolation suddenly!*[^37] He marvels over them, understanding concerning the last things. *They have failed*: truly like smoke, which, while it is lifted up, fails, they have failed. How does he say, *they have failed*? As one who understands concerning the last things. *They have failed: they have perished on account of their iniquity.*

**26.** [v. 20] *Like the dream of one rising up.*[^38] How did they fail? As the dream of one rising up fails. Suppose a man in his sleep see himself to have found treasures: he is rich, but until he wakes. *Like the dream of one rising up*: so they failed, as the dream of one waking. It is sought, then, and is not: nothing in the hands, nothing in the bed. A poor man had slept, made rich in his dreams; if he had not waked, he would be rich: he waked, found the wretchedness which he had left sleeping. And these will find the misery which they had procured for themselves: when they wake from this life, that passes which was held as it were in sleep, *like the dream of one rising up.* And lest it be said: What then? does their splendor seem to you small, does their pomp seem to you small, do the titles, the images, the statues, the praises, the throngs of clients seem to you small? *O Lord,* he says, *in your city you will bring their image to nothing.* And so, my brethren, speaking freely about this place, or wherever it is allowed; because, when we are mingled with you, we rather bear with you than teach you: in the name of Christ and in his fear I exhort you, that whoever of you do not have these things, may not desire them; whoever have them, may not presume on them. Behold, I have said to you: I do not say: You are condemned, because you have them; but, you are condemned, if you presume on such things, if you are puffed up about such things, if on account of such things you seem great to yourselves, if on account of such things you do not recognize the poor, if you forget the common condition of the human race on account of excelling vanity. For then it is necessary that God requite in the last things, and in his city bring the image of such to nothing. But he who is rich, let him be in that manner which the Apostle prescribed: *Charge,* he says, *the rich of this world not to be proud-minded; nor to hope in the uncertainty of their riches, but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy*[^39]. He took away the pride of the rich; he gives counsel. As though they said: We are rich; you forbid us to be proud, you forbid us to boast the pomps of our riches; what, then, are we going to do with these riches? Is there so far nothing to do with them? *Let them be rich,* he says, *in good works; let them give readily, share with others.* And what does this profit? *Let them treasure up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^40]. Where ought they to treasure up for themselves? Whither this one sent his eye, entering into the sanctuary of God. Let all our rich brethren shudder, abounding in money, gold, silver, household, honors; let them shudder at what was just now said: *O Lord, in your city you will bring their image to nothing.* Are they not worthy to suffer these things, that God in his city bring their image to nothing; because they too in their earthly city brought the image of God to nothing? *In your city you will bring their image to nothing.*

**27.** [v. 21] *Because my heart was delighted.*[^41] He says by what things he is tempted: *Because,* he says, *my heart was delighted; and my reins were changed.* When these temporal things delighted me, my reins were changed. It can also be understood thus: *Because my heart was delighted,* in God; *and my reins were changed,* that is, my lusts were changed, and I was made wholly chaste. *My reins were changed.* And hear how.

**28.** [v. 22] *And I was brought to nothing, and did not know.*[^42] I myself, who now say these things about the rich, at some time desired such things: therefore *I too was brought to nothing,* when *my steps were almost poured out*[^43]. *And I was brought to nothing, and did not know*: there is, then, not to be despair even of those, against whom I was saying such things.

**29.** [v. 23] What is, *I did not know*? *I was made as a beast before you; and I am always with you.*[^44] There is a great difference between this one and others. This one was made as a beast by desiring earthly things, when, brought to nothing, he did not know the eternal things: but he did not withdraw from his God, because he did not desire those things from demons, from the devil. For this I have already commended to you: it is the voice of the Synagogue, that is, of that people which did not serve idols. I was made indeed a beast, desiring earthly things from my God; but I never withdrew from my God himself.

**30.** [v. 24] Because, then, although made a beast, I did not withdraw from my God, there follows: *You have held the hand of my right hand.*[^45] He did not say, my right hand; but, *the hand of my right hand.* If it is of the right hand, the hand has a hand: *You have held the hand of my right hand,* that you might lead me. What did he set by *hand*? Power. For we say that one has in hand what he has in power; as the devil said to God about Job: *Send your hand, and take what he has*[^46]. What is, *send your hand*? Give power. He called the hand of God the power of God; as it is written in another place: *Death and life are in the hands of the tongue*[^47]. Surely the tongue has hands? But what is, *in the hands of the tongue*? In the power of the tongue. What is, in the power of the tongue? *From your mouth you will be justified, and from your mouth you will be condemned*[^48]. *You have held, then, the hand of my right hand,* the power of my right hand. What was my right hand? Because *I am always with you.* On the left I had it, because *I was made a beast;* that is, because earthly concupiscence was in me: but it was my right hand, because I was always with you. The hand of this my right hand you held, that is, you ruled the power. What power? *He gave them power to become sons of God*[^49]: he began to be now among the sons of God, pertaining to the New Testament. See how the hand of his right hand was held. *In your will you have led me.* What is, *in your will*? Not in my merits. What is, *in your will*? Hear the Apostle, who was a beast first, desiring earthly things, and living according to the Old Testament; what does he say? *I who was first a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent; but I obtained mercy*[^50]. What is, *in your will*? *By the grace of God I am what I am*[^51]. *And with glory you have taken me up.* Now whither he was taken up, and into what glory, who unfolds? who tells? Let us await this, because it will be in the resurrection; it will be in the last things: *With glory you have taken me up.*

**31.** [v. 25] And he began to think of that very heavenly happiness, and to reprove himself, because he was a beast, and desired earthly things. *For what is there for me in heaven, and from you what did I wish upon earth?*[^52] From your voice I see that you have understood. He compared to his earthly will the heavenly reward which he is going to receive; he saw what was reserved there for him, and, thinking, and burning in the thought of a certain ineffable thing, which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man, he did not say: This, or that is there for me in heaven; but: *What is there for me in heaven?* What is that which I have in heaven? what is it? how great is it? what sort is it? *And,* since that which I have in heaven does not pass away, *from you what did I wish upon earth?* You reserve for me: so I will say as I can, but pardon me; receive my attempt, the devotion of striving; for of unfolding there is no power: you reserve, he says, for me in heaven immortal riches, you yourself; and I wished from you upon earth what the ungodly too have, what the evil too have, what the criminal too have; money, gold, silver, gems, households; what many wicked too have, what many base women have, many base men: these I desired for a great thing from my God upon earth, when my God reserves himself for me in heaven. *For what is there for me in heaven?* He has to show that very "what." *And from you what did I wish upon earth?*

**32.** [v. 26.] *My heart and my flesh have failed; O God of my heart.*[^53] This, then, is what is kept in store for me in heaven: *God of my heart, and God my portion.*[^54] What is this, brothers? Let us find out where our riches are; let the human race choose its own portions. We see people torn apart by the diversity of their desires: some choose military service, some the law courts, some this or that branch of learning, some commerce, some farming; they make these their portions out of human affairs. Let the people of God cry out, *God my portion.*[^55] Not my portion for a time, but *God my portion forever.*[^56] Even if I always have gold, what do I have? Even if I did not always have God, how great a good would I have! And to this is added that he promises me his very self, and promises that I shall have him forever. So much do I have, and I never fail to have it! What great happiness! *God my portion.*[^57] For how long? *Forever.*[^58] See, then, how the psalmist has loved him; he has made his heart chaste: *God of my heart, and God my portion forever.*[^59] His heart has been made chaste; now God is loved freely, and no other reward is sought from him. Whoever seeks some other reward from God, and for that reason is willing to serve God, holds dearer what he wants to receive than the very one from whom he wants to receive it. What then? Is there no reward from God? None, except God himself. The reward of God is God himself. This he loves, this he holds dear; if he loved anything else, his love would not be chaste. If you withdraw from the immortal fire, you will grow cold, you will be corrupted. Do not withdraw; corruption will be yours, fornication will be yours. But now this man returns, now he repents, now he chooses repentance, now he says, *God my portion.*[^60] And how he delights in the very one he has chosen as his portion!

**33.** [v. 27.] *Behold, those who put themselves far from you shall perish.*[^61] This man, then, has withdrawn from God, but not far off; for *I have become like a beast of burden,* he says, *and I am always with you.*[^62] Those others, however, have withdrawn far off, because they not only desired earthly things but sought them from the demons and from the devil. *Those who put themselves far from you shall perish.*[^63] And what is it to put oneself far from God? *You have destroyed everyone who is false to you.*[^64] The opposite of this fornication is chaste love. What is chaste love? The soul now loves its bridegroom; what does it seek from him, from its bridegroom whom it loves? Perhaps it is the way people choose sons-in-law for themselves, or women choose husbands; perhaps such a one chooses riches, and loves his gold, his estates, his silver, his money, his horses, his household, and the rest. Far from it: this soul loves him alone, loves him freely; for in him it has all things, since through him all things were made. *You have destroyed,* he says, *everyone who is false to you.*[^65]

**34.** [v. 28.] But what do you do? *For me it is good to cling to God.*[^66] This is the whole good. Do you want more? I grieve for those who want more. Brothers, what more do you want? There is nothing better than to cling to God, when we shall see him face to face. For now, then, what? Since I still speak as a pilgrim: *It is good,* he says, *to cling to God;*[^67] but for now, in our pilgrimage, since the reality has not yet come: *To put my hope in God.*[^68] As long, then, as you have not yet clung to him, put your hope there. You are tossed about; cast your anchor ahead, to land. You do not yet cling to him by his presence; cling to him by hope. *To put my hope in God.*[^69] And what will you be doing here, while you put your hope in God? What will your business be, except to praise the one you love, and to make others love him together with you? See, if you loved a charioteer, would you not be carrying others off to love him along with you? The lover of a charioteer, wherever he passes, speaks of him, so that others too may love him with him. Shameful men are loved freely; yet from God a reward is sought, that he may be loved! Love God freely; begrudge God to no one. Carry him off, as many of you as can, as many as are going to possess him: he does not become cramped; you will set no boundaries in him; each one will possess the whole, and all will have the whole. Do this, then, while you are here, that is, while you put your hope in God. For what follows? *That I may proclaim all your praises in the courts of the daughter of Zion. That I may proclaim all your praises:*[^70] but where? *In the courts of the daughter of Zion;*[^71] for the preaching of God outside the Church is empty. It is not enough to praise God and to proclaim all his praises: proclaim them *in the courts of the daughter of Zion.*[^72] Strive toward unity, do not divide the people; but carry them off to the one, and make them one. I have forgotten how long I have been speaking. The Psalm is now finished, and from this fragrance I gather that I have given a long sermon: yet I am not equal to your eagerness; you are too forceful. Would that with such force you might seize the kingdom of heaven!
