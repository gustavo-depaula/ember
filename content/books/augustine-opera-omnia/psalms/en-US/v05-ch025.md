# Exposition of Psalm 118: Prologue

I have expounded all the other psalms that we know are contained in the book of Psalms, which the Church customarily calls the Psalter, partly by preaching to congregations and partly by dictation, as best I could with the Lord's gift. But I kept postponing Psalm 118, not so much because of its well-known length as because of its depth, understood by few. My brothers were very troubled that its exposition alone, among the psalms of that collection, was missing from my writings, and pressed me strongly to pay this debt; yet for a long time I did not yield to their requests and commands, because whenever I tried to think about it, it always exceeded the strength of my concentration. The clearer it appears, the deeper it usually seems to me, so that I could not even demonstrate how deep it is. In other psalms that are difficult to understand, even if their meaning lies hidden in obscurity, the obscurity itself is apparent; but in this one even that is not apparent, because its surface is such that it is thought to need a reader and a listener, rather than an expositor. Now that I finally approach its treatment, I have no idea at all what I can accomplish in it; yet I hope that God will be present and help me to accomplish something. This is what he has done in everything I have adequately expounded, although it had previously seemed difficult and almost impossible for me to understand or explain. I have decided to do this through sermons to be delivered to congregations, which the Greeks call ὁμιλίας. For I think it only right that church assemblies should not be deprived of understanding this psalm too, whose singing they commonly enjoy, as they do that of the others. But let this suffice as a prologue: now we must speak of the psalm itself, concerning which it seemed appropriate to say these introductory words.

## Exposition of Psalm 118

**1.** [v. 1.] From its very beginning this great psalm, dearest ones, exhorts us to blessedness, which there is no one who does not seek. For who anywhere either can, or could, or will be able to be found who does not wish to be blessed? What need, then, is there of exhortation to that thing which the human mind seeks of its own accord? For surely he who exhorts does this, that the will of the one with whom he deals may be roused to that for which the address of exhortation is applied. Why, then, is it dealt with us, that we may will what we cannot but will; except because all indeed covet blessedness, but how it is reached, very many do not know? And therefore this one teaches it, who says: *Blessed are the immaculate in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord.* As if he said: I know what you wish, you seek blessedness; if, then, you wish to be blessed, be immaculate. For all wish the former, but few the latter, without which one does not reach what all wish. But where will anyone be immaculate, except in the way? In what way, except in the law of the Lord? And through this: *Blessed are the immaculate in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord,* is not said to us superfluously, but a necessary exhortation is applied to our minds. For what good there is to which many are lazy, that is, to walk immaculate in the way, which is the law of the Lord, is so shown, when it is indicated that those who do this are blessed, that for the sake of that which all wish, even this may be done which very many do not wish. For to be blessed is so great a good, that both the good and the bad wish it. Nor is it a wonder that the good are for this reason good; but this is a wonder, that even the bad are for this reason bad, that they may be blessed. For whoever, given over to lusts, is corrupted by luxury and debaucheries, seeks blessedness in this evil, and thinks himself wretched, when he does not reach the pleasure and gladness of his cupidity, but does not hesitate to boast himself blessed when he reaches it. And whoever burns with the torches of greed, gathers riches by every means for this, that he may be blessed: whoever desires to shed the blood of enemies, whoever affects dominion, whoever feeds his cruelty on the slaughters of others, seeks blessedness in all crimes. These, then, erring, and by true misery seeking false blessedness, that divine voice recalls to the way, if it is heard: *Blessed are the immaculate in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord*: as if saying: Where are you going? You are perishing, and do not know it. One does not go where you wish to arrive by the way you press on: for surely you desire to be blessed; but wretched are these journeys by which you run, and they lead to greater misery. Do not seek so great a good through evils: if you wish to arrive there, come here, go this way. Leave the malignity of the perverse way, you who cannot leave the will for blessedness. In vain are you wearied by tending where, by arriving, you are defiled. But not blessed are the defiled in error, who walk in the perversity of the world; but: *Blessed are the immaculate in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord.*

**2.** [vv. 2, 3.] Attend, further, to what he adds: *Blessed are those who scrutinize his testimonies, who seek him out with their whole heart.* It does not seem to me that another kind of the blessed is commemorated by these words, than that which was said before. For to scrutinize the testimonies of the Lord, and to seek him out with the whole heart, this is to walk immaculate in the way, in the law of the Lord. Finally there follows and he says: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways.* If, then, those walking in the way, that is, in the law of the Lord, are themselves those who scrutinize his testimonies, and seek him out with their whole heart; surely those who work iniquity do not scrutinize his testimonies. And yet we know that the workers of iniquity scrutinize the testimonies of the Lord for this, that they prefer to be learned rather than just: we know too others who scrutinize the testimonies of the Lord for this, not that they may now live rightly, but that they may know how they ought to live. Such, then, not yet immaculate, walk in the law of the Lord, and through this not yet blessed. How, then, is it to be understood: *Blessed are those who scrutinize his testimonies;* when we see men scrutinizing his testimonies not blessed, because not immaculate? For the Scribes and Pharisees, sitting upon the seat of Moses, of whom the Lord says: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not do*[^1]; certainly scrutinized the testimonies of the Lord, that they might have good things to say, although they did bad things. But let us pass over these: for it will rightly be answered us that these do not scrutinize the testimonies of the Lord. For they do not seek these themselves, but seek something else through them, that is, to be glorified by men, or enriched. This is not to scrutinize the testimonies of God, not to love what they show, not to wish to arrive where they lead, that is, to God. Or if they too scrutinize the testimonies of God, although not so as to find and acquire him, but something else from them; certainly they do not *seek him out with their whole heart,* which we see was not added here in vain. For the Spirit who says these things, knowing that many scrutinize his testimonies for something else, not for that for which it was established, did not only say: *Blessed are those who scrutinize his testimonies;* but added, *they seek him out with their whole heart,* as if teaching how, or for what reason, the testimonies of the Lord are to be scrutinized. Finally, in the book of Wisdom, Wisdom herself speaking: *The bad seek me,* she says, *and do not find me; for they have hated wisdom*[^2]. Which is nothing else than: They have hated me. They seek me, she says, and do not find me, those who hate me. How, then, are they said to seek what they hate, except because they seek there not this, but something else? For they do not wish to be wise unto the glory of God, but wish to seem wise for the sake of the glory of men. For how do they not hate wisdom, which bids and teaches that what they love is to be scorned? Accordingly, *blessed are the immaculate in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord. Blessed are those who scrutinize his testimonies, who seek him out with their whole heart.* For so scrutinizing his testimonies, that they seek him out with their whole heart, they walk immaculate in the law of the Lord. Yet did not he scrutinize his testimonies, nor seek him, who said: *Good Master, what good shall I do, that I may obtain eternal life?*[^3] But how did he seek him out with his whole heart, to whose counsel he preferred his riches, and, having heard it, went away sad? For the prophet Isaiah too says: *Seek the Lord; and when you have found him, let the impious forsake his ways, and the unjust man his thoughts*[^4].

**3.** And so both the impious and the unjust seek God, that, having found him, they may not be impious, nor unjust. How, then, are they already blessed, when they still scrutinize his testimonies and seek him out, since this even the impious can do, and the unjust can do? But that the impious and unjust are blessed, who, even impious or unjust, would say? Therefore blessed in hope, as *blessed are those who suffer persecution for righteousness*: not because of what is present, while they suffer evils; but because of what will be present, *for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*: and *blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness;* not because they hunger and thirst, but because of what follows, *for they shall be satisfied*: and *blessed are those who weep;* not because they weep, but because of what follows, *for they will laugh*[^5]. Accordingly: *Blessed are those who scrutinize his testimonies, who seek him out with their whole heart;* not because they scrutinize and seek out, but because they will find what they seek out: for they seek out with the whole heart, not negligently. If, then, blessed in hope, perhaps immaculate too in hope. For surely in this life, although we walk in the law of the Lord, although we scrutinize his testimonies, and seek him out with our whole heart, *if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^6]. But this must be inquired into more diligently. For there follows: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways.* From which it can seem that those who walk in the way of the Lord, that is, in the law of the Lord, by scrutinizing his testimonies, and seeking him out with their whole heart, can now be immaculate, that is, without sin, because of the following words: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways. But he who commits sin, commits iniquity too,* says blessed John; and he added: *Sin is iniquity*[^7]. But this sermon must now be closed, nor must so great a question be confined into a narrow space.

[^2]: See Prov 1:28; 8:36
[^3]: Matt 19:16
[^4]: Isa 55:6-7
[^5]: Matt 5:10, 6, 5; Luke 6:21
[^6]: 1 John 1:8
[^7]: 1 John 3:4
