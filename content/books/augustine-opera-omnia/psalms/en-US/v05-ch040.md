# On the Same Psalm 118 (15)

**1.** [v. 57.] We now undertake to treat, in God's will, these verses of this great psalm: *The Lord is my portion;* which some have as *My portion, O Lord.* Whether this is said because whoever clings to him becomes a partaker of him, as it is written, *But it is good for me to cling to God*[^1]—for men are not gods by existing, but become so by partaking of that one who is the true God—or because men choose, or are allotted, their portions in this world, one this, another that, by which each one lives, and in a way the portion of the godly is God, by which they may live for ever: neither is an absurd sense. But let us hear what follows: *I said, I will keep your law.* What is *My portion, O Lord; I said, I will keep your law,* except that the Lord will be each one's portion thus, when he has kept his law?

**2.** [vv. 58-59.] But how does he keep it, unless the life-giving Spirit grant this and help toward it; lest the letter kill, and sin, taking occasion through the commandment, work in man every kind of desire[^2]? He is therefore to be invoked; for thus faith obtains from him what the law commands, since *whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved*[^3]. And so see what he adds: *I entreated your face with my whole heart.* And saying how he entreated, *Have mercy,* he says, *on me according to your utterance.* And as if heard and helped by him whom he entreated, *I considered,* he says, *my ways, and turned my feet to your testimonies. I turned* them, namely, away from my ways, which displeased me, so that they might go into your testimonies, and there have their way. For more manuscripts do not have *Because I considered,* as some read; but only *I considered.* And what is here set down, *And I turned my feet,* some have as *Because I considered, and you turned my feet;* so that this may rather be attributed to the grace of God, according to what the Apostle says, *For it is God who works in you*[^4]; to whom too it is said, *Turn away my eyes that they may not see vanity*[^5]. If the eyes, that they may not see vanity, why not also the feet, that they may not follow error? On account of which that too is written, *My eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he will pluck my feet out of the snare*[^6]. But whether one reads *you turned my feet* or *I turned my feet,* it is brought about by him that we act—him whose face he entreated in his heart, and to whom he said, *Have mercy on me according to your utterance,* that is, according to the word of your promise. For the children of the promise are reckoned as Abraham's seed[^7].

**3.** [v. 60.] Finally, this benefit of grace having been obtained: *I was ready,* he says, *and was not troubled, to keep your commandments;* which some have rendered *for keeping your commandments;* some, *that I might keep;* some, *to keep,* as the Greek put it.

**4.** [v. 61.] How ready he had been made to keep the divine commandments he added, saying: *The cords of sinners have entangled me, and I have not forgotten your law. The cords of sinners* are the hindrances of enemies, whether spiritual, such as the devil and his angels, or carnal, in whom, the sons of unbelief, the devil works. For this noun which is said, *of sinners,* is not derived from what "sins" are, but from what "sinners" are; which appears plainly in the Greek. So when they threaten evils by which to terrify the righteous, lest they suffer for God's law, they in a way entangle them with cords, as with their own strong and stout rope. For they drag sins along like a long rope[^8]. And hence they try to entangle the saints, and are sometimes permitted. But if they entangle the body, they do not entangle the mind, where this man was not forgetful of God's law; because *the word of God is not bound*[^9].

**5.** [v. 62.] *At midnight,* he says, *I rose to confess to you, over the judgments of your righteousness.* For this very thing, that the cords of sinners entangle the righteous, is among the judgments of God's righteousness. On account of which the apostle Peter says that it is the time *that judgment may now begin at the house of the Lord. And if it begins,* he says, *with us, what will be the end of those who do not believe the Gospel of the Lord? And if the righteous man is scarcely saved, where will the sinner and the ungodly appear*[^10]? For he says this of the persecutions the Church was suffering, when the cords of sinners were entangling it. Accordingly I think the midnight is to be understood as the more grievous part of tribulation. In which he said, *I rose;* because it did not so afflict him as to cast him down, but exercised him, that he might rise, that is, that by that very tribulation he might advance to confess more strongly.

**6.** [vv. 63-64.] But now, because these things come about by the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord, the Savior himself joins the voice of his own person, through this prophecy, to his body. For I judge that what follows pertains properly to the Head himself: *I am a sharer with all who fear you, and who keep your commandments.* As it is in the Epistle inscribed to the Hebrews: *For he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all of one; for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren*[^11]. And a little later: *Therefore, then,* he says, *because the children shared in flesh and blood, he too in like manner shared in them*[^12]. What is this but: he became a sharer with them? For we would not be made sharers in his divinity unless he himself became a sharer in our mortality. For in the Gospel too, that we have been made sharers in his divinity, it is said thus: *He gave them power to become sons of God, to those who believe in his name, who were born not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God*[^13]. And that this might come about—because he himself was also made a sharer in our mortality—there follows: *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^14]. Through this sharing of his, grace is supplied to us, that we may chastely fear God, and keep his commandments. Accordingly Jesus himself speaks in this prophecy: but some things in his members and in the unity of his body, as in some one man spread through the whole world, and growing through the rolling ages; but some things in himself, our Head. Hence too is this, that he says, *I am a sharer with all who fear you, and who keep your commandments.* And because, on account of this—that he was made a sharer of his brethren, the God of men, the immortal of mortals—therefore the grain fell into the earth, that, dying, it might bear much fruit[^15]; concerning that fruit he went on and added, *Your mercy, O Lord, fills the earth.* And whence is this, except when the ungodly is justified? That there may be progress in the knowledge of his grace, he adds, *And teach me your statutes.*
