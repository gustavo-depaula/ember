# Exposition of Psalm 36 (3)

**1.** [v. 25] The last part of this psalm had remained with you undiscussed and untreated. Accordingly, as I see, not indeed according to our arrangement, but yet not apart from his arrangement, the Lord has recalled us to pay the debt. Be attentive, then, brothers, that, if we can with God's help, we may even now pay what we know we owe. Who is it that says what we just now sang? *I was young, and behold I have grown old, and I have not seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread*[^1]. If it speaks as one man, how long a time is the age of one man; and what great thing is it that a man, set in some one part of the earth, through his whole life so brief, as human life is brief, although he came from youth to old age, did not see the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread? It is not to be wondered at. For it could come to pass that before his life there was some just man seeking bread, it could come to pass that in another part of the earth, not where he was. Then hear another thing that troubles: Behold, each one of you, who perhaps has now grown old, looking back at the courses of his age already accomplished, when he has turned over in thought those whom he has known, perhaps does not meet a just man seeking bread, or the son of a just man seeking bread: but yet he looks back to the divine Scriptures, and finds the just Abraham straitened, and suffering famine in his region, having changed for another region; he finds his son Isaac too, for the cause of the same famine, having gone into other regions seeking bread. And how will it be true: *I have never seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread?* And even if he finds this true in the span of his own life; yet otherwise he finds it in the divine reading, which is more faithful than the life of men.

**2.** What, then, do we do? Let your pious studies aid us, that we may inspect in these verses of the psalm the will of God, what he wishes us to understand. For there is fear lest each weak one, not grasping how to understand the Scriptures spiritually, go to human examples and see that sometimes good servants of God are in some need and want of seeking bread; especially also that he think of the apostle Paul, who says, *In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness*[^2]: and be scandalized within himself, saying: Is what I sang really true? is it really true that, standing in the church, I sounded forth with so devout a voice, *I have never seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread?* The Scriptures deceive us, let him say within himself; and let all his members be loosed from good work; which members within—and this is more serious—being relaxed even in the inner man, let him now desist from good work, and say to himself: Why do I work well? Why do I break my bread to the hungry, and clothe the naked, and bring the homeless into my house, attending to what is written, *I have never seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread*: when I see so many men living well, very often laboring under hunger? But if perhaps I err, so as to suppose that both he who lives well and he who lives badly live well, and God knows him otherwise, that is, to be unjust, whom I think just; what do I do about Abraham, whom Scripture itself commends as just? what do I do about the apostle Paul himself, who says, *Be imitators of me, as I too am of Christ*[^3]? Or that I too should be amid such evils as he endured, *in hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness*?

**3.** One thinking thus, and, as I said, with all his inner members loosed from the faculty of good work, can we, brothers, like a paralytic, take him up, and open the roof of this Scripture, and let him down to the Lord? For you see that it is obscure. Further, if it is obscure, it is roofed over: and I behold a certain man paralytic in mind. And I see this roof, and under this roof I recognize that Christ lies hidden. I will do, as much as I can, what was praised in those who, the roof being opened, let down the paralytic to Christ, that he might say to him, *Be steadfast, son, for your sins are forgiven you.* For so he made the inner man well from his paralysis, by forgiving sins, and by binding fast faith. But there were men there who did not have eyes to see that the inner paralytic was now healed, and they thought the healing physician was blaspheming. *Who is this,* they say, *who forgives sins? This man blasphemes. Who can forgive sins, but God alone?*[^4] And because he was God, he heard those thinking such things. They thought this true thing about God, but they did not see God present. The physician, then, did something also in the body of the paralytic, that he might heal the inner paralysis of those too who said such things. For he did what they might see, and gave what they might believe. Come, then, whoever you are so weak and languishing in heart, that, attending to human examples, you wish to desist from good works, and are loosed by a certain inner paralysis; come, if we can, this roof being opened, let us let you down to the Lord.

**4.** For the Lord himself, in his body, which is the Church, was younger in the first times, and behold he has now grown old. You know, and recognize, and understand, because in this you are set, and so you have believed, that our head is Christ; the body of that head we are. Is it we alone, and not also those who were before us? All who from the beginning of the world were just, have Christ as head. For they believed that he would come, whom we now believe to have come; and in his faith they too were healed, in whose faith we too are: that he himself might be the head of the whole city Jerusalem, all the faithful being numbered together from the beginning to the end, the legions and armies of angels being joined too, so that there may be that one city under one king, and a certain one province under one emperor, happy in perpetual peace and welfare, praising God without end, blessed without end. But the body of Christ, which is the Church, like a certain one man, was at first younger, and behold now in the end of the world it is in a fat old age; since of it was said, *He shall still be multiplied in a fat old age*[^5]. It has been multiplied through all nations, and it is its voice, attending, as one man, to his first age, and this latest, who looked back through all things, because he has all ages known through the Scriptures; and he says, exulting and admonishing: *I was young,* in the first times of the world; *and behold I have grown old,* I am also in the latest times of the world; *and I have never seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread.*

**5.** We have recognized the man younger and old, and, as it were, the roof being opened, we have come to Christ. But who is the just one, who was not seen forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread? If you understand the bread, you understand him. For the bread is the word of God, which never departs from the mouth of the just. For in his head too this just one, tempted, answered this. For when the devil said to the Lord himself, hungering and suffering famine, *Tell these stones to become bread*; he answered, *Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*[^6]. Then, my brothers, attend, when the just man does not do the will of God. For he always does it, and lives according to his will. The will of God does not depart from his heart: because the will of God, it is the law of God. And what was said of such a one? *And on his law he shall meditate day and night*[^7]. That bread you eat for one hour, and let go; that bread of the word, day and night. For when you hear, or when you read, you eat; when you think of it, you chew the cud, that you may be a clean animal, not unclean. Which wisdom too signifies through Solomon, saying, *A desirable treasure rests in the mouth of the wise; but the foolish man swallows it down*[^8]. For he who swallows it down, so that what he has devoured does not appear in him, has forgotten what he heard. But he who has not forgotten, thinks, and by thinking chews the cud, by chewing the cud is delighted. Whence it is said, *Holy thought shall keep you*[^9]. And behold, if holy thought keeps you in the chewing of this bread, *you have never seen the just forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread.*

**6.** [v. 26] *All the day he shows mercy, and lends*[^10]. In Latin "feneratur" is said both of one who gives a loan and of one who receives it: but this is said more plainly if we say "fenerat." What is it to us what the grammarians wish? You understand better in our barbarism than in our eloquence you would be left deserted. Therefore this just one *all the day shows mercy, and lends.* But let not money-lenders rejoice. For we have found a certain lender, just as we found a certain bread: so that everywhere, the roof being opened, we may come to Christ. I do not wish you to be money-lenders, and for this reason I do not wish it, because God does not wish it. For if I were unwilling, and God willing, do it: but if God is unwilling, even if I willed it, he who did it would do it to his own harm. Whence does it appear that God does not wish this? It is said in another place, *He who has not given his money at interest*[^11]. And how detestable, how hateful, how execrable it is, I think that the money-lenders themselves too know. But again I myself, rather our God, who forbids you to be a money-lender, commands you to be a money-lender; and it is said to you: Lend to God. If you lend to a man, you have hope; and if you lend to God, will you not have hope? If you have lent to a man, that is, given your money on loan, you expect to receive from him something more than you gave: not money alone, but something more than you gave, whether it be grain, or wine, or oil, or anything else; if you expect to receive more than you gave, you are a money-lender, and in this to be disapproved, not praised. What then, you say, do I do, that I may be a profitable lender? Attend to what the money-lender does. He wishes certainly to give less, and to receive more: do you too do this; give little things, receive great ones. See how widely your interest grows. Give temporal things, receive eternal ones: give earth, receive heaven. And to whom shall I give, perhaps you say? The Lord himself comes forth whom you may lend to, who commanded you not to lend at interest. Hear the Scripture, how you may lend to the Lord: *He lends to the Lord,* it says, *who shows mercy to the poor*[^12]. For the Lord does not need from you, but you have another who needs from you: to him you hold out, he receives. For the poor man does not have what to repay you: and yet he himself wishes to repay, and does not find what; there remains in him only the good will of praying for you. But when the poor man prays for you, he says, as it were, to God: Lord, I have received a loan, stand surety for me. Accordingly, even if you do not hold the poor man as a repayer, yet you hold a suitable surety. Behold, God says to you from his Scripture: Give securely, I repay. As sureties are wont to say? what do they say? I repay, I receive, you give to me. Do we think God says this too: I receive, you give to me? Plainly if God, who is Christ, which is not doubted, himself said, *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat.* And when they said, *When did we see you hungering?* to show himself the surety of the poor, the sponsor of all his members; because he is the head, they the members, and when the members receive, the head receives: *When you did it,* he says, *to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^13]. Come, greedy money-lender, see what you have given, see what you are about to receive! If you had given a little money, and he to whom you had given, for your little money, were to give you a great farm, incomparably more valuable than the money you had given; how many thanks you would give, with how great joy you would be lifted up! Hear what a possession he gives whom you have lent to: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive.* What? what you gave? Far be it. You gave earthly things, which, if you had not given, would rot in the earth. For what would you have done with them, if you had not given them? What was about to perish in the earth, has been kept in heaven. Therefore what was kept, this we are about to receive. The merit has been kept: your treasure has been made your merit. For see what you are about to receive: *Receive the kingdom, which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* On the contrary, those who were unwilling to lend, what will they hear? *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels.* And what is the kingdom called which we receive? Attend to what follows: *These shall go into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^14]. For this strive, this buy, for this lend. You have Christ sitting in heaven, asking on earth. We have found how the just man lends. *All the day he shows mercy, and lends.*

**7.** *And his seed shall be in blessing*[^15]. And here let no carnal thought arise. We see many sons of the just dying of hunger: how, then, *shall his seed be in blessing*? His seed is his remnant, whence here he sows, and afterward will reap. For the Apostle says, *But in doing good let us not fail: for in due time we shall reap, untiring. And so, while we have time,* he says, *let us work good toward all*[^16]. This is your seed, which will be in blessing. You commit it to the earth, and gather so much more: you commit it to Christ, and lose it? See that very seed more expressly named by the Apostle, when he was speaking of alms. For this he says: *He who sows sparingly shall also reap sparingly; and he who sows in blessings, of blessings shall also reap*[^17]. But you labor perhaps when you sow, and you grieve when you show mercy, because you see the wretched. For it will be better that someday we shall not have those to whom we may give these things. When all shall be changed into incorruption, no one will hunger to whom you may hold out bread, no one will thirst to whom you may give drink, no one will be naked whom you may clothe, no one a stranger whom you may take in: but here, amid hardships, temptations, griefs, groans, we cast the seed. But look to another psalm: *Going they went, and wept, casting their seeds.* Attend, because *his seed shall be in blessing: But coming they shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^18].

**8.** [v. 27] See, then, what follows, and do not be slothful: *Turn away from evil, and do good*[^19]. Do not think it enough for you, if you do not despoil the clothed. For by not despoiling the clothed, you have turned away from evil: but do not wither and be barren. So do not despoil the clothed, but clothe the naked: for this is to turn away from evil, and to do good. And what shall I have from it, you will say? Already he has commended to you, whom you lend to, what he will give you: he will give you eternal life; give to him securely. Hear also what follows: *Turn away from evil, and do good; and dwell forever and ever.* And do not suppose, when you give, that no one sees you; or that God forsakes you, when perhaps you have given to a poor man, and some loss has followed, or some sadness over a lost thing, and you say to yourself: What did it profit me to have done good works? I think God does not love the men who do good. Whence this murmuring of yours, whence this clamor, except because these voices abound? Each one now recognizes these voices, either in his own mouth, or in the mouth of his neighbor, or in the mouth of his friend. May God blot them out, and root out the thorns from his field: may he plant good produce, and a fruit-bearing tree. For why are you saddened, O man, that you gave to a poor man, and lost other things? Do you not see that you have lost what you did not give? Why do you not look to your God? Where is faith? why does it sleep so? Rouse it in your heart. Attend to what the Lord himself said to you, when he was exhorting you to good works of this kind: *Make for yourselves purses that do not grow old, a treasure that does not fail in the heavens, where no thief draws near*[^20]. This, then, recall, when you bewail a loss. Why do you weep, foolish one of little heart, or of unsound heart? Why have you lost, except because you did not lend to me? Why have you lost? Who took it from you? You will answer: A thief. Had I not forewarned you of this, that you should not put it where a thief could draw near? If, then, he who has lost grieves, let him grieve at this, that he did not put it where it could not perish.

**9.** [v. 28] *For the Lord loves judgment, and will not forsake his saints*[^21]. When the saints suffer labors, do not suppose that God does not judge, or judges perversely. He who admonishes you to judge justly, will he himself judge perversely? He *loves judgment, and will not forsake his saints.* But how the life of the saints is hidden with him, so that those who now labor on the earth, like trees in winter not having fruit and leaves, when he appears, as a new sun risen, what lived in the root may appear in the fruits. He *loves,* then, *judgment, and will not forsake his saints.* But does a saint labor in hunger? God will not forsake him: he scourges every son whom he receives. You despise him when he is scourged, you tremble when he is enriched. For whence is he scourged? By temporal afflictions. When will he be enriched? When he hears, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^22]. Do not, then, be slothful to be scourged, that you may be among those who deserve to be received. So far does he love judgment, that he does not forsake the saints, whom he scourges for a time. And since he scourges every son whom he receives, he spared not even his Only-begotten, in whom he found no fault. *For the Lord loves judgment, and will not forsake his saints.* Because, then, he will not forsake them, will he perhaps give them this which you love here, to live many years, to grow old? You do not attend to the fact that, if you wish old age to come, you wish for that of which, when it comes, you complain. Let not your soul, then, whether evil, or weak, or little, say: How is it true, *The Lord loves judgment, and will not forsake his saints*? Truly he did not forsake the three young men in the furnace praising him; the fire did not touch them: were not his saints the Maccabees, who in the fires failed in flesh, not in faith? But this, you say, has a greater question, that, since they failed not in faith, he forsook them. Hear what follows: *They shall be preserved forever.* You wished them a few years, which if the Lord gave them, he would not forsake his saints. Visibly he did not forsake the three young men, hiddenly he did not forsake the Maccabees: to those he gave even temporal life, to confound the unbelievers; but those he hiddenly crowned, to judge the impiety of the persecutor: yet neither those, nor these did he forsake, who *will not forsake his saints.* And the three young men received nothing great, if they were not preserved forever. *They shall be preserved forever.*

**10.** *But the unjust shall be punished, and the seed of the ungodly shall perish*[^23]. As his seed will be in blessing, so *the seed of the ungodly shall perish.* For the seed of the ungodly is the works of the ungodly. For we find again the son of an ungodly man flourishing in the world, and sometimes becoming just, and flourishing in Christ. See, then, how you take it, so as to open the roof, and come to Christ: do not take it carnally, for you will be deceived. But the seed of the ungodly, all the works of the ungodly, shall perish, shall have no fruit: for a time they avail something; afterward they will seek, and not find, what they did. For this will be the voice of those who lose what they did: *What did pride profit us, or what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All those things have passed away like a shadow*[^24]. Therefore *the seed of the ungodly shall perish.*

**11.** [v. 29] *The just shall possess the earth by inheritance*[^25]. Again let greed not creep in, nor promise you some great farm, lest what here you are bidden to despise, there you hope for. There is a certain earth of the living, the kingdom of the saints. Whence it is said, *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^26]. For if that is your life, understand what kind of earth you are about to receive. That is the earth of the living: but this is the earth of the dying, about to receive the dead whom it nourished alive. Such, then, as that earth, such too the life itself: if eternal life, eternal earth. And how an eternal earth? *And they shall dwell forever and ever upon it.* Another earth, then, it will be where we shall dwell forever and ever. For of this it was said, *Heaven and earth shall pass away*[^27].

**12.** [vv. 30-32] *The mouth of the just shall meditate wisdom*[^28]. Behold, that is the bread: see how gladly this just man eats, how he turns wisdom over in his mouth. *And his tongue shall speak judgment. The law of his God is in his heart*[^29]. Lest perhaps you should suppose him to have in his mouth what he does not have in his heart: lest perhaps you should reckon him among those of whom it was said, *This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me*[^30]: *And his tongue shall speak judgment. The law of his God is in his heart.* And what does it profit him? *And his steps shall not be supplanted.* The word of God in the heart frees from the snare, the word of God in the heart frees from the crooked way, the word of God in the heart frees from the slippery place. He is with you, whose word does not depart from you. But what evil does he suffer whom God guards? You set a guard in the vineyard, and against thieves you are secure; and that guard can sleep, and himself fall, and let in a thief: but he who guards Israel neither sleeps nor will slumber[^31]. *The law of his God is in his heart: and his steps shall not be supplanted.* Let him live secure, then, and among the wicked live secure, and among the ungodly live secure. For what evil can the ungodly or the unjust do to the just? Behold, see what follows: *The sinner watches the just, and seeks to put him to death*[^32]. For he says what was foretold in the book of Wisdom: *He is grievous to us even to behold, because his life is unlike that of others*[^33]. He seeks, then, to put him to death. What? Does the Lord who guards, who dwells with him, who does not depart from his mouth, from his heart, let him go? Where is that which was said above, *And he will not forsake his saints*?

**13.** [v. 33] Therefore, *The sinner watches the just, and seeks to put him to death: but the Lord will not forsake him in his hands*[^34]. Why, then, did he forsake the martyrs in the hands of the ungodly? Why did they do to them whatever they wished? Some they struck with the sword, some they crucified, some they exposed to beasts, some they burned in fires, some, leading them in chains, they wore out with long-lasting decay. Surely the Lord will not forsake his saints: *But the Lord will not forsake him in his hands.* Finally, why did he leave his own Son in the hands of the Jews? Here too open the roof, if you wish to be bound fast in every inner member; come to the Lord: hear what another Scripture says, foreseeing that the Lord would suffer from the ungodly, what does it say? *The earth was given into the hands of the ungodly*[^35]. What is *The earth was given into the hands of the ungodly*? The flesh was given into the hands of the persecutors. But God did not there leave his just one: from the captured flesh he leads forth an unconquered soul. God would leave his just one in the hands of the ungodly, if he made him consent to the ungodly: against which evil he prays in another psalm, and says, *Do not hand me over, O Lord, from my desire to the sinner*[^36]. There is need that, from your desire, you not be handed over to the sinner, lest, when you desire the present life, you rush upon him, and lose the eternal. From what desire ought one not to be handed over to the sinner? From that of which likewise it is said, *And I did not desire the day of man, you know*[^37]. For he who desires and covets the day of man, when his adversary has threatened him, that he will take away from him the human day, because he will kill him, and he will lose this life; he who does not hope for another life, fails, and consents to the enemy. But he who hears the Lord saying, *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^38]; even if the earth is given into the hands of the ungodly, the earth being captured, the spirit escapes; the spirit escaping, the earth too will rise again. The spirit is changed to the Lord, the earth will be changed into heaven. For nothing of that very earth perishes which is given for a time into the hands of the ungodly: *The hairs of your head are numbered*[^39]. Therefore there is security, but only if God is within. But if the devil is driven out, God is admitted. *But the Lord will not forsake him in his hands. Nor will he condemn him, when he is judged.* Some copies have: *And when he judges him, judgment shall be given for him.* Now *for him* is said when judgment is held with him: for we can speak so as to say to a man: Judge for me, that is, hear my cause. When, then, God shall have begun to hear the cause of his just one—*since all must be presented before the tribunal of Christ, and stand, that there each one may receive the things he did through the body, whether good or evil*[^40]—when, then, it has come to that judgment, he will not condemn him, even if for a time he seems condemned by man. Even though the proconsul pronounced sentence against Cyprian: the earthly seat is one thing, the tribunal of the heavens another; from the lower he received sentence, from the higher a crown. *Nor will he condemn him, when he is judged.*

**14.** [vv. 34, 36] But when will it be? Do not suppose now: it is the time of labor, the time of seed, the time of cold: even amid the winds, even amid the rains, sow; do not be slothful: summer will come to gladden you, in which you will rejoice to have sown. What, then, do I do now? *Wait for the Lord*[^41]. And while I wait, what do I do? *And keep his ways.* And if I keep them, what do I receive? *And he will exalt you, that you may possess the earth by inheritance.* What earth? Again, lest some farm come to mind: that of which it is said, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^42]. What of those who put us in straits, among whom we groaned, whose scandals we endured, for whom, as they raged, we prayed in vain; what will become of them? It follows: *When the sinners perish, you shall see.* And how soon you shall see it: you will be at the right hand, they at the left. But this belongs to the eyes of faith. Those who do not have those eyes of faith grieve at the happiness of the ungodly, and think themselves just without cause, because they see the ungodly flourish here. But he who has that eye of faith, what does he say? *I saw the ungodly highly exalted, and lifted up above the cedars of Lebanon*[^43]. And suppose that he was highly exalted, suppose that he was lifted up: what follows? *And I passed by, and behold he was not; and I sought him, and his place was not found*[^44]. Why was he not, and his place was not found? Because you passed by. But if you still think carnally, and this earthly happiness seems to you true happiness, you have not yet passed by: either you are equal to him, or below him: advance, and pass by; and when, by advancing, you have passed by, you attend to him by faith, you see his last things, and you say to yourself: Behold, this is not the one who had so swelled up; as if you were passing by smoke. For this too was said in this psalm above: *Failing like smoke they shall fail.* Smoke is raised on high, and goes into a swelling ball: the more it is lifted up, the more it swells. But when you have passed by, attend to what is behind you: for behind you is the smoke, if before you is God. Do not attend to the things behind out of desire, as the wife of Lot attended, and remained on the way: but look back out of contempt, and you will see the ungodly nowhere, and you will seek his place. What is his place? In a way he has power, he has riches, he has a certain rank of his own in human affairs, so that many obey him, so that he commands and is heard. This place will not be, but will pass away, so that you may say, *I passed by, and behold he was not.* What is *I passed by*? I advanced, I came to spiritual things, I entered into the sanctuary of God, that I might attend to the last things: *and behold he was not, and I sought him, and his place was not found.*

**15.** [v. 37] *Guard innocence*[^45]. Hold it, as when you were greedy, you held your purse: as you held your purse, lest it be wrested from you by a thief; so guard innocence, lest it be wrested from you by the devil. Let that be your sure patrimony, of which there are rich and poor. *Guard innocence.* What does it profit you, gain of gold, and loss of innocence? *Guard innocence, and look to uprightness.* Have straight eyes, that you may see uprightness: not crooked ones, with which you see evil men; and distorted, so that to you God too seems distorted and crooked, because he favors the ungodly, and persecutes the faithful. Do you not see how distorted a thing you see? Correct your eyes, *and look to uprightness.* What uprightness? Do not attend to present things. And what will you see? *Because there are remnants for the man of peace.* What is *there are remnants*? When you are dead, you will not be dead: this is, *there are remnants.* There will be something for him even after this life; this is that seed which will be in blessing. Whence the Lord: *He who believes in me,* he says, *although he die, shall live*[^46]: *Because there are remnants for the man of peace.*

**16.** [v. 38] *But the unjust shall perish together*[^47]. What is *together*? Forever; or, all at once into one. *The remnants of the ungodly shall perish.* But there are remnants for the man of peace: therefore those who are not peaceful are ungodly. For blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God[^48].

**17.** [vv. 39-40] *But the salvation of the just is from the Lord, and he is their protector in the time of tribulation: and the Lord will help them, and deliver them, and rescue them from the sinners*[^49]. Let the just, then, now endure the sinners, let the wheat endure the tares, let the wheat endure the chaff; because the time of separation will come, and the good seed will be rescued from that which will be consumed by fire: the one will be put into the granary, the other into eternal conflagration; because for this reason the just and the unjust were at first together, that the one might supplant, the other be proved; but afterward that the one might be condemned, the other crowned.

**18.** Thanks to God, brothers, we have paid the debt in the name of Christ; but love always holds us as debtors. For that is the one thing which, even if it is paid daily, is always owed. We have said many things against the Donatists, we have read out to you many things, many documents, many things beyond the canon of the Scriptures, they compelling us. For even if they reprove this, that we read such things to you; let us be reproved, while you are instructed. For we can answer them in this part too thus: *I have become foolish, you compelled me*[^50]. Yet, brothers, before all things guard our inheritance, of which we are secure that we are in the testament of our Father: not in some frivolous document of some man, but in the testament of our Father. From this we are secure; because he who made the testament lives: he who made the testament for his heir, he himself will judge concerning his testament. In human affairs the testator is one, the judge another: and yet he who holds the testament prevails before a judge other than himself, not before another judge who is dead. How secure, then, is our victory, when he will judge who made the testament! for even though Christ died for a time, yet now he lives forever.

**19.** Let them, then, say against us whatever they wish: let us love them even against their will. For we know, brothers, we know their tongues: on account of which let us not be angry at them; bear patiently with us. For they see that in the cause they have nothing, and they turn their tongues against us, and begin to say evil things about us, many things which they know, many things which they do not know. The things they know are our past: for we were once, as the Apostle says, foolish and unbelieving, and reprobate to every good work[^51]. In perverse error we were foolish and raving, we do not deny it; and the more we do not deny our past, the more we praise God who pardoned us. What, then, heretic, do you let go the cause, and go to the man? For what am I? what am I? Am I the Catholic Church? am I the inheritance of Christ spread through the nations? It is enough for me to be in it. You reproach my past evils; what great thing do you do? I am more severe upon my evils than you: what you reproached, I condemned. Would that you would imitate it, so that your error too might someday become past! These are the past evils which they know, especially in this city. For here we lived badly, which I confess: and as much as I rejoice over the grace of God, so over my past, what shall I say? Do I grieve? I would grieve, if I still were what I was. But what shall I say? Do I rejoice? Neither can I say this: for would that I had never been it! Yet whatever I was, in the name of Christ it is past. But what they now reprove, they do not know. There are things still in me which they may reprove: but yet it is much for them to know these. I do many things in my thoughts, fighting against my evil suggestions, and having a long-lasting and almost continual conflict with the temptations of the enemy wishing to subvert me. I groan to God in my weakness; and he knows what my heart is in travail with, he who knows my offspring. *But to me it is a very small thing to be judged by you, or by a human day,* says the Apostle; *but neither do I judge myself*[^52]: for I know myself better than they do, but God better than I. Let them not, then, insult you over us; may Christ not allow it. For they say: And who are they? and whence are they? we knew them here as evil men; where were they baptized? If they knew us well, they know that we once sailed; they know that we went on pilgrimage; they know that we went away as some, and came back as others. We were not baptized here: but where we were baptized, the Church is known to the whole world. And many are our brothers, who both know that we were baptized, and were baptized with us. It is easy, then, to know this, if any of the brothers is concerned about it. But shall we satisfy these men, and show them something of the testimony of the Church, with which they do not communicate? Deservedly they do not know us baptized in Christ across the sea, because across the sea too they do not have Christ. For he has Christ across the sea too, who across the sea too holds the communion of the universal Church. How can he know where I was baptized, when his communion scarcely crosses the sea? Yet, my brothers, what shall I say to them? Suspect what you wish about us. If we are good, we are wheat in the Church of Christ: if we are bad, we are chaff in the Church of Christ, yet we do not withdraw from the threshing-floor. You who by the wind of temptation flew outside, what are you? The wind does not take the wheat from the threshing-floor. From the very fact, then, of where you are, recognize what you are.

**20.** But who are you, he says, who speak so much against us? Whoever I am, attend to what is said, not by whom it is said. But to the sinner, he says, the Lord says: *Why do you take my testament into your mouth?*[^53] Let the Lord say this to the sinner: perhaps there is a kind of sinner to whom the Lord rightly says this; but to whatever sinner the Lord says this, he says it for this reason, that it does not profit the sinner when he speaks the law of God. Does it not profit the hearers too? Both kinds we have in the Church, the Lord saying so, both of the good and of the bad. For when the good preach, what do they say? *Be imitators of me, as I too am of Christ*[^54]. Of the good what is said? *But be a pattern of the faithful*[^55]. This we labor to be: but what we are, he knows to whom we groan. Of the bad, however, something else was said: *Upon the seat of Moses the scribes and Pharisees have sat down; what they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^56]. You see that on the seat of Moses, which the seat of Christ succeeded, even bad men sit: and yet by speaking good things they do no harm to the hearers. Why have you, on account of the bad, forsaken the seat itself? Return to peace, return to the concord which does not offend you. If I speak good things, and do good things, imitate me: but if I do not do what I say, you have the counsel of the Lord; what I say, do; but what I do, do not do; yet do not withdraw from the catholic seat. Behold, in the name of Christ we are about to go, and they will say many things. What is the end? Quickly despise our cause. Say nothing to them except, Brothers, answer to the matter. Augustine is a bishop in the Catholic Church, he carries his own burden, he is to render account to God: I know him among the good; if he is bad, he himself knows it; if he is good, not even so is he my hope. This before all things I have learned in the Catholic Church, that my hope be not in man. Deservedly, then, you reprove men, because you have placed your hope in man. Surely when they reprove us, you too despise it. For we know what place we have in your heart, because we know what place you have in our heart. Do not contend for us against them. Whatever they say about us, pass over quickly: lest, laboring for the cause of our defense, you let go your own cause. For this they do craftily: unwilling and afraid that we should speak of the cause itself, they thrust at us things by which to call us away; so that, while we are intent on clearing ourselves, we may be silent about convicting them. For indeed, you who call me bad, I too say innumerable other things: quickly take it out of the way, finish my cause, deal with the matter, attend to the cause of the Church, see where you are. Let truth speak to you from wherever; hungering, receive it; lest bread never come to you, while, fastidious and slanderous, you always seek what to reprove in the little vessel.
