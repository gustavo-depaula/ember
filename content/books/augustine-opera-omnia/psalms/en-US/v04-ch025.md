# Exposition of Psalm 95

**1.** [v. 1.] My lord and brother Severus still defers our gladness from the sermon which he owes us: for that he is held as a debtor, he acknowledges. For all the Churches through which he made his passage, the Lord made glad through his mouth: how much more, then, is this one to be made glad, for which the Lord propagated him from the rest? But what shall we do, except serve his will? Yet I said he defers it, brothers, not that he cheats us of it. And so hold the debtor, and do not dismiss him, except when he has paid. Let Your Charity therefore attend: as far as the Lord grants, let us say of this psalm something which indeed you already know; but sweet is the recollection of the truth. Perhaps when its title was pronounced, some heard with a certain wonder. For the title of the Psalm is inscribed: *When the house was being built after the captivity.* This title being foretold, you perhaps awaited in the very text of the Psalm what stones would be cut from the mountains, what masses would be dragged, what foundations laid, what beams set in, what columns raised. It sings none of these things: and if something else, does not the Psalm then disagree with its title, and bear one thing on its brow, another in its voice? Rather, nothing else, but it seeks those who understand. For it speaks of the building of a house. Let all the stones of that house understand what they have sung. For a house is built for God, not in that place where Solomon built[^1]. For he built a temple; and of that temple you heard just now what the Lord said. When the disciples marveled at the stones of the temple and the huge masses, they showed the Lord their wonder and astonishment; and the Lord to them: *Amen I say to you, there shall not be left here a stone upon a stone, which shall not be destroyed*[^2]. Not such a house is built. For see where it is built, because not in one place, in some part. For so it begins:

**2.** *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth.* If all the earth sings a new song; so it is built, when it sings: that very singing is building; but if it does not sing the old. The cupidity of the flesh sings the old: the love of God sings the new. Whatever you sing out of cupidity, you sing the old; even if the words of the new song sound in the mouth, praise is not comely in the mouth of a sinner[^3]. It is better that, new, you keep silent, than that, old, you sing; because if you are new, and keep silent, it does not sound to the ears of men: but your heart is not silent of the new song; and it reaches the ears of God, who made you a new man. You love, and you keep silent: love itself is the voice to God, and love itself is the new song. Hear that it is a new song: the Lord says: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*[^4]. All the earth, then, sings a new song: there a house is built. All the earth, then, is the house of God. If all the earth is the house of God, he who does not cling to all the earth is a ruin, not a house; that old ruin, of which that old temple had the shadow. For there the old was being torn down, that the new might be built. And how is the old torn down? *Amen I say to you,* he says, *there shall not be left a stone upon a stone, which shall not be destroyed.* The stone is Christ: but the Apostle says: *As many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ*[^5]. If whoever is baptized in Christ puts on Christ; who sets a stone upon a stone, except he who imposes baptism upon baptism? But do not be terrified; *there shall not be left one which shall not be destroyed.* For the stones for the new building, which is built after the captivity, are so gathered, and love so binds them in unity, that there is not a stone upon a stone, but all the stones are one stone. Do not wonder: this the new song did, that is, this the renewal of love did. To that very structure the Apostle joins us, and in that unity, bound together, he compacts us, saying: *Bearing with one another in love, eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace*[^6]. Where there is unity of the Spirit, one stone; but one stone made of many. How is one made of many? By bearing with one another in love. Therefore the house of the Lord our God is built, is built: this is happening, this is enacted, these very voices enact it, the readings, the preaching of the Gospel throughout the whole earth; still it is built. This house has grown much, and has filled many nations: yet it has not yet occupied all the nations: by growing it has held many, it is to occupy all; and it is contradicted by those who boast that they are its members, and it is said: It has now decreased. Still it grows, still all the nations are to believe which have not yet believed: lest anyone say: Will that tongue too believe? and will the Barbarians believe? And what does it mean that the Holy Spirit appeared in fiery tongues[^7], except that there is no hardness of any tongue which is not loosed by that fire? For we already have many Barbarian nations that have believed in Christ: whither the Roman empire was not yet extended, Christ now possesses; what is still closed to those who fight with iron, is not closed to him who fights with wood. For the Lord reigned from the wood. Who is he who fights with wood? Christ. From his cross he conquered the kings, and to those subjugated he fixed that very cross on the forehead; and they glory in it, because there is their salvation. This is enacted, so the house grows, so it is built: and that you may know it, hear the following of the Psalm; see those working and building the house. *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth.*

**3.** [vv. 2-3.] *Sing to the Lord, bless his name, announce well of his salvation from day to day.* How does the building grow? *Announce well,* he says, *his salvation from day to day.* From day to day let it be preached; from day to day, he says, let it be built; let my house grow, says God. And as if it were said by the workmen: Where do you bid it to be built? where do you wish your house to grow? Choose for us some level place, some spacious place, if you wish an ample house to be built for you. Where do you bid us to announce well from day to day? He shows the place: *Announce among the nations his glory. His glory,* he says, *announce among the nations*: his glory, not your glory. O builders, announce his glory well among the nations. If you wish to announce your own glory, you will fall; if his, you will be built up, while you build. Therefore those who wished to announce their own glory, were unwilling to be in this house; and therefore they do not sing the new song with all the earth. For they do not communicate with the whole world: and therefore they are not builders in the house, but have raised a whitewashed wall. How great things does God threaten the whitewashed wall? There are innumerable testimonies of the Prophets, whence he curses the whitewashed wall[^8]. What is a whitewashed wall, except hypocrisy, that is, pretense? Outside it shines, within it is mud. What I am about to say has already been said, but because it was said by that Spirit which the Lord has deigned to impart to us too, we have said it; and whatever now by the same Spirit we say, those said who were before us. It is not, then, to be passed over, but to be said, because it was said by the gift of God. A certain one, when he spoke of this whitewashed wall, said this: As in a wall not joined to other walls, but raised by itself, if you make a door, whoever enters is outside; so in that part which was unwilling to sing the new song with the house, but wished to construct a wall, and this whitewashed, not solid, what does it avail that it has a door? If you enter, you are found outside. For because they themselves did not enter through the door, their door does not admit. For the Lord says: *I am the door; through me one enters.* Who are they who enter through the door? Those who seek the glory of the Lord, not their own. Who enter through the door? Those who do what was said: *Announce among the nations his glory. He who enters through the door is the shepherd of the sheep,* says the Lord; *but he who climbs up by another way, he is a thief and a robber*[^9]. He who enters through the door is humble; he who climbs up by another way is proud: therefore he said the one enters, the other climbs up. But the one, by entering, is received; the other, by climbing up, is cast headlong. *Announce among the nations his glory.* What is, *among the nations?* Perhaps even a few are named nations; and still that part which raised the whitewashed wall has something to say: Why are Getulia, Numidia, Mauretania, Byzacium not nations? The provinces are nations. Let the word of God, building the house throughout the whole earth, take away the discourse of hypocrisy from the whitewashed wall. It is little that he said: *Announce among the nations his glory;* lest you suppose some nations excepted, it follows and he says: *Among all the peoples his wonders.*

**4.** [v. 4.] *For the Lord is great and exceedingly praiseworthy.* Who is the *Lord,* except Jesus Christ, *great and exceedingly praiseworthy?* You surely know that he appeared as man; you surely know that he was conceived in the womb of a woman, you know that he was born from the womb, you know that he was suckled, that he was carried in hands, that he was circumcised, that a victim was offered for him, that he grew; finally you know that he was struck with palms, spat upon, crowned with thorns, crucified, died, pierced with a lance; you know that he suffered all these things: *He is great and exceedingly praiseworthy.* Do not despise the little one, understand the great one. He was made little, because you were little; let him be understood great, and in him you will be great. For so the house is built, so the masses are raised in that house: the stones grow which are led to the building. Grow, then, understand Christ great: and the little one is great, exceedingly great. He finished the words: he wished to say how great; even if all the day he said: Great, great, what would he say? Saying all the day: Great, he would at some time finish; because the day is finished: his greatness is before the days, beyond the days, without day. What, then, would he say? *For the Lord is great and exceedingly praiseworthy.* For what shall the little tongue say to praise the great one? By saying, *exceedingly,* he uttered a voice, and gave thought something to savor: as though saying: What I cannot make sound, do you think; and when you have thought it, it will be little. What no one's thought explains, does anyone's tongue explain? *The Lord is great and exceedingly praiseworthy.* Let him be praised, let him be preached, let his glory be announced, and the house is built.

**5.** *He is terrible above all gods.* Are there, then, gods above whom he is terrible? Let us see whom he means, and we shall see why he says it. Meanwhile, before he says it, dearly beloved, attend. He who as it were seems terrified among men, *is terrible above all gods.* Did not the nations rage? did not the peoples meditate vain things against the Lord and against his Christ[^10]? Did not those fat bulls surround him? did not that roaring lion rage over him[^11], and, entering into the hearts of the raging, cry out: *Crucify, crucify*[^12]; as though he were terrified by that roaring, who *is terrible,* not above all men, but *above all gods?* For where he wishes to build the house, that very place is wooded, whence it was said yesterday: *We found it in the fields of the wood*[^13]. For he was seeking that very house, when he said: *In the fields of the wood.* And whence is that place wooded? Men worshiped idols: no wonder, because they fed swine. For he was that son who left his father, and consumed all his goods on harlots, living prodigally; he fed swine[^14], that is, he worshiped demons: and by that very superstition of the Gentiles, all the earth had been made a wood. But he uproots the wood who builds the house; and therefore it was said: *When the house was being built after the captivity.* For men were held captive under the devil, and served the demons; but they were redeemed from captivity. They could sell themselves, but could not redeem themselves. The Redeemer came, and gave the price; he shed his blood, he bought the whole world. Do you ask what he bought? See what he gave, and find what he bought. The blood of Christ is the price. Of how great worth is it? What, except the whole world? what, except all the nations? They are very ungrateful for their price, or very proud, who say either that it was so small, that it bought the Africans alone; or that they are so great, that for them alone it was given. Let them not, then, exult, let them not be proud: for the whole he gave, as much as he gave. He knows what he bought, because he knows for how much he bought. From there, because we were redeemed after the captivity, the house is built. And who are they who held us in captivity? Because those to whom it is said: *Announce,* are uprooters of the wood: that they may uproot the wood, free the earth from captivity, and construct, build, by announcing the greatness of the house of the Lord. Whence is the wood of demons uprooted, unless he be preached who is above all? Therefore all the nations had demons as gods: those whom they called gods were demons, as the Apostle says more openly: *For what the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, and not to God*[^15]. Since, then, they were in captivity for this reason, because they sacrificed to demons, and therefore the whole earth had remained wooded; this one is announced, *great and exceedingly praiseworthy.*

**6.** [v. 5.] And how is his greatness shown, that he may root out those superstitions, under which the captive people was held, whom he came to redeem, *terrible above all gods?* And as if it were said to him: Why did you say, *above all gods?* are there gods? it follows and he says: *For all the gods of the Gentiles are demons.* Let Your Charity attend. He was saying something great a little before: *The Lord is great;* and as if failing in his praise, *and exceedingly praiseworthy.* Did I not say this, that he left it to you to think what he himself could not explain in words? But explaining it himself in words, what great thing did he say to me of the Lord Jesus Christ? Is it that he is above all demons? For when he had said: *Terrible above all gods;* he subjoined: *For all the gods of the Gentiles are demons.* It is not a great thing, to be above demons: and you, if you will, will be; but if you believe in him. Is this, then, that greatness of praise: *The Lord is great and exceedingly praiseworthy?* For wishing to explain, as the human tongue could, and although the player on the instrument, the Holy Spirit, be great, yet through the narrows of the human spirit he sounds syllables, but generates thoughts: wishing, then, to explain through this tongue, what does he say? *The Lord is great and exceedingly praiseworthy.* Say, say, how greatly praiseworthy; say. *He is terrible,* he says, *above all gods.* Why did you say, *above all gods? For all the gods of the Gentiles are demons.* And is this the whole praise of him who is exceedingly praiseworthy, that he surpasses all the gods of the Gentiles, who are demons? Wait, and hear what follows: *But the Lord made the heavens.* Now, then, not above the demons only, but above all the heavens which he made. If he said: *Above all gods, for all the gods of the Gentiles are demons,* and there only the praise of the Lord remained, he would have said less than we are wont to think of Christ: but when he said: *But the Lord made the heavens;* see what difference there is between the heavens and the demons; and see what difference there is between the heavens themselves and him who made the heavens; behold how exalted the Lord is. He did not say: But the Lord sits above the heavens; for perhaps another would be supposed to have made those above which he sat: but he said: *But the Lord made the heavens.* If he made the heavens, he made also the angels: he himself made the angels, he himself made the Apostles. To the Apostles the demons yielded: but the Apostles themselves were heavens, who bore the Lord. And what Lord did they bear? Him by whom they were made. Hear that they are heavens: *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^16]. To those very heavens it is said: *Announce among the nations his glory, among all the peoples his wonders. For the Lord is great and exceedingly praiseworthy, he is terrible above all gods.* What gods? *For all the gods of the Gentiles are demons.* And he himself is terrible above all those gods. *But the Lord made the heavens.* O heavens, which he made, announce among the nations his glory! Let the house be built throughout all the earth, let all the earth sing a new song.

**7.** [v. 6.] *Confession and beauty are in his sight.* Do you love beauty? do you wish to be beautiful? Confess. He did not say: Beauty and confession; but: *Confession and beauty.* You were foul, confess that you may be beautiful: you were a sinner, confess that you may be righteous. You could make yourself foul; you cannot make yourself comely. But what kind is our bridegroom, who loved the foul one, that he might make her beautiful? How, says someone, did he love the foul one? *I came not,* he says, *to call the righteous, but sinners*[^17]. Whom do you call sinners, that they may remain sinners? No, he says. And how will they not be sinners? *Confession and beauty are in his sight.* For they confess their sins, they vomit up the evils which they had eagerly devoured; they do not return to their vomit, like an unclean dog[^18]: and there will be confession and beauty. We love beauty; let us first choose confession, that beauty may follow. Again there arises one who loves power, who loves also magnificence; he wishes to be great as the angels are. There is a certain magnificence in the angels; and so great power, that if the angels do whatever they can, it cannot be endured. And every man desires the power of the angels, but does not love the righteousness of the angels. First love righteousness, and power will follow you. For what follows here too? *Holiness and magnificence in his sanctuary.* You were already seeking magnificence: first love holiness; when you have been sanctified, you will be magnificent too. For if, in the wrong order, you first wish to be magnificent, you fall before you rise: for you do not rise, but are exalted. You rise better, if he raises you who does not fall. For he descended to you who does not fall: you had fallen, he descended, he stretched out to you his hand; you cannot rise by your own strength, embrace the hand of the one descending, that you may be raised up by the strong one.

**8.** [v. 7.] What, then? If *confession and beauty are in his sight, holiness and magnificence in his sanctuary* (this we announce, when we build the house; behold it has now been announced to the nations); what ought the nations to do, to whom those announced who uprooted the wood? Now to the nations themselves he says: *Bring to the Lord, you homelands of the nations, bring to the Lord glory and honor*: not to yourselves; because those too who announced to you, announced not their own, but his glory: and you too *bring to the Lord glory and honor;* and say: *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory*[^19]. Do not place your hope in a man. If each of you is baptized, let him say: He baptizes me, of whom the friend of the bridegroom said: *This is he who baptizes*[^20]. For if you say these things, you bring to the Lord glory and honor: *Bring to the Lord glory and honor.*

**9.** [v. 8.] *Bring to the Lord glory to his name.* Not to the name of men, not to your own name, but to his name bring glory. *Take up victims, and enter into his courts. Take up victims*: what will you bring, that you may enter into his courts? For now the house has grown, and courts have been made; let those who bring victims enter the courts. Are we to bring bulls, goats, or sheep? Far be it. *If you had wished a sacrifice, I would surely have given it.* He was commending to us the victim which we ought to offer. See whether that be the victim, of which a while ago he said: *Confession and beauty are in his sight.* Confession is the victim to God. O nations, then, if you wish to enter into his courts, do not enter empty: *Take up victims.* What victims shall we carry with us? *A sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit; a contrite and humbled heart God does not despise*[^21]. With a humble heart enter into the house of God, and you have entered with a victim. But if you are proud, you enter empty. For whence would you be proud, unless you were empty? For if you were full, you would not be puffed up. How would you be full? If you carried with you a victim, which you would bring to the courts of the Lord. Now, lest we hold you too long, let us run through the rest. See the house growing, see the building go throughout the whole earth. Rejoice, because you have entered into the courts; rejoice, because you are built up into a temple of God. For those who enter, they themselves are built, they themselves are the house of God: he is the inhabitant, for whom the house is built throughout the whole earth, and this after the captivity. *Take up victims, and enter into his courts.*

**10.** [vv. 9-10.] *Adore the Lord in his holy court*: in the catholic Church; this is his holy court. Let no one say: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there. For there will arise false prophets.* Say this to them: *A stone upon a stone shall not be left, which shall not be destroyed*[^22]: you call to a whitewashed wall; in the holy court I adore my God.

**11.** *Let the whole earth be moved before his face: say among the nations, The Lord has reigned from the wood. For he corrected the world, which shall not be moved.* What testimonies of the building of the house of God! The clouds of the heavens thunder throughout the whole earth that the house of God is built; and the frogs cry from the marsh: We alone are Christians. What testimonies do I bring forward? Of the Psalter. I bring forward what you sing, deaf: open your ears, you sing these things; you sing with me, and do not agree with me; your tongue sounds what mine sounds, and your heart is discordant from my heart. Do you not sing these things? See the testimonies of the whole earth: *Let the whole earth be moved before his face*: and do you say it is not moved? *Say among the nations, The Lord has reigned from the wood.* Or will they perhaps prevail here, and say that they reign from the wood, who reign from the cudgels of the Circumcellions? Reign from the cross of Christ, if you are to reign from the wood. For this wood of yours makes you wooden: the wood of Christ carries you across the sea. You hear the Psalm saying: *He corrected the world, which shall not be moved;* and you say it not only moved after the correction, but even diminished. Do you speak the truth; does this one lie? Do the false prophets crying: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there,* speak the truth; and does this prophet lie? Brothers, against these most open voices you hear in the corners the din: That man betrayed, and that man betrayed. What do you say? Are your voices to be heard, or the voices of God? *For he corrected the world, which shall not be moved.* I show you the world built up; take up a victim, enter into the courts of the Lord. You have no victims, therefore you are unwilling to enter. What is this? If God enjoined on you a victim, a bull, a goat, a ram, you would find what to bring: he enjoined on you a humble heart, and you are unwilling to enter. For this you do not find in yourself, because you have swelled with pride. *For he corrected the world, which shall not be moved. He will judge the peoples in equity.* Then those will bewail themselves, who now refuse to love equity.

**12.** [v. 11.] *Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth exult.* Let the heavens be glad, who declare the glory of God; let the heavens be glad, which the Lord made: let the earth exult, which the heavens rain upon. For the heavens are the preachers, the earth the hearers. *Let the sea be moved, and its fullness.* What sea? The world. The sea was moved, and the fullness of the sea: the whole world was stirred up against the Church, when it was being spread and built throughout the whole earth. Of that very stirring you heard in the Gospel: *They will deliver you up to the judgments*[^23]. The sea was moved: but when would the sea conquer him who made the heavens?

**13.** [vv. 12-13.] *The fields will rejoice, and all that is in them.* All the gentle, all the meek, all the equitable, are the fields of God. *Then all the trees of the woods will exult.* The trees of the woods are the pagans. Why do they now rejoice? Because they were cut from the wild olive, and grafted into the olive[^24]. *Then all the trees of the woods will exult*: because the huge cedar and cypress trees were cut down, and the imperishable woods were transferred to the building of the house[^25]. They were the trees of the woods, but before they went into the building; they were the trees of the woods, but before they bore the olive.

**14.** [v. 13.] *Then all the trees of the woods will exult, before the face of the Lord, because he comes, because he comes to judge the earth.* First he came, and afterward he is to come. First he came in his Church in the clouds. What are the clouds which bore him? The Apostles preaching, of whom you heard, when the Apostle was read: *We perform an embassy,* he says, *for Christ, beseeching for Christ to be reconciled to God*[^26]. These are the clouds in which he came, apart from his later coming, by which he is to come to judge the living and the dead. He came first in the clouds. This his voice first sounded in the Gospel: *Henceforth you shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds.* What is, *henceforth?* Is not the Lord to come afterward, when all the tribes of the earth will mourn themselves[^27]? He came first in his preachers, and filled the whole earth. Let us not resist the first coming, that we may not dread the second. *And woe then to those with child and giving suck.* You heard just now in the Gospel: *Beware, because you do not know at what hour he comes*[^28]. These were said in a figure. Who are the women with child, and who the women giving suck? Souls are called with child, which have hope in the world. But those which have now obtained what they hoped for, are set for the women giving suck. For example: he wishes to buy a farm; he is with child, for it has not yet been done, the womb swells in hope: he buys it; he has given birth, now he suckles what he bought. *Woe to those with child and giving suck*: woe to those who have hope in the world, woe to those who cling to those things which they have brought forth from the hope of the world. What, then, ought a Christian to do? To use the world, not to serve the world. What is this? Having as though not having. So he says, so he exhorts those whom he does not wish to be found by that day as it were with child and giving suck, so he exhorts them: *For the rest, brothers, the time is short: it remains that those who have wives be as though not having; and those who weep, as though not weeping; and those who rejoice, as though not rejoicing; and those who buy, as though not holding; and those who use this world, as though not using it: for the figure of this world passes away. I wish you to be without anxiety*[^29]. He who is without anxiety, securely awaits when his Lord may come. For what kind of love of Christ is it, to fear lest he come? Brothers, do we not blush? We love, and we fear lest he come. Do we surely love? or do we love our sins more? Therefore let us hate those very sins, and let us love him who is to come to punish sins. He will come, willing or unwilling: for not because he does not come now, is he therefore not to come. He will come, and when you do not know; and if he finds you prepared, it harms you nothing that you do not know. *Then all the trees of the woods will exult, before the face of the Lord, because he comes*: first. And afterward what? *Because he comes to judge the earth.* And *all the trees of the woods will exult.* He came first, and afterward to judge the earth: he will find those exulting who believed in his first coming, *because he comes.*

**15.** For: *He will judge the world in equity*: not a part, because he did not buy a part. He has the whole to judge, because for the whole he gave the price. You heard the Gospel, that when he comes, he says, *he will gather his elect from the four winds*[^30]. He gathers all the elect from the four winds: therefore from the whole earth. For Adam himself too (I said this once) signifies the world according to the Greek tongue. For there are four letters, A, D, A, and M. But as the Greeks speak, the four parts of the world have these as the head letters: they call the East *anatole*; the West *dysis*; the North *arktos*; the South *mesembria*: you have, Adam. That very Adam, then, was scattered throughout the whole earth. He was in one place, and fell, and as it were ground small filled the whole earth: but the mercy of God gathered the fragments from every side, and fused them with the fire of love, and made one what was broken. That craftsman knows how to do this; let no one despair: it is indeed much, but consider who the craftsman is. He remade who made; he reformed who formed. *He will judge the world in equity, and the peoples in his truth.* What is the equity and truth? He will gather with himself his elect to judge, but the rest he will separate from one another: for he is to set some at the right, others at the left. But what is more equitable, what more true, than that they should not expect mercy from the judge, who were unwilling to do mercy, before the judge came? But those who were willing to do mercy, will be judged with mercy. For it will be said to those set at the right: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* And he reckons the works of mercy: *For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink,* and the rest. Again, to those set at the left what is reckoned? That they were unwilling to do mercy. And whither will they go? *Go into the eternal fire*[^31]. This evil hearing will make a great groaning. But what did another psalm say? *The righteous shall be in everlasting memory; he shall not fear from an evil hearing*[^32]. What is, an evil hearing? *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels.* He who will rejoice at the good hearing, will not fear from the evil hearing. How will they rejoice at the good hearing? *Come, blessed of my Father.* And from what hearing will they not fear? *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels.* This is the equity, this the truth. *For he will judge the world in equity, and the peoples in his truth.* Or, because you are unrighteous, will the judge not be righteous? or, because you are a liar, will the truth not be truthful? But if you wish to have him merciful, be merciful before he comes: forgive if anything has been committed against you, give from that in which you abound. And from whose do you give, except from his? If you gave from your own, it would be largesse: since you give from his, it is a rendering. For what have you, that you have not received[^33]? These are the victims most pleasing to God: mercy, humility, confession, peace, love. These let us bring, and we shall securely await the coming of the judge, who *will judge the world in equity, and the peoples in his truth.*
