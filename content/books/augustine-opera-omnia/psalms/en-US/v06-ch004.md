# Exposition of Psalm 125

**1.** [vv. 1-2.] This psalm, which belongs to the number of the songs of ascents (about which title we have already said many things in others, and we do not wish to repeat them, lest we dull you rather than instruct you), teaches us, as we ascend and lift our souls to the Lord our God by the affection of love and devotion, not to look at men who prosper in this world with a happiness that is false and windy and altogether seductive; where they nourish nothing but pride, and their heart freezes hard against God, and becomes hard against the rain of his grace, so that it may bear no fruit. For presuming that all things abound for them which seem necessary to this life, and beyond what is necessary, they are puffed up; and although by their iniquity they are lower than all men, by their pride they think themselves higher than all men. And would that they at least counted themselves to be such as other men. But by gazing at these men sometimes and attending to them too much, even those who worship God waver and totter, as though the reward for which they worship God had perished, when they see themselves in labors, in want, in hardship, in disease, in pain, in some necessity, and see others have health of body, abound in temporal things, rejoice in the safety of their own, flourish in the brightness of honors—those who not only do not worship God, but even oppose all men; attending to them, they totter, and say within themselves what is plainly written in a certain psalm: *How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High? Behold, these very sinners, abounding in this world, have obtained riches.* And it follows: *Have I then in vain justified my heart, and washed my hands among the innocent?*[^1] Have I perhaps been foolish, in wishing to live justly and to conduct myself innocently among men, when I know that those who refuse to keep innocence have such great happiness, and, happy through iniquity, exult over the just?

**2.** But who said that there in the psalm? One whose heart was not yet upright. For that psalm of which we have just given testimony begins thus—not the one which we have now undertaken to consider and treat, but the one where it was said: *How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High? Behold, these very sinners, abounding in this world, have obtained riches. Have I then in vain justified my heart, and washed my hands among the innocent?*[^2] That psalm, then, where you see that the soul is in peril, where you see that the feet totter, begins thus: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart! But my*, he says, *feet were almost moved, my steps had well-nigh slipped.* Why? *Because I was jealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^3]. So from there he said that his feet were moved and his steps had well-nigh slipped, in a fall toward ruin away from God, because he gazed upon and looked closely at the happiness of sinners, and saw that they had peace, but he had labor. But he related this when he had already escaped, when, his heart now set right, he had cleaved to God; he related his past perils. So then: *Good is the God of Israel*: but to whom? *To the upright in heart.* Who are the upright in heart? Those who do not find fault with God. Who are the upright in heart? Those who direct their own will to the will of God; who do not try to bend the will of God to their own will. It is a brief command, that a man direct his heart. Do you wish to have an upright heart? Do what God wills; do not wish to make God do what you will. They are crooked in heart, then—that is, they do not have an upright heart—who sit and dispute how God ought to have acted; not by praising what he did, but by finding fault. They wish to correct him: it is too little that they are unwilling to be corrected by him; and they say: God ought not to have made the poor, but only the rich ought to have existed; and they alone should live. Why was the poor man made? why does he live? He finds fault with the God of the poor. How much better it would be to be a poor man of God, and to be rich from God; that is, to follow the will of God, and to see that one's poverty is temporal and will pass away, but that spiritual riches will come to one in such a way that they can in no way pass away; and to have in the riches of the heart faith, if it does not fall to one to have gold in one's chest? For if one had gold in one's chest, one would fear the thief, and would lose the gold from the chest even against one's will; but one would not lose faith from one's heart, unless one drove it out from there oneself. But it can quickly be answered, beloved. God made the poor man, to test the man; and God made the rich man, to test him by the poor man. And all things that God made, he made rightly. And if we cannot see his counsel, why he made this thus and that thus; it is good for us to submit to his wisdom, and to believe that he did well, even if we do not yet know why he did it: and we will have an upright heart, so as to presume and trust in the Lord, and our feet will not be moved, and there will come to be in us, as we ascend, that with which this psalm began: *Those who trust in the Lord are like Mount Zion; they will not be moved forever*[^4].

**3.** Who are these? *Those who dwell in Jerusalem.* They *will not be moved forever, who dwell in Jerusalem*[^5]. If we understand this Jerusalem, all who dwelt there were shut out by wars and by the overthrow of that city: now you look for a Jew in the city of Jerusalem, and you do not find one. Why, then, will *those who dwell in Jerusalem not be moved forever*, except because there is another Jerusalem, about which you are accustomed to hear many things? She is our mother, for whom we sigh and groan in this pilgrimage, that we may return to her. We had wandered from her, and had no way: her King came, and was made the way for us, that we might be able to return to her. She is that Jerusalem where *our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*[^6], as you heard in the earlier psalm of the songs of ascents, already expounded and treated for you who were present; for whom that man sighed who sang: *Jerusalem, which is built as a city; whose sharing is in the selfsame*[^7]. Those, then, who dwell there *will not be moved forever.* But those who dwelt in this present Jerusalem were moved; first in heart, afterward in exile. When they were moved in heart and fell, then they crucified the King of that heavenly Jerusalem. They were already spiritually outside, and they were shutting the King himself outside. For they cast him out of their city, and crucified him outside[^8]. And he himself cast them out of his city, that is, the eternal Jerusalem, the mother of us all, which is in the heavens.

**4.** What is this Jerusalem like? He briefly describes it. *Mountains are round about it*[^9]. Is it some great thing, that we are in that city which mountains have surrounded? Is this our whole happiness, that we will have a city which mountains have surrounded? For do we not know mountains? or what are mountains, except swellings of the earth? There are, then, other mountains, lovable mountains, lofty mountains: the preachers of truth, whether angels, or apostles, or prophets. They are round about Jerusalem; they encircle it, and make, as it were, a wall for it. About these lovable and delightful mountains Scripture speaks constantly. Take notice when you hear or read: in many places you find delightful mountains, more than we are able to mention. Yet, as far as the Lord prompts, it delights us to say many things about these mountains; and the divine testimonies from the holy Letters have occurred to us. They are the mountains that are illumined by God: and they are illumined first, so that from them the light may come down to the valleys, or to the hills, which are not of such height as the mountains. They are those through whom Scripture is held out to us in advance, whether in prophecy, or in the apostles, or in the Gospel. They are the mountains of which we sing: *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me*[^10]; because from the holy Scriptures we have help in this life. But because these mountains are not protected by themselves, nor do they provide for us of themselves, our hope ought not to be in the mountains, lest we be accursed, who put our hope in man[^11]; he had already said: *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me; My help*, he says, *is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^12]. They are the mountains of which he likewise says: *Let the mountains receive peace for your people, and the hills justice*[^13]. The mountains are great, and the hills are lesser. So the mountains see, the hills believe: those who see have received peace and brought it to those who believe. For those who believe receive justice; because *the just man lives by faith*[^14]. The angels see, they announce what they see, and we believe. That John: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^15]: he was seeing, he preached to us that we might believe. And through the mountains receiving peace, the hills received justice: for of those mountains, what does he say? He did not say: 'They have peace of themselves,' or 'they establish peace,' or 'they generate peace'; but 'they receive peace.' Whence they receive peace is the Lord. So then lift up your eyes to the mountains for the sake of peace, that your help may be from the Lord, who made heaven and earth. Again, mentioning those mountains, the Holy Spirit says this: *You who give light wondrously from the eternal mountains*[^16]. He did not say: The mountains give light; but: *You who give light from the eternal mountains*: through these mountains which you willed to be eternal, preaching the Gospel, *you give light*, not the mountains. Such, then, are the *mountains round about* Jerusalem.

**5.** And that you may know what kind of mountains are round about it: where Scripture mentions good mountains, very rarely and with difficulty—or perhaps never—does it not at once name the Lord there too, or signify him along with them, lest hope remain in the mountains. Behold, see how many things I have mentioned. *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me*: and that you may not remain there: *My help*, he says, *is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth.* Again: *Let the mountains receive peace for your people*: when he said *let them receive*, he showed that there is another fountain of peace, whence they receive. Again: *Giving light from the mountains*: but he said: You; *You who give light wondrously from the eternal mountains.* Likewise in this place, when he had said: *Mountains are round about it*; that you might not again remain in the mountains, he at once added: *And the Lord is round about his people*[^17]: that your hope might not be in the mountains, but in him who gives light to the mountains. For when he dwells in the mountains, that is, in the saints, he himself is round about his people; and he himself has walled his people with a spiritual fortification, that it may not be moved forever. But when Scripture speaks of those evil mountains, it does not add the Lord to them. Such mountains, as we have already often told you, signify certain great souls, but evil ones. For do not think, brothers, that heresies could have been made by some small souls. Only great men made heresies: but the greater they were, the more they were evil mountains. For they were not the kind of mountains that would receive peace, so that the hills might receive justice; but they received dissension from the devil their father. They were mountains, then; take care not to flee to such mountains. For men will come, and will say to you: That man was a great man, and that one was a great man. What a man Donatus was! what a man Maximian is! and what was that Photinus! and what was that Arius! All these I have named as mountains, but shipwrecking ones. You see that some flame of speech shines from them, and some fire is kindled from them. If you are sailing in a ship, and suffer the night, that is, the darkness of this life; let them not deceive you, and do not steer your ship there: there are rocks there, there great shipwrecks happen. So when these mountains have been praised to you, and it has begun to be urged upon you that you come to those very mountains, as to a help, and there find rest; answer: *In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?*[^18] For it is good for you to lift up your eyes to those mountains whence help may come to you from the Lord, that you may escape like a sparrow from the trap of the hunters, not migrate to the mountains. For a sparrow is an unstable thing; it is quickly moved, it quickly flies here and there. But you, trust in the Lord, and you will be like Mount Zion; you will not be moved forever, and you will not migrate to the mountains like a sparrow. Did he there name the mountains in such a way as to speak of the Lord?

**6.** But love such mountains, in which the Lord is. But then those mountains love you, if you do not put your hope in them. See, brothers, what the mountains of God are like. For they are so named in another place: *Your justice is like the mountains of God*[^19]. Not their justice, but your justice. Hear such a mountain, the apostle: *That I may be found in him*, he says, *not having my own justice which is from the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ*[^20]. But those who wished to be mountains through their own justice, like certain Jews or the Pharisees their leaders, are reproved thus: *For being ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not been made subject to the justice of God*[^21]. But those who are made subject are high in such a way as to be humble. And because they are great, they are called mountains; but because they are subject to God, they are valleys: and because they have a capacity for devotion, they receive an abundance of peace and transmit the overflow to the hills. Yet now you, see which mountains you love. If you wish to be loved by the good mountains, do not put your hope even in the good mountains. For what kind of mountain was Paul? when is such a one found? (We are speaking of the greatness of men.) Can anyone of such great grace easily be found? And yet he feared lest that sparrow should put its hope in him: and what does he say? *Was Paul crucified for you?*[^22] But lift up your eyes to the mountains, whence help may come to you; because: *I planted, Apollos watered*: but your help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth; because *God gave the increase*[^23]. So then, mountains are round about it. But just as *mountains are round about it*, so *also the Lord is round about his people, from this time now and forevermore*[^24]. If, then, *mountains are round about it, and the Lord is round about his people*, the Lord binds his people together into one bond of love and peace, so that those who trust in the Lord like Mount Zion may not be moved forever: and this is, *from this time and forevermore.*

**7.** [v. 3.] *For the Lord will not leave the rod of sinners over the lot of the just, so that the just may not stretch out their hands to iniquity*[^25]. Now indeed the just labor somewhat, and now sometimes the unjust have dominion over the just. In what ways? Sometimes the unjust attain to the honors of this world: when they have attained them and have been made either judges or kings—because God does this for the discipline of his people, for the discipline of his people—it cannot but be that the honor due to power be shown to them. For God has so ordered his Church that every power ordained in the world has its honor, and sometimes from those who are better. But for the sake of example I name one thing; from this infer the ranks of all the powers. The first and daily power of man over man is that of master over slave. Nearly all households have power of this kind. There are masters, there are also slaves; the names are diverse: but 'men' and 'men' are equal names. And what does the apostle say, teaching slaves to be subject to their masters? *Slaves, obey your masters according to the flesh*[^26]: for there is a master according to the spirit. He is the true and eternal master; but these are temporal, according to time. You, when you walk on the way, when you live in this life, Christ does not wish to make you proud. It has happened to you that you became a Christian, and have a man as master: you did not become a Christian for this, that you might disdain to serve. For when, at Christ's command, you serve a man, you do not serve him, but the one who commanded it. And he says this: *Obey your masters according to the flesh, with fear and trembling, in simplicity of heart; not serving to the eye, as men-pleasers, but as servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the soul, with good will*[^27]. Behold, he did not make slaves into free men, but bad slaves into good slaves. How much the rich owe to Christ, who orders their household for them! so that if there was an unbelieving slave there, Christ may convert him, and not say to him: Leave your master; you now know him who is the true master: he is perhaps impious and unjust, you are now a believer and just; it is unworthy that one who is just and a believer should serve one who is unjust and an unbeliever. He did not say this to him, but rather: Serve; and to strengthen the slave, he said this: By my example serve; I first served the unjust. For the Lord, enduring such great things in his passion, from whom did he endure them, except a master from servants? and from whom, except from bad servants? For if they had been good servants, they would have honored their master. But because they were bad servants, they did him injury. He, what did he do in return? He repaid love for hatred. For he says: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing*[^28]. If the Lord of heaven and earth, through whom all things were made, served the unworthy, prayed for those who raged and were furious, and showed himself a physician in his coming (for physicians too, though better in skill and in health, serve the sick); how much more ought a man not to disdain to serve with his whole soul, and with all his good will, and with all love, a master, even an evil one? Behold, the better serves the worse, but for a time. But what I have said of master and slave, understand this of powers and kings, of all the heights of this world. For sometimes the powers are good, and fear God; sometimes they do not fear God. Julian was an unbelieving emperor, an apostate, unjust, an idolater: Christian soldiers served an unbelieving emperor; where it came to the cause of Christ, they acknowledged none but him who was in heaven. If ever he wished them to worship idols, to offer incense, they set God before him: but when he said: Draw up the battle line, go against that nation; they at once obeyed. They distinguished the eternal master from the temporal master; and yet they were subject, for the sake of the eternal master, even to the temporal master.

**8.** But will it always be so, that the unjust rule the just? It will not be so. For see what this psalm says: *For the Lord will not leave the rod of sinners over the lot of the just.* The rod of sinners is felt for a time over the lot of the just; but it is not left there, it will not be forever. The time will come, when one God is acknowledged; the time will come, when Christ, appearing in his brightness, will gather before himself all the nations, and divide them, as the shepherd divides the kids from the sheep: he will set the sheep at the right hand, the kids at the left[^29]. And you will see there many slaves among the sheep, and many masters among the kids; and again many masters among the sheep, many slaves among the kids. For it is not the case that, because we have thus consoled slaves, all slaves are good; or that, because we have thus repressed the pride of masters, all masters are bad. There are good faithful masters, and there are bad ones: there are good faithful slaves, and there are bad ones. But as long as good slaves serve bad masters, let them bear it for a time: *For the Lord will not leave the rod of sinners over the lot of the just.* Why is this? *So that the just may not stretch out their hands to iniquity*: so that the just may for a time bear the unjust ruling, and may understand that this is not everlasting, but may prepare themselves to possess the everlasting inheritance. What inheritance? Where every domination will be destroyed, and every power, so that God may be *all in all*[^30]. Keeping themselves for this, and contemplating this in their heart, and still holding it in faith, and enduring so as to see it, they do not stretch out their hands to iniquity. For if they saw that the rod of sinners is always over the lot of the just, they would think within themselves, and say: What does it profit me that I am just? The unjust man will always have dominion over me, and I will always be a slave? So I too will do iniquity; because it profits nothing to hold to justice. But that he may not say this, faith is instilled in him, that the rod of sinners can be over the lot of the just for a time: *the Lord will not leave* it *over the lot of the just, so that the just may not stretch out their hands to iniquity*; but let them keep their hands from iniquity, and bear iniquity, not do it. For it is better to bear injustice than to do it. Whence will it not be forever? *Because the Lord will not leave the rod of sinners over the lot of the just.*

**9.** [v. 4.] This is what those who are upright in heart think, of whom I spoke a little before, who follow the will of God, not their own will. But those who wish to follow God make him go before, and themselves follow; they do not make themselves go before, and him follow: and in all things they find him good, whether correcting, or consoling, or exercising, or crowning, or purifying, or illumining, as the apostle says: *We know that for those who love God, all things work together for good*[^31]. And therefore it follows: *Do good, O Lord, to the good, and to the upright in heart*[^32].

**10.** [v. 5.] But just as the upright in heart turns away from evil, and does good[^33], because he is not jealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners[^34]; so the man of crooked heart, who is scandalized at the ways of the Lord, turns away from God, and does evil, and is baited by the sweetness of the world, and, ensnared and caught by it, pays bitter penalties. But for one who turns away from God, whose discipline he refused to bear, by the judgment of God the false happiness of the wicked becomes for him a true snare. Whence he immediately adds: *But those who turn aside the Lord will lead away into strangulation with the workers of injustice*[^35]: that is, whose deeds they imitated; because they loved the present joys of the wicked, and did not believe in the punishments to come. Those, then, who are upright in heart, and do not turn aside, what will they have? Let us now come to the inheritance itself, brothers, since we are sons. What shall we possess? what is the inheritance? what is our homeland? what is it called? Peace. By this we greet you, this we announce to you, this the mountains receive, and the hills justice[^36]. It is Christ himself: *For he is our peace, who made both one, and broke down the dividing wall of the partition*[^37]. Because we are sons, we will have an inheritance. And what will that very inheritance be called, except peace? And see that those are disinherited who do not love peace: but those do not love peace who divide unity. Peace is the possession of the devout, the possession of heirs. And who are the heirs? Sons. Hear the Gospel: *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God*[^38]. Hear too the conclusion of this psalm: *Peace upon Israel*[^39]. 'Israel' is interpreted 'seeing God'; 'Jerusalem' is interpreted 'vision of peace.' Let your charity understand: 'Israel' is interpreted 'seeing God'; 'Jerusalem' is interpreted 'vision of peace.' Who *will not be moved forever*? *Those who dwell in Jerusalem.* They will not be moved forever, then, who dwell in the vision of peace. *And peace upon Israel.* So Israel, who is 'seeing God,' is 'seeing peace': and Israel is itself also Jerusalem; because that people of God is itself the city of God. If, then, seeing peace is the same as seeing God; rightly God himself is also peace. So then, because Christ the Son of God is peace, therefore he came to gather his own, and to separate them from the wicked. From what wicked? Those who hate Jerusalem, who hate peace, who wish to tear apart unity, who do not believe in peace, who proclaim a false peace among the people, and do not have it. To these the answer is given, when they say: Peace be with you: And with your spirit—they speak falsely, and hear falsely. To whom do they say: Peace be with you? those whom they separate from the peace of the whole world. And to whom is it said: And with your spirit? Those who embrace dissensions, and hate peace. For if there were peace in their spirit, would they not love unity, and abandon dissension? So, proclaiming falsehood, they hear falsehood. Let us proclaim truth, and hear truth. Let us be Israel, and embrace peace; because Jerusalem is the vision of peace, and we are Israel, *and peace upon Israel.*
