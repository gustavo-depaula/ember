# Exposition of Psalm 71

**1.** [v. 1] *For Solomon* indeed the title of this psalm is marked beforehand; but those things are said in it which cannot suit that Solomon, king of Israel according to the flesh, according to those things which the holy Scripture says of him: but to the Lord Christ they can suit most aptly. Whence it is understood that the very name of Solomon was applied to a figurative signification, that in it Christ may be taken. For Solomon is interpreted "Peaceable"; and through this, such a name agrees most truly and most excellently with him, through whom, as mediator, having received the remission of sins, we are reconciled from enemies to God. For *when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son*[^1]. The same is that peaceable one, *who made both one, and breaking down the middle wall of the partition, the enmities in his flesh; abolishing the law of commandments in decrees, that he might create the two in himself into one new man, making peace: and coming he evangelized peace to those who were far off, and peace to those who were near*[^2]. He himself in the Gospel says: *Peace I leave you, my peace I give you*[^3]. And by many other testimonies the Lord Christ is shown to be peaceable: not according to the peace which this world knows and seeks; but that peace of which it is said in the prophet: *I will give them true consolation, peace upon peace*[^4]; when, namely, to the peace of reconciliation is added the peace of immortality. For that after all things rendered which God promised, we ought to await a last peace, by which we may live with God forever, the same prophet shows, where he says: *O Lord our God, give us peace; for you have given us all things*[^5]. That peace will plainly be perfect, when *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^6]. And in whom will this be, except in that peaceable reconciler of ours? *For as in Adam all die; so also in Christ all will be made alive*[^7]. Since, then, we have found the true Solomon, that is, the true peaceable one; let us attend to what the psalm itself then teaches of him.

**2.** [v. 2] *O God, give your judgment to the king, and your righteousness to the king's son.*[^8] The Lord himself in the Gospel says: *The Father judges no one; but has given all judgment to the Son*[^9]: this, then, is: *O God, give your judgment to the king.* Who, the king, is also the son of the king, because God the Father certainly is king too. So it is written, that the king made a wedding for his son. But in the manner of Scripture the same thing is repeated. For what he said, *your judgment;* this he said in another way: *your righteousness*: and what he said, *to the king;* this he said in another way, *to the king's son;* as is that: *He who dwells in the heavens will deride them, and the Lord will mock them*[^10]. What is, *he who dwells in the heavens;* this is, *and the Lord*: and what is, *will deride them;* this is, *will mock them.* And: *The heavens declare the glory of God, and the firmament announces the works of his hands*[^11]. The *heavens* are repeated by the name of the firmament: and what was said, *the glory of God;* was repeated, *the works of his hands*: and what was said, *declare;* was repeated, *announces.* But these repetitions much commend the divine eloquences; whether the same words, or the same sense in other words, be repeated: and they are found especially in the Psalms; and in that kind of speech by which the affection of the mind is to be moved.

**3.** Then there follows: *To judge your people in righteousness, and your poor in judgment.*[^12] To what end the king Father gave to the king Son his judgment and his righteousness is sufficiently shown, when he says: *To judge your people in righteousness,* that is, to judge your people. Such is the expression in Solomon: *The proverbs of Solomon, son of David, to know wisdom and discipline*[^13]; that is, The proverbs of Solomon, to know wisdom and discipline. So, *give your judgment, to judge your people;* that is, give your judgment, to judge your people. But what he said first, *your people;* this he said later, *your poor*: and what he said first, *in righteousness;* this later, *in judgment;* in that manner of repetition. Where indeed he shows that the people of God ought to be poor, that is, not proud, but humble. *For blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^14]. By which poverty blessed Job too was poor, even before he had lost those great earthly riches. Which I thought to be mentioned for this reason, since there are certain ones who more easily distribute all their goods to the poor, than they themselves become poor of God. For they are puffed up with the boasting by which they think it should be attributed to them, not to the grace of God, that they live well; and therefore now they do not even live well, however great good works they seem to do. For they think they have of their own, and glory as though they had not received: rich of themselves, not poor of God; abounding to themselves, not in need of God. But the Apostle says: *If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^15]. As though he said: If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and am not poor of God, it profits me nothing. For *love is not puffed up*[^16]; nor is there true love of God in him who is ungrateful to his Holy Spirit, through whom his love is poured out in our hearts. And therefore such do not pertain to the people of God, because they are not poor of God. For the poor of God say: *But we have not received the spirit of this world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we may know the things that have been given us by God*[^17]. For when in this psalm too, on account of the sacrament of the man assumed by which the Word was made flesh, it is said to God the Father, the king, *Give your righteousness to the king's son;* these are unwilling that righteousness be given them, but trust that they have it of themselves. For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they were not subject to the righteousness of God. They are not, then, as I said, the poor of God, but rich of themselves; because they are not humble, but proud. But he will come to judge the people of God in righteousness, and the poor of God in judgment: and by that judgment he will distinguish his poor from their rich; but those whom by his own poverty he made his rich. For the poor people cries out to him: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^18].

**4.** But that, the order of words being changed, when he had first said, *O God, give your judgment to the king, and your righteousness to the king's son,* first setting judgment, then righteousness; he first set righteousness, then judgment, saying: *To judge your people in righteousness, and your poor in judgment;* he rather shows that he called judgment righteousness, demonstrating that it makes no difference in what order it is set, since it means the same. For a judgment is wont to be called depraved which is unjust; but righteousness we are not wont to call iniquitous or unjust. For if it is depraved, and unjust; it is no longer to be called righteousness. Accordingly, by setting judgment, and repeating it by the name of righteousness; or by setting righteousness, and repeating it by the name of judgment, he sufficiently teaches that he properly calls judgment that which is wont to be set for righteousness, that is, that which cannot be understood as judging badly. For where the Lord says: *Do not judge by persons, but judge a right judgment*[^19], he shows that there can be a depraved judgment, when he says: *Judge a right judgment*: finally, the former he forbids; the latter he commands. But when he says judgment without any addition, he wishes it at once to be understood as just; as is that which he says: *You leave the weightier things of the Law, mercy, and judgment*[^20]. And that which Jeremiah says: *Making his riches not with judgment*[^21]. He did not say: Making his riches with a depraved or unjust judgment, or not with a right or just judgment; but, *not with judgment*: calling nothing judgment, except what is right and just.

**5.** [v. 3] *Let the mountains take up peace for the people, and the hills righteousness.*[^22] The mountains are the greater, the hills the lesser. These, then, are doubtless those whom another psalm has: *The little ones with the great.* For those *mountains exulted like rams,* and those *hills like the lambs of the sheep, at the going out of Israel from Egypt*[^23], that is, at the liberation of the people of God from the servitude of this age. Those eminent by excelling holiness in the Church, then, are the mountains; who are fit both to teach others, by so speaking that they may be faithfully instructed, by so living that they may be savingly imitated: but the hills are those who follow their excellence by their obedience. Why, then, *the mountains peace, and the hills righteousness*? Or perhaps would it make no difference, even if it were so said: Let the mountains take up righteousness for the people, and the hills peace? For to both righteousness, and to both peace is necessary: and it can be that by another name peace was called the very righteousness; for this is true peace, not such as the unjust make among themselves. Or is it rather to be understood with a distinction not to be despised, that he says: *The mountains peace, and the hills righteousness*? For the eminent in the Church ought to consult for peace with watchful attention; lest, on account of their honors, by acting proudly they make schisms, the joining of unity being broken. But the hills should so follow them by imitating and obeying, that they set Christ before them; lest, seduced by the vain authority of evil mountains, since they seem to excel, they break themselves off from the unity of Christ. Therefore it was said: *Let the mountains take up peace for the people.* Let them say indeed: *Be imitators of me, as I too of Christ*[^24]. But again let them say: *Even if we, or an angel from heaven, announce to you other than what you have received, let him be anathema*[^25]. Let them say too: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul*[^26]? So *let them take up peace for the people* of God, that is, for the poor of God; not wishing to reign over them, but with them. Let those too not say: *I am Paul's, I am Apollos', I indeed of Cephas;* but let all say: *I am Christ's*[^27]. This is righteousness: not to set the servants before the Lord, nor to make them equal; so to lift their eyes to the mountains whence help may come to them, that yet they hope their help not from the mountains, but from the Lord who made heaven and earth.

**6.** It can also be most fittingly understood thus: *Let the mountains take up peace for the people,* that we understand peace in the reconciliation by which we are reconciled to God: for the mountains take it up for his people. This the Apostle so testifies: *The old things have passed away; behold, they have become new: but all things are from God, who reconciled us to himself through Christ, and gave us the ministry of reconciliation.* Behold how the mountains take up peace for his people. *For God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself; not reckoning to them their offenses, and placing in us the word of reconciliation*[^28]. In whom, except in the mountains who take up peace for his people? Wherefore the legates of peace subsequently add, and say: *For Christ, then, we discharge a legation, as though God exhorting through us; we beseech for Christ, be reconciled to God*[^29]. This peace the mountains take up for his people, that is, the preaching and legation of his peace: but the hills righteousness; that is, obedience, which is in men and in every rational creature the origin and perfection of all righteousness; so that of two men, that is, Adam who was the head of our death, and Christ who is the head of our salvation, this great distinction is commended: because *as through the disobedience of one man many were made sinners; so also through the obedience of one man many will be made just*[^30]. *Let,* then, *the mountains take up peace for the people, and the hills righteousness;* that in this manner, both being in concord, there may come about what is written: *Righteousness and peace have kissed each other*[^31]. But what other codices have: *Let the mountains take up peace for the people, and the hills*: I think both are to be understood as preachers of the gospel peace; whether those going before, or those following. But in these codices that follows: *In righteousness he will judge the poor of the people.* But those codices are rather approved which have what we expounded above: *Let the mountains take up peace for the people, and the hills righteousness.* But some have, *for your people;* some do not have *your,* but only *for the people.*

**7.** [v. 4] *He will judge the poor of the people, and will save the sons of the poor.*[^32] The poor and the sons of the poor seem to me the same; as the same city is Zion and the daughter of Zion. But if it is to be understood more distinctly; by *the poor* we take the *mountains;* but by *the sons of the poor* the *hills*: as it were the Prophets and Apostles, the *poor;* but their sons, that is, those who under their authority make progress, the *sons of the poor.* But that what was said above, *He will judge,* and afterward, *He will save,* is as it were an exposition how he will judge. For to this end he will judge, that he may save, that is, distinguish from those to be destroyed and condemned, those to whom he grants the salvation prepared to be revealed in the last time. For by such it is said to him: *Do not destroy my soul together with the ungodly*[^33]; and: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^34]. It is also to be considered that he did not say: He will judge the poor people; but, *the poor of the people.* For above where he said: *To judge your people in righteousness, and your poor in judgment,* he called the people of God the same as his poor; that is, only the good and those pertaining to the right part. But because in this age the right and the left are pastured together, who, like lambs and goats, are to be separated in the end; the whole, as it is mingled, he called by the name of people. And because he sets judgment here too in a good sense, that is, for saving; therefore he says: *He will judge the poor of the people*: that is, those he will distinguish unto salvation, who are poor in the people. Now who are the poor we have expounded above: let us understand these also as the needy. *And he will humble the slanderer.* No one is better recognized here as the slanderer than the devil. His slander is: *Does Job worship God for nothing*[^35]? But the Lord Jesus humbles him, by his grace helping his own, that they worship God freely, that is, that they be delighted in the Lord. He humbled him too thus: because, when the devil, that is, the prince of this world, had found nothing in him, he killed him through the slanders of the Jews; whom the slanderer used as it were his vessels, working in the sons of unbelief. For he was humbled, because he whom they had killed rose again, and took away the kingdom of death; whose power that one so wielded, that from the one man whom he had deceived, he dragged all through death to condemnation. But he was humbled: since, if for the offense of one death reigned through one, much more those who receive the abundance of grace and righteousness will reign in life through the one Jesus Christ, who humbled the slanderer, the false charges, the unjust judges, the false witnesses, which he sent in that they might destroy him.

**8.** [v. 5] *And he will remain with the sun,* or *he will remain together with the sun.*[^36] For so some of our people thought it better to be translated, what in the Greek is *symparamenei*. But if it could be said in Latin by one word, it would have to be said "co-remain"; therefore, because in Latin the word cannot be said, that the sense at least might be expressed, it was said, *he will remain together with the sun.* For it is nothing else to co-remain with the sun, than *to remain together with the sun.* But what great thing is it to remain together with the sun for him through whom all things were made, and without whom nothing was made; except that this prophecy was set before on account of those who think that the religion of the Christian name will live in this age unto a certain time, and afterward not be? *He will remain,* then, *together with the sun,* as long as the sun rises and sets: that is, as long as these times roll on, the Church of God, that is, the body of Christ, will not be lacking on earth. But that he adds: *And before the moon, generations of generations*: he could have said: And before the sun, that is, both with the sun, and before the sun; which would be understood: Both with the times, and before the times. For what precedes time is eternal: and this is truly to be held eternal, which is varied by no time, as in the beginning was the Word. But by the moon he preferred to signify the increases and failings of mortals. Finally, when he had said, *before the moon;* wishing in a manner to expound for what reason he set the moon, he said, *generations of generations*: as though he said: *Before the moon,* that is before *the generations of generations,* which pass by the departure and succession of mortals, like the moon's decreases and increases. And through this what is better understood by remaining *before the moon,* than to precede all mortal things by immortality? Which also can not unfittingly be taken thus, that, the slanderer being now humbled, he sits at the right hand of the Father; this is to remain *with the sun.* For the splendor of eternal glory is understood as the Son; as though the Father were the sun, and his splendor his Son; but as these things can be said of the invisible substance of the Creator: not as of this visible creature, in which are the heavenly bodies, of which the sun shines more illustriously, from which this likeness was applied; as it is applied also from earthly things, the rock, the lion, the lamb, a man having two sons, and the rest. Therefore, the slanderer being humbled, he remains *with the sun;* because, the devil being conquered through the resurrection, he sits at the right hand of the Father, where now he dies not, and death will no more have dominion over him. And this *before the moon,* as the firstborn from the dead going before the Church, passing by the departure and succession of mortals. These are the *generations of generations.* Or perhaps because there are generations by which we are mortally begotten; but *generations of generations,* by which we are immortally regenerated. And this is the Church which he went before, that he might remain *before the moon,* the firstborn from the dead. Indeed, what is in the Greek *geneas geneon,* some translated, not *generations,* but *of the generation of generations*: because the case is ambiguous in the Greek, and whether it be the genitive singular *geneas,* that is, of this generation, or the accusative plural *geneas,* that is, these generations, does not evidently appear; except that deservedly that sense was preferred, that as it were expounding what he meant by *moon,* he subsequently added, *generations of generations.*

**9.** [v. 6] *And he will descend like rain upon a fleece, and like drops dripping upon the earth.*[^37] He recalled and admonished that which was done through the judge Gideon, that it has its fulfillment concerning Christ. For he asked a sign from the Lord, that a fleece set on the threshing floor alone should be rained upon, and the threshing floor be dry; and again that the fleece alone should be dry, and the threshing floor be rained upon; and so it was done. Which signified that, as it were on the threshing floor in the whole world, the dry fleece was the former people of Israel. The same Christ, then, descended like rain upon the fleece, when the threshing floor was still dry; whence also he said: *I am not sent except to the sheep that have perished of the house of Israel*[^38]. There he chose the mother from whom he might take the form of a servant, in which he might appear to men; there the disciples to whom he gave this same precept, saying: *Do not go into the way of the Gentiles, and do not enter the cities of the Samaritans: go first to the sheep that have perished of the house of Israel*[^39]. When he says: *Go first* to those, he shows that afterward too, when the threshing floor was now to be rained upon, they would go also to other sheep which were not of the old people of Israel, of which he says: *I have other sheep which are not of this fold; I must lead them too, that there may be one flock and one shepherd*[^40]. Hence the Apostle too: *For I say,* he says, *that Christ was a minister of the circumcision on account of the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers.* So the rain descended upon the fleece, the threshing floor still dry. But since there follows, *but the Gentiles glorify God for his mercy*[^41]; that, as the time came, there might be fulfilled what he says through the Prophet: *A people I did not know served me: at the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^42]: we now see by the grace of Christ the nation of the Jews remaining dry, and the whole world among all the nations rained upon with the full clouds of Christian grace. For by another word he signified the same rain, saying: *Drops dripping;* not now upon a fleece, but *upon the earth.* For what else is rain than drops dripping? But I think that nation is signified by the name of the fleece, either because it was to be despoiled of the authority of doctrine, as a sheep is despoiled of its fleece; or because it held in secret the same rain, which it was unwilling should be preached to the uncircumcision, that is, revealed to the uncircumcised nations.

**10.** [v. 7] *In his days will rise righteousness and abundance of peace, until the moon be taken away.*[^43] That which was said, *be taken away,* some translated, *be removed*: but others, *be exalted;* rendering one Greek word as it seemed to each, which was set there, *antarthei.* But those who said, *be taken away,* and those who said, *be removed,* do not so greatly disagree. For *be taken away* custom rather has so to be said, that it be removed and be not, than that it be lifted higher; but *be removed* cannot at all be understood otherwise, than that it be destroyed, that is, that it be not: but *be exalted* is nothing except that it be lifted higher. Which indeed, when it is set in an evil sense, is wont to signify pride; as is: *In your wisdom do not be exalted*[^44]. But in a good sense it pertains to a fuller honor, as when something is lifted up; as is: *In the nights lift up your hands to the holy place, and bless the Lord*[^45]. Here, then, if we understand *be removed* to be said, what will it be *until the moon be removed,* except that it be brought about that it be not? For perhaps he wished this to be understood, that there be no longer mortality, when *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^46]: that the abundance of peace be carried even to this, that nothing resist the happiness of the blessed from the weakness of mortality; which will be in that age, of which we hold the faithful promise of God through Jesus Christ our Lord, of whom it is said: *In his days will rise righteousness and abundance of peace*: until, death being altogether conquered and destroyed, all mortality be consumed. But if by the name of the moon, not the mortality of the flesh through which the Church now passes, but the Church itself altogether is signified, which remains forever, freed from this mortality; it is to be taken as said thus: *In his days will rise righteousness and abundance of peace, until the moon be exalted,* as though it were said: In his days will rise righteousness, which conquers the gainsaying and rebellion of the flesh, and there will be peace growing and abounding so much, until the moon be exalted, that is, the Church be lifted up, through the glory of the resurrection to reign with him, who went before her in this glory as the firstborn from the dead, that he might sit at the right hand of the Father; so *with the sun* remaining *before the moon,* by which afterward the *moon* too might be exalted.

**11.** [v. 8] *And he will have dominion from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the circle of the earth.*[^47] Of whom certainly he had said: *In his days will rise righteousness and abundance of peace, until the moon be exalted.* If the Church is rightly here signified by the name of the moon, he consequently shows how widely he was going to spread that same Church, when he added: *And he will have dominion from sea to sea.* For the earth is girt by the great sea, which is called Ocean; from which something small flows into the midst of the lands, and makes those seas known to us, which are frequented by ships. Accordingly, *from sea to sea,* from any end of the earth, even to any end, he said he would have dominion, whose name and power were to be preached throughout the whole world, and would much prevail. And that it could not be understood otherwise, *from sea to sea;* he at once added, *and from the river to the ends of the circle of the earth.* What he says, *even to the ends of the circle of the earth;* this he had said before, *from sea to sea.* But what he now says, *from the river;* he evidently expressed that Christ wished to commend his power from there, from where he began to choose the disciples; namely from the river Jordan, where, the Lord being baptized, when the Holy Spirit descended upon him, a voice sounded from heaven: *This is my beloved Son*[^48]. Hence, then, his teaching beginning, and the authority of the heavenly mastership, is spread even to the ends of the circle of the earth, when the Gospel of the kingdom is preached in the whole world, for a testimony to all the nations; and then the end will come.

**12.** [v. 9] *Before him the Ethiopians will fall, and his enemies will lick the earth.*[^49] By the Ethiopians, the whole from a part, he signified all the nations; choosing that nation which he would chiefly name, which is at the ends of the earth. *Before him they will fall,* it is said, they will adore him. And because there were going to be schisms through diverse places of the lands, which would envy the catholic Church spread throughout the whole circle of the earth; and the same schisms moreover were going to divide themselves by the names of men, and, by loving men, by whose authorship they are torn, would be enemies to the glory of Christ, which is through all the lands: therefore, when he had said: *Before him the Ethiopians will fall;* he added, *and his enemies will lick the earth;* that is, they will love men, so as to envy the glory of Christ, to whom it was said: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and over all the earth your glory*[^50]. For man merited to hear: *You are earth, and into earth you will go*[^51]. By licking this earth, that is, delighted by the vaniloquent authority of such, by loving them, and holding them most sweet, they gainsay the divine eloquences, by which the catholic Church was foretold, not to be in some part of the lands, like any schisms; but in the whole world, by bearing fruit and growing, to reach even to the Ethiopians themselves, namely the farthest and foulest of men.

**13.** [vv. 10-11] *The kings of Tarshish and the islands will offer gifts; the kings of the Arabs and Saba will bring presents. And all the kings of the earth will adore him; all nations will serve him.*[^52] This now desires not an expositor, but a beholder; nay, it is brought not only before the sight of the rejoicing faithful, but even of the groaning unbelievers: unless perhaps it must be asked what was said, *will bring presents.* For those things are wont to be brought, which can walk. Surely, then, it could not be said of victims to be immolated? Far be it that such righteousness rise in his days. But these presents which were foretold as to be brought, seem to me to signify men, whom the authority of kings brings into the fellowship of the Church of Christ; although even persecuting kings brought presents, not knowing what they did when they immolated the holy martyrs.

**14.** [v. 12] But when he expounds the causes why so great honor is deferred to him by kings, and he is served by all nations: *Because he freed,* he says, *the needy from the powerful, and the poor for whom there was no helper.*[^53] This needy and poor one is the people of those believing in him. In this people are also the kings adoring him. For they do not disdain to be needy and poor, that is, humbly confessing their sins, and in need of the glory and grace of God, that that king, the son of the King, may free them from the powerful one. For the same is the powerful one, who above was called the slanderer: whom his own power did not make powerful to subject men to himself and hold them in captivity, but human sins. The same too was called the strong one; therefore here too the powerful one. But he who humbled the slanderer, and entered into the house of the strong one, that, binding him, he might plunder his vessels, he *freed the needy from the powerful, and the poor for whom there was no helper.* For neither anyone's power, nor any just man, nor any Angel could accomplish this. Since, then, there was no helper, he himself, coming, saved them.

**15.** [v. 13] But it occurred: If man was held by the devil on account of sins, surely sins did not please Christ who rescued the needy from the powerful? Far be it: but: *He himself will spare the needy and the poor*[^54]; that is, he will forgive sins to the humble, and to one not trusting in his own merits, or hoping for salvation from his own power, but in need of the grace of his Savior. But when he added: *And he will save the souls of the poor,* he commended both helps of grace; both that which is in the remission of sins, when he says: *He will spare the poor and the needy;* and that which is in the participation of righteousness, when he added: *And he will save the souls of the poor.* For no one is fit for himself unto salvation, which salvation is perfect righteousness, unless the grace of God helps; because the fullness of the Law is nothing but love, which does not exist of ourselves in us, but is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.

**16.** [v. 14] *From usuries and iniquity he will redeem their souls.*[^55] What are these usuries, except sins, which are also called debts? But I think they are called usuries, because more evil is found in the punishments, than was committed in the sins. For, for example, when a murderer kills only the body of a man, but can do no harm to the soul; both his soul and body is destroyed in gehenna. On account of such despisers of the present precept, and mockers of the future punishment, it was said: *I, coming, would exact it with usuries*[^56]. From these usuries the souls of the poor are redeemed, by that blood which was poured out for the remission of sins. He will redeem them, then, from usuries, by forgiving sins, which deserved greater punishments: but he will redeem them from iniquity, by helping through grace even to do righteousness. And so the same two are repeated which were said above. For what is above: *He will spare the needy and the poor,* this is understood: *From usuries*: but what he says there: *And he will save the souls of the poor*; this seems said: *From iniquity*: so that *he will redeem* is to be supplied to both. For by sparing he will redeem from usuries; but by saving he will redeem from iniquity. So, when *he will spare the poor and the needy, and will save the souls of the poor;* so *from usuries and iniquity he will redeem their souls. And honorable is his name before them.* For they give honor to his name for so great benefits, who answer that it is worthy and just to give thanks to the Lord their God. Or, as some codices have: *And honorable is their name before him*: because, although the Christians seem contemptible to this age, their name is honorable before him, who gave it to them, now not mindful of their names through his own lips, by which before they were called, when they were bound by the superstitions of the Gentiles, or marked by the names of their own evil merits, before they were Christians: which name is honorable before him, even if it seems contemptible to the enemies.

**17.** [v. 15] *And he will live, and to him will be given of the gold of Arabia.*[^57] It would not be said: *And he will live,* (for of whom could this not be said, living however small a span of time on this earth?) unless that life were commended, in which *he now dies no more,* and *death will no more have dominion over him*[^58]. And therefore *he will live* who was despised dying: since, as another prophet says: *His life will be taken from the earth*[^59]. But what is, *and to him will be given of the gold of Arabia*? For that even that Solomon received gold from there, in this psalm is figuratively transferred to another true Solomon, that is, the true peaceable one: for that one did not have dominion *from the river to the ends of the circle of the earth*[^60]. So, then, it was prophesied that even the wise of this world were going to believe in Christ. But by Arabia we understand the Gentiles: by gold, wisdom, which so excels among all teachings, as gold among metals; whence it is written: *Receive prudence as silver, and wisdom as gold tried by fire*[^61]. *And they will pray concerning him always.* What the Greek has, *peri autou,* some translated, *concerning him;* some, *for him,* or *on his behalf.* But what is, *concerning him,* except perhaps that which we pray, saying: *Your kingdom come*[^62]? For the coming of Christ will present to the faithful the kingdom of God. But how *for him* may be understood, is narrow; except that, when one prays for the Church, one prays for him, because it is his body. For of Christ and the Church the great sacrament was set before: *They will be two in one flesh*[^63]. But now what follows: *All the day long,* that is, all the time, *they will bless him,* is plain enough.

**18.** [v. 16] *And there will be a firmament on the earth, on the tops of the mountains.*[^64] *For all the promises of God in him are Yea*[^65], that is, in him they are made firm; because in him was fulfilled whatever was prophesied for our salvation. For by the tops of the mountains it is fitting to understand the authors of the divine Scriptures, that is, those through whom they were ministered; in whom certainly he himself is the firmament, since to him all things divinely written are referred. But he willed this to be on the earth, because for the sake of those who are on the earth those things were written: whence he himself too came onto the earth, that he might make all those things firm, that is, show them to be fulfilled in himself. *For it behooved,* he says, *all things to be fulfilled which are written in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms about me*[^66]; that is, *on the tops of the mountains.* For so came *in the last times the manifest mountain of the Lord, prepared on the top of the mountains*[^67]; which here he says, *on the tops of the mountains. His fruit will be lifted up above Lebanon.* Lebanon we are wont to take as the dignity of this age; since Lebanon is a mountain having lofty trees, and the name itself is interpreted "Whitening." For what wonder, if above every illustrious summit of this age the fruit of Christ is lifted up, whose lovers of fruit despised all worldly heights? But if we take Lebanon in a good sense, on account of the *cedars of Lebanon which he planted*[^68]; what other fruit is to be understood, which is lifted up above this Lebanon, except that of which the Apostle says, about to speak of his love: *I show you a still more eminent way*[^69]? For this is set first too among the divine gifts, where he says: *But the fruit of the Spirit is love;* and then the other things, certainly consequent, are woven on[^70]. *And they will flourish from the city like the hay of the earth.* Since the city is set down ambiguously, nor is added "his," or "of God"; for it is not said, from his city, or from the city of God, but only *from the city;* it is understood in a good sense, that from the city of God, that is, from the Church, they flourish like hay; but fruitful hay, such as is of wheat: for this too is called hay in holy Scripture; as in Genesis the earth was bidden to produce every tree and every hay, nor was added, every wheat; which without doubt would not be passed over unless this too were understood by the name of hay; and in many other places of the Scriptures this is found. But if it is to be taken thus: *And they will flourish like the hay of the earth,* as it was said: *All flesh is hay, and the splendor of man like the flower of hay;* certainly that city too is to be understood, which signifies the fellowship of this age: for not in vain did Cain first found a city. So, the fruit of Christ being exalted above Lebanon, that is, above the long-lasting trees and the incorruptible wood, because that fruit is eternal; all the splendor of man according to the temporal loftiness of the age is compared to hay, since by those believing and now hoping for eternal life temporal happiness is despised, that there may be fulfilled what is written: *All flesh is hay, and all the splendor of the flesh like the flower of hay: the hay withered, the flower fell. But the word of the Lord abides forever*[^71]. There is his fruit exalted above Lebanon. For always flesh was hay, and the splendor of the flesh like the flower of hay: but because there was not shown what happiness was to be chosen and preferred, the flower of hay was held for a great thing; and not only was it by no means despised, but even most of all desired. As though, then, it began to be so, when whatever flourished in the age was turned away from and despised, it was so said: *His fruit will be lifted up above Lebanon, and they will flourish from the city like the hay of the earth*: that is, that which is promised in eternity will be spread abroad above all things, and whatever is held for a great thing in the age will be compared to the hay of the earth.

**19.** [v. 17] *Let*, then, *his name be blessed unto the ages: before the sun his name remains.*[^72] By the sun the times are signified: therefore unto eternity his name remains; for eternity precedes the times, nor is it shut in by time. *And in him all the tribes of the earth will be blessed.* For in him is fulfilled what was promised to Abraham. *For it does not say*: *In seeds, as of many; but as of one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^73]. But to Abraham it is said: *In your seed all the tribes of the earth will be blessed*[^74]. *Neither the sons of the flesh, but the sons of the promise are reckoned in the seed*[^75]. *All the nations will magnify him.* As though, expounding, what was said above is repeated. For because they will be blessed in him, they will magnify him: not by themselves making him great, who is great through himself; but by praising and confessing him great. For so we magnify God; so too we say: *Hallowed be your name*[^76]: which certainly is always holy.

**20.** [v. 18] *Blessed is the Lord, the God of Israel, who does wonders alone.*[^77] All the things said above being considered, a hymn is burst forth, and the Lord God of Israel is blessed. For there is fulfilled what was said to that barren one: *And he who rescued you, he himself, the Lord, the God of Israel, will be called the God of the whole earth*[^78]. He himself *does wonders alone*: since whoever do them, he himself works in them, *who does wonders alone.*

**21.** [v. 19] *And blessed is the name of his glory forever, and unto the age of the age.*[^79] What else would the Latin translators say, who could not say, forever, and forever of the eternal one? For it sounds as though one thing is said, *forever,* and another *unto the age*: but the Greek has *eis ton aiona, kai eis ton aiona tou aionos,* which would perhaps be more conveniently said, unto the age, and unto the age of the age; so that *unto the age* would be understood, as long as this age lasts; but *unto the age of the age,* that which after the end of this is promised to be. *And all the earth will be filled with his glory: so be it, so be it.* You have commanded, O Lord, so it is done; so it is done, until that which began *from the river,* reach altogether *even to the ends of the circle of the earth*[^80].
