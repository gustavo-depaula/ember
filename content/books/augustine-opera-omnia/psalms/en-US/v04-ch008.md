# Exposition of Psalm 80

**1.** [v. 1.] We have undertaken to speak to you about the present psalm: let your quiet help our voice; for it is somewhat the more dulled: the attention of the hearers will give it strength, and the help of him who bids me to speak. This psalm has the title: *Unto the end, for the winepresses, the fifth of the sabbath, a Psalm of Asaph himself.* Into one title many mysteries are heaped; yet so that the threshold of the Psalm indicates the inner parts. Since we are about to speak of winepresses, let none of you expect that we are going to say anything about the vat, the press, the baskets; because the psalm itself does not have this, and therefore it the more indicates a mystery. For even if the text of the Psalm contained some such thing, there would not be lacking one who would suppose that the winepresses are to be taken literally, and that nothing further is to be sought there, nor anything set mystically and sacredly signified; but he would say: The Psalm speaks simply of winepresses, and you imagine to me I know not what else. You heard nothing of the kind here, when it was read. Therefore take the winepresses as the mystery of the Church, which is now being enacted. In winepresses we observe three things: a pressure, and from the pressure two things; one to be stored up, the other to be cast away. In the winepress there comes about a treading down, a tribulation, a weight: and in these the oil is strained out secretly into the storage-vat; the dregs run publicly through the streets. Attend to this great spectacle. For God does not cease to produce for us something we may watch with great joy: or is the madness of the circus to be compared to this spectacle? That pertains to the dregs, this to the oil. When, then, you hear blasphemers contumaciously chattering, and saying that pressures abound in Christian times; for you know that they love to say this: and it is indeed an old proverb, but one that began from Christian times: God does not rain, lead him to the Christians. Although the earlier ones said these things. But these men now say even when God rains: Lead him to the Christians: God does not rain, we do not sow; God rains, we do not thresh. And from this they wish to be proud, whence they ought rather to supplicate; choosing to blaspheme rather than to pray. When, then, they recall these things, when they boast of these things, when they say these things, and say them contumaciously, not with fear, but with elation; let them not disturb you. For suppose that pressures abound, be you the oil. Let the dregs, black with the darkness of ignorance, insult, and let it insult publicly as though cast out through the streets: do you, within yourself in your heart, where he who sees in secret will repay you[^1], be strained into the storage-vat. The olive on the tree is indeed shaken by certain tempests, yet is not crushed by the pressures of the winepress; therefore both hang together from the tree, both what is to be cast away, and what is to be stored up: but when it has come to the press and the pressures, both are distinguished, separated; and the one is sought, the other rejected. Do you wish to know the force of these winepresses? That I may say one thing, at which even they who do these things murmur: How great, they say, are the robberies in our times, how great the pressures of the innocent, how great the despoilings of others' goods! So indeed you attend to the dregs, because others' goods are seized; to the oil you do not attend, because their own goods too are given to the poor. Antiquity did not have such seizers of others' goods; but neither did antiquity have such givers of their own goods. Be somewhat more curious in the winepress; do not see only what flows publicly: there is something which by seeking you may find. Examine, hear, learn how many do what one rich man, when he had heard it from the mouth of the Lord, withdrew sad. From the Gospel many hear: *Go, sell all that you possess, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^2]: do you not attend to how many do this? They are few, they say. Yet those few are the oil; and those who use well the things they possess pertain to the oil: add all together, and you will see the storehouses of your head of the household full. You see a seizer such as you never saw; see a despiser of his own goods such as you never saw. Praise the winepresses, the prophecy from the Apocalypse is fulfilled: *Let the righteous become more righteous, and the filthy grow more filthy still*[^3]. Behold the winepresses in this sentence: *Let the righteous become more righteous, and the filthy grow more filthy still.*

**2.** Why too *the fifth of the sabbath?* what is this? Let us run back to the first works of God, lest perhaps there we find something by which we may understand the sacrament too. For the sabbath is the seventh day on which God rested from all his works[^4], intimating the great mystery of our future rest from all our works. The first of the sabbath is called the first day, which we also name the Lord's day: the second of the sabbath, the second day; the third of the sabbath, the third day; the fourth of the sabbath, the fourth; the fifth, then, of the sabbath, the fifth from the Lord's day: after which the sixth of the sabbath, the sixth day; and the sabbath itself, the seventh day. See, then, to whom this psalm speaks: for it seems to me that it speaks to the baptized. For on the fifth day God created the animals from the waters; on the fifth day, that is, on the fifth of the sabbath, God said: *Let the waters bring forth creeping things of living souls*[^5]. See, then, you in whom the waters have already brought forth creeping things of living souls. For you pertain to the winepresses; and in you whom the waters have brought forth, one thing is strained out, another cast away. For there are many not living worthily of the baptism they have received: for how many baptized today preferred to fill the circus, rather than this basilica! how many baptized either build cottages in the villages, or complain that they cannot be built! But this psalm *for the winepresses* and *the fifth of the sabbath,* in the pressure of separation, and in the sacrament of baptism, is sung *to Asaph himself.* Asaph was a certain man called by this name, like Jeduthun, like Korah, like the other names which we find in the titles of the Psalms: yet the interpretation of the name intimates the mystery of a hidden truth. For Asaph in Latin is called "gathering." Therefore *for the winepresses, the fifth of the sabbath,* it is sung *to Asaph himself;* that is, for the separating pressure, to the baptized reborn of water, the Psalm is sung to the Lord's gathering. We read the title on the threshold, and in these presses understood what it means: now, if it please you, let us see also the very house of the work, that is, the inner parts of the winepress itself. Let us enter, let us look, let us rejoice, let us fear, let us desire, let us flee. For all these things you will find in this inner house, that is, in the text of the psalm itself, when we begin to read, and, with the Lord helping, to speak what he grants.

**3.** [v. 2.] Behold you, O Asaph, gathering of the Lord: *Exult in God our helper.* You who are gathered today, you today the Asaph of the Lord, since the Psalm is sung to you, to Asaph himself: *Exult in God our helper.* Others exult in the circus, you in God: others exult in their deceiver; do you exult in your helper: others exult in their god their belly; do you exult in your God your helper. *Make a joyful noise to the God of Jacob*: because you too belong to Jacob; rather, you are Jacob, the younger people whom the elder serves[^6]. *Make a joyful noise to the God of Jacob.* Whatever you cannot explain in words, do not for that reason cease from exulting: what you can explain, cry out; what you cannot, *make a joyful noise.* For from the abundance of joys, for which words cannot suffice, one is wont to burst into a joyful noise: *Make a joyful noise to the God of Jacob.*

**4.** [v. 3.] *Receive a psalm, and give a timbrel.* Both *receive,* and *give.* What *receive?* what *give? Receive a psalm, and give a timbrel.* The apostle Paul says in a certain place, reproving and grieving, that no one communicated with him *in the matter of giving and receiving*[^7]. What is *in the matter of giving and receiving,* except what in another place he openly expounded: *If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal things*[^8]? And it is true that the timbrel, which is made of hide, pertains to the flesh. The psalm, then, is spiritual, the timbrel carnal. Therefore, people of God, gathering of God, *receive a psalm, and give a timbrel*: receive spiritual things, and give carnal things. This is what we have exhorted you concerning that table of the blessed Martyr too, that, receiving spiritual things, you should give carnal things. For these things which are built for a time are necessary for receiving the bodies of either the living or the dead, but for a passing time. Shall we, after the judgment of God, raise these structures into heaven? Yet without these, at this time, we shall not be able to do the things that pertain to possessing heaven. If, then, you are eager in receiving spiritual things, be devoted in dispensing carnal things. *Receive a psalm, and give a timbrel*: receive our voice, render your hands.

**5.** *A pleasant psaltery with a harp.* I remember that we once intimated to Your Charity the difference between the psaltery and the harp: let the studious who remember recognize it; let those who either did not hear, or do not remember, learn. Of these two musical instruments, both the psaltery and the harp, this is the difference, that the psaltery has that hollow wood, from which the sounding strings give their sound, in the upper part; the strings are struck below, that they may sound from above: but in the harp this same hollow of the wood holds the lower part; as though the one were from heaven, the other from earth. For heavenly is the preaching of the word of God; but if we await heavenly things, let us not be slothful in working earthly things; because *a pleasant psaltery,* but *with a harp.* This is said in another way what was said above: *Receive a psalm, and give a timbrel*: here for the psalm the psaltery, for the timbrel the harp is set. By this, however, we are admonished, that to the preaching of the word of God we should respond with bodily works.

**6.** [v. 4.] *Sound the trumpet.* That is, preach more clearly and confidently; do not be terrified: as the prophet says in a certain place: *Cry out, and lift up your voice like a trumpet*[^9]. *Sound the trumpet at the beginning of the month of the trumpet.* It was commanded that at the beginning of the month the trumpet should be sounded; and this the Jews do bodily even now, but do not understand it spiritually. For the beginning of the month is the new moon; the new moon is new life. What is the new moon? *If, then, there is any new creature in Christ*[^10]. What is: *Sound the trumpet at the beginning of the month of the trumpet*? With all confidence preach the new life; do not fear the din of the old life.

**7.** [v. 5.] *For it is a precept to Israel itself, and a judgment to the God of Jacob.* Where the precept, there the judgment. For those who have sinned in the law will be judged by the law[^11]. And the giver of the precept himself, the Lord Christ, the Word made flesh: *Unto judgment,* he says, *I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind*[^12]. What is, *that those who do not see may see, those who see may become blind,* except that the humble may be exalted, the proud cast down? for it is not those who see who become blind, but those who seem to themselves to see who are convicted of blindness. This the mystery of the winepress enacts, *that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind.*

**8.** [v. 6.] *He set it as a testimony in Joseph.* Come now, brothers, what is this? Joseph is interpreted "increase." You remember, you know Joseph sold into Egypt: Christ passing over to the Gentiles. There Joseph after tribulations was exalted[^13], and here Christ after the passion of the martyrs was glorified. Therefore to Joseph the Gentiles rather belong: and therefore "increase"; because many are the children of the desolate, more than of her who has a husband[^14]. *He set it as a testimony in Joseph, when he went out of the land of Egypt.* See here too the fifth of the sabbath signified: When Joseph went out of the land of Egypt—that is, the people multiplied through Joseph—it was led across through the Red Sea[^15]. And then too the waters brought forth creeping things of living souls[^16]. The passing of the people through the sea then portended in figure nothing else than the passing of the faithful through baptism; the Apostle is witness: *For I do not wish you,* he says, *to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea*[^17]. The passing through the sea, then, signified nothing else than the sacrament of the baptized; the pursuing Egyptians nothing else than the abundance of past sins. You see most evident sacraments: the Egyptians press, urge; therefore sins are at hand, but only up to the water. Why, then, do you fear, you who have not yet come, to come to the baptism of Christ, to pass through the Red Sea? What is "red"? Consecrated by the blood of the Lord. Why do you fear to come? Perhaps the conscience of some monstrous offenses goads you, and tortures the soul within you, and tells you that what you committed is so great, that you despair of its being forgiven you: fear lest something of your sins remain, if any of the Egyptians has lived. But when you have passed the Red Sea, when you have been led out from your offenses with a mighty hand and a strong arm[^18], you are about to receive mysteries which you did not know; because Joseph himself too, *when he went out of the land of Egypt, heard a tongue which he did not know.* You will hear a tongue which you did not know: which now those hear and recognize, attesting and knowing, who know. You will hear where you ought to have your heart: which just now when I was saying, many understood, and acclaimed; the rest stood mute, because they had not yet heard the tongue which they did not know. Let them hasten, then, let them pass over, let them learn: *He heard a tongue which he did not know.*

**9.** [v. 7.] *He turned away his back from burdens.* Who *turned away his back from burdens,* except he who cried out: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened*[^19]? In another way this same thing is signified: what the pursuit of the Egyptians did, this the loads of sins do. *He turned away his back from burdens.* And as if you said: From what burdens? *His hands served in the basket.* By the basket are signified servile works. To clean, to spread dung, to carry earth, is done with a basket; these are servile works; because everyone who commits sin is the servant of sin; and if the Son frees you, then you will be truly free[^20]. Rightly too the abject things of the world are reckoned as baskets: but God filled even baskets with morsels; he filled twelve baskets of morsels[^21]; because he chose the abject things of this world, to confound the strong[^22]. But also when Joseph served in the basket, he carried earth there, because he made bricks: *His hands served in the basket.*

**10.** [v. 8.] *In tribulation you called upon me, and I rescued you.* Let each Christian conscience recognize itself, if it has devoutly passed the Red Sea[^23], if with the faith of believing and observing it has heard the tongue which it did not know; let it recognize that it was heard in tribulation. For it was itself a great tribulation, to be pressed by the loads of sins; how the relieved conscience rejoices! Behold, you have been baptized; the conscience which yesterday was pressed, today gives thanks. You were heard in tribulation; remember your tribulation. Before you came to the water, what anxiety did you carry? what fastings did you display? what tribulations did you carry in your heart, of inward, pious, devout prayers? Your enemies were killed, all your sins were blotted out: *In tribulation you called upon me, and I rescued you.*

**11.** *I heard you in the hidden place of the storm*: not in the storm of the sea, but in the storm of the heart. *I heard you in the hidden place of the storm; I tested you at the water of contradiction.* Truly, brothers, truly, he who was heard in the hidden place of the storm, must be tested at the water of contradiction. For when he has believed, when he has been baptized, when he has begun to take up the way of God, when he has intended to be strained out into the storage-vat, and has drawn himself out from the dregs running publicly; he will have many harassers, many insulters, many disparagers, dissuaders, threatening too where they can, deterring, depressing: this whole thing is the water of contradiction. I think it is so here today; I suppose there are here some whom their friends wished to drag to the circus, and to I know not what trifles of today's festivity: perhaps they themselves brought them to the church. But whether they themselves brought them, or could not be dragged away by them to the circus, they have been tested at the water of contradiction. Do not, then, blush to preach what you know, to defend even among blasphemers what you have believed: for if you are heard in the hidden place of the storm, with the heart it is believed unto righteousness; if you are tested at the water of contradiction, with the mouth confession is made unto salvation[^24]. For how great is that water of contradiction? it is now almost dried up. Our forebears felt it, when at the word of God, at the mystery of Christ, the nations sharply resisted; the water was troubled—for that waters are sometimes to be understood as peoples the Scripture of the Apocalypse evidently shows, where, when many waters were seen, and it was asked what they were, the answer was given: *They are peoples*[^25]. Those men, then, bore the water of contradiction, when the nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things; when the kings of the earth stood up, and the princes came together against the Lord and against his Christ[^26]. When the nations raged, then that lion, going to meet the strong man Samson coming to take a wife from the foreigners—namely, Christ descending to have a Church from the Gentiles—went roaring against him. But what did he do? He took, held, broke, scattered the lion; it became in his hands like a kid of the goats. For what would the raging people be, except a languid sinner? But that savagery being killed, no longer does the royal power so rage, no longer does the people of the Gentiles so rage against Christ: rather, in the very kingdom of the Gentiles we find laws for the Church, as it were honey in the mouth of the lion[^27]. Why, then, should I now fear the water of contradiction, which is now nearly all dried up? It is now almost silent, if the dregs do not contradict. However much the evil strangers rage, O if only our own evil ones did not help them! *I heard you in the hidden place of the storm, I tested you at the water of contradiction.* You recall what was said of Christ, that he was so born for the fall of many, and the rising again of many, and for a sign that will be contradicted[^28]. We know it, we see it: the sign of the cross has been raised up, and it has been contradicted. The glory of the cross was contradicted; but there was a title above the cross, which was not corrupted. For there is a title in a psalm: *Unto the inscription of a title, do not corrupt*[^29]. It was a sign that would be contradicted: for the Jews said: *Do not write, King of the Jews; but write that he himself said he was King of the Jews.* The contradiction was conquered; the answer was given: *What I have written, I have written*[^30]. *I heard you in the hidden place of the storm, I tested you at the water of contradiction.*

**12.** [vv. 9-10.] All this, from the beginning of the Psalm up to this verse, we have heard about the oil of the winepress. What remains is rather to be grieved over and guarded against: for it pertains to the dregs of the winepress unto the end; perhaps not in vain with an interposed pause too. But it is useful to hear this also, that he who already sees himself in the oil may rejoice; he who is in danger lest he run into the dregs may beware. Hear both: love the one, fear the other. *Hear, my people, and I will speak, and I will testify to you.* For not to an alien people, not to a people not pertaining to the winepress: *Judge,* he says, *between me and my vineyard*[^31]. *Hear, my people, and I will speak, and I will testify to you.*

**13.** *Israel, if you will hear me, there shall not be in you a recent god.* A recent god is one made for a time: but our God is not recent, but from eternity to eternity. And our Christ is perhaps recent as man, but eternal as God. For what was there before the beginning? And surely in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: and our Christ himself is the Word made flesh, that he might dwell among us[^32]. Far be it, then, that there be in anyone a recent god: a recent god is either a stone, or a phantasm. It is not, he says, a stone: I have a silver and a golden one. Rightly did he wish to name those precious things, who said: *The idols of the Gentiles are silver and gold*[^33]. They are great, because they are golden and silver; they are precious; they are shining: but yet they have eyes, and do not see. These gods are recent. What is more recent than a god from the workshop? Although the cobwebs of spiders have already woven over them as old; those that are not eternal are recent: this concerning the pagans. Some other man, taking the name of the Lord his God in vanity, made for himself a Christ a creature, a Christ unmatched and unequal to the one begetting; calling him the Son of God, and denying him the Son of God. For if the Son is the only-begotten, he is this which the Father is, and is this from eternity: but you have thought I know not what else in your heart; you have set up a recent god. Another made for himself a god fighting against the race of darkness, fearing lest he be invaded, busy lest he be corrupted; yet corrupted in part, that he might be wholly safe; but not wholly, because corrupted in part. The Manichees say these things; these too make for themselves a recent god in their heart. Such is not our God, such is not your portion, Jacob: but he who made heaven and earth, he is your God; who has no need of goods, who fears no evils.

**14.** Many heretics, then, together with the pagans, made for themselves various gods, fashioned for themselves various gods; and set them, even if not in temples, yet—which is worse—in their own heart, and themselves became temples of false and laughable idols. It is a great work to break these idols within; and to cleanse a place for the living, not the recent, God. For all these, thinking one thing and another, making for themselves various gods, and varying the faith itself with falsehood, seem to disagree; but all do not depart from earthly thoughts, in earthly thoughts they agree with one another: the opinion is diverse, the vanity is one. Of them it is said in another psalm: *They are of one in vanity*[^34]: although they disagree in the variety of opinions, yet they are bound together by a like vanity. And you know that vanity is behind, is latter: therefore he, forgetting the things that are behind—that is, forgetting vanity—stretched out into those that are before, that is, into the truth, follows on to the palm of the supernal calling of God in Christ Jesus[^35]. So those men agree with one another to their worse, although they seem to disagree with one another. Therefore Samson bound together the tails of the foxes[^36]. The foxes signify the treacherous, and especially the heretics; the crafty, the fraudulent, hiding in cavernous windings and deceiving, stinking too with a foul smell. Against which smell the Apostle says: *We are the good odor of Christ in every place*[^37]. These foxes are signified in the Song of Songs, where it is said: *Catch us the little foxes, that destroy the vineyards, hiding in the twisting caverns*[^38]. *Catch us,* convict for us; for you catch him whom you convict of falsehood. Finally, when the little foxes contradicted the Lord, and said: *By what power do you do these things? Answer me too,* he says, *one word: The baptism of John, whence was it, from heaven, or from men?* Now foxes are wont to have such dens, that they enter from one side, and go out from another: at both openings the catcher of foxes set his nets. Tell me, *is it from heaven, or from men?* They feel that he stretched out on both sides whence he might catch them; and they say among themselves: *If we say,* they say: *From heaven; he will say to us: Why then did you not believe him?* for he bore testimony to Christ. *If we say: From earth; the people stone us, because they hold him as a prophet.* Feeling, then, that on this side and on that they would be caught, they answered: *We do not know.* And the Lord: *Neither do I tell you by what power I do these things*[^39]. You say that you do not know what you do know; I do not tell you what you ask: because from no side did you dare to go out, you have remained in your own darkness. Let us too, then, obey, if we can, the word of God saying: *Catch us the little foxes, that destroy the vineyards*: let us see whether we too can catch certain little foxes; let us set up at both openings, that wherever the fox wishes to go out, he may be caught. For example, to a Manichee making for himself a recent god, and setting in his heart what is not, let us say and question him: Is the substance of God corruptible, or incorruptible? Choose what you will, and go out where you will, but you will not escape. If you say: Corruptible; you will be stoned not by the people, but by yourself: but if you say God is incorruptible; how did the incorruptible fear the race of darkness? what would the race of corruption do to the incorruptible? What remains except that it be said: *We do not know?* But yet if this is said not from craft, but from ignorance, let him not remain in darkness: from a fox let him become a sheep, let him believe in the invisible, incorruptible, sole (solus) God, not the recent one; sole, from that he is sole, not from that he is sun (sol), lest we ourselves seem to have opened another den for the fleeing fox. Although neither shall we dread the name of the sun. For it is in our Scriptures: *The sun of righteousness, and health in his wings*[^40]. From the heat of this sun the shadow is sought: but under the wings of this sun there is flight from the heat; for health is in his wings. This is the sun of which the impious are to say: *Therefore we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shone for us, and the sun has not risen for us*[^41]. The worshipers of the sun are to say: *The sun has not risen for us;* because, although they adore the sun which he makes rise upon the good and the evil[^42], the sun which enlightens the good alone has not risen for them. They make for themselves, then, recent gods, each one such as they wish: for what prevents the workshop of a deceived heart from fashioning a phantasm such as it wishes? But all these agree in the latter things, that is, are held by a like vanity. Whence our Samson, who is also interpreted "their sun"—namely of those for whom he shines; not of all, as is the one rising upon the good and the evil, but the sun of certain ones, the sun of righteousness (for he bore a figure of Christ)—bound together, as I had begun to say, the tails of the foxes, and there bound fire; fire to burn, but the harvests of the foreigners. Accordingly such men, agreeing in the latter things, as it were bound together by the tails, drag a corrupting fire; but they do not burn the crops of our own. *For the Lord knows who are his; and let everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord depart from iniquity. But in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and silver, but also of wood and clay: and some indeed unto honor, but others unto dishonor. But if anyone cleanses himself from such things, he will be a vessel unto honor, useful to the Lord, prepared for every good work*[^43]; and therefore he will dread neither the tails of the foxes, nor the torches of the foxes. But let us see concerning this people: *If you will hear me,* he says, *there shall not be in you a recent god.* It moves me, what he said, *in you*: for he did not say: From you, as though an idol applied from outside; but, *in you,* in your heart, in the image of your phantasm, in the deception of your error you will carry your recent god with you, remaining old. *If,* then, *you will hear me* (*me,* he says, because I am who I am[^44]); *there shall not be in you a recent god, nor will you adore an alien god.* For if it be not in you, *you will not adore an alien god*: if you do not think of a false god, you will not adore a fabricated god; *for there will not be in you a recent god.*

**15.** [v. 11.] *For I am.* Why do you wish to adore what is not? *For I am the Lord your God*: because I am who I am. And I indeed am, he says, who am above every creature: yet to you, in temporal terms, what have I bestowed? *Who led you out of the land of Egypt.* It is said not to that people only: for we have all been led out of the land of Egypt, we have all passed through the Red Sea, our enemies pursuing us perished in the water. Let us not be ungrateful to our God; let us not forget the abiding God, and fabricate in ourselves a recent god. *Who led you out of the land of Egypt*: God speaks. *Open wide your mouth, and I will fill it.* You suffer narrowness in yourself on account of a recent god set up in your heart; break the vain idol, cast down from your conscience the fashioned idol: *open wide your mouth,* by confessing, by loving; *and I will fill it,* since with me is the fountain of life[^45].

**16.** [v. 12.] This indeed the Lord says; but what follows? *And my people did not heed my voice.* For he would not say these things, except to his own people: for we know that whatever the Law says, it says to those who are in the Law[^46]. *And my people did not heed my voice; and Israel did not attend to me.* Who? to whom? *Israel to me.* O ungrateful soul! a soul through me, a soul called by me, a soul led back to hope by me, a soul washed from sins by me: *And Israel did not attend to me.* For they are baptized and pass through the Red Sea; but on the way they murmur, contradict, complain, are disturbed by seditions, ungrateful to him who freed them from the pursuing enemies, who leads them through the dry land, through the desert, yet with food and drink, with light by night and shade by day: *And Israel did not attend to me.*

**17.** [v. 13.] *And I let them go according to the affections of their heart.* Behold the winepress: the openings are opened, the dregs run. *And I let them go,* not according to the salvation of my precepts; but, *according to the affections of their heart*: I gave them over to themselves. The Apostle too says: *God gave them over to the desires of their heart*[^47]. *I let them go according to the affections of their heart; they will go in their own affections.* Hence is that at which you shudder: if at least you are strained out into the hidden storage-vats of the Lord, if at least you have come to love his storehouses, hence is that at which you shudder. Some defend the circus, others the amphitheater, others the cottages in the villages, others the theaters, others this, others that; others, finally, their own recent gods: *They will go in their own affections.*

**18.** [vv. 14-15.] *If my people had heard me; if Israel had walked in my ways.* For perhaps this Israel says: Behold, I sin, it is manifest; I go after the affections of my heart: but what am I doing? The devil does this, the demons do this. What is the devil? who are the demons? Surely your enemies. *If Israel had walked in my ways; I would have humbled all their enemies to nothing.* Therefore: *If my people had heard me*: for why *my,* if it does not hear me? *If my people had heard me.* What is, *my people? Israel.* What is, *heard me? If it had walked in my ways.* It complains, and groans under enemies: *I would have humbled their enemies to nothing, and upon those troubling them I would have set my hand.*

**19.** [v. 16.] But now why do they complain of enemies? They themselves have become worse enemies. For how? what follows? You complain of enemies; what are you? *The enemies of the Lord have lied to him.* Do you renounce? I renounce: and he returns to what he renounces. Surely, what things do you renounce, except evil deeds, diabolical deeds, deeds to be condemned by God; thefts, robberies, perjuries, murders, adulteries, sacrileges, abominable rites, idle curiosities? All these you renounce, and again, bent back, you are overcome by them. Your latter things become worse than the former: the dog returned to its vomit, and the washed sow to its wallowing in the mire[^48]. *The enemies of the Lord have lied to him.* And how great is the patience of the Lord! Why are they not laid low? why are they not slaughtered? why are they not swallowed up by the earth gaping open? why are they not burned by the blazing heaven? Because great is the patience of the Lord. And will they be unpunished? God forbid. Let them not so flatter themselves concerning the mercy of God, as to promise themselves his injustice. Are you ignorant that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But you, according to the hardness of your heart and your impenitent heart, store up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works[^49]. And if he does not render now, then he will render: for if he renders now, he renders for a time; but to one not converted nor corrected, he will render forever. See, then, that they will not be unpunished; attend to what follows: *The enemies of the Lord have lied to him.* You will say: And what did he do to them? Do they not live? do they not breathe the air? do they not drink the light? do they not drink at the fountains? do they not eat the fruits of the earth? *And their time shall be forever.*

**20.** Let no one, then, flatter himself, because he as it were belongs to the winepress: it is well for him, if he belongs to the oil in the winepress. Let no one promise himself, having wicked deeds, which shall not possess the kingdom of God, and say to himself: Since I have the sign of Christ and the sacraments of Christ, I shall not be blotted out forever; and if I am purged, I shall be saved through fire. For what is it that the Apostle says of those who have the foundation? *Other foundation no one can lay, besides that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus.* What is it, they say, that follows? *But let each one see what he builds upon the foundation. For one builds gold, silver, precious stones; another wood, hay, stubble: of what sort each one's work is, the fire will test; for the day of the Lord will declare it, because it will be revealed in fire. If anyone's work which he has built remains, he will receive a reward:* that is, because upon the foundation he has built righteous works; *gold, silver, precious stones.* But if sins; *wood, hay, stubble*: yet on account of the foundation *he himself will be saved; yet so as through fire*[^50]. Brothers, I wish to be exceedingly fearful: for it is better not to give you a bad security. I will not give what I do not receive; fearing, I terrify: I would make you secure, if I myself became secure; I fear the eternal fire. *And their time shall be forever,* I take to be nothing but eternal fire; of which Scripture says in another place: *Their fire shall not be quenched, and their worm shall not die*[^51]. But he spoke of the impious, someone says, not of me; who, although I am a sinner, although an adulterer, although a defrauder, although a robber, although a perjurer; yet I have Christ in my foundation, I am a Christian, I am baptized: I am purged through fire, and on account of the foundation I do not perish. Tell me again: What are you? A Christian, you say. Meanwhile, pass on. What else? A robber, an adulterer, and the rest of which the Apostle says: *That those who do such things shall not possess the kingdom of God*[^52]. Surely, then, not corrected from such things, not doing penance for such things committed, do you hope for the kingdom of heaven? I do not think so; *since those who do such things shall not possess the kingdom of God.* And are you ignorant that the patience of God leads you to repentance[^53]? You, promising yourself I know not what, according to your hardness and impenitent heart store up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works. Attend, then, to the judge himself coming. Well; thanks be to God: he did not keep silent the definitive sentence, he did not put the accused outside and draw the veil. He willed first to foretell what he disposed to do. For surely *all the nations will be gathered before him.* What will he do with them? *He will separate them; and set some at the right hand, others at the left.* Has a middle place been kept? What will he say to those at the right? *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom.* What to those at the left? *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^54]. If you do not fear being sent whither, see with whom. If, then, all those works shall not possess the kingdom of God; rather, not the works, but those who do such things; for such works will not be in the fire: for those burning in that fire will not steal and commit adultery; but *those who do such things shall not possess the kingdom of God*: therefore they will not be at the right with those to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom;* because *those who do such things shall not possess the kingdom of God.* If, then, they will not be at the right, it remains only that they be at the left: to those at the left what will he say? *Go into the eternal fire*: because *their time shall be forever.*

**21.** Expound to us, then, he says, how those who build wood, hay, stubble upon the foundation do not perish, but are saved; yet so as through fire. That question is indeed obscure, but, as I can, I say it briefly. Brothers, there are men altogether despisers of this world, to whom whatever flows temporally is not pleasing; they do not cling by any love to earthly works, holy, chaste, continent, just, perhaps even selling all their goods and distributing to the poor, or possessing as though not possessing, and using this world as though not using it[^55]. But there are others who cling somewhat in love to the things conceded to weakness. He does not seize another's farm; but he so loves his own, that if he loses it, he is disturbed. He does not covet another's wife; but he so clings to his own, so unites with his own, that he does not keep there the measure prescribed by the tablets, for the sake of procreating children. He does not take away others' things; but he reclaims his own, and has a lawsuit with his brother: for to such men it is said: *Now indeed there is altogether a fault in you, that you have lawsuits among yourselves*[^56]. But these very judgments he bids to be conducted in the Church, not dragged to the forum; yet he says they are faults: for the Christian contends for earthly things more than befits him to whom the kingdom of heaven has been promised; he does not lift his whole heart upward, but drags some part of it on the earth. Finally, if there come a temptation of undergoing martyrdom, those who have Christ in the foundation, and build gold, silver, precious stones, what do they say at this opportunity? It is good for me to be dissolved, and to be with Christ[^57]: eager, they run, and grieve nothing or only moderately over earthly frailty. But those loving their own things, their own houses, are gravely disturbed; the hay, and stubble, and wood burn. They have, then, wood, hay, stubble upon the foundation; but of conceded things, not unlawful ones. For I say, brothers: you possess the foundation; cling to heaven, tread the earth. If you are such, you build nothing but gold, silver, and precious stones. But when you say: I love this possession, I fear lest it perish; and loss threatens, and you are saddened: you do not indeed prefer it to Christ; for you so love this possession, that if it were said to you: Do you want it, or Christ? even if sad you lose it, yet you embrace Christ more, whom you have set in the foundation: you will be saved as through fire. Hear another thing: you cannot hold this possession, unless you say false testimony. And not to do this is to set Christ in the foundation: for Truth said: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^58]. Therefore if you love your possession, yet do not for its sake commit robbery, do not for its sake say false testimony, do not for its sake commit murder, do not for its sake swear falsely, do not for its sake deny Christ; in that you do not do these things for its sake, you have Christ in the foundation. But yet because you love it, and are saddened if you lose it; you have set upon the foundation, not gold, or silver, or precious stones, but wood, hay, stubble. You will be saved, then, when what you have built begins to burn; yet so as through fire. For let no one building upon this foundation adulteries, blasphemies, sacrileges, idolatries, perjuries, think that he will be saved through fire, as though these were wood, hay, stubble: but he who builds love of earthly things upon the foundation of the kingdom of heaven—that is, upon Christ; the love of temporal things will burn, and he himself will be saved through the suitable foundation.

**22.** [v. 17.] *The enemies of the Lord have lied to him,* by saying: I go to the vineyard, and not going[^59]: *and their time shall be,* not for a time, but *forever.* And who are these? *And he fed them from the fat of the wheat.* You know the fat of the wheat, from which are fed many enemies who have lied to him. *And he fed them from the fat of the wheat*: he mingled them with his sacraments. Judas too, when he gave the morsel, he fed from the fat of the wheat[^60]: and the enemy of the Lord lied to him, and his time shall be forever. *And he fed them from the fat of the wheat; and with honey from the rock he satisfied them.* O ungrateful ones! *He fed them from the fat of the wheat, and with honey from the rock he satisfied them.* In the desert he brought forth water from the rock[^61], not honey. Honey is wisdom, holding the primacy of sweetness in the foods of the heart. How many enemies of the Lord, then, lying to the Lord, are fed, not only from the fat of the wheat, but even with honey from the rock, from the wisdom of Christ! how many are delighted by his word and by the knowledge of his sacraments, by the loosing of his parables, how many are delighted, how many cry out! And this honey is not from any man whatever, but from the rock: but the rock was Christ[^62]. How many, then, are satisfied with this honey, cry out, say: It is sweet; say: Nothing better, nothing sweeter could be either understood or said! and yet the enemies of the Lord have lied to him. I do not wish to linger longer on things to be grieved over: although the Psalm has ended terribly with this, yet from its end, I beseech you, let us run back to its head: *Exult in God our helper*[^63], turned to God.  
and after the word:

**23.** Not a little have the divine spectacles in the name of Christ held your minds, and held you in suspense, not only to desire certain things, but even to flee certain things. These are useful spectacles, wholesome, edifying, not destroying: rather, both destroying and edifying; destroying recent gods, edifying faith in the true and eternal God. We invite Your Charity even for tomorrow. Tomorrow they have, as we have heard, a sea in the theater: let us have a harbor in Christ. But since on the day after tomorrow—that is, the fourth of the sabbath—we cannot assemble at the table of Cyprian, because it is the festival of the holy Martyrs; tomorrow let us assemble at that table.
