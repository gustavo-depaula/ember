# Exposition of Psalm 5

**1.** [v 1.] The title of the psalm is: *For her who receives the inheritance.* The Church, then, is understood, which receives as its inheritance eternal life through our Lord Jesus Christ, that it may possess God himself, by cleaving to whom it is blessed, according to that saying: *Blessed are the meek, for they will possess the earth as their inheritance*[^1]; what earth? but that of which it is said: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^2]; and that plainer text: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup*[^3]. And the Church is in turn called the inheritance of God, according to that saying: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance*[^4]. Therefore God is called our inheritance, because he himself feeds and sustains us: and we are called God's inheritance, because he himself administers and governs us. For which reason this is the voice of the Church in this psalm, called to the inheritance, that it too may become the inheritance of the Lord.

**2.** [v 2.] *Hear my words, Lord*: called, she calls upon the Lord, that by his help she may pass through the wickedness of this world, and come to him. *Understand my cry*: she shows well what this cry is, how inward it is, that it reaches God from the bed of the heart without the noise of the body; since the bodily voice is heard, but the spiritual is understood. Although this too is for God to hear, not with a carnal ear, but by the presence of his majesty.

**3.** [v 3.] *Attend to the voice of my supplication*: that is, to that voice which she asks God to understand; what sort it is she has already suggested, when she said: *Understand my cry. Attend to the voice of my supplication, my King and my God.* Although the Son is God, and the Father is God, and together the Father and the Son are one God; and if we are asked about the Holy Spirit, nothing else is to be answered but that he is God; and when the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are spoken of together, nothing else is to be understood but one God; yet the Scriptures are accustomed to call the Son king. According, then, to that saying: *Through me one goes to the Father*[^5], rightly first, *my King*, and then, *my God.* Yet she did not say: Attend (plural); but: *Attend* (singular): for the catholic faith does not proclaim two or three gods, but the Trinity itself one God: not that the same Trinity can at one time be called Father, at another the Son, at another the Holy Spirit, as Sabellius believed; but that the Father is none but the Father, and the Son none but the Son, and the Holy Spirit none but the Holy Spirit, and this Trinity none but one God. For when the Apostle had said: *Of whom are all things, through whom are all things, in whom are all things*[^6], he is believed to have suggested the Trinity itself; and yet he did not add, To them be glory; but: *To him be glory.*

**4.** [v 4.] *For to you I will pray, Lord, in the morning you will hear my voice*: what does it mean that above she said, *hear*, as though she desired to be heard in the present, but now says, *in the morning you will hear*, not, hear; and, *to you I will pray*, not, to you I pray; and then, *in the morning I will stand before you and will see*, not, I stand and see? Unless perhaps her earlier prayer shows the invocation itself: but, growing dim amid the storms of this world, she perceives that she does not see what she desires, and yet does not cease to hope: for the hope that is seen is not hope. Yet she understands why she does not see, because the night is not yet ended, that is, the darkness which her sins have deserved. She says, then: *For to you I will pray, Lord*, that is, since you are so great, to whom I will pray, *in the morning you will hear my voice.* You are not, she says, one who can be seen by those from whose eyes the night of sins has not yet withdrawn: when, therefore, the night of my error is ended, and the darkness departs which I have made for myself by my sins, you will hear my voice. Why, then, did she not say above, you will hear; but, *hear*? Is it that, after she cried, *hear*, and was not heard, she perceived what must pass away that she might be able to be heard? Or was she heard even above, but does not yet understand herself to be heard, because she does not yet see by whom she was heard; and what she now says, *in the morning you will hear*, she wished this to be understood, in the morning I shall understand that I have been heard? Such is that expression: Rise up, Lord, that is, make me rise up; but this is taken of the resurrection of Christ. That other surely cannot rightly be taken otherwise: *The Lord your God tests you, that he may know whether you love him*[^7], except that you may know through him, and it may be made manifest to yourselves how much you have advanced in his love.

**5.** [vv 5-7.] *In the morning I will stand before you, and will see*: what is *I will stand*, but I will not lie down? And what else is it to lie down, but to rest on the earth, which is to seek blessedness in earthly pleasures? *I will stand*, she says, *and will see*: we must not, then, cling to earthly things, if we wish to see God, who is beheld with a clean heart. *For you are not a God who wills iniquity. The malicious one will not dwell beside you, nor will the unrighteous remain before your eyes. You hate all who work iniquity, you will destroy all who speak a lie. The man of blood and the deceitful one the Lord will abominate*: iniquity, malice, lying, murder, deceit, and whatever is of this kind, is the very night, at whose passing it becomes morning, so that God may be seen. She has set forth, then, the reason why she will stand in the morning, and will see: *because you are not a God who wills iniquity*, she says. For if he were a God who willed iniquity, he could be seen even by the unrighteous; so that he would not be seen in the morning, that is, when the night of iniquity has passed away.

**6.** *The malicious one will not dwell beside you*: that is, he will not so see as to cleave. Therefore there follows, *nor will the unrighteous remain before your eyes*; because their eyes, that is, their mind, is beaten back by the light of truth on account of the darkness of their sins, whose habit makes them unable to bear the splendor of right understanding. Therefore even those who at times see, that is, who understand the truth, yet are still unrighteous, do not remain there, by loving the things that turn them away from the truth; for they carry their night with them, that is, not only the habit, but also the love of sinning. If this night passes away, that is, if they cease to sin, and that very love and habit is put to flight, it becomes morning; so that they not only understand, but also cleave to the truth.

**7.** *You hate all who work iniquity*: the hatred of God is to be understood from that expression by which every sinner hates the truth. For it seems that he hates also those whom he does not allow to remain in himself: but they do not remain, who are not able to bear it. *You will destroy all who speak a lie*: for this is contrary to truth. But lest anyone suppose that some substance or nature is contrary to truth, let him understand that a lie pertains to that which is not, not to that which is. For if what is is spoken, the truth is spoken; but if what is not is spoken, it is a lie. Therefore, he says: *You will destroy all who speak a lie*; because, withdrawing from that which is, they are turned aside to that which is not. Many things, indeed, seem to be lies told for the salvation or advantage of someone, not out of malice, but out of kindness, such as those of the midwives in Exodus who reported a falsehood to Pharaoh, that the infant sons of Israel might not be killed. But even these are praised not for the deed, but for the disposition; since those who lie only in this way will deserve at some time to be set free from all lying. For in those who are perfect, not even these lies are found; to whom it was said: *Let it be in your mouth: Yes, yes; no, no: whatever is more than this is from evil*[^8]. Nor without reason is it written in another place: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^9]; lest anyone suppose that the perfect and spiritual man ought to lie for the sake of this temporal life, in whose death the soul, whether one's own or another's, is not killed. But since to lie is one thing, to conceal the truth another; since indeed to speak a falsehood is one thing, to be silent about the truth another; if anyone perhaps does not wish to betray a man even to this visible death, he ought to be ready to conceal the truth, not to speak a falsehood, so that he neither betrays nor lies, lest he kill his own soul for another's body. But if he cannot yet do this, let him at least have only the lies of this necessity, so that even from these, if these alone remain, he may deserve to be set free, and to receive the strength of the Holy Spirit, by which he may despise whatever is to be endured for the truth. There are altogether two kinds of lies in which there is no great fault, but yet they are not without fault, when we either jest, or lie in order to be of benefit. That first kind, in jesting, is for this reason not most pernicious, because it does not deceive; for he to whom it is said knows that it is said for the sake of a jest. The second is for this reason milder, because it retains some good will. But that which has no double heart is not even to be called a lie: as, for example, if a sword is entrusted to someone, and he promises to return it when the one who entrusted it asks for it; if perhaps the man, raving, demands his sword back, it is manifest that it is then not to be returned, lest he kill either himself or others, until his sanity is restored to him. Here, then, there is no double heart, because the man to whom the sword was entrusted, when he promised to return it to the one asking, was not thinking that the man could ask for it raving. Now the Lord too concealed the truth, when he said to disciples not yet fit: *I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^10]; and the apostle Paul, when he says: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal*[^11]. From which it is manifest that it is not to be blamed sometimes to be silent about the truth. But to speak a falsehood is not found to have been permitted to the perfect.

**8.** *The man of blood and the deceitful one the Lord will abominate.* Here it can rightly be seen as a repetition of what he says above: *You hate all who work iniquity, you will destroy all who speak a lie*; so that you refer *the man of blood* to the one who works iniquity, and *the deceitful one* to the lie: for deceit is when one thing is done and another is feigned. And he used a fitting word in saying *will abominate*; for those who are disinherited are commonly said to be abominated: but this psalm is *for her who receives the inheritance*, and she adds the exultation of her hope, saying: *But I, in the multitude of your mercy, will enter into your house. In the multitude of your mercy*: perhaps she means in the multitude of perfect and blessed men, of whom that city will consist, which the Church now travails with and little by little brings forth. And who can deny that many regenerated and perfected men are rightly called the multitude of God's mercy, when it was most truly said: *What is man, that you are mindful of him; or the son of man, that you visit him?*[^12] *I will enter into your house*: like a stone, I believe, into the building, it is said. For what else is the house of God than the temple of God, of which it was said: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^13]? whose cornerstone is he whom the Power and wisdom of God, coeternal with the Father, took up.

**9.** *I will worship toward your holy temple, in your fear*: we understand *toward your temple* as near the temple; for she does not say: I will worship in your holy temple, but: *I will worship toward your holy temple.* It is also to be understood as said not of perfection, but of progress toward perfection; so that this signifies perfection: *I will enter into your house.* But that this may come about, first, she says, *I will worship toward your holy temple*; and perhaps for this reason she added, *in your fear*, which is a great safeguard for those advancing toward salvation. But when anyone has arrived, there will come about in him what was said: *Perfected love casts out fear*[^14]; for they do not fear a friend, to whom it was said: *Now I will not call you servants, but friends*[^15], when they have been brought to that which was promised.

**10.** [vv 9.10.] *Lord, lead me in your righteousness because of my enemies*: here she sufficiently declared that she is in progress, that is, in advance toward perfection, not yet in perfection itself, when she longs to be led. *In your righteousness*, however, not in that which seems righteous to men. For to repay evil for evil seems righteousness, but it is not the righteousness of him of whom it was said: *Who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil*[^16]; for even when God punishes sinners, he does not inflict his own evil upon them, but abandons them to their own evils: *Behold*, he says, *he has travailed with injustice, he has conceived toil, and brought forth iniquity: he has opened a pit and dug it out, and has fallen into the ditch that he made: his pain will be turned upon his own head, and his iniquity will come down upon his own crown*[^17]. Therefore, when God punishes, he punishes as a judge those who transgress the law, not inflicting evil upon them from himself, but driving them into what they chose, to fill up the sum of their miseries. But man, when he repays evil for evil, does it with an evil will; and on this account he is himself the worse, while he wishes to punish evil.

**11.** *Direct my way in your sight.* Nothing is plainer than that she commends to him the time in which she advances; for this is the way that is traversed not by places of the earth, but by the affections of the soul. *Direct my way*, she says, *in your sight*: that is, where no man sees, men who are not to be believed when they praise or blame; for they cannot in any way judge of another's conscience, in which the way is directed to God. Therefore she added: *Because there is no truth in their mouth*, who certainly are not to be believed in their judgments, and therefore one must flee inward to conscience and to the sight of God. *Their heart is vain*: how, then, can there be truth in the mouth of those whose heart is deceived about sin and the punishment of sin? Whence men are recalled by that voice: *Why do you love vanity, and seek a lie?*

**12.** [v 11.] *Their throat is an open tomb*: this can be referred to the signification of voracity, on account of which men often lie out of flattery. And he wonderfully said, *an open tomb*; since that voracity always gapes, unlike tombs which, having received the corpses, are closed up. That too can be understood, that by a lie and smooth flattery they draw into themselves those whom they entice to sins, and as it were devour them when they turn them to their own life. And since those to whom this happens die by sin, rightly are those by whom they are led called open tombs; because they themselves too are in a manner lifeless, not having the life of truth, and they receive into themselves the dead, whom, slain by lying words and a vain heart, they turn into themselves. *With their tongues they were acting deceitfully*, that is, with evil tongues: for this is what he seems to signify when he says *their own*, for the evil have evil tongues; that is, they speak evil, when they speak deceit. To whom the Lord says: *How can you speak good things, when you are evil?*[^18]

**13.** *Judge them, God: let them fall from their own counsels.* This is a prophecy, not a curse: for she does not wish that it come to pass, but discerns what is to come to pass: for it befalls them, not because she seems to have wished it, but because they are such that it deservedly befalls them. For thus also what she afterward says: *Let all who hope in you rejoice*, she says by prophecy; since she discerns that they will rejoice. Thus by prophecy it was said: *Stir up your power, and come*[^19]; since he saw that he would come. Although what was said, *let them fall from their own counsels*, can also be taken in this way, so that it may rather be believed to be wished by her as a good, that they fall away from their own evil counsels, that is, that they no longer think evil things. But what follows forbids this from being so understood, *drive them out*; for in no way can it be taken in a good sense, when anyone is driven out by God. For which reason it is understood as said by prophecy, not by ill will; since this was said as what will necessarily befall such men, who shall have wished to persevere in the sins that have been mentioned. *Let them fall*, then, *from their own counsels*, it was said; let them fall, with their own thoughts accusing them, their conscience bearing witness, as the Apostle says: *And of the thoughts accusing or defending, in the revelation of the just judgment of God*[^20].

**14.** *According to the multitude of their impieties drive them out*: that is, drive them far out; for this is *according to the multitude of their impieties*, that they be driven far. The impious, then, are driven out from that inheritance which is possessed by understanding and seeing God; just as diseased eyes are driven out by the brightness of light, when that is a punishment to them which to others is gladness. They themselves, then, will not stand in the morning and see. And as great a punishment is this expulsion, as great a reward is that which is said: *But for me it is good to cleave to God*[^21]. To this punishment is contrary: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^22]. For to this expulsion is similar: *Cast him into the outer darkness*[^23].

**15.** *Because they have embittered you, Lord. I am*, he says, *the bread that came down from heaven*[^24]: and: *Work for the food that does not perish*[^25]: and: *Taste, and see that the Lord is sweet*[^26]. But to sinners the bread of truth is bitter; whence they hate the mouth of one who speaks the truth. They themselves, then, have embittered God, who by sinning have come into such sickness that they cannot bear the food of truth, in which healthy souls rejoice, but find it as gall.

**16.** [v 12.] *And let all who hope in you rejoice*: surely those to whom, tasting, the Lord is sweet. *They will exult forever, and you will dwell in them*: this, then, will be the eternal exultation, when the righteous become the temple of God; and their joy will be he himself, their indweller. *And all who love your name will glory in you*: as when there is present to them, for their enjoyment, what they love. And well *in you*, as those possessing the inheritance of which the title of the psalm speaks, since they themselves too are his inheritance, which is what *you will dwell in them* signifies. From which good those are forbidden whom God drives out according to the multitude of their impieties.

**17.** [v 13.] *Because you will bless the righteous*: this is the blessing, to glory in God, and to be indwelt by God. This sanctification is granted to the righteous; but that they may be justified, the calling goes before, which is not of merits, but of the grace of God: for all have sinned, and lack the glory of God. For whom he called, them he also justified; and whom he justified, them he also glorified. Because, then, the calling is not of our merits, but of the good will and mercy of God, she added, saying: *Lord, you have crowned us as with the shield of your good will*; for the good will of God goes before our good will, that he may call sinners to penance. And these are the arms by which the enemy is vanquished, against whom it is said: *Who will accuse against the elect of God?* and: *If God is for us, who is against us? He who did not spare his only Son, but handed him over for us all*[^27]. *For if, while we were still enemies, Christ died for us; much more, being reconciled, shall we be saved from wrath through him*[^28]. This is the most invincible shield, by which the enemy is repelled, who suggests despair of one's salvation by a multitude of tribulations and temptations.

**18.** The whole text of the psalm, then, is a prayer to be heard; from what is written, *Hear my words, Lord*, down to, *my King and my God.* Then comes the understanding of the things that hinder the seeing of God, that is, that she may know that she has been heard; from what is written, *For to you I will pray, Lord, in the morning you will hear my voice*, down to: *The man of blood and the deceitful one the Lord will abominate.* Third, she hopes that she will be the house of God, and now to draw near to it in fear, before the consummation that drives out fear; from what is written, *But I, in the multitude of your mercy*, down to: *I will worship toward your holy temple in your fear.* Fourth, advancing and progressing amid the very things by which she perceives herself hindered, she prays to be helped inwardly, where no man sees, lest she be turned aside by evil tongues; from what is written, *Lord, lead me in your righteousness because of my enemies*, down to: *With their tongues they were acting deceitfully.* Fifth, there is a prophecy of what punishment awaits the impious, when the righteous one will scarcely be saved, and of what reward the righteous will obtain, who, called, came, and manfully endured all things until they were brought through; from what is written, *Judge them, God*, to the end of the psalm.
