# Exposition of Psalm 36

**1.** The last day, to come terribly, is heard of by those who are unwilling to be secure by living well, and wish for a long time to live badly. But usefully God willed that day to be hidden, so that the heart may always be ready to await what it knows will come, and does not know when it will come. But because our Lord Jesus Christ was sent to us as a teacher, he said that even the Son of man did not know that day, because it was not in his office of teaching that it should be known by us through him. For the Father knows nothing that the Son does not know: since the very knowledge of the Father is that which is his wisdom: but his wisdom is his Son, his Word. But because it did not profit us to know what indeed he knew who had come to teach us—yet not this, which it did not profit us to know—he both, as a teacher, taught something, and, as a teacher, did not teach something. For as a teacher he knew both how to teach what profited, and not to teach what was harmful. And so, by a certain manner of speaking, the Son is said not to know what he does not teach: that is, he is said not to know what he makes us not know, as we speak daily, by a certain manner of speaking, as I have said. For we call a day glad, because it makes us glad; and a day sad, because it makes us sad; and cold sluggish, because it makes us sluggish. As, on the contrary, it is said by the Lord, "Now I have known." It was said to Abraham, *Now I have known that you fear God*[^1]. This God knew even before that testing. For that testing was made for this reason, that we might know what God already knew, and that it might be written for our teaching, what before the proof he knew: and perhaps Abraham himself did not yet know what strength his faith had: for each one comes to know himself, as it were questioned, by temptation: as Peter certainly did not know what strength his faith had, when he said to the Lord, *I am with you even unto death*[^2]. But the Lord, who knew him, foretold where he would fail, foreannouncing to him his infirmity, as though the vein of his heart had been touched. Accordingly Peter, who before the temptation presumed on himself, in the temptation learned himself. So, then, we think it not absurd that our father Abraham too came to know the strength of his faith, when, bidden to immolate his only son, he did not doubt nor tremble to offer to the one who had given; because, as he did not know whence he was going to give one not yet born, so he believed that he could restore the one immolated. God said, then, "Now I have known": which we understand, "Now I have made you know": according to the manners of speaking we have commended: cold sluggish, that makes sluggish; and a glad day, that makes glad: so knowing, that makes one know. Hence is that: *The Lord your God tempts you, that he may know whether you love him*[^3]. For you would assuredly attribute to our Lord God, the supreme God, the true God, a great ignorance—which you will understand to be sacrilegious, if you take it thus: *The Lord tempts you that he may know,* as though he himself conceived knowledge from our temptation, in which before there was ignorance. But what is it: *He tempts you, that he may know*? He tempts you, that he may make you know. Receive, then, from the contrary, the rule of understanding; and as, when you hear God say, *I have known,* you understand, "I have made you know": so when you hear of the Son of man, that is, of Christ, that he does not know that day, understand it said that he makes us not know it. But what is it, that he makes us not know? That he hides it, so that what does not profit to be disclosed to us, may not be known. This is what I said, that the good teacher knows what to disclose, knows what to read out: as we read that he put off certain things. Whence we understand that not all things are to be brought forth, which those to whom they are brought forth cannot grasp. For he says elsewhere, *I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^4]. The Apostle too says, *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal, as to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not food: for neither were you able; but neither indeed are you yet able*[^5]. To what does this discourse tend? That, since we know the last day is to come, and we usefully know it is to come, and usefully are ignorant when it is to come, we may have a heart ready by living well; and not only not fear that day to come, but even love it. For that day, as it increases labor for the unfaithful, so for the faithful it ends it. But which of these two you wish to be, before it comes, is now in your power; when it has come, it will not be. Choose, then, while there is time: because what God mercifully hides, he mercifully defers.

**2.** But now, because in whatever kind of life, that has some profession, not all are found upright, not all reprobate, from this it appears, because of certain kinds of men, which we just now heard set forth through likenesses in the Gospel, it is thus concluded: *One shall be taken, and one shall be left*[^6]. The good shall be taken, the wicked shall be left. Two are seen in the field; the profession is the same, but not the same heart. Men see the profession, God knows the heart. Whatever the field signify, *one shall be taken, and one shall be left.* Not as though half shall be taken, and half left: but he means two kinds of men. And though one of them be among the few, the other among the many: *one shall be taken, and one shall be left*: that is, one kind shall be taken, and the other left. So in the bed, so in the mill. Perhaps you await what these things are: you see they are covered, and wrapped in certain likenesses. One thing can seem so to me, another to someone else: but neither do I, by what I shall have said, prescribe to another against a better understanding, nor he to me against accepting both, if both agree with faith. For it seems to me that those labor in the field who preside over churches; as the Apostle says, *You are God's tillage, God's building.* For he calls himself an architect, when he says, *As a wise architect I laid the foundation*: and a farmer, when he says, *I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase*[^7]. In the mill, then, he said two women, not two men; I believe, because this figure pertains to the peoples: because the prelates rule, the peoples are ruled. And I think the mill means this world; because it is turned by a certain wheel of the times, and grinds down its lovers. There are, then, those who do not withdraw from the actions of the world: but yet there too some work well, others badly; some make for themselves friends of the mammon of iniquity, by whom they may be received into eternal dwellings, to whom it is said, *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*; others neglect these things, to whom it is said there, *I was hungry, and you gave me not to eat*[^8]. Accordingly, because, of those who are occupied in the business and works of this world, some love to do good to the needy, others neglect it: as of the two women in the mill, *one shall be taken, and one shall be left.* But the bed I think is set for rest: because there are those who neither wish to endure the actions of the world, as are married men having houses, households, children; nor do anything in the Church, as the prelates who labor as in tillage; but, as though weak for these things, they withdraw to leisure, and love to be at rest; as if mindful of their infirmity, not committing themselves to great actions, and in a certain way on the couch of their infirmity beseeching God. And this profession too has good ones, has feigned ones: accordingly of these too *one shall be taken, and one shall be left.* To whatever profession you turn, prepare yourself to endure the feigned: otherwise, if you do not prepare yourself, you will find what you did not hope, and you will fail or be disturbed. He, then, makes you ready for all things who speaks to you, while there is a time both for him to speak, not yet to judge, and for you to hear, not yet to repent in vain. For now repentance is not in vain: then it will be in vain. For then it will not be that men will not repent of having lived badly: but in no way does the justice of God recall to them what their own injustice lost. For it is just with God that now he should impart mercy, then exercise judgment. Therefore now there is no silence. Or is there silence? Let anyone reprove, let him murmur, if this Scripture is not recited and sung throughout the whole world; if it ceases even to be carried for sale through the public places.

**3.** [vv. 1-2] But truly this disturbs you, a Christian man, that you see those living badly happy, flowing about with abundance of these things, healthy, eminent in proud dignities, having a sound household, the joys of their own, the homage of clients, the most excellent powers, nothing sad interrupting their life: you see most worthless characters, you observe most copious resources; and your heart says that there is no divine judgment, that all things are borne by chance and winnowed by fortuitous motions. For if God, you say, regarded human affairs, would his iniquity flourish, and my innocence labor? Every disease of the soul has in the Scriptures its own medicine: he, then, who is so sick as to say these things in his heart, let him drink the potion of this psalm. What is it? Let us look again at what you were saying. What was I saying, you say, except what you see? The wicked flourish, the good labor: how does God see these things? Receive, drink: he himself, of whom you murmur these things, has tempered this potion for you: only do not refuse the most healthful cup; apply through the ear the mouth of your heart, and drink what you hear: *Be not envious of the malicious, nor emulate those who do iniquity. For like grass they shall quickly wither, and like the herbs of the meadow they shall quickly fall*[^9]. What seems long to you, is quick to God: subjoin yourself to God, and it will be quick to you. What he says *grass,* this we understand by *the herbs of the meadow.* There are certain cheap things, holding the surface of the earth, having no deep root. Accordingly through winter they are green: but when the summer sun begins to grow hot, they will wither. Now, then, it is the time of winter, your glory does not yet appear: but if the root of your love is deep, as of many trees through winter, the cold passes, summer will come, that is, the day of judgment: then the greenness of the grass will wither, then the glory of the trees will appear. *For you are dead,* says the Apostle: as trees are seen through winter, as it were dry, as it were dead. What hope, then, if we are dead? Within is the root: where our root is, there too is our life; for there is our love. *And your life,* he says, *is hidden with Christ in God.* When does he wither who has a root thus? But when will our spring be? when our summer? when does the dignity of leaves clothe us round about, and the abundance of fruits enrich us? when will this be? Hear what follows: *When Christ shall appear, your life, then you also shall appear with him in glory*[^10]. What, then, now? *Be not envious of the malicious, nor emulate those who do iniquity. For like grass they shall quickly wither, and like the herbs of the meadow they shall quickly fall.*

**4.** [vv. 3-4] What, then, of you? *Hope in the Lord*[^11]. For they hope not in the Lord: their hope is mortal, their hope is falling, fragile, fleeting, transitory, it will be empty. *Hope in the Lord.* Behold, I hope; what do I do? *And do goodness.* Do not the malice which you regard in those flourishing badly: *do goodness, and inhabit the earth.* Lest perhaps you do goodness outside the habitation of the earth. For the earth of the Lord is his Church: this he waters, this he cultivates, that farmer the Father. For many, as it were, exercise good works, but because they do not inhabit the earth, they do not belong to the farmer. Therefore do goodness, not outside the earth, but inhabit the earth. And what shall I have? *And you shall be fed in its riches.* What are the riches of that earth? Its riches are its Lord, its riches are its God. He is the one to whom it is said, *My portion, O Lord*[^12]. He is the one of whom it is said, *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup*[^13]. In a recent sermon we commended to your Charity both that God is our possession, and that we are the possession of God. Hear that the riches of this earth are he himself: see what follows: *Delight in the Lord.* As though you had asked and said: Show me the riches of that earth, in which you bid me dwell; *Delight,* he says, *in the Lord, and he will give you the petitions of your heart*[^14].

**5.** Take note distinctly of *the petitions of your heart.* Distinguish the petitions of your heart from the petitions of your flesh, distinguish as much as you can. Nor was it said in vain in a certain psalm, *God of my heart.* For there it follows and says, *And my portion is God forever*[^15]. For example, a man is blind in body, he asks to be given sight. Let him ask these things, because God does these things too, and God grants these things: but the wicked too ask these things. This is a petition of the flesh. A man is sick, he asks to be made well: he is made well, who is to die. This too is a petition of the flesh: and whatever such things there are. What is the petition of the heart? As the petition of the flesh is to wish one's eyes to be restored, that is, to see this light, which can be seen with such eyes: so the petition of the heart pertains to another light. For blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God[^16]. *Delight in the Lord, and he will give you the petitions of your heart.*

**6.** [vv. 5-6] Behold, I desire, I ask, I wish: shall I myself fulfill it? No. Who, then? *Reveal your way to the Lord, and hope in him, and he will do it*[^17]. Show him what you suffer, show him what you wish. For what do you suffer? *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*[^18]. What, then, do you wish? *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* And because he himself will do it, when you have revealed your way to him, see what follows: *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^19]. What, then, will he do, since it was said, *Reveal your way to the Lord, and hope in him, and he will do it*: what will he do? *And he will bring forth your justice like light.* For now your justice is hidden: it is a matter of faith, not yet of sight. You believe something so as to do it, you do not yet see what you believe. But when you have begun to see what you have believed, your justice will be brought forth into light: because your justice was your faith. For the just lives by faith[^20].

**7.** *And he will bring forth your justice like light, and your judgment like the noonday*[^21]: that is, the bright light. It was too little to say, *like light.* For we call it light even when it grows pale at dawn, we call it light even when the sun rises: but the light is never brighter than at midday. Not only, then, will he bring forth your justice like light, but your judgment will be like noonday. For now you judge that you should follow Christ; this you have resolved, this you have chosen, this is your judgment: no one has shown you what he promised: you still hold the promiser, but you await the giver: in the judgment of your faith, then, you have chosen to follow what you do not see. Your judgment is hidden, it is still rebuked and derided by unbelievers: What have you believed? What has Christ promised you? That you will be immortal, and he will give you eternal life? Where is this? When will he give it? When can it come to pass? Yet you judge it better to follow Christ promising what you do not see, than the ungodly rebuking you for having believed what you do not yet see. And this is your judgment: and what your judgment is, does not yet appear: in this world it is, as it were, night. When, then, will he bring forth your judgment like noonday? When Christ, our life, shall appear, then you also shall appear with him in glory. When the day of judgment comes, and Christ comes, and gathers all nations to be judged, what will be then? Where will the ungodly hide his faithlessness, when I shall see my faith? What, then, now? Straits, tribulations, and temptations. And blessed is he who endures: because he who perseveres to the end, he shall be saved[^22]. Let him not yield to those who insult, lest he choose to flourish here so as to become grass from a tree.

**8.** [vv. 7-9] What, then, ought I to do? Hear what you ought: *Be subject to the Lord, and beseech him*[^23]. Let this be your life, to obey his precepts. For this is to be subject to him, and to beseech him, until he give what he promised. Let the good work persevere, let prayer too persevere. For one ought always to pray, and not to fail[^24]. In what do you appear subject? By doing what he commanded. But you do not yet receive the reward, perhaps because you cannot yet receive it. For he can already give, but you cannot receive. Be exercised in works, labor in the vineyard: when the day is finished, ask for the reward: faithful is he who brought you to the vineyard. *Be subject to the Lord, and beseech him.*

**9.** Behold, I do it; I am subject to the Lord, and beseech him. But what seems to you? That worthless neighbor, doing evil and flourishing; his thefts, his adulteries, his plunderings I know; in all things lifted up, proud, exalted through iniquity, he does not deign to recognize me: in these things how shall I endure? That is a disease, drink against it: *Do not be envious of him who prospers in his way*[^25]. He prospers, but in his way: you labor, but in the way of God. To him is prosperity in the way, in the arriving unhappiness: to you labor in the way, in the arriving happiness; because the way of the ungodly shall perish. The Lord knows the angers of the just, and the way of the ungodly shall perish[^26]. You walk these ways which the Lord knows; and if you labor in them, they do not deceive you. But the way of the ungodly is a transitory happiness: when the way is finished, the happiness is accomplished. Why? Because that way is broad, and its end leads down into the depth of hell. But your way is narrow, and few enter by it: but to what breadth they come you ought to consider. *Do not be envious of him who prospers in his way. In the man who does iniquity, cease from anger, and forsake indignation*[^27]. Why are you indignant? Why through this wrath and indignation do you either blaspheme, or nearly blaspheme? *In the man who does iniquity, cease from anger, and forsake indignation.* Do you not know to what this anger provokes you? You are about to say to God that he is unjust; to that it goes. Behold, why is that man happy, and that one unhappy? See what it brings forth: choke the evil conception. *Cease from anger, and forsake indignation,* so that now, coming to your senses, you may say, *My eye was troubled by anger*[^28]. What eye, but the eye of faith? I ask the eye of your faith: You believed in Christ; why did you believe? What did he promise you? If Christ promised you the happiness of this world, murmur against Christ, murmur against him, when you see the unbeliever happy. What happiness did he promise you? What but in the resurrection of the dead? But what in this life? What he himself had: what he himself, I say. Or do you disdain, servant and disciple, what your Lord, what your master had? Do you not hear from him, *The servant is not greater than his lord, and the disciple is not above the master*[^29]? He suffered for you griefs, scourges, reproaches, the cross, death. And which of these was owed to the just one? what was not owed to you, a sinner? Therefore keep your eye straight, lest it be troubled by anger: *Cease from anger, and forsake indignation. Do not be envious so as to do evil*: as it were by imitating the one who, by doing evil, flourishes for a time. *Do not be envious so as to do evil. For those who do evil shall be cut off*[^30]. But I see their happiness. Believe him who says, *They shall be cut off*: because he sees better than you, whose eye anger cannot trouble. *For those who do evil shall be cut off. But those who endure the Lord.* Not anyone deceitful, but the very truth: not anyone less powerful, but the omnipotent. *But those who endure the Lord, they shall possess the earth by inheritance.* What earth, but that Jerusalem, with love of which whoever burns will come to peace?

**10.** [v. 10] But how long does the sinner flourish? how long shall I endure? You are in a hurry: that will quickly be which to you is long. Infirmity makes that seem long which is quick. How are the desires of the sick found? Nothing is so long as that the cup be tempered for one who thirsts. Surely it is hurried by his own people, lest perhaps the sick man take offense. When will it be done? when will it be cooked? when will it be given? There is speed in those who serve you, but your infirmity thinks long what is quickly done. Therefore see our physician soothing the sick man who says: How long shall I endure? how long will it be? *And yet a little while, and the sinner shall not be*[^31]. Among sinners certainly you groan, over a sinner you groan: a little while, and he shall not be. Lest perhaps, because I said to you, *But those who endure the Lord, they shall possess the earth by inheritance,* you should think this endurance very long; endure a little, you will receive without end what you endure. *Yet a little while*: a little. Recall the years from Adam to the present day, run through the Scriptures: yesterday, almost, he fell from paradise. So many ages have been measured out and rolled away. Where are the times past? So the few that remain will surely pass. If you lived all that time, from when Adam was sent from paradise to the present day, surely you would see that your life had not been long, which had so flown away. But how great is the life of each single man? Add however many years, draw out the longest old age, what is it? Is it not the morning breeze? Therefore let the day of judgment be far off, when there will be the recompense of the unjust and the just: your last day certainly cannot be far off. Prepare yourself for this. For such as you go forth from this life, such are you rendered to that life. After this little life you will not yet be where the saints will be, to whom it will be said, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^32]. You will not yet be there, who does not know? But you will already be able to be there, where that rich man, proud and barren, in the midst of his torments saw from afar resting that poor man once full of sores. Set in that rest, surely you await secure the day of the judge, when you may receive your body too, when you may be changed, so as to be made equal to the angel. Therefore how great is it, that we hurry, and say: When will it be? Will it be slow? This our children will say, and this our grandchildren will say: and when each successive generation is to say these things, so passes what is still to be for a little while, just as has passed the whole that is now past. O weak one! *Yet a little while, and the sinner shall not be.*

**11.** *And you will seek his place, and not find it*[^33]. He shows what he meant by *he shall not be*: not because he will not be at all, but because he will not be able to be of any use. For if he is not to be at all, neither will he be tormented: already, then, security would be given to the sinner, so that he says: I will do whatever I wish as long as I live, afterward I shall not be. Will there be no one to grieve, no one to be tormented? And where is, *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^34]? But perhaps those sent into that fire will not be, and will be consumed. It would not be said to them, *Go into the eternal fire*; because for those who are not to be, it would not be eternal. And yet what is to be there for them—whether utter consumption, or grief and torment—the Lord did not pass over in silence, saying, *There will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^35]. How, then, will they weep and gnash their teeth, if they will not be? How, then, is it here, *Yet a little while, and the sinner shall not be,* except as in the following verse he expounded it: *And you will seek his place, and not find it*? What is *his place*? His use. For does the sinner have any use? He has. Here God uses him to prove the just, as he used the devil to prove Job, as he used Judas to betray Christ. There is, then, in this life something to be done with the sinner. Here, then, is his place, just as in the goldsmith's furnace there is a place for chaff. The chaff burns, that the gold may be purged: so the ungodly rages, that the just may be proved. But when the time of our proving has passed, when there will be none to be proved, there will be none through whom they may be proved. Is it, because we said, "There will be none to be proved," that they themselves will not be? But because there will now be no need of sinners through whom the just may be proved: *And you will seek his place, and not find it.* Now seek the place of the sinner, you will find it. Of a sinner God has made a scourge, he has given him honor too, he has given him power too. For sometimes he does this; he gives the sinner power, by him human affairs are scourged, by him the godly are amended. To that sinner will be rendered what is owed: and yet something has been made of him whereby the godly profits, whereby the ungodly fails. *You will seek his place, and not find it.*

**12.** [v. 11] *But the meek shall possess the earth by inheritance*[^36]. That is the earth of which we have often spoken, the holy Jerusalem, which will be freed from this pilgrimage, and will live forever with God and from God. Therefore *they shall possess the earth by inheritance.* What will be their delights? *And they shall be delighted in the multitude of peace.* Let that ungodly man be delighted here in the multitude of gold, in the multitude of silver, in the multitude of slaves, in the multitude, finally, of baths, of roses, of wine, of the most sumptuous and luxurious banquets. Is this the power you envy, is this the flower that delights you? Even if it were always so, would it not be lamentable? But what will your delights be? *And they shall be delighted in the multitude of peace.* Your gold is peace, your silver is peace, your estates are peace, your life is peace, your God is peace. Whatever you desire will be peace to you. Because here, what is gold cannot be silver to you; what is wine cannot be bread to you; what is light to you cannot be drink: your God will be the whole to you. You will eat him, that you may not hunger; you will drink him, that you may not thirst; you will be enlightened by him, that you may not be blind; you will be supported by him, that you may not fail; he will possess you whole and entire, himself whole and entire. You will not suffer straits there with him with whom you possess the whole: you will have the whole, and he too will have the whole; because you and he will be one, which one whole he too will have who possesses you. These *are the remnants for the man of peace*[^37]. This we have sung: which verse is indeed far in this psalm from these treated verses. But because we have sung it, we ought to close with it. Only be you secure; *guard innocence,* it is a precious thing. You wish to steal something, I suppose, that you may acquire: see where you put your hand, and from where you take. By this you wish to acquire, by this you lose: you acquire money, you lose innocence. Let your heart rather wake up: you who wished to acquire money and lose innocence, rather lose money: *Guard innocence, and look to uprightness*[^38], because God will direct you, so that all things whatsoever he wishes, you too may wish: for that is uprightness. For if you do not wish what God wishes, you will be crooked, and your crookedness will not allow you to be made level with the straight. *Guard,* then, *innocence, look to uprightness*: and do not suppose that, this life being finished, the man is finished; *because there are remnants for the man of peace.*
