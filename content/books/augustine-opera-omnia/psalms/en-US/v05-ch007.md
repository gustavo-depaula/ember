# On the Same Psalm 103

**1.** I know that you hold us as debtors, not by necessity, but, what is more forceful, by love. But we owe this first to the Lord our God, who, dwelling even in you, demands these things of us: then to my lord and father, present and bidding and praying for me: then to your violence, by which you extort it even from our weak strength. Yet, as far as the Lord grants—who may deign to grant the very strength too at your prayers—since we treated the first parts of this psalm on the day now recently past; the following parts too must be taken up, and, with his help, in whose name we began, must be finished. We had commended to your Charity, you who were present, that this whole psalm is woven of figured mysteries. Whence what is sought with more difficulty is wont to be found more sweetly. And do not suppose that these things are withdrawn from you by obscurity, but seasoned by difficulty; for this purpose, as we have often already said, that it may be given to those who ask, found by those who seek, entered by those who knock. But we need somewhat greater both silence on your part, and patience on your part, that the few things we are about to say may not, through noise, occupy more time. For the narrowness of time compels us to say few things, since your Charity too knows that we owe a solemn service to the obsequies of a faithful body. Behold, that we may not be compelled to repeat what has been said, and expound it again: if any were absent who did not hear, let them not be absent; but it profits them perhaps that they do not now hear what those who were present heard, that they too may learn to be present. Let us read, then, in a running way.

**2.** [vv. 1, 2.] *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* Let the soul of us all say it, made one in Christ. *O Lord my God, you have been magnified exceedingly.* Whence have you been magnified? *You have put on confession and comeliness.* Confess, that you may be adorned, that he may clothe you. *Wrapped around with light as with a garment.* Wrapped around with his Church; because she was made light in him, who before was darkness in herself, as the Apostle says, *You were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^1]. *Stretching out the heaven like a skin*: either as easily as you a skin, if it is so easy, as you would take it to the letter: or let us understand the authority of the Scriptures stretched out through the whole world by the name of skin; because mortality is signified in the skin, but all the authority of the divine Scriptures has been dispensed to us through mortal men, whose fame, now that they are dead, is stretched out.

**3.** [v. 3.] *Who covers its higher parts with waters.* The higher parts of what? Of the heaven. What is the heaven? Figuratively at least we said it is divine Scripture. What are the higher parts of divine Scripture? The precept, than which nothing is more eminent, of love. But why is love compared to waters? *Because the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^2]. Whence is the Spirit himself water? Because *Jesus stood, and cried: If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. He who believes in me, rivers of living water will flow from his belly.* Whence do we prove that it was said of the Spirit? Let the Evangelist himself say, who follows and says, *But this he said of the Spirit, whom those who would believe in him were to receive*[^3]. *Who walks upon the wings of the winds*: that is, upon the virtues of souls. What is the virtue of the soul? Love itself. But how does he walk upon it? Because the love of God toward us is greater than ours toward God.

**4.** [v. 4.] *Who makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a blazing fire*: that is, those who are already spirits, who are spiritual, not carnal, he makes his angels, by sending them to preach his Gospel. *And his ministers a blazing fire.* For unless the minister who preaches burns, he does not set on fire the one to whom he preaches.

**5.** [vv. 5, 6.] *He founded the earth upon its firmament.* He made firm the Church upon the firmament of the Church. What is the firmament of the Church, except the foundation of the Church? What is the foundation of the Church, except that of which the Apostle says, *A foundation no one can lay except that which is laid, which is Christ Jesus?*[^4] And therefore, propped up by such a foundation, what did she deserve to hear? *It will not be inclined unto the age of the age. He founded the earth upon its firmness*, that is, he made firm the Church upon the foundation Christ. The Church will totter if the foundation totters: but whence will Christ totter, who before he came to us and took up flesh, *all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^5]; who contains all things by his majesty, and us by his goodness? Christ not tottering, *it will not be inclined unto the age of the age.* Where are those who say that the Church has perished from the world, when it cannot even be inclined?

**6.** But whence did the Lord begin to assert this Church, to reveal, to begin, to show, to spread it? whence did he begin this? what was it first? *Because he founded the earth upon its firmament, it will not be inclined unto the age of the age. The deep is as a garment, his wrap.* Whose? is it perhaps God's? But he had already said of his wrap: *Wrapped around with light, as with a garment.* I hear that God is clothed with light, and the light itself, if we will, is we. What is it, if we will? If we are not now darkness. So if God is clothed with light, whose wrap, again, is the deep? For the deep is called an immense abundance of waters: all water, all moist nature and substance diffused round about through the seas, and rivers, and hidden caverns, is together by one name called the deep. The earth, then, we understand, of which he said, *He founded the earth upon its firmament; it will not be inclined unto the age of the age;* of that, I believe, it was said, *The deep is as a garment, his wrap.* For the water is as it were the garment of the earth, surrounding it and covering it. But at one time, in the flood, this garment of the earth so grew that it covered absolutely everything, and surmounted the highest mountains, as Scripture testifies, by about fifteen cubits. Perhaps this psalm signified that very time, when it said, *The deep is as a garment, his wrap.*

**7.** *The waters will stand upon the mountains*: that is, the garment of the earth, which is the deep, so grew that the waters stood even upon the mountains. We read, as I said, that this happened in the flood. Was the Prophet speaking from there? was he narrating to us past things, or foretelling things to come? But if he were narrating past things, he would not say to us, *The waters will stand upon the mountains;* but, The waters stood upon the mountains. For we are wont to read that past time is set for future in the Scriptures, the Spirit so foreseeing the things that are to come, as if they were already done. Hence it is that we all know that passage in another psalm, where as it were the Gospel is recited: *They dug my hands and feet, they numbered all my bones; over my garment they cast lots*[^6]. All things are commemorated as if already done, which surely were still discerned to be future. But how much can our diligence do? and how much can such occupation do? or when is there such leisure that we can say for certain: It is so? We notice that the Prophets often say in the past tense of verbs the things that are future; but to say past things by a figure of the future does not easily occur to the reader. I do not dare to say: There is none; but certainly I would have enjoined upon the students of those Scriptures what they should seek. If they find it, and bring it to us, we, busy old men, will rejoice in the studies of the young who are at leisure, and from their service we too will learn something. For we do not disdain it; since Christ teaches concerning all things. This, then, he says: *The waters will stand upon the mountains.* The Prophet, caring to foretell future things, not to narrate past, therefore said it, because he wished the Church to be understood as being in the flood of persecutions. For there was a time at some point when the waters of the persecutors had covered the earth of God, the Church of God; and they had so covered it that not even the great ones, who are the mountains, appeared. For when they fled everywhere, how did they not appear less? And perhaps from those waters is that voice: *Save me, O God, for the waters have entered even to my soul*[^7]. Especially the waters that make the sea, stormy, unfruitful. For sea-water, whatever earth it covers, will not make it fruitful, but rather lead it to barrenness. For there were mountains too under the waters; because the waters stood upon the mountains: the resisting peoples had overcome the authority of all who everywhere strongly evangelized the word of God, the waters had covered them, and the waters stood upon them, saying: Press, press; and they pressed: Extinguish, let them not appear. They said these things, and prevailed over the martyrs, and the Christians fled everywhere, and the Apostles were hidden in a kind of flight. Whence were the Apostles hidden in flight? Because the waters stood upon the mountains. The power of the waters was great. But how long? Hear what follows.

**8.** [v. 7.] *At your rebuke they will flee.* And this happened, brothers; at the rebuke of God the waters fled: that is, they withdrew from the pressure of the mountains. Now the mountains themselves stand forth, Peter and Paul: how do they stand out? Those who before were pressed by the persecutors are now venerated by the emperors. For the waters fled at the rebuke of God: because the heart of kings is in the hand of God; he turned it where he willed, he commanded through them that peace be given to the Christians; the apostolic authority shone forth and stood out. Had the greatness of the mountains failed even when the waters were above? But yet, my brothers, that all might see the eminence of the mountains, through which mountains salvation might be to the human race, because *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me*[^8]; at the rebuke of God the waters fled. *At the voice of your thunder they will fear.* Now who is not terrified at the voice of God through the Apostles, the voice of God through the Scriptures, through his clouds? The sea grew calm, the waters feared, the mountains were laid bare, the emperor commanded. But who would have commanded, unless God had thundered? Because God willed it, they commanded, and it was done. Therefore let no man arrogate anything to himself; the waters feared, but *at the voice of your thunder.* For behold, when God willed, the waters fled, lest they press the mountains; before that was done, the mountains were firm even under the waters.

**9.** [vv. 8-10.] *The mountains ascend, and the plains descend, into the place which you founded for them.* He is still speaking of the waters. Let us not here take the mountains as if earthly; nor the plains as if earthly: but waves so great that they are compared to mountains. The sea once surged, and its waves were like mountains, which would cover those mountains the Apostles. But how long do *the mountains ascend, and the plains descend?* They raged, and were appeased. When they raged, they were mountains: when they were appeased, they became plains; for he founded a place for them. There is a certain channel, as it were a deep place, into which were received in a way all the raging hearts of mortals. How many now are both salt, and bitter, and yet quiet? How many are there who are unwilling to grow sweet? Who are those who are unwilling to grow sweet? Those who are still unwilling to believe in Christ. And although there are many who have not yet believed, what do they do to the Church? They were once mountains, now they are plains: yet, my brothers, calm too is sea. For why do they not now rage? why do they not go mad? why do they not take pains? If they cannot overthrow our earth, certainly to cover it. Why not? Hear: *You set a boundary which they will not transgress, nor will they return to cover the earth.*

**10.** What then, since now the most bitter waves have received a measure, so that we may even freely preach such things; since they have received their due boundary, since they cannot transgress the imposed limit, nor will they return to cover the earth; what happens on the earth itself? what operations are done there, which the sea has now laid bare? Even if at its shore the thin waves roar, even if the pagans still murmur, I hear the sound of the shores, I do not shudder at a flood. What, then, what happens on the earth? *You who send forth springs in the valleys. You send forth*, he says, *springs in the valleys.* You know what valleys are, the low places of the earth. For the opposite figure is set against the hills and mountains, valleys or vales. Hills and mountains are swellings of the earth; but valleys or vales are the lowlinesses of the earth. Do not scorn the lowlinesses; thence flow springs: *You send forth springs in the valleys.* Hear a mountain: the Apostle says, *I labored more than all of them.* A certain greatness is commended; yet immediately, that the waters may flow forth, he made himself a valley: *Yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^9]. It is not contradictory that those who are mountains should also be valleys; for as they are called mountains because of spiritual greatness, so also valleys because of the lowliness of their spirit. *Not I*, he says, *but the grace of God with me. Not I*, that is a valley; and *the grace of God with me*, that is a spring. *You who send forth springs in the valleys.* Of the Spirit was said what I just recalled: *If anyone thirsts, let him come to me, and drink. He who believes in me, rivers of living water will flow from his belly. But this he said of the Spirit, whom those who would believe in him were to receive*[^10]. Let us see whether they are valleys, that springs may be sent forth in the valleys. Hear the prophet: *Upon whom will my Spirit rest, except upon the humble, and the quiet, and the one who trembles at my words?*[^11] What is it, *Upon whom will my Spirit rest, upon the humble and quiet?* Who will have my spring? The valley.

**11.** *Through the midst of the mountains the waters will pass.* Up to here the Psalm was read by the Reader, up to here let it suffice your Charity. This we will say, and in the name of God we will end the sermon. What is it, *Through the midst of the mountains the waters will pass?* We have heard who the mountains are, the great preachers of the word, the lofty angels of God, although still in mortal flesh: lofty not by their own virtue, but by his grace; but as far as concerns themselves, they are valleys, humbly they send forth springs. And *through the midst of the mountains*, he says, *the waters will pass.* Let us suppose this was said thus: Through the midst of the Apostles the preachings of the word of truth will pass. What is it, Through the midst of the Apostles? What is called the midst is common. A common thing, from which all live equally, is in the midst, and does not belong to me; but neither to you, nor to me. Therefore we speak thus too of certain men: They have peace among themselves, they have faith among themselves, they have love among themselves; so certainly we say. What is it, among themselves? In the midst of themselves. What is it, in the midst of themselves? It is common to them. Hear the waters through the midst of the mountains. Because faith was common to them, nor did anyone have the waters as it were proper and his own. For if they are not in the midst, they are as it were private, they do not flow publicly; and I have mine, and he has his, there is nothing in the midst that both I and he have; but such preaching is not peaceable. But truly, that through the midst of the mountains the waters may flow, hear the voice of a mountain: *The God*, he says, *of peace give you to be of the same mind toward one another*[^12]. And again: *That you all be of the same mind, and that there be no schisms among you*[^13]. What I think, you think; in the midst flows the water: I do not have it as it were my own private thing, nor you your private thing. Let the truth be the proper thing neither of mine nor of yours, that it may be both yours and mine: *Through the midst of the mountains the waters will pass.* Hear the mountain himself, as I said, that *through the midst of the mountains the waters will pass. But whether I, or they, so we preach, and so you have believed*[^14]. Securely he said: *Whether I, or they, so we preach, and so you have believed*: for through the midst of the mountains the waters flowed; no discord of the mountains over the waters, but the peace of agreement, and the fellowship of love: but if anyone wished to preach something else, he would now preach of his own, not of the midst. And hear what is said of him by the very one who sent forth springs in the valleys: *He who speaks a lie, speaks of his own*[^15]. And so, lest some mountain be taken as flowing not from the midst, but from its own, the Apostle says: *Whoever evangelizes to you anything other than what you have received, let him be anathema*[^16]. And see how he was unwilling for presumption to be made of a mountain: lest perhaps a mountain withdraw from the waters running through the midst, and wish to pour in something of its own. *Even if we* (And how great a mountain said this! how abundantly the water flowed from his valley! Yet he wished it to run through the midst of the mountains, and that there should be the sure faith of the peoples, which the Apostles retained as the midst and common thing among themselves). *Even if we*, he says. And you, Paul, can you preach something otherwise? It is a question about Paul; hear what follows: *Even if we, or an angel from heaven announce to you anything other than what you have received; let him be anathema*[^17]. If a mountain come evangelizing something else, let him be anathema; if an angel come evangelizing something else, let him be anathema. Whence this? Because he wishes to flow from his private store, not from the midst. And perhaps a man, impeded by the carnal cloud, and reduced from the common fountain to his own falsehood, can do this; can an angel too? truly, can an angel too? If an angel flowing from his own had not been heard in paradise, we would not have been cast headlong into death. Water was set in the midst for men, the precept of God: water in the midst, water as it were public, prevailed without fraud, what we have said to your Charity, without stain, it flowed without mire. If that water had always been drunk, one would always have lived. There came an angel fallen from heaven, made a serpent, because he already desired treacherously to scatter poisons: he sent forth poison, he spoke of his own, of his private store; because *he who speaks a lie, speaks of his own*[^18]: and the wretched, by hearing, let go what was common, whence they were blessed; and reduced to their own private store, when they wished perversely to be like God (for this he had said to them: *Taste, and you will be like gods*[^19]), desiring what they were not, they lost what they had received. Therefore, brothers, let what we have said to your Charity avail for this, on account of the springs: that they may flow from you, be valleys, and share with all what you have from God. Let the waters flow through the midst, envy no one; drink, be satisfied; flow when satisfied. Everywhere let the common water of God have glory, not the private lies of men.
