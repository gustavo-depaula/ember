# Exposition of Psalm 63

**1.** Keeping today as the festal day of the suffering of the holy martyrs, let us rejoice in their remembrance, recalling what they suffered, and understanding what they gazed upon. For never would they bear in the flesh so great tribulations, unless they conceived in the mind a great rest. So let us run through this psalm for the sake of this very solemnity. For yesterday Your Charity heard many things, nor yet today could we deny our service to this festivity. And so, since chiefly the Lord's suffering is commended in this psalm, and the martyrs could not have been strong, unless they gazed upon him who first suffered; nor would they endure in suffering such things as he, unless they hoped for such things in resurrection as he showed beforehand of himself; and Your Holiness knows that our head is our Lord Jesus Christ, and all who cleave to him are the members of that head; and you now have his voice most well known, since he speaks not from the head alone, but also from the body; and his voices not only signify or proclaim the Lord Jesus Christ himself, who has already ascended into heaven, but also his members about to follow their own head: let us recognize here not only his voice, but also ours. And let no one say that today we are not in the tribulation of sufferings. For you have always heard this, that in those times the Church was, as it were, all pressed together at once, but now is tempted individually. The devil is indeed bound, that he not do as much as he can, that he not do as much as he wishes; yet he is permitted to tempt only as much as is expedient for those making progress. Nor is it expedient for us to be without temptations: nor let us ask God that we not be tempted, but that we not be led into temptation.

**2.** [v. 2] Let us, then, say too: *Hear, O God, my prayer, while I am troubled: from the fear of the enemy rescue my soul.*[^1] The enemies raged against the martyrs; what was this voice of the body of Christ praying? It was praying this, that they be rescued from the enemies, and that the enemies might not be able to kill them. Were they, then, not heard, because they were killed; and did God forsake his servants contrite in heart, and despise those hoping in him? Far be it. *For who has called upon the Lord, and been forsaken? who has hoped in him, and been deserted by him*[^2]? They were heard, then, and killed; and yet they were rescued from the enemies. Others, fearing, consented, and lived; and yet they themselves were swallowed up by the enemies. The killed were rescued, the living were swallowed up. Hence is also that voice of thanksgiving: *Perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive*[^3]. Many were swallowed up, and swallowed up alive; many dead were swallowed up. Those who thought the Christian faith empty were swallowed up dead: but those who, knowing that the preaching of the Gospel is the truth, knowing that Christ is the Son of God, and believing this, and holding this within, yet gave way to pains, and sacrificed to idols, were swallowed up alive. The former were swallowed up, because dead; the latter, because swallowed up, dead. For being swallowed up they could not live, although they were swallowed up alive. Therefore this is what the voice of the martyrs prays: *From the fear of the enemy rescue my soul*: not that the enemy not kill me, but that I not fear the enemy who kills. This the servant prays in the Psalm to be fulfilled, which the Lord just now was bidding in the Gospel. What was the Lord just now bidding? *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul; but rather fear him who has power to kill both soul and body in the gehenna of fire*[^4]. And he repeated: *So I say to you; fear him*[^5]. Who are those who kill the body? Enemies. What was the Lord bidding? That they not be feared. Let it be prayed, then, that he furnish what he bids. *From the fear of the enemy rescue my soul.* Rescue me from the fear of the enemy, and subject me to your fear. Let me not fear him who kills the body; but let me fear him who has power to kill both body and soul in the gehenna of fire. For I do not wish to be free from fear; but free from the fear of the enemy, a servant under the fear of the Lord.

**3.** [v. 3] *You have protected me from the assembly of the malignant, from the multitude of those working iniquity.*[^6] Let us now gaze upon our very head. Many martyrs suffered such things: but nothing so shines forth as the head of the martyrs; there we better gaze upon what they experienced. He was protected from the multitude of the malignant, God protecting him, the very Son and the man whom he bore protecting his flesh: because he is the Son of man, and is the Son of God; the Son of God on account of the form of God, the Son of man on account of the form of a servant: having in his power to lay down his soul, and to take it up again. What could the enemies do to him? They killed the body; the soul they did not kill. Attend. It was too little, then, for the Lord to exhort the martyrs by word, unless he strengthened them by example. You know what the assembly of the malignant Jews was, and what the multitude of those working iniquity was. What iniquity? That by which they willed to kill the Lord Jesus Christ. *So many good works*, he says, *I have shown you: for which of these do you wish to kill me*[^7]? He bore with all their weak, healed all their sick, preached the kingdom of heaven, was not silent about their vices, that these rather might displease them, not the physician by whom they were healed: ungrateful for all these cures of his, like men frenzied with much fever, raging against the physician who had come to cure them, they devised a plan of destroying him; as though wishing there to prove whether he was truly a man who could die, or something above men, and would not permit himself to die. We recognize their word in the Wisdom of Solomon: *With a most shameful death*, they say, *let us condemn him. Let us question him; for there will be respect in his words: for if he is truly the Son of God, let him free him*[^8]. Let us see, then, what was done.

**4.** [v. 4] *Who have sharpened their tongues like a sword.*[^9] *The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword*, which another psalm too says: so here too: *They have sharpened their tongues like a sword.* Let not the Jews say: We did not kill Christ. For therefore they handed him over to the judge Pilate, that they themselves might seem, as it were, free from his death. For when Pilate had said to them: *Kill him yourselves,* they answered: *It is not lawful for us to kill anyone*[^10]. They wished to refund the iniquity of their crime upon a man as judge: but surely they did not deceive God as judge? What Pilate did, in that very thing which he did, he was in some part a participant; but in comparison with them he himself was far more innocent. For he urged as much as he could, that he might free him from their hands. For therefore he produced him to them scourged. He scourged the Lord, not persecuting him, but wishing to satisfy their fury, that thus at least they might soften, and cease to wish to kill him, when they saw him scourged: he did this too. But when they persisted, you know that he washed his hands, and said that he had not done what he had done, that he was clean of his death: yet he did it. But if he is guilty who did it even unwilling; are those innocent who compelled him to do it? By no means. But he pronounced sentence against him, and ordered him to be crucified, and as it were himself killed him; and you, O Jews, killed him. Whence did you kill him? By the sword of the tongue: for you sharpened your tongues. And when did you strike, except when you cried out: *Crucify, crucify*[^11]?

**5.** For this is not to be passed over, since it comes to mind, lest perhaps a reading of the divine Books trouble someone: a certain evangelist says the Lord was crucified at the sixth hour, and a certain one at the third hour: unless we understand, we are troubled. And the sixth hour now beginning, Pilate is said to have sat on the tribunal; and truly when the Lord was lifted up on the wood, it was the sixth hour. But another evangelist, looking at the mind of the Jews, because they wished to seem free from the Lord's death, by narrating shows them guilty, saying the Lord was crucified at the third hour. But considering the whole circumstance of the reading, how many things could have been done, when the Lord was being accused before Pilate, that he might be crucified; we find it could have been the third hour, when they cried out: *Crucify, crucify.* Therefore more truly they killed him then, when they cried out. The officers of the power crucified him at the sixth hour; the transgressors of the Law cried out at the third hour: what those did with their hands at the sixth hour, these did with their tongue at the third hour. More guilty are these who raged by crying out, than those who served by obeying. This is the whole sharpness of the Jews; this is what they sought as a great thing: Let us kill, and not kill; let us so kill, that we ourselves be not judged to have killed. *They have sharpened their tongues like a sword.*

**6.** [v. 5] *They bent the bow, a bitter thing.*[^12] By the bow he means snares. For he who fights with the sword at close quarters, fights openly: he who shoots an arrow, deceives in order to strike. For the arrow strikes before it is foreseen to come to the wound. But from whom would the snares of the human heart lie hidden? surely not from our Lord Jesus Christ, *who had no need that anyone should bear him witness about man? For he himself knew what was in man,* as the evangelist testifies. Yet let us hear them, and gaze upon them as though doing what they contrive without the Lord knowing it. *They bent the bow, a bitter thing, that they might shoot in secret the immaculate one.* What he said: *They bent the bow;* this is, *in secret;* as if deceiving by snares. For you know with what wiles they did this; how they corrupted with money the disciple cleaving to him, that he might be betrayed to them; how they procured false witnesses; with what snares and wiles they acted, *that they might shoot in secret the immaculate one.* A great iniquity! Behold, from a secret place comes the arrow which strikes the immaculate one, who had not so much of stain as can be pricked by an arrow. For he is a Lamb immaculate, wholly immaculate, always immaculate; not one whose stains have been taken away, but one who has contracted no stains. For he made many immaculate by forgiving sins; he himself immaculate by having no sins. *That they might shoot in secret the immaculate one.*

**7.** [v. 6] *Suddenly they will shoot him, and will not fear.*[^13] O hard heart, to wish to kill a man who raised the dead! *Suddenly*, that is, by snares, as if unexpectedly, as if unforeseen. For the Lord was like one not knowing among those who did not know what he did not know, and what he knew; rather, not knowing that he was ignorant of nothing, and knew all things, and had come for this, that they might do to him what they supposed they did by their own power. *Suddenly they will shoot him, and will not fear.*

**8.** *They have made firm for themselves a malign word.*[^14] *They have made firm*: so great miracles were done; they were not moved, they persisted in the counsel of the malign word. He was handed over to the judge: the judge trembles, and those who handed him over to the judge do not tremble; the power shudders, and the savagery does not shudder; he wishes to wash his hands, and they defile their tongues. But why this? *They made firm for themselves a malign word.* How great things did Pilate do? how great that they might be restrained? what did he say? what did he do? But *they made firm for themselves a malign word: Crucify, crucify!* The repetition is the confirmation of a malign word. Let us see how *they made firm for themselves a malign word. Shall I crucify your king? They said: We have no king but Caesar alone*[^15]. *They made firm the malign word.* He offered the king, the Son of God; they ran back to a man: worthy that they should have him, and not have him. Hear still how *they made firm the malign word. I find nothing in this man*, says the judge, *for which he is worthy of death*[^16]. And those who *made firm the malign word*, said: *His blood be upon us, and upon our children*[^17]! *They made firm for themselves a malign word. They made firm a malign word,* not for the Lord, but *for themselves.* For how not for themselves, when they say: *Upon us, and upon our children*? What they made firm, then, they made firm for themselves: because that very voice is elsewhere: *They dug a pit before my face, and fell into it*[^18]. Death did not kill the Lord, but he killed death: but iniquity killed them, because they were unwilling to kill iniquity.

**9.** Plainly, brethren, it is certain; either you kill iniquity, or you are killed by iniquity. But do not seek to kill iniquity as though it were something outside you. Look to yourself, see what fights with you in you; and beware lest your iniquity, your enemy, conquer you, if it is not slain: for it is from you, and your soul rebels against you, not anything else. From a certain part you cleave to God; from a certain part you are delighted with the age: that by which you are delighted with the age fights against the mind which cleaves to God. Let it cleave, let it cleave, let it not fail, let it not let itself go; it has a great help. It conquers that which rebels against it in it, if it perseveres in fighting. There is sin in your body, but let it not reign. *Let not*, then, *sin reign*, he says, *in your mortal body, to obey its desires*[^19]. But if you do not obey; even if there is that which urges, which delights toward evil; by not obeying you bring it about that what is does not reign, and so it will come about afterward that what was will not be. When? When death is swallowed up in victory, when this corruptible has put on incorruption: there will be nothing to fight against you, there will be nothing else to delight you but God. Therefore these Jews too envied the Lord; the principality delighted them. Some saw the principality taken from them by him, and out of their delight they rebelled against the Lord: who, if they had rebelled against their evil delight, would conquer that very envy, and not be conquered by it, and the Lord would be to them a savior, who had come to heal. But now they favored the fever, they fought against the physician; whatever the fever suggested, they did; whatever the physician bade against it, they neglected. Therefore they rather were killed, not the Lord: for in the Lord death was killed, in them iniquity lived; and, iniquity living in them, they were dead.

**10.** *They told that they might hide traps; they said: Who will see them?*[^20] They thought he was hidden from him whom they were killing, hidden from God. Behold, suppose Christ was a man, like the rest of men; he did not know what was being thought about him: surely God too does not know? O human heart, why did you say to yourself: Who sees me, when he sees who made you? *They said: Who will see them?* God saw, Christ too saw; because Christ too is God. But why did he seem to them not to see? Hear what follows.

**11.** [v. 7] *They have searched out iniquity; they failed, searching out searchings*[^21]: that is, bitter and sharp counsels. Let him not be betrayed by us, but by his disciple: let him not be killed by us, but by the judge: let us do the whole, and seem to have done nothing. And where is the cry of the tongue: *Crucify, crucify*? You are so blind that you are also deaf. Feigned innocence is not innocence: feigned equity is not equity, but a double iniquity; because it is both iniquity, and pretense. There, then, those men *failed, searching out searchings.* The more sharply they seemed to themselves to devise, the more they failed; because from the light of truth and equity they were sunk into the depths of malign counsels. Righteousness has a certain light of its own; it pours over, and illuminates the soul that cleaves to it: but the soul, turning itself away from the light of righteousness, the more it seeks what it may find against righteousness, the more it is repelled from the light, and sunk in dark things. Deservedly, then, these men too, searching out what they were contriving against the just one, withdrew from righteousness; and the more they withdrew from righteousness, the more they failed, searching out searchings. Great counsel of innocence: when Judas himself repented that he had betrayed Christ, and threw back to them the money they had given, they were unwilling to put it into the treasury, and said: *It is the price of blood; let us not put it into the treasury*[^22]. What is the *treasury*? The ark of God, where were gathered the things that were sent for the need of the servants of God. O man, let your heart rather be the ark of God, where the riches of God may dwell, where there may be the coin of God, your mind having the image of your Emperor! Since, then, these things are so, what kind of pretense of innocence was that, not to put the price of blood into the ark, and to put the blood itself into the conscience?

**12.** But what befell them? *They failed, searching out searchings.* Whence? Because he says: *Who will see them?* that is, because no one saw them. This they said, this they supposed within themselves, that no one saw them. See what befalls an evil soul; it withdraws from the light of truth, and because it itself does not see God, it thinks it is not seen by God. So these men too, by withdrawing, went into darkness, so that they themselves did not see God; and they said: Who sees us? He too saw whom they were crucifying; they, by failing, saw neither him the Son, nor the Father. If, then, he too saw, why did he allow himself to be held by them, to be killed by them? Why, if he saw, did he wish their counsels to prevail against him? Why? Because he was man for the sake of man, and God hidden in the man, who had come to give to those who did not know an example of strength; therefore he himself, knowing all things, endured.

**13.** [v. 8] For what follows? *Man will draw near, and a deep heart; and God will be exalted.*[^23] They said: Who will see us? *They failed, searching out searchings,* evil counsels. Man drew near to those very counsels, he allowed himself to be held as a man. For he would not be held, except as man; nor seen, except as man; nor beaten, except as man; nor crucified or die, except as man. Man drew near, then, to all those sufferings, which would avail nothing in him, unless he were man. But if he were not man, man would not be freed. Man drew near, *and a deep heart,* that is, a secret heart: setting man before human gazes, keeping God within; concealing the form of God, in which he is equal to the Father, and offering the form of a servant, in which he is less than the Father. For he himself said both: but one is from the form of God, the other from the form of a servant. He said from the form of God: *I and the Father are one*[^24]. He said from the form of a servant: *Because the Father is greater than I*[^25]. Whence from the form of God: *I and the Father are one*? Because *being established in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God.* Whence from the form of a servant: *Because the Father is greater than I*? Because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^26]. Man drew near, then, and a deep heart; and God was exalted. The man is killed, and God is exalted. For that he was killed, was from human weakness: that he rose again and ascended, was from divine power. *Man will draw near, and a deep heart,* a secret heart, a hidden heart; not showing what he knew, not showing what he was. They, thinking that this was all that was seen, kill the man in the deep heart, and God is exalted in the divine heart: for by the power of his majesty he was exalted. And whither did he go exalted? Whence he did not withdraw humbled.

**14.** *Man will draw near, and a deep heart; and God will be exalted.*[^27] Therefore now attend, my brethren, to the deep heart of the man. Of what man? *Mother Zion, a man will say; and a man was made in it, and he himself, the Most High, founded it*[^28]. In that very city a man was made, which the same Most High founded, who in it was made a man. Therefore man drew near, and a deep heart. Behold the man in the deep heart: see, as far as you can, if you can, God too in the deep heart. Man drew near: and because he was God, and because he was going to suffer willingly, and because he was going to furnish an example to the weak, and because those who raged were going to do nothing to him, as to God who was to suffer, but in man, but in flesh; what follows? *Their wounds have become the arrows of children.* Where is that savagery? where is that roar of the lion, of the people roaring and saying: *Crucify, crucify*? where are the snares of those bending the bow? Have not *their wounds become the arrows of children*? You know how children make themselves arrows out of reeds. What do they strike, or whence do they strike? what hand, or what dart? what weapons, or what limbs? *Their wounds have become the arrows of children.*

**15.** [v. 9] *And their tongues have been weakened against them.*[^29] Let them now sharpen their tongues like a sword, let them make firm for themselves a malign word. Deservedly they made it firm for themselves; because *their tongues have been weakened against them.* Surely this could not be firm against God? *Iniquity*, he says, *has lied to itself*[^30]: *their tongues have been weakened against them.* Behold, the Lord who had been killed has risen again. They passed before the cross, or stood, and gazed at him, as the psalm so long before had foretold: *They dug my hands and feet; they numbered all my bones. But they themselves considered and looked upon me*[^31]. Then they wagged their heads, saying: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross.* They tried, in a manner, whether he was the Son of God, and as it were found that he was not, because, as they insulted, he did not come down from the cross: if he came down from the cross, he would be the Son of God. What seems to you of one who did not come down from the cross, but rose from the tomb? What, then, did they accomplish? Even if the Lord had not risen again, what would they have accomplished, except what the persecutors of the martyrs also accomplished? For the martyrs too have not yet risen, and yet those men did not accomplish anything: we now celebrate the birthdays of those not yet risen. Where is the fury of the raging? *Their wounds have become the arrows of children, and their tongues have been weakened against them.* Whither did they bring those searchings of theirs, by searching out which they failed, so that even with the Lord dead and buried, they set guards at the tomb? For they said to Pilate: *That seducer;* by this name was the Lord Jesus Christ called, for the consolation of his servants when they are called seducers: so those men to Pilate: *That seducer*, they say, *said, while still alive: After three days I will rise. So order the tomb to be guarded until the third day; lest perhaps his disciples come, and steal him, and say to the people, He rose from the dead; and the last error will be worse than the first. Pilate said to them: You have a guard; go, guard it as you know how. But they, going away, secured the tomb, sealing the stone with the guards*[^32]. They set soldiers as guards at the tomb. The earth being shaken, the Lord rose again: such miracles were done about the tomb, that even the soldiers themselves who had come as guards became witnesses, if they were willing to announce true things; but that avarice which captivated the disciple, the companion of Christ, captivated also the soldier, the guard of the tomb. *We give you*, they say, *money; and say that, while you were sleeping, his disciples came, and took him away*[^33]. Truly *they failed, searching out searchings.* What is it you said, O wretched cunning? Do you so forsake the light of godly counsel, and sink into the depths of craft, as to say this: Say that, while you were sleeping, his disciples came, and took him away? You bring forward sleeping witnesses: truly you yourself have fallen asleep, who by searching out such things failed. If they were sleeping, what could they have seen? if they saw nothing, how are they witnesses? But *they failed, searching out searchings*: they failed from the light of God, they failed in the very effect of their counsels: when they could accomplish nothing of what they willed, they certainly failed: Why this? Because man drew near, and a deep heart; and God was exalted. Therefore afterward, when the resurrection of Christ had become known, and the Holy Spirit coming filled certain fearing disciples with confidence, so that they dared, no longer fearing death, to preach what they had seen, God being exalted in his majesty, who on account of our weakness was judged humble; and when the heavenly trumpets began to sound that the judge would come, whom first they had seen judged: *All who saw them were troubled.* God being exalted, as I said, Christ being preached, the Jews were seen by certain Jews, were seen failing in their searchings. For those men saw, in the name of one crucified, and killed by their own hands, so great miracles done: they withdrew in heart from those who remained in impiety; their hardness displeased them; they sought counsel of their salvation, and said to the Apostles: *What shall we do? All*, then, *who saw them were troubled*[^34]: that is, those who understood that their tongues had been weakened against them, who understood that in all their evil searchings of counsels they had everywhere failed. These men were troubled.

**16.** [v. 10] *And every man feared.*[^35] Those who did not fear, were not even men. *Every man feared*: that is, everyone using reason to understand the things that had been done. Whence those who did not fear are rather to be named cattle, rather huge and savage beasts. A lion seizing and roaring, that people still was. But every man feared: that is, those who were willing to believe, who trembled at the coming judgment. *And every man feared: and they announced the works of God.* He who said: *From the fear of the enemy rescue my soul, every man feared.* He was being rescued from the fear of the enemy, but was being subjected to the fear of God. He did not fear those who kill the body, but him who has power to send both body and soul into gehenna. They preached the Lord: for first Peter feared, and feared the enemy; his soul was not yet rescued from the enemy. Questioned by a maidservant whether he was among his disciples, he denied the Lord three times. The Lord rose again, made firm the column: now Peter preaches without fear, and with fear; without fear of those who kill the body, with fear of him who has power to kill both body and soul in the gehenna of fire. *Every man feared: and they announced the works of God.* For these Apostles, announcing the works of God, the chief priests produced them, and threatened them, saying *that they should not preach in the name of Jesus. And they said: Tell us whom we ought rather to obey, God, or men*[^36]? What would they say? Men rather than God? Without doubt they would answer only: God rather. But those men knew what God commanded, and despised the threatening priests. Since, then, *every man feared,* man did not terrify: *and they announced the works of God.* If man fears, let not man terrify: for man ought to fear him by whom man was made. Fear what is above men, and men will not terrify you. Fear everlasting death, and you will not care for the present. Desire that incorrupt pleasure, and rest without blemish; and you will deride one who promises these temporal gifts, and the whole world. Love, then, and fear: love what God promises, fear what God threatens; and you will neither be corrupted by what he promises, nor terrified by what man threatens. *And every man feared: and they announced the works of God, and understood his deeds.* What is, *understood his deeds*? This was that, O Lord Jesus Christ, which you kept silent about, and like a sheep you were led to the victim, and did not open your mouth before the shearer, and we thought you were set in plague and grief, and one knowing how to bear weakness? Was this that, that you hid your form, beautiful in form before the sons of men? Was this that, that you seemed to have no form nor comeliness? You bore on the cross those insulting and saying: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross.* Who perhaps, knowing your power, your servant and beloved, did not cry out in his heart, and say: O if only he would now come down, and all those who insult would be confounded? But it was not so: he had to die for the sake of those who would die, and to rise for the sake of those who would live forever. This those who wished him to come down from the cross did not understand; but when he rose again, and, glorified, ascended into heaven, they understood the works of the Lord. *They announced the works of God, and understood his deeds.*

**17.** [v. 11] *The just one will rejoice in the Lord.*[^37] Now the just one is not sad. For the disciples were sad when the Lord was crucified; saddened, mourning, they withdrew; they thought they had lost hope. He rose again; even appearing, he found them sad. He held the eyes of two walking on the way, lest he be recognized by them, and found them groaning and sighing; and he held them until he expounded the Scriptures, and through the Scriptures themselves showed that it had to be done as it was done. For he showed in the Scriptures that after the third day the Lord had to rise again. And how would he rise on the third day, if he came down from the cross? Now you who are sad on the way, if you saw the Lord, as the Jews insulted, come down from the cross, how greatly would you be lifted up? You would rejoice that he had so stopped up the mouths of the Jews. Await the counsel of the physician: that he does not come down, that he wishes to be killed, he grinds the antidote. Behold, now he has risen, behold, now he speaks: he is not yet recognized, that he may be more gladly recognized. Afterward he opened their eyes in the breaking of bread: they recognize him, they rejoice, they cry out. *The just one will rejoice in the Lord.* It is announced to a certain harder one: The Lord has been seen, the Lord has risen: he is still sad, he does not believe. *Unless I put*, he says, *my hand, and touch the scars of the nails, I will not believe.* And his body is offered to be touched, he puts his hand, handles it, cries out. *My Lord and my God! The just one will rejoice in the Lord.* Those just ones, then, rejoiced in the Lord, who saw, touched, and believed: what of the just ones who are now, because they do not see, because they do not touch, do they not rejoice in the Lord? And where is that voice of the Lord to Thomas himself: *Because you have seen me, you have believed: blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed*[^38]? Therefore let us all rejoice in the Lord; let us all according to faith be one just one, and all in one body have one head, and rejoice in the Lord, not in ourselves; because our good is not we ourselves to ourselves, but he who made us. He himself is our good to make us glad. And let no one rejoice in himself, let no one presume, let no one despair of himself, let no one of any man, whom he ought to lead so as to be a companion of his hope, not the giver of hope.

**18.** *The just one will rejoice in the Lord, and will hope in him; and all who are upright of heart will be praised.*[^39] Now because the Lord has risen, now because he has ascended into heaven, now because he has shown that there is another life, now because it is manifest that his counsels by which he lay hidden in a deep heart were not empty, because for this reason that blood was poured out, that there might be a price of the redeemed; now because all things are manifest, because all things have been preached, because all things have been believed, under the whole heaven *the just one will rejoice in the Lord, and will hope in him; and all who are upright of heart will be praised.* Who are upright of heart? Come now, my brethren, we always say it, and it is good that you know it. Who are upright of heart? Those who, whatever they suffer in this life, do not attribute it to folly, but to the counsel of God for their healing; nor presume of their own righteousness, so as to think that they suffer unjustly what they suffer, or that God is therefore unjust, because one who sins more does not suffer more. See, brethren; these things we often say. You feel something, whether according to the body, or according to loss of family property, or through some bereavement of those dearest to you: do not look at those whom you know to be more wicked than you (for perhaps you do not dare to call yourself just, but you know those worse than you), and to flourish in these things, and not be scourged; so that the counsel of God displease you, and you say: Behold, suppose I am a sinner, and therefore I am scourged; why is that man not scourged, whose great crimes I know? However much evil I have done, surely I have not done as much as he? Your heart is distorted. *How good is God to Israel; but to the upright of heart!* But your feet slip, because you are zealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners. Let him be; he knows what he does, who knows the wound. Another is not cut. What, if he is despaired of? what if you are cut for this reason, because you are not despaired of? Endure, then, whatever you endure with an upright heart: God knows what to give you, what to withdraw from you. What he gives you, let it avail for consolation, not for corruption; and what he withdraws from you, let it avail for endurance, not for blasphemy. But if you blaspheme, and God displeases you, and you please yourself; you are of a perverse and distorted heart: and this is worse, because you wish to correct the heart of God to your heart, so that he does what you wish; when you ought to do what he wishes. What then? Do you wish to twist the heart of God, which is always upright, to the crookedness of your heart? How much better do you correct your heart to the uprightness of God? Did not your Lord teach you this, of whose suffering we were just now speaking? Did he not bear your weakness, when he said: *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^40]? Did he not figure you yourself in himself, when he said: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me*[^41]? For there are not two hearts and diverse ones, of the Father, and of the Son; but in the form of a servant he bore your heart, that he might teach it by his example. Behold now, as it were another, your heart finds tribulation, wishing that what threatened might pass: but God was unwilling. God does not consent to your heart; do you consent to the heart of God. Hear his voice: *But not what I will, but what you will*[^42], Father.

**19.** *All*, then, *the upright of heart will be praised.* What follows? If *all the upright of heart will be praised,* the perverse of heart will be condemned. Two things are now set before you; choose while there is time. If you are of an upright heart, you will be at the right hand, and will be praised. How? *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^43]. But if you are of a perverse heart; if you mock God, if you deride his providence, if you say in your mind: Truly God does not care for human affairs: if he cared for human affairs, would that robber have so much, and I, innocent, be in want? you are made of a perverse heart. That judgment will come; all things will appear on account of which God does all these things; and you who were unwilling in this life to correct your heart to the uprightness of God, and to prepare yourself for the right hand, where *all the upright of heart will be praised,* will be at the left hand, where you will then hear: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^44]. And surely there will not then be time to correct the heart? Now, then, correct it, brethren; now correct it. Who prohibits it? A Psalm is sung, the Gospel is read, the reader sounds, the discusser sounds: the Lord is patient; you sin, and he spares; you still sin, he still spares, and you still add. How long is God patient? You will feel God just too. We terrify, because we fear; teach us not to fear, and we will not terrify. But God teaches us to fear better than any man teaches not to fear. For *every man feared: and they announced the works of God.* Among those let God reckon us, who feared, and announced. Because we fear, we announce to you, brethren. We see your alertness for hearing the word, and for demanding it we see your vows, we see your affections. The earth is well rained upon; let it bring forth grain, not thorns: for grain a barn is prepared; fire for thorns. You know what to do with your field; and does God not know what to do with his servant? What rains upon a fruitful field is sweet; and what rains upon a thorny field is sweet. Surely one who has brought forth thorns does not accuse the rain? Will not that rain be a witness in the judgment of God; and will it say: I came sweet upon all? Do you, then, see what you bring forth, that you may attend to what is prepared for you. You bring forth grain; hope for the barn: you bring forth thorns; hope for fire. But the time of the barn or the time of fire has not yet come: now, then, let it be prepared, and it will not be feared. In the name of Christ we who speak both live, and you to whom we speak live: surely there is a place, there is time, for the counsel of correcting and of changing an evil life into a good one? Surely if you wish, it is done today? surely if you wish, it is done now? What are you going to buy to do it? what perfumer's shop are you going to seek? to what Indians are you going to sail? what ship are you going to prepare? Behold, while I speak, change the heart; and that is done which is so often and so long cried out to be done, and which gives birth to eternal punishment, if it is not done.
