# Exposition of Psalm 39

**1.** All the things which our Lord Jesus Christ foretold, partly we know to have been done, partly we hope will be: yet all will be fulfilled, because the truth says these things, and as faithfully as it says them, so faithful ones it requires. He who believes, will rejoice when they come: he who does not believe, will be confounded when they come. Yet they will come, whether men are willing or unwilling, whether believing or not believing, as the Apostle says, *If we deny him, he too will deny us: if we do not believe, he remains faithful, he cannot deny himself*[^1]. Yet before all things, brothers, remember this brief thing, and hold what we just now all heard from the Gospel. *He who perseveres to the end, he shall be saved*[^2]. Now our fathers were delivered up to councils, they pleaded their causes before the enemies whom they loved; they showed them both as much correction as they could, and as much love as they were able: and the just blood was shed, and by that blood, as by a sowing made throughout the whole world, the harvest of the Church arose. But the time following is one of scandals and dissimulation and temptations, through those who say, *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there*[^3]. That enemy of ours was then a lion, when he openly raged: now he is a dragon, when he secretly lies in wait. But he to whom it was said, *You shall trample the lion and the dragon*[^4], since we are his body and his members, as he trampled the lion openly raging by the feet of our fathers, and dragging the martyrs to passions, so now let him trample the dragon, lest he lie in wait for us. Of which dragon, making us cautious, the Apostle says, *I have prepared you to present a chaste virgin to one husband, Christ: but I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so too your minds be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ Jesus*[^5]. This serpent, then, the ancient adulterer, seeks the virginity to be corrupted, not of the flesh, but of the heart. But just as an adulterous man rejoices in his wickedness when he corrupts the flesh; so too the devil rejoices when he corrupts the mind. And as our fathers had need of patience against the lion, so we have need of vigilance against the dragon. Yet persecution, whether from the lion or from the dragon, never ceases for the Church: and he is more to be feared when he deceives, than when he rages. At that time he forced Christians to deny Christ; but at this time he teaches Christians to deny Christ: then he forced, now he teaches. Then, therefore, he brought in violences, now treacheries: then he seemed to roar, now slippery and wandering he is seen with difficulty. But how then he forced Christians to deny Christ, is open. For they were dragged to deny Christ, and confessing Christ they were crowned. But now he teaches them to deny Christ; and therefore he deceives, because he who is taught to deny Christ, seems to himself, as it were, not to withdraw from Christ. For now what is said by heretical men to a catholic Christian? Come, be a Christian. Is "be" said for this, that he may say: I am not? It is one thing, far different: Come, be a Christian; another: Come, deny Christ. An open evil, the roar of a lion, is heard from afar, guarded against from afar. The slippery dragon applies himself, the serpent creeping with hidden slidings, stealing up with a gentle trail, murmuring with a cunning hiss, and does not say: Deny Christ. For who would hear him, when the martyrs are crowned? But he says: Be a Christian. And that man, struck by the wonderful voice, if he is not yet penetrated by the poison, answers: I am plainly a Christian. But if he is moved, and is caught by the tooth of the dragon, he answers: Why do you say to me: Be a Christian? what then, am I not a Christian? And he: No. Am I then not? No. Then now make me a Christian, if I am not. Come. But when you begin to be questioned by the bishop what you are, do not say, I am a Christian, or I am a believer, but say that you are not, that you may be able to be. For when he has heard the confession of a faithful Christian, he does not dare to rebaptize: but when he has heard that he is not, he gives him, as it were, what he did not have; so that he himself may be, as it were, outside fault, because he acts according to his voice. Here I ask of you, heretic, why you think yourself outside fault. What do I hear by this voice? that you do not deny, but he denies? If he who denies has fault, what of him who teaches the one who denies to deny? Are you then truly outside fault, because you make by teaching a Christian what the pagan made by threatening? And what do you do? Do you take away what he has, because he denied what he has? You do not make him not have it, but have it to his punishment. For what he has, he has. That baptism is, as it were, a character imprinted: it adorned the soldier, it convicts the deserter. For what do you do? You set Christ upon Christ. If you were simple, you would not double Christ. Then, I ask you, have you forgotten that Christ is a stone, and *The stone which the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner*[^6]? If, then, Christ is a stone, and you wish to set Christ upon Christ, has it slipped from you what you heard in the Gospel, that stone shall not stand upon stone[^7]? But so great is the value of the joining of love, that although many living stones come together in the structure of God's temple, one stone is made of all. But you have cut yourself off; you call back from building, you call to ruin; and these treacheries abound, and do not rest: and we see, and we endure, and as far as we can we try to repress, by disputing, convicting, conferring, terrifying, yet in all things loving. And when, as we do these things, they persevere in evil, and our heart pines away over the deaths of brothers, when it grieves over those who are outside, fears for those who are within, through manifold straits and unceasing temptations, with which this life abounds, what shall we do? For from the abundance of iniquity comes a certain torpor of love: *because iniquity has abounded, the love of many grows cold*[^8]. And what shall we do, except what follows, if at least with his help we can: *He who perseveres to the end, he shall be saved*[^9]?

**2.** [vv. 2-3] Therefore let us say what this psalm says: *Waiting I waited for the Lord. Waiting I waited,*[^10] not any promising man, who can deceive and be deceived; not any consoling man, who can pine away with his own sadness before he refreshes me. Let a brother man console me when he is sad with me; together let us groan, together let us weep, together let us pray, together let us wait; whom, except *the Lord* who does not take away his promises, but defers them? He will surely fulfill, he will fulfill; because he has already fulfilled many things: and we ought to fear nothing about the truth of God, even if he had as yet fulfilled nothing. Behold, let us suppose it thus, that he has promised all things, and not yet given anything: he is a suitable promiser, a faithful repayer; only be you a godly demander, even if little, even if weak, demand mercy. Do you not see tender lambs strike the udders of their mothers with their heads, that they may be satisfied with milk? *Waiting,* he says, *I waited for the Lord.* And what did he? Did he turn away from you, or despise you waiting, or perhaps not see you? Plainly not so. But what? *And he attended to me, and heard my supplication.* He attended, and heard. Behold, not in vain did you wait: his eyes are upon you, his ears toward you. *For the eyes of the Lord are upon the just, and his ears unto their prayers*[^11]. What then, when you did evil, when you blasphemed him, did he not see? did he not hear? And where is that which is said in that very psalm: *But the countenance of the Lord is upon those who do evil*? But for what? *To cut off their memory from the earth*[^12]. Therefore even when you were evil, he attended to you, but he did not attend for you. Accordingly this man, who waiting waited for the Lord, it was too little for him to say, He attended to me: *He attended to me,* he says, that is, by consoling he attended, that he might profit me. What did he attend? *And he heard my supplication.*

**3.** [v. 4] And what did he grant you? what did he do for you? *And he led me out of the lake of misery, and from the mud of the slime: and he set my feet upon a rock, and directed my steps. And he put into my mouth a new song, a hymn to our God*[^13]. He has granted great goods, and is still a debtor: but he who now holds these things repaid, let him believe concerning the rest, who ought to have believed even before he received anything. By the very deeds our Lord has persuaded us that he is a faithful promiser, a generous giver. What, then, has he now done? *He led me out of the lake of misery.* What is the lake of misery? The depth of iniquity, from carnal lusts. For this is, *and from the mud of the slime.* Whence did he lead you out? From a certain depth. Whence in another psalm you cried, *Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord*[^14]. And those who now cry from the depth, are not utterly in the depth: the very cry now lifts them. There are others more deeply in the depth, who do not even perceive that they are in the depth. Such are the proud despisers, not godly supplicants, not tearful criers: but such as Scripture designates in another place: *The sinner, when he has come into the depth of evils, despises*[^15]. For he to whom it is too little to be a sinner, unless he be also not a confessor of his sins, but a defender, is more deeply in the depth. But that man who cried out from the depth, has now lifted his head from the bottom of the depth to cry: he was heard, he was led out of the lake of misery, and from the mud of the slime. Now he has faith, which he did not have; he has hope, without which he was; he walks in Christ, who erred in the devil. For therefore, *He set,* he says, *my feet upon a rock, and directed my steps.* And the rock was Christ[^16]. Let us be upon the rock, let our steps be directed: yet it is still necessary that we walk, that we may come to something. For the apostle Paul, now upon the rock, his feet now directed, what did he say? *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect: brothers, I do not reckon myself to have apprehended*[^17]. What, then, has been granted you, if you have not apprehended? Whence do you give thanks, when you say, *But I obtained mercy*[^18]? Because his feet have been directed, because he now walks upon the rock. For what does he say? *But one thing, forgetting the things that are behind.* What is behind? The lake of misery. What is behind? The mud of the slime, carnal lusts, the darkness of iniquities. *Forgetting the things that are behind, stretched out toward the things that are before.* He would not call himself stretched out, if he had already arrived. For the mind is stretched out by the desire of the thing coveted, not by the gladness of the thing obtained. *Toward the things that are before,* he says, *stretched out, I follow toward the prize of the supernal calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^19]. He was running, he was following toward the prize. And in another place, now near the prize: *I have finished the course*[^20], he says. When, therefore, he said, *I follow toward the prize of the supernal calling,* because his feet were now directed upon the rock, he was now walking the good way: he had whence to give thanks, he had what to ask, giving thanks for things received, asking for things owed. Of what things received? Of the pardon of sins, of the illumination of faith, of the strength of hope, of the flame of love. But whence did he still hold the Lord a debtor? *For the rest,* he says, *there remains for me a crown of justice.* Still, then, something is owed me. What is owed? *A crown of justice, which the Lord will repay me in that day, the just judge*[^21]. First a kind father, that he might lead out of the lake of misery, that he might forgive sins, that he might free from the mud of the slime: afterward a just judge, repaying to the one who walks well what he promised, to whom he first granted that he might walk well. The just judge, then, will repay: but to whom? *He who perseveres to the end, he shall be saved*[^22].

**4.** [v. 4] *He put into my mouth a new song*[^23]. What new song? *A hymn to our God.* Perhaps you used to say hymns to strange gods, old hymns; because the old man used to say them: let the new man be made; let the new man say a new song: renewed, let him love new things by which he is renewed. For what is more ancient than God, who is before all things, and without end, and without beginning? He becomes new to you when you return; because by departing you had become old, and you had said, *I have grown old among all my enemies*[^24]. We say, then, a hymn to our God, and the hymn itself frees us. *For praising I will call upon the Lord, and I shall be saved from my enemies*[^25]. For a hymn is a song of praise. Call upon him praising, not reproaching. For when you call upon God to press your enemy, when you wish to rejoice over another's evil, and for this evil you call upon God, you make him a partner of your malice. If you make him a partner of malice, then you do not call upon him praising, but reproaching. For you think God such as you are. Whence in another place it is said to you: *These things you did, and I kept silence; you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you*[^26]. Praising, then, call upon the Lord: do not think him like you, that you may become like him. *For be perfect as your Father, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^27]. So, then, praise the Lord, that you wish no evil to your enemies. And how much good, you say, would I wish them? As much as to yourself. For they are not going to receive it from yours, that they may be good, nor will what is given them be diminished from you. Your enemy, because he is evil, is an enemy: he becomes good, and will be a friend, and will be a companion; so that you may together wish to possess what you loved, he will now be a brother. Praising, then, call upon him, say a hymn to your God. *The sacrifice of praise,* he says, *shall glorify me*[^28]. And what? will there be greater glory to God, because you glorify him? or do we add glory to God, when we say to him, I glorify you, my God? or do we make him holier, when we say, I bless you, my God? He, when he blesses us, makes us holier, makes us happier; when he glorifies us, makes us more glorious, makes us more honored: when we glorify him, it profits us, not him. For how do we glorify him? By calling him glorious, not by making him so. Accordingly, what did he say following, when he said, *The sacrifice of praise shall glorify me*? Lest you should suppose you grant something to God by offering him the sacrifice of praise: *And there is the way,* he says, *in which I will show him the salvation of God*[^29]. You see that it will profit you to praise God, not God. Do you praise God? You walk the way. Do you reproach God? You have lost the way.

**5.** *And he put into my mouth a new song, a hymn to our God.* Perhaps someone asks what person speaks in this psalm. Briefly I would say, it is Christ. But as you know, brothers, and it must often be said, Christ sometimes speaks from himself, that is, from our head: for he himself is the Savior of the body, our head, the Son of God born of the Virgin, suffering for us, rising again to justify us, sitting at the right hand of God to intercede for us, about to render all things in the judgment, good things to the good, evil to the evil. He is our head, he deigned to become the head of the body, by taking flesh from us, in which he might die for us: which also he raised up for our sake, that in that flesh of the resurrection he might furnish us an example, that we might learn to hope for what we despaired of, and might now have feet upon the rock, might walk in Christ. He speaks, then, sometimes from our head, he speaks sometimes also from us, that is, from his members: because even when he said, *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^30], he was speaking from his members, not from himself. And when he said, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^31] the head was crying out for the members: and yet he did not say: Why do you persecute my members? but, *why do you persecute me?* If in us he himself suffers, in him we too shall be crowned. This is the love of Christ. What can be compared to it? Of this matter he put a hymn into our mouth, and says this from his members.

**6.** *The just shall see, and shall fear, and shall hope in the Lord. The just shall see*[^32]. What just? The faithful: because the just lives by faith[^33]. For in the Church this order exists: some go before, others follow: and those who go before furnish themselves as an example to those following; and those who follow imitate those going before. But do even those who furnish themselves as an example to those following follow no one? If they follow no one, they will err. Therefore they too follow someone, Christ himself. Whoever are the better in the Church, for whom no man remains whom they may imitate, because by advancing they have surpassed all, Christ himself remains for them, whom even to the end they may follow. And you discern the ordered degrees through the apostle Paul, saying, *Be imitators of me, as I too am of Christ*[^34]. Therefore let those who now have their steps directed on the rock be a pattern to the faithful: *But be,* he says, *a pattern to the faithful*[^35]. These faithful are the just, who, attending to those who go before them in good, by imitating follow. How do they follow? *The just shall see, and shall fear.* They will see, and will fear to follow evil ways, when they see certain better ones now choosing good ways; and they say in their mind, as travelers are wont to say, attending to others walking with confidence on the way, while they themselves are still uncertain of the way, and as it were fluctuating which way they should go, they say to themselves: Not in vain do these go this way, since they go where we wish to go; and why do they go this way with great confidence, except because to go that way is destructive? *The just,* then, *shall see, and shall fear.* They see a narrow way this way, they see a broad way that way: this way they see few, that way many. But if you are just, do not count, but weigh; bring an even balance, not a deceitful one, because you are called just: *The just shall see, and shall fear,* was said of you. Do not, then, count the crowds of men walking the broad ways, filling tomorrow's circus, celebrating by shouting the Birthday of the city, disgracing the city itself by living badly. Do not, then, attend to them; they are many, and who counts them? But few by the narrow way. Bring forth, I say, the balance, weigh: see against how few grains how much chaff you lift. Let the faithful just who follow do this. What of those who go before? Let them not be proud, let them not exalt themselves, let them not deceive those following. How can they deceive those following? By promising them salvation in themselves. What, then, ought those who follow to do? *The just shall see, and shall fear, and shall hope in the Lord*: not in those by whom they are preceded: but by attending to those going before them, they indeed follow and imitate, but because they consider from whom those received that they should go before, they hope in him. Therefore, although they imitate these, yet they place their hope in him, from whom these too received whence they are such. *The just shall see, and shall fear, and shall hope in the Lord*: just as in another psalm: *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains,* we understand the mountains, all the bright and great spiritual men of the Church, great in solidity, not in swelling. Through them all Scripture has been dispensed to us; they are prophets, they are evangelists, they are good teachers: thither *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help shall come to me.* And lest you should think the help human, it follows and says, *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^36]. *The just shall see, and shall fear, and shall hope in the Lord.*

**7.** [v. 5] Come now, you who wish to hope in the Lord, who see and fear, let them fear to walk evil ways, broad ways; let them choose the narrow way, where the steps of certain ones are now directed upon the rock: what they ought to do, let them now hear. *Blessed is the man whose hope is the name of the Lord, and who has not looked back to vanities and lying follies*[^37]. Behold, the way you wished to go, behold the crowd of the broad way: not in vain does it lead to the amphitheater, not in vain does it lead to death. The broad way is death-bearing: its breadth delights for a time, its end is narrow forever. But the crowds make a din, the crowds hasten, the crowds rejoice together, the crowds run together. Do not imitate, do not be turned aside; they are vanities, and lying follies. Let the Lord your God be your hope: do not hope for anything else from the Lord your God, but let your Lord himself be your hope. For many hope money from God, many hope from God passing and perishing honors, anything else from God except God himself: but you, seek your God himself: rather indeed, the other things being despised, go on to him; forgetting other things, remember him; leaving other things behind, stretch yourself toward him. He surely corrected you when turned away, he leads you straight, he leads you through: therefore let him himself be your hope, who leads and leads through. Whither does earthly greed lead and lead through? You sought farms, you desired to possess land, you shut out your neighbors; they being shut out, you gaped after other neighbors; and so long you extended your greed, until you came to the shores: coming to the shores, you covet the islands; the land possessed, you wish perhaps to seize heaven. Leave all loves: more beautiful is he who made heaven and earth.

**8.** *Blessed is the man whose hope is the name of the Lord, and who has not looked back to vanities and lying follies.* For whence lying follies? A lying folly, a truthful soundness. The things you see you think good, you are deceived: you are not sound, by too much fever you have been made frenzied; what you love is not true. You praise the charioteer, you shout for the charioteer, you are mad over the charioteer. It is vanity, it is lying folly. It is not so, he says: nothing better, nothing more delightful. What do I do for the fevered? If there is mercy in you, pray for such people. For the physician himself too, very often in desperation, turns to those who stand around weeping in the house, who hang upon his mouth to hear his judgment about the sick and imperiled one: the physician stands doubtful, he does not see good to promise, he fears to pronounce evil, lest he terrify; this one plainly conceives a modest judgment: God is good, can do all things, pray for him. Which, then, of these mad ones shall I lay hold of? Who will hear me? which of them will not call us wretched? Because we are not mad with them, they think we have lost the great and varied pleasures in which they themselves are mad: nor do they see that they are liars. When I give that one an egg even unbidden, or hold out the saving cup to the wounded; and how shall I refresh, when I find one? Lest by fasting he fail, and not reach health, I exhort that he refresh himself: he makes fists, he wishes to rage against the physician. And if he strike, let him be loved; and if he do injury, let him not be abandoned: he is to return to his mind, he is to give thanks. How many here recognize themselves, see one another in turn, and speak of themselves in the Church of God; in the bosom of holy Church they attend to their now good studies about the word of God, about the offices and services of love, about frequenting the flock of Christ not withdrawing from the Church, they see and speak to one another about one another. Who is this circus-fan? Who is this lover and praiser of that hunter, that actor? He speaks of another, and that one of himself. Surely these things are so, surely we rejoice over such people. If we rejoice over such people, let us not despair over such people. Let us pray for them, dearly beloved brothers: thence grows the number of the saints, from the number that was of the ungodly. *And he has not looked back to vanities and lying follies.* That man conquered, joined such a horse, he announces, he wishes to be as it were divine: he affects divinity by losing the fountain of divinity: and often he announces, and often he is deceived. Why this? Because they are lying follies. But why do the things they say sometimes come about? That they may lead away the mad, that, by loving there the appearance of truth, they may run into the snare of falsehood: let them be behind, let them be left, let them be cut off. If they were our members, let them be mortified: *Mortify,* he says, *your members which are upon the earth*[^38]. Let God be our hope. He who made all things, is better than all things; he who made beautiful things, is more beautiful than all; he who made strong things, is stronger; he who made great things, is greater: whatever you have loved, he will be to you. Learn to love in the creature the Creator, and in the work the maker; lest what was made by him hold you, and you lose him by whom you yourself too were made. Therefore: *Blessed is the man whose hope is the name of the Lord, and who has not looked back to vanities and lying follies.*

**9.** [v. 6] Perhaps he is about to say to us, whoever, struck by this verse, has wished to be corrected, and whom that fear of the justice of faith has occupied, and, willing, has begun to walk the narrow way; he is about to say to us: I will not endure to walk, if I shall watch nothing. What, then, do we do, brothers? Are we to dismiss him without a spectacle? He will die, he will not hold out, he will not follow us. What, then, do we do? Let us give spectacles for spectacles. And what spectacles are we to give to a Christian man, whom we wish to call back from those spectacles? I give thanks to our Lord God: in the following verse of the psalm he has shown us what spectacles we ought to furnish and show to spectators wishing to watch spectacles. Behold, let him be turned away from the circus, from the theater, from the amphitheater, let him seek what he may watch, let him plainly seek; we do not abandon him without a spectacle. What shall we give for those? Hear what follows: *You have done many things, O Lord my God, your wonders*[^39]. He was gazing at the miracles of men, let him attend to the wonders of God. The Lord has done many wonders of his; let him look at these. Why have those grown cheap to him? He praises a charioteer guiding four horses, and running without fault and offense: did the Lord perhaps not do such spiritual miracles? Let him guide luxury, let him guide cowardice, let him guide injustice, let him guide imprudence, those motions which, too much slipped, make these vices, let him guide them and subject them to himself, and hold the reins, and not be carried off; let him lead where he wishes, not be dragged where he does not wish. He praised the charioteer, the charioteer will be praised: he shouted that the charioteer be covered with a garment, he will be clothed with immortality. These gifts, these spectacles God puts on. He cries from heaven: I watch you; struggle, I will help; conquer, I will crown. *You have done many things, O Lord my God, your wonders; and in your thoughts there is none who is like you.* Now watch the actor. For a man learned with great effort to walk on a rope, and, hanging, he held you in suspense. Attend to that exhibitor of greater spectacles. This one learned to walk on a rope, did he make anyone walk on the sea? Forget your theater, attend to our Peter, not a rope-walker, but, so to speak, a sea-walker. Walk you too, not on those waters, where Peter, signifying something, walked, but on others; because this world is a sea. It has noxious bitterness, it has waves of tribulations, tempests of temptations; it has men like fish rejoicing over another's evil, and as it were devouring one another: walk here, trample this. You wish to watch; be a spectacle. Lest you fail, see the one going before and saying, *We have been made a spectacle to this world, and to angels, and to men*[^40]. Tread the sea, lest you be sunk in the sea. You will not go, you will not tread, unless he commands who first walked on the sea. For so Peter says: *If it is you, bid me come to you upon the waters.* And because it was he, he heard the one asking, granted to the one desiring, called the one walking, raised the one sinking[^41]. These wonders the Lord did, gaze at these; let the eye of the watcher be faith. And do you such things; because even if the winds disturb, even if the waves roar, and human fragility brings you to some doubt of your salvation, you have how to cry; you say, Lord, I perish. He does not allow to perish who bade you walk. Because you now walk upon the rock, you do not even fear the sea: if you are without the rock, you are sunk in the sea, because one must walk upon such a rock as was not sunk in the sea.

**10.** See the wonders of God. *I have announced, and I have spoken, they have been multiplied beyond number*[^42]. There is a number, there are those beyond number. The number is fixed, pertaining to that heavenly Jerusalem. For the Lord knows who are his, Christians fearing, Christians faithful, Christians keeping the precepts, walking the ways of God, abstaining from sins, confessing if they have fallen: these pertain to the number. But are they alone? There are also those beyond number. For although now they are few, few in comparison with the multitude of the greater throngs: with how great crowds the churches are filled, the walls are packed, men press one another with crushings, they almost suffocate one another by the multitude. Again, from these very ones, if there is a public show, there is running to the amphitheater: these are beyond number. But for this we say these things, that they may be in the number: because they are not present, they do not hear from us; but when you go out, let them hear from you. *I have announced,* he says, *and I have spoken.* Christ says it: he himself announced from our head, he himself announced from his members, he himself sent announcers, he himself sent apostles: into all the earth their sound went out, and to the ends of the world their words[^43]. How many of the faithful gather, how many crowds run together, many truly converted, many falsely converted; and fewer are the truly converted, more the falsely converted; because *they have been multiplied beyond number.*

**11.** [v. 7] *I have announced and I have spoken, they have been multiplied beyond number. Sacrifice and oblation you did not wish*[^44]. These are the wonders of God, these are the thoughts of God, to which no one is like: that that watcher may be led away from curiosity, and may seek with us these better, more fruitful things, over which, when found, he will rejoice; and so will rejoice, that he will not fear lest the one he loves be conquered: for he loves a charioteer, over whose defeat he bears insults. When the charioteer conquers, he himself is clothed. Is the poor man who shouts for him? The victor himself is clothed, for the conquered one he is insulted. Why do you take reproach for the one with whom you do not share a garment? Here something else in our spectacles. *All indeed run,* the apostle Paul said, in that stadium, in that spectacle, *yet one receives the prize*[^45]: the rest withdraw conquered. And they persevered in running: but when one has received it, the rest remain who likewise labored. Here it is not so. As many as run, let them run perseveringly, all receive: and he who comes first waits, that he may be crowned with the latter. For this contest love makes, not greed: all who run love one another, and that very love is the course.

**12.** *Sacrifice and oblation you did not wish,* says the psalm to God. For the ancients, when as yet the true sacrifice which the faithful know was foretold in figures, celebrated the figures of the thing to come: many knowing, but more being ignorant. For the prophets and holy patriarchs knew what they celebrated: but the rest, the unjust, carnal multitude, was such that there might be made of it what it signified should come afterward: and it came, that first sacrifice being taken away, the holocausts of rams, of goats, of calves, and the other victims being taken away; God did not wish those things. Why did he not wish those things? why did he first wish them? Because all those things were as it were words of one promising; and words of promise, when what they promise has come, are no longer uttered. So long is each one a promiser, until he gives: when he has given, he changes the words. He no longer says: I will give, what he said he would give; but he says: I have given: he has changed the word. Why did this word first please him, and why did he change it? Because it was the word of its own time, and for its own time it pleased. When it was being promised, then it was being said: but when what was promised has been given, the words of promise are taken away, the words of fulfillment are given. Those sacrifices, then, as words of promise, were taken away. What is it that has been given, the fulfillment? The body which you know, which not all of you know; which would that you who know it, all knew not unto judgment. See when it was said; for Christ is our Lord, now speaking from his members, now speaking from his own person. *Sacrifice,* he says, *and oblation you did not wish.* What then? Are we now in this time dismissed without a sacrifice? Far be it. *But a body you have perfected for me.* Therefore you did not wish those things, that you might perfect this: those you wished before you perfected this. The perfection of the things promised took away the promising words. For if they are still promising, what was promised is not yet fulfilled. This was being promised by certain signs: the promising signs are taken away, because the promised truth has been exhibited. In this body we are, we are partakers of this body, what we receive we know: and you who do not know it shall know it, and when you have learned, would that you not receive it unto judgment. For he who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself[^46]. A body has been perfected for us, let us be perfected in the body.

**13.** [v. 8] *Sacrifice and oblation you did not wish, but a body you have perfected for me. Holocausts also for sin you did not require: then I said: Behold, I come*[^47]. Must it be expounded: *Sacrifice and oblation you did not wish, but a body you have perfected for me? Holocausts also for sin you did not require*: which he required before. *Then I said: Behold, I come.* It is time that the things which were being promised should come, because the things through which they were promised are taken away. And truly, my brothers, attend to those things taken away, these fulfilled. Let the Jewish people now give me a priest. Where are their sacrifices? Surely they have perished, surely they are taken away now. Would we then reject them? We reject them now: because if you now wish to do them, it is out of time, it is not opportune, it does not fit. You still promise, I have already received. There remained for them something to celebrate, lest they remain altogether without a sign. For Cain, the elder brother, who killed the younger brother, received a sign, lest anyone kill him, as it is written in Genesis: *God set a sign upon Cain, that no one might kill him*[^48]. Accordingly that Jewish nation too remains. All nations subject to Roman law have flowed together into Roman law, they have shared their superstitions; afterward they began to be separated from there through the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ: but that nation has so remained with its sign, with the sign of circumcision, with the sign of unleavened bread it has so remained: Cain was not killed, was not killed, he has his sign. He was cursed by the earth, which opened its mouth to receive the blood of his brother from his hand. For he shed the blood, did not receive it: he shed it, another earth received it: and by that earth which opened its mouth and received it, he was cursed: and that very earth which received the blood with its mouth, is the Church. By this, then, he was cursed. And that blood cries from the earth to me. For of this earth the Lord said: *The voice of your brother's blood cries to me from the earth*[^49]. It cries, he says, to me from the earth. It cries to the Lord: but he who shed the blood is deaf, because he does not drink. They, then, are thus, like Cain with the sign. But the sacrifices which were made there, are taken away; and what remained to them for the sign of Cain, is now perfected, and they do not know it. They kill a lamb, they eat unleavened bread: *Our Passover, Christ, has been immolated*[^50]. Behold, I recognize the lamb slain, because Christ has been immolated. What of the unleavened bread? *And so,* he says, *let us celebrate the feast, not in the old leaven, nor in the leaven of malice and wickedness:* (he shows what is old, the old is meal, it sours), *but in the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth*[^51]. They have remained in the shadow, they cannot bear the sun of glory: we are now in the light, we hold the body of Christ, we hold the blood of Christ. If we have a new life, let us sing a new song, a hymn to our God. *Holocausts for sin you did not require: then I said: Behold, I come.*

**14.** [v. 9] *In the head of the book it is written of me, that I should do your will: my God, I willed it, and your law in the midst of my heart*[^52]. Behold, he looked back to the members, behold, he himself too did the will of the Father. But in what head of the book is it written of him? Perhaps in the head of this book of Psalms. For why should we seek far, or inquire into other books? Behold, in the head of this book of Psalms, it is written: *Blessed is the man who has not gone in the counsel of the ungodly, and in the way of sinners has not stood, and in the seat of pestilences has not sat, but in the law of the Lord was his will*: this is: *My God, I willed it, and your law in the midst of my heart*: this is, *and on his law he shall meditate day and night*[^53].

**15.** [v. 10] *I have well announced your justice in a great Church*[^54]. He addresses his members, he exhorts them to do what he did. He announced, let us announce; he suffered, let us suffer with him; he was glorified, we shall be glorified together with him. *I have announced your justice in a great Church.* How great? Throughout the whole world. How great? In all nations. Why in all nations? Because it is the seed of Abraham, in whom all nations shall be blessed. Why in all nations? Because into all the earth their sound went out. *In a great Church. Behold, my lips I will not restrain, O Lord, you have known.* My lips speak, I will not restrain them from speaking. My lips indeed sound to the ears of men, but you have known my heart. *My lips I will not restrain, O Lord, you have known.* One thing a man hears, another God recognizes. Lest the announcement should be in the lips alone, and it should be said of us, *What they say to you, do; but what they do, do not do*[^55]: or to that very people, praising God with the mouth, not with the heart, it should be said, *This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me*[^56]: sound with the lips, draw near with the heart. For with the heart one believes unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation[^57]. Such was that robber found, hanging on the cross with the Lord, recognizing on the cross the Lord. Others did not recognize him doing miracles, that man recognized him hanging on the wood. He was fixed in all his members; his hands clung to the nails, his feet were transfixed, his whole body was joined to the wood; that body was not free for the other members, the tongue was free and the heart: with the heart he believed, with the mouth he confessed. *Remember me,* he said, *Lord, when you come into your kingdom.* He hoped his salvation far in the future, and was content to receive it after a long time: he hoped for the long term, the day was not deferred. He said, *Remember me, when you come into your kingdom*: he answered, *Amen I say to you, today you shall be with me in paradise. Today,* he says, *you shall be with me in paradise*[^58]. Paradise has happy trees: today with me on the wood of the cross, today with me on the wood of salvation.

**16.** *Behold, my lips I will not restrain, O Lord, you have known*[^59]: let him not believe with the heart, and from fear restrain his lips from announcing what he believed. For there are Christians, they have faith in the heart, even if among bitter pagans, wretchedly urbane, sordid, unbelieving, inept, insulting, if they have begun to be harassed, because they are Christians; they have faith in the heart, and fear to confess with the lips, they restrain their lips from sounding what they know, from sounding what they have within. But the Lord reproves these: *He who is ashamed of me before men, I will be confounded about him before my Father*[^60], that is, I will not know him: because he was ashamed to confess me before men, I will not confess him before my Father. Let the lips, then, say what the heart has: this against fear. Let the heart have what the lips say: this against pretense. For sometimes there is fear, and you do not dare to say what you know, what you believe: sometimes there is pretense, you say it, and do not have it in the heart. Let your lips agree with your heart. Seeking peace from God, be at peace with yourself: let there not be between your mouth and your heart an evil quarrel. *Behold, my lips I will not restrain, O Lord, you have known.* How that man? what did the Lord know? Within in the heart, where man does not see. Therefore that man too: *I believed,* he says. Behold, he has the heart, he now has what God may see: let him not restrain his lips. He does not restrain them. For what does he say? *Wherefore I have spoken.* And because he spoke what he believed, seeking what he might render to the Lord for all the things he rendered to him, he adds: *I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord*[^61]. He did not shudder at the Lord saying, *Can you drink the cup which I am to drink?*[^62] For he confesses with the lips what he had in the heart, he comes to the passion. And because he comes to the passion, what did the enemy harm? Surely *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his just ones*[^63]. The deaths in which the pagans raged, in those we today are refreshed. We celebrate the birthday of the martyrs, we set before ourselves the examples of the martyrs, we attend to their faith, how they were found, how they were dragged, how they stood before the judges. In the Catholic Church having nothing of pretense, compacted by the joining of unity, they confessed Christ: the head which had gone before, like the members, they longed to follow. But who longed? Patient in torments, faithful in confession, truthful in speech. For they hurled into the faces of those questioning them the arrows of God, and wounded them to anger; they wounded many also to salvation. All these things we set before ourselves, and gaze at them, and desire to imitate. These are Christian spectacles, these God sees from above, to these he exhorts, in these he helps; for these struggles he sets and gives prizes. *Behold, my lips I will not restrain.* See lest you fear, and restrain your lips. *O Lord, you have known*: because there is in the heart too what sounds in the lips.

**17.** [v. 11] *I have not hidden your justice in my heart*[^64]. What is, *your justice*? My faith: because the just lives by faith. As, for instance, the persecutor questions under penalty, which they were once permitted: What are you? a pagan, or a Christian? A Christian. This is his justice: he believed, he lives by faith. He does not hide his justice in his heart. He did not say within himself: I believe indeed in Christ, but to this persecutor of mine, raging and threatening, I will not say what I have believed: my God knows within my heart that I believe, he himself knows that I do not renounce him. Behold, you say you have this within in the heart: in the lips what? Am I not a Christian? Against your heart your lips bear witness. *I have not hidden your justice in my heart.*

**18.** *Your truth and your salvation I have told*[^65]. I have told your Christ, that is, *Your truth and your salvation I have told.* Whence is Christ truth? *I am the truth*[^66]. Whence is Christ his salvation? Simeon recognized the infant in the hands of his mother in the temple, and said, *For my eyes have seen your salvation*[^67]. The old man recognized the infant, made a boy in the boy, renewed by faith. For he had received a response, and said this: the Lord had told him that he would not depart from this life before he saw the salvation of God. This salvation of God it is good that it be shown to men: but let them cry, *Show us, O Lord, your mercy, and give us your salvation*[^68]. But the salvation of God is in all nations: because when he had said in a certain place, *May God have mercy on us, and bless us, may he make his countenance shine upon us, that we may know your way on the earth,* he added: *among all nations your salvation*[^69]. First he said: *That we may know your way on the earth,* and there followed: *among all nations your salvation.* As if it were said to him: What is the way which you desire to know? Men come to the way, does the way come to men? Our way came to men, found them wandering, called to itself those walking outside. In me, he says, walk, and you will not err: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^70]. Lest you should say: Where is the way of God? to what region shall I go? what mountain shall I ascend? what plains shall I seek out? Do you seek the way of God? The salvation of God is the way of God, and this is everywhere: because among all nations is your salvation. *Your truth and your salvation I have told.*

**19.** *I have not hidden your mercy and your truth from the great assembly*[^71]. Let us be there, let us too be numbered in this body, let us not hide the mercy of the Lord and the truth of the Lord. Do you wish to hear the mercy of the Lord? Withdraw from sins, he will forgive sins. Do you wish to hear the truth of the Lord? Hold to justice, justice will be crowned. For now mercy is preached to you, afterward truth will be displayed. For God is not so merciful that he be unjust; nor so just that he be not merciful. Is the mercy small to you? He will not impute all the former things: you have lived badly up to the present day, you still live, live well today, this mercy you will not hide. If this is the mercy, what is the truth? All nations will be gathered before him, and he will divide them as a shepherd divides the sheep from the goats; he will set the sheep at the right, the goats at the left. To the sheep what? *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you.* To the goats what? *Go into the eternal fire*[^72]. There is no place for repentance there. Because you despised the mercy of God, you will feel the truth: but if you did not despise the mercy, you will rejoice in the truth.

**20.** [vv. 12-13] *But you, O Lord, do not put your mercies far from me*[^73]. He looked back to the wounded members. Because I have not hidden your mercy and your truth from the great assembly, from the Church of the unity of the whole world: attend to the wounded members, attend to the offenders and sinners, and do not remove your mercies. *Your mercy and your truth have always upheld me.* I would not dare to be converted, unless secure about remission: I would not hold out to persevere, unless secure about the promise. *Your mercy and your truth have always upheld me.* I attend that you are good, I attend that you are just: I love the good, I fear the just. Love and fear lead me through: because *your mercy and your truth have always upheld me.* Why do they themselves uphold, and the eye is not to be turned away from them? *Because evils have surrounded me, of which there is no number*[^74]. Who counts sins? who counts iniquities, others' and his own? Under what heap did he groan who said, *Cleanse me from my hidden faults, O Lord, and from the faults of others spare your servant*[^75]. Our own were few, others' are added: I fear for myself, I fear for the good brother, I endure the bad brother; and under this heap what shall we be, if the mercy of God cease? *But you, O Lord, do not put far*: be near. To whom is the Lord near? To those who are contrite of heart. Far from the proud, near to the humble. For the Lord is exalted, and regards the lowly. But let not those who are proud think they lie hidden: for the lofty things he knows from afar. He knew from afar the Pharisee boasting himself, he came near to the confessing Publican. The one boasted his merits, and covered his wounds: the other did not boast merits, but offered wounds. He had come to the physician, he knew he was languishing, he knew he was to be healed: he did not dare lift his eyes to heaven, he beat his breast: he did not spare himself, that the other might spare him; he recognized himself, that the other might pardon him; he punished himself, that the other might free him[^76]. Such are the voices here; let us hear them piously, and love them piously: with the heart, the tongue, all our marrow let us say these things. Let no one think himself just: he lives who speaks; he lives, and would that he might live! Still he lives here, still he lives with death: and if the spirit is life because of justice, yet the body is dead because of sin. And the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that thinks of many things[^77]. It belongs to you, then, to cry, it belongs to groan, it belongs to confess, not to exalt yourself, not to boast yourself, not to glory in your own merits: because even if you have something to rejoice over, what is it that you have not received? *Because evils have surrounded me, of which there is no number.*

**21.** *My iniquities have taken hold of me, and I could not see*[^78]. There is something for us to see: what presses us so that we do not see? Is it not iniquity? Your eye, that you might not be able to see this light, was perhaps pressed by inrushing humor, pressed perhaps by smoke, dust, something thrown in; and you could not lift your wounded eye to this light: what, then, will you lift your wounded heart to God? Is it not first to be healed that you may see? Are you not found proud, when you say: First let me see, and so I will believe? Who says it? For who, about to see, says: Let me see, and so I will believe? I am about to show the light, rather indeed the light itself wishes to show itself. To whom? To a blind man it cannot, he does not see. Whence does he not see? With the eye weighed down by many sins. For what does he say? *My iniquities have taken hold of me, and I could not see.* Let the iniquities, then, be removed, let the sins be forgiven, let the weight be lifted from the eye, let what is wounded be healed, let the biting precept be applied like an eye-salve. First do what is enjoined on you: heal the heart, cleanse the heart, love your enemy. And who loves his enemy? This the physician commands; it is bitter, but wholesome. What shall I do for you, he says? You have been so afflicted, that from it you may be healed. And he says more: To the healed it will not be burdensome, healed you will delightfully love your enemy; you, strive that you may be healed. In tribulations, in straits, in temptations be strong, hold out: it is the hand of a physician, not of a robber. Behold, he says, the precepts received, and faith retained, first I will heal the heart, as you bid: the heart healed and cleansed, what shall I see? Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God. In this way, he says, I cannot. *My iniquities have taken hold of me, and I could not see.*

**22.** *They have been multiplied beyond the hairs of my head*[^79]. He recalls the hairs of the head to the multitude of number. Who counts the hairs of his head? Much less sins, which exceed the number of hairs. They seem small, but they are many. You have guarded against great ones; you no longer commit adultery, you no longer commit murder, you do not seize others' goods, you do not blaspheme, you do not bear false witness: these are the masses of sins. You have guarded against great ones, what do you do about the small? Or do you not fear the small? You have thrown off the mass, see lest you be buried by sand. *They have been multiplied beyond the hairs of my head.*

**23.** [vv. 13-14] *And my heart has forsaken me*[^80]. What wonder is it, if your heart is deserted by your God, when it has deserted itself? What is, *my heart has forsaken me*? My heart is not fit to know itself. This he said, *My heart has forsaken me*: With my heart I wish to see the Lord, and I cannot, on account of the multitude of my sins: it is too little, not even my heart grasps itself. For no one grasps himself, let no one presume about himself. Did Peter grasp his heart with his heart, who said, *I will be with you even unto death*[^81]? In the heart was a false presumption, in the heart lay hidden a true fear: and the heart was not fit to grasp the heart. The sick heart lay hidden, but lay open to the physician. What was pronounced about him, this was fulfilled. God knew in him what he himself did not know in himself; because his heart had forsaken him, his heart lay hidden from his heart. *And my heart has forsaken me.* What then? what do we cry? what do we say? *Let it please you, O Lord, to deliver me*: as though he said, *If you will, you can cleanse me*[^82]. *Let it please you to deliver me, O Lord, look to my help*[^83]. The members in penitence, the members set in griefs, the members crying under the irons of the physician, but hoping. *O Lord, look to my help.*

**24.** [v. 15] *Let them be confounded and put to shame together, who seek my soul to take it away*[^84]. For in a certain place he accuses, and says, *I looked to the right, and saw, and there was no one who sought my soul*[^85]; that is, there was no one who imitated me. Christ speaks in the passion: And I attended to the right, that is, not to the ungodly Jews, but to that right itself, to the apostles themselves; *and there was no one who sought my soul.* So far was there no one who sought my soul, that he who had presumed denied my soul. But because a man is sought in two ways, either that you may enjoy him, or that you may persecute him: therefore here he means others, whom he wishes to be confounded and put to shame, who seek his soul. But lest you should understand it thus, as when he complains of certain ones not seeking his soul; *to take it away,* he says, that is, they seek my soul unto death: he added, *Let them be confounded and put to shame.* And truly many sought his soul, and were confounded and put to shame; they sought his soul, and, as it seemed to them, took away his soul: but he had power to lay down his soul, and had power to take up his soul again. Therefore they rejoiced when he laid it down, they were confounded when he took it up. *Let them be confounded and put to shame together, who seek my soul to take it away.*

**25.** *Let them be turned back, and put to shame, who wish me evils. Let them be turned back*[^86]: let us not take this in an evil sense either. He wishes them well; and it is the voice of him who said from the cross, *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^87]. Why, then, does he say to them, that they should turn back? Because those who before were proud so as to fall behind, are made humble so as to rise again. For when they are in front, they wish to go before the Lord, they wish to be better than the Lord: but if they are behind, they recognize him better, him first, themselves later, that he may go before, they follow. Accordingly he so refuted Peter giving himself bad counsel. For the Lord was to suffer for our salvation, and was foretelling what would come about that very passion: and Peter said, *Far be it, Lord; be merciful to yourself, this shall not be.* He wished to go before the Lord, and to give counsel to the master. But the Lord, that he might not make him one going before, but one following: *Get behind me, Satan*[^88], he said. Therefore, he says, you are Satan, because you wish to go before the one you ought to follow: but if you are behind, and follow, you are no longer Satan. What then? *Upon this rock I will build my Church*[^89].

**26.** [vv. 16-17] *Let them be turned back and put to shame, who wish me evils*[^90]. They are ill-willing, who, even when they bless, as far as is in their heart, curse. You say to someone: Be a Christian: Be a Christian, but you. He said a good thing, but it is not imputed to him what he said, but with what mind he said it: as it was imputed even to the Jews, when the man born blind was made seeing: when they pressed him with insults and urged him, he said to them, *Do you too wish to be his disciples?* And they cursed him. This the evangelist says: *They cursed him, saying: You be his disciple*[^91]. While they cursed, the Lord blessed: he did what they said, but repaid them for what they cursed. *Let them be turned back, and put to shame, who wish me evils.* But there are others, not good, who wish good things, and these too are to be guarded against. For just as those curse, and speak our good things, but with an evil mind; so many our evil things with a good mind. This I say: He who says to you, Be a Christian, says your good with an evil mind: but he who says to you thus, No one is better than you; if in evil deeds, since the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and he who does iniquitous things is blessed; speaks your evils by praising. As the former said your good things by cursing, so this one your evils by blessing: but flee both kinds of enemy, beware both. The one rages, the other flatters; both evil: the one is angry, and the other deceitful in praise; the one is a reprover, the other a praiser: but both the one in reproof is an enemy, and the other in praise is deceitful. Beware both, against both pray. For he who prayed, *Let them be turned back, and confounded, who wish me evils,* looked back at another kind, deceitfully ill-willing, and falsely well-speaking: *Let them at once bear their confusion, who say to me, Aha, aha*[^92]. They praise falsely: A great man, a good man, lettered, learned, but why a Christian? They praise those things of yours which you would not wish to be praised: they reprove that over which you rejoice. But if perhaps you say: What do you praise in me, O man, that I am a good man, that I am a just man? If you think this, Christ made me this, praise him. But that man: Far be it; do not do yourself wrong, you yourself made yourself such. *Let them be confounded, who say to me: Aha, aha.* And what follows? *Let all who seek you exult and rejoice, O Lord.* They do not seek me, but they seek you: they do not say to me, Aha, aha; but they see me glory in you, if I have any glory. For he who glories, let him glory in the Lord[^93]. *Let all who seek you exult and rejoice, O Lord, and let them always say: Let the Lord be magnified.* Because even if a just man is made from a sinner, give glory to him who justifies the ungodly. Whether, then, one be a sinner, let him be praised who calls to pardon: or whether one already walk in the way of justice, let him be praised who calls to a crown. *Let the Lord always be magnified,* by those *who love your salvation.*

**27.** [v. 18] *But I*: to whom they wished evils. *But I*: whose soul they sought, to take it away. But turn yourself to another kind of men. *But I,* to whom they said, Aha, aha, *am needy and poor*[^94]. There is nothing in me of my own to be praised. Let him tear my sackcloth, let him cover me with his robe. For I live now, not I, but Christ lives in me[^95]. If Christ lives in you, and all the good you have is Christ's, all that you will have is Christ's: what are you of yourself? *I am needy and poor.* But I am not rich, because I am not proud. Rich was that one who said, *I give you thanks, O Lord, that I am not as the rest of men*: but the Publican was poor, who said, *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^96]. The one belched from his fullness, the other wept from his hunger. *I am needy and poor.* And what will you do, O needy and poor one? Beg before the door of God; knock, and it shall be opened to you. *But I am needy and poor: the Lord will have care of me.* Cast your care upon the Lord, and hope in him, and he will do it. What will you care for yourself? what will you provide for yourself? Let him who made you have care of you. He who had care of you before you existed, how will he not have care now that you are already what he willed you to be? For now you are faithful, now you walk in the way of justice. Will he not have care of you, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust? you, now just, living by faith, will he neglect, desert, dismiss? Rather indeed both here he cherishes, and here he helps, and here he supplies necessary things, and cuts off the harmful. By giving he consoles that you may remain, by taking away he corrects that you may not perish. The Lord has care of you, be secure. He carries you who made you: do not fall from the hand of your maker: if you fall from the hand of the maker, you will be broken. But that you may remain in the hand of the maker, good will does it. Say, My God willed it, and he himself will carry, and he himself will hold. Cast yourself upon him; do not think it empty, as though you were to be thrown headlong: let it not so seem to you. He said: I fill heaven and earth. Nowhere is he lacking to you: do you not be lacking to him, do not be lacking to yourself. *The Lord has care of me.*

**28.** *You are my helper and my protector: my God, do not delay*[^97]. He calls upon, he implores, he fears lest he fail: *Do not delay.* What is, *Do not delay*? *Unless those days had been shortened, no flesh would be saved*[^98]: just now it was read, of the days of tribulations. For as one man he asks God, the members of Christ, the body of Christ spread everywhere, one beggar, one poor man; because that one is poor too, who, being rich, was made poor, of whom the Apostle says, *Though he was rich, he was made poor, that by his poverty you might be enriched*[^99]: the true poor he enriches, the false rich he makes poor. He cries to him: *From the ends of the earth I cried to you, when my soul was wearied*[^100]. There will come days of tribulations and of greater tribulations: they will come, as Scripture says; and the more the days advance, the more the tribulations are increased. Let no one promise himself what the Gospel does not promise. My brothers, I beseech you, attend to our Scriptures, whether they have deceived in anything; whether they have said anything, and it happened otherwise than they said: it is necessary that to the end all things so come about, as they have said. Our Scriptures do not promise us in this world anything but tribulations, oppressions, straits, increases of griefs, an abundance of temptations. For these above all let us prepare ourselves, lest, unprepared, we fail. *Woe to those who are with child and giving suck*[^101]: just now you heard. They are with child, who swell with hope: those giving suck, that is, nursing, are those who have now obtained what they coveted. For a pregnant woman swells in hope of a son, she does not yet see the son: but she who now suckles, embraces what she hoped. Therefore let us set, for example, a likeness: This estate of my neighbor is good: oh, that it were mine; I would join it, and would make of this farm and that a unity. Greed too loves unity: what it loves is good, but where it ought to be loved, it does not know. Behold, it has coveted the neighbor's estate; but this neighbor is rich, not needy, having honor, having even power, from whose power perhaps even something is to be feared for you, nothing to be hoped from his farm: hoping nothing, the soul does not conceive, is not pregnant. But if a poor neighbor is near, who either is set in such need that he can sell, or can be pressed so as to be forced to sell, the eye is cast on it, the estate is hoped for: the soul is made pregnant, it hopes it can obtain the little estate and possession of the poor neighbor. And when this poor man suffers need, he comes to his richer neighbor, whom perhaps he is wont to wait on, to defer to, to rise up for when he comes, to greet with bowed head: Give to me, I beg you; I suffer need, I am pressed by a creditor. And the other: I have nothing now in my hands. If he wished to sell, he would have it. We recognize this: there were such in us, let them no longer be in us. Did we not live yesterday, and do we live today? There is place for correcting; that separation has not yet been made, of some to the right, of others to the left; we are not yet among the dead, where that rich man was, thirsting and desiring a drop: let us hear while we live, let us be corrected. Let us not hope for others' things, and pregnant, swell: nor let us come to them, and by obtaining them kiss them as sons. *For woe to those who are with child and giving suck in those days.* The heart must be changed, the heart must be lifted up, the heart must not dwell here: it is an evil region. Let it suffice that we must still be here in the flesh; what is not necessary, let it not be done: let the day's evil suffice it; let us dwell with our heart above. *If you have risen with Christ,* he says to the faithful, he says to those receiving the body and blood of the Lord: *If you have risen with Christ, relish the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^102]. For what is promised you does not appear: and it is now prepared, but you do not see it. You wish to be made pregnant, be made pregnant from here; let that be your hope: your offspring will be certain, it will not be a miscarriage, not temporal: you will embrace what you have brought forth forever. For so it is said through Isaiah: *We have conceived, and brought forth the spirit of salvation*[^103]. Therefore it is behind, and is not given now, but will be given. How great things have been given, my brothers, who numbers them according to the Scriptures? There it is written of the Church, and it is seen that it is; there it is written of idols that they shall not be, and it is seen that they are not; there it is written that the Jews would lose the kingdom, and it is seen; there it is written of heretics, that they would be, and it is seen: there it is written also of the day of judgment; there it is written also of the reward of the good, and of the punishment of the evil: in all things we find God faithful; in the last will he fail and deceive? *The Lord will have care of me. You are my helper and my protector: my God, do not delay. Unless those days were lessened, no flesh would hold out: but for the sake of the elect they will be shortened*[^104]. Those days will be of tribulation, but not so long as they are feared. Quickly they will pass; the rest that comes will not pass. Although long, the evil ought to be borne for an infinite good.
