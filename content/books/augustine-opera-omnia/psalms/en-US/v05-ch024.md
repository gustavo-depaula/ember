# Exposition of Psalm 117

**1.** We have heard, brothers, the Holy Spirit admonishing and exhorting us, that we offer to God the sacrifice of confession. But confession is either of his praise, or of our sins. And that confession indeed, by which we confess our sins to God, is known to all; so that the less learned multitude supposes that this alone is called confession in the holy Scriptures: for where this word sounds from the mouth of the reader, immediately a pious noise of those beating their breasts follows. But they ought to notice how it was said in another psalm: *For I will enter into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, even to the house of God, in the voice of exultation and confession, of one celebrating the sound of a festival*[^1]. For here certainly it is manifest that the voice and sound of confession pertain not to the sorrow of penitence, but to the gladness of a most celebrated festival. Or if anyone still doubts of so manifest a testimony, what will he say to that which is written in Ecclesiasticus: *Bless the Lord, all his works, give magnificence to his name, and confess in his praise in the songs of the lips and in harps; and thus say in confession: For all the works of the Lord are very good*[^2]? Here certainly no one, even the slowest, doubts that confession is set in the praises of God; unless perhaps there is such perversity in anyone's mind that he dares to say that even the Lord Jesus Christ himself confessed his sins to the Father. But if any impious man tried, because of the name of confession, to object this, he would most easily be refuted by the very context of the words. For he speaks thus: *I confess to you,* he says, *Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones; yes, Father, for so it was pleasing before you*[^3]. Who does not understand that he says these things in praise of the Father? Who does not see that this confession pertains not to grief of heart, but to joy; since the Evangelist too went before, saying: *In that very hour he exulted in the Holy Spirit, and said: I confess to you, Father*[^4]?

**2.** [v. 1.] Wherefore, dearest ones, since it is by no means at all to be doubted, by so many agreeing testimonies, of which you can notice similar ones in the Scriptures through yourselves, that not only in sins, but also in the praises of God, confession is wont to be named in the divine letters; what more fitting, in this psalm, when we sing *Alleluia,* which is: Praise the Lord, do we understand ourselves admonished, when we hear: *Confess to the Lord,* than this very thing, namely that we praise the Lord? The praise of God could not be more briefly unfolded than to say: *For he is good.* What is greater than this brevity, I do not see; since it is so proper to God that he is good, that, when addressed by a certain man, the Son of God himself, when he heard: *Good Master,* namely from one who, gazing on his flesh, and not understanding the fullness of divinity, supposed him to be only a man; answered: *Why do you ask me about the good? No one is good, except the one God*[^5]. Which is nothing else than to say: If you wish to call me good, understand me to be God too. But since it is said to the people, in the foretelling of future things freed from all labor and captivity of pilgrimage and from all mixture of the wicked, which was bestowed on it through the grace of God, not only not rendering evil for evil, but even rendering good for evil; most fittingly it was added: *For his mercy is unto the age.*

**3.** [vv. 2-5.] *But let the house of Israel say that he is good, that his mercy is unto the age. But let the house of Aaron say that he is good, that his mercy is unto the age. Let all who fear the Lord now say that his mercy is unto the age.* You recognize, I believe, dearest ones, which is the house of Israel, which is the house of Aaron, and that both are those who fear the Lord. For they are the little ones with the great, already well insinuated into your hearts in another psalm: in whose number let us all rejoice to be associated, in the grace of him who is good and whose mercy is unto the age; since those were heard who said: *May the Lord add upon you, upon you and upon your sons*[^6]: so that to the Israelites believing in Christ, of whose number are the Apostles our fathers, both for the eminence of the perfect and for the obedience of the little ones the number of the Gentiles might be joined; that we may all say, made one in Christ, made one flock under one shepherd, and as the body of that head as one man may say: *In tribulation I called upon the Lord, and he heard me into a broad place.* The narrowness of our tribulation is finished: but the breadth to which we pass over has no end. *For who shall accuse against the elect of God?*[^7]

**4.** [vv. 6, 7.] *The Lord is my helper; I will not fear what man may do to me.* But does the Church have only men as enemies? What is a man given over to flesh and blood, except flesh and blood? But the Apostle says: *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against the princes and rulers of the world of this darkness*: that is, the rulers of the wicked, loving this world, and therefore of darkness; because we too were once darkness, but now light in the Lord. *Against the spiritual forces,* he says, *of wickedness in the heavenly places*[^8]: that is, the devil and his angels; which devil in another place he calls the prince of the power of this air. Hear, then, what follows: *The Lord is my helper, and I will despise my enemies.* From whatever kind enemies rise up, whether from the number of bad men, or from the number of bad angels; in the help of the Lord they will be despised, to whom we confess praise, to whom we sing Alleluia.

**5.** [vv. 8, 9.] But the enemies being despised, let not a good man as a friend so set himself before me, that he bid my hope to be placed in himself: *For it is good to trust in the Lord, rather than to trust in man.* Nor let whoever can in a certain way be called a good angel be so thought of by me, that I ought to trust in him: for no one is good, except God alone; and when a man or an angel seems to help, when they do this with true love, he does it through them, who made them good according to their measure. *It is good,* then, *to hope in the Lord, rather than to hope in princes.* For angels too are called princes, as we read in Daniel: *Michael your prince*[^9].

**6.** [vv. 10, 11.] *All the nations surrounded me, and in the name of the Lord I avenged myself on them: surrounding they surrounded me, and in the name of the Lord I avenged myself on them.* What he says, *All the nations surrounded me, and in the name of the Lord I avenged myself on them,* signifies the labors and victory of the Church; but as if a cause were sought whence it could overcome so great evils, regard was had to the example, and he said what it first suffered in its head, by adding what follows: *Surrounding they surrounded me*: and well there it is not repeated, *all the nations;* because that was done by the Jews alone. *And in the name of the Lord I avenged myself on them*: because there the pious people itself, which is the body of Christ, felt the persecutors, of whose kind that flesh was taken up and hung, and for whom was done whatever was done mortally by virtue immortal, by that inner divinity through the outer flesh.

**7.** [v. 12.] *They surrounded me like bees a honeycomb, and they blazed up like fire among thorns, and in the name of the Lord I avenged myself on them.* Here now, from the order of the things done, the order of the words too is woven. For we rightly take the Lord himself, the head of the Church, to be surrounded by the persecutors, as bees surround a honeycomb. For what was done by those who did not know, the Holy Spirit speaks with mystical subtlety. For bees work honey in the honeycombs: but the persecutors of the Lord, not knowing, made him sweeter to us by that very passion; that we may taste and see how sweet the Lord is, who died for our offenses, and rose again for our justification. But what follows: *And they blazed up like fire among thorns*: is better understood in his body, that is, in the people spread everywhere; which all the nations surrounded, since it is gathered from all the nations. *They blazed up* indeed *like fire among thorns,* when by the burning of persecution they burned the sinful flesh and the most troublesome prickings of this mortal life. *And in the name,* he says, *of the Lord I avenged myself on them*: either because they too, the malice extinguished which in them persecuted the just, were joined to the Christian people; or because the rest of those who scorned in this time the mercy of him who calls, are going to feel the truth of him who judges at the end.

**8.** [v. 13.] *Like a heap of sand I was pushed that I might fall, and the Lord received me.* For although there was now a great multitude of believers, to be compared as it were to innumerable sand, and led into one society as it were into a heap; yet *what is man, except that you are mindful of him?*[^10] He did not say: The number of the nations could not overcome the abundance of my number; but: *The Lord,* he says, *received me.* The persecution of the nations, then, had not where to push the multitude of the faithful dwelling together in the unity of faith, so as to cast it down: since belief was in him who would receive both each one and all and everywhere; because to them calling upon him he could nowhere be lacking.

**9.** [v. 14.] *My strength and my praise is the Lord; and he became my salvation.* Who, then, fall, when they are pushed, except those who wish to be their own strength to themselves, and their own praise to themselves? For no one falls in the contest, except him whose strength and praise falls. Wherefore he whose strength and praise is the Lord, so does not fall as the Lord does not fall. And therefore he became their salvation; not because he himself was made something which he was not before, but because they themselves, when they believed in him, were made what they were not, and now he himself began to be salvation, not to himself, but to them—which to those turned away from him he was not—to those converted.

**10.** [v. 15.] *The voice of gladness and salvation in the tabernacles of the just*: where those who raged against their bodies supposed there was the voice of grief and ruin. For they did not perceive the inner joys of the saints over the future hope. Whence the Apostle too says: *As sorrowful, but always rejoicing*[^11]; and again: *And not only so, but we glory too,* he says, *in tribulations*[^12].

**11.** [v. 16.] *The right hand of the Lord did virtue.* What virtue does he mean? *The right hand,* he says, *of the Lord exalted me.* A great virtue to exalt the humble, to deify the mortal, to provide from weakness perfection, from subjection glory, from passion victory, to give help from tribulation; so that to the afflicted the true salvation of God might be laid open, but to the afflicters the vain salvation of man might remain. These things are great; but why do you wonder? hear what he repeats. Not man exalted himself, not man perfected himself, not did man give himself glory, not did man conquer, not was man salvation to himself: *The right hand of the Lord did virtue.*

**12.** [v. 17.] *I will not die, but live, and tell the works of the Lord.* But those, making slaughter of corpses everywhere, supposed that the Church of Christ was dying. Behold, now it tells the works of the Lord. Everywhere Christ is the glory of the blessed martyrs. He conquered by being beaten those who struck; by suffering, the impatient; by loving, the raging.

**13.** [v. 18.] Yet why the body of Christ, the holy Church, the people unto adoption, endured so many unworthy things; let it indicate to us. *Correcting,* he says, *the Lord corrected me, and did not hand me over to death.* Let not the raging of the impious, then, suppose that anything was permitted to its own strength: it would not have that power, unless it were given to it from above. Often the householder commands his sons to be corrected by the worst servants; while he prepares for the latter the inheritance, for the former the fetters. What is this inheritance? Is it of gold, or of silver, or of gems, or of estates, and pleasant farms? See by what one enters, and recognize what it is.

**14.** [v. 19.] *Open to me,* he says, *the gates of righteousness.* Behold, we have heard the gates. What is within? *Entering,* he says, *into them I will confess to the Lord.* That confession is of praise, *wonderful even to the house of God, in the voice of exultation and confession, of one celebrating the sound of a festival*[^13]: this is the eternal blessedness of the just, by which those are blessed who dwell in the house of God, praising him for ages of ages.

**15.** [v. 20.] But see how one enters into the gates of righteousness. *These are the gates of the Lord,* he says, *the just will enter into them.* Let no unjust one enter at least these, into that Jerusalem which does not receive the uncircumcised, where it is said: *The dogs outside*[^14]. Let it suffice that in my far-off pilgrimage *I dwelt with the tabernacles of Kedar,* and *with those who hate peace I was peaceable*[^15]: I endured to the end the mixture of the bad, but *these are the gates of the Lord; the just will enter into them.*

**16.** [v. 21.] *I will confess to you, O Lord, because you have heard me, and become my salvation.* How often it is shown that this confession is of praise, not showing the wounds to the physician, but giving thanks for health received. But the physician himself is the salvation.

**17.** [v. 22.] But whom do we mean by this? *The stone which the builders rejected*: for *this became the head of the corner; that he might found the two in himself, into one new man, making peace, and connect both into one body to God*[^16], namely the circumcision and the uncircumcision.

**18.** [v. 23.] *It was made by the Lord for it*: that is, the head of the corner was made by the Lord. For although he was not made this, unless he had suffered; yet he was not made this by those by whom he suffered. For those who were building rejected it; but in that which the Lord was secretly building, he made the head of the corner what those rejected. *And it is wonderful in our eyes*: in the eyes of the inner man, in the eyes of those who believe, hope, love; not in the carnal eyes of those who, scorning him as it were a man, rejected him.

**19.** [v. 24.] *This is the day which the Lord made.* This man remembers that he said in the earlier psalms: *Because he inclined his ear to me, and in my days I called upon him*[^17]; commemorating his old days: whence now he says: *This is the day which the Lord made;* that is, on which he gave me salvation. This is the day of which he said: *In an acceptable time I heard you, and in the day of salvation I helped you*[^18]; that is, the day on which that mediator was made the head of the corner. *Let us exult,* then, *and rejoice in it.*

**20.** [v. 25.] *O Lord, make me safe; O Lord, prosper well the way.* Because it is the day of salvation, *make me safe*: because, returning from the far-off pilgrimage, we are separated from those who hated peace, with whom we were peaceable, and when we spoke to them, they warred on us without cause; *prosper well the way* for those returning, because you have become for us the way.

**21.** [v. 26.] *For blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord.* Cursed, then, is he who comes in his own name; as he says in the Gospel: *I came in the name of my Father, and you did not receive me: if another comes in his own name, him you will receive*[^19]. *We have blessed you from the house of the Lord.* I believe that this is the voice of the great to the little ones, namely of those great ones who touch with the mind, as far as they can in this life, the Word God with God; and yet temper their discourse for the sake of the little ones, that they may be able sincerely to say what the Apostle says: *For whether we are beside ourselves to God, or whether we are sober for you; for the love of Christ compels us*[^20]. They themselves bless the little ones from the inner house of the Lord, where that praise for ages of ages does not fail: and therefore see what they announce from there.

**22.** [v. 27.] *God is the Lord, and he has given light to us.* That Lord who comes in the name of the Lord, whom the builders rejected, and who was made the head of the corner; that mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus, is God, is equal to the Father, and has given light to us, that we might understand what we believed, and announce to you who do not yet understand, but already believe. But that you too may understand: *Establish a feast day in the throngs, even to the horns of the altar*: that is, even to the inner house of God, from which we have blessed you, where are the high places of the altar. *Establish a feast day,* not lukewarmly and slothfully, but *in the throngs.* For this is the voice *of exultation, of one celebrating the sound of a festival,* of those walking in the place *of the wonderful tabernacle, even to the house of God*[^21]. For if there is a spiritual sacrifice there, an everlasting sacrifice of praise, both the priest is everlasting, and the everlasting altar is the very pacified mind of the just. This we say more openly, brothers: whoever wish to understand God the Word, let not the flesh suffice them, that for their sake the Word was made flesh, that they might be nourished with milk; nor let this feast day on earth, on which the lamb was slain, suffice them: but let it be established in the throngs, until one arrives, our minds being exalted by the Lord, even to his inner divinity, who deigned to provide his outer humanity to us to be nourished with milk.

**23.** [vv. 28, 29.] And what else there, except that we will sing his praises? What else there will we say, except: *You are my God, and I will confess to you; you are my God, and I will exalt you: I will confess to you, O Lord, because you have heard me, and become my salvation?* We will say these things not with the din of words, but love clinging to him by itself cries out this voice, and love itself is this voice. And so, as it began with praise, so it ends: *Confess to the Lord, for he is good, for his mercy is unto the age.* Hence the Psalm began, here it ends; since, just as from the beginning we deserted him, so at the end we return to him, there is nothing which more wholesomely delights than the praise of God, and always *Alleluia.*
