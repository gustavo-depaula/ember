# Exposition of Psalm 40

**1.** Since the solemn day of the martyrs has dawned; on account of the glory of the passion of Christ, the emperor of the martyrs, who did not spare himself by commanding his soldiers to the fight, but fought first, conquered first, that he might exhort those fighting by his example, and aid them by his majesty, and crown them by his promise: let us hear something in this psalm which pertains to his passion. But we commend rather often, nor does it weary us to repeat what is useful for you to retain, that our Lord Jesus Christ very often speaks from himself, that is, from his own person, which is our head; very often from the person of his body, which are we and his Church; but in such a way that the words sound as from the mouth of one man, that we may understand that head and body subsist in the unity of integrity, and are not separated from one another; like that marriage of which it was said, *They shall be two in one flesh*[^1]. If, then, we recognize two in one flesh, let us recognize two in one voice. First, what, answering the reader, we sang, although it be from the middle of the psalm, yet let us take from here the beginning of the discourse. *My enemies spoke evil things to me: When will he die, and his name perish?*[^2] This is the person of our Lord Jesus Christ: but see whether the members too are not understood there. This too was said, when our Lord himself walked here on earth in the flesh. For when they saw the multitude follow his authority, and his divinity and majesty presented by miracles; when the Jews saw this, of whom the Lord himself set forth a likeness, that they said, *This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*[^3], they said among themselves, that is, to one another, whence is the voice of that high priest Caiaphas: *You see that a great crowd follows him, and the world has gone after him: if we let him live, the Romans will come, and take away from us both our place and our nation. It is expedient that one man die, rather than the whole nation perish.* But the evangelist expounded to us the words of one not knowing what he said, and said: *But this he did not say of himself; but being high priest he prophesied that Jesus must die for the people and the nation*[^4]. Yet they, when they saw the people go after him, said: *When will he die, and his name perish?* that is, when we have killed him, his name will no longer be on earth, nor will he, being dead, seduce any; but by his very slaying men will understand that they were following a man, that in him there was no hope of salvation; and they will forsake his name, and it will not be. He died; and his name did not perish, but his name was sown: he died: but it was a grain, by whose dying the harvest would at once rise up. When, then, our Lord Jesus Christ was glorified, they began much more and in much more numerous fashion to believe in him; and his members began to hear what the head heard. Now, then, our Lord Jesus Christ being set in heaven, and he himself laboring in us on earth, his enemies still said: *When will he die, and his name perish?* For from this the devil stirred up persecutions in the Church to destroy the name of Christ. Unless perhaps you think, brothers, that those pagans, when they raged against Christians, did not say this to themselves, to blot out the name of Christ from the earth. That Christ might die again, not in the head, but in his body, the martyrs too were slain. The holy blood shed availed to multiply the Church, to the sowing was added also the death of the martyrs. *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his just ones*[^5]. Christians were multiplied more and more, and that was not fulfilled which the enemies said: *When will he die, and his name perish?* Still even now it is said. The pagans sit, and count for themselves the years, they hear their fanatics saying: Sometime there will be no Christians, and those idols are to be worshiped, as they were worshiped before, still they say: *When will he die, and his name perish?* Twice conquered, or thrice, be wise: Christ has died, his name will not perish; the martyrs have died, the Church has been multiplied the more, the name of Christ grows through all nations. He who foretold of his own death and of his resurrection, who foretold of the deaths of his martyrs and of the crown, he himself foretold also of his Church to come: if he spoke truly twice, did he lie the third time? Vain, then, is what you believe against him; it is better that you believe in him, that you *may understand concerning the needy and poor*[^6]; since *he was made poor, though he was rich, that by his poverty,* he says, *you might be enriched*[^7]. But now, because he was made poor, he is despised, and it is said: He was a man. What was he? He died, he was crucified; you worship a man, you have hope in a man, you adore a dead man. You are deceived. Understand concerning the needy and poor, that by his poverty you may be made rich. What is, Understand concerning the needy and poor? That you may receive Christ himself as needy and poor, saying in another psalm, *But I am needy and poor, the Lord has care of me*[^8]. What is it to understand concerning the needy and poor? Because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^9]: rich with the Father, and poor with us; rich in heaven, poor on earth; rich God, poor man. Does this, then, disturb you, that you see a man, that you behold flesh, that you regard death, that you deride the cross? does this disturb you? Understand concerning the needy and poor. What is this? Understand that where weakness is exposed to you, there divinity lies hidden. Rich, because he is so; poor, because you were so. But yet his poverty is our riches: as his weakness is our strength; as his foolishness is our wisdom; as his mortality is our immortality. What it is to be poor, attend: not when you measure it by the poverty of others. He came to fill the poor, who was made poor. Therefore open the bosom of faith; receive the poor one, lest you remain poor.

**2.** [v. 2] *Blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor: in the evil day the Lord will deliver him*[^10]. For the evil day will come: whether you will or not, it will come; the day of judgment will be at hand, an evil day, if you do not understand concerning the needy and poor. For what now you are unwilling to believe, will be manifest in the end. But you will not escape, when it is manifest, because you do not believe while it is hidden. You are invited to believe what you do not see, lest, when you see, you blush. Understand, then, concerning the needy and poor, that is, concerning Christ; understand in him the hidden riches, whom you see poor. For in him are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden[^11]. From this he will deliver you in the evil day, from that which he is God: but from that which he is man, both that which was human in him he raised up, and changed to the better, lifted to heaven. But he who is God, who willed to have one person in man and with man, could neither decrease nor increase, neither die nor rise again. He died from the weakness of man, but God does not die. For that the Word of God does not die, do not wonder, when the soul does not die in the martyr. Did we not just now hear the Lord himself saying, *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^12]? Therefore, when the martyrs die, the souls of the martyrs are not dead, and when Christ died, was the Word to die? Surely the Word of God is much more than the soul of man: because the soul of man was made by God; and if it was made by God, it was made through the Word: because all things were made through him. Therefore neither does the Word die, since the soul made through the Word does not die. But as we rightly say: The man has died, even though his soul does not die; so we rightly say: Christ has died, even though his divinity does not die. Dead whence? Because needy and poor. Let not his death constrain you, and turn you from beholding the divinity. *Blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor.* Regard also the poor, the needy, the hungry and thirsty, the pilgrims, the naked, the sick, those set in prison; understand also concerning such a poor one, because even if you understand concerning such a one, you understand concerning him who said, *I was hungry, I was thirsty, naked, a stranger, sick, in prison*[^13]. So in the malign day the Lord will deliver you.

**3.** [v. 3] And see your blessedness. *The Lord preserve him*[^14]. The prophet rightly wishes well to the man understanding concerning the needy and poor. This wish is a promise; let those who do this await it secure. *The Lord preserve him, and give him life.* What is, *preserve him, and give him life*? To what does *give him life* pertain? To the future life. For he is given life who was dead. But can a dead man understand concerning the needy and poor? But he promises us that giving of life, of which the Apostle says: *The body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of justice: but if he who raised Christ from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Christ from the dead will give life also to your mortal bodies because of his Spirit dwelling in you*[^15]. This, then, is the giving of life which is promised to him who understands concerning the needy and poor. But because the Apostle says to Timothy, *Having the promise of the present life and of the future*[^16]; lest those who understand concerning the needy and poor should think that they are indeed to be received into heaven, but neglected on earth; and should hope for nothing but what is to be forever, but should think that what pertains to the present God does not care for in his saints and faithful; where he said what we ought above all to await, *The Lord preserve him, and give him life;* he looked back to this life, *And make him blessed,* he says, *on the earth.* Lift up, then, your eyes to these promises with Christian faith: God does not desert you on earth, and promises something in heaven. For many bad Christians, inspectors of almanacs, and inquirers and observers of seasons and days, when they begin to be rebuked for it by us, or by certain good and better Christians, why they do these things, answer: These things are necessary for this time; but we are Christians on account of eternal life; for this we believed in Christ, that he might give us eternal life; for this temporal life in which we are engaged does not pertain to his care. It remains, that they say this briefly, that God is to be worshiped for the sake of eternal life, and the devil for the sake of the present life. Christ himself answers them: *You cannot serve two masters*[^17]. You worship one for what you expect in heaven, and worship another for what you expect on earth: how much better do you worship the one who made heaven and earth? He who took care that there should be earth, does he neglect his image on earth? Therefore: *The Lord preserve him and give him life,* the one understanding concerning the needy and poor. Moreover, although he give life forever, *let him make him blessed on the earth.*

**4.** *And let him not deliver him into the hands of his enemy*[^18]. That enemy is the devil. Let no one regard a human enemy of his, when he hears these words. Now perhaps he was thinking of his neighbor, of the one who had a lawsuit with him in the forum, of the one who wishes to take away his possession, who wishes to press him to sell his house to him. Do not think of these things; but think of that enemy, of whom the Lord says, *An enemy man did this*[^19]. For he is the one who suggests that God be worshiped for the sake of earthly things; because this enemy cannot overthrow the Christian name: for he saw himself conquered by the fame and praises of Christ, he saw, in the fact that he slew the martyrs of Christ, them crowned, and himself triumphed over; and he began to be unable to persuade men of this, that Christ is nothing; and because by disparaging Christ he now hardly deceives; by praising Christ he tries to deceive. Before, what did he say? Whom do you worship? A dead Jew, crucified, a man of no moment, who could not drive death from himself. When in his name he saw the human race run, saw that in the name of the crucified the temples are overthrown, the idols broken, the sacrifices extinguished; and all these things foretold in the prophets are attended to by men, stupefied with wonder, and now closing the heart against the disparagement of Christ; he clothes himself in the praises of Christ, and began to deter from the faith in another way. Great is the Christian law, that law is powerful, divine, ineffable: but who fulfills it? In the name of our Savior trample the lion and the dragon. Openly reproving, the lion roared; cunningly praising, the dragon lies in wait. Let those come to the faith who doubted: let them not say: Who fulfills this? If they presume on their own strength, they will not fulfill it. By presuming on the grace of God let them believe, by presuming let them come, let them come to be helped, not judged. All the faithful live in the name of Christ, each according to his degree fulfilling the precepts of Christ, whether married, or celibate and virgins, they live as much as the Lord grants them to live; nor do they presume on their own strength, but they know that they ought to glory in him. For what do you have that you have not received? But if you have received, why do you glory as if you had not received[^20]? Do not say to me: Who fulfills it? He fulfills it in me, who came rich to the poor: poor indeed to the poor, but full to the empty. Thinking these things, because he understands concerning the needy and poor, and does not spurn the poverty of Christ, he understands the riches of Christ, he is made blessed on the earth: and he is not delivered into the hands of his enemy, who wishes to persuade him that God be worshiped for the sake of heavenly things, the devil for the sake of earthly things. *Let him not deliver him into the hands of his enemy.*

**5.** [v. 4] *May the Lord bring him help*[^21]. But where? Perhaps in heaven, perhaps in eternal life, so that there remains the worship of the devil for earthly want, for the needs of this life? Far be it. You have the promise of the present life and of the future. He came to you on earth, through whom heaven and earth were made. Finally, attend to what he says: *May the Lord bring him help upon the bed of his grief.* The bed of grief is the weakness of the flesh. Do not say: I cannot hold and carry and bridle my flesh: you are helped that you may be able. May the Lord bring you help upon the bed of your grief. The bed carried you, you did not carry the bed; but you were a paralytic within: he is present who says to you, *Take up your pallet, and go into your house*[^22]. *May the Lord bring him help upon the bed of his grief.* And he turns himself to the Lord, as though it were asked: Why, then, when God brings us help, do we suffer such great evils in this life, such great scandals, such great labors, such great unrest of the flesh and of the world? he turns himself to God, and as it were setting forth to us the counsel of his medicine: *His whole couch,* he says, *you have turned in his weakness.* What is, *His whole couch you have turned in his weakness*? By the couch is understood something earthly. Every weak soul in this life seeks for itself something earthly where it may rest; since it can hardly perpetually bear the intention of labor and of a mind stretched out toward God: it acquires for itself something on earth where it may rest and in a way recline by a certain pause, such as are those things which even the innocent love. For we are not now to speak of the lusts of the wicked, because many take their rest in theaters, many take their rest in the circus, in the amphitheater, many in dice; many in the luxury of taverns, many in the lust of adulteries, many in the violences of plunderings, many in the deceit and treacheries of frauds: men take their rest in all these things. What is, take their rest? They are delighted there. But let us remove all these things, let us come to the innocent man: he takes his rest in his house, in his household, in his wife, in his children, in his poverty, in his little estate, in the young plant set with his own hands, in some building made by his own care: the innocent take their rest in these things. But yet God, wishing us to have love only of eternal life, mingles bitterness even with these as it were innocent delights; so that in these too we suffer tribulations, and he turns our whole couch in our weakness. *His whole couch you have turned in his weakness.* Let him not, then, complain, when in these things which he innocently has, he suffers some tribulations. He is taught to love better things through the bitterness of lower ones: lest the traveler, tending toward the homeland, love the inn instead of his house. *His whole couch you have turned in his weakness.*

**6.** [v. 5] But why these things? Because he scourges every son whom he receives. Why these things? Because to sinning man it was said, *In the labor of your countenance you shall eat your bread*[^23]. Therefore, because man ought to recognize that these corrections, in which our whole couch is turned in our weakness, he suffers on account of sins; let him turn himself, and say what follows: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^24]. O Lord, exercise me in tribulations: you judge that every son whom you are to receive is to be scourged, you who spared not even your Only-begotten. He indeed was scourged without sin: but I say, *Have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.* If he was cut who had no rottenness, if that medicine of ours did not refuse the medicinal fire; ought we impatiently to bear the burning and cutting physician, that is, the one exercising us in all tribulations, and healing us from sin? Let us plainly commit ourselves to the hand of the physician; for he does not err, so as to cut the sound instead of the rotten: he knows what he inspects, he knows the vice, because he himself made the nature; he distinguishes what he himself founded, what came in from our lust. He knows that he gave a precept to sound man, lest he incur sickness; that he said in paradise: Eat this, and do not this. The sound man did not hear the physician's precept, that he might not fall; let at least the sick man hear, that he may rise. *I said: Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.* In my deeds, in my sins I do not accuse fortune, I do not say: This fate did to me; I do not say: Venus made me an adulterer, and Mars made me a robber, and Saturn made me greedy. *I said: Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.* Did Christ say this? did that head of ours without sin say this? did he who paid what he did not seize? did he, alone free among the dead? For free among the dead, because without sin: *because everyone who commits sin is the servant of sin*[^25]. Did he himself, then? Rather he himself from his members, because the voice of his members is his voice; because the voice of our head too is our voice. For we were in him, when he said, *My soul is sorrowful even unto death*[^26]. For he did not fear to die, who had come to die; nor refuse to die, who had power to lay down his soul, and had power to take it up again: but the members were speaking in the head, and the head was speaking for the members. In him, then, we find our voice: *Heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.* For we were in him, when he said, *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* For in that very psalm where this verse is in the head, there follows consequently: *The words of my transgressions*[^27]. Of what transgressions in him, except because our old man was crucified together with him, that the body of sin might be emptied out, and we no longer serve sin[^28]? To him and in him let us say: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.*

**7.** [v. 6] *My enemies spoke evil things to me: When will he die, and his name perish?*[^29] We have already said these things, and from here we began; and that we may say other things, there is no need to repeat what has been impressed on your ears and hearts by so recent a discourse.

**8.** [v. 7] *And they came in to see*[^30]. What Christ suffered, the Church too suffers; what the head suffered, the members too suffer. For is the servant greater than his lord, or the disciple above the master? *If they have persecuted me,* he says, *they will persecute you also. If they have called the head of the household Beelzebub, how much more his domestics*[^31]? *They came in to see.* That Judas was at our head, he came in to our head to see, that is, to spy: not to have what he might believe, but to find what he might betray. Behold, he came in to see, and this example was set forth in our head. What of those members after the assumption of our head? Does not the apostle Paul say: *On account of false brothers brought in secretly, who came in secretly to spy out our liberty*[^32]? Therefore these too came in to see, for they are hypocrites, evil dissemblers joining themselves with a feigned love, catching all the movements, all the words of the saints, seeking snares in all things. And what becomes of them? See what follows: *Their heart spoke vain things*[^33]: that is, they speak as it were out of a feigned love; what they speak is vain, it is not true, it is not solid. And because they catch at something whence they may find an accusation, what does he say? *They gathered iniquity to themselves.* For preparing slanders, the enemies, as it were, seem great to themselves, because they have something to accuse. *To themselves they gathered iniquity. To themselves,* he says, not to me. As Judas to himself, not to Christ, so also the dissemblers of the Church to themselves, not to us: because of them too it is said elsewhere, *And iniquity has lied to itself*[^34]. *They gathered iniquity to themselves.* And because they came in to see: *They went outside, and spoke.* He who came in to see, went outside and spoke. Would that he were within, and spoke true things! he would not go outside where he speaks false things. He is a betrayer and persecutor, gone outside he speaks. If you pertain to the members of Christ, come within, cleave to the head. Endure the tares if you are wheat; endure the chaff, if you are grain; endure the bad fish within the nets, if you are a good fish. Why before the time of winnowing have you flown away? Why before the time of harvest have you even rooted up the grains together with yourself? Why before you came to the shore have you broken the nets? *They went outside, and spoke.*

**9.** [v. 8] *Against me all my enemies whispered together*[^35]. Against me all together: how much better with me together? What is, *against me together*? With one counsel, one conspiracy. Christ, then, speaks to them: You agree against me, agree with me: why against me? why not with me? If you always had the "together," you would not divide yourselves into schisms. For the Apostle says: *I beseech you, brothers, that you all say the same thing, and that there be no schisms among you*[^36]. *Against me all my enemies whispered together: against me they devised evils for me.* Rather for themselves, because *they gathered iniquity to themselves*: but therefore for me, because they are to be weighed from their own mind. For it was not because they could do nothing, that they wished to do nothing. For the devil too desired to extinguish Christ, and Judas wished to kill Christ; but Christ being killed and rising again, we have been given life: yet to the devil and Judas the wage of an evil will is repaid, not of our salvation. For that you may know that each is to be weighed from his mind for the recompense of either reward or punishment, we find men to have said a good thing to someone, and such a good thing as we desire, and yet they were called revilers. When the Jews, seeing with the body, blind in heart, were being convicted by that man once blind, now enlightened both in body and in heart: that man now seeing said to them, *Do you too wish to be his disciples?* And they, says the Gospel, *reviled him, saying: You be his disciple*[^37]. May this befall us all, which they said by reviling. That reviling was called so from the ill-willing error of those saying it, not from any evil in the words: with what mind they said it, not what they said, did he attend, who narrated that they reviled. *Against me they devised evils for me.* And what evils to Christ, what evils to the martyrs? God turns all things to good.

**10.** [v. 9] *An iniquitous word they appointed against me*[^38]. What iniquitous word? Attend to the head itself: *Let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*[^39]. Fools! how will the inheritance be yours? because you have killed him? Behold, both you have killed him, and the inheritance will not be yours. *Does not he who sleeps add that he should rise again?*[^40] When you exulted that you had killed him, he slept: for he says also in another psalm, *I slept.* They raged, and wished to kill: *I slept.* For if I had been unwilling, I would not even have slept. *I slept*: because *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again*[^41]. *I slept, and took rest, and rose up*[^42]. Let the Jews, then, rage, let the earth be delivered into the hands of the ungodly, let the flesh be permitted to the hands of the persecutors, let them hang on the wood, transfix with nails, pierce with a lance: *Does not he who sleeps add that he should rise again?* Why did he sleep? Because Adam was a figure of him who was to come: and Adam slept, when Eve was made from his side. Adam in the figure of Christ, Eve in the figure of the Church: whence she was called the mother of the living. When was Eve fashioned? While Adam slept. When did the sacraments of the Church flow forth from the side of Christ? When he slept on the cross. *Does not he who sleeps add that he should rise again?*

**11.** [v. 10] And whence did he sleep? From that one who came in to see, and gathered iniquity to himself. *For the man of my peace, in whom I hoped, who ate my bread, has enlarged the heel against me*[^43]. He raised his foot against me: he wished to trample me. Who is this man of his peace? Judas. And in him Christ hoped, because he said, *in whom I hoped*? Did he not know him from the beginning? did he not know, before he was born, that he would be such? had he not said to all his disciples, *I have chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil*[^44]? How, then, did he hope in him, except because he himself is in his members, and what many faithful hoped about Judas, the Lord transfigured into himself? For when many who had believed in Christ saw Judas walk among the twelve disciples, some hoped in him; because he was such as the rest also: but Christ, because he was in his members hoping this, as he is in those hungering and thirsting; as he said *I was hungry,* so he said *I hoped.* Accordingly, if we say to him thus, Lord, when did you hope? as it was said to him, Lord, when were you hungry? as he there said to us, *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^45]; so he can say: When one of these least of mine hoped, I hoped. In whom did I hope? *The man of my peace, in whom I hoped, who ate my bread.* How did he point him out in the passion, from these words of prophecy? By the morsel he designated him, that it might appear it was said of him, *Who ate my bread*[^46]. Again, when he came to betray him, he gave him a kiss, that it might appear it was said of him, *The man of my peace.*

**12.** [v. 11] *But you, O Lord, have mercy on me*[^47]. This from the form of a servant, this from the form of the needy and poor. *For blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor. Have mercy on me, and raise me up, and I will repay them.* See, when it was said, it has now been done. For the Jews killed Christ, lest they lose their place: he being killed, they lost their place; uprooted from the kingdom, they were scattered. Raised up, he repaid them tribulation: he repaid it for admonition, not yet for damnation. For that city in which the people roared, like a lion seizing and roaring, crying out, *Crucify, crucify*[^48], the Jews being uprooted from it, has Christians, is inhabited by no Jew. The Church of Christ has been planted there, whence the thorns of the Synagogue were uprooted. Accordingly truly their fire blazed up, as in thorns: but the Lord was as green wood. This he himself said, when certain women bewailed Christ as about to die: *Do not weep over me, but over yourselves weep, and over your children*: from this foretelling: *Raise me up, and I will repay them. For if they do these things in the green wood, what will be done in the dry?*[^49] When could green wood be consumed by the fire of thorns? For they blazed up like fire in thorns. The fire consumes the thorns, and when applied to any green wood, is hardly kindled, for the moisture of the wood resists the slow and weak flame, yet one fit for consuming thorns. *And raise me up, and I will repay them.* Lest perhaps indeed, brothers, you think the Son less powerful than the Father, from the fact that he said, *Raise me up,* as though he himself could not raise himself. For he raised up that which could die: that is, the flesh died, the flesh was raised up. Yet lest you should think that God the Father of Christ could raise Christ, that is, the flesh of his Son; and Christ himself, although he is the Word of God equal to the Father, could not raise his own flesh: hear from the Gospel: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.* But this the evangelist, lest we should doubt even from this, *said,* he says, *of the temple of his body*[^50], *and raise me up, and I will repay them.*

**13.** [v. 12] *In this I have known that you willed me, because my enemy will not rejoice over me*[^51]. Because the Jews rejoiced, when they saw Christ crucified; they thought they had fulfilled their will to harm: they saw the accomplished fruit of their cruelty, Christ hanging on the cross; they shook the head: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^52]. He did not come down, who could: he was not demonstrating power, but teaching patience. For if, when they said these things, he had come down from the cross, he would have seemed, as it were, to yield to those insulting him, and would have been believed conquered, unable to bear reproaches: rather he remained on the cross while they insulted, fixed while they wagged. For therefore they wagged the head, because they did not cleave to the true head. He plainly taught us patience. For he did the stronger thing, who was unwilling to do what the Jews provoked. For it is much more powerful to rise from the tomb, than to come down from the cross. *Because my enemy will not rejoice over me.* They rejoiced, then, then: Christ rose again, Christ was glorified. They see now the human race converted in his name, now let them insult, now let them wag the head: rather now let them fix the head; or if they wag the head, let them wag it being amazed and wondering. For now they say: Is this perhaps he whom Moses and the prophets foretold? For of him they said: *Like a sheep he was led to be slain, and like a lamb before its shearer without voice, so he opened not his mouth: by his wounds we are healed*[^53]. For we see that this crucified one leads the human race after him; and not without cause did our fathers say, *Let us kill him, lest the world go after him*[^54]. Perhaps it would not go after him, if he had not been killed. *In this I have known that you willed me, because my enemy will not rejoice over me.*

**14.** [vv. 13-14] *But me, on account of my innocence, you have upheld*[^55]. Truly innocence: integrity without sin, repayment without debt, scourging without desert. *On account of my innocence you have upheld me, and confirmed me in your sight forever.* You confirmed me forever, you weakened me for a time: you confirmed me in your sight, you weakened me in the sight of men. What then? Praises to him, glory to him. *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel*[^56]. For he is the God of Israel, our God, the God of Jacob, the God of the younger son, the God of the younger people. Let no one say: This he said of the Jews, I am not Israel. Rather the Jews are not Israel. For the elder son, he is the elder people, reprobated; the younger, the beloved people. *The elder shall serve the younger*[^57], now it is fulfilled: now, brothers, the Jews serve us, they are as it were our book-carriers, they carry codices for us who study. Hear in what the Jews serve us, and not without cause. That elder brother Cain, who killed his younger brother, received a sign lest he be killed, that is, that the people itself may remain. Among them are the prophets and the Law; in which Law and in which prophets Christ was foretold. When we deal with the pagans, and show that this now comes about in the Church of Christ, which was foretold before about the name of Christ, about the head and body of Christ, lest they should think we have feigned those predictions, and from these things which have happened, as though they were to be, that we have written them, we bring forth the codices of the Jews. The Jews surely are our enemies, the adversary is convicted from the papers of an enemy. All things, then, the Lord distributed, all things he ordained for our salvation. He foretold before us, he fulfilled it in our time, and what he has not yet fulfilled, he will fulfill. And so we hold the repayer, that we may believe the debtor: because even those things which he has not yet given, he will give; just as those which he had not yet given, he has given. If anyone wishes to prove where they are written, let him read Moses and the prophets. If some enemy makes a din, and says: You have feigned the prophecies for yourselves; let the codices of the Jews be brought forth, because the elder shall serve the younger. There let them read these things foretold, which we now see fulfilled, and let us all say: *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, from age and unto age: and let all the people say: So be it, so be it.*
