# On the Same Psalm 58

**1.** Yesterday's discourse, drawn out at length, left me a debtor for today: because, then, the Lord willed it, the time for repaying has come. As devoted as we are in repaying, so eager ought you to be as exactors: that is, so that what he gives, and we repay (for he is the Lord, we are servants), you so receive that there may be fruit in your hearing from your life. For a cultivated field not yielding fruit, and, ungrateful to the farmer, bringing forth thorns instead of crops, seeks not the barn, but the fire. But the Lord our God, as you see him visit this earth with seasonal rains, so deigns to visit our heart with his word as his own field; and he seeks fruit from our heart, because he knows both what he sows there, and how much he rains. And because in truth we are nothing without him, because before we were, we were nothing, and whoever is now a man and wishes to be without him, will be nothing else than a sinful man; and it is true what was said here: *My strength I will keep toward you*, since whatever we can do, unless we keep it with him and toward him, by withdrawing we lose: our mind ought always to be watchful not to withdraw from him, but even if it was far off, more and more to draw near and approach; not by the step of the feet, not by the conveyance of vehicles, not by the swiftness of animals, not by the lifting of wings, but by purity of affections, and uprightness of holy ways.

**2.** [vv. 12-14] Let us see, then, what remains of this psalm. For at this point we had left off, when he had begun to speak of his enemies, saying to God: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law.* Although he said they were his enemies, yet he asked God that they not be killed and forget his law. Yet to hold the law, that is, not to forget the law, is already perfection, and as it were a security about the reward, and no anxiety about punishment. For there are those who hold the law in memory, and do not fulfill it in life: but those who fulfill it in life, cannot fail to hold it in memory. Therefore he who does the precepts of God in his conduct, and in a manner by living, always acts in his heart that it not be erased what he holds, and by living reminds himself what is written in his heart about the law of God, he himself fruitfully holds the law of God; he himself will not be reckoned an enemy. For behold the enemies, the Jews, whom this psalm seems to signify, hold the law of God, and therefore it was said of them: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law;* that the nation of the Jews might remain, and, it remaining, the multitude of Christians might grow. Through all the nations they certainly remain, and are Jews, nor have they ceased to be what they were: that is, that nation did not so pass into the laws of the Romans as to lose the form of the Jews; but is so subject to the Romans that it still holds its own laws, which laws are God's. But what has happened in them? *You tithe mint and cumin, and have left the weightier things of the Law, mercy and judgment; straining out the gnat, but swallowing the camel*[^1]. This the Lord says to them. And truly they are so; they hold the Law, they hold the Prophets; they read everything, they sing everything: the light of the Prophets they do not see there, which is Christ Jesus. Not only do they not now see him, when he sits in heaven: but neither did they see him then, when he walked humble among them, and they became guilty by pouring out his blood; but not all. This too today we commend to Your Charity. Not all: because many of them, converted to him whom they killed, and by believing in him, deserved pardon even for his poured-out blood; and they furnished an example to men how they ought not to distrust that any sin whatever be forgiven them, when even the killing of Christ was forgiven to those confessing. Hence, then, it was said: *Because God is my upholder; my God, his mercy will anticipate me*: that is, before all my good merits his mercy anticipates me; and even if it finds nothing good in me, he himself makes me good, and he himself justifies the converted, and he himself admonishes the averted to be converted. *My God*, he says again, *showed me concerning my enemies*: that is, how much he loves me, and how much he bestows on me of his goodness, he showed me from the comparison of my enemies; because, since out of one mass there are vessels of wrath and vessels of mercy, through the vessels of wrath the vessels of mercy learn how much good God bestows on them. And then: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law*: this was said of the Jews. But what will you do to them? *Scatter them in your power.* Show them that you are strong, not they who, by presuming on their own power, did not recognize your truth: not such strong ones as they are, of whom it was said: *Strong ones rushed in upon me;* but such strong one as you are to scatter them. *And lead them down, my protector, O Lord*: that is, so scatter them that you do not forsake them, *lest at some time they forget your law;* and in that very thing protect me, that from their scattering I may have the testimony of your mercy.

**3.** And there follows: *The offenses of their mouth, the word of their lips.*[^2] To what is this sentence joined, to what knit? *The offenses*, he says, *of their mouth, the word of their lips.* What follows is not so knit as to teach us to what this sentence is joined. *The offenses*, he says, *of their mouth, the word of their lips: and let them be caught in their pride. And from cursing and lying their consummations will be announced, in the wrath of consummation, and they will not be.* This obscurity we had spoken of yesterday too, and therefore we deferred the exposition to your fresher minds. Now, then, since you are not yet wearied in hearing, lift up your hearts that you may help me by your attention; lest perhaps in the obscurity and perplexity our discourse not suffice for your attention: and you ought to bring something from yourselves too, so that what we do not fulfill by speaking, you may supply by understanding. So, then, this sentence is set in the middle, that we do not easily see to what it is joined: *The offenses of their mouth, the word of their lips.* Let us run back, then, to the earlier things. Since he had said: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law*, whom however he had called enemies; he added two little verses: *Scatter them in your power, and lead them down, my protector, O Lord*: and at once he brought in: *The offenses of their mouth, the word of their lips,* that is, kill that, not them. *Do not*, then, *kill them, lest at some time they forget your law*: but there is what you may kill in them, so as to fulfill what was said above: *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity*[^3]. *Scatter*, then, *them, and lead them down*: that is, do not forsake them, when you scatter them; because by not forsaking them you have what to do in them, when you do not kill them. What, then, will you kill? *The offenses of their mouth, the word of their lips.* What will you kill in them? *Crucify, crucify*[^4]; what they cried out, not those who cried out. For they wished to blot out Christ, to slay him, to destroy him: but you, by raising up Christ whom they wished to destroy, kill *the offenses of their mouth, the word of their lips.* For him whom they cried out should be destroyed, they dread to be alive; and him whom they despised on earth, they marvel to be adored in heaven by all nations: so are killed the offenses of their mouth, and the word of their lips.

**4.** *And let them be caught in their pride.*[^5] What is, *let them be caught in their pride*? Because in vain the strong ones rushed in, and, as it were, it was granted them to seem to have done something for themselves, and to have prevailed against the Lord. They could crucify a man, weakness could prevail, and power be killed; and they seemed to themselves something, as it were strong, as it were powerful, as it were prevailing, as it were a lion ready for prey, as it were fat bulls, as he mentions them in another place: *Fat bulls beset me*[^6]. But what did they do to Christ? They killed not life, but death. For death being extinguished in the dying one, and life rising from death in the living one—for he himself too raised himself up, because there was in him that which could not die—what did they do? Hear what they did: they destroyed the temple. But what did he himself do? On the third day he raised it up. Through this, then, were killed the offenses of their mouth, and the word of their lips. And what now has been done in those who were converted? *And let them be caught in their pride.* For it was said to them that he whom they killed has risen again. They believed him to have risen, because they saw him set in heaven, and to have sent the Holy Spirit from there, and to have filled those who believed in him; and they found that they had both condemned nothing and done nothing. Their deed came to nothing; the sin remained. Since, then, the deed was made void, but the sin remained upon those doing it; they were caught in their pride, they saw themselves under their iniquity. It remained, then, that they confess the sin, and that he forgive who had yielded to those sinning, and grant his death, killed by the dead, and giving life to the dead. They were caught, then, in their pride.

**5.** *And from cursing and lying their consummations will be announced, in the wrath of consummation, and they will not be.*[^7] This too is hard to understand, to what is joined *and they will not be.* What will they *not be*? Let us see, then, the earlier text: when they have been caught in their pride, *their consummations will be announced from cursing and lying.* What are consummations? Perfectings: for to be consummated is to be perfected. It is one thing to be consummated, another to be consumed. For that is consummated which is so finished as to be perfected: that is consumed which is so finished as to be no more. Pride did not permit the man to be perfected; nothing so hinders perfection. Let Your Charity attend a little to what I say; and see an evil exceedingly troublesome, exceedingly to be guarded against. What evil do you think it? How long should I exaggerate what evil is in pride? The devil is to be punished for that alone. Surely he is the prince of all sins, surely he is the seducer to sinning: adultery is not imputed to him, not drunkenness, not fornication, not robbery of others' goods; by pride alone he fell. And since the companion of pride is envy, it cannot be that the proud man not envy: from this vice, which necessarily follows pride, he, fallen, envied the one standing, and gave his effort to seduce man, lest he be lifted up to that from which he was cast down. And therefore he is busy to persuade to true sins, because we have such a judge before whom he cannot lay false charges. For if our case were pleaded before a man as judge, whom he could deceive with false accusations; he would not labor much that we sin; because, by deceiving the judge, he could both oppress the innocent, and drag the circumvented over to himself, and make them be condemned with him: but now, because he knows such a judge who cannot be deceived, and because he knows that he, being just, cannot accept persons; he wishes to bring to him such guilty ones as it is necessary for him to condemn, because he is just. And so he gives his effort that we sin by envy alone, which envy must necessarily accompany pride. This evil, then, is pride, hindering perfection. Let each one, then, boast of riches, boast of beauty and of strength of body: these are all certainly mortal; those who boast of mortal things are to be laughed at, from which things they are very often either forsaken while alive, or, dead, must forsake them. That is the capital vice, that when anyone has made good progress, he is tempted by pride, so as to lose all that he has made progress in. Finally, all vices are to be feared in evil deeds; pride is more to be dreaded in good deeds. It is no wonder, then, that the Apostle is so humble that he says: *When I am weak, then I am strong.* For lest he himself be tempted by this vice, what medicine does he say was applied to him against the swelling, by a physician who knew what he was curing? *Lest by the greatness*, he says, *of the revelations I be lifted up, there was given me a thorn of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me. On account of which I asked the Lord three times, that it depart from me; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for power is made perfect in weakness*[^8]. See what consummations are. The Apostle, teacher of the Gentiles, father of the faithful through the Gospel, received a thorn of the flesh by which he was buffeted. Which of us would dare to say this, unless he were not ashamed to confess it? For if we say that Paul did not suffer this; while we as it were defer him honor, we make him a liar. But because he is truthful, and spoke truly; we must believe that an angel of Satan was given him, lest he be lifted up by the greatness of the revelations. Behold how the serpent of pride is to be feared! What, then, has happened to these men? They were caught in their sin, because they killed Christ, and from the very greatness of the sin they were more humbled, and by the greater humility deserved to be raised up: this is: *Let them be caught in their pride. And from cursing and lying their consummations will be announced*: that is, they will be the more perfected, because they were caught in cursing and lying. For pride did not permit them to be perfected: the crime took away the pride through their confession; pardon blotted out the crime through God's mercy, and from cursing and lying their consummations were announced: that is, it was said to the man: You have seen what you are, you have felt what you are, you erred, you were blinded, you sinned and fell, you have acknowledged your weakness; beseech the physician, do not seem to yourself healthy. Where is your frenzy? Behold, you killed the physician, whom by killing you could not destroy: yet, as far as concerned you, you killed. *From cursing and lying their consummations will be announced.* You did what pertained to cursing, O Jews: *For cursed* is *everyone who hangs on the wood*[^9]. You crucified Christ; you thought him as it were a curse. Add lying to cursing: you set guards at the tomb; that they might lie, you gave money. Behold, Christ has risen: where is the curse of the cross that you made? where is the lie of the guards whom you corrupted?

**6.** *From cursing and lying their consummations will be announced, in the wrath of consummation, and they will not be.*[^10] What is, *in the wrath of consummation their consummations will be announced*? There is a wrath of consummation, and there is a wrath of consuming. For all vengeance of God is called wrath: but sometimes God takes vengeance to this end, that he may perfect; sometimes he takes vengeance to this end, that he may condemn. How does he take vengeance to perfect? *He scourges every son whom he receives*[^11]. How does he take vengeance to condemn? When he has set the ungodly on the left, and said to them: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^12]. That is the wrath of consuming, not of consummation. But *their consummations will be announced in the wrath of consummation;* that is, it will be preached by the Apostles that *where sin abounded, grace superabounded*[^13], and the weakness of man pertained to the medicine of humbling. Those, thinking on this, and finding and confessing their iniquities, *will not be.* What will they *not be*? In their pride. For above he had said: *Let them be caught in their pride. And from cursing and lying their consummations will be announced in the wrath of consummation, and they will not be*, namely, in the pride in which they were caught.

**7.** *And they will know that God will have dominion over Jacob, and over the ends of the earth.*[^14] For before they seemed just to themselves, because the Jewish nation had received the Law, because it had kept God's precepts: it is shown to them that it did not keep them, since in those very precepts of God it did not understand Christ, because blindness in part has come upon Israel. The Jews themselves too see that they ought not to despise the Gentiles, whom they thought as it were dogs and sinners: for as they were found alike in iniquity, so alike will they come to salvation. *Not only for the Jews*, says the Apostle, *but also for the Gentiles*[^15]. For therefore *the stone which the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner*[^16], that it might join two in itself: for a corner couples two walls. The Jews seemed to themselves lofty and great: the Gentiles as it were weak, as it were sinners, as it were servants of demons, as it were adorers of idols; and yet in both was iniquity. The Jews too were shown to be sinners; because *there is none that does good, there is not even one*[^17]: they laid down pride and did not envy the salvation of the Gentiles, because they recognized their weakness equal to that of the others; and, coupled in the cornerstone, they adored the Lord together. *And they will know that God will have dominion over Jacob, and over the ends of the earth.* He will have dominion not only over the Jews, but also over the ends of the earth: which they would not know, if they were still in their pride; but they would be in their pride, if they still seemed just to themselves: but that they might not seem just to themselves, their consummations were announced to them from cursing and lying in the wrath of consummation; because they were caught in their pride, from the cursing which they did, when they killed Christ. Behold what our Lord Jesus Christ did. He died among the hands of the Jews, and redeemed the multitude of the Gentiles: with this his blood was poured out, with this it profited; but it profited all who were converted; because they too recognized him whom they killed, and from him deserved pardon for that very killing and their great offense.

**8.** [v. 15] What, then, of them too? What was said above: *They will be converted toward evening*[^18]: that is, even if late, that is, after the killing of our Lord Jesus Christ. *They will be converted toward evening; and they will suffer hunger like dogs.* But *like dogs*, not like sheep, or calves: *like dogs*, like Gentiles, like sinners; because they too recognized their sin who seemed just to themselves. Of whom it was said in another psalm: *Afterward they hastened*: this was said here: *Toward evening.* For there it is thus: *Their weaknesses were multiplied; afterward they hastened*[^19]. Whence did they *hasten afterward*? Because *their weaknesses were multiplied*: for if they still seemed healthy to themselves, they would never hasten. What, then, was said there: *Their weaknesses were multiplied;* this was said here: *Let them be caught in their pride, and from cursing and lying their consummations will be announced in the wrath of consummation.* And what was said there: *Afterward they hastened;* this was said here: *And they will not be*, in their pride. *And they will know that God will have dominion over Jacob, and over the ends of the earth:* and *they will be converted toward evening.* It is good, then, for the sinner to be humbled; and no one is more incurable than he who seems healthy to himself. *And they will go round the city.* Now yesterday too we expounded the city, that is, the city of encirclement, all the nations.

**9.** [v. 16] *They will be scattered that they may eat*[^20]: that is, that they may gain others, that they may transform believers into their own body. *But if they are not filled, they will also murmur.* Because above too he had spoken of their murmur, of those saying: *Because who has heard? And you, O Lord*, he says, *will deride them*, saying: *Who has heard?* Why? Because *you will hold all the nations as nothing.* So here too: *But if they are not filled, they will also murmur.*

**10.** [vv. 17-18] Let the Psalm be concluded. See the corner exulting, now rejoicing over each wall. The Jews were proud; they were humbled: the Gentiles were despairing; they were raised up. Let them come to the corner; there let them meet, there let them run together, there let them find the kiss of peace: let them come from different directions, but let them not come into opposition; those from the circumcision, these from the uncircumcision. The walls were far apart, but before they came to the corner: but in the corner let them hold to each other, and the whole Church now from each wall, what does it say? *But I will sing your power, and will exult in the morning at your mercy.*[^21] In the morning, the temptations being over, in the morning, when the night of this age has passed, in the morning, when we no longer dread the snares of robbers and of the devil and his angels, in the morning, when we no longer walk by the lamp of prophecy, but contemplate the very Word of God as a sun. *And will exult in the morning at your mercy.* Deservedly in another psalm it is said: *In the morning I will stand before you, and will contemplate*[^22]. Deservedly too the resurrection of the Lord himself was at dawn, that there might be fulfilled what was said in another psalm: *In the evening weeping will tarry, and in the morning exultation*[^23]. For at evening the disciples lamented our Lord Jesus Christ dead; at dawn they exulted at him rising. *I will exult in the morning at your mercy.*

**11.** *Because you have become my upholder, and my refuge in the day of my tribulation. My helper, to you I will play, because you, O God, are my upholder.*[^24] What was I, unless you had come to my aid? How despaired of was I, unless you had cured me? Where was I lying, unless you had come? Surely I was in danger from a huge wound, but that wound of mine required the almighty physician. To the almighty physician nothing is incurable; he does not give anyone up: it is needful that you wish to be cured, it is needful that you not flee his hand. But even if you are unwilling to be cured, your wound admonishes you to be cured: and it calls back the averted, and in a manner compels the fleeing to return to it, and draws him. In all things he fulfills what was said: *His mercy will anticipate me*[^25]. Think on what was said, *will anticipate me.* If you brought something of your own first, and by some good of your own first deserved God's mercy, he does not anticipate you. But when do you understand that you have been anticipated, unless you understand what the Apostle says: *For what have you that you did not receive? And if you have received it, why do you glory as though you had not received it*[^26]? This is, *His mercy will anticipate me.* Finally, attending that all the goods whatever we can have, whether in nature, or in upbringing, or in the very conduct, in faith, in hope, in love, in good morals, in righteousness, in the fear of God, are nothing except from his gifts, he so concluded: *My God, my mercy.* He did not find, filled with the goods of God, what to call his God, except his mercy. O name, under which no one is to despair! *My God*, he says, *my mercy.* What is, *my mercy*? If you say: My salvation, I understand that he gives salvation; if you say: My refuge, I understand that you flee to him; if you say: My strength, I understand that he gives you strength: *My mercy*, what is it? All, whatever I am, is from your mercy. But I merited you, by calling upon you! That I might be, what did I do? that I might be one who would call upon you, what did I accomplish? For if I did anything that I might be, I already was before I was. Further, if I was altogether nothing before I was, I merited nothing from you that I might be. You made me to be, and did you not make me to be good? You gave me to be, and could another give me to be good? If you gave me to be, and another gave me to be good; better is he who gave me to be good than he who gave me to be. Further, because no one is better than you, no one more powerful than you, no one more bountiful than you in mercy; from whom I received that I might be, from him I received that I might be good. *My God, my mercy.*
