# On the Same Psalm 88

**1.** Attend your mind to the rest of the psalm of which we spoke in the morning, and demand the pious debt; for he will render it through us, who made both us and you. The Lord Christ in the earlier parts of the Psalm was announced from the promise of God, and still in these words too which we are about to treat, he himself is announced. For of him, among other things, a little above it had been said: *And I will set him the firstborn, high above the kings of the earth. Forever I will keep for him my mercy, and my testament faithful to him. And I will set his seed forever and ever, and his throne as the days of heaven.* Of these and of all the things above, from the very beginning, we have said what we could.

**2.** [vv. 31-35.] But it follows: *If his sons forsake my law, and walk not in my judgments; if they profane my justifications, and keep not my commandments: I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their offenses with scourges. But my mercy I will not scatter from him, nor will I harm in my truth: nor will I profane my testament; and what proceeds from my lips I will not reject.* A great firmament of the promise of God. The sons of this David are the sons of the bridegroom: all Christians, then, are called his sons. But it is much that God promises, that *If* Christians—that is, *his sons forsake,* he says, *my law, and walk not in my judgments; if they profane my justifications, and keep not my commandments;* I will not despise them, nor leave them in perdition: but what shall I do? *I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their offenses with scourges.* Therefore it is not only the mercy of the one calling, but also of the one beating and scourging. Let the paternal hand, then, be over you, and if you are a good son, do not repel the discipline: for what son is there to whom his father does not give discipline? Let him give discipline, while he does not take away mercy; let him beat the stubborn one, while yet he renders the inheritance. If you have well recognized the promises of the father, do not fear to be scourged, but to be disinherited: for whom the Lord loves, he rebukes; and he scourges every son whom he receives[^1]. Does a sinful son disdain to be scourged, when he sees the only-begotten without sin scourged? *I will visit,* then, he says, *their iniquities with the rod.* So too the Apostle threatens: *What do you wish? shall I come to you with the rod*[^2]? Far be it that pious sons should say: If you are to come with the rod, do not come. For it is better to be instructed by the rod of the father, than to perish in the flattery of the robber.

**3.** *I will visit,* he says, *their iniquities with the rod, and their offenses with scourges. But my mercy I will not scatter from him.* From whom? From that David, namely, to whom I promised such things, whom I anointed with my holy oil above his fellows[^3]. Do you recognize him from whom God will not scatter his mercy? Lest perhaps someone, anxious, should say: Since he speaks of Christ, from whom he affirms that he will not scatter his mercy, what then of the sinner? For did he say this, But my mercy I will not scatter from them? *I will visit,* he says, *their iniquities with the rod, and their offenses with scourges.* You were waiting for, unto your security: *But my mercy I will not scatter from* them. And indeed some codices have this, but the more correct ones do not have it: yet even those that have it have nothing beside the point. For how does he not scatter his mercy from Christ? Did the Savior of the body sin either on earth or in heaven; who sits at his right hand, and intercedes for us[^4]? Yet from Christ: but from his members, from his body which is the Church. For he says it as something great that from him he does not scatter his mercy, as though we do not recognize the only-begotten Son who is in the bosom of the Father: for the man is not there reckoned for his own person, but God and man are one person. Therefore he does not scatter his mercy from him, when from his members, when from his body he does not scatter his mercy, in which he himself too on earth was suffering persecutions, when he was already sitting in heaven; and from heaven cried: *Saul, Saul,* not: Why do you persecute my servants; not: Why do you persecute my saints; not: Why do you persecute my disciples: but: *Why do you persecute me*[^5]? As, then, when no one was persecuting him sitting in heaven, he cried: *Why do you persecute me?* when the head recognized its members, and love did not separate the head from the framework of its body: so, when he does not scatter his mercy from him, surely from us he does not scatter it, who are his members and his body. Nor yet ought we therefore to sin securely, and perversely promise ourselves that whatever we do, we shall not perish. For there are certain sins and certain iniquities, of which to discuss and define is either impossible for us, or, if it were possible, surely too long for the time. For no one can say that he is without sin; because if he says it, he will lie: *For if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^6]. Therefore each one is necessarily scourged for his sins; but from him, if he is a Christian, the mercy of God is not scattered. Plainly, if you go into such great iniquities that you repel from yourself the rod of the one beating, if you repel the hand of the one scourging, and are indignant at the discipline of God, and flee from the Father beating, and refuse to bear him as Father, because he does not spare the sinner; you have estranged yourself from the inheritance, he himself has not cast you off: for if you remained scourged, you would not remain disinherited. *But my mercy,* he says, *I will not scatter from him, nor will I harm in my truth.* For to this end the mercy of the one freeing will not be scattered, that the truth of the one taking vengeance may not harm.

**4.** *Nor will I profane my testament; and what proceeds from my lips I will not reject.* Not because his sons sin, shall I therefore be found a liar: I promised, I do it. Suppose that they wish to sin even with despair, and so to go altogether into sins, that they offend the eyes of the Father, and deserve to be disinherited; is he not the God of whom it was said: *From these stones he will raise up sons to Abraham*[^7]? And so I say to you, brothers: many Christians sin tolerably, many are corrected from sin by the scourge, and are amended, and healed; many, altogether turned away, with a hard neck struggling against the discipline of the Father, and refusing altogether the very fatherhood of God, yet having the sign of Christ, go into such iniquities, that nothing can be done against them but to recite: *That those who do such things shall not possess the kingdom of God*[^8]. Yet on account of these Christ will not remain without an inheritance: not on account of the chaff will the wheat too perish[^9]; not on account of the bad fishes will nothing from that net be put into the vessels[^10]. The Lord knows who are his[^11]. For he promised securely, who predestined us before we were: *But whom he predestined, those he also called; and whom he called, those he also justified; and whom he justified, those he also glorified*[^12]. Let the desperate sin as much as they will: let the members of Christ answer: *If God is for us, who is against us*[^13]? Therefore God will not harm in his truth, nor will he profane his testament. His testament remains immovable, because he, foreknowing for himself, predestined heirs: and what proceeds from his lips, he will not reject.

**5.** [vv. 36-38.] Hear unto your confirmation, hear unto your security, if you recognize yourself to be among the members of Christ. *Once I have sworn in my holy one, if I lie to David.* Do you wait for God to swear again? How often will he swear, if by swearing once he lies? He had one oath for our life, who for us sent his Only-begotten to death. *Once I have sworn in my holy one, if I lie to David: his seed shall abide forever.* Forever his seed abides; because the Lord knows who are his. *And his seat as the sun in my sight, and as the moon perfected forever; and a witness in heaven faithful.* His seats, those over whom he has dominion, in whom he sits, in whom he reigns. If his seats, then his members too; because our members too are the seat of our head. See how all the other members of ours bear the head: but the head itself bears nothing over itself, but is itself borne by the rest of our members, so that the whole body of man is as it were the seat of the head. His seats, then, all in whom God reigns, shall be, he says, in my sight as the sun: because the righteous will shine in the kingdom of my Father as the sun[^14]. But the sun spiritually; not bodily, as that one shines from heaven, which he makes rise upon the good and the evil[^15]. Finally that sun is in the sight not of men alone, but even of cattle and the tiniest flies: for what of any most worthless animals does not see this sun? But of that sun what does he say properly? *As the sun in my sight.* Not in the sight of men, not in the sight of the flesh, not in the sight of mortal animals, but *in my sight. And as the moon*: but what moon? *Perfected forever.* For even though this moon which we know is perfected, on another day it begins to wane, after it has been perfected. *As the moon,* he says, *perfected forever*: so his seat will be perfected as the moon; but a moon perfected forever. If the sun, why also the moon? By the moon the Scriptures are wont to signify the mortality of this flesh, on account of its increase and decrease, on account of its transitory appearance. Finally Jericho too is interpreted "Moon": and surely a certain man who was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho fell among robbers[^16]; for he was going down from immortality to mortality. This flesh, then, is like the moon, which at every time and every month suffers increases and decreases: but this our flesh will be perfected in the resurrection; *and a witness in heaven faithful.* And so if we were perfected in soul alone, he would compare us only to the sun; again, if we were perfected in body alone, he would compare us only to the moon: but because God will perfect us both in soul and in body, according to the soul it was said, *as the sun in my sight;* because the soul none sees but God: *and as the moon,* now the flesh; *perfected forever,* in the resurrection of the dead: *and a witness in heaven faithful;* because all the things said of the resurrection of the dead were true. I beseech you, hear this again more plainly, and remember it: for I know that some have understood, but others perhaps still inquire what I have said. In no matter is the Christian faith so contradicted as in the resurrection of the flesh. Finally he who was born for a sign that will be contradicted[^17], raised even his own flesh, that he might meet the contradictor; and he who could so heal his members that they would not appear to have been wounded, kept the scars in his body, that he might heal the wound of doubt in the heart. In no matter, then, is the Christian faith so vehemently, so stubbornly, so obstinately and contentiously contradicted as concerning the resurrection of the flesh. For of the immortality of the soul many even of the philosophers of the Gentiles disputed many things, and left to memory in many and manifold books that the human soul is immortal: but when it comes to the resurrection of the flesh, they do not waver, but most openly contradict, and their contradiction is such, that they say it cannot be that this earthly flesh can ascend into heaven. Therefore this moon perfected forever, and against all contradictors a witness in heaven faithful.

**6.** [vv. 39-46.] These things were promised of Christ, how certain, how firm, how open, how undoubted. For even though some are covered with mysteries, yet some are so manifest, that from them the obscure are most easily opened. Since these things are so, see what follows: *But you have cast off, and brought to nothing, you have deferred your Christ. You have overturned the testament of your servant; you have profaned on the earth his holiness. You have destroyed all his walls; you have set his fortifications a terror. All who pass along the way have plundered him; he has become a reproach to his neighbors. You have exalted the right hand of his enemies; you have made glad all his enemies. You have turned away the help of his sword, and have not aided him in battle. You have loosed him from cleansing: his seat you have dashed to the earth. You have lessened the days of his seat; you have poured over him confusion.* What is this? All those things you promised; but you have done these contrary things. Where are the promises over which a little before we rejoiced, of which we so eagerly applauded, of which secure we gave thanks? As though one promised, another overturned. Which is to be wondered at, not another; but: *But you,* you who promised, you who confirmed, you who, on account of human doubt, even swore, you promised those things, and did these. Where shall I hold your oath, where shall I find your promise? What is this? would God indeed so promise a falsehood, or swear a falsehood? Why, then, did he promise those things, and do these? I say that he did these things, to confirm the promises. But who am I who say this? Let us see whether the truth says this; then what I say will not be empty. David had been set, to whom all these things were promised to be fulfilled in his seed, which is Christ. On account of the promises, then, which were said to David, men waited for them to be fulfilled in David. Accordingly, lest any Christian, when he said: He spoke of Christ; another should say: No, but he spoke of that David; and should err, when he saw all things fulfilled in David: he destroyed them in David, so that when you see them not fulfilled in him, which must needs be fulfilled, you may seek another in whom they may be shown fulfilled. So too of Esau and Jacob, we find the elder adored by the younger, since it is written: *The elder shall serve the younger*[^18]; so that when in those two preceding you see it not fulfilled, you may await two peoples, in whom may be fulfilled what God, not lying, deigns to promise. *Behold, from your seed,* he said to David, *I will set upon your seat*[^19]. He promised something forever from his seed; and Solomon being born, he became of such great wisdom, that the promise of God concerning the seed of David was supposed fulfilled in him: but Solomon fell, and gave place to hope in Christ; so that, since God could neither be deceived nor deceive, he set not his promise in him whom he knew to be about to fall, but after his fall you might look to God, and demand the promise. Therefore, O Lord, have you lied? Do you not fulfill what you promised? do you not show what you swore? Perhaps here God was about to say to you: I swore indeed and promised, but this man refused to persevere. What then? you, O Lord God, did you not foreknow that this man would not persevere? Surely you knew. Why then in one who would not persevere did you promise me what would be eternal? Did you not say: *If they forsake my law, and walk not in my judgments, and keep not my commandments, and profane my testament;* yet my promise shall abide, my oath shall be fulfilled? *Once I have sworn in my holy one,* within in a certain secret, in that very fountain whence the Prophets drank, who poured these things forth for us: *Once,* he says, *I have sworn, if I lie to David.* Show, then, what you swore, render what you promised. It was taken away from that David, lest it be awaited in that David. Await, then, what I promised.

**7.** David himself too knew that. For see what he says: *But you have cast off, and brought to nothing.* Where then is what you promised? *You have deferred your Christ.* Although he enumerates a certain sadness, yet in this word he refreshes us: it remains altogether, O God, what you promised; for your Christ you have not taken away, but *deferred.* To this David, then, in whom the ignorant hoped that God would fulfill his promises, see what happened, that the promises of God might be fulfilled, more firmly hoped in another: *You have deferred your Christ: you have overturned the testament of your servant.* For where is the old testament of the Jews? where is that land of promise, in which dwelling they sinned, which destroyed they left behind? You seek the kingdom of the Jews, it is not: you seek the altar of the Jews, it is not: you seek the sacrifice of the Jews, it is not: you seek the priesthood of the Jews, it is not. *You have overturned the testament of your servant; you have profaned on the earth his holiness.* Those holy things which they had, you showed to be earthly. *You have destroyed all his walls,* by which you had fortified him: for when would he be plundered, unless the walls were broken down? *You have set his fortifications a terror.* What is, *a terror?* That it may be said to those sinning: *If God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you*[^20]. *All who pass along the way have plundered him*: all the Gentiles, namely, passing along *the way*—that is, through this life—plundered Israel, plundered David. First see his pieces among all the nations: for of them it was said: *They shall be the portions of foxes*[^21]. For Scripture called the impious kings foxes, the crafty and timid whom another's strength terrifies. Therefore the Lord himself, when he spoke of the threatening Herod: *Tell,* he said, *that fox*[^22]. The king who fears no man, he himself is not a fox: that lion of the tribe of Judah, to whom it is said: *You have gone up, reclining, you have slept as a lion*[^23]. By power you went up, by power you slept; because you willed it, you slept. Therefore in another psalm he says: *I slept.* Was the sentence not full: *I slept, and took my sleep: and I rose up, because the Lord will accept me*[^24]? why was added: *I?* And it is to be pronounced with great weight: *I; I slept.* They raged, they persecuted, but if I had not willed, I would not have slept. *I slept.* Therefore of whom it was said: *They shall be the portions of foxes;* of these it is now said: *All who pass along the way have plundered him; he has become a reproach to his neighbors. You have exalted the right hand of his enemies; you have made glad all his enemies.* Attend to the Jews, and see all things fulfilled that were foretold. *You have turned away the help of his sword.* How were a few wont to fight, and to lay low many? *You have turned away the help of his sword, and have not aided him in battle.* Rightly conquered, rightly captured, rightly estranged from his kingdom, rightly scattered: for he lost that land, for which he killed the Lord. *You have turned away the help of his sword, and have not aided him in battle. You have loosed him from cleansing.* What is this? Among all the evils, this is a great terror. For however much God may beat, however much he be angry, however much he beat, scourge; yet let him scourge bound the one whom he cleanses, not loose him from cleansing: for if he looses from cleansing; now he has not one to be cleansed, but to be cast away. From what cleansing, then, is the Jew loosed? From faith. For from faith we live[^25]; and of faith it was said: *Cleansing their hearts by faith*[^26]: and because only the faith of Christ cleanses, by not believing in Christ they were loosed from cleansing. *You have loosed him from cleansing; his seat you have dashed to the earth*: rightly you have also broken it. *You have lessened the days of his seat*: they supposed they would reign forever. *You have poured over him confusion.* All these things befell the Jews, yet not with Christ taken away, but deferred.

**8.** [v. 47.] Let us see, then, whether God fulfills his promises. After these hard things which he recalled to have befallen that people and that kingdom, lest there God should be supposed to have fulfilled what he had promised, and should not give another kingdom in Christ, of which kingdom there will be no end; the Prophet addresses him, and says: *How long, O Lord, will you turn away unto the end?* For perhaps not unto the end even from them; because blindness in part has happened to Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in, and so all Israel might be saved[^27]. Meanwhile, however: *Your anger shall blaze like fire.*

**9.** [vv. 48-49.] *Remember what my substance is.* This David says, set in the flesh among the Jews, set in hope in Christ: *Remember what my substance is.* For not because the Jews failed, did my substance fail. For of that people is the Virgin Mary, of the Virgin Mary the flesh of Christ; and that flesh not sinful, but the cleanser of sinners: there, he says, is my substance. *Remember what my substance is.* For the root did not perish altogether; the seed will come to which the promise was made, ordained through angels in the hand of the Mediator[^28]. *Remember what my substance is: for you have not in vain established all the sons of men.* Behold, all the sons of men have gone into vanity; yet you established them not in vain. Since, then, all have gone into vanity, whom you established not in vain; whence would you cleanse them from vanity, did you keep nothing for yourself? That which you kept for yourself whence you might cleanse men from vanity, that holy one of yours, in him is my substance. For from him all are cleansed, whom you established not in vain from their own vanity: to whom it is said: *Sons of men, how long heavy of heart? why do you love vanity, and seek a lie?* Perhaps, made anxious, they would be converted from vanity, would find themselves stained by vanity, would seek whence to be cleansed; help, make them secure. *And know that the Lord has made his holy one marvelous*[^29]. He made his holy one marvelous; thence he cleansed all from vanity: there, he says, is my substance, remember it. *For you have not in vain established all the sons of men.* Therefore you kept something whence they might be cleansed. Who is this whom you kept? *What man is there who shall live, and not see death?* Therefore that man who shall live and not see death, he himself cleanses from vanity. For he did not establish all the sons of men in vanity, nor can he despise those whom he made, so as not to convert them and not cleanse them.

**10.** *What man is there who shall live, and not see death?* For rising from the dead he now dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^30]. Finally, since in another psalm we read it written: *You will not leave my soul in hell, nor will you give your Holy One to see corruption*[^31], the apostolic teaching takes up this testimony, and so disputes against the unbelieving in the Acts of the Apostles, saying: *Men, brothers, we know that the patriarch David died, and his flesh saw corruption;* therefore it was not said of him: *Nor will you give your Holy One to see corruption*[^32]. If, then, it was not said of him, of whom was it said: *What man is there who shall live, and not see death?* But perhaps there is none. Rather: *What man is there,* was said that you might inquire, not that you might despair. But perhaps there is some man *who shall live and not see death;* yet did he not say it of Christ, who died? There is no man at all who *shall live and not see death,* except him who has already died for mortals. For that you may know that he said it of him, see what follows. *What man is there who shall live, and not see death?* Was he then never in death? He was. How then *shall he live and not see death? He shall rescue his soul from the hands of hell.* Behold, truly alone, altogether singularly alone, *he shall live and not see death; he shall rescue his soul from the hands of hell*: for even though the rest of his faithful will rise from the dead, and they too will live forever, and not see death; yet they themselves will not rescue their soul from the hands of hell. He who rescued his soul from the hands of hell, he himself rescues the souls of his faithful; they themselves cannot rescue themselves. Prove that he himself rescued his soul. *I have power to lay down my soul, and power to take it up again. No one takes it from me;* because *I slept*[^33]: but *I lay it down of myself, and take it up again*[^34], because he himself it is who rescued his soul from the hands of hell.

**11.** [v. 50.] But even in the very faith of Christ there was some labor, and long the Gentiles, raging, said: *When will he die, and his name perish*[^35]? On account of these, then, now believing in Christ, but for some time about to labor, it follows and says: *Where are your ancient mercies, O Lord?* Now we have recognized Christ the cleanser, now we hold in whom you fulfill the promises; show in him what you promised. He himself is he who shall live and not see death, he himself is he who rescued his soul from the hands of hell: and yet we still labor. These things the martyrs said, whose birthdays we celebrate. He himself shall live and not see death, he himself rescued his soul from the hands of hell; and we for your sake are put to death all the day, we are reckoned as sheep for the slaughter[^36]: *Where are your ancient mercies, O Lord, which you swore to David in your truth?*

**12.** [v. 51.] *Remember, O Lord, the reproach of your servants.* Now with him living, now sitting in heaven at the right hand of the Father, reproaches were cast at Christians: long the Christians had a charge from Christ. That widow bearing children, and having children more abundant than she who had a husband[^37], heard ignominies, heard reproaches: but the Church, multiplied, stretching to the right and the left, is no longer mindful of the ignominy of her widowhood. *Remember, O Lord,* with whom is the abundance of the sweetness of your memory; *Remember,* do not forget. What: *Remember? Remember the reproach of your servants, which I have held in my bosom of many nations.* I was going, he says, to preach, and I heard reproaches, and I held them in my bosom; because I was fulfilling: *We are blasphemed, and we entreat; we have become as it were the offscourings of the world, the refuse of all*[^38]. Long the Christians held reproaches in their bosom, in their heart; nor dared they resist those reviling: before, when it seemed a crime to answer a pagan, now it is a crime to remain a pagan. Thanks to the Lord; he was mindful of our reproaches: he exalted the horn of his Christ, he made him marvelous among the kings of the earth. Now no one insults Christians, or if he insults, he does not insult publicly: he so speaks, that he fears more to be heard, than he wishes to be believed. *Which I have held in my bosom of many nations.*

**13.** [v. 52.] *With which your enemies have reproached, O Lord*: both Jews and pagans. *With which they have reproached*: what did they reproach? *The change of your Christ.* This they reproached, *the change of your Christ*: for this they cast up, that Christ died, that Christ was crucified. What do you cast up, you mad ones? Although now there is no one who casts it up; yet if any remain, what do you cast up that Christ died? He was not slain, but changed. He is called dead on account of the three days. Behold what your enemies reproached: not loss, not perdition: but plainly *the change of your Christ.* He was changed from temporal life to eternal, he was changed from the Jews to the Gentiles, he was changed even from earth to heaven. Let your vain enemies now go, and still reproach *the change of your Christ.* Would that they too were changed; they would not reproach the change of Christ. But the change of Christ displeases them, because they themselves refuse to be changed: for there is no change for them, and they did not fear God[^39]. *With which your enemies have reproached, the change of your Christ.*

**14.** [v. 53.] They reproached the change: but you, what? *The blessing of the Lord forever: so be it, so be it.* Thanks to his mercy; thanks to his grace. For we give thanks; we do not give, nor render, nor repay, nor recompense: we give thanks in words, we hold it in reality. He freely saved us, he did not regard our impieties; he sought us not seeking him, found us, redeemed us, freed us from the dominion of the devil and the power of demons: he bound us, to be cleansed by faith, whence he loosed those enemies who do not believe, and therefore cannot be cleansed. Let those who remain say daily whatever they wish, day by day they will remain fewer and fewer: let them cast up, mock, reproach, not the destruction, but *the change of your Christ.* Do they not see that when they say these things, they fail either by believing, or by dying? For their cursing is temporal; but *the blessing of the Lord is forever.* And to confirm the blessing, lest anyone fear: *so be it, so be it.* This is the subscription of the bond of God. Secure, then, of his promises, let us believe the past, recognize the present, hope for the future. Let not the enemy turn us away from the way, that he who gathers us as his chicks under his wings may cherish us; lest we stray from his wings, lest the airy hawk snatch the chicks still unfledged. For a Christian ought not to hope in himself: if he wishes to be firm, let him be nourished by the maternal warmth. For that is the hen gathering her chicks, of which it is reproached to that unbelieving city Jerusalem: *How often have I wished to gather your children as a hen her chicks under her wings, and you would not? Behold, your house will be left to you desolate*[^40]. Hence it was said: *You have set his fortifications a terror.* Since, then, those refused to be covered under the wings of this hen, and gave such an example, by which we ought to fear the fluttering unclean spirits seeking daily what they may snatch; let us enter under the wings of this hen of the divine Wisdom, since for the sake of her chicks she was made weak even unto death. Let us love the Lord our God, let us love his Church: him as a father, her as a mother; him as Lord, her as his handmaid; for we are the sons of his handmaid. But this marriage is bound together with great love: no one offends the one, and earns favor with the other. Let no one say: I go indeed to the idols, I consult the possessed and the soothsayers: but yet I do not leave the Church of God; I am a catholic. Holding the mother, you have offended the father. Another likewise says: Far be it from me; I do not consult the soothsayer, I do not seek the possessed, I do not seek sacrilegious divinations, I do not go to adore demons, I do not serve stones: but yet I am in the party of Donatus. What does it profit you that the Father is not offended, who avenges the offended mother? what does it profit if you confess the Lord, honor God, preach him, acknowledge his Son, confess him sitting at the right hand of the Father; and you blaspheme his Church? Do not the examples of human marriage correct you? If you had some patron, whom you daily attended, whose threshold you wore by serving, whom daily, I do not say you greeted, but even adored, to whom you rendered faithful service; if you said one charge against his wife, would you enter his house? Hold, then, dearly beloved, hold all of you with one mind God the Father, and the mother the Church. Celebrate the birthdays of the saints with sobriety, that we may imitate those who went before, and they may rejoice over you who pray for you; that *the blessing of the Lord forever* may remain upon you: *so be it, so be it.*
