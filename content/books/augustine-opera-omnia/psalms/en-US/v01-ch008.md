# Exposition of Psalm 8

**1.** [v 1.] *To the end, for the winepresses, a psalm of David himself.* He seems to say nothing about winepresses in the text of that psalm whose title this is. In which it appears that one and the same thing is often suggested in the Scriptures by many and various likenesses. Winepresses, then, we can take as the Churches, by the same reasoning by which we also understand the threshing floor as the Church. For whether on the threshing floor, or in the winepress, nothing else is done but that the fruits are cleansed from their coverings, which were necessary both that they might be born and that they might grow, and come to the maturity either of the harvest or of the vintage. By these coverings or supports, then, that is, by the chaff on the threshing floor the grains, and by the husks in the winepresses the wines, are stripped: just as in the Churches, by the multitude of worldly men, which is gathered together along with the good, and by which it was necessary, that they might be born and made fit for the divine word, that this is done, that by spiritual love, through the working of God's ministers, they be separated. For now it is done that the good are separated from the evil not by place, but for the time being by affection: although they live together in the Churches, as far as bodily presence is concerned. But there will be another time, when either the grains will be set apart into the barns, or the wines into the cellars. *The grains*, he says, *he will store in the barns, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire*[^1]. The same thing can be understood under another likeness thus: The wines he will store in the storerooms, but the husks he will cast to the cattle: so that we may be permitted to understand, by the likeness, the bellies of the cattle as the punishments of the lower regions.

**2.** There is another understanding of the winepresses, provided we do not depart from the signification of the Churches. For the divine Word too can be understood as a grape: for the Lord too was called a cluster of grapes, whom those who had been sent ahead by the people of Israel brought, hung on a piece of wood, from the land of promise, as though crucified. The divine Word, then, when by the necessity of utterance it employs the sound of the voice, by which it may be conveyed to the ears of hearers, by the same sound of the voice, as by husks, the understanding, as the wine, is enclosed: and so this grape comes into the ears, as into the treading-vats of the winepresses. For there it is discerned, so that the sound reaches as far as the ears; but the understanding is received by the memory of those who hear, as by a kind of vat, and thence passes into the discipline of conduct and the habit of mind, as from the vat into the cellars, in which, if negligence has not soured it, by age it will be made firm. For it soured in the Jews, and with this vinegar they gave the Lord to drink. For that wine which the Lord, of the generation of the vine of the New Testament, is to drink with his saints in the kingdom of his Father, must needs be most sweet and most firm.

**3.** The winepresses are also commonly taken as the martyrdoms, as though, by the affliction of persecutions, those who confessed the name of Christ being trodden, their mortal parts remained on the earth as husks, but their souls flowed out into the rest of the heavenly dwelling. But neither by this understanding is there a departure from the fruitfulness of the Churches. The psalm, then, is sung *for the winepresses*, for the establishment of the Church, when our Lord, after he rose again, ascended into the heavens. For then he sent the Holy Spirit, filled with whom the disciples preached the word of God with confidence, that the Churches might be gathered.

**4.** [v 2.] And so it is said: *Lord our Lord, how wonderful is your name in all the earth!* I ask, from what is his name wonderful in all the earth? The answer is given: *Because your magnificence is lifted up above the heavens*; so that this is the sense: Lord, who are our Lord, how all who dwell on the earth admire you! since your magnificence has been lifted up from earthly humility above the heavens. For from this it appeared whence you descended, when by some it was seen and by the rest believed whither you ascended.

**5.** [v 3.] *Out of the mouth of infants and nurslings you have perfected praise, because of your enemies.* I cannot take other infants and nurslings than those to whom the Apostle says: *As to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not food*[^2]. These were signified by those who went before the Lord, praising him; and the Lord himself used this testimony of them, when, the Jews saying that he should rebuke them, he answered: *Have you not read: Out of the mouth of infants and nurslings you have perfected praise?*[^3] And well he did not say: You have made; but, *you have perfected praise.* For there are in the Churches also those who are no longer given milk to drink, but feed on food; whom the same Apostle signifies, saying: *We speak wisdom among the perfect*[^4]: but the Churches are not perfected from these alone, because if they were alone, no provision would be made for the human race. But provision is made, when those too who are not yet capable of the knowledge of spiritual and eternal things are nourished by faith in temporal history, which, for our salvation, after the Patriarchs and Prophets, was administered by the most excellent Power and Wisdom of God even by the sacrament of the man he took up, in which there is salvation for everyone who believes: so that, moved by authority, one may serve the precepts, by which each one, purified and rooted and grounded in love, may be able to run with the saints, no longer a little one on milk, but a young man on food, to comprehend the breadth, length, height, and depth, and to know also the surpassing knowledge of the love of Christ.

**6.** *Out of the mouth of infants and nurslings you have perfected praise, because of your enemies.* As enemies to this dispensation which was made through Jesus Christ, and him crucified, we ought generally to take all who forbid believing things unknown, and promise certain knowledge; as all heretics do, and those who in the superstition of the gentiles are called philosophers. Not that the promise of knowledge is to be blamed; but that they think the most wholesome and necessary step of faith is to be neglected, by which one must ascend to something certain, which can be nothing but eternal. Hence it appears that they do not even have that knowledge which, despising faith, they promise; because they are ignorant of its so useful and necessary step. *Out of the mouth*, then, *of infants and nurslings* our Lord perfected *praise*, commanding first through the Prophet: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^5], and himself, present, saying: *Blessed are those who have not seen and will believe*[^6]. *Because of your enemies*: against whom also that is said: *I confess to you, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise, and revealed them to little ones*[^7]. For he said, from the wise, not those who are wise, but those who think themselves to be. *That you may destroy the enemy and the defender.* Who, but the heretic? For he himself too is both enemy and defender, who, while he assails the Christian faith, seems to defend it. Although the philosophers of this world too are well understood as enemies and defenders: since the Son of God is the Power and Wisdom of God, by which everyone is enlightened who is made wise by truth. Of this they profess themselves lovers, whence they are also called philosophers: and therefore they seem to defend it, while they are its enemies; since they do not cease to persuade harmful superstitions, that the elements of this world be worshiped and venerated.

**7.** [v 4.] *For I will see your heavens, the works of your fingers.* We read that the Law was written by the finger of God, and given through Moses his holy servant: which finger of God many understand as the Holy Spirit. For which reason, if by the fingers of God we rightly take those same ministers filled with the Holy Spirit, on account of the Spirit himself who works in them, since through them all divine Scripture was composed for us; we fittingly understand in this place the books of both Testaments to be called heavens. Now it was said even of Moses himself by the magi of King Pharaoh, when they had been overcome by him: *This is the finger of God*[^8]; and what is written: *The heaven will be folded like a book*[^9], even if it was said of this ethereal heaven, yet by this same likeness the books are fittingly named heavens in the allegory. *For I will see*, he says, *the heavens, the works of your fingers*: that is, I will discern and understand the Scriptures, which, by the working of the Holy Spirit, you composed through your ministers.

**8.** And so the heavens named above too can be taken as the same books, where he says: *Because your magnificence is lifted up above the heavens*; so that this is the whole sense: Because your magnificence is lifted up above the heavens: For your magnificence exceeds the eloquence of all the Scriptures; out of the mouth of infants and nurslings you have perfected praise, that those who desire to come to the knowledge of your magnificence, which is lifted up above the Scriptures (because it passes and surpasses the proclamations of all words and tongues), might begin from faith in the Scriptures. God, then, inclined the Scriptures down to the capacity of infants and nurslings, as in another psalm it is sung: *And he inclined the heaven, and came down*[^10]. And this he did because of his enemies, who, being through the pride of their loquacity enemies of the cross of Christ, even when they say some true things, yet cannot profit the little ones and nurslings. Thus is destroyed the enemy and defender, who, whether he seems to defend wisdom or even the name of Christ, yet, from this step of faith, assails that truth which he most readily promises. Therefore he is convicted of not having it either; because, by assailing its step, which is faith, he is ignorant how one ascends to it. Hence, then, is destroyed the rash and blind promiser of truth, who is enemy and defender, when the heavens are seen as the works of God's fingers, that is, when the Scriptures are understood, brought down even to the slowness of infants, and, by the humility of historical faith, which was enacted in time, raise to the sublimity of the understanding of eternal things those whom they have well nourished and strengthened in the things they confirm. For these heavens, that is, these books, are the works of God's fingers; for they were composed by the Holy Spirit working in the saints—for those who attended to their own glory rather than to the salvation of men spoke without the Holy Spirit, in whom are the bowels of God's mercy.

**9.** *For I will see the heavens, the works of your fingers, the moon and the stars which you have founded.* The moon and the stars are founded in the heavens: because both the universal Church, in the signification of which the moon is often put, and the Churches in particular places one by one, which I think are suggested by the name of stars, are placed in these same Scriptures, which we believe are put under the term of heavens. But why the moon rightly signifies the Church will be more opportunely considered in another psalm, where it was said: *Sinners have bent the bow, that they may shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart*[^11].

**10.** [v 5.] *What is man, that you are mindful of him; or the son of man, that you visit him?* What difference there is between man and the son of man can be asked; for if there were no difference, it would not be put thus, *man, or the son of man*, by disjunction. For if it had been written thus: What is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you visit him: it would seem a repetition of what was said, *man*; but now, when it sounds, *man, or the son of man*, a clearer difference is suggested. This indeed is to be held, that every son of man is a man, although not every man can be understood as a son of man: for Adam was a man, but not a son of man. For which reason it is now permitted to attend and discern what in this place differs between man and son of man: that those who bear the image of the earthly man, who is not a son of man, are signified by the name of men; but those who bear the image of the heavenly man are rather called sons of men. For the one is called the old man, the other the new: but the new is born from the old, since spiritual regeneration is begun by a change of the earthly and worldly life; and therefore this one is named son of man. Man, then, in this place is the earthly one, but son of man the heavenly: and the former far sundered from God, but the latter present to God: and therefore he is mindful of the former, as one set far off; but he visits the latter, whom, present, he illumines with his countenance: for *Salvation is far from sinners*[^12], and: *The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord*[^13]. So in another psalm he says that men associated with beasts are saved, not by present inner illumination, but by the multiplication of God's mercy, by which his goodness extends even to the lowest, together with the very beasts; since the salvation of carnal men is carnal, like that of cattle. But the sons of men, sundering them from those whom he joined as men to beasts, he proclaims are made blessed in a far more sublime way, by the illumination of the truth itself, and by a kind of flooding of the living fountain. For thus he says: *Men and beasts you will save, Lord; as your mercy is multiplied, O God. But the sons of men will hope under the protection of your wings. They will be made drunk with the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delights. For with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light. Extend your mercy to those who know you*[^14]. He is mindful, then, of man by the multiplication of mercy, as of beasts; because multiplied mercy reaches even those set far off: but he visits the son of man, to whom, set under the protection of his wings, he extends mercy, and in his light gives light, and gives him to drink of his delights, and makes him drunk with the abundance of his house, to forget the troubles and errors of his past conduct. This son of man, that is, the new man, the penance of the old travails with in pain and groaning. He, though new, is yet still called carnal, while he is nourished with milk: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal*, says the Apostle; and to show that they were already regenerated: *As to little ones*, he says, *in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not food.* This one, when he slips back, which often happens, to the old life, hears with reproach that he is a man: *Are you not men*, he says, *and walk according to man?*[^15]

**11.** [vv 6.7.] The son of man, then, was first visited in that man, the Lord, born of the Virgin Mary. Of whom, on account of that very weakness of flesh which the Wisdom of God deigned to bear, and the humility of the passion, it is rightly said: *You have made him a little less than the angels.* But there is added that glorification by which, rising again, he ascended into heaven: *With glory*, he says, *and honor you have crowned him; and you have set him over the works of your hands.* Since even the angels are works of God's hands, we take the only-begotten Son to be set even over the angels, whom we hear and believe to have been made a little less than the angels by the humility of carnal generation and of the passion.

**12.** [vv 8.9.] *All things*, he says, *you have subjected under his feet.* He excepts nothing, when he says *all things.* And lest it be permitted to understand it otherwise, the Apostle bids us so believe, when he says, *except him who subjected all things to him*[^16]; and he uses this very testimony of this psalm to the Hebrews, when he so wishes all things to be understood as subjected to our Lord Jesus Christ, that nothing is excepted. And yet he seems to add nothing great, as it were, when he says: *All the sheep and oxen, moreover also the cattle of the field; the birds of the air, and the fish of the sea, that traverse the paths of the sea.* For he seems, leaving aside the heavenly Virtues and Powers and all the armies of the angels, leaving aside even men themselves, to have subjected to him only cattle; unless we understand the sheep and oxen to be holy souls, either giving the fruit of innocence, or even working that the earth may be fruitful, that is, that earthly men may be regenerated to spiritual fruitfulness. These holy souls, then, we ought to take as not only of men, but also of all angels, if we wish from this to understand that all things are subjected to our Lord Jesus Christ; for no creature will be unsubjected, to which the chief, so to speak, spirits are subjected. But whence shall we prove that the sheep can be taken also as those sublimely blessed, not men, but spirits of the angelic creation? Perhaps from the fact that the Lord says he left ninety-nine sheep in the mountains, that is, in the higher places, and came down on account of one. For if we take the one fallen sheep to be the human soul in Adam (because Eve too was made from his side, all of which are not now the time to treat and consider spiritually), it remains that the ninety-nine left in the mountains be understood not as human, but as angelic spirits. For of the oxen the explanation of this sentence is easy; since men themselves are called oxen for no other reason than that by evangelizing the word of God they imitate the angels, where it was said: *You shall not muzzle the ox that treads out the grain*[^17]. How much more easily, then, do we take the angels themselves, messengers of truth, as oxen; when the Evangelists, by sharing their name, were called oxen? *You have subjected*, then, he says, *all the sheep and oxen*, that is, every holy spiritual creature: in which we also take the holy men who are in the Church, namely in those winepresses which were suggested under another likeness of the moon and stars.

**13.** *Moreover*, he says, *also the cattle of the field.* In no way is it idle that *moreover* is added. First, because the cattle of the field can be understood as both sheep and oxen; so that if the cattle of crags and steep places are goats, the sheep are well understood as the cattle of the field. And so, even if it had been put thus: All the sheep and oxen and the cattle of the field, it would rightly be asked what the cattle of the field meant, since the sheep and oxen too can be understood as this; but the fact that *moreover* too is added compels us altogether to recognize some difference. But under this word that is put, *moreover*, not only the cattle of the field, but also the birds of the air, and the fish of the sea, that traverse the paths of the sea, are to be taken. What, then, is this difference? Let there come to mind the winepresses, having husks and wine; and the threshing floor, containing chaff and grain; and the nets, in which are enclosed good fish and bad; and Noah's ark, in which were both unclean and clean animals: and you will see the Churches, in the meantime in this time until the last time of judgment, to contain not only sheep and oxen, that is, holy laymen and holy ministers, but *moreover also the cattle of the field, the birds of the air, and the fish of the sea, that traverse the paths of the sea.* For the cattle of the field are most fittingly taken as men rejoicing in the pleasure of the flesh, where they ascend nothing steep, nothing toilsome. For the field is also the broad way that leads to destruction; and in a field Abel is slain. Wherefore it is to be feared, lest anyone descending from the mountains of God's righteousness (*For your righteousness*, he says, *is like the mountains of God*[^18]), choosing the broad and easy ways of carnal pleasure, be slaughtered by the devil. See now also the birds of the air, the proud, of whom it is said: *They have set their mouth against heaven*[^19]. See how high they are carried by the wind, who say: *We will magnify our tongue, our lips are our own, who is our Lord?*[^20] Behold also the fish of the sea, that is, the curious who traverse the paths of the sea, that is, inquire in the depth of this world into temporal things, which, like paths in the sea, vanish and perish as quickly as the water is again merged, after it has given place to the passing of ships, or of any walking or swimming. For he did not say only: They walk the paths of the sea; but, *they traverse*, showing the most stubborn zeal of those who seek out vain and fleeting things. Now these three kinds of vices, that is, pleasure of the flesh, and pride, and curiosity, include all sins. Which seem to me to be enumerated by John the Apostle, when he says: *Do not love the world, for all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the world*[^21]. For through the eyes especially curiosity prevails; but to what the rest pertain is manifest. And that threefold temptation of the Lord as man: through food, that is, through the desire of the flesh, where it is suggested: *Tell these stones to become bread*[^22]: through empty boasting, where, set on a mountain, all the kingdoms of this earth are shown, and promised if he will worship: through curiosity, where from the pinnacle of the temple he is urged to cast himself down, to test whether he will be received by the angels. And so, after the enemy could prevail with him by none of these temptations, this is said of him: *After the devil had completed every temptation*[^23]. On account of the signification of the winepresses, then, there were subjected under his feet not only the wines, but also the husks: not only the sheep and oxen, that is, the holy souls of the faithful, whether in the people or in the ministers; but moreover also the cattle of pleasure, and the birds of pride, and the fish of curiosity: all which kinds of sins we now see mixed with the good and holy in the Churches. Let him, then, work in his Churches, and separate the wine from the husks: let us strive to be wine and sheep or oxen; not husks, or cattle of the field, or birds of the air, or fish of the sea that traverse the paths of the sea. Not that these names can be understood and explained in this way only, but according to the places; for elsewhere they signify another thing. And this rule is to be held in every allegory, that what is said by likeness be considered according to the sense of the present place; for this is the Lord's and the apostolic teaching. Let us, then, repeat the last verse, which is also put at the beginning of the psalm, and praise God, saying: *Lord our Lord, how wonderful is your name in all the earth!* Fittingly indeed, after the text of the discourse, there is a return to the head, to which the whole same discourse is to be referred.
