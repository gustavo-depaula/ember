# Exposition of Psalm 13

**1.** [v 1.] What *to the end* is, is not to be more often repeated. *For Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^1], as the Apostle says. We believe in him, when we begin to enter upon the good way; we shall see him, when we have arrived: and therefore he is the end.

**2.** *The imprudent one has said in his heart: There is no God.* For not even those sacrilegious and detestable philosophers, who hold perverse and false views about God, have dared to say, There is no God: and therefore, *He has said in his heart*; because no one dares to say this, even if he has dared to think it. *They are corrupted and have become abominable in their affections*: that is, while they love this world, and do not love God: these are the very affections that corrupt the soul, and so blind it, that the imprudent one can even say in his heart: *There is no God;* for, as they did not approve of having God in their knowledge, God gave them up to a reprobate sense. *There is none who does goodness, there is none even to one. Even to one*: either it can be understood with that one included, so that no man is understood: or except one, so that the Lord Christ is taken; as we say, This field is even to the sea; we certainly do not reckon in the sea along with it. And this is the better understanding, that no one is understood to have done goodness up to Christ; because no man can do goodness, unless he himself has shown it: and that is true; because until anyone knows the one God, he cannot do goodness.

**3.** [v 2.] *The Lord looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see if there is one understanding or seeking God.* It can be understood of the Jews, so that he called them more honorably sons of men, on account of the worship of the one God, in comparison with the gentiles, of whom I think it was said above: *The imprudent one has said in his heart, There is no God*, and the rest. But the Lord looks down, that he may see through his holy souls: which is signified by what was said, *from heaven*; for through himself nothing is hidden from him.

**4.** [v 3.] *All have turned aside, together they have become useless*: that is, the Jews have become such as the gentiles too, of whom it was said above. *There is none who does good, there is none even to one*: to be understood similarly as above. *Their throat is an open tomb.* Either the voracity of the gaping gullet is signified; or, in allegory, those who slay and as it were devour the slain, those whom they persuade to the perversity of their own ways. To which is similar, on the contrary, what was said to Peter, *Kill and eat*[^2]; that he might convert the gentiles to his own faith and good ways. *With their tongues they were acting deceitfully*: flattery is the companion of the voracious and of all the evil. *The venom of asps is under their lips*: by *venom* he means deceit; by *of asps*, because they refuse to hear the precepts of the law, as asps refuse to hear the words of the charmer, which is said more evidently in another psalm. *Whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness*: this is the *venom of asps. Their feet are swift to shed blood*: by the habit of doing evil. *Contrition and unhappiness are in their ways*: for all the ways of evil men are full of labors and misery; therefore the Lord cries: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke, and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble of heart. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light*[^3]. *And the way of peace they have not known*: this surely which the Lord, as I said, recalls, in the easy yoke and light burden. *There is no fear of God before their eyes*: these do not say, There is no God; but yet they do not fear God.

**5.** [v 4.] *Will they not know, all who work iniquity?* he threatens judgment. *Who devour my people as the food of bread*: that is, daily; for the food of bread is daily. But they devour the people who take their own advantages from them, not referring their ministry to the glory of God, and to the salvation of those over whom they preside.

**6.** [v 5.] *They have not called upon the Lord*: for he does not truly call upon him who desires those things which displease him. *There they trembled with fear, where there was no fear*: that is, in the loss of temporal things. For they said: *If we leave him thus, all will believe in him, and the Romans will come, and take away from us both our place and our nation*[^4]. They feared to lose an earthly kingdom, where there was no fear; and they lost the kingdom of heaven, which they ought to have feared: and this is to be understood of all temporal advantages, the loss of which, when men fear it, they do not come to eternal things.

**7.** [v 6.] *Because God is in the righteous generation*: that is, he is not in those who love the world. For it is unrighteous to forsake the founder of the ages, and to love the world, and to serve the creature rather than the Creator. *You have confounded the counsel of the needy one, because the Lord is his hope*: that is, you have despised the humble coming of the Son of God, because in it you did not see the pomp of the world: so that those whom he called might place their hope in God alone, not in things that pass away.

**8.** [v 7.] *Who will give from Zion the salvation of Israel?* is understood, but he whose humility you despised? For he himself will come in brightness to the judgment of the living and the dead, and to the kingdom of the righteous; so that, since by that humble coming blindness came in part upon Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in, in that other there may come about what follows, and so all Israel might be saved: for of the Jews the Apostle takes also that testimony of Isaiah, which was said, *He will come from Zion who will turn away impiety from Jacob*[^5]; as here it is put, *Who will give from Zion the salvation of Israel? When the Lord will have turned away the captivity of his people, Jacob will exult, and Israel will be glad*: it is a repetition, as is the custom; for I think *Israel will be glad* is the same as *Jacob will exult.*

[^1]: Rom 10:4
[^2]: Acts 10:13
[^3]: Matt 11:28-30
[^4]: John 11:48
[^5]: Rom 11:26; Isa 59:20
