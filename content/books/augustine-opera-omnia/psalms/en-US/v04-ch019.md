# Exposition of Psalm 90

**1.** This is the psalm with which the devil dared to tempt our Lord Jesus Christ. Let us hear it, then, that, instructed, we may be able to resist the tempter, not presuming in ourselves, but in him who was first tempted, lest we be overcome in temptation. For to him temptation was not necessary: the temptation of Christ is our teaching. But if we attend to what he answered the devil, that we too may answer this when he tempts us similarly, we enter through the door, as you heard the reading of the Gospel. For what is it to enter through the door? To enter through Christ. For he himself said: *I am the door*[^1]. But what is it to enter through Christ? To imitate the ways of Christ. In what shall we imitate the ways of Christ? in that magnificence by which God was in the flesh? Or does he exhort us to this, or exact this from us, that we do such miracles as he himself did? Or indeed does our Lord Jesus Christ not, both now and always with the Father, govern the whole world? And does he call man, making him his imitator, even to this, that he govern with him heaven and earth, and all things that are in them; or that he too be a creator, through whom all things are made, as through Christ all things were made? God our Savior the Lord Jesus Christ does not invite you to these works, which he did from the beginning, of which it is written: *All things were made through him*[^2], nor to those which he did on earth. He does not say this to you: You will not be my disciple, unless you walk on the sea[^3], or unless you raise a four-days-dead man[^4], or unless you open the eyes of one born blind[^5]. Not this either. What is it, then, to enter through the door? *Learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart*[^6]. What he was made for your sake, this you ought to attend to in him, that you may imitate it. For he did miracles even before he was born of Mary: for who ever did them, except he of whom it was said: *Who alone does great wonders*[^7]? For it was in his power that even those before who did them could do something: in the power of Christ Elijah raised the dead[^8]. Or is Peter perhaps greater than Christ, because Christ raised the sick by his voice[^9], but Peter, when he passed by, the sick were brought out to be touched by his shadow[^10]? Is Peter, then, more powerful than Christ? Who, most demented, would say this? Why, then, so great power in Peter? Because Christ was in Peter. Therefore he said: *All who came are thieves and robbers*[^11]: that is, those who came of their own accord, were not sent by me, who came without me, in whom I was not, whom I did not lead in. As many miracles, then, as were done, whether by those preceding, or by those following, the same Lord did them, who did even those done by his presence. Not to these miracles, then, does he exhort, which he himself did even before he was man: but to what does he exhort you? To imitate what he could not, except being made man. For could he endure sufferings, except as a man? To die and be crucified and be humbled he could not, except as a man. So, then, you too, when you suffer the troubles of this world, which the devil makes, whether openly through men, or secretly as with Job, be strong, be enduring; you will dwell in the help of the Most High, as this psalm says: for if you withdraw from the help of the Most High, not being able to help yourself, you will fall.

**2.** For many are strong, when they suffer persecution from men, and see them openly raging against them; and they suppose that then they imitate the sufferings of Christ, if men openly persecute them: but if they are struck by the hidden persecution of the devil, they suppose that they are not crowned by Christ. Do not fear when you imitate Christ. For even when the devil tempted the Lord, no man was in the desert, he tempted him secretly; but he was overcome, and openly raging he was overcome[^12]. So you too, if you wish to enter through the door, when the enemy secretly tempts, when he asks for a man that he may harm him something through bodily troubles, through fevers, through sicknesses, through some labors of the body, as Job labored. He did not see the devil, but he understood the power of God. He knew that the devil could do nothing against him, unless he were permitted by him whose is the supreme power: he gave all the glory to God, he did not give the power to the devil. For even when the devil took away all things, he said this: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away*[^13]. He did not say: The Lord gave, the devil has taken away: because the devil would have taken away nothing, unless the Lord had permitted. But the Lord permitted, that the man might be tested, the devil overcome. And when the plague struck him, he permitted it. For when from head to foot he flowed in the rottenness of worms, not even then did he attribute any power to the devil: but when his wife, whom alone the devil had left, not as a consoler of her husband, but as a helper of himself, suggested to him, and said to him: *Say some word against God, and die;* he said to her: *You have spoken as one of the foolish women: if we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure evil things*[^14]?

**3.** [vv. 1-2.] Therefore he who so imitates Christ, that he endures all the troubles of this world, let his hope be in God, that he be neither caught by enticement, nor broken by fear, he is the one *who dwells in the help of the Most High, and shall abide in the protection of the God of heaven*: as you heard the psalm, and sang; for thence the Psalm begins. But with what words the devil tempted the Lord, when we come to them, you will recognize: for they are known. *He shall say to the Lord: You are my upholder, and my refuge, my God.* Who says this to the Lord? *He who dwells in the help of the Most High.* Who is *he who dwells in the help of the Most High?* He who does not dwell in his own help. Who is *he who dwells in the help of the Most High?* He who is not proud, like those who ate that they might be as gods, and lost what they had been made, immortal men. For they wished to dwell in their own help, not in the help of the Most High: therefore they heard the suggestion of the serpent, despised the precept of God; and found that this befell them which God threatened, not what the devil promised[^15].

**4.** [v. 3.] Therefore you too say so: *I will hope in him; for he will rescue me,* not I myself. See whether he teaches anything else, except that our whole hope be not in ourselves, be not in man. Whence will he rescue you? *From the snare of the hunters, and from the harsh word. From the snare of the hunters,* it is something great: *from the harsh word,* what is great? Many have fallen into the snare of the hunters through the harsh word. What is it that I say? The devil and his angels lay snares, as hunters lay snares; and far from those snares walk the men who walk in Christ: for he does not dare to lay a snare in Christ; he sets it around the way, not in the way. But let Christ be your way, and you will not fall into the snare of the devil. To one straying from the way, there already is the snare. Here and there he sets nooses, here and there he sets the snare; among the nooses you walk. But do you wish to walk securely? Do not turn aside to the right or to the left; and let him be your way who was made for you the way[^16], that he may bring you to himself through himself; and you will not fear the nooses of the hunters. But what is, *from the harsh word?* Many through the harsh word the devil has sent into the snare: for example, those who wished to be Christians among the pagans, suffer the insulting pagans; they blush among the insulters, and withdrawing from the way at the harsh word, they fall into the nooses of the hunters. And what will the harsh word do to you? Nothing. Will the noose, to which the enemy drives you through the harsh word, do nothing to you? As nets are often stretched, they are stretched at the top of a hedge for the birds, and stones are thrown into the hedge: those stones will do nothing to the birds. For when does he who throws a stone into the hedge strike the bird? But the bird, fearing the empty noise, falls into the nets: so men, fearing the vain and empty words of the insulters, and blushing at the superfluous reproaches, fall into the nooses of the hunters, and are taken captive by the devil. But why do I not say, brothers, what is not to be kept silent, what God compels me to say? However you take this, God compels me to say it; and if I do not say it, I myself fall into the nooses of the hunters: for if I fear the disparagements of men, so as not to say it; I myself, at the harsh word, fall into the nooses of the hunters, I who admonish you not to fear the words of men. What is it, then, that I am about to say? As he who is a Christian among pagans hears from the pagans harsh words, at which if he blushes, he falls into the snare of the hunters: so among Christians those who wish to be more diligent and better, will hear insults from the Christians themselves. And what does it profit, brother, that you sometimes find a city where there is no pagan? No one there insults a Christian because he is a Christian, because no pagan is found there: but there are many badly living Christians, among whom he who wishes to live well, and to be sober among the drunken, and chaste among the fornicators, and to worship God sincerely among the consulters of astrologers and to seek nothing of the kind, and among the spectators of the trifling theaters to be unwilling to go except to church, suffers the very Christians as insulters, and suffers harsh words; and they say: You are great, righteous, you are Elijah, you are Peter, you have come from heaven. They insult; wherever he turns himself, he hears here and there the harsh word. But if he fears and withdraws from the way of Christ, he falls into the nooses of the hunters. But how, when these words are heard, is one not to withdraw from the way? What is it, not to withdraw from the way? When he hears harsh words, whence has he to make himself solace, that he not heed the harsh words, nor withdraw from the way, and may enter through the door? Let him say: what words do I hear, a servant, a sinner? My Lord heard: *You have a demon*[^17]. Just now you heard the harsh word that was said against the Lord: it was not necessary that the Lord hear this, but he admonished you against harsh words, lest you fall into the nooses of the hunters.

**5.** [v. 4.] *Between his shoulders he will overshadow you, and under his wings you shall hope.* He says this, lest your protection be from yourself to yourself, lest you suppose that you can protect yourself: he will protect you, that he may rescue you; and he will rescue you from the snare of the hunters, and from the harsh word. *Between his shoulders he will overshadow you,* you can understand both behind and before: for the shoulders are around the head. But because he says, *under his wings you shall hope,* it is manifest that the protection of the spread wings makes you to be between the shoulders of God, that here and there the wings of God may set you in the middle: and you will not fear lest anyone harm you; only do not withdraw from there, whither no enemy dares to approach. If a hen protects her chicks under her wings; how much more will you be safe under the wings of God, both against the devil and his angels, which airy powers fly around like hawks, to snatch the weak chick? For not without cause is the hen compared to the very Wisdom of God; for Christ himself our Lord and Savior called himself as it were a hen: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, how often have I wished to gather your children, as a hen her chicks, and you would not*[^18]? That Jerusalem would not; let us be willing. That one was snatched by the airy powers, fleeing the wings of the hen, presuming on her own strength, when she was weak: let us, confessing our weakness, flee under the wings of God; for he will be to us as a hen protecting her chicks. For the name of a hen is not insulting. Attend to the other birds, brothers: many birds bear young before us, warm their chicks; no bird is so weakened with her chicks as a hen. Let Your Charity attend: swallows, sparrows, and storks we see outside their nests, nor do we know whether they have young; but the hen we recognize in the weakness of her voice, in the relaxation of her feathers: she is wholly changed by affection for her chicks; because they are weak, she makes herself weak. Since, then, we too were weak, the Wisdom of God made herself weak; because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us[^19], that under his wings we might hope.

**6.** [vv. 5-6.] *His truth will surround you with a shield.* What the wings are, this is the shield; because they are neither wings nor a shield. If any of these were so properly, could the wings be a shield, or the shield wings? But because all these things can be said figuratively through likenesses, therefore both wings and shield they could be. If Christ were truly a stone, he would not be a lion; and if he were a lion, he would not be a lamb: therefore both lion[^20], and lamb[^21], and stone, and calf[^22], and whatever of this kind; because he is neither stone, nor lion, nor lamb, nor calf, but the Savior of all, Jesus Christ. For these are likenesses, not proprieties. *His truth,* he says, *will surround you.* His truth is as it were a shield; that he not mingle those who hope in themselves, with those who hope in God. He is a sinner, and he is a sinner: but suppose a sinner presuming on himself, despising, not confessing his sins, and he will say: If my sins displeased God, he would not permit me to live. But the other did not dare to lift his eyes, but struck his breast, saying: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^23]. And that one a sinner, and this one: but that one mocking, this one mourning; that one a despiser, this one a confessor of his sins. But the truth of God, which does not accept persons, distinguishes the penitent from the one defending himself, distinguishes the humble from the proud, distinguishes the one presuming on himself from the one presuming on God. Therefore *his truth* will surround you as with a shield.

**7.** [vv. 5-6.] *You shall not fear from the nocturnal terror, from the arrow flying by day, from the business walking in darkness, from ruin and the noonday demon.* To the two things he said above, the two are rendered which he said below. *You shall not fear from the nocturnal terror, from the arrow flying by day*: and, for the nocturnal terror, *from the business walking in darkness;* and, for the arrow flying by day, *from ruin and the noonday demon.* What is to be feared in the night, and what in the day? When anyone sins in ignorance, he sins as it were in the night: but when he sins knowingly, he sins as it were in the day. Those two, then, are lighter; the very ones which are repeated are heavier. Attend, that this may be diligently, if the Lord grant, expounded to you: for it is obscure, and there will be great fruit if you understand it. The temptation which befalls those who are ignorant, the light one, he called the nocturnal terror; and the temptation which befalls those who know, the light one, he called the arrow flying by day. What are the light temptations? Those which do not so press, do not so urge, as to compel, but can quickly, being turned aside, pass. The same again, make them heavy. If a persecutor presses, and vehemently terrifies the ignorant—that is, those not yet firm in faith, nor knowing that they are Christians for this end, that they may hope for the future life; when they begin to be terrified about temporal evils, they suppose that Christ has deserted them, and that they are Christians without cause; for they do not know, as I said, that they are Christians for this end, that they may overcome present things, and hope for future things: the business walking in darkness finds them, and takes them. But there are certain ones who know that they are called to future hope; because what God promised us is not of this earth, is not of this life; because all these temptations are to be endured, that we may receive that, may acquire that which God promised us forever; they know these things: but when the persecutor begins to press more vehemently, to act by threats, punishments, torments, sometimes they yield, and knowing, as it were in the day, they fall.

**8.** But why at noon? Because the persecution boils much: he called the greater heats noon. Let Your Charity attend to me proving this from the Scriptures. When the Lord spoke of the sower, that the sower went out to sow, and some fell on the way, some on rocky ground, some among thorns; he deigned himself to expound the likeness, and when he came to the rocky ground, he said this: *These are those who hear the word, and at the hour rejoice at the word; and in the tribulation which comes on account of the word, they are immediately scandalized.* For what had he said of those things which had gone out onto the rocky places? *When the sun arose,* he said, *they withered, because they had not a deep root*[^24]. *These,* then, *are those who at the hour rejoice at the word, and when persecution arises on account of the word, they dry up.* Why do they dry up? *Because they had not a firm root.* What is the root? Love: for this the Apostle says: *That you be rooted and grounded in love*[^25]. For as the root of all evils is cupidity[^26], so the root of all good things is love. You know this, and it has often been said: but why did I wish to recall this? That you may understand the Psalm, because the noonday demon is set on account of the heat of vehement persecution. For so the Lord says: *The sun arose, and the grass withered, because it had no root.* And expounding to us what it is for the grass to wither from the sun, he said that, persecution being made, those do not abide, *because they had not a deep root.* Rightly here we understand the noonday demon as vehement persecution. What such a persecution was once, brothers, let me recall, from which the Lord freed his Church, let Your Charity deign to attend. First, that the emperors and kings of the world supposed that by persecuting they could take away from the earth the name of Christ and the name of Christians, they ordered that whoever confessed himself a Christian should be struck. Whoever was unwilling to be struck, denied that he was a Christian, knowing what evil he was doing; the arrow flying by day reached him. But whoever did not care for the present life, but with certainty hoped for the future, turned aside the arrow flying by day, confessed himself a Christian: struck in the flesh, he was freed in spirit; he began to await, set in rest with God, even the redemption of his body in the resurrection of the dead: he escaped from that temptation, from the arrow flying by day. Therefore: Whoever confesses himself a Christian, let him be struck; this was as it were the arrow flying by day. Not yet was the noonday demon, blazing with vehement persecution, and making great heats even for the strong. For hear what followed: when the enemies had seen that many hastened to martyrdom, and so many more believed in Christ, the more suffered, they said among themselves: We shall kill the human race, so many thousands who believe in this name; if we kill all, nearly no one will remain on earth. The sun began to grow hot, the heat began to grow hot. For hear what they ordered: as before they had ordered: Whoever confesses himself a Christian, let him be struck; they ordered afterward: Whoever confesses himself a Christian, let him be tortured, and let him be tortured so long, until he deny that he is a Christian. Compare the arrow flying by day, and the noonday demon. What was the arrow flying by day? He who confesses himself a Christian, let him be struck. What faithful man would not turn it aside by the swiftness of death? But that: If he confesses himself a Christian, let him not be killed; but let him be tortured, until he deny; if he denies himself, let him be dismissed: this was the noonday demon. Many, then, not denying, failed amid the torments; for they were tortured so long, until they denied. But to those persevering in not denying Christ, what would the sword do, by one blow killing the body, sending the soul to God? This the long torments too did: but who at last would be found who would endure against so great and so long torments? Many failed: and, I believe, those failed who presumed on themselves, who did not dwell in the help of the Most High, and in the protection of the God of heaven; who did not say to the Lord: *You are my upholder;* who did not hope under the shadow of his wings, but gave much to their own strength. They were cast down by God, that he might show them that he himself protects, he himself tempers the temptations, he himself permits only so much to come, as the one to whom it comes can bear.

**9.** [v. 7.] Many, then, fell from the noonday demon. How many, do you wish to know? It follows, and he says: *A thousand shall fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand: but to you it shall not approach.* To whom is this said? to whom, brothers, except the Lord Jesus Christ? For the Lord Jesus is, not only in himself, but also in us. Recall those words: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^27]? When no one was touching him, and he said: *Why do you persecute me?* did he not reckon himself in us? When he said: *What you did to one of the least of mine, you did to me*[^28]; did he not reckon himself in us? For the members are not divided from one another, head and body. What is head and body? The Savior and the Church. How, then, was it said: *A thousand shall fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand?* For they will fall from the noonday demon. A great terror, brothers, to fall from the side of Christ, to fall from the right hand of Christ. How will they fall from the side? Why those from the side, those from the right hand? Why a thousand from the side, and ten thousand from the right hand? What is, a thousand from the side? Because a thousand are fewer than the ten thousand which will fall from the right hand. Who are these? Now it will be plain in the name of Christ, now it will be open. To certain ones Christ promised that they would judge with him; namely to the Apostles, who left all things, and followed him. For Peter said to him: *Behold, we have left all things, and followed you;* and he promised them this: *You shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^29]. Do not suppose that the Lord promised to them alone. For where will the apostle Paul sit, who labored more than all of them[^30], if none will sit there but twelve? For he is the thirteenth. For of the twelve Judas fell; but in the place of Judas the betrayer Matthias was ordained: we read it in the Acts of the Apostles[^31]. The twelve seats were filled. Will he not sit there who labored more than all of them? Or are the twelve seats the perfection of the tribunal? For thousands will sit on twelve seats. But whence do you prove to me, someone says, that Paul too will be among the judges? Hear him saying: *Do you not know that we shall judge angels*[^32]? *We shall judge,* he says. And he himself did not doubt, in the presumption by which he believed, to reckon himself among those who will judge with Christ. Those, then, who will judge with Christ are the princes of the Church, are the perfect. To such he said: *If you wish to be perfect, go, sell all your goods, and give to the poor*[^33]. What is: *Do you wish to be perfect?* Do you wish to judge with me, and not be judged. That man went away sad; but many have done this, and many do this: therefore these will judge with him. But many promise themselves that they will judge with Christ, because they leave all their goods and follow Christ, but they have presumption about themselves, they have a certain swelling and pride, which God alone can know, and they cannot avoid the noonday demon, that is, the fall of the boiling heat of excessive persecution. Since many such were at that time, who had distributed all their goods to the poor, and already promised themselves that they would sit with Christ, and judge the nations, with the heat of persecution boiling, as it were from the noonday demon they failed amid the torments, and denied Christ. These are they who fell from the side: as those about to sit with Christ to judge the world, they fell.

**10.** But let me say who fall from the right hand. You know that when that very tribunal appears, where they will judge with the Lord Christ who wished to be and truly were perfect, rooted and grounded in love, that they might not dry up from the sun and the noonday demon, the Lord says this: *All the nations shall be gathered before him, and he will separate them from one another, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats; and he will set the sheep at the right, but the goats at the left;* and they will be judged. Many there will be who will judge, but they will be fewer than those who will stand before the tribunal: because those are as it were a thousand, those as it were ten thousand. To those set at the right what will he say? *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was a stranger, and you took me in*[^34]. It is manifest that he will say this to those who have substance of this world, whence to do these human kindnesses. Yet these too will reign with those: because those are as it were soldiers, those as it were provincials providing the supplies; yet under one Emperor both soldier and provincial are in the kingdom. The soldier strong, the provincial devout: the strong soldier fights with prayers against the devil, the devout provincial gives supplies to the soldiers. Let Your Charity understand. And those set at the right will hear at the end: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^35]. There were, then, at that time, when the sun of persecution and the noonday demon had grievously grown hot, those who promised themselves that they would judge with Christ; they could not bear the heat of persecution, and fell from his side: there were others there, who did not promise themselves the seats of judges; but through almsgiving promised themselves that they would be at the right, to whom Christ was to say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* And because many fell from that hope of judging, but many and many more fell from that hope of the right hand, therefore it was said to Christ: *A thousand shall fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand.* And because there will be many with him, who did not care for all those things, with whom as members Christ is one: *But to you,* he says, *it shall not approach.* Did he say to the head alone, *it shall not approach?* Surely not; but neither to Peter, nor to Paul, nor to all the Apostles, nor to all the martyrs who did not fail amid the torments. How, then, *shall it not approach?* for they were tortured so. The torment approached the flesh, but did not reach the place of faith. And so their faith was far from the terror of the torturers. Let them torture, and the terror will not approach; let them torture, but they will mock the torment, presuming on him who first conquered, that the rest might conquer. And who conquer, except those who did not presume on themselves? Let Your Charity attend; for to this end he said all the things above. *He shall say to the Lord: You are my upholder, and my refuge;* and: *I will hope in him; for he will rescue me from the snare of the hunters. He himself will rescue me,* not I myself. *Between his shoulders he will overshadow you*: but when? When *under his wings you shall hope. His truth will surround you with a shield.* Since, then, you presumed on him, and placed all your hope in him, what follows? *You shall not fear from the nocturnal terror, from the arrow flying by day, from the business walking in darkness, from ruin and the noonday demon.* Who will not fear? He who presumes not on himself, but on Christ. But those who presume on themselves, even if they already hoped for themselves the side of Christ as about to judge, even if they hoped to be at the right hand of Christ, as though he would say to them: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world;* the noonday demon came, that is, the boiling heat of persecution, vehemently terrifying, and many fell from the hope of the judiciary seat, of whom it was said: *A thousand shall fall at your side;* and many from the hope of the reward of their services, of whom it was said: *And ten thousand at your right hand. But to you,* that is, to the head and body, *it shall not approach,* ruin and the noonday demon; since the Lord knows who are his[^36].

**11.** [v. 8.] *Yet with your eyes you shall consider, and shall see the retribution of sinners.* What is this? why *yet?* Because it was permitted to the impious to be proud against your servants, it was permitted to the impious to persecute your servants. Shall it then be with impunity for the impious, because they persecuted your servants? It shall not be with impunity. For although you permitted it, and rather thence your own were crowned; *Yet with your eyes you shall consider, and shall see the retribution of sinners.* For the evil which they willed, not the good which was done through those not knowing, will be rendered to them. Now we need to have the eyes of faith, and to see that they are exalted for a time, and will mourn forever; and to those to whom power over the servants of God is given temporally, it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^37]. But if anyone has eyes, as he said, *with your eyes you shall consider;* it is no light thing to see the impious flourishing in this world, and to have eyes for him, that you may consider by faith what he will suffer at the end, if he does not correct himself: because those who now wish to thunder, afterward are struck by lightning. *Yet with your eyes you shall consider, and shall see the retribution of sinners.*

**12.** [vv. 9-12.] *For you, O Lord, are my hope.* Behold he has come to that, why he does not fall from ruin and the noonday demon: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge.* What is it that your refuge is on high? For many so make themselves a refuge of God, whither they may flee from temporal heats. For on high is the refuge of God, very much in secret it is, whither you may flee from the wrath to come. Within, *you have set the Most High your refuge. Evils shall not come to you, and the scourge shall not approach your tabernacle. For he has commanded his angels concerning you, that they keep you in all your ways. In their hands they shall bear you up, lest at any time you dash your foot against a stone.* These are the words which the devil said to the Lord Jesus Christ, when he tempted him[^38]. But because they are to be considered more diligently, lest we weary you, let us defer them to tomorrow (because tomorrow too a sermon is owed to you), that from this place of the Psalm we may begin again, on account of your weariness; lest, when we wish to finish obscure matters, we rush, and they do not arrive at your understanding.
