# On the Same Psalm 90

**1.** As I do not doubt Your Charity remembers, you who attended yesterday's sermon, the narrowness of time impeded the psalm which we had begun to expound from coming to its end; and a part of it was deferred to this present day. This, you who were present yesterday, recall; you who were not present, learn. Therefore we caused that very reading of the Gospel to be recited, where the Lord was tempted by those words of the Psalm, which you heard here[^1]. For this reason Christ was tempted, lest a Christian be conquered by the tempter. For he, the master, willed to be tempted in all things, because we are tempted; as he willed to die, because we die; as he willed to rise, because we are to rise. For those things which he showed in the man, who was made man for our sake, when he was God through whom we were made, he showed for our sake. And we have often commended to Your Charity, what we are not loath often to repeat; that, since perhaps many of you cannot read, because they have no leisure to read, or do not know letters, at least by hearing assiduously they may not forget their wholesome faith. Surely by repeating these things we may seem troublesome to some, while yet we edify some. For we know that there are many both prevailing in memory, and studious of the divine reading, who know the things we are about to say; and perhaps they wish us to say the things they do not know. But if they are quicker, let them consider that they walk the way with the slower. But when two companions walk one way, of whom one is quicker, the other slower, it is in the power of the quicker to walk with the slower, not in the power of the slower; because if the quicker should wish to do as much as he can, the slower does not follow him. It is necessary, then, that the quicker rein in his speed, and not leave the slower companion behind. This, then, is what I said I have often said, and now again I say: as the Apostle says: *To write the same things to you, to me indeed is not irksome, but to you it is safe*[^2]. Our Lord Jesus Christ, as it were a whole perfect man, both head and body: the head we recognize in that man, who was born of the Virgin Mary, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was buried, rose again, ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father, whence we await him as judge of the living and the dead; this is the head of the Church. The body of this head is the Church, not that which is in this place, but both that which is in this place and throughout the whole world: nor that which is at this time, but from Abel himself unto those who will be born unto the end and will believe in Christ, the whole people of the saints belonging to one city; which city is the body of Christ, of which Christ is the head. There the angels too are our fellow-citizens: but because we are pilgrims, we labor; but they await our coming in the city. And from that city from which we are pilgrims, letters have come to us: these are the Scriptures, which exhort us to live well. Why do I say letters have come? The king himself descended, and was made for us a way in the pilgrimage; that, walking in him, we neither err, nor fail, nor fall among robbers, nor rush into the nooses which are set beside the way. Knowing, then, this whole and entire Christ together with the Church; but himself alone born of the Virgin, the head of the Church, namely the mediator between God and men, Christ Jesus[^3]: a mediator for this end, that he might reconcile through himself those who had withdrawn; for there is no mediator except between two. We had withdrawn from the majesty of God, and by our sin had offended him: the Son was sent as mediator, who by his blood might loose our sins, by which we were separated from God; and, interposed, might render us to him and reconcile us, from whom, turned away in our sins and offenses, we were held. He himself is our head, he himself is God equal to the Father, the Word of God through whom all things were made[^4]: but God to create, man to recreate; God to make, man to remake. Looking upon him, let us so hear the Psalm: let Your Charity attend. This is the discipline and teaching of this school, which may avail you not for understanding one psalm, but many, if you hold this rule. Sometimes a psalm, not only a psalm, but also all prophecy, sometimes so speaks of Christ, that it commends the head alone, and sometimes goes from the head to the body, that is, to the Church, and does not seem to have changed the person; because the head is not separated from the body, but speaks as it were of one. For let Your Charity see what I say. The psalm is surely manifest to all where it is said of the Lord's passion: *They pierced my hands and feet; they numbered all my bones: they divided my garments among themselves, and over my garment they cast lots*[^5]. This too the Jews, when they hear it, blush; for most evidently the prophecy was uttered of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. But our Lord Jesus Christ had no offenses; and yet in the head of that same psalm he says: *O God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Far from my salvation are the words of my offenses*[^6]. You see, then, what is said from the person of the head, what from the person of the body. The offenses pertain to us, the passion for us pertains to the head: but on account of his passion for us, even what pertains to us of offenses is loosed. So too in this psalm.

**2.** [vv. 1-8.] We treated these verses yesterday: let us briefly recall them. *He who dwells in the help of the Most High, shall abide in the protection of the God of heaven.* This we commended to Your Charity in these verses, that no one place his hope in himself, but place his whole hope in him in whom are our strength; for by his help we conquer, not by our presumption. Therefore the God of heaven protects us, if we say to the Lord what follows: *He shall say to the Lord: You are my upholder, and my refuge, my God; I will hope in him; for he will rescue me from the snare of the hunters, and from the harsh word.* We said that many, fearing the harsh word, fall into the snare of the hunters. A man is insulted, because he is a Christian; and he repents that he was made a Christian, and from the harsh word falls into the snare of the devil. A man is insulted too, because among many Christians he lives better; and, fearing the harsh words of the insulters, he falls into the nooses of the devil, so that he is not wheat in the threshing-floor, but follows the chaff. But he who hopes in God is rescued from the snare of the hunters and from the harsh word. But how does God protect you? *Between his shoulders he will overshadow you*: that is, he will set you before his breast, that with his wings he may protect you; if only you acknowledge your weakness, that as a weak chick you flee under the wings of your mother, lest you be snatched by the kite. For the kites are the airy powers, the devil and his angels: they wish to snatch our weakness. Let us flee under the wings of the mother Wisdom, because she too, Wisdom, was made weak for our sake; because the Word was made flesh[^7]. As a hen is weakened with her chicks, that she may protect them with her wings[^8]: so our Lord Jesus Christ, who, though he was in the form of God, did not think it robbery to be equal to God, that he might be weak with us, and protect us with his wings, emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man[^9]. *And under his wings you shall hope. His truth will surround you with a shield; you shall not fear from the nocturnal terror.* The temptations of ignorance are the nocturnal terror; the sins of knowledge, the arrow flying by day: for in the night ignorance is understood, in the day manifestation. There are those who sin in ignorance, there are those who sin knowingly: those who sin in ignorance are tripped up by the nocturnal terror; those who sin knowingly are struck by the arrow flying by day. But when these things happen in graver persecutions, until it comes as it were to noon, whoever falls in that heat, will fall as it were from the noonday demon. And many fell from this heat, as we already expounded yesterday to Your Charity; because, with persecution boiling, it was said: Let the Christians be tortured, until they deny that they are Christians. Whereas before those confessing were wont to be struck, afterward they were tortured to this end, that they might deny: and since every accused is tortured so long, as long as he denies; the confession of the Christians was tortured, the denial was dismissed. There was, then, a great heat of the persecutors at that time. Whoever fell in that temptation, fell as it were from the noonday demon. And how many fell! Many who hoped that they would sit with the Lord and judge, fell from the side. Likewise many who hoped that they would be at the right hand, in the holy stipendiary people as it were provincials, who provide supplies to the soldiers, to whom it is to be said: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat* (for at the right hand there will be many); and they fell from that hope, and more from there, because more there. For fewer are those who will judge with the Lord, and more those who will stand before him: but their condition is not one. Some at the left, some at the right; some to reign, some to be punished; some to hear: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom;* some to hear: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^10]. Therefore those who *from ruin and the noonday demon shall fall, a thousand at the side, and ten thousand at the right. But to you it shall not approach*: what? The noonday demon does not cast you down. What wonder if it does not cast down the head? But it does not cast down those either who so cling to the head, as the Apostle says: *The Lord knows who are his*[^11]. For there are certain ones so predestined, that the Lord knows who belong to his body. Since, then, to these that temptation will not approach, so as to cast them down; these are understood in what is written: *But to you it shall not approach.* But lest those sinners to whom so much was permitted to exercise against the Christians be attended to by certain weak ones, and it be said: Behold, what did God will, that so much be permitted to the impious and the wicked against the servants of God? consider a little with your eyes, with the eyes of faith, and you will see the retribution of the sinners at the end, to whom now so much is permitted, that you may be exercised. For this follows: *Yet with your eyes you shall consider, and shall see the retribution of sinners.*

**3.** [vv. 9-12.] *For you, O Lord, are my hope, you have set the Most High your refuge. Evils shall not come to you.* He says to the Lord: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge. Evils shall not come to you; and the scourge shall not approach your tabernacle.* Then now the words which you heard said by the devil: *For he has commanded his angels concerning you, that they keep you in all your ways. In their hands they shall bear you up, lest at any time you dash your foot against a stone.* To whom does he say this? To whom he said: *For you, O Lord, are my hope.* Now I think it need not be expounded to Christians who the Lord is. If they understand God the Father, how will the angels bear him up in their hands, lest he dash his foot against a stone? You see, then, that Christ the Lord too, while speaking of the body, suddenly began to speak of the head. Our head was named just now, when it was said: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge.* Therefore *you have set the Most High your refuge, because you, O Lord, are my hope.* What does this mean? Let Your Charity attend: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge.* Now it is not to be wondered at: for therefore it follows: *Evils shall not come to you,* because *you have set the Most High your refuge; and the scourge shall not approach your tabernacle,* because *you have set the Most High your refuge.* But we do not read in the Gospel that anywhere the angels carried the Lord, lest he dash his foot against a stone: and yet we understand. For these things have already been done, and were not foretold without cause, except because they were to be. And we cannot say: Christ will come afterward, so as not to dash his foot against a stone: for he will come to judge. Where, then, was it fulfilled? Let Your Charity attend.

**4.** First hear these verses: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge.* The human race knew that man dies, did not know that he rises again: therefore it had what to fear, it had not what to hope. Therefore, that he who, for the sake of discipline, made the fear of death, might, for the sake of the reward of the future eternal life, give the hope of resurrection, our Lord Jesus Christ first rose again. He died after many; he rose before all. He suffered by dying that which many had already suffered; he did by rising that which no one before him: for when will the Church receive this, except at the end? It went before in the head what the members may hope: for Your Charity knows how they say to one another. Let the Church, then, say to her Lord Jesus Christ, let the body say to its head: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge*: that is, therefore you rose from the dead, and ascended into heaven, that you might set on high your refuge by ascending, and become my hope, who on earth was despairing, and did not believe that I would rise; now I already believe, because my head has ascended into heaven; whither the head went before, the members too will follow. I think it is now manifest what was said: *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge.* Let me say this more plainly. That I might have the hope of resurrection which I did not have, therefore you rose first, that whither you went before, thither I might hope to follow. For it is the voice of the Church to her Lord, it is the voice of the body to its head.

**5.** Do not wonder, then: *Evils shall not come to you, and the scourge shall not approach your tabernacle.* The tabernacle of God is the flesh. In the flesh the Word dwelt, and the flesh was made the tabernacle for God: in that very tabernacle the Emperor fought for us; in that very tabernacle he was tempted by the enemy, lest the soldier fail. And because he showed that very flesh to our eyes, because our eyes rejoice in this light, and are delighted by this visible light, because in manifestation he set his flesh, that all might see; therefore the psalm says: *In the sun he set his tabernacle*[^12]. What is, *in the sun?* In the open, in the manifest, in the earthly light; that is, in the light which floods the earth from heaven, there he set his tabernacle. But how would he set his tabernacle there, if he did not go forth as a bridegroom from his chamber? for this follows. *In the sun he set his tabernacle*: and as if it were said to him: How? *And he himself,* he says, *as a bridegroom going forth from his chamber; he exulted as a giant to run the way*[^13]. What is the tabernacle, that is the bride. The Word the bridegroom, the flesh the bride, and the chamber the womb of the Virgin. And what does the Apostle say? *And they shall be two in one flesh. This is a great sacrament; but I say in Christ and in the Church*[^14]. And what does the Lord himself in the Gospel? *Therefore now they are not two, but one flesh*[^15]. Out of two one, out of the Word and the flesh one man, one God. But this tabernacle of his felt scourges on earth; it is manifest that the Lord was scourged[^16]. Does he feel scourges in heaven? By no means. Why? Because he has set on high his refuge, that he might be our hope; and evils shall not come to him, nor will the scourge approach his tabernacle. He is far above all the heavens, but he has feet on earth: the head is in heaven, the body on earth. But when his feet were being scourged and trodden on by Saul, the head cried out: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^17]? Behold no one persecutes the head, behold the head is in heaven: because Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^18]: *Evils shall not come to you, and the scourge shall not approach your tabernacle.* But lest we suppose that the head is separated from the body: for it is distinguished in places, but joined in affection; that very joining of affection cried out from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* For he laid him low with a rebuking voice, and lifted him up with a pitying right hand. He who persecuted the body of Christ was made a member of Christ; so that what he was doing, he himself there might suffer.

**6.** What then, brothers? what was said of our head? *For you, O Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High your refuge. Evils shall not come to you, and the scourge shall not approach your tabernacle.* These things were said. *For he has commanded his angels concerning you, that they keep you in all your ways.* You heard just now, when the Gospel was recited: attend. The Lord, baptized, fasted[^19]. Why baptized? That we might not disdain to be baptized. For when John himself said to the Lord: *You come to me to be baptized; I ought to be baptized by you;* and the Lord: *Allow it now; for so it befits us to fulfill all righteousness*[^20]: he willed to fulfill humility, that he might be washed who had no filth. Why this? On account of the pride of those to come. For sometimes there exists some catechumen who perhaps in teaching and conduct surpasses many of the faithful; he attends now to many baptized who are unskilled, and many not living as he himself lives, not in such great continence, not in such great chastity; now he neither seeks a wife, and he sees sometimes a faithful man, if not fornicating, yet using his wife rather intemperately: he can lift up the neck of pride, and say: What need is there now for me to be baptized, that I may receive what this man has, whom I already surpass both in life and in teaching? To him the Lord: What have you surpassed? how much have you surpassed? as much as I you? *The servant is not above his lord, nor the disciple above his master: it is enough for the servant that he be as his lord, and for the disciple that he be as his master*[^21]. Do not be exalted, so as not to seek baptism. You will seek the baptism of the Lord, I sought that of the servant. The Lord, then, was baptized, and after baptism was tempted, he fasted forty days in mystery, which I have often recalled to you. Not all things can be said at one time, lest they occupy the necessary times. After forty days he hungered. He could even never hunger: but how would he be tempted? Or if he did not conquer the tempter, how would you learn to fight with the tempter? He hungered: and now the tempter: *Tell these stones to become bread, if you are the Son of God*[^22]. What great thing was it for the Lord Jesus Christ to make bread from stones, who from five loaves satisfied so many thousands[^23]? From nothing he made bread. For so great a multitude of food, which satisfied so many thousands, whence did it come forth? Fountains of bread were in the hands of the Lord. It is no wonder: for he himself made from five loaves much bread to satisfy so many thousands, who daily makes on earth from a few grains huge harvests. For these are the miracles of the Lord; but by their constancy they have become cheap. What, then, brothers, was it impossible for the Lord to make bread from stones? He made men from stones, as John the Baptist himself says: *God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*[^24]. Why, then, did he not do it? That he might teach you to answer the tempter, that if perhaps you are set in some strait, and the tempter suggests to you: If you were a Christian and belonged to Christ, would he desert you now? would he not have sent you help? And perhaps the physician is still cutting, and therefore deserts: but he does not desert. As he did not hear Paul himself, because he heard him. For Paul says that he was not heard concerning the thorn of his flesh, the angel of Satan, by whom he said he was buffeted: *For which I asked the Lord three times to take it from me, and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for power is perfected in weakness*[^25]. As though it were said to a physician, having perhaps applied a poultice: This plaster is troublesome to me; I ask you, take it away. And the physician: No, it must be there a long time; for otherwise you cannot be healed. The physician did not hear the sick man according to his will, because he heard him according to his health. Therefore be strong, brothers; and when you are tempted by some want, with God scourging and instructing you, for whom he both prepares and keeps an eternal inheritance, let not the devil suggest to you: If you were righteous, would he not send you bread through a raven, as he sent to Elijah[^26]? where is what you read: *I have never seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread*[^27]? Do you answer the devil: Scripture says truly: *I have never seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed seeking bread;* for I have my bread which you do not know. What bread? Hear the Lord: *Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*[^28]. Do you suppose the word of God is bread? If the Word of God, through whom all things were made, were not bread, he would not say: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^29]. You have learned, then, what to answer the tempter in the strait of hunger.

**7.** What, if he so tempts you, as to say to you: If you were a Christian, you would do miracles, as many Christians did? You, now deceived by an evil suggestion, would tempt the Lord your God, so as to say to our Lord God: If I am a Christian, and am before your eyes, and you reckon me in some number of yours, I too will do something, such as your saints did many things? You have tempted God, as though you are not a Christian if you do not do this. Many desiring such things have fallen. For that Simon the magician desired such things from the Apostles, who wished to buy the Holy Spirit with money[^30]. He loved the power of miracles, and did not love the imitation of humility. Therefore a certain disciple, or a certain one of the crowd, when he wished to follow the Lord, attending to the miracles which he did; the Lord saw him, proud, not seeking the way of humility, but the swelling of power, and said: *The foxes have holes, and the birds of heaven nests; but the Son of man has nowhere to lay his head*[^31]. The foxes have holes in you; the birds of heaven have nests in you. The fox is craft; the birds of heaven are pride: for as the birds seek the heights, so the proud; as the foxes have crafty caverns, so all plotters. What, then, did the Lord answer? Pride and craft can dwell in you; Christ has not where to dwell in you, where to lay his head. For the laying of the head is the humility of Christ. Unless he laid his head, you would not be justified. Such things desiring, the disciples too, and wishing already to seek the seat of the kingdom, before they took the way of humility, when it was suggested to him by the mother of the disciples: *Say that one may sit at your right hand, and another at your left;* they sought power: but through the passion of humility one comes to the power of the kingdom. The Lord says: *Can you drink the cup that I am to drink*[^32]? Why do you think of the height of the kingdom, and do not imitate my humility? Therefore, what if he so tempts you: Do miracles? lest you tempt God, what ought you to answer? What the Lord answered. The devil said to him: *Cast yourself down; for it is written: He has commanded his angels concerning you, that in their hands they bear you up, lest perhaps you dash your foot against a stone.* If you cast yourself down, the angels will take you up. And it could indeed happen, brothers, that if the Lord had cast himself down, the services of the angels would take up the flesh of the Lord: but what did he say to him? *Again it is written: You shall not tempt the Lord your God*[^33]. You think me a man. For to this end the devil approached, that he might tempt whether he himself were the Son of God. He saw the flesh; but the majesty appeared in his works. The angels had borne testimony. He saw the mortal one, that he might tempt, so that, Christ being tempted, a Christian might be taught. What, then, is written? *You shall not tempt the Lord your God.* So let us not tempt the Lord, so as to say: If we belong to you, let us do a miracle.

**8.** Let us return on account of the words of the Psalm. *His angels,* he says, *he has commanded concerning you, that they keep you in all your ways. In their hands they shall bear you up, lest at any time you dash your foot against a stone.* Christ was borne up in the hands of the angels, when he was taken up into heaven[^34]; not because, if the angels did not carry him, he was about to fall; but because they were rendering service to the king. Lest perhaps you say: Better are those who carried than he who was carried. Are beasts then better than men? But because beasts carry the weakness of men; we ought not to say even this: for beasts, if they withdraw themselves, those who sit fall. But how ought we to say it? For of God too it was said: *Heaven is my seat*[^35]. Since, then, heaven carries it, and God sits, is heaven therefore better? So, then, of the service of the angels in this psalm too we shall be able to understand, it does not pertain to the weakness of the Lord, but to their honoring, to their servitude. But the Lord Jesus Christ rose again, for what? Hear the Apostle. *He died for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^36]. Likewise of the Holy Spirit the Gospel: *The Spirit,* it says, *was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^37]. What is the glorification of Jesus? He rose again, and ascended into heaven. Glorified by God by his ascension into heaven, he sent his Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost. But in the Law, in the book of Moses, Exodus, from the day of the lamb slain and eaten fifty days are numbered; and the Law was given written on stone tablets by the finger of God. What the finger of God is, the Gospel expounds for us: that the finger of God is the Holy Spirit. How do we prove it? The Lord, answering those who said that he cast out demons in the name of Beelzebub, says: *If I cast out demons by the Spirit of God*[^38]: another evangelist, when he narrated this: *If I,* he says, *by the finger of God cast out demons*[^39]. Therefore what was set openly in the one, was set obscurely in the other: you did not know what the finger of God is, the other evangelist expounds, saying that it is the Spirit of God. By the finger of God, then, the Law was given written, on the fiftieth day from the slaying of the lamb; and the Holy Spirit came on the fiftieth day from the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. The lamb was slain, the Passover was made, the fifty days were fulfilled, the Law was given. But that Law unto fear, not unto love: but that fear might be turned into love, the righteous one was slain now in truth; of whom that lamb which the Jews slew was the type. He rose again; and from the day of the Lord's Passover, as from the day of the Passover of the slain lamb, fifty days are numbered; and the Holy Spirit came, now in the fullness of love, not in the punishment of fear[^40]. Why did I say this? Therefore for this end the Lord rose again and was glorified, that he might send the Holy Spirit. And I had said already that the head is in heaven, the feet on earth. If the head is in heaven, the feet on earth; what is, the feet of the Lord on earth, except the saints of the Lord on earth? Who are the feet of the Lord? The apostles sent through the whole world. Who are the feet of the Lord? All the Evangelists, in whom the Lord traverses all the nations. It was to be feared lest the Evangelists dash against a stone: for, the head being set in heaven, the feet which labored on earth could dash against a stone. Against what stone? Against the Law given on the stone tablets. Lest, then, they become guilty of the Law, grace not having been received, and be held guilty in the Law; for that is the offense, the guilt: those whom the Law held guilty, the Lord absolved, that they might no longer offend against the Law. Lest the feet of this head should incur the guilt of the Law, the Holy Spirit was sent that he might make love, and loose from fear. Fear did not fulfill the Law, love fulfilled it. Men feared, and did not fulfill it; they loved, and fulfilled it. How did they fear, and not fulfill; loved, and fulfilled? Men feared, and were seizing the goods of others; they loved, and gave their own. It is not to be wondered at, then, that for this end the Lord was taken up into heaven in the hands of the angels, lest his foot dash against a stone: lest those who labored on earth in his body, traversing the whole world, become guilty of the Law, he withdrew from them fear, and filled them with love. By fear Peter thrice denied[^41]; for he had not yet received the Holy Spirit: having afterward received the Holy Spirit, he began to preach with confidence. He who at the voice of a maidservant thrice denied; having received the Holy Spirit, amid the scourges of the princes confessed him whom he had denied[^42]. No wonder; because the Lord loosed that threefold fear by a threefold love. For rising from the dead he said to Peter: *Peter, do you love me?* He did not say: Do you fear me? For if he still feared, he would dash his foot against a stone. *Do you love me?* he says; and that one: *I love you.* It would suffice once. Perhaps it would suffice for me, who do not see the heart; how much more would it suffice for the Lord, who saw with what marrow of his inmost parts Peter was saying: *I love you?* It does not suffice the Lord to answer once; he asks again, and that one answers: *I love you.* He asks a third time, and now, affected with weariness, Peter, as though the Lord doubted his love: *Lord, you,* he says, *know all things, you know that I love you*[^43]. But the Lord dealt with him so, as though he said: Three times you denied me by fearing, three times confess by loving. With this love and this charity he filled his disciples. Why? Because he set on high his refuge: because, glorified, he sent the Holy Spirit, he loosed the believers from the guilt of the Law, lest their feet dash against a stone.

**9.** [v. 13.] Now the rest is manifest, brothers, because it has often been treated. *Upon the asp and the basilisk you shall walk, and you shall trample the lion and the dragon.* Who the serpent is, you know: how the Church treads upon him, which is not conquered, because it guards against his craftiness. But how he is a lion and a dragon, I think Your Charity knows this too. The lion rages openly; the dragon lies in wait secretly: the devil has both force and power. When the martyrs were being killed, he was a raging lion: when heretics lie in wait, he is a creeping dragon. You have conquered the lion, conquer the dragon too: the lion did not break you, let not the dragon deceive you. Let us prove that he was a lion, when he raged openly. Peter, exhorting the martyrs, says: *Do you not know that your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, goes about, seeking whom he may devour*[^44]? Openly raging, the lion was seeking whom he might devour: how does the dragon lie in wait? Through heretics. Fearing such, lest by them the Church be corrupted from the virginity of faith which it bears in heart, Paul says: *For I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ; and I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his craftiness, so your minds too be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ*[^45]. Virginity of the body few women have in the Church; but virginity of the heart all the faithful have. In that very faith he feared lest virginity of the heart be corrupted by the devil; which those who lose are virgins of body without cause. Corrupt in heart, what does she keep in body? To such a degree does a catholic married woman go before a heretic virgin. For that one is not a virgin in body, this one is made a woman in heart; and a woman not by God her husband, but by the serpent. But what of the Church? *Upon the asp and the basilisk you shall walk.* The basilisk is the king of serpents, as the devil is the king of demons. *And you shall trample the lion and the dragon.*

**10.** [v. 14.] Now the words of God to the Church: *Because he hoped in me, I will rescue him.* Therefore not only the head, which now sits in heaven, because he set on high his refuge, to which evils shall not come, and the scourge shall not approach his tabernacle; but we too who labor on earth, and still live in temptations, whose steps are feared, lest they fall into the nooses, let us hear the voice of the Lord our God consoling us, and saying to us: *Because he hoped in me, I will rescue him; I will protect him, because he knew my name.*

**11.** [v. 15.] *He shall call upon me, and I will hear him; I am with him in tribulation.* Do not fear when you are troubled, as though God were not with you. Let faith be with you, and God is with you in tribulation. There are the waves of the sea, you are tossed in the ship, because Christ sleeps. Christ was sleeping in the ship, men were perishing[^46]. If your faith sleeps in your heart, as it were in your ship Christ sleeps: because Christ dwells in you through faith. When you have begun to be tossed, rouse the sleeping Christ; lift up your faith, and you will know that he does not desert you. But you suppose yourself deserted for this reason, because he does not rescue you when you wish. He rescued the three boys from the fire[^47]. Did he who rescued the three boys desert the Maccabees[^48]? Far be it. Both those he rescued, and these: those bodily, that the unbelieving might be confounded; these spiritually, that the faithful might imitate them. *I am with him in tribulation; and I will deliver him, and I will glorify him.*

**12.** [v. 16.] *With length of days I will fill him.* What is the length of days? Eternal life. Brothers, do not suppose length of days is meant, as in winter the days are shorter, in summer the days are longer. Such days has he to give us? That length is the one which has no end, the eternal life which is promised us in long days. And truly, because it suffices, not without cause he said, *I will fill him.* Whatever is long in time does not suffice us, if it has an end; and therefore it ought not even to be called long. And if we are covetous, we ought to be covetous of eternal life: desire such a life, which has no end. Behold whither our covetousness may be stretched. Do you want silver without end? Desire eternal life without end. Do you not want your possession to have an end? Seek eternal life. *With length of days I will fill him.*

**13.** *And I will show him my salvation.* Nor is this, brothers, to be passed over briefly. *I will show him my salvation*: this he says: I will show him Christ himself. Why? was he not seen on earth? What great thing has he to show us? But he was not seen with such a vision as we shall see. He was seen with such a vision, as those who saw him crucified him: behold, those who saw him crucified him; we did not see him, and we believed. Did they have eyes, and we do not have them? Rather, we too have the eyes of the heart; but through faith we still see, not through vision. When will there be vision? When we shall see face to face[^49], which the Apostle says: which God promises us as the great reward of all our labors. Whatever you labor at, you labor for this end, that you may see. It is something great that we are to see, when our whole reward is vision: and that very great thing to be seen is our Lord Jesus Christ. He himself who was seen humble, will be seen great, and will make us glad, as he is now seen by the angels: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^50]. Attend to him who promised this, the Lord himself saying in the Gospel: *He who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him.* And as if it were said to him: And what will you give to him who loves you? *I will show,* he says, *myself to him*[^51]. Let us desire, and love: let us burn, if we are the bride. The bridegroom is absent, let us endure: he will come whom we desire. He gave so great a pledge, let not the bride fear lest she be deserted by the bridegroom: he does not abandon his pledge. What pledge did he give? He shed his blood. What pledge did he give? He sent the Holy Spirit. Will that bridegroom abandon such pledges? If he did not love, he would not give such pledges. Now he loves. O if we so loved! *No one has greater love than to lay down his soul for his friends*[^52]: and we, how can we lay down our souls for him? What does it profit him, since he has already set on high his refuge, and the scourge does not approach his tabernacle? But what does John say? *As Christ laid down his soul for us, so we too ought to lay down our soul for the brethren*[^53]. Each one laying down his soul for a brother, lays it down for Christ; as feeding a brother, he feeds Christ: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^54]. Let us love, and imitate; let us run after his ointments, as it is said in the Song of Songs: *After the odor of your ointments we will run*[^55]. For he came and gave off an odor, and his odor filled the world. Whence the odor? From heaven. Follow, then, to heaven, if you do not answer falsely when it is said: Hearts on high, the thought on high, the love on high, the hope on high; lest it rot on earth. You do not dare to put wheat on damp earth, lest it rot, because you have labored and reaped, threshed and winnowed. You seek a place for your wheat, and do you not seek a place for your heart? do you not seek a place for your treasure? Do whatever you can on earth: give out; you will not lose, but store up. And who keeps it? Christ, who also keeps you. He knows how to keep you, does he not know how to keep your treasure? But why does he wish you to change the place of your treasure, except that you may change the place of your heart? For no one thinks except of his treasure. How many are here who hear me now, and their heart is nowhere but in their money-bags? You are on earth, because on earth is what you love: let it be sent into heaven, and your heart will be there. For where your treasure is, there your heart too will be[^56].
