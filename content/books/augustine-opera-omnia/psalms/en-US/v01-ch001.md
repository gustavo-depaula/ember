# Exposition of Psalm 1

**1.** [v 1.] *Blessed is the man who has not walked in the counsel of the ungodly*[^1]: this is to be understood of our Lord Jesus Christ, that is, of the Lord as man. *Blessed is the man who has not walked in the counsel of the ungodly*: unlike the earthly man who consented to his wife when she had been deceived by the serpent, so that he transgressed God's commands. *And has not stood in the way of sinners*: because he did indeed come in the way of sinners, being born as sinners are; but he did not stand, because the enticement of the world did not hold him. *And has not sat in the seat of pestilence*: he refused an earthly kingdom with its pride; and this is rightly understood as the seat of pestilence, because there is scarcely anyone who is free from the love of domineering and does not crave human glory; for pestilence is a disease that has spread far and wide, involving all or nearly all. Yet the seat of pestilence may more fittingly be taken as a destructive teaching, whose word *spreads like a cancer*[^2]. Then we must consider the order of the words: *walked, stood, sat*. For he walked away when he withdrew from God; he stood when he took delight in sin; he sat when, confirmed in his pride, he could not return, except by being set free through him who neither walked in the counsel of the ungodly, nor stood in the way of sinners, nor sat in the seat of pestilence.

**2.** [v 2.] *But his will was in the law of the Lord, and on his law he will meditate day and night. The law is not laid down for the righteous man*[^3], as the Apostle says; but it is one thing to be in the law, another to be under the law: he who is in the law acts according to the law; he who is under the law is driven according to the law. The former, then, is free, the latter a slave. Again, the law that is written and imposed on one who serves is one thing; the law that is beheld by the mind, by one who does not need letters, is another. *He will meditate day and night*: this is to be understood either as without interruption; or *by day* in gladness, *by night* in tribulations; for it is said: *Abraham saw my day, and was glad*[^4]; and of tribulation it is said: *Moreover even till night my reins have chastened me*[^5].

**3.** [v 3.] *And he will be like the tree that is planted beside the running streams of water*: that is, either according to Wisdom itself, which deigned to take up a man for our salvation, so that the man himself is the tree planted beside the running streams of water; for this can also be understood in the sense in which it is said in another psalm: *The river of God is full of water*[^6]. Or according to the Holy Spirit, of whom it is said: *He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit*[^7]; and that other text: *Let him who thirsts come and drink*[^8]; and that other: *If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that asks water of you, you would have asked of him, and he would have given you living water, so that whoever drinks of it will not thirst forever; but it will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life*[^9]. Or *beside the running streams of water* may mean according to the sins of the peoples, since in the Apocalypse waters are interpreted as peoples; and the running of them is not absurdly understood as a falling, which pertains to sin. So that tree, namely our Lord, drawing them from the running waters, that is, from the sinful peoples, into the way, into the roots of his discipline, *will give fruit*, that is, he will establish Churches; *in its season*, that is, after he was glorified by his resurrection and ascension into heaven. For then, when the Holy Spirit was sent upon the Apostles and they were confirmed in confidence in him and directed to the peoples, he made the Churches fruitful. *And its leaf will not fall*: that is, his word will not be void; for *all flesh is grass, and the glory of man like the flower of grass: the grass has withered, and the flower has fallen, but the word of the Lord remains forever*[^10]. *And whatever he does will prosper*: that is, whatever that tree brings forth; and all of this is to be understood as fruits and leaves, that is, deeds and words.

**4.** [v 4.] *Not so the ungodly, not so: but they are like the dust that the wind casts from the face of the earth.* Here the earth is to be understood as stability itself in God, according to which it is said: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance, for my inheritance is glorious to me*[^11]; according to this it is said: *Wait for the Lord, and keep his ways, and he will exalt you to possess the earth*[^12]; according to this it is said: *Blessed are the meek, for they will possess the earth as their inheritance*[^13]. Now the likeness is drawn from this: that just as this visible earth nourishes and sustains the outer man, so that invisible earth sustains the inner man. From the face of this earth the wind casts the ungodly man, that is, pride, because it puffs up. Guarding against it, the one who was made drunk with the abundance of God's house and given to drink from the torrent of his delight says: *Let not the foot of pride come to me*[^14]. From this earth pride cast out the one who said: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^15]. From the face of this earth it also cast out the one who, when he had consented and tasted of the forbidden tree, that he might be like God, hid himself from the face of God. That this earth pertains to the inner man, and that pride casts a man from it, can best be understood in what is written: *Why is earth and ashes proud? for in his life he has cast away his inmost parts*[^16]; for the one who has cast away from where he was cast out is not absurdly said to have cast himself away.

**5.** [v 5.] *Therefore the ungodly do not rise again in judgment*: therefore, namely, because they are cast like dust from the face of the earth. And it was well said that what the proud aspire to is taken from them, namely, that they should judge; so that this very thing is understood as more plainly stated in the following sentence: *Nor sinners in the counsel of the righteous.* For in this way what is said above is usually repeated more plainly, so that sinners are understood as the ungodly; and what was said above, *in judgment*, is here said, *in the counsel of the righteous.* Or else, if the ungodly are one thing and sinners another, such that, although every ungodly person is a sinner, yet not every sinner is ungodly: *the ungodly do not rise again in judgment*, that is, they will indeed rise, but not so as to be judged, because they are already destined for the most certain punishments; whereas *sinners* do not rise again *in the counsel of the righteous*, that is, so as to judge, but perhaps so as to be judged, so that of them it is said: *Each one's work, of what sort it is, fire will test: if anyone's work remains, he will receive a reward; but if anyone's work is burned up, he will suffer loss; yet he himself will be saved, but only as through fire*[^17].

**6.** [v 6.] *For the Lord knows the way of the righteous.* Just as it is said: Medicine knows health, but does not know diseases; and yet diseases too are recognized by the art of medicine; so it can be said that the Lord knows the way of the righteous, but does not know the way of the ungodly: not because there is anything the Lord does not know, and yet he says to sinners: *I do not know you*[^18]. *But the way of the ungodly will perish* is put for what would be said: But the way of the ungodly the Lord does not know. Yet it was stated more plainly, so that to be unknown to the Lord is to perish, and to be known to the Lord is to remain: so that being pertains to God's knowledge, but not-being to his ignorance; for the Lord says: *I am who I am*; and: *He who is has sent me*[^19].
