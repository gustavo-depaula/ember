# On the Same Psalm 103 (2)

**1.** Your Charity remembers that we are debtors for treating the remaining parts of this psalm. There is no need, then, that I move your attention by any preface. For I see you suspended with all eagerness to understand the prophetic sacraments; nor is there need that my sermon make attentive those whom the Spirit of God has already made so. Let us rather do what presses. Of the springs sent forth in the valleys, and of the waters passing through the midst of the mountains, it has already been said: but said thus far; from here let us begin afterward.

**2.** [v. 11.] For there follows: *All the beasts of the forest will drink.* What will they drink? The waters passing through the midst of the mountains. What will they drink? The springs sent forth in the valleys. And who will drink? The beasts of the forest. We see this indeed in this creation too, the beasts of the forest drinking from the springs and from the streams running among the mountains: but since now it has pleased God to hide his wisdom in figures of such things, not to take it away from the studious, but to shut it off from the negligent, to open it to those who knock; it has pleased the Lord our God himself to exhort you through us to this, that in all these things, which are said as it were of bodily and visible creation, we seek something spiritually hidden, in finding which we may rejoice. The beasts of the forest we understand to be the gentiles; and Scripture testifies this in many places. But yet two most evident proofs especially occur, that in Noah's ark, by which none of us doubts that the Church was prefigured, all kinds of animals would not be enclosed, unless in that unity of structure all the nations were signified: unless perhaps we suppose that, if all such things were utterly destroyed by the flood, God would have lacked the power to command that the earth bring them forth, as by his first word it had brought them forth. Not in vain, then, not rashly, not from any need of God or lack of power, were those animals commanded to be enclosed in the ark. For after the time came (for now we must add the other most evident testimony too): when, then, the time came that what was prefigured in the ark should now be fulfilled in the Church, the apostle Peter, doubting to give the evangelical sacrament to the uncircumcised gentiles—or rather not doubting, but thinking that it ought not at all to be given—on a certain day, being hungry, when he wished to dine, went up to pray. This is known in the Acts of the Apostles to all who read well and hear well. So while he was praying, there came upon him an alienation of mind, which the Greeks call ecstasy: that is, his mind was turned away from bodily custom to contemplate a certain vision, alienated from present things. Then he saw a certain vessel like a sheet let down from heaven by four corners, where there were all animals, beasts of every kind; and a voice sounded to him: *Peter, kill, and eat*[^1]. But he, who had been instructed in the Law, and had grown up in Jewish custom, and retained the precept of God through Moses his servant, and had faithfully kept his whole life, answered: *Far be it from me, Lord; never has anything common entered my mouth*[^2]. That "common" is called unclean by the Jews and by the Law, those who have learned the ecclesiastical writings well know. And a voice to him: *What God has cleansed, do not call unclean*[^3]. And this was done three times, and that dish was taken away which was shown to be let down three times from heaven. The dish which was held by four corners was the world in its four parts. These four parts Scripture often commemorates, east and west, north and south. For this reason, because the whole world was being called through the Gospel, four Gospels were written. But the vessel let down three times from heaven signifies this, because it was said to the Apostles, *Go, baptize all nations in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit*[^4]. From this is gathered too, as you already know, the number twelve of the disciples. For not in vain did he wish to have twelve; and so that number was hallowed, that in the place of one who had fallen, none but another could be ordained. Why twelve Apostles? Because, since there are four parts of the world, and the whole world was being called in the Gospel, whence four Gospels were written, and the whole world is called in the name of the Trinity, that the Church may be gathered: four taken three times make twelve. Therefore let us not wonder if, of those waters passing through the midst of the mountains, in that apostolic doctrine flowing in the midst on account of the concord of communion, all the beasts of the forest drink. For all were in the ark, all in the dish, all Peter kills and eats; because Peter is the rock, the rock is the Church. What is it to kill and eat? To slay in them what they were, and to take them up into one's own bowels. You dissuaded the pagan from sacrileges, you slew what he was; having given the sacrament of Christ, you incorporated him into the Church, you ate.

**3.** These beasts, then, drink these waters, but passing: not abiding, but passing. For all doctrine that is dispensed throughout this whole time passes away. Hence the Apostle says: *And knowledge will be destroyed, and prophecy will be made void.* Why will these be made void? *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part: but when that which is perfect has come, that which is in part will be made void*[^5]. Unless perhaps your Charity supposes that in that city to which it is said, *Praise the Lord, Jerusalem, praise your God, Zion; for he has strengthened the bars of your gates*[^6], when the bars are now strengthened and the city shut, whence, as we said a while ago, no friend goes out, and into which no enemy enters; a manuscript is to be read to us there, or a sermon treated, in the way it is now treated for you. For this reason it is treated now, that there it may be held; for this reason it is now divided through syllables, that there it may be contemplated whole and entire. The word of God will not be lacking there; but yet not through letters, not through sounds, not through manuscripts, not through a reader, not through an expositor. How, then? As *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* For it did not so come to us that it departed from there; because *it was in this world, and the world was made through it*[^7]. Such a Word we are going to contemplate. *For the God of gods will appear in Zion*[^8]. But when this? After the pilgrimage, the way finished; if, indeed, after the way is finished we are not handed over to the judge, that the judge send us into prison. But if, the way finished, as we hope, and desire, and strive, we come to the homeland, there we will contemplate what we will always praise: nor will that fail which is available to us, nor we who enjoy it; nor will the one who eats grow weary, nor will what he eats fail. Great and wonderful will that contemplation be. And who speaks worthily of it at this time, when the waters flow among the mountains? Meanwhile, then, let the waters flow among the mountains, and pass: when the waters pass, there is drinking in the pilgrimage, lest we fail of thirst on the way. *All the beasts of the forest will drink.* Thence you came, you were gathered from the forest. And what sort of forest? No man passed there, because no prophet had been sent there. But for building the ark, trees were cut from the forests: thence the wood, thence the beasts, thence you came. Therefore drink. *All the beasts of the forest will drink.*

**4.** *The wild asses will take up into their thirst.* By wild asses he means certain great beasts. For who does not know that wild asses are called untamed donkeys? He means, then, certain great untamed ones. For the gentiles had no yoke of the Law; many nations lived by their own ways, wandering in proud boastfulness, as if in a desert. And all the beasts indeed are thus, but the wild asses are set to signify greatness. They too will drink into their thirst; for waters flow for them too. From there the hare drinks, from there the wild ass: the hare small, and the wild ass great; the hare timid, and the wild ass fierce; each drinks from there, but each *into his own thirst.* The water does not say: I suffice for the hare, and drive away the wild ass; nor does it say this: Let the wild ass approach, but if the hare approaches, it will be snatched away. So faithfully and temperately it flows, that it so satisfies the wild ass as not to frighten the hare. The din of Tully's voice sounds, Cicero is read, there is some book, his dialogue, whether his own, or Plato's, or any of such men: the unlearned hear, the weak of lesser heart; who dares to aspire there? A din of water, and perhaps troubled; certainly yet flowing so rapaciously that a timid animal does not dare to approach and drink. To whom did it sound, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^9], and he did not dare to drink? To whom does the Psalm sound, and let him say: It is too much for me? Behold, now what the Psalm sounds, certainly the mysteries are hidden; yet it sounds in such a way that it delights even boys to hear, and the unlearned approach to drink, and, satisfied, belch in singing psalms. So the lesser beasts and the greater drink; but the greater more capaciously, because *the wild asses will take up into their thirst.* Let the lesser drink what was said: *Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also loved the Church. Wives, be subject to your husbands*[^10]. Let the lesser drink. But it was said to the Lord: *Is it lawful to dismiss a wife for any cause?* The Lord forbade it, and said it was not lawful. *Do you not know*, he says, *that God from the beginning made male and female? What God has joined, let not man separate.* Then he added: *Whoever dismisses his wife, except for the cause of fornication, makes her commit adultery; and if he marries another, he commits adultery*[^11]. He confirmed the fetter: this is expedient for the bound; let him first take care not to be bound. *Are you bound to a wife? do not seek release. Are you loosed from a wife? do not seek a wife*[^12]. If you are not yet a wild ass, and are loosed from a wife, you have there what you may drink even as a hare: and if you have taken a wife, you have not sinned. But the disciples, when they heard the Lord say that marriages cannot by any means be sundered except for the cause of fornication: *If such is the case*, they said, *with a wife, it is not expedient to marry.* And the Lord: *Not all grasp this word*[^13]. For you speak truly, that if such is the case with a wife, it is not expedient to marry; but will the wild asses alone drink? Not all grasp this word, many do not grasp it. And who are those who grasp it? *The wild asses will take up into their thirst.* What is it, *The wild asses will take up into their thirst? He who can grasp it, let him grasp it*[^14].

**5.** [v. 12.] Then the Psalm follows in its sequence: *Above them the birds of heaven will dwell.* Above whom? above the wild asses, or rather above the mountains? For from there this sense is directed: *Through the midst of the mountains the waters will pass; all the beasts of the forest will drink; the wild asses will take up into their thirst: above them the birds of heaven will dwell.* We more fittingly understand above the mountains, because this too is similar in this creation. Above the mountains the birds can dwell, above the wild asses they cannot: we would understand this, if necessity compelled. Above the mountains, then, the birds of heaven will dwell. We see these birds dwell above the mountains: but many of them dwell in the plains, many in the valleys, many in the groves, many in the gardens, not all above the mountains. There are certain birds which dwell only above the mountains. This name signifies certain spiritual souls: the birds are spiritual hearts, which enjoy the free air. These birds rejoice in the serenity of the sky: but yet their feeding is in the mountains; there they will dwell. You know the mountains; they have already been treated. The mountains are the Prophets, the mountains the Apostles, the mountains all the preachers of truth. Whoever wishes to be spiritual, let him dwell there: let him not stray in his own heart; let him dwell, let him persevere by vowing. We have birds signifying something spiritual. Not in vain was it said, *Your youth will be renewed like the eagle's*[^15]. Not in vain was it said of Abraham, *But the birds he did not divide*[^16]. Abraham in that sufficiently mystical sacrifice took three animals; a three-year-old ram, a three-year-old cow, a three-year-old she-goat, and a turtledove, and a dove. The ram was divided, and the parts set against one another; the she-goat divided, the parts likewise set against one another; the cow divided, so it was done with its flesh too: and Scripture added: *But the birds he did not divide.* Then a three-year-old ram is mentioned, a three-year-old cow, a three-year-old she-goat: of the age of the birds it is silent. Whence, I ask you, except that in the birds are signified certain spiritual men, whose temporal age is for this reason passed over in silence, because they meditate on eternal things, and by desire and understanding transcend all temporal things? Spiritual men who judge all things, and are judged by no one: and so they alone are not divided into heresies and schisms. By the ram are understood the superiors; for they lead the flocks: by the cow is understood the people of the Jews; for it had the yoke of the Law, under which it labored: by the she-goat is understood the Church of the gentiles; for it leaped in certain free pastures, and fed on the bitter wild olive. These animals are called three-year-old, because grace was revealed in the third time. For the first was before the Law; the second, from when the Law was given; the third, which is now, from when the kingdom of heaven is preached. What then do we say, that the ram is not divided? Were not bishops the authors of schisms and heresies? But further, if the peoples themselves were not divided, that is, if the cow were not divided, if the she-goat were not divided; perhaps those would have blushed in their divisions, and returned to the union. The leaders are divided, the peoples too are divided, so that the blind follows the blind, and they fall together into the pit: they are set against one another. *But the birds he did not divide.* The spiritual have no division, they do not contemplate schisms: peace is in them, they keep it in others as far as they can; where they fail in others, they hold it in themselves. *If there will be there*, he says, *a son of peace, your peace will rest upon him; if not, it will return to you*[^17]. He is not a son of peace, he wished to be divided, your peace will return to you; because the birds he did not divide. The furnace will come too: for Abraham sat there until evening, and a great terror of the day of judgment came. For that evening is the end of the age; and that furnace, the coming day of judgment. The furnace too divided between those things in the midst which had been divided. If the furnace passed through the midst, it separated some to the right, some to the left. There are, then, certain carnal men, and yet contained in the bosom of the Church, living according to a certain way of their own, for whom we fear lest they be seduced by heretics: for as long as they are carnal, they are divisible. *The birds* indeed *he did not divide;* the carnal are divided. *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal*[^18]. And how is it proved that the carnal are divided? He adds: *For when each of you says: I am of Paul, but I of Apollos, and I of Cephas; are you not carnal, and walking according to man?*[^19] I ask you, brothers, hear, and make progress; shake yourselves out of the carnal place, pass over into the turtledove and the dove: *for the birds he did not divide.* But whoever remains such, and according to a certain way of life apt for the carnal, and does not withdraw from the bosom of the Church, and is not seduced by heretics, so as to be divided to the opposite side; the furnace will come, and he cannot be set to the right without the furnace. But if he does not wish to suffer the furnace, let him pass over into the turtledove and the dove. He who can grasp it, let him grasp it. But if it will not be so, and he has *built upon the foundation wood, hay, stubble*, that is, has built worldly loves upon the foundation of his faith; yet if Christ be in the foundation, so that he himself hold the first place in the heart, and nothing at all be set before him; such men too are carried, such too are put up with: the furnace will come, it will burn the wood, hay, stubble: *but he himself*, he says, *will be saved, yet so as through fire*[^20]. This the furnace will do; some it will separate to the left, others it will in a way refine to the right. *But the birds he did not divide.* But let the birds see, if they are such birds as dwell above those mountains; they ought not to follow the loftiness of their own heart, of such as it is said, *They set their mouth in heaven*[^21]. Lest they be carried off by the winds, let them rest in the mountains. They have the authority of the saints, let them rest in the mountains, in the Apostles, in the Prophets: there let such birds dwell, because in the mountains they find rocks, certain firmaments of precepts. For just as that one rock is Christ, the Word of God; so the many words of God are many rocks, and these rocks are in the mountains. See the birds dwelling there: *Above them the birds of heaven will dwell.*

**6.** But do not suppose that these birds of heaven follow their own authority; see what the Psalm says: *From the midst of the rocks they will give their voice.* Now if I say to you: Believe, for Cicero said this, Plato said this, Pythagoras said this; who of you will not laugh at me? For I would be a bird who do not utter my voice from the rock. What ought each of you to say to me? what ought he to say who is so instructed? *If anyone evangelizes to you anything other than what you have received, let him be anathema*[^22]. Why do you tell me of Plato, and of Cicero, and of Virgil? You have before you the rocks of the mountains, from the midst of the rocks give me your voice. *From the midst of the rocks they will give their voice.* Let those be heard who hear from the rock: let them be heard, because in those many rocks too the rock is heard: *for the rock was Christ*[^23]. Let them, then, be heard gladly, giving their voice from the midst of the rocks: nothing is sweeter than such a voice of the winged ones. They sound, and the rocks resound: they sound, the spiritual men dispute; the rocks resound, the testimonies of Scripture answer. Behold, thence the birds from the midst of the rocks give their voice; for they dwell in the mountains.

**7.** [v. 13.] Whence do the mountains themselves and those rocks have a voice? For, that we may be watered by the Scriptures, we flee for refuge to the apostle Paul. Whence does he have it? We flee for refuge to Isaiah. Isaiah, whence? Hear whence: *Watering the mountains from his higher places.* Now if a gentile man, uncircumcised, comes to us, about to believe in Christ, we give him Baptism, nor do we call him back to those works of the Law. And if a Jew asks us why we do so, we sound from the rock, we say: This Peter did, this Paul did: from the midst of the rocks we give our voice. But that rock, Peter himself, the great mountain, when he was praying and seeing that vision, was watered from the higher places. The apostle Paul says to the gentiles: *If you are circumcised, Christ will profit you nothing*[^24]. Paul says this, as a mountain: thence we say it, sounding from the rock. Let the Lord water the rock itself from his higher places. For when this rock was still rough in unbelief, wishing to water it from his higher places that water might flow in the valley, he cried out: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^25] He did not read him a prophet, he did not read him another apostle; for a great mountain would scorn all these things: he watered him from his higher places; and immediately, watered and now wishing to flow: *Lord*, he says, *what do you bid me do?*[^26] Take that mountain or rock, whence you may give your voice; take it, and see it watered from the higher places, and flow out in the lower places. Hear this in one place too: *Whether*, he says, *we are beside ourselves to God, or whether we are sober for you*[^27]. What he says, *we are beside ourselves;* you cannot grasp: for we go beyond all these carnal things, you are still carnal. To God, then, we are beside ourselves; and what we see when we go beyond, we cannot speak: for there *he heard ineffable words, which it is not lawful for a man to speak*[^28]. What then, those carnal men, those hares, say, shall we not be watered? will nothing reach us? And how does he send forth springs in the valleys? and how through the midst of the mountains will the waters pass? To this, then, pertains: *Or whether we are sober for you.* Whence this? whom do we imitate? *The love*, he says, *of Christ compels us*[^29]. You, partaker of the Word, though today spiritual, yesterday carnal, do you disdain to descend to the carnal, when the Word himself was made flesh, that he might dwell in you?

**8.** Let us, then, bless the Lord, and praise him who waters the mountains from his higher places. Thence will come the watering to the earth, thence the lowly too will be satisfied: for there follows, *From the fruit of your works the earth will be satisfied.* What is it, *From the fruit of your works?* Let no one glory in his own works; but *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^30]. By your grace it is satisfied, when it is satisfied: let it not say that grace was given for its own merits. If it is called grace, it is given freely: if it is rendered for works, a wage is rendered. Receive it freely, then, because, impious, you are justified. *From the fruit of your works the earth will be satisfied.*

**9.** [v. 14.] *Bringing forth hay for the cattle, and grass for the service of men.* It is true, I see, I recognize the creation: the earth brings forth hay for the cattle, and grass for the service of men. But I see other cattle of the Lord too, which are signified when it is said, *You shall not muzzle the mouth of the threshing ox*: for he says of this very ox, *Is it about the oxen that God is concerned?* For our sake, then, Scripture says it. How, then, does the earth bring forth hay for the cattle? Because *the Lord ordained that those who announce the Gospel should live from the Gospel*[^31]. He sent preachers, and said to them: *Eat all the things that are set before you by them; for the laborer is worthy of his wage*[^32]. For when he had said, *Eat the things that are set before you;* lest they should say: We will be shameless toward others' tables when we are in need, do you wish us to be so brazen? No, he says, that is not their gift, but your wage. The wage of what? What do they give? what do they receive? They give spiritual things, they receive carnal; they give gold, they receive hay. *For all flesh is hay, and the glory of the flesh as the flower of hay*[^33]. All temporal things that overflow and abound for you are the hay of the cattle. Why? Because they are carnal. Hear of which cattle it is the hay. *If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal things?*[^34] This the Apostle said, such an announcer, so laborious, so untiring, so practiced, that he gave the hay itself to the earth. *I*, he says, *have used none of these things*[^35]. He showed that it was owed to him, and did not take it; nor did he condemn those who took what was owed. For those were to be condemned who demanded what was not owed, not those receiving their wage: yet he gave even his own wage. Not because another has given to you, do you not owe to another; otherwise you will not be the watered earth that brings forth hay for the cattle. *From the fruit of your works*, he says, *the earth will be satisfied: bringing forth hay for the cattle.* Do not be barren, bring forth hay for the cattle: if the cattle do not want your hay, let them yet not find you barren. You receive spiritual things, render carnal: they are owed to the soldier, you render them to the soldier; you are a provincial of Christ. *Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? who plants a vineyard, and does not eat of its fruit? who feeds a flock, and does not partake of its milk?*[^36] I do not say this so that these things may be done toward me. There was a certain soldier who even gave the provincial his ration: but yet let the provincial render the ration. But that I may rather say it from this; they are cattle: *You shall not muzzle the mouth of the threshing ox. Bringing forth*, he says, *hay for the cattle;* and as if expounding this he added, *and grass for the service of men:* lest you should not understand what was said, *Bringing forth hay for the cattle*, by repetition he expounded what he set down before. For what he called *hay* above, he afterward named *grass;* but what he had said *for the cattle*, this he said *for the service of men.* Therefore for service, and not for liberty. Where, then, is, *You were called to liberty?* But hear the same man himself: *For though I am free from all, I have made myself the servant of all, that I might gain the more*[^37]. And to whom did he say, *You were called to liberty?* and what did he add? *Only do not give your liberty as an occasion for the flesh, but through love serve one another*[^38]. Those he had made free, he made servants; not by condition, but yet by the redemption of Christ; not by necessity, but by love: *Through love*, he says, *serve one another.* But we serve one another in Christ, he says; not the peoples, not the carnal, not the weak. You serve Christ well, if you serve those whom Christ served. Was it not said of him, *Serving many well?* The prophet is read: it is wont to be taken of no one but Christ. Yet let us hear his voice properly in the Gospel too: *Whoever*, he says, *among you wishes to be greater, will be your servant*[^39]. He made you his servant, who made you free by his blood. Tell us this, for you speak truly. Hear him in another place: *But ourselves your servants through Jesus*[^40]. Love your servants well, but in your Lord. Let him grant us to serve well. For, willing or unwilling, we are servants: and yet if we are willing, we serve not by necessity, but by love. For in a way the pride of the servants seemed to be annoyed, when the Lord said: *He will be your servant who wishes to be greater among you.* For now the sons of Zebedee were seeking the highest seats: one wished to sit at the right, the other at the left, asking through their mother what they themselves desired. The Lord did not envy them the seats, but first showed them the valley of weeping, as if he said: Do you wish to come there, where I am? come the way I come. What is it, come the way I come? Through humility. I descended from on high, and humbled, I ascend: you I found on earth, and you wish to fly before you are fed; first be nourished, be raised, bear the nest. For what does he say? how did he call them back to humility, now seeking loftiness? *Can you drink the cup that I am about to drink?* And they, proud even in this: *We can.* As Peter: *With you even unto death.* A brave man, until a woman said, *This man too was with them*[^41]. So these too: *We can. Can you? We can.* And he: *My cup indeed you will drink*, though now you cannot, *you will drink*: as to Peter, *You cannot follow me now; you will follow me afterward*[^42]. *My cup indeed you will drink; but to sit at my right hand or at my left is not mine to give you*[^43]. What is it, *It is not mine to give you?* It is not mine to give to the proud. Now, those to whom I speak, you are proud; therefore I said, *It is not mine to give you.* But perhaps they would say: We will be humble. Then it will not be you: I said, *to you.* I did not say: I will not give to the humble: but, I will not give to the proud. And the king who from proud becomes humble will not be what he was.

**10.** Therefore the preachers of the word are both cattle and servants. Let the earth, if it is watered, bring forth *hay for the cattle, and grass for the service of men.* For this is the fruit, that what was said in the Gospel may come to be: *That they too may receive you into the eternal dwellings*[^44]. See what you do with the hay, see what you buy with a cheap thing. *That they may receive you*, he says, *into the eternal dwellings*: where they will be, there let them receive you. Why this? Because *he who receives a just man in the name of a just man will receive the reward of a just man: and he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet will receive the reward of a prophet: and he who gives a cup of cold water to one of these least ones, only in the name of a disciple; amen I say to you, he will not lose his reward*[^45]. What reward will he not lose? They will receive you into the eternal dwellings. Who will not hasten? who will not run more eagerly? If you are earth, be watered from the fruit of the works of God: do not say: There are none with whom we may do it; our preachers, the threshing cattle, the serving men do not need us. Yet seek, lest anyone be in need: in short, even the one who is not in need, let him find in you what he is unwilling to take. For he takes the good will, when you take peace: for though he does not seek the gift, he seeks the fruit. Yet seek, lest anyone be in need; and do not say: If he asks, I will give. Do you wait, then, until he asks? Do you so feed the ox of God, as a passing beggar? To that one asking you give, because it is written, *Give to everyone who asks you*[^46]. Of this one, what is written? *Blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor*[^47]. Seek the one to whom you may give: *for blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor*, who anticipates the voice of the one about to ask. If the soldiers of Christ are so in need among you that they even ask; see lest they judge you, before they ask. How, you say, do I seek? Be curious, be provident; look ahead, attend whence each one lives, whence he supports himself, whence he has. This curiosity of yours will not be reproved; you will be earth *bringing forth hay for the cattle, and grass for the service of men.* Be curious, and understand concerning the needy and poor. One comes to you, to ask; another anticipate, lest he ask. For as of the one who seeks you it was said, *Give to everyone who asks you;* so of the one whom you ought to seek it was said: Let alms sweat in your hand, until you find a just man to whom to give it. For though it must be given to these poor who ask—for God did not forbid alms to them, since Christ says of them, *When you make a feast, call the blind, the lame, the weak, who have not the means to repay you; but it will be repaid to you in the resurrection of the just*[^48]: call them too, feed them too; feast, when they feast; delight, when they are fattened; for they on your bread, you on the righteousness of God. Let no one say to you: It was commanded by Christ that it be given to a servant of God, not to a beggar. Far from it; an utterly impious man says this. Give to him, but much more to that one. For that one asks, and in the voice of the one asking you recognize to whom you give: but that other, the less he asks, the more you must be watchful to anticipate the one about to ask; or perhaps the one not now about to ask, and at some time about to condemn. So be curious about these things, my brothers; you will find the need of many servants of God, only if you wish to find it. But because the excuse delights you by which you wish to say: We did not know; for this reason you do not find it.

**11.** The Lord himself had purses, into which necessaries were put, and money was possessed for the uses of those who were with him and his: for when it is said, *He hungered*[^49], the evangelist does not lie. He willed to hunger for your sake, lest you hunger in him, who became poor, though he was rich, that by his poverty we might be enriched. For he had purses; and it is said of certain religious women that they walked where his feet went, evangelizing, and ministered to him from their substance. Those women are named in the Gospel, where there was a certain wife too of a certain Chuza, Herod's steward. See what was done. Paul was to be one who would at some time seek no such thing, and give all to the provincials. But because many weak men were going to seek these things, Christ rather took up the person of the weak. Was Paul more sublime? surely not more than Christ! Christ more sublime, because more merciful. For when he saw that Paul would not seek these things, he provided that he might not condemn the one who would seek, and gave an example to the weak: just as, when he saw that many would go ready and rejoicing to the martyrdom of passion, about to exult in the very passion, brave, hundredfold, ripe for the barn; yet, lest certain weak ones, whom he saw able to be disturbed by the coming passion, should fail, but rather join the human will to the will of the Creator, Christ willed to take up their person in the passion, saying, *My soul is sorrowful unto death;* and again: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me*[^50]. He taught what the weak one would say, but going on he showed what the weak one ought to do: *Yet not what I will, but what you will, Father*[^51]. So as in the passion he sustained the person of the weak, that being prefigured in his body, because they too are his members; for not in vain was it said, *Your eyes saw my unformed substance, and in your book all will be written*[^52]: so too in having purses, and in as it were demanding a ration—not asking, but providing—he took up neediness. Zacchaeus received him, and rejoices. To whom was this a good? to Christ, or to Zacchaeus? Truly, if Zacchaeus had not received him, was there nowhere for the fabricator of the world to stay? or if Zacchaeus had not fed him, would he be in need who satisfied so many thousands of men from five loaves? When, then, anyone receives a holy man, it is bestowed not on the one received, but on the receiver. Was not Elijah fed in that famine? did not a raven bring bread and meat, the creature serving a servant of God? Yet he was sent to be fed to a widow; that something might be bestowed, not as on a soldier, but as on a provincial.

**12.** Therefore, because we were speaking, brothers, also of feeding the poor; though the Lord had purses, yet when he said to Judas who would betray him, *What you do, do quickly*[^53]; the others, not understanding what he had said, supposed that he had said it that he might prepare something to be given to the poor. For he had purses, this is written in the Gospel. Could they have suspected this, unless the Lord had this in his custom? Of these things, then, which were given and put into the purses, it was yet given also to those poor, whom God taught are not to be scorned. But if you do not scorn that one, how much more the ox by which this threshing floor is threshed? how much more your servant? He does not need food, perhaps he needs clothing. He does not need clothing, perhaps he needs a roof, perhaps he is building a church, perhaps he is laboring at something useful in the house of God; he waits for you to attend, he waits for you to understand concerning the needy and poor. You, on the contrary, hard earth, stony, not watered, or watered in vain, keep yourself ready to say: I did not know, I was not aware, no one told me. No one told you! Christ does not cease to say it, the prophet does not cease to say it: *Blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor*[^54]. You do not see the empty chest of your superior: you certainly see at least the rising building, which you are going to enter and pray in. Is it not brought before your eyes? Unless perhaps you suppose, brothers, that your superiors lay up treasure: and we know many who lay up no treasure, and are in need of daily things, of whom it is not at all believed; whom you too would find, if you wished, if you looked around, if you were watchful for this, to give fruit. I have said what I could, as much as I could. But I think we are manifest to you, as the Apostle says, that we do not say these things that you may do them toward us. May God grant that I have not said these things in vain: may God grant that you be watered earth, not stony, like that of the Jews, whence they deserved to receive stone tablets; but fruitful earth, earth which, watered, repays the husbandman. Those very men of a stony heart, which was signified by the stone tablets, gave tithes. You even groan, and nothing yet comes out. If you groan, give birth; if you are in labor, bring forth. Why an empty groaning? why a barren groaning? Are the bowels twisted, or is there nothing within to be brought forth? *Watering the mountains from his higher places: from the fruit of your works the earth will be satisfied.* Blessed are those who do it, blessed are those who hear these things fruitfully, blessed are those who do not cry out in vain. *From the fruit of your works the earth will be satisfied: bringing forth hay for the cattle, and grass for the service of men.* Why this? *That he may bring forth bread from the earth.* What bread? Christ. From what earth? From Peter, from Paul, from the other stewards of truth. Hear that it is from the earth: *We have*, he says, *this treasure in earthen vessels, that the eminence may be of the power of God*[^55]. He is the bread that came down from heaven, that he may be brought forth from the earth, when he is preached through the flesh of his servants. The earth brings forth hay, that it may bring forth bread from the earth. What earth brings forth hay? The pious peoples, the holy peoples. From what earth that bread may be brought forth? The word of God from the Apostles, from the stewards of the Sacraments of God, still walking on the earth, still carrying an earthly body.

**13.** [v. 15.] *And wine gladdens the heart of man.* Let no one prepare himself for drunkenness; rather let every man prepare himself for drunkenness. *How splendid is your inebriating cup!*[^56] We do not wish to say: Let no one be inebriated. Be inebriated, but see by what. If the splendid cup of the Lord inebriates you, this inebriation will be seen in your works, it will be seen in the holy love of righteousness, it will be seen finally in the alienation of your mind, but from earthly things to heaven. *That he may make his face glad with oil.* I see how much fruit the earth brings forth, if it produces hay for the cattle. These servants do not sell what they give; for they are not sellers of the Gospel: they give freely, because they received freely. They rejoice in your good works; for this profits you: for they do not seek the gift, but they seek the fruit. What is the gladdening of the face with oil? The grace of God, a certain brightness unto manifestation; as the Apostle says, *But to each is given the Spirit unto manifestation*[^57]. A certain grace which is perspicuous in men toward men, to win holy love, is called oil, in divine brightness; and since it appeared most excellently in Christ, the whole world loves him: who, though he was scorned here, is now adored by every nation: *For his is the kingdom, and he will have dominion over the nations*[^58]. For his grace is so great that many who do not believe in him praise him, and therefore say that they are unwilling to believe in him, because no one can fulfill what he commands. By praising they are impeded, who used to rage against him by faultfinding. Yet he is loved by all, preached by all; because excellently anointed, therefore Christ. For Christ means anointed, called Christ from the chrism. In Hebrew Messiah, in Greek Christ, in Latin Anointed: but he anoints all over his whole body. All who come, then, receive grace, that the face may be made glad with oil.

**14.** *And bread strengthens the heart of man.* What is this, brothers? He as it were compelled us to understand of what bread he was speaking. For this visible bread strengthens the stomach, strengthens the belly: there is another bread which strengthens the heart, because it is the bread of the heart. For he had already said of bread above, *That he may bring forth bread from the earth*, but he had not said what sort of bread that is. *And wine gladdens the heart of man.* He seems now to speak of spiritual wine; for that gladdens the heart of man. But still it might be supposed to be said of that wine, because the drunken seem as it were to appear glad of heart. Would that they were glad, and did not quarrel. You say to me: What is gladder than a drunk man? On the contrary, what is madder than a drunk man? what is for the most part more irascible? There is, then, a wine that truly gladdens the heart, and knows nothing but to gladden the heart. But lest you suppose that this indeed ought to be taken of spiritual wine, but not that bread of spiritual bread; that it too is spiritual, he expounded it too: *And bread*, he says, *strengthens the heart of man.* Take it of the bread, then, as you take it of the wine; hunger within, thirst within: *for blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied*[^59]. That bread is righteousness, that wine is righteousness: it is truth, the truth is Christ. *I am*, he says, *the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^60]; and: *I am the vine, you the branches*[^61]. *And bread strengthens the heart of man.*

**15.** [v. 16.] *The trees of the field will be satisfied*: but with this grace brought forth from the earth. *The trees of the field*, the peoples of the nations. *And the cedars of Lebanon which he planted.* The cedars of Lebanon, the powerful in the world, they too will be satisfied. The bread, and wine, and oil of Christ reached the senators, the nobles, the kings; the trees of the field were satisfied. First the humble were satisfied, then the cedars of Lebanon too, but *those which he himself planted*: pious cedars, religious faithful; for such he planted. For there are impious cedars of Lebanon too, because *The Lord will break the cedars of Lebanon*[^62]. For Lebanon is a mountain; there those trees, even according to the letter, are most aged and most excellent. But Lebanon is interpreted, as we read in those who wrote on these things, "whitening." Lebanon is called whitening: but the whitening of this world seems to be that which now shines and gleams in its pomps. There are there cedars of Lebanon which the Lord planted; those very ones will be satisfied which the Lord planted. For *a tree*, he says, *which my Father has not planted, will be rooted up*[^63]. *And the cedars of Lebanon which he planted.*

**16.** [v. 17.] *There the sparrows will nest. The house of the coot is their leader.* Where will the sparrows nest? In the cedars of Lebanon. We have already heard who the cedars of Lebanon are, the nobles of the world, lofty in birth, wealth, honors: and those cedars too are satisfied, yet those which he himself planted. In these cedars the sparrows nest. Who are the sparrows? Sparrows are indeed birds and birds of heaven, but small birds are usually called sparrows. There are, then, certain spiritual men nesting in the cedars of Lebanon: that is, there are certain servants of God hearing in the Gospel, *Leave all your things*, or, *sell all your things, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^64]. And not only the great heard this, but the small heard it too, and the small too wished to do this, and to be spiritual; not to be joined to wives, not to be worn down by the care of children, not to have their own dwellings to which they would be bound, but to go into a certain common life. But what did these sparrows leave? For they seem the small sparrows of this world. What did they leave? what great thing did they leave? One was converted, left his father's needy cell, scarcely one bed and one chest. Yet he was converted, he became a sparrow, he sought spiritual things. Good, excellent; let us not insult him, let us not say: You left nothing. Let not the one who left much be proud. Peter, that he might follow the Lord, we know was a fisherman, what could he leave? Or his brother Andrew, or the sons of Zebedee, John and James, themselves too fishermen; and yet what did they say? *Behold, we have left all, and followed you*[^65]. The Lord did not say to him: You have forgotten your poverty; what did you leave, that you should receive the whole world? He left much, my brothers, he left much, who left not only whatever he had, but even whatever he desired to have. For what poor man does not swell with hope of this world? who does not daily desire to increase what he has? This cupidity was cut off: it was going on to immensity, it received a measure, and was nothing left? Surely Peter left the whole world, and Peter received the whole world. As having nothing, and possessing all things. Many do this: those do these things who have little, and come, and become useful sparrows. They seem small, because they do not have the loftiness of secular dignity: they nest in the cedars of Lebanon. For the cedars of Lebanon too, the nobles and rich and lofty of this world, since they hear with fear, *Blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and poor*[^66]; attend to their goods, their estates, and all their superfluous abundance, by which they seem lofty, and offer them to the servants of God; they give fields, they give gardens, they build churches, monasteries, they gather sparrows, that the sparrows may nest in the cedars of Lebanon. Therefore the *cedars of Lebanon which* the Lord *planted* are satisfied, and *there the sparrows will nest.* Attend to the whole earth, whether it is not so. That I might say these things, I not only believed, but also saw; experience itself gave me the understanding. Ask the broadest lands, you who know them, and see in how many cedars of Lebanon those sparrows, of whom I spoke, nest.

**17.** But yet, my brothers, the sparrows themselves, if they are spiritual, although they nest in the cedars of Lebanon, ought not to hold the cedars of Lebanon for a great thing, and suppose those to be superior, by whom their necessities are supplied. For they are sparrows; but those are cedars of Lebanon. Therefore *the house of the coot is the leader* of the sparrows. Although the sparrows nest in the cedars of Lebanon, yet the cedars of Lebanon are not the leaders of the sparrows. Behold, the trees of the field will be satisfied, all the peoples; and the cedars of Lebanon will be satisfied, which the Lord planted, all the noble and lofty faithful. There, that is in the cedars of Lebanon, the sparrows will nest; they will offer the branches of their resources for gathering the small spiritual ones. These things the cedars of Lebanon which the Lord planted offer, do; they do it, and gladly do it; they know what they do, they know what they receive. But the sparrows, although they will nest in the cedars of Lebanon: *The house of the coot is their leader.* What is the house of the coot? The coot, as we all know, is a sea bird; it is either in marshes, or in the sea. It has a certain house not easily on the shore of the land, or never; but in those things that are in the midst of the water: usually, then, in the rocks which the water surrounds. We understand, then, that the rock is the fit house of the coot; nowhere does it dwell more strongly and firmly than in the rock. In what sort of rock? One set in the sea. Even if it is beaten by the waves, yet it breaks the waves, it is not broken: this is what the rock set in the sea has great. How many waves have beaten our rock, the Lord Christ? The Jews were dashed against him; they were broken, he remained whole. And let each one, imitating Christ, be so in this world, that is, in this sea, where he can feel only storms and tempests, as to yield to no wind, to no wave, but to receive all things, and remain whole. Therefore the house of the coot is both strong and humble. The coot does not have a house in lofty places; nothing is firmer than that house, and nothing humbler. In the cedars indeed the sparrows nest, because of present necessity: but they have that rock as their leader, which is beaten by the waves, and not broken; for they imitate the passions of Christ. And if perhaps the cedars of Lebanon are angry, and stir up some trouble or scandal to the servants of God in their branches, the sparrows will indeed fly from there; but woe to the cedar remaining without the nests of sparrows. For the sparrows will not be shipwrecked, will not perish; because *the house of the coot is their leader.*

**18.** [v. 18.] What then follows? *The highest mountains for the stags.* The stags, the great, the spiritual, surpassing in their course all the thorny things of brambles and forests. *Who perfects*, he says, *my feet like a stag's, and will set me upon high places*[^67]. Let them hold the high mountains, the high precepts of God; let them think on lofty things, let them hold those things that stand out greatly in the Scriptures, let them be justified in the highest things: for those highest mountains are for the stags. What of the lowly beasts? what of the hare? what of the hedgehog? The hare is a small and weak animal, the hedgehog also thorny: the one a timid animal, the other an animal covered with thorns. What do the thorns signify, except sinners? He who sins daily, even if not great sins, is covered with the tiniest thorns. In that he fears, he is a hare; in that he is covered with the tiniest sins, he is a hedgehog: and he cannot hold those lofty and perfect precepts. For those highest mountains are for the stags. What then? do these perish? No. For the highest mountains are for the stags in such a way that you may see for these too what follows: *The rock is a refuge for the hedgehogs and the hares.* Because the Lord was made a refuge for the poor. Set that rock on the land, it is a refuge for the hedgehogs and hares: set it in the sea, it is the house of the coot. Everywhere the rock is useful. And in the mountains it itself is useful: the mountains without the foundation of the rock would fall into the deep. Was it not said a while ago of the mountains, *There the birds of heaven will dwell; from the midst of the rocks they will give their voices?* Everywhere, then, the rock is our refuge: whether it be lofty in the mountains, or be beaten by the waves in the sea and not broken, or be made solid on the land; to it the stags, to it the coot, to it the hare and the hedgehog. Let the hares beat their breasts, and let the hedgehogs confess their sins: though they be covered with small and daily sins; yet the rock is not lacking to them, which taught them to say, *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^68]. *The rock is a refuge for the hedgehogs and the hares.*

**19.** [v. 19.] *He made the moon for the seasons.* We understand spiritually the Church growing from the least, and in this mortality of life as it were growing old; but in order to draw near to the sun. I do not mean this moon visible to the eyes, but that which is signified by this name. When the Church was obscure, when it did not yet appear, did not yet stand out, men were seduced, and it was said: This is the Church, here is Christ; *that they might shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart*[^69]. Now how blind is one who errs at the full moon? *He made the moon for the seasons.* For here the Church passes temporally; for this mortality will not always be here: this being increased and diminished will at some time pass away; it was made for the seasons. *The sun knew its setting.* And here, what sun, except that sun of righteousness, whose not rising for them the impious will lament on the day of judgment? Who are going to say on that day: *So we erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness did not shine for us, and the sun did not rise for us*[^70]. That sun rises for the one who understands Christ. But Christ withdraws from the understanding of one who has so been angry with his brother as to hold a hatred. And so, *be angry, and do not sin*[^71]. Love, even if it is angry sometimes, in order to correct, is not held guilty; because the anger has not grown old, so as to turn into hatred. But if anger has turned into hatred, the sun sets upon your wrath. *Let not*, therefore, *the sun set upon your wrath*[^72].

**20.** Nor suppose, brothers, that for this reason the sun is to be adored by some, because the sun in the Scriptures sometimes signifies Christ. For such is the madness of men; as though something were said to be adorable, when it is said: The sun signifies Christ. Adore the rock, then, because it signifies Christ. *As a sheep he was led to the victim*[^73]; adore the sheep too, because it signifies Christ. *The lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered*[^74]; adore the lion too, because it signifies Christ. See how many things signify Christ; all these are Christ in likeness, not in property. Do you seek the property of Christ? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*[^75]. Behold the property of Christ, through which you were made. Do you wish to hear too the property by which you were restored? *And the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us*[^76]. The rest are likenesses. Understand, be capable of Scripture, that you may see one thing set before your eyes, another hinted to your heart.

**21.** That sun, then—now we say it securely—that sun of righteousness, not without cause does not rise for the impious, even if they wish it: for Wisdom herself says, *The wicked will seek me, and will not find me. They will seek, and will not find.* And why? Because they hate wisdom. Wisdom herself speaks, and says, *The wicked will seek me, and will not find me; for they have hated wisdom*[^77]. If, then, they hate it, why do they seek it? They seek it, not to enjoy it, but to be puffed up; they seek it in words, they hate it in their ways. *For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the deceitful, and will take himself away from thoughts that are without understanding*[^78]. That sun, then, does not rise for the impious, does not rise for the bad. But truly of this sun what is said? *Who makes his sun rise on the good and the bad, and rains on the just and the unjust*[^79]. Therefore of that sun of righteousness this psalm signifies something or other according to the mystery; for we see these things happen in the creation also according to the appearance of visible things: *The sun knew its setting.* What is it, *The sun knew its setting?* Christ knew his passion: the setting of Christ is the passion of Christ. But does the sun so set that it does not rise? *Shall not he who sleeps add that he rise again?* did not he himself say, *I slept, troubled?* and of him is it said, *Be exalted above the heavens, O God?*[^80] Therefore: *The sun knew its setting*: what is it, *knew?* He approved it, it pleased him. And whence do we show that he knew it, that is, that it pleased him? For what does God not know? what does Christ not know? And yet he is going to say to some at the end: *I do not know you*[^81]. As, then, there, *I do not know you*, is not as it were: You are unknown to me, but, You do not please me; so here too, *he knew his setting*, his setting pleased him. For if it had displeased him, how would he suffer it? For to a man, because he is not that sun, even if his passion displeases him, he suffers even what he does not wish. But he would not suffer, unless it pleased him: that is, unless he knew his setting, he would not set; because he himself says, *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again: no one takes it from me, but I myself lay it down of myself*[^82]. Therefore, *he knew his setting.*

**22.** [vv. 20, 21.] And what, when the sun set, when the Lord suffered? A certain darkness came upon the Apostles, their hope failed, to whom he first seemed great and the redeemer of all. Why? *You set the darkness, and night was made: in it all the beasts of the forest will pass through. The young lions roaring, to seize, seeking from God their food.* What shall I understand spiritually by the young lions, except the spiritual forces of wickedness? what shall I understand except the evil demons, which demons are fed on the errors of men? For there are princes of demons, and there are certain contemptible demons. These demons seek to seduce souls, but where the sun has not risen; for it is dark. And when it is dark, the young lions seek whom they may devour. The greater lion himself, the prince of all such lions, what is said of him? *Do you not know that your adversary the devil, like a roaring lion, goes about, seeking whom he may devour?*[^83] Therefore *seeking from God their food;* because no one can even be tempted by the devil, except with God permitting. Holy Job was before the devil, and yet far from him: before him in sight, removed from him in power. When would he dare to tempt even the flesh, or the resources which Job possessed, unless he had received power? But why is power given? Either to condemn the impious, or to prove the pious. Justly does the Lord do all this; and the devil has power over no one, or over anything of his, unless he grant it whose power is highest and most sublime. So to the devil, so to man there is no power over a man, unless it be given from above. The judge of the living and the dead stood before a human judge, and the human judge puffed himself up, seeing Christ before him, and said, *Do you not know that I have power to kill you and to release you?* But he who had come even to teach him, by whom he was being judged, said, *You would have no power over me, unless it were given you from above*[^84]. Both man and devil and any demons harm only with power received: but they do not harm those who make progress. To the bad they are as fire to hay; to the good they are as fire to gold. Judas was eaten as hay; Job was proved as gold. *You set the darkness, and night was made: in it all the beasts of the forest will pass through.* Now the beasts of the forest in another way: for these things are always understood in one way and another; as the Lord himself is a lamb, himself a lion. What is so diverse as a lamb and a lion? But what sort of lamb? One that would conquer the wolf, conquer the lion. Himself the rock, himself the shepherd, himself the door. The shepherd enters through the door: and he says, *I am the good shepherd;* and he says, *I am the door*[^85]. The name of the lion itself signifies the Lord; because *The lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered*[^86], and the devil too; because *He trampled on the lion and the dragon*[^87]. Learn to understand thus, when these things are said figuratively; lest perhaps where you have read that the rock signifies Christ, you suppose everywhere that the rock is Christ. It signifies one thing and another, just as with a letter, see in what place it is set, and there you understand its force. If you hear the first letter in the name of God, and suppose it must always be set there, you will delete it from the name of the devil. For from the same letter the name of God begins, from which the name of the devil begins; and nothing is so disjoined as God and the devil. See, then, how absurd, in things both human and divine, would be one who said of the single letter D: It ought not to be at the head of the name of the devil, and when you ask, why? he answers: I read this letter in the name of God. Such a man is laughed at; for he is not even such a one to whom you would deign to render a reason. Do not, then, so childishly understand even these divine things, that perhaps some one of you, because I said above that the beasts of the forest signify the gentiles, but now I say that the beasts of the forest signify demons and angels of transgression, should suppose that I am saying something contrary to himself. For they are likenesses, and in whatever place they are, they are expounded by their context. *In it all the beasts of the forest will pass through.* Where? In the night which the Lord set, because *the sun knew its setting. The young lions roaring, to seize, seeking from God their food.* Rightly the Lord, about to come to his setting, himself the sun of righteousness knowing his setting, said to his disciples, as to a coming darkness, with the lion going about to seek whom he might devour, because that lion would devour no one unless he asked: *This night*, he says, *Satan asked to sift you like wheat; and I prayed for you, Peter, that your faith might not fail*[^88]. Was not Peter, when he denied three times, already among the teeth of the lion? *The young lions roaring, to seize, and to seek from God their food.*

**23.** [v. 22.] *The sun rose.* He who said, *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again; knew his setting*, and laid it down; *the sun rose*, and he took it up again. *The sun rose*, because the sun set, but the sun was not extinguished. Still to those who do not understand Christ, it is night; still for them the sun has not risen: let them press on, that they may understand, lest they be seized by the roaring lion. For behold, those for whom it has risen, the young lions do not dare to invade. For there follows: *The sun rose, and they were gathered together, and they will lie down in their dens.* More and more, where this sun rises, that it may be understood by the world, and Christ glorified in the whole world, the young lions are gathered together; those demons withdraw from the persecution of the Church, which were instigating the persecution of the house of God, by working in the sons of distrust. For it is said: *According to the prince of the power of the air, who now works in the sons of unbelief*[^89]. Now, then, because none of them dares to persecute the Church, *the sun rose, and they were gathered together.* And where are they? *And they will lie down in their dens.* Their dens are the hearts of the unbelievers. How many carry lions lying down in their hearts? They do not burst out thence, they do not make an attack on this pilgrim Jerusalem. Why do they not do it? Because now *the sun rose*, and shines in the whole world.

**24.** [v. 23.] Therefore see what follows, that *the sun rose, and they were gathered together, and they will lie down in their dens.* What of you, O man of God? what of you, O Church of God? what of you, O body of Christ, whose head is in heaven? what do you do, O man, his unity? *Man will go out*, he says, *to his work.* Let this man, then, work good works in the security of the peace of the Church, let him work to the end. For at some time there will be a certain darkening, and there will be a certain attack, but in the evening, that is, at the end of the world: but now in peace and tranquillity the Church works; because *man will go out to his work, and to his labor until evening.*

**25.** [v. 24.] *How magnified are your works, O Lord!* Rightly great, rightly lofty. Where were these works so great made? what was that standing-place of God where he stood, or what that sitting-place where he sat, and worked these things? what place where he worked these things? whence did these so beautiful things first proceed? If you take it to the letter, all the ordered creation, running in order, beautiful in order, rising in order, setting in order, carrying out all the seasons in order, whence did it proceed? But the Church herself, how did she receive increases, successions, perfection? How is she destined to a certain end of immortality? By what proclamations is she preached? by what mysteries is she commended? by what sacraments is she hidden? by what preaching is she revealed? Where did God do these things? I see great works: *How magnified are your works, O Lord!* I seek where he did them, I find no place; but I see what follows: *You made all things in wisdom.* Therefore you made all things in Christ. He scorned, he slapped, he spat upon, he crowned with thorns, he crucified, all things you made in him. I hear, I hear what you announce to men of that soldier of yours; what you preach to the nations of that holy herald, Christ the Power of God, and the Wisdom of God. Let the Jews mock Christ crucified, because he is a scandal to them; let the pagans mock Christ crucified, because he is foolishness to them: *But we*, he says, *preach Christ crucified, to the Jews indeed a scandal, but to the gentiles foolishness; but to those called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the Power of God, and the Wisdom of God*[^90]. *You made all things in wisdom.*

**26.** *The earth is filled with your creation.* The earth is filled with the creation of Christ. And how? As we see: for what was not created by the Father through the Son? Whatever walks and creeps on the earth, whatever swims in the waters, whatever flies in the air, whatever is carried around in the sky, how much more the earth, the whole world is the creation of God. But something or other here signifies a certain new creation, of which the Apostle says: *If anyone is a new creation in Christ, the old things have passed away; behold, all things have been made new: but all things are from God*[^91]. The new creation that was made is all who believe in Christ, putting off the old man, and putting on the new. *The earth is filled with your creation.* In one place of the earth he was crucified, in one small place that grain fell into the earth, and was put to death; but it brought forth great fruit. You were solitary, Lord Jesus, until you should pass over; I recognize in another psalm your voice, by which you said, *I am solitary, until I pass over*[^92]. Solitary, then, you were, until you should pass over; solitary you were, when you knew your setting: but from setting to rising you passed over. You rose, you shone, you were glorified, when you ascended into heaven, and *the earth is filled with your creation.* The Psalm, brothers, we have not yet finished; but let us defer something of it too, in the name of Christ, to the Lord's Day.
