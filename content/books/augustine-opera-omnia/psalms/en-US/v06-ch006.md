# Exposition of Psalm 127

**1.** [v. 1.] Among all the songs whose title is *A Song of ascents*, this psalm received something more in its title, which is added: *of Solomon.* For it is headed thus: *A Song of ascents, of Solomon.* And so its title, more unusual than the rest, has made us attentive, so that we ask why *of Solomon* has been added. For what *A Song of ascents* is need not be repeated often; for many things have been said on this, since the voice of one ascending sings, in the affection of devotion and love, to that heavenly Jerusalem, for which we sigh as long as we are pilgrims, and where we will rejoice when we have returned from our pilgrimage. To her everyone who makes progress ascends; from her everyone who fails falls. Do not seek to ascend with the feet, nor think that you descend with the feet: by loving God, you ascend; by loving the world, you fall. These, then, are the songs of those who love, who burn with a certain holy desire. They are aflame who sing these things from the heart, whose burning heart is found also in their ways, in good conduct, in works according to the commandments of God, in contempt for temporal things, in love for things eternal. But why *of Solomon* has been added, I will tell your charity as far as the Lord has inspired.

**2.** Solomon in his time was the son of David, that great man, through whom the Holy Spirit worked many holy precepts and wholesome admonitions and divine mysteries in the divine Letters. For Solomon himself was a lover of women, and was reproved by God: and that desire was so much a snare to him that he was even forced by women to sacrifice to idols, as Scripture testifies of him[^1]. But if, when he fell, the things that were said through him were blotted out, it would be judged that he himself had said them, that they were not said through him. So in this too the mercy of God and his Spirit acted most excellently, that whatever good was said through Solomon should be attributed to God; but the sin of the man, to the man. What wonder that Solomon fell among the people of God? Did not Adam fall in paradise? Did not the angel fall from heaven, and become the devil? Therefore we are taught that our hope is to be placed in no man. For this Solomon too had built a temple to the Lord, in a type indeed and a figure of the future Church and of the body of the Lord[^2]; whence he says in the Gospel: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^3]: because, then, he had built that temple, the true Solomon built a temple for himself—our Lord Jesus Christ, the true peacemaker. For the name 'Solomon' is interpreted 'Peacemaker': but he is the true peacemaker, of whom the apostle says: *For he is our peace, who made both one*[^4]. He is the true peacemaker, who joined to himself the two walls coming from different directions, for which he was made the cornerstone: the people coming from the circumcision of believers, and the people coming from the foreskin of the Gentiles and of those of them who believed; he made one Church out of two peoples, he was made for them the cornerstone, and therefore is truly the peacemaker. Because, then, he is the true Solomon; but that Solomon, son of David by the woman Bathsheba, king of Israel, bore a figure of this peacemaker, when he built the temple[^5]: therefore, lest you should think that Solomon who built the house for God to be meant, Scripture, showing you another Solomon, began thus in the Psalm: *Unless the Lord build the house, in vain have they labored who build it*[^6]. The Lord, then, builds the house, the Lord Jesus Christ builds his house. Many labor in building; but if he does not build, *in vain have they labored who build it.* Who are they who labor in building? All who in the Church preach the word of God, the ministers of God's Sacraments. We all run, we all labor, we all build now; and before us they ran, labored, built: but *unless the Lord build the house, in vain have they labored who build it.* Therefore, seeing some men collapse, the apostles—and Paul in particular—say: *You observe days, and years, and months, and seasons; I fear for you, lest perhaps I have labored over you in vain*[^7]. Because he knew that he himself was being built within by the Lord, he lamented over those, because he had labored over them in vain. We, then, speak outside; he builds within. How you hear, we observe; what you think, he alone knows who sees your thoughts. He builds, he admonishes, he frightens, he opens the understanding, he applies your perception to faith: and yet we too labor as workmen; but *unless the Lord build the house, in vain have they labored who build it.*

**3.** But what is the house of God is also the city. For the house of God is the people of God; for the house of God is the temple of God. And what does the apostle say? *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^8]. But all the faithful, who are the house of God—not only those who are now, but also those who were before us and have already fallen asleep, and those who will be after us, who are yet to be born among human affairs until the end of the world—the faithful gathered into one, innumerable, but numbered by the Lord, of whom the apostle says: *The Lord knows who are his*[^9]; those grains which now groan among the chaff, which are to make one mass, when the threshing floor at the end has been winnowed[^10]: so the whole number of the holy faithful, of men to be changed so that they become equal to the angels of God, themselves joined also to the angels, who now are not pilgrims, but await us, when we shall return from our pilgrimage; all together make one house of God, and one city. This is Jerusalem. It has guardians: as it has builders, laboring that it may be built, so it has also those who guard. For to guarding belongs what the apostle says: *I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so your minds too be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ*[^11]. He was guarding, he was a guardian; he was watching, as much as he could, over those over whom he was set. And bishops do this. For the higher place is set for bishops precisely so that they may oversee, and as it were guard the people. For what is called in Greek 'bishop' (episcopus) is interpreted in Latin 'overseer'; because he oversees, because he looks from above. For just as a higher place is made for the vinedresser to guard the vineyard, so too a higher place is made for bishops. And from this high place a perilous account is rendered, unless we stand here with this heart, that in humility we be beneath your feet, and pray for you, that he who knows your minds may himself guard you. For we can see you coming in and going out; but we so far cannot see what you think in your hearts, that we cannot even see what you do in your homes. How, then, do we guard? As men; as much as we can, as much as we have received. And because we guard as men, and cannot guard perfectly, will you therefore remain without a guardian? Far from it. For where is he of whom it is said: *Unless the Lord guard the city, in vain has he labored who guards it*[^12]? We labor in guarding, but our labor is in vain, unless he guards who sees your thoughts. He guards when you keep awake, he guards also when you sleep. For he slept on the cross once, and rose; he no longer sleeps. Be Israel; for *he who guards Israel does not sleep, nor will he slumber*[^13]. Come, brothers, if we wish to be guarded under the shadow of God's wings, let us be Israel. For we guard you by the office of stewardship; but we wish to be guarded along with you. We are as it were shepherds to you, but under that Shepherd we are sheep along with you. We are as it were teachers to you from this place; but under that one Master, in this school, we are fellow disciples with you.

**4.** [v. 2.] If we wish to be guarded by him who was humbled for our sake, and exalted to guard us, let us be humble. Let no one arrogate anything to himself. No one has anything good, except he has received it from him who alone is good. But he who would arrogate wisdom to himself is a fool. Let him be humble, that wisdom may come and illumine him. But if, before wisdom comes into him, he thinks himself wise, he rises before the light, and walks in darkness. And what does he hear in this psalm? *It is in vain for you to rise before the light*[^14]. What does it mean: *It is in vain for you to rise before the light*? If you rise before the light rises, you must necessarily remain in vanity, because you will be in darkness. Our light, Christ, has risen; it is good for you to rise after Christ, not to rise before Christ. Who rise before Christ? Those who wish to set themselves before Christ. And who are they who wish to set themselves before Christ? Those who wish to be lofty here, where he was humble. Let them, then, be humble here, if they wish to be lofty there, where Christ is lofty. For he says of those who had cleaved to him in faith, among whom we too are, if we too believe in him with a pure heart: *Father*, he says, *those whom you have given me, I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^15]. A great gift, a great grace, a great promise, my brothers! And who does not wish to be with Christ, where Christ is? But Christ is now lofty: do you wish to be there where he is lofty? Be humble, where he too was humble. Therefore the Light himself says to them: *The disciple is not above the master, nor the servant above his lord*[^16]. Those who wished to be disciples above the master, and servants who wished to be above their lord, wished to rise before the light; they went in vain, because they did not go after the light. To them, then, this psalm says: *It is in vain for you to rise before the light.* Such were the sons of Zebedee, who, before they were humbled according to the Lord's passion, were already choosing places for themselves where they would sit, one at the right hand, the other at the left: they wished to rise before the light; therefore they went in vain. The Lord called them back to humility, when he heard this, and said to them: *Can you drink the cup which I am to drink?*[^17] I came to be humble, and do you wish to be lofty before me? By the way I go, follow there, he says. For if you wish to go this way, by which I do not go: *It is in vain for you to rise before the light.* And Peter had risen before the light, when he wished to give the Lord counsel, that he should not suffer for us. For the Lord had spoken of his passion, in which we were to be saved, of that very humility; for he suffered as a humble man: when, then, he spoke of his coming passion, Peter was terrified, who a little before had called him the Son of God; he feared lest he should die, and said to him: *Far be it from you, Lord; be merciful to yourself: this shall not happen*[^18]. He wished to rise before the light, and to give the light counsel. But what did the Lord do? He made him rise after the light: *Get behind me, Satan!*[^19] For Satan, precisely because you wish to rise before the light. *Get behind me*, so that I may go before, you follow: by the way I go, may you go; not by the way you wish to go, may you wish to lead me there.

**5.** So to those who wished to rise before the light, the Psalm says: *It is in vain for you to rise before the light.* And when shall we rise? When we have been humbled: *Rise after you have sat down*[^20]. Rising signifies exaltation, sitting signifies humility. In some places sitting is understood as the honor of judging, in others it shows humility. How is sitting in the honor of judging? *You will sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^21]. How is sitting a sign of humility? *At the sixth hour the Lord, wearied, sat at the well*[^22]. The weariness of the Lord was the weakness of the Lord, the weakness of strength, the weakness of wisdom: but that very weakness is humility. So if he sat from weakness, that sitting signifies humility. And his very sitting, that is, his humility, itself made us safe; because: *What is weak of God is stronger than men*[^23]. Therefore he says in a certain psalm: *Lord, you have known my sitting down and my rising up*[^24]; that is, my humility, and my exaltation. Why, then, do you wish to be exalted before the light, O sons of Zebedee? For let us speak thus, and name them rather, who are not angry at us; because these things were written about them precisely so that others might beware the pride in which they were rebuked. Why, then, do you wish *to rise before the light*? *It is in vain for you.* Do you wish to be exalted before you are humbled? Your Lord himself, who is your light, was humbled that he might be exalted. Hear Paul saying: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^25]. Why was it not robbery for him? Because it was his nature, because he was born to this, that he should be equal to the one by whom he was begotten. But what did he do? For our sake *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man. He humbled himself, becoming obedient even to death, even the death of the cross*[^26]. This is his sitting down. But hear his rising: *For which cause God also exalted him, and gave him a name which is above every name*[^27]. Now you hasten to that name; *rise*, but *after you have sat down.* You wish to rise, but first sit down: and rising from humility, you come to the kingdom. For if you snatch the kingdom prematurely, you fall from the kingdom before you rise. *Can you drink*, he says, *the cup which I am to drink?* And they: *We can.* And he: *You will drink*, he says, *my cup; but to sit at my right hand, or at my left*, he says, *is not mine to give you: it is prepared for others by my Father*[^28]. What does it mean, *it is not mine to give you*? It is not mine to give to the proud; for this they still were. But if you wish to receive it, do not be what you are. *It is prepared for others*; you too be others, and it is prepared for you. What does it mean: Be others? First be humbled, you who already wish to be exalted. So they understood that humility would profit them, and they were corrected. So let us too hear this, because this psalm says: *Rise after you have sat down.*

**6.** But lest anyone think that he sits for this purpose, to be honored; to show that by that sitting he wished to commend humility; lest anyone think he was ordered to sit either to judge, or to feast and rejoice, and there seek a greater pride; for the signifying of humility he added: *You who eat the bread of sorrow*[^29]. Those, then, eat the bread of sorrow who groan in this pilgrimage. They are in the valley of weeping. For God makes ascents in the heart. But where does he set them? *Ascents*, he says, *he has set in his heart*[^30]. Who? God. If ascents in the heart, that is why they sing a song of ascents. Let us be humbled in the world: let us ascend. How? In heart. For the very ascent of the heart rises from the valley of weeping. *In the valley*, he says, *of weeping*[^31]. As the mountains have been raised up, so the valleys have sunk down: for valleys is the name for the low places of the earth; but hills are the higher places, yet lower than mountains; the very lofty places of the earth are called mountains. It is a small thing: he did not say: Rise from a hill; nor did he say: From a plain; but: From a valley, so that there might be something lower than the plain is. If, then, in the valley of weeping you eat the bread of sorrow, and you say: *My tears have been my bread day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God?*[^32] you rise well, because you have sat down.

**7.** And as though you were to say: When shall we rise? now we are ordered to sit; when will our rising be, as the Lord's was. Attend to him who has gone before you: for if you do not attend to him, it is in vain for you to rise before the light. When was he exalted? When he died. So hope for your exaltation too after death, hope for it in the resurrection of the dead, because he rose again and ascended. But where did he sleep? On the cross. When he slept on the cross, he bore a sign—nay, he fulfilled what was signified in Adam: for when Adam slept, a rib was taken from him, and Eve was made[^33]; so too the Lord, when he slept on the cross, his side was struck with a lance, and the Sacraments flowed out, from which the Church was made[^34]. For the Church was made the spouse of the Lord from his side, as Eve was made from the side. But just as she was made only from the side of one sleeping, so this one was made only from the side of one dying. If, then, he rose only after dying, do you hope for exaltation except after this life? But that this psalm may teach you, as though you asked: When shall I rise? perhaps before I have sat down? *When I have given*, he says, *sleep to his beloved*[^35]. God gives this, when his beloved have slept; then his beloved will rise, that is, Christ's. For all will rise, but not as his beloved. The resurrection of the dead is for all; but what does the apostle say? *We shall indeed all rise, but we shall not all be changed*[^36]. Those rise to punishments; we rise as our Lord rose, that we may follow our head, if we are his members. But if we are his members, then we are his beloved, then to us belongs that resurrection which has gone before in the Lord, so that the light rose before us, we after the light: because it is in vain for us to rise before the light, that is, to seek the heights before we die; since Christ our light was not exalted in the flesh except after he died. Established, then, as his members, and in his members his beloved, when we have received sleep, then we will rise in the resurrection of the dead. One has risen, no more to die. Lazarus rose, but to die again[^37]; the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue rose, but to die again[^38]; the widow's son rose, but to die again[^39]: Christ rose, not to die again. Hear the apostle: *Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^40]. Hope for such a resurrection; and for this be a Christian, not for the sake of the happiness of this earth. For if for the sake of the happiness of this life you wish to be a Christian, when that light of yours did not seek worldly happiness here; you wish to rise before the light; you must necessarily persevere in darkness. Be changed, follow your light; by the way it rose, rise: sit first, and so rise, *when he has given sleep to his beloved.*

**8.** [v. 3.] As though you were to ask again: To which beloved? *Behold, the inheritance of the Lord is sons, the reward, the fruit of the womb*[^41]. When he says *the fruit of the womb*, these sons have been brought forth in travail. There is a certain woman, in whom is spiritually shown what was said to Eve: In groaning you will bring forth[^42]. For the Church, the spouse of Christ, brings forth sons; and if she brings forth, she travails. As a figure of her, Eve too was called *the mother of the living*[^43]. Among the members of the travailing one was he who said: *My little children, with whom I am again in travail, until Christ be formed in you*[^44]. But not in vain did she travail, nor in vain did she bring forth: there will be a holy seed in the resurrection of the dead; the just will abound, who are now scattered throughout the whole world. The Church groans over them, the Church travails with them: but in that resurrection of the dead the offspring of the Church will appear, the pain and groaning will pass away. And what will be said? *Behold, the inheritance of the Lord is sons, the reward, the fruit of the womb.* The reward of this fruit, not this fruit; *the reward* of this *fruit of the womb.* What is that reward? To rise from the dead. What is that reward? To rise, after you have sat down. What is that reward? To rejoice, after you have eaten the bread of sorrow. Of what womb? Of the Church: in whose womb, because Rebecca bore her type, those two twins, as two peoples, struggled[^45]. One mother held in her inward parts the disagreeing brothers not yet born: they beat upon their mother's inward parts with internal discords; she groaned, she suffered violence; but in bringing forth she distinguished those twins whom, pregnant, she had carried. So too now, brothers, as long as groaning has been given to the Church, as long as the Church travails, the good and the evil are themselves within her. But the fruit of the womb was in Jacob, because the mother loved him. *Jacob I have loved*, says God, *but Esau I have hated*[^46]. Both came forth from one womb; one deserved to be loved, the other to be reproved. In his beloved, then, the fruit will be. So *the reward, the fruit of the womb.*

**9.** [v. 4.] *As arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out*[^47]. Whence, then, has this inheritance come about, brothers? whence has so great an inheritance come about, of which it is to be said at the end: *Behold, the inheritance of the Lord is sons, the reward, the fruit of the womb*? Certain men were shaken out from the hand of the Lord, like arrows, and went far, and filled the whole earth, whence the saints sprout up. For this is the inheritance of which it is said: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession*[^48]. And how is this possession extended and grows even to the ends of the earth? Because *as arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out.* From the bow the arrows are shaken out, and the more strongly the shooter shakes it out, the farther the arrow goes. But what is stronger than the Lord shooting? From his bow he sends his apostles: there could be no remainder to which the arrow, shot by one so strong, would not reach; it reached even to the ends of the earth. Therefore no farther, because there is nothing beyond the human race. For he has such great strength that, even if there were beyond it somewhere for the arrow to be extended, he would shoot the arrow there. But the sons of those who have been shaken out are such as those who were shaken out. For it has been asked about this phrase, even by those who treated these things before us, why they are called the sons of those who have been shaken out, or who are to be understood as the sons of those who have been shaken out; and it seemed to some, as I just said, that the sons of those who have been shaken out are the sons of the apostles.

**10.** Let your charity attend a little. It has been asked why the apostles were shaken out: it has been said by some that they were called shaken out because the Lord commanded them: *If you go out of the city in which they do not hear you, shake the dust off your feet*[^49]. But another says: Then they ought not to have been called *sons of those who have been shaken out*, but sons of those who shook out: for the Lord made shakers-out of those to whom he said: *Shake the dust off your feet*; not shaken-out ones. Subtly indeed, as though wishing to contradict the preceding opinion, did he by whom these things had already been treated and said. Nevertheless, as far as the Lord helps, seeking how those too could rightly be called shaken out, to whom the Lord said: *Shake the dust off your feet*; we find that it could be said not absurdly. For although they themselves shook out, they shook out themselves. This I say: he who shakes out either shakes out himself, or something else: if he shakes out something else, he is a shaker-out, not shaken out; but if he shakes out himself, he is both a shaker-out and shaken out. Attend; I will say this more plainly, if I can. If he shakes out something else, he is a shaker-out, not shaken out; if he is shaken out by another, he is shaken out, not a shaker-out: but if he shakes out himself, he is a shaker-out, because he shakes out himself; and shaken out, because he is shaken out by himself. It is asked, then, whom the apostles shook out? Surely themselves: for from their feet they shook the dust. But someone says: They did not shake out themselves, but the dust. This is plainly said captiously. For in two ways we say that something is shaken out: either that which is shaken out from it, or that from which it is shaken out. For we say both: The dust has been shaken out; and: The garment has been shaken out. Some hold and shake out a garment, and the dust which had clung to it leaps out from it. What do you say of the dust? The dust has been shaken out. What do you say of the garment? The garment has been shaken out. If, then, both what leaped out by being shaken out from it, and that from which leaped out what had clung, are called shaken out; both the dust has been shaken out, and the apostles have been shaken out. Why, then, should the sons of the apostles not be called *sons of those who have been shaken out*?

**11.** But there is another opinion which we ought not to pass over. For perhaps it was set down rather obscurely, so that it might generate many understandings, and men might depart the richer, because they found shut up what could be opened in many ways, than if they had found it opened in one way. We say that something too is shaken out, so that what is perhaps hidden may come out of it. For in one way we say a garment is shaken out, so that they may shake the dust out of it; and in another way we say a sack is shaken out, so that what was lurking inside may come out. So I understand, brothers, as far as I can, that the sons of those who have been shaken out are perhaps the apostles themselves, called the sons of the prophets. For the prophets contained shut up and covered mysteries: they were shaken out, so that from them manifest things might proceed. Suppose, then, that a prophet said, as indeed he did: *The ox knew its owner, and the ass its master's manger; but Israel has not known me*[^50]. This for now occurs to me at present, that I might say it of a prophet: if something else had occurred, I would have said this. But a man hearing this, if he thinks of the ass and the ox, and the cattle and beasts that he sees, he will be handling outwardly something or other set, as it were, in a wrapping; what is there he does not know. The ass and the ox signify something. What, then, is said to the man who already wishes to pronounce? Wait; what you touch is shut up, shake out the wrapping: the prophet has woven something or other under those veils of names; and he signifies some ass or other, some ox or other. For the ass, as a figure of the people of God, is God's beast, carrying its rider the Lord, that he may not stray on the way: and that ox of which the apostle says: *You shall not muzzle the ox that treads out the grain. Is it about oxen that God is concerned?* he said. *But for our sake Scripture says it*[^51]. So everyone who preaches the word of God, admonishes, rebukes, frightens; he treads out the threshing floor, and fulfills the office of the ox. The ox came from the nation of the Jews; for from there were the preachers, the apostles: the ass too came from the nation of the foreskin, that is, from the Gentiles. For it came to carry the Lord: and therefore the Lord sat on the ass, which had never carried a man; since to the Gentiles neither the Law nor the prophets had been sent. So because our Lord Jesus Christ willed to be our food, and was therefore born and placed in a manger: *The ox knew its owner, and the ass its master's manger*[^52]. But would these things come out, unless the sack were shaken out? Unless the prophecy, wrapped up, were examined with diligence applied, would the covered things come out to us? So all these things were shut up before the Lord came. The Lord came and shook out those shut-up things, and they were made manifest; the prophets were shaken out, and the apostles were begotten. Because, then, when the prophets were shaken out the apostles were begotten, the apostles are sons of those who have been shaken out. They themselves, set in the hand of the mighty one like arrows, reached even to the ends of the earth. Whence it is to be said at the end: *Behold, the inheritance of the Lord is sons, the reward, the fruit of the womb.* Because for this reason this inheritance is gathered from the ends of the earth: because: *As arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out*; that is, the sons of the prophets, the apostles, were like arrows in the hand of a mighty man. If he is mighty, he shot mightily; if he shot mightily, those he shot reached even to the ends of the earth.

**12.** [v. 5.] *Blessed is the man who has filled his desire from them*[^53]. Come, my brothers, who fills his desire from them? He who does not love the world. He who is full of the desire of the world has no room for what they preached to enter. Pour out what you carry, and become capable of receiving what you do not have. That is, you desire riches; you cannot fill your desire from them: you desire honors on earth, you desire the things which God has given even to beasts—that is, temporal pleasure, and health of body, and such things; you will not fill your desire from them. But if you desire as the deer desires the fountains of waters[^54]; if you say: *My soul desires and faints for the courts of the Lord*[^55]: you fill your desire from them; not because they can now fill such a desire, but because, by imitating such men, you come to him who filled their desire.

**13.** *He will not be confounded, when he speaks to his enemies in the gate*[^56]. Brothers, let us speak in the gate, that is, let all know what we speak. For he who does not wish to speak in the gate wishes to hide what he speaks; and perhaps he wishes to hide it because it is evil. If he is confident, let him speak in the gate; as it is said of Wisdom: *But in the gates of the cities she speaks boldly*[^57]. As long as the innocent hold to justice, they will not blush: this is to preach in the gate. And who is it that preaches in the gate? He who preaches in Christ; for Christ is the gate by which we enter into that city. I lie, if he did not himself say: *I am the door*[^58]. If he is the door, he is also the gate. For a house has a door; a city's door is a gate, a house's gate is a door. But perhaps it is not well said 'gate,' if it is not well said 'city' of what was called a house. For both were said a little before: *Unless the Lord build the house, in vain have they labored who build it*; and lest, hearing 'house,' you should think it something small: *Unless the Lord*, he says, *guard the city, in vain has he labored who guards it.* That, then, is the house which is the city. So it has a door as a house, and a gate as a city. He, then, who is the door of the house is the gate of the city. So if Christ is the gate of the city, he is not confounded who stands in Christ, and so preaches. But he who preaches against Christ, the gate is shut against him. Who are those who preach against Christ? Those who deny that arrows were sent from the hand of the mighty one, and reached even to the ends of the earth; and that this is the inheritance of the Lord, of which it was said: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession*[^59]. It was preached, it was heard, before it came to pass; and now that it has come to pass they refuse to acknowledge it. Those, then, who dispute against Christ are outside the gate; because they seek their own honors, not Christ's. But he who preaches in the gate seeks Christ's honor, not his own; and therefore he who preaches in the gate says: Do not presume upon me; for not through me, but through the gate, you will enter. But those who wish men to presume upon themselves do not wish them to enter through the gate: no wonder if the gate is shut against them, and idle they knock that it may be opened. Be present, then, in mind, brothers, also for tomorrow's sermon, which, by our promise, with the Lord helping, is to be rendered to you from the Gospel concerning the dove. In whose name we promised it, in his mercy we will render it. But that we may be able to be fit renderers, lest we have been bold promisers, pray for us.
