# Exposition of Psalm 129

**1.** The psalm which we have sung is brief: but as it is written in the Gospel of Zacchaeus, short in stature, and great in deed[^1]; as it is written of that widow who put two mites into the treasury, small money, but great love[^2]: so this psalm too, if you count the words, is brief; if you weigh the meanings, is great. So it will not be able to detain us longer to the point of weariness. Why? Let your prudence take notice, and let Christian attention be present: let the word of God sound to the willing and the unwilling, in season and out of season. It finds a place for itself, it finds hearts where it may rest, it finds soil where it may sprout and bear fruit. For that many are evil and unjust whom the Church bears until the end, is plain: and these are they for whom the word of God is superfluous; and either it falls on them as seed is trodden on the way, and gathered by the birds; or it falls on them as seed on rocky places, where it has not much soil, comes up at once, and, the sun being hot, withers, because it has no root; or as among thorns, which, even if it sprouts and tries to rise into the air, is yet choked by the multitude of thorns. But such are those who despise the word of God like the way, or who rejoice for an hour, and, tribulation having come, like the heat of the sun, wither; or who, by the thoughts and cares and anxiety of this world, as by the thorns of avarice, choke what had begun to sprout in them. But there is also good soil, into which the seed, when it has fallen, brings forth fruit, some thirtyfold, some sixtyfold, some a hundredfold[^3]: whether little or much, all will be in the barn. There are, then, such people, and for these we speak. For these Scripture speaks, for these the Gospel does not keep silent. But let those others too hear, lest perhaps they be one thing today, another tomorrow; lest perhaps they be changed by hearing, and either plow the way, or clear the stones, or pull out the thorns. Let the Spirit of God speak, let him speak to us, let him sing to us; whether we wish to dance, or do not wish, let him himself sing. For just as one who dances moves his limbs to the song; so those who dance to the command of God obey the sound by their works. Therefore to those who refused to do this, what does the Lord say in the Gospel? *We sang to you, and you did not dance; we mourned, and you did not lament*[^4]. So let him sing; we trust in the mercy of God, that there will be those over whom he will console us. For those who are obstinate, persevering in malice, although they hear the word of God, daily disturb the Church with scandals. Of such this psalm speaks; for it began thus:

**2.** [vv. 1-3.] *Often have they assailed me from my youth*[^5]. The Church speaks of those whom she endures, and as though it were said: Is it only now? The Church is of old; from the time that the saints are called, the Church is on earth. At one time the Church was in Abel alone, and he was assailed by his evil and lost brother Cain[^6]. At one time the Church was in Enoch alone, and he was translated from among the wicked[^7]. At one time the Church was in the house of Noah alone, and it bore all who perished in the flood, and the ark alone floated on the waves, and escaped to dry land[^8]. At one time the Church was in Abraham alone, and how much he bore from the wicked, we know. The Church was in Lot alone, the son of his brother, and in his house in Sodom, and it bore the iniquities and perversities of the Sodomites, until God freed him from their midst[^9]. The Church began to be also in the people of Israel; it bore Pharaoh and the Egyptians. There began to be also in that very Church, that is, in the people of Israel, a number of the saints: Moses and the other saints bore the wicked Jews, the people of Israel. We have come also to our Lord Jesus Christ, the Gospel was preached, he said in the Psalms: *I announced and spoke, they were multiplied above number*[^10]. What does it mean, *above number*? Not only did those believe who belong to the number of the saints; but they entered also *above number*: many just, but more unjust; and the just bore the unjust. When? In the Church. Is it only now, from the time when she counts, from the time when she calls to mind? Therefore, lest the Church should now wonder, or lest anyone in the Church, wishing to be a good member of the Church, should wonder, let him hear the Church herself his mother saying to him: Do not wonder at these things, son: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.*

**3.** It is a great affection for the Psalm to have begun thus: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.* As though it were already saying something: for it seems not to have begun, but to have answered. But whom did it answer? Those thinking and saying: How many evils we endure, how many scandals daily grow more frequent, when the wicked enter into the Church and we bear them? But let the Church answer for certain ones, that is, let her answer with the voice of the stronger to the complaints of the weaker, and let the firm strengthen the infirm, and the grown-up the little ones, and let the Church say: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.* *Let Israel now say: Often have they assailed me from my youth.* Let her say it, let her not fear it. For what does it avail, that when she had said: *Often have they assailed me*, she added: *from my youth*? Now indeed the old age of the Church is assailed, but let her not fear: let her say: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.* Did she therefore not come to old age, because they did not cease assailing? could they destroy her? *Let Israel now say*; and let Israel console herself, let the Church herself console herself by past examples, and say: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.*

**4.** Why did they assail me? *For they could not prevail against me. Upon my back the sinners built; they made their injustice long*[^11]. Why did they assail me? *Because they could not prevail against me.* What does it mean, *they could not prevail against me*? To build. What does it mean, *they could not prevail against me*? I did not consent with them to evil. For every evil man persecutes the good man precisely because the good man does not consent with him to evil. Let someone do something evil; let the bishop not rebuke it, and he is a good bishop: let the bishop rebuke it, and he is a bad bishop. Let him plunder something; let the one from whom he plunders be silent, and he is good: let him at least speak and reprove, even if he does not demand it back, and he is bad. He who reproves the plunderer is bad, and he who plunders is good! Let him walk on: *Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we shall die*[^12]. On the contrary the apostle: *Evil conversations corrupt good morals. Be sober, you just, and do not sin*[^13]. The word sounds, the discourse sounds, contradicting his lust: but he, the friend of his own lust, and the enemy of the discourse contradicting his beloved, is hostile, and hates the word of God. Avarice has been made his friend, God his enemy. For God contradicts avarice, and wills that nothing be possessed by avarice. 'Let me be possessed,' avarice cries. Why do you wish to be possessed by avarice? It commands hard things, I command light things: its burden is heavy, my burden is light; its yoke is rough, my yoke is gentle[^14]. Do not wish to be possessed by avarice. Avarice commands you to cross the sea, and you obey: it commands you to commit yourself to the winds and the storms; I command you to give to the poor man before your door out of what you have: you are lazy to do the good work before you, and energetic to cross the sea. Because avarice commands, you serve; because God commands, you hate. And what then? When he has begun to hate, he begins to wish to accuse those from whom he hears good precepts, and to wish, by his suspicions, to seek out crimes of the servants of God. Those who say these things to us, do they not themselves not do them? And these things, whether they are done or not done, are said to be done; and the things that are done well are said to be done badly: and the things we endure are charged to our fault. What do we answer? Do not attend to me; attend to this discourse: he himself speaks to you through anyone whomsoever; to him you are an enemy. Agree with your adversary, while you are with him on the way[^15]: you have made the word of God your adversary. Do not attend to the fact that he speaks to you: evil is the one through whom he speaks to you; but he is not evil who speaks to you, the word of God. Accuse God, accuse him, if you can.

**5.** Do you believe, brothers, that those of whom it is said: *Often have they assailed me from my youth*, have come so far as not even to hesitate to accuse God himself? You accuse the avaricious man, and he accuses God because he made gold. Do not be avaricious. And God would not make gold. Now this remains, that because you cannot restrain your evil works, you accuse the good works of God: the creator and maker of the world displeases you. He would not have made even the sun; for many quarrel at their windows over the lights, and drag one another to court. Oh, if only we would restrain our vices! because all things are good, because good is the God who made all things; and his works praise him, considered as good by one who has the spirit of considering, the spirit of devotion and wisdom. On every side God is praised by his works. How do his works praise him through the mouth of the three young men? what was left out? The heavens praise, the angels praise, the stars praise, the sun and moon praise, the days and nights praise, whatever sprouts from the earth praises, whatever swims in the sea praises, whatever flies in the air praises, all mountains and hills praise, cold and heat praise; and all the other things which God made, you heard that they praise God[^16]: did you hear there that avarice praises God, that luxury praises God? These do not praise, because he himself did not make them. There men praise God; God is the creator of man. Avarice is the work of an evil man; man himself is the work of God. And what does God will? To kill in you what you yourself have made, and to save what he himself has made.

**6.** Do not lend at interest. You accuse Scripture, which says: *He who has not given his money at interest*[^17]. I did not write that, it did not come out of my mouth first: hear God. And he says: Let the clergy not lend at interest. And perhaps he who speaks to you does not lend at interest: but if he does lend at interest, grant that he too lends at interest; does he who speaks through him lend at interest? If he does what he tells you, and you do not do it; you go into the fire, he into the kingdom. If he does not do what he tells you, and equally does the evil things which you do, and tells the good things which he does not do; both equally go into the fire[^18]. The hay will burn, but the word of the Lord abides forever. Does the discourse burn which has spoken to you through him? Either he is a Moses who speaks to you, that is, a good and just servant of God; or he is a Pharisee sitting in the seat of Moses. You heard also of these: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not wish to do*[^19]. You have no way to excuse yourself, when the word of God speaks to you. Because you cannot kill the word of God, you seek to accuse those through whom the word of God speaks to you. Seek as much as you wish, say as much as you wish, blaspheme as much as you wish: *Often have they assailed me from my youth; but let Israel say: Often have they assailed me from my youth.* Usurers too dare to say: I have nothing else to live on. This the robber too would say to me, caught at the throat; this the burglar too would say, caught about another's wall; this the pimp too would say to me, buying girls for prostitution; this the sorcerer too would say, chanting evils, and selling his wickedness; whatever such thing we tried to forbid, all would answer that they had nothing to live on, that they fed themselves from it; as though this very thing were not most of all to be punished in them, that they have chosen an art of wickedness whereby to pass their life, and wish to feed themselves from that very thing by which they offend him by whom all are fed.

**7.** But when you have cried this out and said it, they answer: If so, we do not come here; if so, we do not enter the Church itself. Let them come, let them enter, let them hear: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.* *For they could not prevail against me: upon my back the sinners built*: that is, they could not bring it about that I consent; they brought it about that I carry. How splendidly it was said, how excellently it signified: *For they could not prevail against me: upon my back the sinners built.* They deal with us, first, that we consent to them in evil deeds: if we do not consent: Bear with us, they say. So because you could not prevail against me, climb onto my back; I have to carry you until the end comes: for so it has been commanded me, that I bring forth fruit with endurance[^20]. I do not correct you, I bear you: or perhaps, while I bear you, you correct yourself. If you do not correct yourself until the end, I bear you until the end; and until the end you will be upon my back, for a time. Will you be upon my back forever? For he will come who shakes you off from there: the time of harvest will come, the end of the world will come; God will send the reapers. But the reapers are the angels: they separate the evil from the midst of the just, as the weeds from the wheat; the grain they will store in the barn, but the chaff they will burn with unquenchable fire[^21]. I have carried as long as I could, now rejoicing I pass into the barn of God, and securely I sing: *Often have they assailed me from my youth.*

**8.** For what could they do to me, in that *they assailed me from my youth*? They exercised me, they did not crush me. They availed for me like fire to gold, not like fire to hay. For fire, applied to gold, takes away the dross; applied to hay, it turns it to ashes. *For they could not prevail against me*, because I did not consent, because they did not make me such as they themselves are. *Upon my back the sinners built, they made their injustice long.* They made something for me to endure, and did not make one to whom I should consent. So now their injustice is far from me. The evil are mingled with the good, not only in the world, but even within the Church itself the evil are mingled with the good. You know it, and have proved it; and you will prove it more, if you are good. *For when the plant had grown and made fruit, then the weeds appeared*[^22]. The evil in the Church appear to no one, except to him who is good. So you know that they are mingled, and Scripture always and everywhere says that they will not be separated except at the end. So, mingled as they are, they are far from one another. So then, lest anyone, from the fact that the evil are mingled among the good, should think that iniquity is next to justice: *They could not*, he says, *prevail against me*; that is, they spoke, and spoke evilly: *Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we shall die*[^23]. Evil discourses did not corrupt good morals: in what I heard from God, I did not yield to the discourses of men. The sinners made for me something to carry, not one to be mingled with, and their iniquity has become far from me. For what is so near as two men in one Church? what so far as iniquity from justice? For where there is consent, there is nearness. Two are bound and sent to the judge; a robber, and one bound with him: the one criminal, the other innocent, are bound by one chain, and are far from each other. How far are they from each other? As far as crime is from innocence. Behold, these are far from one another. Another robber in Spain commits crimes, nearest to him is one who commits them in Africa. How near? As near as crime is joined to crime, as robbery is joined to robbery. Let no one, then, fear the evil mingled with him in body. Let him be far from them in heart, and securely he carries what he does not fear: *They made their injustice long.*

**9.** [v. 4.] What occurs? Those flourish who reign wickedly; and, to use the speech of the common people, the wicked thunder, they are exalted in haughtiness, in slanders. What then? always this? No. Hear what follows: *The just Lord will cut down the necks of sinners*[^24]. Let your charity attend. *The Lord*, he says, *the just one, will cut down the necks of sinners.* Who would not tremble? For who has not sinned? *The just Lord will cut down the necks of sinners.* Into the heart of all who hear, trembling enters, if they believe the Scripture of God. For if men beat their breasts without cause, they lie in beating their breasts, if they are just; and in that they lie to God, they become sinners. If, then, they truly beat their breasts, they are sinners. And which of us does not beat his breast? and which of us does not strike his eyes to the ground, like that Publican, and say: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^25]? If, then, all are sinners, and no one is found without sin; the sword upon the neck is to be feared by all, because *the just Lord will cut down the necks of sinners.* I do not think, my brothers, that he means the necks of all sinners; but in the member which he strikes, there he indicates which sinners he strikes. For he did not say: The just Lord will cut down the hands of sinners; or, The just Lord will cut down the feet of sinners, he did not say: but because he wished the proud sinners to be understood, and the proud are all stiff-necked, who not only do evil, but do not even wish to acknowledge it, and when they are rebuked, justify themselves (Behold, you did it; at least acknowledge what you did: God hates the sinner, do you too hate him; join yourself to God, together let us both persecute your sin. No, he says: I did well, God did badly. What is this? I have done nothing evil, he says: because Saturn did it, because Mars did it, because Venus did it; I did nothing, the stars did it. You justify yourself, you accuse God who made the stars, who adorned the heaven): therefore, since you justify your sin, and grow proud against God, because you make yourself free of fault, and God in fault, and so raised your neck, and ran against God, as it is written in Job (he was speaking of the impious sinner): *Running against God on the thick neck of his shield*[^26]; and there he named the neck; because you so raise yourself, and do not strike your eyes to the ground, and beat your breast, and say: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*; but you boast of your merits, and wish, says God, to contend with me in judgment, to enter into judgment with me; when you ought, in your guilt, to make satisfaction to God, and to cry to him what is cried in another psalm: *If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?*[^27] to cry to him what is cried in another psalm: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^28]: since you do not wish to say these things, but justify your deeds against the word of God; there comes upon you what Scripture goes on to say: *The just Lord will cut down the necks of sinners.*

**10.** [v. 5.] *Let them be confounded, and turned backward, all who hate Zion*[^29]. Those who hate Zion hate the Church: Zion is the Church. And those who enter the Church feignedly hate the Church. Those who do not wish to observe the word of God hate the Church: *Upon my back they built.* What will the Church do, except carry them until the end?

**11.** [v. 6.] But what does he say of them? It follows: *Let them become like the grass of the housetops, which withered before it was plucked up*[^30]. The grass of the housetops is the grass that grows on roofs, on a tiled terrace. It is seen on high, and has no root. How much better it would be born lower, and would grow green more luxuriantly? As it is, it is born higher, to a quicker withering. It has not yet been plucked up, and it has withered: they have not yet been finished in the judgment of God, and already they have not the sap of greenness. Attend to their works, and see that they have withered. But they live, and are here: so they have not yet been plucked up. They have withered, but not yet been plucked up: they have become *like the grass of the housetops, which withered before it was plucked up.*

**12.** And the reapers will come, but they do not fill their sheaves from those. For the reapers are to come, and to gather the wheat into the barn, and to bind the weeds, and cast them into the fire. So too the grass of the housetops is cleared away, and whatever is plucked up from there is cast into the fire; because even before it was plucked up it withered. The reaper does not fill his hands from it. For it follows and says: *The reaper has not filled his hand, nor his bosom he who gathers the sheaves*[^31]. *But the reapers are the angels*[^32], the Lord says.

**13.** [v. 8.] *And those passing by on the way did not say: The blessing of the Lord be upon you; we have blessed you in the name of the Lord*[^33]. For you know, brothers, that when one passes by people working, it is the custom to say to them: *The blessing of the Lord be upon you.* And this custom was more in the nation of the Jews. No one passed by and saw some doing some work in the field, or in the vineyard, or in the harvest, or anything of this kind; it was not allowed to pass without a blessing. There are some who gather the sheaves, others who pass by on the way. Those who gather the sheaves do not fill their hands from those; because the grass of the housetops is not gathered into the barn. Who are those who gather the sheaves? The reapers. Who are the reapers? The Lord said: *But the reapers are the angels.* Who are those passing by? Those who have already passed by this way, that is, through this life, from here to the homeland: the apostles were those passing by in this life, the prophets were those passing by. Whom did the prophets and apostles bless? Those in whom they saw the root of love. But those whom they found prominent on the rooftops and proud *on the neck of their shield*, they spoke against them what they were to be, but did not give the blessing upon them. So all those evil ones whom the Church carries, you who read in the Scriptures find marked as accursed, belonging to antichrist, belonging to the devil, belonging to the chaff, belonging to the weeds. And many innumerable things are said of them by way of likeness: *For not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^34]. You find no Scripture that speaks well of them, because those passing by on the way did not bless them. The prophets, passing by, said all evil things of them. Behold, this man too whom we carry, David, passed by on the way; you heard what he said of them: *The just Lord will cut down the necks of sinners. Let them be confounded, and turned backward, all who hate Zion. Let them become like the grass of the housetops, which withered before it was plucked up. The reaper has not filled his hand, nor his bosom he who gathers the sheaves.* These things he said of them. So this passer-by did not bless over them, and there was fulfilled, even through him, what he himself said: *And those passing by on the way did not say: We have blessed you in the name of the Lord.* And these passers-by, whether prophets, or patriarchs, or apostles, whoever passed by, if we live well, brothers, blessed us *in the name of the Lord.* When, you say, did Paul bless me? when did Peter bless me? Attend in the Scriptures, see whether you live well, and see there that you were blessed. All who live well have blessed you. And how did they bless? *In the name of the Lord*; not in their own name, like the heretics. For those who say: What we give, this is holy; wish to bless in their own name, not in the name of the Lord. But those who say: None sanctifies except God, nor is anyone good except by the gift of God; they bless in the name of the Lord, we in his name: because they are friends of the bridegroom, they do not wish to be adulterers of the bride[^35].
