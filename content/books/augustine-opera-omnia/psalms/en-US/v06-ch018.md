# Exposition of Psalm 138

**1.** [v. 1.] The title of this psalm is brief and simple, nor does it detain us, since we know whose figure David bore, and recognize in him ourselves too, because we too are members of that body. Let us, then, recognize here the voice of the Church, and at the same time rejoice that we have deserved to be in her, whose voice as she sang we have heard. The whole title is: *Of David himself.* Let us see, then, what David himself says.

**2.** *I will confess to you, O Lord, with my whole heart*[^1]. The title of a Psalm is accustomed to indicate to us what goes on within: but here, since the title does not indicate this, but only indicates to whom it is sung; what goes on in the whole Psalm the first verse announces: *I will confess to you, O Lord, with my whole heart.* Let us, then, hear this confession. But first I remind you that confession in the Scriptures, when we confess to God, is accustomed to be said in two ways, either of sins, or of praise. But the confession of sins all know; the confession of praise few take note of. For the confession of sins is so well known, that in whatever place of the Scriptures it has been heard: *I will confess to you, O Lord;* or: *We will confess to you*: at once, by the habit of so understanding it, the hands run to beat the breast; so much do men not usually understand confession to be other than of sins. But was even our Lord Jesus Christ himself a sinner, who says in the Gospel: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^2]? For he went on and said what he confessed; that we might understand the confession of praise, not of iniquity: *I confess*, he says, *to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones.* He praised the Father, he praised God, because he does not despise the humble, but despises the proud. And here we are about to hear such a confession, of the praise of God, and of thanksgiving. *I will confess to you, O Lord, with my whole heart.* My whole heart I place on the altar of your confession, I offer you a holocaust of praise. For a sacrifice is called a holocaust where the whole is burned: for in Greek *holon* in Latin is said 'whole.' See how he offers a spiritual holocaust, who says: *I will confess to you, O Lord, with my whole heart.* Let the flame of your love, he says, kindle my whole heart: let nothing in me be left to me, nor anything by which I may look back to myself; but let me wholly glow toward you, wholly burn toward you, wholly love you, as one set on fire by you. *I will confess to you, O Lord, with my whole heart; for you have heard the words of my mouth.* Of which *mouth of mine*, except of my heart? For there we have the voice which God hears, which the human ear does not know at all. Surely those who accused Susanna were crying out, and did not lift their eyes to heaven; she was silent, and cried out with her heart: whence she deserved to be heard, thence they to be punished[^3]. So there is a mouth within; there we ask, thence we ask: and if we have prepared a lodging or house for God, there we speak, there we are heard. For he is not set far from each one of us, in whom we live, move, and are[^4]. Nothing makes you far from God but iniquity alone: cast down the dividing wall of sin, and you are with him whom you ask. *You have heard*, he says, *the words of my mouth; I will confess to you.*

**3.** *And before the angels I will sing to you*[^5]. Not before men will I sing, but before the angels will I sing. My psaltery, my joy: but my joy concerning lower things is before men; my joy concerning higher things is before the angels. For the impious man does not know the joy of the just: *There is no rejoicing for the impious, says the Lord*[^6]. The wicked man rejoices in the tavern, the martyr rejoices in the chain. How rejoiced that holy Crispina, whose solemnity is celebrated today? She rejoiced when she was seized, when she was led to the judge, when she was put into prison, when she was brought out bound, when she was lifted onto the scaffold, when she was heard, when she was condemned; in all these things she rejoiced: and the wretched thought her wretched, who rejoiced before the angels.

**4.** [v. 2.] *I will adore toward your holy temple*[^7]. What is your holy temple? Where we will dwell, where we will adore. For we run, that we may adore. Our heart, pregnant, is in travail, and seeks where it may give birth. What is that place where God is to be adored? what world? what building? what seat, finally, in heaven and among the stars? We seek in the holy Scriptures, and we find in the words of Wisdom: *I*, she says, *was with him; I was she in whom he rejoiced daily*[^8]. And she tells his works, and sets out for us his seat. Which? *When he made the clouds strong above*, she says, *when he set apart his seat above the winds*[^9]. But his seat is his temple. Where, then, shall we go? Are we to adore above the winds? If he is adored above the winds, the birds surpass us. But if we understand the winds as souls, that is, souls signified by the name of winds, as Scripture says in a certain place: *He flew above the wings of the winds*[^10]; that is, above the powers of souls: whence the soul too is called a breath of God, as it were a wind, not that this wind is to be understood by which we feel the body driven, but that by its name something invisible is signified, which can neither be discerned by the eyes, nor felt by the ears, nor led by smell, nor tasted by the palate, nor handled by the hand; which is surely a certain life, by which we live, which is called the soul: if we understand these winds, there is no reason to seek visible wings, that we may fly with the birds to adore toward the temple of God; but above ourselves we will find God sitting, if we wish to be faithful to him. See whether this is not so: *For the temple of God is holy*, says the apostle, *which you are*[^11]. Yet certainly, what is manifest, God dwells in the angels. So when our joy concerning spiritual things, not concerning earthly goods, takes up a song to God, that it may sing before the angels; that very congregation of angels is the temple of God, we adore toward the temple of God. The Church below, and the Church above: the Church below in all the faithful, the Church above in all the angels. But the Lord of the angels descended to the Church below, and the angels ministered to him on earth while he ministered to us[^12]. *For I came not*, he says, *to be ministered to, but to minister*[^13]. What did he minister to us, except that which even today we eat and drink? Since, then, the Lord of the angels ministered to us, let us not despair of being equal to the angels one day. For he, greater than the angels, descended to man, and the Creator of the angels took up man, the Lord of the angels died for man. *I will adore*, then, *toward your holy temple*: I understand your temple not made with hands, but which you made for yourself.

**5.** *And I will confess to your name, in your mercy and your truth*[^14]. In these two we confess: for so it is read in another psalm too: *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth*[^15]. In these two we confess; *in your mercy, and in your truth.* By mercy you looked upon the sinner, by truth you rendered your promise. *In your*, then, *mercy and truth* I will confess to you. These too I, after my strength, render back, which you gave me, doing mercy and truth; mercy in coming to aid, truth in judging. By these we are helped by God, by these we win God's favor. Rightly, then, *are mercy and truth all the ways of the Lord.* There are no other ways by which he may come to us; there are no other ways by which we may come to him.

**6.** *Because you have magnified above all your holy name*[^16]. What manner of thanksgiving is this, brothers? He magnified his holy name above Abraham. For Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for justice[^17]. All the other nations sacrificed to idols, served demons. From Abraham was born Isaac; God was magnified over that house: thence Jacob; God was magnified, who says: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^18]: thence the twelve sons, thence the people of Israel freed from Egypt, led through the Red Sea, exercised in the desert, the Gentiles being cast out, settled in the land of promise. The name of the Lord was magnified over Israel. But thence too the Virgin Mary, thence the Lord Christ, dead on account of our sins, rising again on account of our justification[^19], filling the faithful with the Holy Spirit, sending them to preach among the nations: *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^20]: and behold he magnified above all his holy name.

**7.** [v. 3.] *On whatever day I shall call upon you, quickly hear me*[^21]. Why *quickly*? Because you said: *While you are still speaking I will say, Behold, I am here*[^22]. Why *quickly*? Because I now no longer ask for earthly happiness, I have learned holy desire from the new testament. I do not ask for land, not fleshly fruitfulness, not temporal health, not the subjection of enemies, not riches, not honors; none of these I ask: therefore *quickly hear me.* Because you have taught what I should ask, give what I ask. Let us say to this man: Do you ask for something like this? Let us hear, let him pour out his petition, and let us see what he asks; and let us learn through him to ask, that we may deserve to receive. You had come to the Church, about to ask I know not what today. What do we think you had come to ask? You had come with your desire, I know not what, would that an innocent one, even if a fleshly one. But remove iniquity, remove carnality; learn what to ask, attend to what you are celebrating today. You celebrate the birthday of a holy and blessed woman, and perhaps you desire earthly happiness. She, on account of holy desire, let go the happiness she had on earth; she let go her sons weeping and grieving over her as over a cruel mother, because she, who was hastening to the divine crown, had as it were lost human compassion. But did she not know what she desired, what she trampled on? Rather she knew how to sing before the angels of God, and to desire their fellowship, a holy and pure friendship, where she would no longer die, where she would know the judge with whom a lie could not prevail. What then? in that life are there no goods? Rather, there alone are goods, goods not mixed with evils; securities in which you may rejoice as much as you wish, and no one says to you: Restrain yourself. But here to rejoice over earthly goods is troublesome, and exceedingly perilous; lest you so rejoice as to cleave to them, and by rejoicing wickedly perish. For why does God mix tribulations with earthly joys, except that, feeling tribulation and bitterness, we may learn to desire eternal sweetness?

**8.** What, then, does he ask, let us see; by what merit he said: *Quickly hear me.* For what do you ask, that you may be quickly heard? *You will multiply me.* In many ways multiplication can be understood. There is the multiplication of earthly generation, according to the first blessing of our nature, which we have heard: *Increase, and multiply, and fill the earth, and have dominion over it*[^23]. Did he wish to be multiplied thus, who said: *Quickly hear me*? And that multiplication indeed is fruitful, and comes only from the blessing of the Lord. But what shall I say of the other multiplications? One is multiplied in gold, another in silver, another in cattle, another in household, another in possessions, another in all these. Many are the earthly multiplications; but happier is that of sons: although to avaricious men even fruitfulness itself is troublesome. For they fear lest those be left poor who could have been born many. Which anxiety has driven very many to impiety, so that they forget that they are parents, and, stripped of all human affection, expose their sons, that they may make them strangers; that she who bore them should cast them away, and she who did not bear them should gather them up; that the one should despise them, the other love them; the one a mother in vain by the flesh, the other truer by will. Since, then, there are many multiplications, and many kinds of multiplications, what multiplication does he seek who said: *Quickly hear me*? For he says: *You will multiply me.* We wait to hear in what. Hear, then: *In my soul.* Not in my flesh, but *in my soul: You will multiply me in my soul.* Is there still something to be added, lest perhaps even multiplication in the soul does not at once signify happiness? For by cares men are multiplied in the soul. He seems multiplied in soul, in whom even vices are multiplied. One is only avaricious, another only proud, another only luxurious; this one is both avaricious, and proud, and luxurious: he is multiplied in his soul, but to his own harm. That multiplication is of want, not of abundance. So what is it, you who said: *Quickly hear me*, and have taken yourself away from every body, from every earthly thing, from every worldly desire, that you may say to God: *You will multiply me in my soul*? Explain further what you desire: *You will multiply me*, he says, *in my soul, with virtue*[^24]. An expressed vow, an expressed desire, cut around from all confusion. If he said: *You will multiply me;* you would think of I know not what earthly things: he added, *in my soul.* Again, lest you think of vices in the soul, he added, *with virtue.* There is nothing further you should desire from God, if you wish with a good and true brow to say: *Quickly hear me.*

**9.** [v. 4.] *Let all the kings of the earth confess to you, O Lord*[^25]. So it will be, and so it is, and daily it comes to pass; and it is shown not to have been said in vain, except that it was to be. *Let all the kings of the earth confess to you, O Lord.* But let them too, when they confess to you, when they praise you, not desire earthly things from you. For what will the kings of the earth desire? Do they not already have the empire itself? Whatever more a man desires on earth, his desire goes as far as the empire. What more can he have? A higher loftiness is needed. But perhaps the higher, the more perilous. And so kings, the greater the earthly loftiness they are in, the more they ought to be humbled before God. What do they do? *Because they have heard all the words of your mouth!* O Lord, *all the words of your mouth!* In I know not what nation the Law and the Prophets were hidden, *all the words of your mouth.* But in the nation of the Jews alone were *all the words of your mouth*: which nation, praising it, the apostle says: *What, then, has the Jew more? or what is the profit of circumcision? Much in every way. First indeed, because the eloquence of God was entrusted to them*[^26]. There were the words of God. But give me that Gideon, a certain holy man in the time of the Judges; see what kind of sign he asked of the Lord: *I will set*, he said, *a fleece on the threshing floor; let the fleece be rained upon, let the threshing floor be dry*[^27]. And it was done: the fleece alone was rained upon, the threshing floor was dry. He asked again for a sign: *Let the whole threshing floor be rained upon, and let the fleece alone be found dry*[^28]. And this too was done: the threshing floor was rained upon, the fleece dry. First the fleece rained upon, the threshing floor dry; afterward the threshing floor rained upon, the fleece dry. What does the threshing floor seem to you, brothers? Is it not the world? What the fleece? As it were the Jewish nation in the midst of the world, having the sacrament of grace, not in manifestation, but in the cloud of secrecy, as it were the rain in the fleece under a veil. The time came when the rain should be revealed on the threshing floor; it was made manifest, not covered. So there came to pass what was said: *Let all the kings of the earth confess to you, O Lord; because they have heard all the words of your mouth.* What is it, Israel, that you were hiding? how long were you hiding it? The fleece was wrung out, and from you went forth the rain. Christ alone is the sweetness of the rain: him alone you do not recognize in the Scriptures, on account of whom the Scriptures were made. But truly *let all the kings of the earth confess to you, O Lord; because they have heard all the words of your mouth.*

**10.** [v. 5.] *And let them sing in the ways of the Lord, because great is the glory of the Lord*[^29]. Let the kings of the earth sing in the ways of the Lord. In what ways let them sing? Of which it was said above: *In your mercy and your truth;* since *all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth.* So let not the kings of the earth be proud, but let them be humble. Then let them sing in the ways of the Lord, if they are humble: let them love, and they will sing. We know that travelers are singers; they sing, and hasten to arrive. There are evil songs, as it were of the old man; but the new song belongs to the new man. So let the kings of the earth too walk in your ways, let them walk and sing in your ways. What let them sing? *Because great is the glory of the Lord*: not of the kings.

**11.** [v. 6.] See how he wished the kings to sing in the ways, humbly bearing the Lord, not exalting themselves against the Lord. For if they exalt themselves, what follows? *Because the Lord is lofty, and looks upon the lowly*[^30]. Do the kings, then, wish to be looked upon? Let them be humble. What? if they exalt themselves into pride, can they lie hidden from his eyes? Lest perhaps, because you have heard, *he looks upon the lowly*, you should wish to be proud, and say in your soul: God looks upon the lowly, he does not look upon me; I will do what I wish. For who sees me? It is hidden from man; God does not wish to see me, because I am not lowly, but he looks upon the lowly: I do what I wish. O importunate man, would you say this, if you knew what you ought to love? Behold, if God should be unwilling to see you, do you not fear this very thing, that he does not wish to see you? If you greet some greater patron of yours, and, intent on something else, he does not see you, how does your soul grieve? But if God does not see you, do you think yourself secure? The Savior does not see you, and the despoiler does see you. And yet God himself too sees you. Do not think yourself unseen; rather, ask that you may deserve to be seen by him by whom you are seen. For it was said: *The eyes of the Lord are upon the just*[^31]. Because not upon the unjust, then let the unjust do what they wish: *The eyes of the Lord are upon the just.* Let it follow further: *And his ears toward their prayers.* So the unjust, who thought themselves secure because the eyes of the Lord were not upon them, do they not fear that his ears too are not toward their prayers? Is it not better that both his eyes be upon us, and his ears toward our prayers? But when you do those things over which you do not wish the eyes of the Lord to be, you do not deserve also the ears of the Lord for your prayers; and yet by doing evil, you do not turn away from yourself the eyes of the Lord. For what follows there? *But the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil.* But to what end? *To destroy their memory from the earth*[^32]. Do you see that you are seen? do you see that you cannot lie hidden? Since, then, whatever you do you are seen, why do you not do that whereby you may deserve to please? So here too what? *Because great is the glory of the Lord; because the Lord is lofty, and looks upon the lowly.* But the lofty he as it were does not look upon: for he *looks upon the lowly.* The lofty, what? *He knows from afar.* What, then, does the proud man gain for himself? That he be seen from afar; not that he be not seen. And do not think that you ought therefore to be secure, because he sees you less well who sees you from afar. For you, what you see from afar, you do not see well: God, even if he sees you from afar, sees you perfectly, and is not with you. This you bring about, not that you be seen less perfectly; but that you be not with him by whom you are seen. But what does the humble man gain for himself? *The Lord is near to those who have crushed their heart*[^33]. So let the proud man exalt himself as much as he wishes; surely God dwells on high, God is in heaven: do you wish him to draw near to you? Humble yourself. For he will be as much higher than you, as you are more lifted up: *but the lofty he knows from afar.*

**12.** [v. 7.] *If I shall walk in the midst of tribulation, you will give me life*[^34]. It is true: in whatever tribulation you are, confess, call upon him; he frees you, he gives you life. But yet we ought here to understand something better, whereby we may now more familiarly cleave to God, and say to him: *Quickly hear me.* For he had said: *The lofty he knows from afar*: and the lofty proud do not know tribulation. They do not know, I say, the tribulation of which it is said in another place: *I found tribulation and sorrow, and I called upon the name of the Lord*[^35]. For what great thing is it, if tribulation finds you? If you can do anything, you yourself find tribulation. And who is there, you say, who finds tribulation? or who would even seek it? You are in the midst of tribulation, and you do not know it? Is this life a small tribulation? If it is not a tribulation, it is not a pilgrimage; but if it is a pilgrimage, either you love the homeland too little, or without doubt you are in tribulation. For who would not be in tribulation, not to be with that which he desires? But whence does this tribulation not seem so to you? Because you do not love. Love the other life, and you will see that this life is a tribulation: with whatever prosperity it shines, with whatever delights it abounds and overflows; since that joy most sure, without any temptation, which God keeps for us at the end, is not yet present, without doubt it is a tribulation. So let us understand the tribulation of this too, brothers. *If I shall walk in the midst of tribulation, you will give me life.* He does not say it as though to say: If perhaps some tribulation befall me, you will free me from it. But how? *If I shall walk in the midst of tribulation, you will give me life;* that is, you will not otherwise give me life, unless I shall have walked in the midst of tribulation. *If I shall walk in the midst of tribulation, you will give me life. Woe to those who laugh; blessed are those who mourn*[^36]: *If I shall walk in the midst of tribulation, you will give me life.*

**13.** *Above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand, and your right hand has saved me*[^37]. Let the enemies rage; what can the enemies do? Take away money, despoil, proscribe, send into exile, torture with pains and torments; finally, if they are permitted, even kill: is there anything more? But you, O Lord, *above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand*: above what the enemies can do to me, you have stretched out your hand. For the enemies cannot separate me from you; but you avenge more, because you still defer me: *Above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand.* Let the enemy rage as much as he can, he does not separate me from God: but you, O Lord, do not yet receive me, you still grind me down in pilgrimage, you do not yet give your joy and sweetness; you have not yet inebriated me with the richness of your house, and with the torrent of your delight you have not yet given me to drink. For with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light[^38]. But behold I have given the firstfruits of the spirit, and have believed in you, and serve the law of God in my mind; yet within ourselves we still groan, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body[^39]. This life God gave to us when we sinned, where it is even necessary that Adam be ground down in the sweat and labor of his face, when the earth brings forth for him thorns and thistles[^40]. Could any enemy procure more? *Above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand*: but yet not unto despair. For it follows: *And your right hand has saved me.*

**14.** It can also be understood thus, *above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand*: the enemies were angry, you avenged me on the enemies. *The sinner will see and be angry, he will gnash with his teeth and waste away*[^41]. Where are those who said: Let the name of the Christians perish from the earth? Surely they either die, or are converted. So *above the wrath of the enemies you have stretched out your hand*, as long as that was said which is written: *My enemies have spoken evils of me, when will he die and his name perish*[^42]? when will the name of the Christians be blotted out from the earth? When they say these things, some have believed, some have perished, a few fearful ones have remained. How great a wrath of the enemies raged, when the blood of the martyrs was poured out! How they thought to blot out the name of the Christians from the earth! *Above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand, and your right hand has saved me.* Behold, those who persecuted the martyrs seek out the memorials of the martyrs, either where they may adore, or where they may inebriate themselves; yet they seek them. *Above the wrath of my enemies you have stretched out your hand, and your right hand has saved me.* According to desire *your right hand has saved me.* There is a certain salvation in the right hand; for there is another salvation in the left: temporal and fleshly salvation is in the left hand; eternal salvation with the angels is in the right hand. Therefore Christ, now set in that very immortality, is said to sit at the right hand of God[^43]. For God does not have in himself a right hand or a left; but the right hand of God is said to be that happiness which, because it cannot be shown to the eyes, has received such a name. By this your right hand you have saved me, not according to temporal salvation. For Crispina was killed; but did God therefore desert her? He did not make her safe in the left hand, but made her safe in the right. How great torments the Maccabees endured[^44]! But the three young men, walking in the midst of the fires, praised God[^45]. The salvation of the former in the right hand, of the latter in the left hand too. So sometimes he does not save his saints in the left hand; but he always saves in the right hand. But the impious he very often saves in the left hand, he does not save in the right. For those who persecuted Crispina were healthy in body: she was killed, they live. Their salvation in the left hand, hers in the right: *And your right hand has saved me.*

**15.** [v. 8.] *O Lord, you will repay for me*[^46]. I do not repay; you will repay for me. Let the enemies rage as much as they please; you will repay what I cannot. *O Lord, you will repay for me.* Attend to it in our head himself. For he left us an example, that we might follow his footsteps. *Who committed no sin, nor was guile found in his mouth: who, when he was reviled, did not revile in return*, saying: *O Lord, you will repay for me: when he was judged, he did not threaten; but commended himself to him who judges justly*[^47]. What is: *O Lord, you will repay for me*? *I*, he says, *do not seek my own glory; there is one who seeks it and judges*[^48]. *Not avenging yourselves, dearly beloved*, the apostle says: *but give place to wrath. For it is written: Vengeance is mine, and I will repay, says the Lord*[^49]. *O Lord, you will repay for me.*

**16.** There is here another understanding not to be neglected, and perhaps rather to be chosen. *O Lord* Christ, *you will repay for me.* For if I repay, I have plundered; you, what you did not plunder, you have paid: *O Lord, you will repay for me.* See him repaying for us. There came those who exacted tribute; they exacted the tribute of a didrachma, that is, two drachmas on one man: they came to the Lord, that he might pay the tribute; rather, not to him, but to the disciples; and it was said to them: *Does your Master not pay tribute?* They reported it to him. And he: *The kings of the earth, from whom do they exact tribute? from their sons, or from strangers?* They answered: *From strangers. So, then*, he said, *the sons are free. But yet, lest we scandalize them*, he said to Peter, *go, cast a hook into the sea, and the first fish that comes up, open its mouth, and you will find a stater*[^50]; that is, two didrachmas. A stater is a kind of weight, having four drachmas. *You will find it* there; *give it to them for me and you.* *O Lord, you will repay for me.* Rightly we have the first fish caught with the hook, seized with the hook; the first rising from the sea, the firstborn from the dead. In his mouth we find two didrachmas, that is, four drachmas: in his mouth we find the four Gospels. By these four drachmas we are freed from the exaction of this world; through the four Gospels we will not remain debtors: for there all our sins are paid. So he repaid for us: thanks to his mercy. He owed nothing; he did not pay for himself, but he paid for us. *Behold*, he says, *the prince of this world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^51]. What is, *in me he will find nothing*? He will find no sin in me; he has no reason to kill me. *But that all may know*, he says, *that I do the will of my Father, arise, let us go hence*[^52]. What is, *arise, let us go hence*? That is, not by necessity, but by will I suffer, paying what I do not owe: *O Lord, you will repay for me.*

**17.** *O Lord, your mercy is forever*[^53]. What should I desire? Not the day of men. *I have not labored following you, O Lord, and I have not coveted the day of man; you know*[^54]. Behold, the holy martyr Crispina, if she desired the day of men, would deny Christ: she would live longer here, but would not live forever. She preferred to live forever, than to live a little longer in time. Finally, *O Lord, your mercy is forever;* I do not wish to be freed for a time. *Your mercy is forever*, by which you freed the martyrs, and so quickly took them from this life. *O Lord, your mercy is forever.*

**18.** *The works of your hands do not despise*[^55]. I do not say, O Lord: Do not despise the works of my hands; I do not boast of my works. *I sought the Lord with my hands in the night before him, and I was not deceived*[^56]; but yet I do not commend the works of my hands: I fear lest, when you look, you find more sins than merits. This alone I ask, this I say, this I desire to obtain: *The works of your hands do not despise.* See your work in me, not mine; for if you see mine, you condemn; if you see yours, you crown. Because whatever good works of mine there are, they are to me from you, and therefore are yours rather than mine. For I hear from your apostle: *By grace you have been saved through faith, and this not of yourselves, but it is the gift of God; not of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up. For we are his handiwork, created in Christ Jesus in good works*[^57]. So, whether in that we are men, or in that we have been changed from our impiety and justified, O Lord, *the works of your hands do not despise.*
