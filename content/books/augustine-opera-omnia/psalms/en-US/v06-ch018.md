# Exposition of Psalm 139

**1.** We had prepared for you a short psalm, which we had ordered to be sung by the Reader; but on the spot, as it seems, being confused, he read one instead of the other. We have preferred to follow, in the Reader's error, the will of God, rather than our own in our own purpose. If, then, we shall have detained you somewhat in its length, do not impute it to us; but believe that God willed us not to labor fruitlessly. For not in vain did we receive, in our first sin, the punishment, that in the sweat of our face we should eat bread[^1]. Only attend, if it is bread. And it is bread, if it is Christ: *I am*, he says, *the living bread who came down from heaven*[^2]. Whom we have manifest in the Gospel, let us seek him also in the Prophets. Him those do not see over whose heart a veil is still set[^3], of which your charity heard yesterday. But for us, because the evening sacrifice of the Lord's cross tore the veil, that the secrets of the temple might now lie open; as long as Christ is preached to us, even if with labor and sweat, the bread must be eaten.

**2.** But our Lord Jesus Christ speaks in the Prophets sometimes from the person of our head, who is Christ himself the savior, sitting at the right hand of the Father[^4]; who also, for our sake born of a virgin, and under Pontius Pilate, suffered such things as you know[^5]; with innocent blood poured out, which is our price, he redeemed the guilty from the captivity in which we were held by the devil, forgiving us our offenses, and by that very price, his own blood, blotting out the bond by which we were held debtors[^6]. He is the ruler and bridegroom and redeemer of the Church, our head. And surely if he is the head, he has a body. But his body is the holy Church, which is also his spouse; to whom the apostle says: *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^7]. So the whole Christ is head and body, as it were a complete man: because the woman too was made from the man, and belongs to the man; and it was said of the first marriage: *The two will be in one flesh*[^8]. But the apostle interprets that this was not said in vain of those two men, as a mystery, except that in them Christ and the Church were already figured. For the apostle expounds it thus: *The two will be*, he says, *in one flesh: this is a great sacrament; but I speak in Christ and the Church*[^9]. He says too that Adam himself is the form of the one to come: *Who is*, he says, *the form of the one to come*[^10]. If, then, Adam is the form of the one to come; just as from the side of the sleeping man Eve was made[^11], so from the side of the sleeping Lord, that is, dying in the passion, and on the cross struck by the lance, flowed the Sacraments, by which the Church would be formed[^12]. For of that future passion of his he says thus in another psalm: *I slept, and took rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will receive me*[^13]. So sleeping is understood as the passion. Eve from the side of the sleeping man, the Church from the side of the suffering man. So our Lord Jesus Christ speaks in the Prophets sometimes from his own voice, sometimes from our voice, because he makes himself one with us; as it was said: *The two will be in one flesh.* Whence the Lord himself too says in the Gospel, when he was speaking of marriage: *So then they are now not two, but one flesh*[^14]. One flesh, because he took flesh from our mortality: but not one divinity; because he is the Creator, we the creature. Whatever, then, the Lord speaks from the person of the assumed flesh, belongs both to that head which has now ascended into heaven, and to these members which still labor in the earthly pilgrimage: for which laboring members, when Saul was persecuting them, he cried out from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^15]? Let us, then, hear the Lord Jesus Christ speaking in prophecy. For these Psalms were sung long before the Lord was born of Mary, not before the Lord existed: for he is always the Creator of all things; but at one time also born of a creature. Let us believe that divinity, and, as far as we can, understand it equal to the Father. But that divinity equal to the Father was made a partaker of our mortality, not of its own, but of ours; that we too might be made partakers of his divinity, not of our own, but of his.

**3.** [v. 1.] *O Lord, you have proved me, and have known me*[^16]. Let the Lord Jesus Christ himself say this, let him too say: *O Lord*, to the Father. For the Father is not his Lord, except because he deigned to be born according to the flesh. He is the Father of God, the Lord of the man. Do you wish to know of whom he is the Father? Of the Son equal to himself. The apostle says: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^17]. Of this form God is the Father, of the form equal to himself, of the only-begotten Son born of his own substance. But for our sake, that we might be remade, and be made partakers of his divinity, restored to eternal life, because he himself, as I said, was made a partaker of our mortality; what does the apostle say of him there, where he had said: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*? *But he emptied himself*, he says, *taking the form of a servant; made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^18]. But he was in the form of God equal to the Father; and he took the form of a servant, by which he would be less than the Father. Whence he says both in the Gospel: *I and the Father are one*; and: *Because the Father is greater than I.* *I and the Father are one*[^19], according to the form of God; *the Father is greater than I*[^20], according to the form of a servant. So because he is both Father and Lord—Father of the form of God, Lord of the form of a servant—let him say it himself, and let us not wonder, nor be scandalized, because the only Son of God says: *O Lord, you have proved me, and have known me. You have proved, and have known*: not because he did not know, but because he had made known to others. *You have proved me*, he says, *and have known me.*

**4.** [v. 2.] *You have known my sitting down and my rising up*[^21]. What is here the sitting down? what here the rising up? He who sits humbles himself. So the Lord sat in the passion, he rose in the resurrection. *You*, he says, *have known* this: that is, you willed it, you approved it; according to your will it was done. But if you wish to take the voice of the head from the person of the body, let us too say: *You have known my sitting down and my rising up.* For a man sits, when he humbles himself in penance; but he rises, his sins forgiven, when he is raised up in the hope of eternal life. Therefore in another psalm too it is said: *Rise after you have sat down, you who eat the bread of sorrow*[^22]. The penitents eat the bread of sorrow, who sing in another psalm, and say: *My tears have been my bread day and night*[^23]. What, then, is: *Rise after you have sat down*? Do not be exalted, unless you have been humbled. For many wish to rise before they have sat down; they wish to seem just, before they have confessed that they are sinners. So if you take it from the person of our head, understand thus: *You have known my sitting down and my rising up*, my passion and my resurrection. If from the person of the body: *You have known my sitting down and my rising up;* before your eyes I both confessed my sins, and was justified by your grace.

**5.** [vv. 3-4.] *You have understood my thoughts from afar: my path and my boundary you have searched out, and all my ways you have foreseen*[^24]. What is, *from afar*? While I am still in pilgrimage, before I come to that homeland, you have known my thought. Attend to that younger son; for he too was made the body of Christ, the Church coming from the Gentiles. For the younger son had gone into a far country. For a certain head of a household had two sons: the elder had not gone far, but was working in the field, and signifies the saints in the Law doing the works and precepts of the law[^25]. But the human race which had turned aside into the worship of idols had gone into a far country on pilgrimage. What is so far from him who made you, as the figment which you have made for yourself? So the younger son set out into a far country, carrying with him his substance, and, as we know in the Gospel, he squandered it living prodigally with harlots; and, suffering hunger, he attached himself to a certain prince of that region; and he set him over the feeding of swine, with whose husks he longed to be filled, and could not. After labor, and threshing, and tribulation, and want, the father came to his mind, and he wished to return; and he said thus: *I will arise, and go to my father. I will arise*, he said; for he had sat down. So recognize here his voice, saying: *You have known my sitting down and my rising up.* I sat in want, I rose in the desire of your bread. *You have understood my thoughts from afar.* For I had set out far; but where is he whom I had deserted not? *You have understood my thoughts from afar.* Therefore the Lord says thus in the Gospel, that *his father ran to meet him as he came*[^26]. Truly, because he had understood his thoughts from afar. *My path and my boundary you have searched out. My path*, he says: which, except the evil one which he had walked, to desert his father, as though he could be hidden from the eyes of the avenger; or rather that he would be ground down in that want, or be set to feeding swine, unless the father wished to scourge him while far off, that he might receive him near? So, as a fugitive caught, with the lawful vengeance of God following him—God, who avenges himself in our affections, wherever we go and wherever we proceed; so, as a fugitive caught, he speaks: *My path, and my boundary you have searched out.* What is, *my path*? By which I set out. What is, *my boundary*? As far as I came. *My path and my boundary you have searched out.* That far boundary of mine was not far from your eyes: I had gone far, and you were there. *My path and my boundary you have searched out.*

**6.** *And all my ways you have foreseen.* He did not say, you have seen; but, *you have foreseen.* Before I went them, before I walked them, you foresaw them; and you permitted me to go my ways in labor, that, if I were unwilling to labor, I might return to your ways. *Because there is no guile in my tongue*[^27]. Why did he say this? Because, behold, I confess to you; I walked my path, I became far from you; I departed from you, with whom it was well for me, and to my own good it was ill for me without you. For if it were well with me without you, I would perhaps be unwilling to return to you. So this man, confessing his sins, the body of Christ saying that it is justified, not in itself, but in his grace, said: *There is no guile in my tongue.*

**7.** [v. 5.] *Behold, you, O Lord, have known all things, the last and the ancient*[^28]. You have known my last things, when I fed swine; you have known my ancient things, when I asked of you the portion of my substance. The ancient things were for me the beginnings of the last evils. Ancient was the sin, when we fell; the last was the punishment, when we came into this laborious and perilous mortality. And would that this might be the last for us! It will be the last, if we now wish to return. For there is another last thing for certain impious men, to whom it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels*[^29]. But we, brothers, have thus far deserted God: let our labor up to the mortality of this life suffice. Let us remember the bread of our Father, let us recall the blessedness of our Father's house; let not the husks of the swine, the doctrines of demons, delight us. *Behold, you, O Lord, have known all things, the last and the ancient: the last*, to where I came; *the ancient*, where I offended you. *You formed me, and set your hand upon me. You formed me*: where? In this mortality; now unto the labors to which we are all born. For no one is born, except whom God has formed in his mother's womb; nor is there any creature of which he is not the maker. But *you formed me* in this labor: *and you set your hand upon me;* the avenging hand, weighing upon the proud. For he so wholesomely cast down the exalted, that he might raise up the humble. *You formed me, and set your hand upon me.*

**8.** [v. 6.] *Your knowledge has been made wondrous from me: it has prevailed; I shall not be able to reach it*[^30]. Something which is indeed obscure, but is understood with no small sweetness, hear with attention. Moses, the holy servant of God, with whom God spoke through a cloud—because, speaking in time, he surely spoke to his servant through an assumed creature; that is, not through his own substance, but through some assumed bodily creature, through which those voices were made, and sounded to human and mortal ears (For so God then spoke, not as he speaks in his own substance. For how does he speak in his own substance? The speech of God is the Word of God; the Word of God is Christ: that Word does not sound and pass away, but always abides unchangeably the Word, through which all things were made[^31]. To which Word it is said (for it is also the Wisdom of God): *You will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the same*[^32]. And in another place, when Scripture said of Wisdom: *Abiding in herself*, she says, *she renews all things*[^33]. So that Wisdom, standing—if it should be said even 'standing': but it is said on account of unchangeableness, not on account of immobility—and always being in the same way, varied by no place, by no time, nowhere otherwise than here or there, never otherwise than now or before, is itself the speech of God. But that speech which was made to Moses, was made to a man through syllables, through passing sounds. But these things would not be made, unless God assumed such a creature, through which he might emit speech and voices of this kind): Moses the holy one knew that this speech of God was made through certain assumed bodily creatures; and he desired and coveted to see the very form of God, and said to God speaking with him: *If I have found grace before you, show me yourself*[^34]. When he coveted this vehemently, and wished, by a certain (if it should be said) friendly familiarity, by which God himself deigned to treat him, to wring it from God, that he might see his majesty and face, in whatever way the face of God can be spoken of; he said to him: *You cannot see my face: for no one has seen my face, and lived. But I will place you in a cleft of the rock, and I will pass by, and I will place my hand over you; but when I have passed by, you will see my hinder parts*[^35]. And from these words another riddle was born, that is, a certain obscure figure of things. *When I have passed by, you will see my hinder parts*, says God; as though on one side he has a face, on another a back. Far be it from us to think any such thing of that Majesty. For he who thinks this of God, what does it profit him that the temples are closed? He fashions an idol in his own heart. So there are in those words great mysteries. The Lord was speaking, as I said, through a creature, as he willed, to his servant. There was understood the person of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ himself. Who indeed, according to the form of God, by which he is equal to the Father[^36], is likewise invisible to human eyes, as the Father too is. For if human wisdom cannot be seen with the eyes, can the Power and Wisdom of God be seen with fleshly eyes? But because the Lord was, at an opportune time, to assume flesh, that he might appear even to fleshly eyes, for the wholesome end of healing the mind within, when it was needful so to appear; foretelling this to Moses figuratively, he said: *You cannot see my face; you will see my hinder parts*, but *when I have passed by*[^37]: but that you may not see my face, my hand will be over you. What was it for the Lord to pass by, except what the evangelist says: *But when the hour had come, that Jesus should pass from this world to the Father*[^38]? For the Pasch itself signifies the passing. For what in the Hebrew word is called Pasch, in Latin is interpreted 'Passing.' What is: *You will not see my face*, but *you will see my hinder parts*? Whose person did that Moses bear, when it was said to him: *You will not see my face*, but *you will see my hinder parts*, and this *when I have passed by*: but that you may not see my face, *I will set my hand over you*? Did he call his face his first things? and in a certain way his hinder parts, the passing of his passion from this world? He appeared to the Jews; they did not recognize him. Moses bore their person, when it was said to him: *You cannot see my face.* Whence they did not see God set in the flesh. Because the hand of the Lord was heavy upon them. For of them Isaiah had said: *Make fat the heart of this people, and weigh down its eyes*[^39]: and it is their voice in another psalm: *Because your hand was heavy upon me*[^40]. So that they might not then recognize the divinity of Christ (for if they had recognized it, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory[^41]: but the Lord not being crucified, his blood would not redeem the world), what did God do, except what the apostle says, the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God, where he exclaims: *O depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how incomprehensible are his judgments, and unsearchable his ways! For who has known the mind of the Lord? or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, and it shall be repaid to him? For from him, and through him, and in him are all things; to him be glory forever and ever*[^42]. The apostle says this; because above he had said: *Blindness in part has come upon Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter, and so all Israel might be saved*[^43]. So the Jews were blinded in part, deservedly for their pride, because they called themselves just; and, blinded, they crucified the Lord. He set his hand over them, that they might not see him, until he passed by, namely from this world to the Father. Let us see whether, after he passed by, they saw his hinder parts. The Lord rose, he appeared to his disciples and to all who had already believed in him; not to those by whom he had been crucified, because he had set his hand over them, until he passed by. But he ascended into heaven, forty days having been spent with his disciples. But, the day of Pentecost being fulfilled, he sent them the Holy Spirit. Filled with the Holy Spirit, they began to speak in the tongues of all, who had been born in one, and had learned only one[^44]. Thousands of those who had crucified the Lord were amazed, and shuddered at so great a miracle: pricked in heart at so great a miracle, they asked counsel of the apostles, what they should do, after Christ was preached to them, as they wondered whence unlearned men spoke in all tongues. So, Christ being announced to them through the apostle Peter—Christ whom they had despised on the cross, whom they had mocked as a mortal man; over whom they exulted because he did not come down from the cross, when surely it was much greater what he did, to rise from the tomb, than to come down from the cross—Christ, then, being announced to them, they said: *What shall we do*[^45]? Those who had raged against the Lord whom they saw, now ask counsel of salvation; and it was said to them: *Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ; and your sins will be forgiven you*[^46]. Behold, they saw his hinder parts, whose face they could not see. For his hand was over their eyes; not always, but until he passed by. After he passed by, he took away his hand from their eyes: the hand being taken from their eyes, they say to the disciples: *What shall we do?* First savage, afterward devout; first angry, afterward fearful; first hard, afterward bent; first blind, afterward enlightened.

**9.** I think that in this psalm too we recognize voices of this kind even of the Gentiles recalling their own unbelief. *For God has shut up all in unbelief, that he may have mercy on all*[^47]. *You formed me, and set your hand upon me. Your knowledge has been made wondrous from me: it has prevailed; I shall not be able to reach it.* As it were, you set your hand upon me: you became wondrous to me; I do not comprehend you with whom I was. How easy for me was the face of my father, when I said: *Give me my substance, which falls to me*[^48]. Behold, having set out into a far country and ground down by hunger, it is much for me, and labor is before me; I cannot grasp what I let go. *For your knowledge*, he says, *has been made wondrous from me.* From my sin it came about that it was made wondrous to me, and existed incomprehensible to me. For there was for me an easiness of contemplating you, when I had not by pride deserted you. *Your knowledge has been made wondrous from me: it has prevailed; I shall not be able to reach it*: but you supply, *from me. I shall not be able to reach it from myself.* So when I shall be able, I shall not be able except from you.

**10.** [v. 7.] Behold, you find that the fugitive far off does not lie hidden from the eyes of him from whom he flees. And where will he now go, whose boundary has been searched out? See what he says: *Where shall I go from your Spirit?* For the Spirit of the Lord has filled the world[^49]: who can flee in the world from that Spirit, with which the world is full? *Where shall I go from your Spirit? and where shall I flee from your face?* He seeks a place where he may flee from the wrath of God. What is the place that will receive a fugitive from God? Men who receive fugitives ask of them, from whom they have fled; and whatever servant they find of some less powerful master, they receive as it were without any fear, saying in their heart: This man does not have such a master as can search him out. But when they have heard of a powerful master, either they do not receive him, or they receive him with great fear: because even a powerful man can be deceived. Where is God not? who deceives God? whom does God not see? from whom does God not demand back his fugitive? Where, then, will this fugitive go from the face of God? He turns himself this way and that, as it were seeking a place for his flight.

**11.** [v. 8.] *If I shall ascend*, he says, *into heaven, you are there; if I shall descend into hell, you are present*[^50]. At last you have known, evil fugitive, that in no way can you become far from him from whom you wished to go far. Behold, he is everywhere: you, where will you go? He found counsel, and this inspired by him who now deigns to call him back. *If I shall ascend into heaven, you are there; if I shall descend into hell, you are present.* If I lift myself up, I find you the represser; if I hide myself, I find you the searcher; and not only the searcher, but the investigator. For if I grow proud of my justice, you are there, whose is the true justice. If by sinning I come into the depth of evils, and despise confessing[^51], saying: *Who sees me*? for *in hell who will confess to you*[^52]? there too you are present, to avenge. Where, then, shall I go, that I may flee from your face, that is, that I may not feel you angry?

**12.** [v. 9.] He found this counsel: So will I flee, he says, from your face, so will I flee from your Spirit; from the avenging Spirit, from the avenging face, so will I flee. How? *If I shall take up my wings straight on, and shall dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea*[^53]: so can I flee from your face. If in the uttermost parts of the sea he wishes to flee from the face of God, will he who flees not be there, of whom he said: *If I shall descend into hell, you are present*? It is a wonder if he is not in the uttermost parts of the sea, who is not absent even among the dead. But I know, he says, how I may flee from your wrath. My wings are to be taken up not crookedly, but straight on, that I may neither be raised into proud presumption, nor sunk into lost despair. What wings does he wish to take up, except two wings, the two precepts of love? On which two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets hang[^54]. These wings, he says, these feathers, if I shall so take up, and shall dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea, I can flee from your face to your face, from the face of the angry one to the face of the appeased one. For what is the uttermost part of the sea, except the end of the world? Thither let us now fly by hope and desire, having the wings of the twofold love: let there be no rest for us, except in the uttermost part of the sea. For if we wish rest elsewhere, we will be cast headlong into the sea. Let us fly until the sea is ended, let us suspend ourselves on the wings of the twofold love: let us meanwhile fly to God by hope, and let us premeditate by faithful hope that end of the sea.

**13.** [v. 10.] But who may lead us through, attend: that very one whose face, angry, we wish to flee. For what follows? *If I shall descend into hell, you are present. If I shall take up my wings straight on. I shall take up*, he says: so he had lost them. *If I shall take up my wings straight on, and shall dwell in the last parts of the sea. For even there your hand will lead me through, and your right hand will bring me*[^55]. Let us meditate on this, dearest brothers; let this be our hope, let this be our consolation. Let us take up by love the wings which we lost by cupidity. For cupidity has become the birdlime of our wings; it dashed us down from the freedom of our air, that is, of those free breezes of the Spirit of God. Dashed down from there, we lost our wings, and were as it were taken captive in the power of the fowler: thence he redeemed us by his blood, whom we fled that we might be caught. He nourishes wings for us from his precepts; we now raise them without birdlime. Let us not love the sea, but let us fly to the uttermost parts of the sea. Let no one be afraid; but let no one presume upon his own wings: because even the winged, unless he himself raises us, unless he himself leads us, will be cast headlong into the depths of the sea, wearied and tired, as it were presuming upon our own strength. So there is need that we have wings, and there is need that he himself lead us through; for he is our helper. We have free choice; but by that very free choice, what can we do, unless he helps who commands? *For even there your hand will lead me through, and your right hand will bring me.*

**14.** [v. 11.] And considering the length of the way, what did he say to himself? *And I said: Perhaps the darkness will trample me*[^56]. For behold, I have now believed in Christ, I am now raised up by the two wings of the twofold love, and the iniquity of this world abounds; and because iniquity has abounded, the love of many grows cold. So the Lord said: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*[^57]. In this life, amid so many scandals, amid so many sins, amid such crowds of daily temptations, of daily evil suggestions, what do I do, he says? how shall I come to the uttermost parts of the sea? I hear with terror from the Lord: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*: then he added: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^58]. Attending to the length of the way, I said to myself: *Perhaps the darkness will trample me. And the night is illumination in my delights.* The night has become illumination for me: because in the night I had despaired of being able to cross so great a sea, and to overcome so great a way, and to come to the end by persevering to the end. Thanks to him who sought me the fugitive, who struck my back with the blow of the scourge, who, by calling me, called me back from destruction, who made the night illumined for me. For it is night as long as this life is lived. How is the night illumined? Because Christ descended into the night. Christ took flesh from this world, and illumined the night for us. For that woman had lost a drachma[^59]; she lit a lamp. The Wisdom of God had lost a drachma. What is the drachma? A coin, on which coin was the image of our Emperor. For man was made to the image of God[^60], and had perished. And what did the wise woman do? She lit a lamp. The lamp is of clay, but it has a light by which the drachma may be found. So the lamp of wisdom, the flesh of Christ, was made of clay; but it shines by his Word, it finds the lost. *And the night is illumination in my delights:* the night has become for me in my delights. Our delight is Christ. See how we now rejoice over him. These shouts of yours, these joys of yours, whence are they, except from delights? But whence these delights, except because the night has been illumined, except because Christ the Lord is preached to us? Because he sought you before you sought him, and found you that you might find him. *And the night is illumination in my delights.*

**15.** [v. 12.] *Because the darkness will not be darkened by you*[^61]. Do not, then, darken your own darkness; and God does not darken it, but rather illumines it: because it was said to him in another psalm: *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, you will illumine my darkness*[^62]. But who darken their darkness, which God does not darken? Evil men, perverse men: when they sin, they are surely darkness; while they do not confess the sins they committed, but moreover defend them, they darken their darkness. So now if you have sinned, you are in darkness: but by confessing your darkness, you will deserve to have your darkness illumined; but by defending your darkness, you darken your darkness. And when will you escape from a double darkness, you who labored in the single? But whence does the Lord not darken our darkness? Because he does not let us have our sins unpunished: he scourges us in these labors, and instructs us. This whole misery of the human race in which the world groans, know, brothers, that it is a medicinal pain, not a penal sentence. You see that pain is everywhere, everywhere fear, everywhere necessity, everywhere labors. Avarice grows, but among the evil. If for this God here instructs us with such scourges, that our darkness may not be darkened; let us acknowledge that we are under the punishment of the scourge, and let us bless God who mixes bitternesses with the sweetness of temporal life, lest, blinded by the delight of temporal delights, we should not desire the eternal delights, nor wish the sea to be ended and to dwell in the last parts of the sea. So let the waves of the sea rage; the more the waves of the sea rage, the more that dove suspends itself with its wings. So God does not darken our darkness; because he mixes scourges with our sins, and bitternesses with our crooked sweetnesses. Let us not darken our darkness by defending our sins; and the night is illumination in our delights. *Because the darkness will not be darkened by you.*

**16.** *And the night will be illumined like the day. The night like the day*[^63]: the day for us is the prosperity of the world, the night for us is the adversity of the world: but if we acknowledge that we suffer adversities deservedly for our sins, and the scourges of the Father are sweet to us, lest the sentence of the judge be bitter; we will so have the darkness of this night as the light of this night. If it is night, how is there light there? It is night, because here the human race goes astray: it is night, because we have not yet come to that day which yesterday and tomorrow do not confine; but it is a perpetual day, without rising, because without setting. So it is night here; but this night has a certain light of its own, and its own darkness. We have said why in general it is night: what is the light of this night? The prosperity and happiness of this world, temporal joy, temporal honor, is as it were the light of this night. But adversity and the bitterness of tribulations or lowliness are as it were the darkness of this night. In this night, in this mortality of human life, men have light, men have darkness; light, prosperity; darkness, adversity. But when Christ the Lord has come, and dwelt in the soul through faith, and promised another light, and inspired and granted patience, and admonished a man not to be delighted by prosperous things, lest he be broken by adverse ones; the faithful man begins to use this world indifferently, neither to be exalted when prosperous things happen, nor to be broken when adverse things are present, but everywhere to bless the Lord; not only when he abounds, but also when he loses; not only when he is healthy, but also when he is sick; so that there may be in him that true song: *I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall ever be in my mouth*[^64]. If, then, *always*, both when this night shines, and when this night is dark; when prosperity smiles, when adversity is sad, let his praise ever be in your mouth: and there will come to you what was just now said: *As his darkness, so also his light.* His darkness does not trample me, because his light does not exalt me.

**17.** Behold, you have his light in Job: he abounded in all things. The light of the night is first described in his riches: with how many things and how great abundance he overflowed, was the light of his night. The enemy thought that such a man worshipped God for this reason, because he had granted him all those things; and he asked that they be taken from him: the darkness of that night was made which first had light. Yet he knew, whether there was light there or darkness, that it was a night in which he was a pilgrim away from his God; and he had as his inner light God himself, by which inner light he held indifferently either the darkness of that night, or the light. Therefore, because in the light of that night, that is, in the abundance of things, he worshipped God, when all his things were taken from him, after his darkness was made, what did he say? *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^65]. I am in a certain night of this life: my Lord, he says, dwells in my heart; he illumined this night for me with certain consolations, when he gave abundance of temporal things: he withdrew that very temporal light, and the night was as it were darkened. But because *as his darkness, so also his light: The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done: blessed be the name of the Lord.* I am not sad in this night, because *as his darkness, so also his light.* Both pass away, that those who rejoice may be as not rejoicing, and those who weep as not weeping[^66]; because *as his darkness, so also his light.*

**18.** [v. 13.] *Because you have possessed my reins, O Lord*[^67]. Not without cause: *As his darkness, so also his light.* The possessor is within: he holds not only the heart, but also the reins; not only the thoughts, but also the delights. So he himself possesses that whence something of light might delight me in the night; he himself holds my reins: I do not know how to be delighted except by the inner light of his wisdom. What then? are you not delighted by the prosperity of things, by the happiness of the times, by honors, by riches, by household? I am not delighted, he says. Why? Because *as his darkness, so also his light.* Whence comes to you this indifference, that as his darkness, so also his light is to you? whence? *Because you have possessed my reins, O Lord; you took me up from my mother's womb*[^68]. While I was in my mother's womb, I did not hold indifferently the darkness of that night and the light of that night. For the womb of my mother was the custom of my city. What is that city? The one that first bore us in captivity. We know that Babylon of which we spoke yesterday, from which set out all who believe and sigh for that light, the heavenly Jerusalem. So I said: From my mother's womb I was taken up by the Lord; thence the darkness of this night and the light of this night became indifferent to me. But he who is in the womb of that mother Babylon rejoices in the prosperous things of the world, is broken by the adversities of the world; he does not know how to rejoice unless something prosperous happens according to time, nor does he know how to be made sad unless something adverse happens according to time. Now go out of the womb of Babylon, begin to sing a hymn to the Lord: go out, and be born; God will take you up from your mother's womb. Which God? That God of the apostle Paul, who said: *But when it pleased God, who set me apart from my mother's womb, to reveal his Son in me*[^69]. For who was his mother? The synagogue. And there what had he learned, except what the Jews themselves and that very people had and had learned? The name of the praise of God had remained in them; but deeds were not found in them: there were in them the words of God, as leaves on a tree, and fruit nowhere. Such a fig tree, as you know, when the Lord found it, he withered with a curse[^70]. For he found on it leaves, and found no fruit: it figured a certain tree for us. For the time of those fruits was not yet: did the maker of heaven and earth not know what all men knew? So he who set apart Paul from his mother's womb, he himself set us apart too from our mother's womb. From which our mother? That Babylon. Taken up, then, from that womb, let us now begin to have another hope. He has promised, brothers, why you should rejoice; bear fruit, set in another hope. We now know no evil except to offend God, and not to be led to those things which he promised; nor do we know any good except to win God's favor, and to be led to those things which he promises. What are those goods of this world, and the evils of this world? Let us hold them indifferently; because now, taken up from the womb of that our mother, holding them indifferently, we say: *As his darkness, so also his light.* Neither does the happiness of the world make us blessed, nor adversity wretched. It is needful to hold to justice, to love faith, to hope in God, to love God, to love also the neighbor. After these labors we will have unwearying light, we will have a day without setting: whatever is bright or dark in this night passes away. *Because you have possessed my reins, O Lord; you took me up from my mother's womb.*

**19.** [v. 14.] *I will confess to you, O Lord, because you have been made wondrous terribly. You have been made wondrous terribly*[^71]: by this very thing, that we marvel at you, you are terrible; with trembling we rejoice. For we fear lest, exalting ourselves over your gifts through pride, we deserve to lose what we received by humility. *I will confess to you, O Lord, because you have been made wondrous terribly: wondrous are your works, and my soul knows them greatly.* Now my soul knows greatly; because you took me up from my mother's womb: but before, your knowledge had been made wondrous from me; it had prevailed, nor could I reach it. So from myself it had prevailed, nor could I reach it. Whence now does my soul know greatly, except because the night is illumination in my delights? except because your grace came to me, and illumined my darkness? except because you have possessed my reins? except because you took me up from my mother's womb?

**20.** [v. 15.] *My bone is not hidden from you, which you made in secret*[^72]. He says his bone: what is commonly called 'ossum' is in Latin called 'os.' This is found in the Greek. For we could here think it to be 'os' (mouth), from which come 'ora' (mouths); not 'os' (bone) with the short vowel, from which come 'ossa' (bones). *It is not*, then, *hidden*, he says, *my bone, from you, which you made in secret.* I have in secret a certain bone. For let us rather speak thus: it is better that the grammarians reprove us, than that the people not understand. So there is, he says, a certain bone of mine within in secret; you made within a bone for me in secret, and it is not hidden from you. For you made it in secret; but did you hide this even from yourself? This bone of mine, made by you in secret, men do not see, men do not know; but you know, who made it. What, then, does he mean by 'bone,' brothers? Let us seek it; it is in secret. But because, Christians in the name of the Lord, we speak to Christians, now we find what such a bone is. It is a certain inner firmness; because in bones firmness and strength are understood. So there is a certain inner firmness of the soul, where it is not broken. Whatever torments, whatever tribulations, whatever adversities of this world rage on every side; that which God made firm in secret cannot be broken in us, does not yield. For by the Lord there has been made a certain firmness of our patience, of which it is said in another psalm: *But yet my soul will be subject to God; for from him is my patience*[^73]. And attend to the apostle Paul, having this kind of firmness within: *As though sorrowful*, he says; *yet always rejoicing*[^74]. Whence *as though sorrowful*? By insults, reproaches, persecutions, scourges, blows, stonings, prisons, chains. Who would not then think them wretched? Nor would those persecutors rage against them, unless they thought they were made wretched by their persecutions. For they conjectured them from their own weakness, who did not have the hidden bone within: but those who had it seemed sorrowful to men outwardly, but within rejoiced in God; from whom their bone was not hidden, which he had made in secret. But the same apostle Paul opens this bone made by God in secret, in these words: *And not only that*, he says, *but we also glory in tribulations*[^75]. Is it too little that you are not sorrowful, but you even glory? Let it suffice you not to be sorrowful. It is too little, he says, for Christians: he made such a bone for me in secret, that it is too little not to be broken, unless I even glory. Whence do you glory? *In tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience.* See how that firmness was formed within in the heart: *Knowing that tribulation works patience, patience proof, proof hope; but hope does not confound: because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^76]. So that hidden bone was formed and made firm, that it makes us even glory in tribulations. But we seem wretched to men, because what we have within is hidden from them. *My bone is not hidden from you, which you made in secret: and my substance, in the lower parts of the earth.* Behold, my substance is in the flesh, my substance is in the lower parts of the earth; and yet I have within a bone, which you formed, which makes me not yield to all the persecutions of this lower region, where my substance still is. For what great thing is it if an angel is strong? It is a great thing if the flesh is strong. And whence is the flesh strong, whence is the earthen vessel strong, except because a bone was made there in secret? *And my substance in the lower parts of the earth.*

**21.** [v. 16.] What of those who are less firm? For it is Christ who speaks, as I have already commended. But many things were said from the person of the body; hear also from the person of the head: and he does not as it were distinguish, so as to introduce those persons, now the head, now the body. For if he distinguishes, he as it were divides; the two will not be in one flesh. But if two are in one flesh[^77]; do not wonder if two are in one voice. When our Lord Jesus Christ suffered, the disciples did not yet have that inner bone; the strength of patience was not yet made firm in them: and they were hidden from themselves, they did not know their own strength; and Peter dared to promise fellowship in death with the Lord's passion, and did not know himself to be sick; but the physician knew the sick man. But what happened? *With you*, he said, *even unto death. Amen I say to you; before the cock crows, you will deny me three times*[^78]. The answer of the physician was found truer than the presumption of the sick man. Of these, then, he says: *My bone is not hidden from you, which you made in secret*: in those in whom that bone is made firm within, and especially the strength of suffering in our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ himself, who, when he willed, sat; when he willed, rose; when he willed, slept; when he willed, woke; because: *I have power*, he says, *to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it again*[^79]. What of those in whom that fortitude was not formed and made firm? what does he say of them? See what he says to God the Father: *My imperfect part your eyes saw. My imperfect part*, my Peter promising and denying, presuming and failing: yet your eyes saw him. For that the Lord himself too looked back upon him, as it is written in the Gospel, now after the third denial reminded what the Lord had foretold him, *he went out, and wept bitterly*[^80]: that weeping was from the looking-back of God; because: *My imperfect part*, he says, *your eyes saw.* For that imperfect one, wavering in the Lord's passion, without doubt would have perished: but your eyes saw him; not only him, but also all who were imperfect, until they were made firm by Christ's resurrection. For it appeared to their eyes that what was dead had not perished in the Lord; and that bone was made in their secret, so that they themselves now no longer feared to die. *My imperfect part your eyes saw; and in your book all will be written*[^81]: not only the perfect, but also the imperfect. Let not the imperfect fear, only let them make progress. Nor, because I said: Let them not fear, let them love imperfection, and remain there where they were found. Only let them make progress, as much as is in them. Daily let them add, daily let them advance: yet let them not withdraw from the body of the Lord; that, framed together in one body, and in these members, they may be able to deserve that this voice was said of them: *My imperfect part your eyes saw; and in your book all will be written.*

**22.** *By day they will go astray, and no one in them*[^82]. The day here was still our Lord Jesus Christ: whence he said: *Walk, while you have the day*[^83]. But *by day* his imperfect ones *will go astray.* They too thought our Lord Jesus Christ to be only a man; not to have in himself a hidden divinity, not to be secretly God, but to be only what he seemed: this they too thought. For even Peter himself (for we speak especially of him, in whom an example of weakness not despaired of is set before us): the same Peter had said to the Lord, when he asked what men said he was: *You are the Christ the Son of the living God.* And the Lord had said to him: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^84]. Why? Because he had called him the Son of God. There, a little after, in the same place, in the very context of the words, the Lord began to speak of his coming passion. But that Peter, who had now confessed him the Son of God, feared lest he should die like a son of man. He was the Son of God, he was also the son of man: Son of God, in the form of God, equal to the Father; son of man, in the form of a servant, by which he is less than the Father[^85]. He was to come to the passion surely from the form of a servant[^86]; why did Peter fear lest in the form of a servant the form of God should perish, and did he not rather presume that from the form of God the form of a servant would come to life again? He said to him: *Far be it from you, Lord; be merciful to yourself*[^87]. And the Lord, who had called him blessed in that voice: *Get behind me, Satan*, he said; *for you do not savor the things of God, but the things of men*[^88]. A little before, because he had said: *You are the Christ the Son of the living God;* he heard: *Flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*: therefore the rock, therefore blessed. But now, because he had answered not from the revelation of the Father, but from the weakness of the flesh, he was called Satan; *You do not savor*, he said, *the things of God, but the things of men.* And this was Christ, brothers: he had walked among them, had commanded the winds, before their eyes had trodden upon the waves[^89], before their eyes had raised a man four days dead, before their eyes had done such great miracles; and yet they trembled in his passion, as though they had lost him in whom they had vainly presumed. But *by day they will go astray, and no one in them.* No one at all, not even he who had said: *With you even unto death*[^90]. For he had said to them: *The hour will come, that you will leave me alone, and each one will go his own way: but I am not alone, because the Father is with me*[^91]. With him was the Father, and he with the Father; and in him the Father, and he in the Father; and he and the Father one; and they feared when he died. Why, except because by day they went astray, and there was no one in them? *By day they will go astray, and no one in them.*

**23.** [v. 17.] But what is, *they will go astray by day*? Will they perish? And where is: *My imperfect part your eyes saw, and in your book all will be written*? When, then, did they go astray by day? When they did not understand the Lord set here. And what follows? *But to me your friends have been greatly honored, O God*[^92]. The very same who went astray by day, and there was no one in them, have been made your friends, and have been greatly honored to me. That bone was made in them in secret after the Lord's resurrection, and they suffered for the name of him in whose passion they trembled. *But to me your friends have been greatly honored, O God; their princedoms have been greatly strengthened.* Made apostles, made leaders of the Church, made rams leading the flocks, *their princedoms have been greatly strengthened.*

**24.** [v. 18.] *I will number them, and they will be multiplied above the sand*[^93]. Through those who went astray by day, and there was no one in them, behold there has been born this so great multitude, which now, like the sand, cannot be numbered, except by God. For he said: *They will be multiplied above the sand;* and yet he had said: *I will number them.* Those very ones, numbered, *will be multiplied above the sand.* For the sand is numbered by him, by whom the hairs of our head are numbered[^94]. *I will number them, and they will be multiplied above the sand.*

**25.** *I rose up, and am still with you. I rose up, and am still with you*[^95], what is it? I have now suffered, he says, I have been buried; behold I have risen, and they still do not understand me to be with them. *I am still with you*, that is, not yet with them; because they do not yet acknowledge me. For so it is read in the Gospel, that after the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, when he appeared to them they did not at once know him[^96]. There is also another sense: *I rose up, and am still with you*: so that he wished to signify this time, in which he is still in secret at the right hand of the Father, before he is revealed in the brightness in which he will come to judge the living and the dead.

**26.** [vv. 19-20.] And then he says what, meanwhile, through this whole time, while he has now risen and is still with the Father, he suffers here through the mingling of sinners in his body, which is the Church, and through the separation of heretics. For it follows and says: *If you slay sinners, O God; you men of blood, depart from me, because you will say in thought: They will take their cities in vanity*[^97]. The order of the words seems to be connected thus: *If you slay sinners, O God, they will take their cities in vanity.* For he wishes them to be understood as slain, when by the pride with which they are puffed up, they lose the grace by which they live. *For the holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit, and will take himself from thoughts that are without understanding*[^98]. So sinners are slain, because, darkened in understanding, they are alienated from the life of God[^99]. For they lose confession on account of pride: and so in them, when slain, there comes to pass what is written: *From a dead man, as from one who is not, confession perishes*[^100]. And so they take *their cities in vanity*, that is, their vain peoples, following their vanity; when, puffed up with the name of justice, they persuade men that, the bond of unity being broken, they should follow them, the blind and unskilled following them as though more just. And because they very often find from this an occasion of separating themselves from the unity of Christ, while they accuse the evil, with whom they pretend they are unwilling to have communion; and because it can happen that they not only defame the innocent, whom they pretend to flee as evil, but also say true things about certain evil men like themselves, among whom the wheat of Christ groans, the bond of unity being kept[^101]: therefore he interposed: *You men of blood, depart from me, because you will say in thought, they will take their cities in vanity;* that is, therefore they will seduce their peoples into their own separation, to be corrupted by their own vanity, *because* you *will say in thought: You men of blood, depart from me*: so that, namely, the sinners slain in spirit deservedly for their pride, may therefore take their cities, that is, their peoples, in vain, that is, may lead them over into the vanity of error by separating, and, as though offended by the mingling of the chaff, may, the unity being broken, desert the wheat; because he admonishes the wheat itself, that is, the good faithful, that before the winnowing, which will be at the last, they should not openly separate themselves from the evil, lest they desert the good who are still mingled with them; but by good conduct and unlikeness of life should as it were silently say to them: *You men of blood, depart from me.* For he says this to them by the voice of God, which voice is in thought, just as God says it in the thought of his holy people. But who are the men of blood, except those who hate their brothers; as John says: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^102]? So, not understanding this, the slain sinners—how God says to the evil in the thought of the good: *You men of blood, depart from me*—accuse in them the communion of the evil; and by separating themselves through these slanders, they take *their cities in vanity.* This voice, which is now said to the evil in the thought of the good, will be open on that day, when it is said to them by our head himself: *I never knew you; depart from me, all you who work iniquity*[^103].

**27.** [v. 21.] But now the body of Christ, which is the Church, says: What is it that the proud slander me with, as though another's sins stained me, and therefore, by separating themselves, take *their cities in vanity*? *Did I not hate those who hated you, O Lord*[^104]? Why do worse men exact of me even a bodily separation from the evil, so that before the time of harvest the wheat too is uprooted together with the weeds[^105]; so that before the time of winnowing I lose the endurance of bearing with the chaff; so that before all kinds of fish come, at the end of the age as it were to the shore, to be separated[^106], I break the nets of peace and unity? Are the Sacraments which I receive the evil men's? do I communicate with their life and deeds by consenting? *Did I not hate those who hated you, O Lord; and did I not waste away over your enemies?* did I not, when the zeal of your house consumed me, see the senseless, and waste away[^107]? did weariness not hold me from the sinners who forsake your law[^108]? For who are your enemies, except those who by their life show how they hate your law? These, then, since I hated them, why do those slander me who take *their cities in vanity*, that the sins of those whom I hated, and over whom by the zeal of God's house I wasted away, can be imputed to me? But where is: *Love your enemies*[^109]? Or is it because he said *your* (enemies), not God's? *Do good*, he says, *to those who hate you*: he does not say, those who hate God. Therefore this man, going on, said: *Did I not hate those who hated you, O Lord?* he did not say, those who hate me. *And I wasted away over your enemies:* *your* he said; not, mine. But those who hate us for this reason, and are enemies to us for this reason, because we serve God, what else do they but hate him, and are his enemies? Ought we, then, not to love such enemies of ours? Or do they not suffer persecution for the sake of God, to whom it is said: *Pray for those who persecute you*[^110]? So attend to what follows.

**28.** [v. 22.] *With a perfect hatred I hated them*[^111]. What is, *with a perfect hatred*? I hated in them their iniquities, I loved your creation. This is to hate with a perfect hatred, that you neither hate men on account of their vices, nor love vices on account of men. For behold, see what he adds: *They have become enemies to me.* Now he shows them to be enemies not only of God, but his own. How, then, will he fulfill in them both what he himself said: *Did I not hate those who hated you*, and what the Lord commanded: *Love your enemies*? How will he fulfill this, except by that perfect hatred; so that he hates in them this, that they are wicked, and loves this, that they are men? For even in the time of the Old Testament, in which the carnal people was restrained by visible punishments, a man belonging by understanding to the New Testament, the servant of God Moses, how did he hate those who sinned, when he prayed for them? and how did he not hate them, when he slew them, except because with a perfect hatred he hated them? For with that perfection he so hated the iniquity which he punished, that he loved the humanity for which he prayed.

**29.** [v. 23.] Since, then, the body of Christ, to be separated even bodily from the impious and wicked at the end, now meanwhile groans among them; and since those slain sinners, by slandering the good as it were over the communion of the evil, and by separating themselves from the good and innocent as it were on the occasion of the evil, so take their cities in vanity, that many evil men still remain who do not follow their separation, but, to be borne by the good until the end, remain in the same mingling: what does the body of Christ do amid these things, bearing fruit a hundredfold, or sixtyfold, or thirtyfold with endurance[^112]? What does the close one of Christ do in the midst of the daughters, like a lily in the midst of thorns[^113]? What are her voices? what her conscience? what the beauty of the king's daughter within[^114]? Behold, hear what she says: *Prove me, O God, and know my heart*[^115]. You, God, you prove, you know; not man, not the heretic, who neither knows how to prove, nor can know my heart, where you prove and know that I do not consent to the deeds of the evil, and they think I can be contaminated by the sins of others: so that, while I in my far-off pilgrimage do what in another psalm I groan, that is, am peaceable with those who hate peace[^116], until I come to that vision of peace, which is interpreted Jerusalem, which is the mother of us all, the eternal city in the heavens; they themselves, by quarreling and slandering and separating themselves, take, not indeed in eternity, but *in vanity their cities.* *Prove me*, then, *O God, and know my heart; search me, and know my paths.* For what purpose this? Attend to what follows.

**30.** [v. 24.] *And see*, he says, *if there is a way of iniquity in me, and lead me in the eternal way*[^117]. *Search*, he says, *my paths*, that is, my counsels and my thoughts: *and see if there is a way of iniquity in me*, whether by doing or by consenting; *and lead me in the eternal way.* What else does he say, than, lead me in Christ? For who is the eternal way, except he who is the eternal life? For he is eternal who said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^118]. If, then, you have found anything in my way which displeases your eyes, since my way is mortal; you *lead me in the eternal way*, where there is no iniquity: because *even if anyone sins, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just. He is the propitiation for our sins*[^119]; he is the eternal way without sin; he is the eternal life without punishment.

**31.** Great mysteries, brothers. How the Spirit of God speaks with us! how he makes delights for us in this night! What is this, we ask you, brothers? whence are they the sweeter, the more obscure? He prepares for us a draught toward his love, by certain wondrous ways. He makes his very sayings wondrous, so that, although we said the things you already knew, yet, because they were dug out of those places which seemed obscure, the knowledge itself became as it were new. Did you not know, brothers, that in the Church of God the evil must be borne, and schisms must not be made? Did you not already know that within those nets which catch good and bad fish, one must endure to the shore, nor must the nets be broken[^120]; because on the shore the good will be separated for the vessels, and the evil cast out? You knew these things already; but these verses of this psalm you did not understand: what you did not understand has been expounded, and what you knew has been renewed.
