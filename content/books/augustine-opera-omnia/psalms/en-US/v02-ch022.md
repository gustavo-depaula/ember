# Exposition of Psalm 49

**1.** [v. 1] How much the word of God avails us for the correction of our life, and for the hoping of his rewards and the dreading of his punishments, let each one measure in himself; and let him set his conscience before his eyes without deceit, nor flatter himself in so great peril; since the Lord our God himself, you see, flatters no one: and even if he consoles us by promising his good things, and confirming our hope; yet to those living badly, and despising his word, he in no way spares. Let each one question himself while there is time, and see where he is, and either persevere in good, or be changed from evil. For as he says in this psalm, not any man, or any angel, but *the God of gods, the Lord, has spoken*[^1]. But by speaking what did he do? *He called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting.* He who called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting, is our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. The Word made flesh, that it might dwell in us. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, is the God of gods; because through him all things were made, and without him was made nothing. The Word of God, if he is God, surely is the God of gods: but whether he is God, the Gospel answers: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^2]. And if all things were made through him, as it consequently says, even the gods, if any were made, were made through him. For there is one God not made, and truly God he alone. But he alone God, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God.

**2.** The gods, then, whose God is the true God, who are they, or where are they? Another psalm says: *God stood in the synagogue of gods; and in the midst he discerns the gods.* We do not yet know whether perhaps some gods are gathered in heaven, and in their congregation, for this is in the synagogue, God stood to discern them. See in the same psalm to whom he says: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High: but you shall die like men, and fall like one of the princes*[^3]. It is manifest, then, that he called men gods, deified by his grace, not born of his substance. For he justifies, who is just through himself, not from another; and he deifies, who is God through himself, not by participation in another. But he who justifies, he himself deifies, because by justifying he makes sons of God. *For he gave them power to become sons of God*[^4]. If we have been made sons of God, we have also been made gods: but this is of the grace of the one adopting, not of the nature of the one begetting. For the only-begotten Son of God, God and with the Father one God, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, in the beginning the Word and the Word with God, the Word God. The rest who are made gods, are made by his grace, are not born of his substance so as to be what he is, but that by his benefit they may come to him, and be coheirs of Christ. For so great is the love in that heir, that he wished to have coheirs. What greedy man would wish this, to have coheirs? But even he who is found to wish it, will divide the inheritance with them, himself having less by dividing than if he possessed it alone: but the inheritance in which we are coheirs of Christ, is not diminished by the abundance of possessors, nor made narrower by the number of coheirs; but is as great for the many as for the few, as great for individuals as for all. *See,* says the Apostle, *what love God has given us, that we be called sons of God and be so.* And in another place: *Beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be.* Therefore we are in hope, not yet in reality. *But we know,* he says, *that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^5]. The Only-begotten like by being born, we like by seeing. For we are not so like as he, who is what he is from whom he was begotten: for we are like, not equal; he, because he is equal, therefore is like. We have heard who are the gods made, justified, because they are called sons of God; and the gods who are not gods, to whom that God of gods is terrible. For another psalm says: *He is terrible above all gods.* And as though you should ask: What gods? *Because all the gods of the nations are demons*[^6]. To the gods of the nations, the demons, terrible; to the gods made by him, the sons, lovable. Accordingly I find both confessing the majesty of God, both the demons confessed Christ, and the faithful confessed Christ. *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,* said Peter. *We know who you are: you are the Son of God,* said the demons[^7]. I hear an equal confession, but I do not find an equal love; rather indeed here love, there fear. To whom, then, he is lovable, they are sons; to whom he is terrible, they are not sons: to whom he is lovable, he made them gods; to whom he is terrible, he convicts them of not being gods. For these become gods, those are thought gods: these the truth makes gods, those error reckons.

**3.** *The God of gods, the Lord, then, has spoken*[^8]. He has spoken in many ways. Through angels he himself spoke, through the prophets he himself spoke, through his own mouth he himself spoke, through his apostles he himself spoke, through his faithful he himself speaks, through our humility, when we say anything true, he himself speaks. See, then, by speaking in manifold ways, in many ways, through many vessels, through many instruments, he himself yet sounds everywhere, by touching, modulating, inspiring: see what he did. *He spoke,* for, *and called the earth.* What earth? Or perhaps Africa? on account of those who say: The Church of Christ is the party of Donatus. He did not indeed call Africa alone, but neither did he separate Africa. For he who *called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting,* leaving no parts which he did not call, found Africa in his calling. Let it rejoice, then, in unity, let it not grow proud in division. We rightly say that the voice of the God of gods came even into Africa, did not remain in Africa. For *he called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting:* there is nowhere the snares of heretics may lie hidden, they have no shadow of falsehood in which to hide themselves: for there is no one who hides himself from his heat[^9]. He who called the earth, called the whole earth too: he who called the earth, called as much as he fashioned. Why do false christs and false prophets rise up against me? what is it that they strive to ensnare me with crafty words, saying: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there he is*[^10]? I do not hear those showing parts: the God of gods shows me the whole: he who *called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting,* redeemed the whole; but the parts which slander he condemned.

**4.** [v. 2] But we have heard, the earth was called from the rising of the sun even to its setting: from where did he begin to call who called? Hear this too: *From Zion is the appearance of his beauty*[^11]. Surely the psalm agrees with the Gospel saying: *Through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem.* Hear: *Through all the nations: He called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting.* Hear: *Beginning from Jerusalem: From Zion is the appearance of his beauty.* Therefore, *He called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting,* agrees with the words of the Lord saying: *It behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day, and that penance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations*[^12]. For all the nations are from the rising of the sun even to its setting. But that *From Zion is the appearance of his beauty,* that from there began the beauty of his Gospel, that from there began to be announced him beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, agrees with the words of the Lord saying: *Beginning from Jerusalem.* The new agree with the old, the old with the new: the two seraphim say to one another: *Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God of hosts*[^13]. And the two Testaments accord, and the two Testaments have one voice: let the voice of the according Testaments be heard, not of the slandering disinherited. God did these things, then, the God of gods: *He called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting,* his beauty proceeding from Zion. For there were the disciples, who received the Holy Spirit sent from heaven on the fiftieth day after his resurrection. Thence the Gospel, thence the preaching, thence the world of the earth was filled, and this in the grace of faith.

**5.** For when the Lord himself had come, because he came to suffer, he came hidden: and although he was strong in himself, he appeared weak in the flesh. For it behooved him to be seen, that he might not be understood; to be despised, that he might be killed. There was an appearance of glory in the divinity; but this lay hidden in the flesh. For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory[^14]. So, then, among the Jews, among enemies he walked hidden, doing wonders, suffering evils, until he was hung on the wood; and the Jews, seeing him hanging, despised him more and more, and before the cross wagging their heads, said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^15]. The God of gods, then, was hidden, and uttered voices more from compassion for us, than from his majesty. For whence those voices, except taken from us: *God, my God, why have you forsaken me*[^16]? But when did the Father forsake the Son, or the Son the Father? Is not the Son and the Father one God? is not this most true: *I and the Father are one*[^17]? Whence, then: *God, my God, why have you forsaken me,* except because in the flesh of weakness was recognized the voice of the sinner? For he who took up the likeness of the flesh of sin, why should he not take up the likeness of the voice of sin? Hidden, then, the God of gods both when he walked among men, and when he hungered and thirsted, and when, wearied, he sat, and when, his body tired, he slept, and when seized, and when scourged, and when set before the judge, and when he answered the proud one: *You would have no power over me, unless it had been given from above*[^18]: and that, led to the victim, before his shearer he opened not his mouth, and that crucified, and that buried, always hidden the God of gods. What after he rose again? The disciples marveled and at first did not believe, until they touched and handled. But the flesh had risen, because the flesh had died: the divinity which could not die, still even in the flesh of the one rising lay hidden. The form could be seen, the members held, the scars handled: the Word through whom all things were made, who sees? who holds? who handles? And yet the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. And Thomas, who held the man, understood, as he could, God. For having handled the scars, he cried out: *My Lord and my God*[^19]. Yet the Lord showed that form, and that flesh which they had seen on the cross, which had been set in the burial. He spent with them forty days. To the ungodly Jews he did not demonstrate himself: he demonstrated himself to those who had believed in him before he was crucified; that those whom, crucified, he had left tottering, rising he might make strong. Then on the fortieth day, commending his Church, that is, the earth called from the rising of the sun even to its setting, lest those have an excuse who wish to perish in schism, he ascended into heaven, saying to them: *You shall be witnesses to me in Jerusalem,* (whence is *the appearance of his beauty*) *and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the whole earth.* For when these things were said, a cloud received him. They were beholding him whom they knew: yet they knew him in humility, not yet in brightness. And when he was going from them into heaven, they were admonished by the angelic voice saying: *Why do you stand, men of Galilee? This Jesus whom you see going, so shall he come as you saw him going into heaven*[^20]. He ascended, then: they returned rejoicing, and remained in the city, according to his precept, until they were filled with the Holy Spirit. But what had been said to Thomas handling him? *Because you have seen, you have believed; blessed are those who do not see, and believe*[^21]. We were foretold. That earth called from the rising of the sun even to its setting does not see, and believes. The God of gods, then, was hidden, both to those among whom he walked, and to those by whom he was crucified, and to those to whose eyes he rose again, and to us who believe in him sitting in heaven, whom we did not see walking on earth. But even if we saw him, would we not see this which the Jews saw and crucified? It is more that not seeing Christ we believe him God, than that those seeing thought him only a man. For they, by thinking badly, put him to death, we by believing well are made alive.

**6.** [v. 3] What then, brothers? That God of gods, both then hidden, and now hidden, is he always hidden? Plainly not: hear the following: *God shall come manifest*[^22]. He who came hidden, shall come manifest: he came hidden to be judged, he shall come manifest to judge; he came hidden to stand before the judge, he shall come manifest to judge even the judges: *He shall come manifest, and shall not be silent.* For what? does he now keep silence? And whence are the things we say? whence these precepts? whence these warnings? whence this trumpet of terror? He is not silent, and is silent: he is not silent from warning, he is silent from avenging; he is not silent from precept, he is silent from judgment. For he endures sinners daily doing evil things, not caring for God, neither in their conscience, nor in heaven, nor on earth: all these things are not hidden from him, and universally he warns all, and when he scourges some on earth, it is a warning, not yet a damnation. He is silent, then, from judgment, hidden in heaven, still he intercedes for us: he is patient over sinners, not exercising wrath, but awaiting penance. He says in another place: *I have kept silence; shall I always keep silence?*[^23] When, then, he will not keep silence: *God shall come manifest.* What God? *Our God.* And he is God, who is our God: for he is not God, who is not our God. For the gods of the nations are demons: the God of Christians, the true God. He himself shall come, but manifest, not now to be mocked, not now to be buffeted and scourged; he shall come, but manifest, not now to be struck on the head with a reed, not now to be crucified, killed, buried: for all these things the hidden God willed to suffer. *He shall come manifest, and shall not be silent.*

**7.** But that he shall come to judgment, the following teach. Fire shall go before him. Do we fear? Let us be changed, and we shall not fear. Let the chaff fear the fire: what does it do to gold? But now it is in your power what you do, that you not experience uncorrected that which is to come even though you are unwilling. For if we could bring it about, brothers, that the day of judgment not come, I think that not even so should one live badly. If the fire did not come on the day of judgment, and only separation from the face of God threatened sinners, in whatever affluence of delights they were, not seeing him by whom they were created, and separated from that sweetness of his ineffable countenance, in whatever eternity and impunity of sin, they ought to bewail themselves. But what shall I say, or to whom shall I say it? This is a punishment to those who love, not to those who despise. Those who have begun somehow to perceive the sweetness of wisdom and truth, know what I say, how great a punishment it is to be separated only from the face of God: but those who have not tasted that sweetness, if they do not yet desire the face of God, let them fear at least the fire; let punishments terrify whom rewards do not invite. The sweetness of presence is cheap to you, tremble at what he threatens. The sweetness of his presence will come; you are not changed, you are not roused, you do not sigh, you do not desire it: you embrace your sins and the delights of your flesh, you heap stubble upon yourself, the fire will come. *Fire shall burn in his sight.* That fire will not be like your hearth; yet if you were compelled to put your hand into it, you would do whatever he wished who threatens this. If he should say to you: Write against the life of your father, write against the lives of your sons; for if you do not do it, I put your hand into your hearth: you would do it, lest your hand burn, lest your member burn for a time, not always to be in pain. The enemy, then, threatens so light an evil, and you do evil: God threatens an eternal evil, and you do not do good! To do evil not even threats ought to compel you: from doing good not even threats ought to deter you. But by the threats of God, by the threats of eternal fire you are forbidden from evil, you are invited to good. Whence does it irk you, except because you do not believe? Let each one, then, shake out his heart, and see what faith holds there. If we believe that judgment is to come, brothers, let us live well. The time of mercy is now, the time of judgment will be then. No one will say: Recall me to former years. He will repent even then, but in vain he will repent: let him now repent, while there is fruit of repenting; let there now be applied to the roots of the tree the basket of dung, the mourning of the heart and of tears, lest he come and root it up. For when he has rooted it up, now the fire is awaited. Now even if the branches are broken, they can again be grafted in: then every tree which does not make good fruit shall be cut down, and cast into the fire[^24]. *Fire shall burn in his sight.*

**8.** *And round about him a mighty tempest*[^25]. A mighty tempest, about to winnow so great a threshing-floor. By this tempest will be that winnowing, by which there shall be separated from the saints everything unclean, from the faithful every dissimulation, from the godly and those trembling at the word of God every despiser and proud one. For now a certain mixture lies from the rising of the sun even to its setting. Let us see, then, how he will do who is to come, by that tempest what he will do, which will be *round about him a mighty tempest.* Without doubt this tempest is about to make a certain separation. This is that separation, which those did not await who, before they came to the shore, broke the nets. But in that separation there comes about a certain distinction of bad and good. For some are now following Christ, with shoulders unencumbered, without the burden of worldly cares, who not in vain heard: *If you wish to be perfect, go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor, and you shall have treasure in heaven, and come, follow me*: to such it is said: *You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^26]. Others, then, will be judging with the Lord: but others judged, but to be set at the right. For that there will be certain ones judging with the Lord, we have most open testimony which I just now recalled: *You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.*

**9.** But someone says: there twelve apostles will sit, no more. Where, then, will the apostle Paul be? Will he be separated from there? Far be it that we say this, far be it that we even silently think it. What, then, if he himself will sit in the place of Judas? But the divine Scripture has made manifest who was ordained in the place of Judas: for Matthias is expressly named in the Acts of the Apostles, so that we could not doubt about him. Judas, then, falling, the number of twelve was filled. When, therefore, that number of twelve has occupied the twelve thrones, will the apostle Paul not judge? Or will he perhaps judge standing? It is not so: that rewarder of justice will not do this: he will not at all judge standing who labored more than all of them. Surely this one apostle Paul compels us to think and search more diligently why twelve thrones were said. For we find other numbers too in the Scriptures signifying a multitude. Five virgins are admitted, five are shut out. Understand virgins wherever: whether in the chastity and integrity of heart, where the whole Church ought to be a virgin, to which it is said: *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ*[^27]; or in those women who have even the integrity of the flesh dedicated to God: are there among so many thousands only five? But in the number five is understood the continence of the five senses of the flesh. For to many corruption comes through the eyes, to many through the ear, to many through unlawful smelling, to many through wicked tasting, to many through adulterous embrace: from all these five doors of corruption whoever restrain themselves, and so restrain themselves that in their conscience they have glory, and do not expect praise from men, are the five wise virgins, having oil with them. What is, having oil with them? This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience. Again, that man who was tormented among the dead: *I have,* he says, *five brothers*[^28]. There is understood the people of the Jews set under the law: because Moses the lawgiver wrote five books. Likewise the Lord after the resurrection bids the nets be cast into the right part, and a hundred fifty-three fish are lifted. And though they were so great, says the evangelist, the nets were not broken. For before the passion he bade the nets be cast, not saying into the right part nor into the left: because if he said into the right, he would signify the good alone; if into the left, the evil alone: but where right and left are passed over in silence, good and bad are caught commingled. And there were caught then, as the truth of the Gospel attests, so many that the nets were torn. For that catch signified this time: the torn nets signified the divisions and tearings of heretics and schismatics. But what the Lord did after his resurrection, he signified would be for us after our resurrection, in that number of the kingdom of heaven, where there will be no bad one. Therefore the nets which were cast into the right part expressed the right-hand ones, the left-hand ones being removed. Yet will in those right-hand ones only a hundred fifty-three be just? Scripture signifies thousands of thousands. Read the Apocalypse: twelve times twelve thousand perhaps, as it is there understood, are to be from the people of the Jews alone. Attend to the multitude of the martyrs: that one alone nearby which is called the White Mass has more than a hundred fifty-three martyrs. Finally those seven thousand of which it is answered to Elijah: *I have left for myself seven thousand men, who have not bent the knee before Baal*[^29], far exceed this number of fish. A hundred fifty-three fish, then, signifies not so great a number of the saints; but Scripture signifies the whole number of the saints and the just by so great a number for a sure reason, that all who pertain to the resurrection of eternal life may be understood in those hundred fifty-three. For the Law has ten precepts: but the Spirit of grace, through which alone the Law is fulfilled, is read as sevenfold. The number is to be examined, then, what ten and seven mean: ten in the precepts, seven in the grace of the Holy Spirit; through which grace the precepts are fulfilled. Ten and seven, then, contain all who pertain to the resurrection, to the right, to the kingdom of heaven, to eternal life; that is, those fulfilling the Law through the grace of the Spirit, not as if through their own work or their own merit. But ten and seven, if you count from one even to seventeen, adding all the numbers by degrees, so that to one you add two, you add three, you add four, that they become ten, by adding five that they become fifteen, by adding six that they become twenty-one, by adding seven that they become twenty-eight, by adding eight that they become thirty-six, by adding nine that they become forty-five, by adding ten that they become fifty-five, by adding eleven that they become sixty-six, by adding twelve that they become seventy-eight, by adding thirteen that they become ninety-one, by adding fourteen that they become a hundred five, by adding fifteen that they become a hundred twenty, by adding sixteen that they become a hundred thirty-six, by adding seventeen, there are made a hundred fifty-three: you will find a vast number of all the saints to pertain to this number of a few fish. As, then, in the five virgins are innumerable virgins, as in the five brothers of him who was tormented among the dead are thousands of the people of the Jews, as in the number of a hundred fifty-three fish are thousands of thousands of saints: so in the twelve thrones there are not twelve men, but a great number of the perfect.

**10.** But I see what is consequently asked of us: As of the five virgins the reason was rendered why to the five many pertain, and why to those five the many Jews, and why to a hundred fifty-three the many perfect, show why and how to the twelve thrones, not twelve men, but many pertain. What do the twelve thrones mean, which signify all from everywhere who could be so perfect, as it was said to the perfect: *You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^30]? And why do all from everywhere pertain to the number twelve? Because the very "everywhere" which we say, we say of the whole world: but the world of the earth is contained by four designated parts, east, west, south, and north: from all these parts those called in the Trinity, and perfected in the faith and precept of the Trinity, since three times four make twelve, you recognize why the saints pertain to the whole world, who will sit upon the twelve thrones to judge the twelve tribes of Israel; because the twelve tribes of Israel too are the twelve tribes of the whole Israel. For as they are to judge from the whole world, so they are to be judged from the whole world. The Apostle Paul, when he rebuked the faithful laity, because they did not bring their suits to the Church, but dragged those with whom they had business to the public court, says: *Do you not know that we shall judge angels?*[^31] See how he made himself a judge; not only himself, but also all who judge rightly in the Church.

**11.** Since, then, it is manifest that many are to judge with the Lord, but others, not however equally, but according to their merits, are to be judged; he will come with all his angels, when before him all the nations shall be gathered, and among all the angels are to be reckoned those who shall have been so perfect that, sitting upon twelve thrones, they judge the twelve tribes of Israel. For men are called angels: the Apostle says of himself: *You received me as an angel of God*[^32]. Of John the Baptist it is said: *Behold, I send my angel before your face, who shall prepare your way before you*[^33]. Therefore coming with all his angels, he will have together with himself the saints too. For Isaiah too says openly: *He will come to judgment with the elders of the people*[^34]. These elders of the people, then, these now named angels, these thousands of many perfect ones coming from the whole world, are called heaven. But that other, the earth, but a fruitful one. What fruitful earth? To be set at the right, to which it will be said: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*: truly a fruitful earth, over which the Apostle rejoices, when they sent to him for his needs: *Not that I seek the gift,* he says, *but I require the fruit.* And he gives thanks, saying: *Because at length you have flourished again to be wise for me*[^35]. *You have flourished again* he says as to trees, which by a certain barrenness had dried up. The Lord, then, coming to judgment, that we may now hear the psalm, brothers, what will he do? *He will call the heaven above*: the heaven, all the perfect saints who are to judge; he will call them above, those sitting with him to judge the twelve tribes of Israel. For how will *He call the heaven above,* when the heaven is always above? But those whom he here calls heaven, the same he elsewhere calls the heavens. What heavens? Which declare the glory of God: *For the heavens declare the glory of God*: of whom it is said: *Into all the earth their sound went out, and to the ends of the world of the earth their words*[^36]. For see the Lord discerning in judgment: *He will call the heaven above, and the earth, to discern his people.* From whom, except from the bad? Of whom afterward here there is no mention, as though already judged unto punishment. See these good ones, and distinguish. *He will call the heaven above, and the earth, to discern his people.* He calls the earth too, yet not to be commingled, but to be discerned. For first he called them commingled, when *the God of gods spoke, and called the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting;* he had not yet discerned: those servants had been sent to invite to the nuptials, who had gathered the good and the bad. But when the God of gods *shall come manifest, and shall not be silent,* so *he will call the heaven above,* that it may judge with him. For what is heaven, are the heavens; as what is earth, are the earths; as what is the Church, are the churches. *He will call the heaven above, and the earth, to discern his people.* Now with the heaven he discerns the earth, that is, the heaven with him discerns the earth. How does he discern the earth? So as to set some at the right, others at the left. But to the discerned earth what does he say? *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat,* and the rest. But they: *When did we see you,* they say, *hungry?* And he: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^37]. The heaven shows the earth his least ones now called above, and exalted from humility: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me. He will call,* then, *the heaven above, and the earth, to discern his people.*

**12.** [v. 5] *Gather to him his just ones*[^38]. The divine and prophetic voice, seeing future things as present, exhorts the gathering angels. For he will send his angels, and before him shall be gathered all the nations. *Gather to him his just ones.* What just ones, except those living from faith, doing the works of mercy? For those works are works of justice. You have the Gospel: *Beware of doing your justice before men, to be seen by them.* And as though it were asked: What justice? *When you do alms,* he says. Therefore he signified that alms are works of justice[^39]. Gather his very just ones: gather those who have compassion on the needy, who have understood concerning the needy and poor: gather those, let the Lord preserve them, and give them life. *Gather to him his just ones: who arrange his testament over sacrifices*: that is, who think of his promises over those things which they do. For these are the sacrifices, God saying: *I desire mercy more than sacrifice*[^40]. *Who arrange his testament over sacrifices.*

**13.** [v. 6] *And the heavens shall announce his justice*[^41]. Truly this justice of God the heavens have announced to us, the evangelists foretold. Through them we have heard that there will be certain ones at the right, to whom the head of the household says: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive.* Receive what? *The kingdom.* For what thing? *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat.* What so cheap, what so earthly, as to break bread to the hungry? At so great a price is the kingdom of heaven. Break your bread to the hungry, and bring the needy without a roof into your house; if you see one naked, clothe him. But you have not the means of breaking bread, you have no house into which to bring him, you have no garment with which to cover him: give a cup of cold water, cast two mites into the treasury. The widow bought as much with two mites, as Peter bought leaving his nets, as Zacchaeus bought giving half his patrimony. It is worth as much as you have. *The heavens shall announce his justice, because God is judge.* Truly a judge, not commingling, but discerning. For the Lord knows who are his. Even if the grains lie hidden in the chaff, they are known to the farmer. Let no one fear to be a grain even among the chaff; the eyes of our winnower are not deceived. Do not fear lest that tempest which will be round about him commingle you with the chaff. Surely the tempest will be mighty; yet it will take no grain from the part of the wheat to the chaff: because not any rustic with a fork, but God the Trinity is judge. *And the heavens shall announce his justice: because God is judge.* Let the heavens go, let the heavens announce, into all the earth let their sound go out, and to the ends of the world of the earth their words: and let that body say: *From the ends of the earth I cried to you, when my heart was in anguish*[^42]. For now, commingled, it groans, discerned it will rejoice. Let it cry, then, and say: *Do not destroy my soul together with the ungodly, and my life with the men of blood*[^43]. He does not destroy it together, because God is judge. Let it cry to him, and say: *Judge me, O Lord, and discern my cause from the unholy nation*[^44]: let it say, he will do it: his just ones will be gathered to him. He called the earth, that he may discern his people.

**14.** [v. 7] *Hear, my people, and I will speak to you*[^45]. He who will come and will not be silent, see that even now, if you hear, he is not silent: *Hear, my people, and I will speak to you.* For if you do not hear, I will not speak to you. *Hear and I will speak to you.* For if you do not hear, even if I speak, it will not be to you. When, then, shall I speak to you? If you hear. When do you hear? If you are my people. For *Hear, my people*: you do not hear, if you are an alien people. *Hear, my people, and I will speak to you; O Israel, and I will testify to you.* O Israel, hear; my people, hear. Israel is a name of election: *You shall not be called,* he says, *Jacob, but you shall be called Israel*[^46]. Therefore hear as Israel, as one seeing God; even if not yet by sight, yet now by faith. For this Israel is interpreted, One seeing God. He who has ears to hear, let him hear; and he who has eyes to see, let him see. *Hear, O Israel, and I will testify to you.* What above he said, *my people;* this in what follows, *O Israel*: and what he said above, *I will speak to you;* this in what follows, *I will testify to you.* What will the Lord our God speak to his people? To his Israel what will he testify? Let us hear: *God, your God, I am.* God I am, and your God I am. How *God I am*? As it was said to Moses: *I am who am*[^47]. How *your God I am*? I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. I am God, and your God I am: and even if I am not your God, I am God. To my good I am God, to your harm I am not your God. For *your God* is properly said to him whom God has more familiarly, as in his bondservant, as in his peculium. *God, your God, I am.* What do you wish more? You seek a reward from God, that God may give you something, that what he has given you may be yours? Behold, the very God who will give, is yours. What is richer than he? You were seeking gifts, you have the giver himself. *God, your God, I am.*

**15.** [v. 8] What he seeks from man, let us see. Our God, our emperor and king, what tribute does he impose on us; since he willed to be our king, and willed us to be his province? Let us hear his impositions. Let not the poor man tremble under the imposition of God: what God imposes to be given him, he himself who imposes first grants: only be you devout. God does not exact what he did not give, and he gave to all what he exacts. For what does he exact? Let us now hear: *Not over your sacrifices will I rebuke you*[^48]. I will not say to you: Why have you not slain me a fat bull? why have you not chosen from your flock the best goat? why does that ram walk among your sheep, and is not set on my altar? I will not say: Inspect your fields and your barnyard, and your walls, seeking what to give me. *Not over your sacrifices will I rebuke you.* What then? Do you not accept my sacrifices? *But your holocausts are in my sight always.* Certain holocausts, of which in another psalm it is said: *If you had wished sacrifice, I would surely have given it; you will not be delighted with holocausts*: and again he turns himself: *A sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit; a contrite and humbled heart God does not spurn*[^49]. What, then, are the holocausts which he does not spurn? Which holocausts are in his sight always? *Deal kindly, O Lord, in your good will with Zion, and let the walls of Jerusalem be built: then you shall accept the sacrifice of justice, oblations and holocausts*[^50]. He says God will accept certain holocausts. But what is a holocaust? Wholly consumed by fire: *kausis* is burning, *holon* is whole: a holocaust, then, is wholly consumed by fire. There is a certain fire of most flagrant love: let the mind be inflamed with love, let that same love seize the members for its use, let it not permit them to serve cupidity, that he may be wholly burned with the fire of divine love who wishes to offer God a holocaust. Such *holocausts of yours are in my sight always.*

**16.** [v. 9] Still this Israel perhaps does not understand what holocausts of his he has in his sight always, and still thinks of oxen, of sheep, of goats: let him not think; *I will not receive from your house calves.* I named holocausts; now in mind and thought you were running to earthly flocks, from there you were choosing me something fat: *I will not receive from your house calves.* He foretells the New Testament, where all those old sacrifices have ceased. For they were then foretelling a certain future sacrifice, by whose blood we are cleansed. *I will not receive from your house calves, nor from your flocks goats.*

**17.** [v. 10] *For mine are all the beasts of the forest*[^51]. Why should I seek from you what I myself founded? Is this more yours, to whom I gave it to possess, than mine who made it? *For mine are all the beasts of the forest.* But perhaps that Israel says: The beasts are God's, those wild beasts which I do not shut up in my barnyard, which I do not tie to my manger: but that ox and sheep and goat, these are mine. *The cattle on the mountains and the oxen.* Mine are those which you do not possess, mine are these which you possess. For if you are my servant, your whole peculium is mine. For it is not the peculium of the Lord which the servant has procured for himself, and it will not be the peculium of the Lord which the Lord himself created for the servant. Therefore mine are the beasts of the forest which you did not catch; mine too are the cattle on the mountains which are yours, and the oxen which are at your manger: all things are mine, because I created them.

**18.** [v. 11] *I have known all the birds of the air*[^52]. How did he know them? He weighed, he numbered. Who of us knows all the birds of the air? But even if God should give someone the knowledge of all the birds of the air, he himself does not so know, as he gives a man to know. The knowledge of God is one thing, of man another: as the possession of God is one thing, of man another; that is, the possessing of God is one thing, the possessing of man another. For you do not have in your power the whole of what you possess, nor is it in your power how long your ox may live, or that it not perish, or that it not graze. With whom is the highest power, with him too is the highest and secret knowledge. Let us attribute this to God, praising God. Let us not dare to say: How does God know? Lest perhaps you await this of me, brothers, that I unfold to you how God may know; this only I say: he does not know as a man, he does not know as an angel; and how he knows, I do not dare to say, since I cannot even know it. Yet one thing I know, that even before all the birds of the air were, God knew what he was to create. What is that knowledge? O man, you began to see the birds, after you were formed, after you received the sense of seeing. These birds were born of water at the word of God, saying: *Let the waters bring forth the flying things*[^53]. Where did God know what he commanded the water to bring forth? Surely he already knew what he had created, and before he created he knew it. So great, then, is the knowledge of God, that with him in a certain ineffable way they were before they were created: and does he await from you that he may receive what before he created it he had? *I have known all the birds of the air,* which you cannot give me. What you are about to slay for me, I have known all: not because I made it I knew it, but that I might make it. *And the beauty of the field is with me.* The beauty of the field, the abundance of all things being born on the earth, *is with me,* he says. How with him? Whether even before they were? For with him were all future things, and with him are all past things: future in such a way, that all past things are not taken from him. With him are all things by a certain knowledge of the ineffable Wisdom of God, set in the Word, and the Word itself is all things. Or is the beauty of the field in some way with him, because he himself is everywhere, and he himself said: *I fill heaven and earth*[^54]? What is not with him, of whom it is said: *If I ascend into heaven, you are there, and if I descend into hell, you are present*[^55]? With him is the whole: but it is not so with him, that he should suffer any contagion from those things which he created, or any need of them. For perhaps a column near which you stand is with you, and when you are weary, you lean on it. You need that which is with you, God does not need the field which is with him. With him the field, with him the beauty of the earth, with him the beauty of heaven, with him all the birds, because he himself is everywhere. And why are all things with him? Because even before all things were, or were created, all things were known to him.

**19.** [v. 12] Who unfolds, who expounds that which is said to him in another psalm: *For you have no need of my goods*[^56]? He said that he needs nothing necessary from us. *If I shall be hungry, I will not tell you*[^57]. He will not hunger, nor thirst, nor labor, nor sleep who guards Israel. But behold, according to your carnality I speak: because you, when you have not eaten, suffer hunger, you perhaps think that God too hungers that he may eat. Even if he should hunger, he does not tell you: all things are before him, whence he wishes he takes what is necessary for him. These things are said to convince a little sense, not because God professed his hunger. Although on our account that God of gods deigned even to hunger. He came to hunger and to fatten, he came to thirst and to give drink, he came to be clothed with mortality and to clothe with immortality, he came poor to make rich. For he did not lose his riches by taking up our poverty, because in him are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden[^58]. *If I shall be hungry, I will not tell you, for mine is the world of the earth and its fullness.* Do not labor, then, what to give me, without labor I have what I wish.

**20.** [v. 13] Why, then, do you still think of your flocks? *Shall I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?*[^59] You have heard what he does not seek from us, who wishes to impose on us I know not what. If you were thinking of such things, now take away your thoughts from such things: do not think to offer God anything such. If you have a fat bull, kill it for the poor: let them eat the flesh of bulls, even if they will not drink the blood of goats. And when you have done this, he will impute it to you who said: *If I shall be hungry, I will not tell you;* and he will say to you: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^60]. *Shall I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?*

**21.** [v. 14] Say, then, O Lord our God, what do you impose on your people, on your Israel? *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise*[^61]. Let us too say to him: *In me, O God, are your vows, which I will render of praise to you*[^62]. I had been afraid lest you should impose something which was outside me, which I reckoned in my barnyard, and which perhaps had now been taken away by a thief. What do you impose on me? *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise.* Let me return to myself, where I may find what I may immolate: let me return to myself, in me I will find the immolation of praise: let your altar be my conscience. *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise.* We are secure, we do not go into Arabia to seek frankincense, we do not shake out the bundles of a greedy trader: God seeks from us a sacrifice of praise. Zacchaeus had this sacrifice of praise in his patrimony, the widow had it in her little purse, some poor guest had it in his cask; another had nothing either in his patrimony, or in his purse, or in his cask, he had the whole in his soul: salvation to the house of Zacchaeus; and this widow cast in more than those rich men; this one holding out a cup of cold water will not lose his reward; but also peace on earth to men of good will[^63]. *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise.* O free sacrifice, given by grace! For I did not buy this which I might offer, but you granted it: for neither would I have this. *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise.* And this immolation of the sacrifice of praise is, to give thanks to him from whom you have whatever good you have, and by whose mercy is forgiven you whatever evil of yours you have. *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise: and render to the Most High your prayers.* With this odor the Lord is delighted. *Render to the Most High your prayers.*

**22.** [v. 15] *And call upon me in the day of your tribulation: and I will rescue you, and you shall glorify me*[^64]. For you ought not to presume on your own strength, all your helps are lies. *Call upon me in the day of tribulation: I will deliver you, and you shall glorify me.* For for this I permitted the day of tribulation to be made for you: because perhaps if you were not in tribulation, you would not call upon me; but when you are in tribulation, you call upon me; when you call upon me, I will rescue you; when I rescue you, you shall glorify me, so that you no longer withdraw from me. Someone had grown numb and cold from the fervor of prayer, and said: *I found tribulation and grief, and I called upon the name of the Lord*[^65]. He found tribulation as something useful; he had rotted with the decay of his sins, he had now remained without sense, he found tribulation as a burning and a cutting. *I found,* he says, *tribulation and grief, and I called upon the name of the Lord.* And indeed, brothers, there are tribulations known to all. Behold those which abound in the human race: one, struck by loss, laments; another, struck by bereavement, mourns; another, exiled from his homeland, grieves, and longs to return, reckoning his pilgrimage intolerable; another's vineyard is hailed on, he attends to his labors, and all his toil consumed in vain. When can a man not be saddened? He suffers an enemy from a friend. What greater misery in the human race? All bewail these things, and grieve, and these are tribulations: and in all these they call upon the Lord, and they do rightly. Let them call upon God, he is able either to teach the bearing, or to heal what is borne. He knows not to let us be tempted beyond what we can bear[^66]. Let us call upon God in these tribulations too: but these tribulations find us, as it is written in another psalm: *A helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly*[^67]: there is a certain one which we ought to find. Let these tribulations find us: there is a certain tribulation which we ought to seek and find. What is this? That very felicity in this world, the affluence of temporal things: it is not indeed itself a tribulation; they are the consolations of our tribulation. Of what tribulation? Of our pilgrimage. For this very thing, that we are not yet with God, this very thing, that we are engaged amid temptations and troubles, that we cannot be without fear, is a tribulation: for that is not the security which has been promised us. He who has not found this tribulation of his pilgrimage, does not think of returning to the homeland. That is a tribulation, brothers. Surely now we do good works, when we hold out bread to the hungry, a gift to the pilgrim, and the rest: this too is a tribulation. For we find wretched ones over whom we show mercy; and the misery of the wretched makes us compassionate. How much better would you now be there, where you find no one hungry whom you may feed, where you find no pilgrim whom you may take in, no naked one whom you may clothe, no sick one whom you may visit, no one litigating whom you may reconcile? For all things there are highest, are true, are holy, are eternal. Our bread there is justice, our drink there is wisdom, our garment there is immortality, our home eternal in the heavens, our firmness immortality. Does sickness creep in? does weariness draw to sleep? No death, no quarrel: there peace, rest, joy, justice. No enemy enters, no friend slips. What rest is there! If we consider, and attend to where we are, and where he promised we should be who knows not how to lie, from his very promise we find in what tribulation we are. This tribulation no one finds, except one who seeks it. You are healthy, see whether you are wretched: for it is easy that he who is sick should feel himself wretched: when you are healthy, see whether you are wretched; because you are not yet with God. *I found tribulation and grief, and I called upon the name of the Lord. Immolate,* then, *to God a sacrifice of praise.* Praise the promiser, praise the caller, praise the exhorter, praise the helper: and understand in what tribulation you are set. Call upon him, you will be rescued, you will glorify, you will remain.

**23.** [v. 16] But see what follows, my brothers. For now someone, because God had said to him: *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise,* and had in a way imposed this tribute, was thinking to himself, and saying: I will rise daily, I will go to the Church, I will say one hymn in the morning, another in the evening, a third or fourth in my house; daily I sacrifice the sacrifice of praise, and immolate to my God. You do well indeed, if you do this: but see lest you now be secure, because you now do this, and perhaps your tongue blesses God, and your life curses God. O my people, the God of gods, the Lord, who spoke, says to you, calling the earth from the rising of the sun even to its setting, although you are still set among the tares, *immolate a sacrifice of praise to your God, and render to him your prayers*: but see lest you live badly, and sing well. Why this? *For to the sinner God says: Why do you declare my justices, and take up my testament through your mouth?*[^68] You see, brothers, with what trembling we say these things. We take up the testament of God through our mouth, and preach to you the instruction and justices of God. And what does God say to the sinner? *Why do you?* Does he, then, forbid sinful preachers? And where is that: *What they say, do, but what they do, do not do*[^69]? Where is that: *Whether in truth or by occasion Christ is announced*[^70]? But these things were said, that those who hear may not fear from whomever they hear: not that those may be secure who say good things, and do bad. Now, then, brothers, you are secure: if you hear good things, you hear God, through whomever you hear. But God was unwilling to dismiss without rebuke those who say; lest by this alone, that they say, they sleep secure for themselves in a bad life, and say to themselves: For God will not destroy us, through whose mouth he willed so great goods to be said to his people. Rather indeed hear what you say, whoever you are who say it; and you who wish to be heard, hear yourself first; and say what someone says in another psalm: *I will hear what the Lord God speaks in me, for he will speak peace to his people*[^71]. What kind of one am I, then, who do not hear what he speaks in me, and wish that others hear what he speaks through me? Let me hear first, let me hear, and especially let me hear what the Lord God speaks in me, for he will speak peace to his people. Let me hear, and chastise my body, and bring it into servitude, lest perhaps, preaching to others, I myself be found a reprobate[^72]. *Why do you declare my justices?* Why to you what does not profit you? He admonishes him to hear: not that he lay down the preaching, but that he take up obedience. But you, why *do you take up my testament through your mouth*?

**24.** [v. 17] *But you have hated instruction*[^73]. You have hated discipline. When I spare, you sing and praise; when I chastise, you murmur: as though when I spare, I am your God; and when I chastise, I am not your God. Those whom I love, I rebuke and chastise[^74]. *But you have hated instruction: and you have cast my words behind you.* The things which are said through you, you cast behind you. *And you have cast my words behind you*: where they may not be seen by you, but may burden you. *And you have cast my words behind you.*

**25.** [v. 18] *If you saw a thief, you ran with him, and with adulterers you set your portion*[^75]. Lest perhaps you should say: I did not commit theft, I did not commit adultery. What, if he who did it pleased you? Did you not by that very pleasure run with him? did you not set your portion with him who did it, by praising it? For this it is, brothers, to run with the thief, and to set your portion with the adulterer: because even if you do not do it, and you praise what is done, you are an approver of the deed; since the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and he who does iniquitous things is blessed. You do not do evil things, you praise those doing evil. For is this a small evil? *With adulterers you set your portion.*

**26.** [v. 19] *Your mouth has abounded with malice, and your tongue embraced deceit*[^76]. The ill will and deceit, brothers, of certain men he means, who through flattery, although they know the things to be evil which they hear, lest they offend those from whom they hear them, not only by not reproving, but by being silent consent. It is too little, that they do not say: You did badly; but they say: And you did well: and they know it to be evil; but their mouth abounds with malice, and their tongue embraces deceit. Deceit is a certain fraud in words, of bringing forth one thing, and feeling another. He did not say: Your tongue admitted deceit, or, perpetrated deceit; but, to show you a certain delight in the very evil deed, *embraced,* he said. It is too little that you do it; and you are delighted: you praise to the face, you mock within. You hurl headlong a man incautiously bringing forth his vices, and not knowing whether they are vices: you who know it to be a vice, do not say: Where do you rush? If you saw him walking incautiously in the dark where you knew a well to be, and were silent, what kind of one would you be? would you not be reckoned an enemy of his soul? And yet if he fell into the well, he would die not in soul, but in body. He throws himself headlong into his vices, he preaches before you his evil deeds; you know them to be evil, and you praise, and you mock within. Oh, if he is at some time converted to God, he whom you mock, and whom you were unwilling to rebuke, and he say: Let those be confounded who say to me: Aha, aha. *And your tongue embraced deceit.*

**27.** [v. 20] *Sitting, you slandered your brother*[^77]. And the very *sitting* pertains to what he said above, *embraced.* For he who, standing or passing by, does it, does not do it with pleasure: but he who sits for this, how much leisure does he seek that he may do it! *Sitting, you slandered your brother*: you did that very evil slander diligently, you did it by sitting; you wished to be occupied there, you embraced your evil, you kissed your deceit. *Sitting, you slandered your brother: and against the son of your mother you set a scandal.* Who is the *son of the mother*? is it not a brother? This, then, he wished to repeat, what he had said above, *your brother.* Or did he insinuate some distinction for us to understand? Plainly, brothers, I think it is to be distinguished. A brother slanders a brother, for example, as it were a firm one and now a teacher of some weight and learned, slanders his brother, perhaps teaching well and walking well: but there is another, weak; against him he sets a scandal by slandering this one. For when the good are slandered by those who seem to be of some weight and to be learned, the weak who do not yet know how to judge fall into scandal. Therefore that weak one is called the *son of the mother,* not yet of the father, still needing milk and clinging to the breasts. He is still carried in the bosom of mother Church, he is not able to come to the solid food of his father's table, but draws sustenance from the breast of the mother, ignorant of judging, since he is still animal and carnal. *For the spiritual man judges all things; but the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God, for it is foolishness to him*[^78]. To such ones the Apostle says: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal: as to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not able, but neither indeed are you yet able*[^79]. I was a mother to you: as it is said in another place: *I was made a little one in your midst, as a nurse cherishing her sons*[^80]. Not a nurse nursing the sons of others, but a nurse cherishing her own sons. For there are mothers who, when they have given birth, give them to nurses: those who gave birth do not cherish their own sons, because they gave them to be nursed; but those who cherish, do not cherish their own, but others'; but this one himself had given birth, himself cherished, committed to no nurse the one he had borne; for he had said: *Whom I again bring forth in birth, until Christ be formed in you*[^81]. He cherished, then, and gave milk. But there were certain as it were learned and spiritual ones who slandered Paul. *His epistles indeed,* they say, *are weighty and strong, but the presence of his body is weak, and his speech contemptible*[^82]: he himself says in his Epistle that certain slanderers of his said these things. They sat, and slandered their brother, and against that son of his mother to be nursed they set a scandal. Deservedly they caused the mother herself to bring forth in birth again. *And against the son of your mother you set a scandal.*

**28.** [v. 21] *These things you did, and I kept silence*[^83]. Therefore the Lord our God will come, and will not be silent. Now: *These things you did, and I kept silence.* What is, *I kept silence*? I held back from vengeance, I deferred my severity, I prolonged my patience for you, I awaited your repentance for a long time. *These things you did, and I kept silence.* But I, while I awaited that you should repent, according to the Apostle saying: *But you, according to the hardness of your heart, and your impenitent heart, treasure up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God*[^84]. *You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you.* It is too little that your evil deeds please you, you think they please me too. Because you do not suffer God an avenger, you wish to hold him a partner, and as it were a corrupt judge you wish to have a sharer of the booty. *You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you*: while you do not wish to be like me. *For be,* he says, *perfect, as also your Father who is in heaven, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the bad*[^85]. Him you were unwilling to imitate, who grants good things even to the bad; so that, sitting, you might slander even the good. *You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you. I will rebuke you.* When *God shall come manifest, our God, and shall not be silent, I will rebuke you.* And what shall I do to you by rebuking you? what shall I do to you? Now you do not see yourself; I will make that you see yourself. For if you saw yourself, and were displeasing to yourself, you would be pleasing to me: but because, not seeing yourself, you were pleasing to yourself, you will be displeasing both to me and to yourself; to me when you are judged; to yourself, when you burn. For what shall I do to you, he says? *I will set you before your own face.* For why do you wish to lie hidden from yourself? You are at your own back, you do not see yourself: I will make that you see yourself; what you put behind your back, I will set before your face; you will see your foulness, not to correct it, but to blush at it. Now because he says these things, brothers, is he to whom they are said to be despaired of? Was not that city of which it was said: *Three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown,* within three days fit to be converted, to pray, to lament, to merit mercy from the imminent punishment? Let those, then, who are such, hear, while it is permitted to hear even one keeping silence. For he will come, and will not be silent, and will rebuke, when there will be no place for correction. *I will set you,* he says, *before your own face.* Now, then, do you, whoever you are who are such, what God threatens to do to you. Take yourself from your back, where you do not wish to see yourself, dissembling about your deeds, and set yourself before yourself. Ascend the tribunal of your mind, be a judge to yourself, let fear torment you, let confession break forth from you, and say to your God: *Because I know my iniquity, and my sin is before me always*[^86]. What was behind you, let it be before you: lest you yourself afterward be set before yourself by God the judge, and there be nowhere to flee from yourself.

**29.** [v. 22] *Understand these things, you who forget God*[^87]. See that he cries out, and is not silent, does not spare. You had forgotten the Lord, you did not think of your evil life. Understand that you have forgotten the Lord. *Lest he ever snatch you like a lion, and there be no one to rescue.* What is, *like a lion*? Like a strong one, like a powerful one, like one whom no one can resist. To this he referred, that he said *lion.* For it is set in praise, it is set in disparagement too. The devil is called a lion. *Your adversary,* he says, *like a roaring lion goes about seeking whom he may devour*[^88]. Is it that, because that one is called a lion on account of his huge savagery, Christ is not called a lion on account of his vast strength? And where is that: *The lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered*[^89]? Let your Charity attend a little to the small thing that remains: I beseech you to shake off weariness; he will be present who has given you strength even to this hour. A little before he had said, as though imposing on us, as you heard, a certain tribute of his praise: *Immolate to God a sacrifice of praise, and render to the Most High your prayers.* But afterward: *For to the sinner God said: Why do you declare my justices, and take up my testament through your mouth?* As though he said to him: It profits you nothing that you praise: I imposed the sacrifice of praise on those who live well; for it profits them that they praise: but you, if you praise, it profits you nothing; why do you praise me? Praise is not comely in the mouth of a sinner. Afterward he concludes as if to both, and rebuking the bad who forget God he says: *Understand these things, you who forget God, lest he ever snatch you like a lion, and there be no one to rescue.*

**30.** [v. 23] *A sacrifice of praise shall glorify me*[^90]. How *shall a sacrifice of praise glorify me*? Surely the sacrifice of praise profits the bad nothing, because they take up your testament through their mouth, and do damnable things which displease your eyes. Plainly, he says, I say this even to them: *A sacrifice of praise shall glorify me.* Now you thought praise did not profit you: praise, it will profit you. For if you live badly and say good things, you do not yet praise: but again if, when you have begun to live well, you attribute to your own merits that you live well, you do not yet praise. I do not wish you to be a robber insulting the cross of the Lord: but neither do I wish you to be that one in the temple boasting his merits, and hiding his wounds. If you are iniquitous and persevering in that iniquity, I do not say to you: Praise will not profit; but: You do not praise me, I do not reckon this to be praise: again if you are as it were just (for no one is just but the humble and godly), and you walk inflated with your justice, and despise others in comparison with yourself, and exalt yourself as it were glorying in your merits, you do not praise me. Neither does he praise me who lives badly; nor does he praise me who, as if of his own, lives well. But was that Pharisee as it were of his own such, when he said: *I give you thanks, that I am not as the rest of men*? He gave thanks to God for the good he had in himself. Although, then, there is some good in you, although you now understand that the good which is is not from you, but that you have received it from God; yet in this very thing, if you exalt yourself over another not having it, you are held envious, you will not yet be my praiser. First, then, be corrected from the worst way, begin to live well: understand that you are not corrected except by the gift of God; for *by the Lord the steps of a man are directed*[^91]. When you have understood this, favor others too, that they may be what you too are: because this you too were, what they are. Favor as much as you can, and do not despair; for God is not rich only as far as you. He does not praise, then, who by living badly offends the Lord; he does not praise, who, when he has now begun to live well, thinks it of his own that he lives well, not received from God: nor does he praise, who, although he knows that what he lives well he has received from God, yet wishes God to be rich only as far as himself. So that one who said: *I give you thanks, God, that I am not as the rest of men, the unjust, robbers, adulterers, as also this Publican;* did he not there have whence to say: Grant to this Publican too what you granted me, supply to me too the things you have not yet given? But as it were satiated he belched: he did not say: *But I am needy and poor*; which that Publican said: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner.* Therefore the Publican went down justified rather than that Pharisee[^92]. Therefore hear, you who live well, hear, you who live badly: *A sacrifice of praise shall glorify me.* No one offers me this sacrifice of praise, and is bad. I do not say: Let not the bad offer me this; but: No one bad offers me this. For he who praises, is good: because if he praises, he also lives well; because if he praises, he praises not only with the tongue, but his life too agrees with the tongue.

**31.** *A sacrifice of praise shall glorify me: and there is the way, in which I will show him the salvation of God*[^93]. In the sacrifice of praise *is the way, in which I will show him the salvation of God.* What is the *salvation of God*? Christ Jesus. And how in the sacrifice of praise is Christ shown to us? Because Christ came to us with grace. This the Apostle says: *But I live now, not I, but Christ lives in me: but that I live in the flesh, I live in the faith of the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me*[^94]. Let the sinners, then, recognize that there would be no need of a physician, if they were healthy. For Christ died for the ungodly[^95]. When, therefore, they recognize their ungodliness, and first imitate that Publican saying: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*: they show their wounds, they implore the physician; and because they do not praise themselves, but reprove themselves, that he who glories may glory not in himself, but in the Lord, they recognize the cause of the coming of Christ, because he came for this, to make sinners safe: *For Jesus Christ came,* he says, *into this world to save sinners, of whom I am the first*[^96]. Accordingly those Jews, glorying in their own work, the same Apostle so rebukes, that he says that they do not pertain to grace, who thought a reward owed to their merits and works. He, then, who knows himself to pertain to grace, which is Christ, and which is Christ's, knows that he needs grace. If it is called grace, it is given freely; if it is given freely, no merits of yours preceded that it be given. For if your merits preceded, the reward is not imputed according to grace, but according to debt[^97]. If, then, you say that your merits preceded, you wish yourself to be praised, not God: therefore you do not recognize Christ, who came with the grace of God. Turn yourself, then, to your merits, see that they were evil, so that nothing was owed you but punishment, not a reward. And when you have seen what is owed you by merit, you recognize what is granted by grace; and by the sacrifice of praise you glorify God. For there is the way, in which you may know Christ the salvation of God.
