# On the Same Psalm 118 (22)

**1.** [v. 105.] We now undertake to search out and treat, according to the strength God grants, these verses of this psalm, of which the first is: *Your word is a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths.* What is *lamp* is repeated as *light;* what is *to my feet,* as *to my paths.* What, then, is *your word?* Is it that which in the beginning was God with God, the Word, namely, through whom all things were made? It is not so. For that Word is light, but is not a lamp. For a lamp is a creature, not the Creator, which is kindled by participation in the unchangeable light. This was John, of whom the Word, God, says, *He was a burning and shining lamp*[^1]. But a lamp too is light: and yet in comparison with the Word, of whom it was said, *The Word was God*[^2], he was not the light; but he was sent to bear witness to the light. For that was the true light, not which is enlightened, like a man, but which enlightens every man[^3]. But unless a lamp too were light, he would not say to the Apostles, *You are the light of the world*[^4]. And lest, hearing this, they should think themselves to be that which he who said this was—for of himself too he said in a certain place, *I am the light of the world*[^5]—he said to them about themselves: *A city cannot be hidden, set on a mountain; nor do men light a lamp and put it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house: so let your light shine before men*[^6]; that they might know themselves to be, as it were, lamps kindled from that light which shines unchangeably. For no creature, however rational and intellectual, is enlightened by itself, but is kindled by participation in the everlasting truth: even if it is sometimes called day, not the day the Lord, but the day the Lord has made; and therefore it hears, *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*[^7]. On account of which participation, inasmuch as he is man, the Mediator himself too is called a *lamp* in the Apocalypse[^8]. But this assumption is unique: for of none of the saints could it be said divinely, or is it in any way lawful to say, *The Word was made flesh*[^9], except of the one Mediator of God and men. Since, then, the only-begotten Word, equal to the Begetter, is called light, and a man too enlightened by that Word is called light, who is also called a lamp, as John, as the Apostles, and yet none of them, a man, is the Word, and that Word by which they were enlightened is not a lamp; what is this word, which is so called light that it is a lamp (for he says, *Your word is a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths*), unless we understand the word that came to the Prophets, or that was preached through the Apostles? Not Christ the Word, but the word of Christ, of which it is written: *Faith is from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ*[^10]. For the apostle Peter too, comparing the prophetic word to a lamp, says: *We have a more sure prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp shining in a dark place*[^11]. So what he here says, *Your word is a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths,* is the word that is contained in all the holy Scriptures.

**2.** [v. 106.] *I have sworn,* he says, *and have resolved to keep the judgments of your righteousness;* as one walking well to that lamp, and having straight paths. But in the following word what precedes is expounded. For as if we asked what *I have sworn* was, he added, *and have resolved.* For he called an oath what he resolved by a sacred bond; because the mind ought to be so fixed in keeping the judgments of God's righteousness that what it has resolved is altogether in place of an oath.

**3.** [v. 107.] But the judgments of God's righteousness are kept by faith; when, under God the righteous judge, neither is a right deed believed to be fruitless, nor a sin unpunished: but because for this faith the body of Christ has borne many and most grievous evils, *I have been humbled,* he says, *exceedingly.* For he does not say "I humbled myself," so that the humility which is in the precept would necessarily be understood: but he says, *I have been humbled exceedingly;* namely, having suffered the greatest persecution, for the reason that he swore and resolved to keep the judgments of God's righteousness. And lest in so great a humiliation the faith itself should fail, he added: *O Lord, give me life according to your word;* that is, according to your promise. For the word too of God's promises is a lamp to the feet, and a light to the paths. So above too, that God might give him life, he prayed in the humiliation of persecution, where he says: *They have almost made an end of me on the earth; but I have not forsaken your commandments: according to your mercy give me life, and I will keep the testimonies* (that is, the martyria) *of your mouth*[^12]. Where it is understood that, unless he himself gives life by granting patience—on account of which it was said, *In your patience you will possess your souls*[^13]; and of which it was said, *For from him is my patience*[^14]—it is not the body that is put to death in persecution, but the soul, by not keeping the martyria and the judgments of God's righteousness.

**4.** [v. 108.] *Make the freewill offerings of my mouth well-pleasing, O Lord;* that is, let them please you; do not reject, but approve. But the *freewill offerings of the mouth* are well understood as sacrifices of praise, offered by the confession of love, not by the fear of necessity: whence it is said, *I will sacrifice to you willingly*[^15]. But what is it that he adds: *And teach me your judgments?* Had he not himself said in the verses above, *From your judgments I have not turned aside?* How is this, if he did not know them? Further, if he knew them, how does he here say, *And teach me your judgments?* Is it as that is: *You have dealt sweetly with your servant;* and afterward he says, *Teach me sweetness?* Which we so expounded that we understood the words of one advancing, and asking that there be added to him beyond what he had received.

**5.** [v. 109.] *My soul is in your hands always.* Some manuscripts have *in my hands;* but more, *in yours;* and this indeed is plain. For the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God; in whose hand are both we and our words[^16]. *And of your law,* he says, *I have not been forgetful;* as though, toward not forgetting the law of God, his memory is helped by his hands, where his soul is. But *My soul in my hands,* how it is to be understood, I do not know. For these are the words of the righteous, not of the unrighteous; of one returning to the Father, not of one departing from the Father. For that younger son can seem to have wished to have his soul in his own hands, when he said to his father, *Give me my substance that falls to me*[^17]: but for that reason he was dead, for that reason he had perished. Or perhaps it was said thus, *My soul in my hands,* as though he offered it to God to be given life? Whence elsewhere it is said, *To you I have lifted up my soul*[^18]. For here too he had said above, *Give me life.*

**6.** [v. 110.] *Sinners,* he says, *have set a snare for me, and from your commandments I have not gone astray.* Whence this, except that his soul, in the hands of God, or in his own, is offered to God to be given life?

**7.** [v. 111.] *By inheritance I have acquired your testimonies for ever.* Some, wishing to say in one word what is put in one word in the Greek, rendered it *I have inherited.* Which, even if it could be Latin, would rather signify the one who gave the inheritance than the one who received it; so that *I have inherited* would be like "I have enriched." Better, then, the whole sense is conveyed in two words; whether it be said, *By inheritance I have possessed,* or, *By inheritance I have acquired:* not the inheritance, but *by inheritance.* But if it is asked what he acquired *by inheritance, Your testimonies,* he says. Wishing what to be understood, except that, that he might become a witness of God, and confess his testimonies; that is, that he might become a martyr of God, and speak his martyria, as the martyrs do, conferred on him by the Father, whose heir he is? For many wished, and could not: yet none could, except those who wished; because they could not, if they had wished to deny the testimonies of God. But even their will was prepared by the Lord[^19]. Therefore this man testifies that he acquired these by inheritance, and this *for ever;* because in them there is not the empty temporal glory of men seeking vain things, but the eternal glory of those who suffer for a brief time, and reign without end. Whence there follows: *For they are the exultation of my heart;* even if there is affliction of the body, yet exultation of the heart.

**8.** [v. 112.] Then he added: *I have inclined my heart to do your statutes for ever, on account of the reward.* He who says, *I have inclined my heart,* had himself already said, *Incline my heart to your testimonies*[^20]; that we may understand this to be at once both of the divine gift and of his own will. But are we going to do the statutes of God for ever? Those works indeed which we do concerning the needs of our neighbors cannot be eternal, just as neither can the needs themselves: but if we do not do them by loving, there is no justification; but if by loving, that love itself is eternal, and for it an eternal reward is prepared; on account of which reward he says that he inclined his heart to do the statutes of God, that, loving for ever, he may for ever deserve to have what he loves.
