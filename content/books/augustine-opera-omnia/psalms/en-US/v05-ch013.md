# Exposition of Psalm 107

**1.** The hundred and seventh psalm I did not think I should expound; since I have already expounded it in the fifty-sixth psalm, and in the fifty-ninth psalm, of whose last parts this one consists. For the last part of the fifty-sixth is the first of this, up to that verse where it is said: *And over all the earth your glory.* But from here to the end, it is the last part of the fifty-ninth: just as the last part of the hundred and thirty-fourth is the same as that of the hundred and thirteenth, from that verse where it is said: *The images of the nations are silver and gold*: just as the thirteenth and the fifty-second, some middle parts being changed, have all things the same from beginning to end. Whatever, then, in this hundred and seventh psalm is set somewhat otherwise than in those two of whose parts it consists, does not have a difficult meaning: as in the fifty-sixth it is said, *I will sing, and sing psalms; arise, my glory*; but in this one: *I will sing, and sing psalms in my glory.* For there it was said, *Arise*, that in that glory there might be singing and singing of psalms. Likewise there: *For your mercy is magnified even to the heavens*; or, as others translated, *is lifted up*: but here: *For your mercy is great above the heavens.* For it is *magnified even to the heavens* for this reason, that it may be great in the heavens; for this is what he wished to say by *above the heavens.* Likewise in the fifty-ninth: *I will be glad, and divide Shechem*: but here: *I will be exalted, and divide Shechem.* Where it is shown that what was signified about dividing Shechem was foretold as future after the exaltation of the Lord, and that gladness pertains to this exaltation; so that he is glad for this reason, because he is exalted. Whence elsewhere he says: *You have turned my mourning into joy for me; you have torn my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness*[^1]. Likewise there: *And Ephraim the strength of my head*; but here: *And Ephraim the upholding of my head.* For by upholding strength comes to be, that is, by upholding he makes them strong, fructifying in us: for Ephraim is interpreted "fructification." But upholding can be referred to both, whether when we take up Christ, or when he himself takes us up, who is the head of the Church. And what there he says, *those who trouble us*; but here, *our enemies*: certainly they are the very same.

**2.** We are admonished indeed by this psalm that those titles which are set as it were about history are most rightly so done that we understand them according to prophecy, according to which we see the psalms to have been written. For what is so diverse according to history as that which is in the title of the fifty-sixth: *Unto the end, do not corrupt; for David himself, for the inscription of a title, when he fled from the face of Saul into the cave*: and in the title of the fifty-ninth: *Unto the end, for those who will be changed, for the inscription of a title, for David himself, for instruction, when he set fire to Mesopotamia, Syria, and Syria Sobal, and Joab turned back, and struck in the valley of salt-flats twelve thousand?* For apart from what is set, *For the inscription of a title,* and, *for David himself*, and, *unto the end*; the rest are so diverse, that the one has the humility of David, the other his strength; the one a flight, the other victories. And yet from the later parts of these two, whose titles are so diverse, this psalm is composed. Where it is signified that both run together to some one thing, not on the surface of history, but in the height of prophecy, the ends of both being joined in this one, whose title is: *A song of a psalm, for David himself*; like neither of those titles, except that here too is set, *for David himself.* Since in many parts and in many ways, as the Epistle to the Hebrews says, God of old spoke to the Fathers through the Prophets; yet he spoke him whom he afterward sent, that the eloquences of the Prophets might be fulfilled: *For as many as are the promises of God, in him too*[^2].

[^1]: Ps 30:11 (29:12)
[^2]: 2 Cor 1:20
