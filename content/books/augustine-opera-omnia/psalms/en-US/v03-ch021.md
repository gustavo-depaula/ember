# Exposition of Psalm 69

**1.** [v. 2] Thanks to the grain of wheat, because it willed to die and be multiplied: thanks to the only Son of God, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who did not disdain to undergo our death, that he might make us worthy of his life. Behold, he who was single, until he passed over, as he said in the psalm: *I am single, until I pass over*[^1]: because he was so a single grain, that he had in himself a great fruitfulness of multitude; in how many grains imitating his passion do we exult, when we celebrate the birthdays of the Martyrs! Many, then, his members, bound under one head, our Savior himself, by the bond of love and peace, as you deign to know, since you have very often heard, are one man: and their voice, as of one man, is very often heard in the Psalms; and so one cries out as though all, because all in one are one. Let us hear, then, that the martyrs labored, and amid the great storms of hatreds in this age were in peril; not so much in body, which at some time they were to lay down, but in faith itself, lest, failing, and perhaps yielding to the bitter pains of persecutions, or to the love of this life, they lose what God had promised: who not only by word, but also by example had taken away all fear; by word, saying: *Do not fear those who kill the body; but the soul they cannot kill*[^2]; by example, doing what by word he commanded, so that he would not wish to avoid the hands of those beating, nor the slaps of those striking, nor the spittle of those spitting, nor the crown of thorns of those imposing it, nor the cross of those killing him: none of these did he wish to avoid, who had no need of it, for the sake of those who had need of it; making of himself a medicine for the sick. The martyrs, then, labored; and unless he were always present who says: *Behold, I am with you unto the consummation of the age*[^3], they would certainly have failed.

**2.** There is, then, in this psalm the voice of the afflicted; and therefore certainly of the martyrs in peril amid sufferings, but presuming on their head. Let us hear them, and speak with them from the affection of the heart, even if not in the likeness of suffering. For they are already crowned; we are still in peril: not because such persecutions press us as pressed them; but perhaps worse in every kind of so great scandals. For our times more abound in that *Woe* which the Lord cried: *Woe to the world from scandals*[^4]. *And because iniquity has abounded, the love of many will grow cold*[^5]. For neither did that holy Lot suffer in Sodom from anyone a bodily persecution, nor was it said to him that he should not dwell there; his persecution was the evil deeds of the Sodomites. Now, then, with Christ now sitting in heaven, now glorified, the necks of kings now subjected to his yoke, and their foreheads set under his sign; now with no one remaining who openly dares to insult Christians, yet we still groan amid instruments and choristers; still those enemies of the Martyrs, because they cannot with voices and the sword, persecute them with their luxury. And would that we grieved over pagans only! there would be some consolation, to await those not yet signed with the cross of Christ, when they may be signed, and when, bound by his authority, they cease to rage. We see even those carrying his sign on the forehead, at the same time carrying on that very forehead the impudence of luxuries, and on the days and solemnities of the martyrs not exulting, but insulting. And amid these things we groan, and this is our persecution, if there is in us the love which says: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I do not burn*[^6]? No servant of God, then, is without persecution; and that is true which the Apostle says: *But all who wish to live godly in Christ will suffer persecution*[^7]. You may see whence, you may see how; that devil is two-formed. He is a lion in attack, a dragon in lying-in-wait. Let the lion threaten, he is an enemy; let the dragon lie in wait, he is an enemy. When are we secure? Behold, let all become Christians, will the devil too be a Christian? He does not cease to tempt, then; he does not cease to lie in wait. He is bridled and bound in the hearts of the ungodly, lest he rage against the Church, and do as much as he wishes. The teeth of the ungodly gnash against the dignity of the Church and the peace of Christians, and because they have nothing to do by raging; by dancing, by blaspheming, by being luxurious, they do not strike the bodies of Christians, but tear the souls of Christians. Let us cry out, then, all with one voice these words: *O God, attend to my help.* For we have need of everlasting help in this age. But when not! Yet now especially, set in tribulation, let us say: *O God, attend to my help.*

**3.** [v. 3] *Let those who seek my soul be confounded and put to shame.*[^8] Christ says it: whether the head says it, or the body says it; he says it, who said: *Why do you persecute me*[^9]? he says it, who said: *When you did it to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^10]. The voice, then, of this man is known, of the whole man, head and body: not often to be commended, because it is known. *Let them be confounded,* he says, *and put to shame who seek my soul.* In another psalm he says: *I considered to the right, and saw, and there was no one who recognized me: flight perished from me, and there is no one who seeks out my soul*[^11]. There he says of the persecutors that there was no one who sought out his soul; but here: *Let those who seek my soul be confounded and put to shame.* There he grieved that he was not sought to be imitated; here he groaned that he was sought to be oppressed. You seek the soul of the just man, when you think to imitate him; you seek the soul of the just man, when you think to kill him. Since the soul of a just man is sought in two ways, each is expressed in separate psalms. There he grieves, because there is no one who seeks out his soul to imitate his sufferings; but here: *Let those who seek my soul be confounded and put to shame.* They seek his soul, not that they may have two. For they did not so seek his soul as a robber seeks the tunic of a traveler: he kills for this, that he may despoil, that he may have. But he who persecutes that he may kill, shuts out the soul, does not clothe himself. They seek my soul; they wish to kill me. What, then, do you wish for them? *Let them be confounded, and put to shame.* And where is what you heard from your Lord: *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute you*[^12]? Behold, you suffer persecution, and you curse those from whom you suffer it: how do you imitate the preceding sufferings of your Lord, hanging on the cross, and saying: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^13]? The martyr answers those saying such things, and says: You set before me the Lord saying: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do;* recognize my voice too, that it may be yours too. For what did I say of my enemies? *Let them be confounded, and put to shame.* Now such vengeance has been taken on the enemies of the martyrs. That Saul who persecuted Stephen was confounded and put to shame. He breathed slaughter, he sought whom he might drag off and kill: hearing the voice from above: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* he was confounded and prostrated, and raised up to obey who had been inflamed to persecute. This, then, the martyrs wish for their enemies: *Let them be confounded, and put to shame.* For as long as they are not confounded and put to shame, it is necessary that they defend their deeds; they seem glorious to themselves, because they hold, because they bind, because they beat, because they kill, because they dance, because they insult: of all these deeds let them at some time be confounded, and put to shame. For if they are confounded, they will also be converted; because they cannot be converted, unless they have been confounded, and put to shame. Let us wish these things, then, for our enemies; let us wish them secure. Behold, I have said it, and let me have said it with you: let all who still dance and sing and insult the Martyrs *be confounded, and put to shame;* at some time within these walls let them, confounded, beat their breasts.

**4.** [v. 4] *Let those who think evils for me be turned backward, and blush.*[^14] First there was the attack of those persecuting; now there remains the ill will of those thinking. Plainly there are distinct successive times of persecution in the Church. An attack was made on the Church, when kings persecuted: and because kings had been foretold both as going to persecute and as going to believe, one being fulfilled, certainly the other followed. There came about also what was consequent; the kings believed, peace was given to the Church, the Church began to be at the summit of dignity, even on this earth, even in this life: but the raging of the persecutors is not lacking; they turned their attacks to thoughts. In those thoughts, as in an abyss, the devil is bound; he rages, and does not burst out. For it was said of these times of the Church: *The sinner will see, and will be angry.* And what will he do? Surely what at first? Drag, bind, strike. He does not do this. What then? *He will gnash with his teeth, and waste away*[^15]. And at these the martyr is as it were angry; and yet for these the martyr prays. For as he well wished for those of whom he said: *Let those who seek my soul be confounded and put to shame;* so now too: *Let those who think evils for me be turned backward, and blush.* Why? That they may not go before, but follow. For he who reprehends the Christian religion, and wishes to live by his own counsel, wishes as it were to go before Christ; so that he, namely, would have erred, and been weak and feeble, who either willed to suffer among the hands of the Jews, or was able to: but he himself is wise, avoiding all these things, declining death, lying even unjustly lest he die, killing his soul that he may live in body, and seems to himself of great and prudent counsel. He goes before, reprehending Christ; he as it were goes ahead of Christ: let him believe in Christ, and follow Christ. For what was wished now for the persecutors thinking evils, the Lord himself said this to Peter. For in a certain place Peter wished to go before the Lord. For the Savior was speaking of his passion, which if he had not undertaken, we would not be saved; and Peter who a little before had confessed him the Son of God, and in that confession had been called the Rock, on which the Church would be built, a little after, when the Lord spoke of his future passion, said: *Far be it, Lord; be merciful to yourself, this shall not happen.* A little before: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar Jonah, for flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*: now suddenly: *Get behind me, Satan.* What is, *Get behind me*? Follow me. You wish to go before me, you wish to give me counsel; it is better that you follow my counsel: this is: Go back, get behind me. He restrains the one going before, that he may go back; and calls him Satan, because he wishes to go before the Lord. A little before: *Blessed*: now: *Satan.* Whence a little before: *Blessed*? *Because flesh and blood,* he says, *did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* Whence now, *Satan*? *Because you relish not,* he says, *the things that are of God, but the things that are of men*[^16]. Therefore we who wish rightly to celebrate the birthdays of the martyrs, let us desire the imitation of the martyrs; let us not wish to go before the martyrs, and to seem to ourselves of better counsel, because we avoid the sufferings for righteousness and for the faith which they did not avoid. Therefore those who think evils, and nourish their hearts in luxury, *let them be turned backward, and blush.* Let them hear from the Apostle afterward saying: *But what fruit had you once in those things of which you are now ashamed*[^17]?

**5.** What follows? *Let those be turned away at once, blushing, who say to me: Aha, aha.*[^18] There are two kinds of persecutors; of those reviling, and of those flattering. The tongue of the flatterer persecutes more than the hand of the killer; for Scripture called even that a furnace. Certainly when Scripture spoke of persecution, it said: *Like gold in the furnace he proved them,* of the martyrs killed, *and like the victim of a holocaust he received them*[^19]. Hear that the tongue of those flattering is also such: *The proving,* it says, *of silver and gold is fire; but a man is proved by the mouth of those praising him*[^20]. That is fire, this too is fire; from each you must come out safe. The reprehender broke you, you were broken in the furnace like an earthen vessel: the word formed you, and the temptation of tribulation came: that which is formed, must be baked; if it is well formed, fire comes to make it firm. Whence that one said in the passion: *My strength has dried up like a potsherd*[^21]. For the passion and the furnace of tribulation had made him stronger. Again, if you are praised by those flattering and assenting, and consent to them, as it were buying oil, not carrying it with you, like the five foolish virgins; the mouth of those praising you will also be the furnace of your breaking. But without these we cannot be; it is necessary both that we enter into these things, and come out from there: let us enter into some reviling of the evil and wicked, let us enter also into some assenting of the flatterers; but it is needful to come out from there. Let us ask him of whom it was said: *May the Lord guard your going in and your going out*[^22]; that, entering whole, you may so come out whole. For the Apostle too says: *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you are able.* Behold, you have the entrance. He did not say: Be not tempted: for he who is not tempted is not proved; and he who is not proved does not make progress. What, then, did he wish? *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you are able.* You have heard the entrance; hear also the exit: *But he will make with the temptation also an exit, that you may be able to bear it*[^23]. Therefore also let those *be turned away at once, blushing, who say to me: Aha, aha.* For why do they praise me? Let them praise God. For who am I, that I should be praised in myself? or what have I done? what have I that I have not received? *If you have received,* he says, *why do you glory as though you had not received*[^24]? *Let,* then, *those be turned away at once, blushing, who say to me: Aha, aha.* With such oil the head of the heretics is made fat, when they say: I am, I am, and it is said to them: You, Lord. They received *Aha, aha;* they followed *Aha, aha*: they were made blind leaders of the blind following. With the most open voices these things are sung to Donatus: *Aha, aha,* good leader, illustrious leader. And he did not say: *Let those be turned away at once, and blush, who say to me: Aha, aha;* nor did he wish to correct them, that they should say to Christ: Good leader, illustrious leader. But the Apostle, dreading the *Aha* of men, that he might truly be praised in Christ, was unwilling to be praised in place of Christ; and to certain saying: *I am Paul's,* he answered in the liberty of the Lord: *Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul*[^25]? Therefore let the martyrs say in the persecution even of those flattering: *Let those be turned away at once, blushing, who say to me: Aha, aha.*

**6.** [v. 5] And what comes about, when those are turned away, and all blush; whether those who seek my soul, or those who think evils for me, or those who with perverse and feigned benevolence wish with the tongue to soften what they strike; when they too have been turned away and confounded, what will come about? *Let them exult, and be glad in you*[^26]: not in me, not in that one, or that one; but in him in whom those who were darkness have been made light. *Let them exult, and be glad in you, all who seek you.* It is one thing to seek God, another to seek a man. *Let those be glad who seek you.* They will not be glad, then, who seek themselves, whom you first sought before they sought you. That sheep did not yet seek the shepherd; it had strayed from the flock: and he descended to it; he sought it, carried it back on his shoulders. Will it despise you, O sheep, who seek it, who first sought it despising you, and not seeking you? Now, then, begin to seek him, who first sought you, and carried you back on his shoulders. Do what he says: *My sheep hear my voice, and follow me*[^27]. If, then, you seek him who first sought you, and you have been made his sheep, and you hear the voice of your shepherd, and follow him; see what he shows you of himself, what of his body, lest you err in him, lest you err in the Church, lest anyone say to you: It is Christ, who is not Christ; or it is the Church, which is not the Church. For many said that Christ did not have flesh, and that Christ did not rise again in his body: do not follow their voices. Hear the voice of the shepherd himself, who was clothed with flesh, that he might seek the lost flesh. He rose again, and says: *Handle, and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^28]. He showed himself to you; follow his voice. He showed also the Church; lest anyone deceive you by the name of the Church: *It behooved,* he says, *Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^29]. You have the voice of your shepherd; do not follow the voice of strangers: and you will not fear the thief, if you have followed the voice of the shepherd. But whence do you follow? If you say to none of men, as if by his merit, *Aha, aha;* nor hear it with congratulation, lest your head grow fat with the oil of a sinner. *Let them exult, and be glad in you, all who seek you; and let them say.* What do they say who exult? *Let the Lord always be magnified.* Let all say this who exult and seek you. What? *Let the Lord always be magnified, those who love your salvation.* Not only *let the Lord be magnified,* but also *always.* Behold, you were erring, and turned away from him: he called you; *let the Lord be magnified.* Behold, he inspired in you the confession of sins: you confessed, he gave pardon; *let the Lord be magnified.* Now you have begun to live justly; now I think it is as it were just that you too be magnified. For when he called you erring, the Lord was to be magnified; when he forgave you confessing your sins, the Lord was to be magnified: now, hearing his words, you have begun to make progress, you have been justified, you have come to a certain excellence of virtue; it is worthy that you too at some time be magnified. *Let them say: Let the Lord always be magnified.* You are a sinner; *let him be magnified* that he may call: you confess; *let him be magnified* that he may pardon: now you live justly; *let him be magnified* that he may rule: you persevere unto the end; *let him be magnified* that he may glorify. *Always,* then, *let the Lord be magnified.* Let the just say this; let those say this who seek him. Whoever does not say this, does not seek him. Behold, *let the Lord be magnified. Let all exult and be glad who seek* him; *and let them say: Let the Lord always be magnified, those who love* his *salvation.* For from him is salvation to them, not from themselves. The salvation of the Lord our God is our Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ: whoever loves the Savior, confesses himself healed; whoever confesses himself healed, confesses that he was sick. *Let them say,* then: *Let the Lord always be magnified, those who love your salvation*: not their own salvation, as if they themselves save themselves; not as the salvation of a man, as though they were saved by him. *Do not,* he says, *trust in princes, and in the sons of men, in whom there is no salvation.* Why this? *Salvation is the Lord's, and over your people your blessing*[^30]. Therefore: *Let the Lord always be magnified.* Who say this? *Those who love your salvation.*

**7.** [v. 6] Behold, *let the Lord be magnified;* never you, nowhere you? In him something, in me nothing: but if in him whatever I am; it is he, not I. You, then, what? *But I am needy and poor.*[^31] He rich, he abounding, he needing no one. Behold my light, behold whence I am enlightened; since I cry out: *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, you will enlighten my darkness*[^32]. *The Lord looses those in chains; the Lord raises up those cast down; the Lord makes the wise blind; the Lord guards the proselytes*[^33]. What, then, you? *But I am needy and poor.* I as it were an orphan; my soul as it were a widow forsaken and desolate: I seek help; always I confess my weakness. *But I am needy and poor.* My sins have been forgiven me; I have now begun to follow the precepts of God: yet still *needy and poor.* Whence still *needy and poor*? Because *I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind*[^34]. Whence *needy and poor*? Because: *Blessed are those who hunger, and thirst for righteousness*[^35]. Still I hunger, still I thirst: my fullness is deferred; not taken away. *But I am needy and poor; O God, help me.* Whence it began, *O God, attend to my help; O God, help me.* Most rightly Lazarus too is said to be interpreted "Helped"; that needy and poor man, who was carried away into the bosom of Abraham: and he bears the figure of the Church of God, which ought always to confess that it has need of help. This is true, this is godly. *I said to the Lord: You are my God.* Why? *Because you have no need of my goods*[^36]. He has no need of us; we have need of him: therefore the true Lord. For you are not very much the true lord of your servant; both are men, both have need of God. But if you think your servant has need of you, to give bread; you too have need of your servant, that he help your labors: each of you needs the other. And so none of you is truly lord, and none of you is truly servant. Hear the true Lord, of whom you are the true servant: *I said to the Lord: You are my God.* Why are you Lord? *Because you have no need of my goods.* But what of you? *But I am needy and poor.* Behold, *needy and poor;* let God feed me, let God lift me up, let God help me: *O God,* he says, *help me.*

**8.** *You are my helper and my rescuer, O Lord; do not delay.*[^37] You are my helper and rescuer: I need help, help me; I am entangled, rescue me. For no one will rescue from entanglements, but you. The knots of diverse cares surround us; on this side and that we are torn as it were by thorns and hedges, we walk the narrow way; perhaps we have stuck in the hedges: let us say to God: *You are my rescuer.* He who showed the narrow way, brought it about that we should follow it. Let this voice persevere in us, brethren. However long we have lived here, however much we have made progress here, let no one say: It is enough for me; I am just. He who says it, has remained on the way, does not know how to arrive. Where he says: It is enough; there he has stuck. Attend to the Apostle, for whom it is not enough; see how he wishes to be helped, until he arrives: *Brethren,* he says, *I do not reckon myself to have laid hold*; lest those should seem to themselves to have laid hold, to whom again he says: *He who thinks he knows something, does not yet know as he ought to know*[^38]. What, then, does he say? *Brethren, I do not reckon myself to have laid hold.* Above he had said: *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect*; and there it follows: *Brethren, I do not reckon myself to have laid hold.* If he has not yet received, he is *needy and poor*: if he is not yet perfect, he is *needy and poor.* Well does he say: *O God, help me.* But he understands something, and understands more loftily. See yet what he says: *But to him who is able to do above all things more abundantly than we ask and understand*[^39]. See, then, that he has not yet arrived, has not yet laid hold. What, then, does he say? *Brethren, I do not reckon myself to have laid hold: but one thing, forgetting the things behind, stretching forth to the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the palm of the supernal calling*[^40]. He, then, runs, you have stuck. He says he is not yet perfect; and you already glory of perfection! Let those be confounded who say to you: *Aha, aha.* Let you too be confounded among them, because you say to yourself: *Aha, aha.* For he who praises himself, says to himself: *Aha, aha*: he who is praised by others and consents, does not carry oil with him; the torches fail, and he is going to shut the door.

**9.** This, then, dearly beloved, this psalm has taught us briefly, from the admonition of the solemnity of the martyrs, that we may understand that the martyrs suffered here bodily tribulation: we, in however great peace we are, must suffer spiritual tribulation; and it is necessary that amid the scandals, and tares, and chaff the Church and that mass groan, until the harvest comes, until the winnowing-fan, until the last winnowing comes, that the chaff may be separated from the grain, until it be brought to the barn. Which until it comes about, let us cry out: *But I am needy and poor; O God, help me: you are my helper, O Lord; do not delay.* What is, *do not delay*? Because many say: It is long until Christ comes. What then, because we say: *Do not delay*? is he going to come before he appointed to come? What does this vow mean: *Do not delay*? Let not that which you are going to do seem too late to me. For to you it seems long; not to God does it seem long, to whom a thousand years is one day, or three watches of the hours. But if you do not have endurance, it will be late for you: and when it is late for you, you will be turned away from him, and you will be like those who in the wilderness were wearied, and hastened to ask of God the delights which he was reserving for them in the homeland; and when the delights were not given on the way, by which perhaps they would be corrupted, they murmured against God, and returned in heart to Egypt; whence in body they had been separated, in heart they returned. Do not, then, you so; do not so: fear the word of the Lord saying: *Remember Lot's wife*[^41]. She too on the way, now freed from Sodom, looked back; where she looked back, there she remained: she was made a pillar of salt, to season you. For she was given as an example to you, that you may have a heart, may not remain foolish on the way. Attend to her remaining, and pass over; attend to her looking back, and be you stretched forth to the things before, like Paul. What is it not to look back? *Forgetting the things behind,* he says. Therefore you follow, called to the supernal palm of which you may afterward glory. For the same Apostle says: *There remains for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me in that day*[^42].
