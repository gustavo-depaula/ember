# Exposition of Psalm 61

**1.** [v. 1] The delight of the divine words, and the sweetness of understanding the word of God, with the help of him who gives the sweetness, that our earth may give its fruit, exhorts both us to speak and you to hear. I see that you hear without disgust, and I rejoice at the palate of your heart, by which that which is wholesome is not rejected, but received with eagerness, and usefully retained. Let us, then, speak to you today too, as much as the Lord grants, of this psalm which we have just sung. Its title is: *Unto the end, for Idithun, a Psalm for David himself.*[^1] I recall that it has already been conveyed to you what Idithun is. For from the interpretation of the Hebrew tongue, as it has come to us, in Latin Idithun is called "Leaping over them." He who sings, then, leaps over certain ones, whom he looks down upon from above. Let us see how far he has leaped, and whom he has leaped over, and where he is set, though he has leaped over certain ones: from which spiritual and secure place he may gaze at the lowest things; not looking back so as to fall, but so that he who has leaped over may move the lazy to follow, and praise the place to which by leaping over he has come. For this one leaping over is so above something that he is under something: whence he wished first to convey to us under whom he is safe, that the fact that he has leaped over may be not of pride but of advancement.

**2.** [vv. 2-3] Set, then, in a certain fortified place, he says: *Shall not my soul be made subject to God?*[^2] For he had heard: *He who exalts himself will be humbled; and he who humbles himself will be exalted*[^3]: and, anxious lest by leaping over he grow proud, not lifted up by those things that were below, but humble because of him who was above; as if to the envious who threatened him with ruin, who had grieved that he had leaped over, he answered: *Shall not my soul be made subject to God?* What is it that you seek snares for me as I leap over? You wish to cast me down by insult, or deceive me by seduction. Surely do I so remember what I am above, as to have forgotten under whom I am? *Shall not my soul be subject to God?* However much I draw near, however much I ascend, however much I leap over, I shall be under God, not against God. Secure, then, I transcend the rest, when he holds me under himself who is above all things. *Shall not my soul be subject to God? For from him is my salvation. For he himself is my God and my savior, my upholder, I shall not be moved any more.* I know who is above me, I know who extends his mercy to those who know him, I know under whose covering of wings I hope: *I shall not be moved any more.* You indeed strive, he says to certain ones, leaping over those very ones to whom he speaks: you indeed strive that I be moved, but *let not the foot of pride come to me.* For from this comes about that which follows in the same psalm too: *Nor let the hand of sinners remove me*[^4]: with which agrees: *I shall not be moved any more.* For to that which was said there: *Nor let the hand of sinners remove me,* he answers here: *I shall not be moved any more*: but to that which was said there: *Let not the foot of pride come to me,* he answers here: *Shall not my soul be subject to God?*

**3.** Therefore, from a higher place fortified and safe, to whom the Lord has become a refuge, to whom God himself is in the place of a fortified spot, he looks back at those whom he has leaped over, and, looking down on them, speaks as though from a lofty tower; for this too was said of him: *A tower of strength from the face of the enemy*[^5]: he attends, then, to them, and says: *How long do you set upon a man?*[^6] By insulting, by hurling reproaches, by lying in wait, by persecuting, you set burdens upon a man; you set upon a man as much as a man can bear: but that a man may bear it, he is under him who made man. *How long do you set upon a man?* If you attend to the man: *Kill all of you.* Behold, set upon, rage, *kill, all of you. As against a leaning wall and a thrust-against fence*: press on, push, as though about to cast it down. And where is: *I shall not be moved any more*? But why: *I shall not be moved any more*? Because *he himself is my saving God, my upholder.* So you men can set burdens upon a man: surely not upon God who guards the man?

**4.** [v. 4] *Kill all of you.*[^7] What so great an extent of body is there in one man, where he could be killed by all? But we ought to understand our person, the person of our Church, the person of the body of Christ. For Jesus Christ is one man with his head and his body, the Savior of the body and the members of the body, two in one flesh, and in one voice, and in one passion; and, when iniquity has passed, in one rest. And so the sufferings of Christ are not in Christ alone; nay, the sufferings of Christ are only in Christ. For if you understand Christ as head and body, the sufferings of Christ are only in Christ: but if you understand Christ as head alone, the sufferings of Christ are not in Christ alone. For if the sufferings of Christ are in Christ alone, that is, in the head alone; whence does a certain member of his, the apostle Paul, say: *That I may fill up what is lacking of the pressures of Christ in my flesh*[^8]? If, then, you are in the members of Christ, whoever you are, whoever hear these things, whoever do not now hear these things (but yet you do hear, if you are in the members of Christ); whatever you suffer from those who are not in the members of Christ, was lacking to the sufferings of Christ. Therefore it is added, because it was lacking; you fill up the measure, you do not overflow it: you suffer as much as was to be contributed from your sufferings to the whole suffering of Christ, who suffered in our head, and suffers in his members, that is, in our very selves. To this common, as it were, commonwealth of ours each of us, according to our small measure, pays what we owe, and according to the possession of our strength we contribute, as it were, a quota of sufferings. The full discharge of all sufferings will not be, except when the age is ended. *How long do you set upon a man?* Whatever the Prophets suffered from the blood of just Abel even to the blood of Zechariah, was set upon a man, because certain members of Christ went before the coming of the incarnation of Christ: as in one being born, the head not yet coming forth, the hand came forth, but yet the hand too was connected to the head. Do not, then, think, brethren, that all the just who suffered the persecution of the unrighteous, even those who came sent before the Lord's coming to foretell the Lord's coming, did not pertain to the members of Christ. Far be it that he not pertain to the members of Christ, who pertains to the city which has Christ as king. That one is the heavenly Jerusalem, the holy city: this one city has one king. The king of this city is Christ: for he himself says to it: *Mother Zion, a man will say.* He says to it: *Mother;* but *a man. For Mother Zion, a man will say; and a man was made in it, and he himself, the Most High, founded it*[^9]. He, then, is its king, who founded it, the Most High; he himself in it was made a man, most humble. He himself, then, before the coming of his incarnation, sent before certain of his members, after whom, foretelling that he would come, he too came, joined to them. Refer it to the likeness of that one being born; because the hand coming forth before the head is both with the head, and under the head. For of Christ it was said, when the excellence of the first people was praised, and the broken natural branches were grieved over: *Whose is the adoption*, he says, *and the testaments and the establishing of the Law; whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all things God blessed forever*[^10]. *From whom is Christ according to the flesh*, as from *Zion*, because *a man was made in it*: because *Christ is over all things God blessed forever*, because *he himself, the Most High, founded it*[^11]. *From whom is Christ according to the flesh*, the son of David: *who is over all things God blessed forever*, the Lord of David. The whole of that city, then, speaks, from the blood of just Abel even to the blood of Zechariah. From there and thenceforward from the blood of John, through the blood of the Apostles, through the blood of the martyrs, through the blood of the faithful of Christ, one city speaks, one man says: *How long do you set upon a man? Kill all of you.* Let us see whether you blot out, let us see whether you extinguish, let us see whether you take from the earth his name, let us see whether you do not, as the peoples, meditate empty things, saying: *When will he die, and his name perish*[^12]? *As against a leaning wall, and a thrust-against fence*, lean on, push. Hear above: *My upholder, I shall not be moved any more*[^13]; because as a heap of sand I have been pushed that I might fall, and the Lord has upheld me.

**5.** [v. 5] *But yet they thought to repel my honor*[^14]: conquered while they kill those who yield, multiplying the faithful by the blood of the slain, yielding to these and no longer able to kill. *But yet they thought to repel my honor.* Now, because the Christian cannot be killed, it is contrived that the Christian be dishonored. For now the hearts of the ungodly are tortured by the honor of Christians: now that spiritual Joseph, after being sold by his brothers, after being transferred from his homeland to the nations as it were into Egypt, after the humiliation of prison, after the contrivance of false witness, after there was done concerning him what was said: *The iron passed through his soul*[^15]; now he is honored, now he is no longer subject to his selling brothers, but distributes grain to the hungry. Conquered by his humility and chastity, his incorruption, his temptations, his sufferings, they now see him honored, and they think to repel his honor. For in their thoughts is that: *The sinner will see*: for he will see, when the city set upon a mountain cannot be hidden. *The sinner*, then, *will see, and will be angry; he will gnash with his teeth, and waste away*[^16]. The poison lies hidden in the heart, and is covered by the brow, in those raging and indignant. Therefore here too he states their thoughts: *They thought*, he says, *to repel my honor.* For they do not dare to bring forth in words what they think. Let us wish them good, even if they wish evil. Judge them, O God; let them fall from their thoughts. For what is better for them, what more useful, than that they fall from there where they stand ill? that they themselves, corrected, may be able to say: *You have set my feet upon a rock*[^17].

**6.** *But yet they thought to repel my honor.*[^18] All against one, or one against all; or all against all, or one against one? Meanwhile, when he says: *You set upon a man;* as if upon one: and when he says: *Kill all of you;* as though all against one: but yet also all against all, because Christians too are all, but in one. But those diverse errors, enemies of Christ, are they all only to be called "all"? are they not also one? Plainly I dare to say even "one": because there is one city and one city, one people and one people, king and king. What is, one city and one city? Babylon is one; Jerusalem is one. By whatever other, even mystical, names it be called, yet it is one city and one city: that one with the devil as king; this one with Christ as king. For I attend to the Gospel in a certain place, and it moves me, I think it moves you too. After many were invited to the wedding, the good and the evil, and the wedding was filled with those reclining; for the servants sent, as it had been commanded, invited both the good and the evil; and the king entered to inspect those reclining, and found a man not having a wedding garment, and said to him what you know: *Friend, how did you come in here, not having a wedding garment? But he was silent*[^19]. And he ordered his hands and feet to be bound, and him to be cast into the outer darkness. He was lifted from the banquet, and sent to punishment, some man, I know not who, in so great a throng of those reclining. But yet the Lord, wishing to show that that one man was one body which consists of many, where he ordered him to be cast out, and sends him to deserved punishments, at once added: *For many are called, but few chosen*[^20]. What is this? You called the throngs, a huge multitude came; you announced, you spoke, they were multiplied beyond number, the wedding was filled with those reclining; one alone was cast out from there, and you say: *For many are called, but few chosen.* Why not rather: All called, many chosen, one cast out? If he said: For many are called, and more chosen, but few reprobate; among the few perhaps we would more verisimilarly understand that one. But now he says that one was cast out from there, and adds: *For many are called, but few chosen.* Who are the chosen, except those who remained? With one cast out, the chosen remained. How, with one cast out from many, are the few chosen, except because in that one are the many? All who relish earthly things, all who prefer earthly happiness to God, all who seek their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's, pertain to that one city, which is mystically called Babylon, and has the devil as king. But all who relish the things that are above, who meditate heavenly things, who live in the age with anxiety lest they offend God, who beware of sinning, who are not ashamed to confess when sinning, the humble, the meek, the holy, the just, the godly, the good; all pertain to one city, which has Christ as king. For that one on earth is as it were greater in time; not in loftiness, not in honor. That city was born first; this city was born later. For that one began from Cain; this from Abel. These two bodies, acting under two kings, pertaining to their several cities, are opposed to one another even unto the end of the age, until from the mingling there is a separation, and some are set at the right, others at the left, and it is said to them: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world;* but to those: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^21]. For Christ says this: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world.* The king of his own city, victor over all things. But to those set at the left, as to the city of the unrighteous: *Go*, he says, *into the eternal fire.* Surely he does not separate the king from them? No: for he added, *which is prepared for the devil and his angels.*

**7.** Attend, brethren, attend, I beg you. For it delights me to speak still a little to you of this sweet city. *For most glorious things have been said of you, O city of God*[^22]. And *if I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget me*[^23]. For sweet is the one homeland, and truly the one homeland, the only homeland: besides that, whatever is to us is a pilgrimage. I will say, then, what you may recognize, what you may approve: I will recall what you know; I will not teach what you do not know. *Not first*, says the Apostle, *that which is spiritual, but that which is animal; afterward that which is spiritual*[^24]. Therefore that city is greater in age, because Cain was born first, and afterward Abel: but among these *the greater will serve the lesser*[^25]. That one greater in age; this one greater in dignity. Why is that one greater in age? Because *not first that which is spiritual, but that which is animal.* Why is this one greater in dignity? Because *the greater will serve the lesser.* But Cain built a city, as we read: before there was any city, in the beginning of human affairs, Cain built a city. Without doubt you may understand that many men had already been born from those two, and from those they had begotten, that there might be a fit and suitable number on which the name of city might be imposed. Cain, then, built a city, where there was no city. There was built also afterward Jerusalem, the kingdom of God, the holy city, the city of God; and set in a certain form of a shadow signifying things to come. Understand, then, the great mystery, and hold what I said beforehand: *Not first that which is spiritual, but that which is animal, afterward the spiritual*: therefore Cain first built a city, and built there where there was no city. But that Jerusalem might be built, it was not built there where there was no city, but there was a city first which was called Jebus, whence the Jebusites. That being captured, conquered, subjected, a new city was built as it were, the old being destroyed; and it was called Jerusalem, "Vision of peace," the city of God. Everyone born of Adam, then, does not yet pertain to Jerusalem: for he carries with him the stock of iniquity, and the punishment of sin, assigned to death; and pertains in a manner to a certain old city. But if he is to be in the people of God, the old will be destroyed, and the new built. Therefore Cain built a city where there was no city. For from mortality and from malice each one begins, that he may afterward be made good. *For as through the disobedience of one man many were made sinners; so through the obedience of one man many will be made just*[^26]. *And in Adam we all die*[^27]; and each of us was born from Adam. Let him pass over to Jerusalem; the old will be destroyed, and the new built. As, the Jebusites being conquered, that Jerusalem might be built, it is said: *Put off the old man, and put on the new*[^28]. And to those already built up in Jerusalem, and shining with the light of grace, it is said: *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^29]. The evil city, then, runs from the beginning even to the end; and the good city is founded by the change of the evil.

**8.** And these two cities are for the present mingled; in the end to be separated: striving against one another; one for iniquity, the other for righteousness; one for vanity, the other for truth. And sometimes that very temporal mingling brings it about that certain ones pertaining to the city of Babylon administer affairs pertaining to Jerusalem; and again certain ones pertaining to Jerusalem administer affairs pertaining to Babylon. I seem to have promised something difficult. Be patient, until it be shown by examples. For all things in the old people, as the Apostle writes, *happened to them in figure: but they were written for our correction, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^30]. Look back, then, at that first people, set also to signify the later people; and see there the things I say. There were great kings in Jerusalem; it is known; they are counted, they are named. All, then, who were unrighteous were citizens of Babylon, and administered the affairs of Jerusalem: all, in the end to be separated from there, pertain only to the devil. Again we find citizens of Jerusalem administering certain affairs pertaining to Babylon. For those three young men, conquered by a miracle, Nebuchadnezzar made administrators of his kingdom, and set them over his satraps; and citizens of Jerusalem administered the affairs of Babylon. Look now at this being fulfilled and done in the Church, and in these times. All of whom it was said: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^31], are citizens of Babylon, administering the commonwealth of the city of Jerusalem. For if they administered nothing of the city of Jerusalem, whence: *What they say, do*? whence, *they sit on the seat of Moses*[^32]? Again, if they are citizens of that very Jerusalem who will reign forever with Christ, whence: *What they do, do not do*, except because they too are to hear: *Depart from me, all you who work iniquity*[^33]? It is known to you, then, that citizens of the evil city administer certain acts of the good city. Let us see whether even now citizens of the good city administer certain acts of the evil city. Every earthly commonwealth is at some time certainly to perish; whose kingdom is to pass away, when that kingdom comes of which we pray: *Your kingdom come*[^34], and of which it was foretold: *And of his kingdom there will be no end*[^35]: the earthly commonwealth, then, has our citizens administering its affairs. For how many of the faithful, how many good men are both magistrates in their cities, and judges, and generals, and counts, and kings? All just and good, having in their heart nothing but the most glorious things that have been said of you, O city of God. And they perform, as it were, a conscripted service in the city that is to pass away, and there by the teachers of the holy city they are bidden to keep faith with their superiors, *whether to the king as preeminent, or to governors as sent by him for the punishment of the evil, but the praise of the good*[^36]; or as servants, that they be subject to their masters, and Christians to pagans; and let the better keep faith with the worse, for a time to serve, forever to have dominion. For these things are done until iniquity passes away. Servants are bidden to bear unjust and difficult masters: citizens of Babylon are bidden to be borne with by the citizens of Jerusalem, and showing more obedience than if they were citizens of Babylon itself; as fulfilling: *He who has conscripted you a thousand paces, go with him another two*[^37]. This whole city, scattered, spread, mingled, he addresses with these words and says: *How long do you set upon a man? Kill all of you*: both you who are outside as thorns in the hedges, or as fruitless wood in the forests; and you who are within as tares, or as chaff; all, however many you are, separated, mingled, to be borne with, to be separated, *kill, all of you: as against a leaning wall and a thrust-down fence. But yet they thought to repel my honor.* They did not say it, but yet they thought it. *They thought to repel my honor.*

**9.** *I ran in thirst.* For they were rendering evil for good. They were killing, they were repelling; I was thirsting for them: they thought to repel my honor; I was thirsting to draw them over into my body. For by drinking, what do we do, except send into our members the moisture set outside, and lead it into our body? This Moses did with that head of the calf. The head of the calf, a great sacrament. For the head of the calf was the body of the ungodly, in the likeness of a calf eating hay, of those seeking earthly things: because *all flesh is hay*[^38]. It was, then, as I said, the body of the ungodly. Moses, angry, cast it into the fire, broke it to pieces, scattered it in water, gave it to the people to drink; and the anger of the Prophet became the administration of prophecy. For that body is cast into the fire of tribulations, and broken to pieces by the word of God. For gradually they cease from the unity of his body. For as a garment, so it is consumed over time. And each one who becomes a Christian is separated from that people, and is, as it were, broken off from the mass. In conspiracy they hate; broken to pieces they believe. And what now is more evident, than that into that body of the city of Jerusalem, whose image the people of Israel was, men were to be drawn over through Baptism? Therefore it was scattered in water, that it might be given for drink. This unto the end this one thirsts; he runs, and thirsts. For he drinks many; but never will he be without thirst. Hence is that: *I thirst, woman, give me to drink*[^39]. That Samaritan woman at the well perceived the Lord thirsting, and was satisfied by the thirsting one: she first perceived him thirsting, that he might drink her believing. And set on the cross: *I thirst, he said*[^40]; although they did not give him that for which he thirsted. For he was thirsting for them: but they gave vinegar; not new wine with which new wineskins are filled, but old wine, but badly old. For the old men too are called vinegar, the old men of whom it was said: *For there is no change for them*[^41], that, the Jebusites being overthrown, Jerusalem might be built.

**10.** So too the body of this head runs in thirst from the beginning even to the end. And as though it were said to it: Why in thirst? what is lacking to you, O body of Christ, O Church of Christ? set in so great honor, in so great loftiness, in so great elevation even in this age, what is lacking to you? There is fulfilled concerning you what was foretold: *All the kings of the earth will adore him; all nations will serve him*[^42]. Why, then, do you thirst? why do you thirst? are you not satisfied with so great peoples? What peoples do you mean? *With their mouth they were blessing, and in their heart they were cursing. Many are called, but few chosen*[^43]. The woman suffering an issue of blood touched the fringe of his garment, and was healed: and when the Lord marveled at the one touching him, because he had perceived that power had gone out from him, namely to heal the woman, he said: *Who touched me?* And the disciples, wondering: *The crowds press you, and you say: Who touched me?* And he: *Someone touched me*[^44]. As if to say: One touched; the crowds press. Those who at the solemnities of Jerusalem fill the churches, at the solemnities of Babylon fill the theaters: and yet they serve, honor, obey, not only those who carry the sacraments of Christ, and hate the precepts of Christ, but even those who do not carry the sacraments themselves, whether they be pagans, whether they be Jews; they honor, praise, proclaim, but *with their mouth they were blessing.* I do not attend to the mouth; he knows who instructed me, *in their heart they were cursing.* There they were cursing, where they thought to repel my honor.

**11.** [v. 6] What of you, O Idithun, body of Christ, leaping over them? What of you amid all these things? what of you? will you fail? will you not persevere unto the end? will you not hear: *He who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved,* although *because iniquity abounds, the love of many will grow cold*[^45]? And where is that which is, that you leaped over them? where is that your citizenship is in heaven? But they cleave to earthly things; as earth-born they relish earth, and are earth, the serpent's food. What of you amid these things? But yet, although they do these things, although they think these things, although they push, although they press as on a leaning thing, although they feel it now upright, and think to repel my honor; although they bless with their mouth and curse in their heart; although they lie in wait where they can, slander where they can: *But yet my soul will be made subject to God.*[^46] And who could endure so much, whether open wars, or hidden snares? who could endure so much among open enemies, among false brethren? who could endure so much? Surely a man? and if a man, surely not a man of himself? I have not so leaped over that I should be lifted up and fall: *My soul will be made subject to God: because from him is my patience.* What patience amid so great scandals, except because if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience? My grief has come; my rest too will come: my tribulation has come; my purging too will come. Does gold shine in the goldsmith's furnace? In the necklace it will shine, in the ornament it will shine: yet let it endure the furnace, that, purged of dross, it may come to the light. This furnace; there chaff, there gold, there fire; to this the goldsmith blows: in the furnace the chaff burns, and the gold is purged; the former is turned to ashes, the latter is stripped of its dross. The world is the furnace, the unrighteous the chaff, the just the gold, tribulation the fire, the goldsmith God: what the goldsmith wishes, then, I do; where the craftsman sets me, I endure: I am bidden to endure, he knows how to purge. Let the chaff burn to set me on fire, and as it were to consume me: it is turned to ashes, I am free from dross. Why? Because *my soul will be made subject to God: because from him is my patience.*

**12.** [v. 7] Who is this for you, from whom is your patience? *Because he himself is my God and my savior, my upholder, I shall not move away.*[^47] *Because he himself is my God;* therefore he calls me: *and my savior;* therefore he justifies me: *and my upholder;* therefore he glorifies me. For here I am called and justified, but there I am glorified; and from there where I am glorified, *I shall not move away.* For I shall not remain in my pilgrimage: there is here that from which I may move away; but I shall come to that from which I shall not move away. For I am a lodger with you on earth, like all my fathers. Therefore I shall move away from the lodging; from the heavenly home I shall not move away.

**13.** [v. 8] *In God is my salvation, and my glory.*[^48] I shall be saved in God; I shall be glorious in God: for not only saved, but also glorious; saved, because just out of ungodly, justified by him; but glorious, because not only justified, but also honored. For *whom he predestined, them he also called.* Calling them, what did he do here? *Whom he called, them he also justified; but whom he justified, them he also glorified*[^49]. Justification, then, pertains to salvation, glorification to honor. That glorification pertains to honor, there is no need to argue. That justification pertains to salvation, let us seek some proof. Behold, it occurs from the Gospel: there were those who seemed to themselves just, and reprehended the Lord, because he admitted sinners to the banquet, and ate with publicans and sinners: so to such proud ones, mighty of the earth, greatly lifted up, glorying much in their health, which they supposed they had, not which they held, what did the Lord answer? *The healthy do not need a physician, but those who are ill.* Whom does he call healthy? whom does he call ill? It follows, and he says: *I came not to call the just, but sinners to repentance*[^50]. He called, then, the just healthy; not because the Pharisees were this, but because they thought themselves to be this; and therefore they were proud, and envied the physician to the sick, and the more, being sick, they killed the physician. Yet he called the just healthy, the sinners ill. So that I might be justified, says this one leaping over, is from him; and that I might be glorified, is from him: *In God is my salvation and my glory. My salvation,* that I may be saved; *my glory,* that I may be honored. This then: now what? *The God of my help, and my hope is in God*: until I come to perfect justification and salvation. *For by hope we have been saved: but hope which is seen is not hope*[^51]. Until I come to that glorification, where the just will shine in the kingdom of their Father like the sun. Meanwhile now, amid temptations, amid iniquities, amid scandals, amid open assaults and crafty speeches, amid those who bless with their mouth, and curse in their heart, amid those who think to repel my honor, what here? *The God of my help*: for he gives help to those fighting. Fighting against whom? *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood; but against principalities and powers*[^52]. *The God*, then, *of my help, and my hope is in God.* Hope, as long as that which is promised is not yet, and that which is not yet seen is believed; but when it has come, there will be salvation and glorification: yet, while those things are deferred, we are not forsaken; for *the God of my help, and my hope is in God.*

**14.** [v. 9] *Hope in him, all the assembly of the people.*[^53] Imitate Idithun; leap over your enemies: those fighting against you, resisting your journey, hating you, leap over. *Hope in him, all the assembly of the people; pour out your hearts before him.* Do not yield to those who say to you: Where is your God? *My tears*, he says, *have become bread to me day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God?* But what does he say there? *These things I meditated, and poured out my soul above me*[^54]. I recalled what I hear: *Where is your God?* I remembered these things, *and poured out my soul above me.* Seeking my God, I poured out my soul above me, that I might reach him; I did not remain in myself. Therefore: *Hope in him, all the assembly of the people. Pour out your hearts before him*: by beseeching, by confessing, by hoping. Do not retain your hearts within your hearts: *Pour out your hearts before him.* What you pour out does not perish. For he is my upholder. If he upholds, why do you fear to pour out? Cast your care upon the Lord, and hope in him. *Pour out your hearts before him: God is our helper.* What do you fear amid the whisperers, the detractors hateful to God; where they can openly assailing, where they cannot secretly lying in wait; falsely praising, truly being enemies? amid them what do you fear? *God is our helper.* Do they rival God? Surely they are not stronger than he? *God is our helper;* be secure. If God is for us, who is against us? *Pour out your hearts before him,* leaping over to him, lifting up your souls: *God is our helper.*

**15.** [v. 10] And now, set in a fortified place, in a tower of strength from the face of the enemy, have mercy on those you feared: for you ought to run in thirst. Look down, then, on them, now set in that place, and say: *But yet vain are the sons of men, lying are the sons of men.*[^55] Sons of men, how long heavy of heart? Vain sons of men, lying sons of men; sons of men, why do you love vanity, and seek a lie? Say these things with compassion, and relish them. If you have leaped over, if you love your enemies, if you desire to destroy that you may build, if you love him who judges among the nations, and fills the ruins; so say these things to them, not hating them, not rendering evil for evil. *Lying are the sons of men in the balances, that they themselves may deceive, out of vanity, into one.* Surely they are many: behold, there is that one, that one who was cast out of the throng of those banqueting. They conspire, all seek temporal things, and whoever are carnal, carnal things, and hope for the future, whoever hope: even if from a variety of opinions they go in different directions, yet out of vanity they are into one. Diverse indeed and manifold errors, and a kingdom divided against itself will not stand: but a will alike in all, vain and lying, pertaining to one king, with whom it is to be hurled headlong into eternal fire: *they themselves out of vanity into one.*

**16.** [v. 11] And see that he thirsts; see that he runs in thirst. He turns himself, then, to them, thirsting for them: *Do not hope upon iniquity.*[^56] For my hope is in God. *Do not hope upon iniquity.* You who are unwilling to draw near and leap over, *do not hope upon iniquity.* For I who have leaped over, my hope is in God: and surely there is no iniquity with God? *Do not hope upon iniquity.* Let us do this, let us do that, let us think that, let us so arrange the snares; out of vanity into one. You thirst: those who think these things against you are betrayed by those whom you drink. *Do not hope upon iniquity.* Iniquity is vain, iniquity is nothing; nothing is powerful but righteousness. The truth can be hidden for a time; it cannot be conquered. Iniquity can flourish for a time, it cannot remain. *Do not hope upon iniquity: and for plunder do not lust.* You are not rich, and do you wish to plunder? What do you find? what do you lose? O ruinous gains! You find money; you lose righteousness. *For plunder do not lust.* I am poor, I do not have. Do you therefore wish to plunder? What you plunder, you see: by whom you are plundered, do you not see? Do you not know that the enemy goes about as a roaring lion, and seeking what to plunder? That prey which you wish to plunder is in the trap: you hold, and are held. For plunder, then, do not lust, O poor man! but lust after God who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy. He will feed you who made you. He who feeds the robber, will he not feed the innocent? He will feed you who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust. If he feeds those to be condemned, will he not feed those to be freed? Therefore for plunder do not lust. This was said to the poor man, perhaps about to plunder something out of necessity. Let the rich man come forward: I, he says, do not have the necessity of plundering; nothing is lacking to me; all things abound. And do you hear: *If riches flow in, do not set your heart on them.* The one does not have; the other has: let the one not seek to plunder what he does not have; let the other not set his heart on what he has. *If riches flow in,* that is, if they overflow, as though they run from a fountain. *Do not set your heart on them*: do not presume of yourself, do not fix yourself there: at least fear even this, *If riches flow in.* Do you not see that if you set your heart there, you too will flow away? You are rich, and behold you now do not lust to have still more, because you have much: hear: *Charge the rich of this world not to be proud-minded.* And what is: *Do not set your heart on them*? *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*[^57]. Therefore: *If riches flow in, do not set your heart on them,* do not trust in riches, do not presume, do not hope; lest it be said: *Behold the man who did not make God his helper; but hoped in the abundance of his riches, and prevailed in his vanity*[^58]. Therefore, vain sons of men, lying sons of men, neither plunder, nor, if riches flow in, set your heart on them; no longer love vanity, and seek a lie. For blessed is he whose hope is the Lord his God, and who has not looked back upon vanities and lying follies. You wish to deceive, you wish to commit fraud; what do you bring to deceive? Deceitful balances. *For lying*, he says, *are the sons of men in the balances,* that they may deceive by bringing forth deceitful balances. With a false scale you deceive those looking on: do you not know that it is one who weighs, another who judges of the weight? He does not see to whom you weigh; but he sees who weighs both you and him. Therefore do not lust after fraud, after plunder; do not set your hope in the things you have: I have admonished, I have foretold, says this Idithun.

**17.** [vv. 12-13] What follows? *Once has God spoken, these two things I have heard; that power is God's, and to you, O Lord, is mercy: because you will render to each one according to his works.*[^59] Idithun spoke, he sounded from on high, whither he leaped over; he heard there something, and said it to us: but I am somewhat troubled in this which he said to us, brethren; and until I share with you either my trouble or some breathing-space of mine, I wish you attentive. For we have brought the Psalm to its end with the Lord's help: after these things which we are going to say, nothing remains that we should expound further of it. So strive with me, whether we can understand this: and if I cannot, and someone of you understands what I cannot; I will rejoice rather than envy. It is altogether difficult to investigate how it is set down first: *Once has God spoken;* then, when he once spoke, I *heard two things.* For if it said: Once has God spoken, this one thing I heard; it would seem to have cut off part of this question, so that we should seek only what is: *Once has God spoken.* But now we are going to seek both what is: *Once has God spoken;* and what is, *these two things I have heard,* when he spoke once.

**18.** *Once has God spoken.*[^60] What do you say, Idithun? If you speak, who leaped over them: *Once has he spoken*? I consult another Scripture, and it says to me: *In many parts, and in many ways, of old God spoke to the fathers in the Prophets*[^61]. What is: *Once has God spoken*? Is it not he who is God, who in the beginning of the human race spoke to Adam? Is it not the same who spoke to Cain, to Noah, to Abraham, to Isaac, to Jacob, to all the Prophets, and to Moses? Moses was one; and how often did God speak to him? Behold, even to one not once, but often did God speak. Then he spoke to the Son here set down: *You are my beloved Son*[^62]. God spoke to the Apostles, he spoke to all the saints, even if not by a voice sounding through a cloud, yet in the heart where he himself is the master. Whence that one says: *I will hear what the Lord God says in me, because he will speak peace to his people*[^63]. What is, then: *Once has God spoken*? This one had leaped over much, to come there, where God once spoke. Behold, I have said it briefly to Your Charity. Here among men, to men, often, in many ways, in many parts, through the manifold creation God spoke: with himself God spoke once, because God begot one Word. This Idithun, then, leaping over them, had leaped over with the strong, vigorous, and most confident edge of the mind, had leaped over the earth, and whatever is on earth; the air, all the clouds from which God spoke many things, and often, and to many: had leaped over even all the angels by the edge of faith. For this one leaping over was not content with earthly things, but, like an eagle flying, was carried past all the mist by which all the earth is covered. For Wisdom says: *And with a cloud I covered all the earth*[^64]. He came to something clear, leaping over the whole creation, and seeking God, and pouring out his soul above himself; he came to the beginning, and the Word God with God; and found the one Word of the one Father; and saw that God spoke once; he saw the Word through which all things were made, and in which all things are at once, not diverse, not separate, not unequal. For what God made through the Word, he himself was not ignorant of; but if he knew what he made, it was in him before that came to be which came to be. For if that which came to be was not in him before it came to be, where did he know what he made? For you cannot say that God made things unknown. So God knew what he made. And where did he know it before he made it, if things cannot be known except when made? But by things made they cannot be known except before they are made; by you, namely, by man set at the bottom and made at the bottom: but before all these things came to be, they were known by him by whom they were made, and what he knew he made. Therefore in that Word through which he made all things, before they came to be all things were; and when they were made, there all things are: but in one way here, in another there; in one way in the proper nature in which they were made, in another in the art through which they were made. Who could unfold this? We can try: go with Idithun, and see.

**19.** Now, then, as we could, we have said how God spoke once: let us see how he heard these two things: *These two things I have heard.*[^65] Perhaps it does not follow that he heard only these two: but, *these two things*, he says, *I have heard;* certain two things which it is needful for us that they be said, he heard. He heard perhaps many other things; but it is not needful that they be said to us. For the Lord too says: *I have many things to say to you; but you cannot bear them now*[^66]. What is, then: *These two things I have heard*? These two things which I am going to say to you, I do not say to you from myself, but I say what I have heard. *Once has God spoken*: he has one Word, the only-begotten God. In that Word are all things, because through the Word all things were made. He has one Word, where all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden. He has one Word; *once has God spoken. These two things,* which I am going to say to you, there *I heard;* I do not speak from myself, I do not say from myself: to this pertains *I heard. But the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him*[^67], that he may speak truth. For he hears him, lest by speaking a lie he speak from his own: lest you say: Who are you who say this to me? whence do you say this to me? I heard these two things, and I speak them to you because I heard these two things, who also have come to know that God spoke once. Do not despise the hearer telling you certain two things necessary for you, him who by leaping over the whole creation came to the only-begotten Word of God, where he might know that God spoke once.

**20.** Let him now, then, say certain two things. For these two things pertain much to us. *That power is God's, and to you, O Lord, is mercy.*[^68] Are these the two things; power, and mercy? These plainly: understand the power of God; understand the mercy of God. By these two almost all the Scriptures are contained. For the sake of these two, the Prophets; for the sake of these two, the Patriarchs; for the sake of these, the Law; for the sake of these, our Lord Jesus Christ himself; for the sake of these, the Apostles; for the sake of these, all the announcing and celebration of the word of God in the Church: for the sake of these two; for the sake of the power of God and his mercy. Fear his power; love his mercy. Neither so presume of his mercy as to despise his power: nor so fear his power as to despair of his mercy. With him is power; with him is mercy. This one he humbles; and this one he exalts: this one he humbles by power; that one he exalts by mercy. *For if God, willing to show wrath, and to demonstrate his power, brought in much patience vessels of wrath, which are perfected for perdition.* You have heard the power: seek the mercy. *And that he might make known*, he says, *his riches in vessels of mercy*[^69]. It pertains, then, to his power to condemn the unrighteous. And who would say to him: What have you done? *For you, O man, who are you, that you should answer God*[^70]? Fear, then, and tremble at his power; but hope in his mercy. The devil is a certain power; yet very often he wishes to harm, and cannot, because that power is under a power. For if the devil could harm as much as he wishes, none of the just would remain, nor would there be any of the faithful on earth. He himself, through his vessels, pushes, as it were a leaning wall; but he pushes only as much as he receives the power. But that the wall not fall, the Lord will uphold it; for he who gives power to the tempter, himself furnishes mercy to the tempted. For by measure the devil is permitted to tempt: *And you will give us to drink in tears in measure*[^71]. Do not, then, fear the tempter permitted to do something; for you have a most merciful Savior. He is permitted to tempt only as much as profits you, that you may be exercised, that you may be proved; that you who did not know yourself, may be found out by yourself. For where, or whence, except from this power of God, and his mercy, ought we to be secure, according to that apostolic sentence: *God is faithful, who does not permit you to be tempted beyond what you are able*[^72]?

**21.** Therefore, *power is God's: For there is no power except from God*[^73]. Do not say: And why does he give it so great a power? Suppose he did not give the power. He who gives power, does he have equity? You can murmur unjustly, but he cannot lose equity. *Surely there is no iniquity with God? Far be it*[^74]. Fix this in your heart; let not the enemy shake this out of your thought. God can do something, so that you do not know why he does it: yet he cannot do it unjustly, with whom there is no iniquity. For behold, you reprehend God as if for iniquity (I deal somewhat with you, attend a little): you would not reprehend iniquity, except by seeing righteousness. A reprehender of iniquity cannot be one who does not discern righteousness, to which, compared, he reprehends iniquity. For whence do you know that this is unjust, unless you know what is just? For what if this too is just, which you call unjust? Far be it, you say, it is unjust: and you cry out as with seeing eyes, seeing this to be unjust, namely by some rule of righteousness, to which, comparing this which you see crooked, and discerning that it does not agree with the straightness of your rule, you reprehend; as a craftsman discerning the just from the unjust. Therefore I ask of you: Whence do you see that this is just? Where, I say, do you see this just, by seeing which you reprehend the unjust? Whence is that something, I know not what, with which your soul is sprinkled, set in haze from many parts, that something which flashes upon your mind? whence is this just? Does it then not have its own fountain? Is it from yourself to you that there is what is just, and can you give yourself righteousness? No one gives himself what he does not have. Therefore, since you are unjust, you cannot be just, except by turning yourself to a certain abiding righteousness; from which if you withdraw, you are unjust; to which if you draw near, you are just. With you withdrawing it does not fail; with you drawing near it does not grow. Where, then, is that righteousness? Do you seek it on earth? Far be it. For you do not seek gold or precious stones, when you seek righteousness. Seek in the sea, seek in the clouds, seek in the stars: seek in the angels; you find it in them, but they too drink from the fountain. For the righteousness of the angels is in all of them, but is received from one. Look, then, transcend, go there where God spoke once; and there you will find the fountain of righteousness, where is the fountain of life: *Because with you is the fountain of life*[^75]. For if from a scant dew you wish to judge what is just, and what is unjust; surely there is no iniquity with God, whence righteousness flows to you as from a fountain, in so far as you relish what is just, although from many parts you unjustly relish wrongly? God has, then, the fountain of righteousness. Do not seek iniquity there, where light is without shadow. But plainly the cause can lie hidden from you. If the cause lies hidden from you, look back at your ignorance, see who you are; attend to these two things: *That power is God's, and to you, O Lord, is mercy. Do not seek things stronger than you, and do not search out things higher than you; but the things the Lord has commanded you, those think on always*[^76]. Because to these things the Lord has commanded you pertain these two: *That power is God's, and to you, O Lord, is mercy.* Do not fear the enemy: he does only as much as he has received the power. Fear him who has the supreme power; fear him who does as much as he wills, and who does nothing unjustly, and whatever he does is just. We thought something was unjust, I know not what: from the fact that God did it, believe it to be just.

**22.** Therefore, you say, if someone kills an innocent man, does he act justly, or unjustly? Unjustly, certainly. Why does God permit this? See first whether you do not owe this: *Break your bread to the hungry, and bring the needy without shelter into your house; if you see one naked, clothe him*[^77]. For this is your righteousness; for this the Lord has commanded you: *Wash yourselves, be clean, take away wickedness from your hearts, and from before my eyes; learn to do good, judge for the orphan and the widow: and come, and let us reason together, says the Lord*[^78]. You wish to reason, before you do that whereby you may be worthy to reason, why God permitted this. The counsel of God, O man, I cannot tell you: yet this I say, that both the man who killed an innocent man acted unjustly, and it would not be done unless God permitted; and although that man acted unjustly, yet God did not permit this unjustly. Let the cause lie hidden in this someone, I know not who, about whom you are moved, whose innocence stirs you. For I could quickly answer you: He would not be killed unless he were guilty: but you think him innocent. I could quickly say this to you. For you would not have searched out his heart, examined his deeds, weighed his thoughts, so as to be able to say to me: He was unjustly killed. I could, then, easily answer: but a certain just man is set against me, without controversy just, without doubt just, who had no sin, killed by sinners, betrayed by a sinner; Christ the Lord himself, of whom we cannot say that he had any iniquity, because he paid back the things he did not plunder, is set against me. And what shall I say of Christ? I deal with you, you say. And I with you. About him you put forth a question; about him I solve the question. For there we know the counsel of God, which we would not know except with him himself revealing it: so that when you have found the counsel of God by which he permitted his innocent Son to be killed by the unjust, and such a counsel as pleases you, and such as, if you are just, cannot displease you, you may believe that in other matters too God does this by counsel, but it lies hidden from you. Come now, brethren, there was need of the blood of the just one to blot out the handwriting of sins; there was need of an example of patience, an example of humility; there was need of the sign of the cross to subdue the devil and his angels; there was need for us of the suffering of our Lord: for by the suffering of the Lord the world was redeemed. How great goods the suffering of the Lord wrought! And yet the suffering of this just one would not be, unless the unjust had killed the Lord. What then, is this good which has been furnished to us from the Lord's suffering to be imputed to the unjust killers of Christ? Far be it. They willed it; God permitted it. They would be guilty, even if they had only willed it; but God would not have permitted it, unless it were just. They willed to kill: suppose they could not: they would be unjust, they would be murderers; who would doubt it? *For the Lord questions the just and the ungodly*[^79]; and: *In the thoughts of the ungodly there will be questioning*[^80]. God examines what each one willed, not what he could. Therefore if they had willed, and could not, and had not killed, those would remain unjust; the suffering of Christ would not be furnished to you. The ungodly willed, then, to do it, so that he might be condemned; he was permitted, so that it might be furnished to you: what he willed is imputed to the iniquity of the ungodly; that he was permitted is imputed to the power of God. That one, then, unjustly willed; God justly permitted. And so, my brethren, both Judas the malign betrayer of Christ, and the persecutors of Christ, all malign, all ungodly, all unjust, all to be condemned: and yet the Father *did not spare his own Son; but handed him over for us all*[^81]. Arrange this, if you can; distinguish it, if you can: render to God your vows, which your lips distinguished: see what the unjust did there, what the just one did. The one willed; the other permitted: the one unjustly willed; the other justly permitted. Let the unjust will be condemned; let the just permission be glorified. For what evil befell Christ, because Christ died? Both they are evil, who willed to do evil; and he suffered no evil, to whom they did it. The mortal flesh was killed, slaying death by death, furnishing a lesson of patience, sending before an example of resurrection. How great goods of the just one were done out of the evil of the unjust! This is the great thing of God, that both the good you do he himself gave you, and out of your evil he himself does good. Do not, then, marvel; God permits, and permits by judgment: he permits, and permits by measure, number, weight. With him there is no iniquity. Only do you pertain to him, place your hope in him; let him be your helper, your salvation; in him let there be a fortified place, a tower of strength; let him be your refuge, and he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, but will make with the temptation also a way out, that you may be able to endure; so that the fact that he lets you suffer temptation is his power; the fact that he does not let more be done in you than you can bear is his mercy: *Because power is God's, and to you, O Lord, is mercy, because you will render to each one according to his works.*

**23.** That thirst of the Church wishes to drink even this man whom you see. At the same time, too, that you may know how many in the mingling of Christians bless with their mouth, and in their heart curse, this man, from a Christian and faithful, returns repentant, and, terrified by the power of the Lord, is converted to the mercy of the Lord. For, seduced by the enemy when he was faithful, he was long an astrologer; seduced he seduced, deceived he deceived, he enticed, he beguiled, he spoke many lies against God, who gave men the power of doing what is good, and of not doing what is evil. This man used to say that adultery was not done by one's own will, but by Venus; and murder was not done by one's own will, but by Mars; and the just was not done by God, but by Jupiter: and many other sacrilegious things, no small ones. From how many Christians, do you think, did he take coins? How many bought a lie from him, to whom we used to say: Sons of men, how long heavy of heart? why do you love vanity, and seek a lie? Now, as is to be believed of him, he has shuddered at the lie, and has perceived that he, an enticer of many men, was once enticed by the devil; he is converted, repenting, to God. We think, brethren, that this happened from a great fear of heart. For what shall we say? For if an astrologer were converted from a pagan, there would indeed be great joy; but yet it could seem that, if he had been converted, he would seek clerical office in the Church. He is a penitent; he seeks nothing but mercy alone. He is to be commended, then, both to your eyes and your hearts. Him whom you see, love with your hearts, guard with your eyes. See him, know him, and wherever he goes, point him out to the other brethren who are not now here: and this diligence is mercy, lest that seducer draw back his heart, and assail it. Guard yourselves; let not his conduct, his way, lie hidden from you; so that by your testimony it may be confirmed to us that he is truly converted to the Lord. For the report of his life will not be silent, when he is thus offered to you both to be seen and pitied. You know it is written in the Acts of the Apostles that many who were lost, that is, men of such arts, and followers of nefarious teachings, brought all their codices to the Apostles; and so many books were burned, that it pertained to the writer to make an estimate of them, and write down the sum of the price. This certainly for the glory of God, lest even such lost ones be despaired of by him who knows how to seek what had perished. This man, then, had perished; now sought, found, brought back, he carries with him codices to be burned, through which he was to be burned, so that, those being cast into the fire, he himself may pass into refreshment. Know, however, brethren, that he has long been knocking at the Church before Easter: for before Easter he began to ask of the Church of Christ a medicine. But because such is the art in which he was practiced, which was suspect of lying and deceit, he was deferred lest he tempt; and yet at length he was admitted, lest he be more dangerously tempted. Pray for him through Christ. Plainly pour out today's prayer for him to the Lord our God. For we know and are certain that your prayer blots out all his impieties. The Lord be with you.
