# Exposition of Psalm 114

**1.** [v. 1] *I loved, because the Lord will hear the voice of my prayer.* Let the soul sing this which is a pilgrim from the Lord, let that sheep sing this which had erred, let that son sing this who had been dead and revived, had perished and was found; let our soul sing this, dearest brothers and sons. Let us be instructed, and abide, and with the saints sing these things: *I loved, because the Lord will hear the voice of my prayer.* Is that itself the cause of loving, *because the Lord will hear the voice of my prayer?* and do we not rather either love because he heard, or love that he may hear? What is it, then: *I loved, because he will hear?* Or, because hope is wont to kindle love, did he say that he loved, because he hoped that God would hear the voice of his prayer?

**2.** [v. 2.] But whence did he hope this? *Because he inclined,* he says, *his ear to me, and in my days I called upon him.* Therefore I loved for this reason, because he will hear; he will hear for this reason, *because he inclined his ear to me.* But whence do you know, O human soul, that God inclined his ear to you, unless you say: I believed? These three, then, abide, faith, hope, love: because you believed, you hoped; because you hoped, you loved. Now if I ask whence the soul believed that God had inclined his ear to it; will it not answer: Because *he first loved us, and did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all*[^1]? *For how shall they call upon him in whom they have not believed?* says the Teacher of the Gentiles: *or how shall they believe him whom they have not heard? But how shall they hear without a preacher? or how shall they preach, unless they are sent?*[^2] When I beheld all these things done toward me, how should I not believe that the Lord inclined his ear to me? And so he commended his love in us, that Christ died for the impious. When, then, the beautiful feet of those who announced peace, who announced good things, announced these things to me, that *everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^3], I believed his ear inclined to me, *and in my days I called upon him.*

**3.** [v. 3.] And what are your days, since you said: *In my days I called upon him?* or perhaps those in which the fullness of time came, and God sent his Son, who had already said: *In an acceptable time I heard you, and in the day of salvation I helped you*[^4]? You heard from the mouth of the preacher coming to you with beautiful feet: *Behold, now is the acceptable time, behold, now is the day of salvation*[^5]: and you believed, and in your days called upon him, and said: *O Lord, rescue my soul*[^6]. These things are indeed so: but my days rather, he says, I can call the days of my misery, the days of my mortality, the days according to Adam, full of labor and sweat, the days according to the oldness of rottenness. For I, lying, *fixed in the mire of the depth*[^7], cried out in another psalm too: *Behold, you have set my days old*[^8]: *in* these *my days I called upon him.* For my days differ from the days of my Lord. I call my days those which I myself made for myself by private audacity, by which I deserted him: and since he everywhere reigns, and is almighty and all-holding, I deserved prison; that is, I received the darkness of ignorance, and the fetters of mortality. *In* these *my days I called upon him;* because I too cry out elsewhere: *Lead my soul out of prison*[^9]: and since in the day of salvation, which he bestowed on me, he helped me; the groaning of those in chains enters into his sight. For in these my days: *The sorrows of death surrounded me; the dangers of hell found me*: which would not find me unless I were straying from you. But now they found me: but I did not find them, who was rejoicing in the prosperity of the world, in which the dangers of hell deceive the more.

**4.** [vv. 4-5.] But after I too *found tribulation and grief, I called upon the name of the Lord.* For useful tribulation and grief lay hidden from me; tribulation from which he gives help, to whom it is said: *Give us help from tribulation, and vain is the salvation of man*[^10]. For I supposed that one should rejoice and exult over the vain salvation of man; but when I had heard from my Lord: *Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted*[^11], I did not wait until I should first lose the temporal goods in which I was badly delighting, and then mourn: but I attended to that very misery of mine in which I was delighted by such things, which I both feared to lose, and could not hold; I attended to it vehemently and strongly, and saw myself not only tortured by the adversities of this age, but even bound by its prosperities; and so I *found tribulation and grief* which lay hidden from me, *and called upon the name of the Lord. O Lord, rescue my soul. For wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death, except the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord?*[^12] Let the holy people of God say, then: *I found tribulation and grief, and called upon the name of the Lord*: and let the remnants of the nations hear, who do not yet call upon the name of the Lord; let them hear and seek, that they may find tribulation and grief, and call upon the name of the Lord, and be made safe. We do not say this to them, that they may seek the misery which they do not have, but that they may find that which, unknowingly, they have: nor do we wish this for them, that the earthly necessaries be lacking to them, of which they have need while they live mortally; but that they may lament this, that, the heavenly fullness lost, they have deserved to be in need of earthly things, not stable goods to enjoy, but necessary ones to sustain them. Let them recognize and mourn this misery; he will make them blessed mourners, who did not wish them to be always wretched.

**5.** [vv. 5, 6.] *Merciful is the Lord, and just, and our God has mercy.* Merciful, and just, and has mercy. *Merciful* first, because he inclined his ear to me; and I did not know that the ear of my God had drawn near to my mouth, unless by those beautiful feet I were roused to call upon him: for who called upon him, except the one whom he himself first called? Hence, then, first *merciful. Just,* but because he scourges; and again *has mercy,* because he receives: for he scourges every son whom he receives, nor ought what he scourges to be so bitter to me, as what he receives to be sweet. For how should he not scourge: *The Lord guarding the little ones,* whom, grown, he seeks as heirs? For what son is there to whom his father does not give discipline? *I was humbled, and he made me safe.* He made me safe, because I was humbled: for it is not a penal, but a salutary grief which the physician makes by cutting.

**6.** [vv. 7, 9.] *Turn back,* then, *O my soul, into your rest; because the Lord has done well to you*: not by your merits, or your strength; but *because the Lord has done well to you. Because he rescued,* he says, *my soul from death.* It is a wonderful thing, dearest ones, that when he had said that his soul was to be turned back into rest, because the Lord had done well to it; he subjoined: *Because he rescued my soul from death.* Was it to be turned back into rest for this reason, because it was rescued from death? Is not rest rather wont to be said to be in death? What, then, is its action, whose life is rest, and death disquiet? Such, then, ought to be the action of the soul, which tends toward quiet security, not which increases restless labor: since he rescued it from death who, having mercy on it, said: *Come to me, all you who labor, and I will refresh you: take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls; for my yoke is sweet, and my burden is light*[^13]. Meek and humble, then, as following Christ the way, ought to be the action of the soul tending toward rest: yet not lazy and slothful; that it may finish the course, as it is written: *In meekness perfect your works*[^14]. For, lest meekness be led to sluggishness, it was added: *Perfect your works.* For not, as in this life sleep refreshes us for action; but good action leads through to ever-watchful rest.

**7.** But God bestows all things, exhibits all things, of whom it is here said: *Because the Lord has done well to me, because he rescued my soul from death, my eyes from tears, my feet from falling.* For these things, fulfilled in hope, whoever understands the bond of this flesh sings of himself. For it was truly said: *I was humbled, and he made me safe*: but the Apostle too says that truly: That *by hope we were made safe*[^15]. And that we have been rescued from death, it is rightly said to be already fulfilled, so that we understand the death of those who do not believe, of whom he says: *Let the dead bury their dead*[^16]: and that in the earlier psalm: *The dead will not praise you, O Lord, nor all who go down into hell; but we who live, bless the Lord*[^17]. From this death, then, a faithful man can rightly understand his soul to be already rescued, by the very fact that from an unbeliever it has been made faithful: whence the Savior himself says: *He who believes in me,* he says, *makes a passage from death to life*[^18]. But the rest of the things are fulfilled in hope in those who have not yet departed from this life. For now, when we think of our most dangerous falls, the eyes do not cease from tears: but then he will rescue our eyes from tears, when our feet too from falling. Because then there will be no falling of walking feet, when there will be no slipperiness of weak flesh. But now, although our way be firm, which is Christ; yet because we spread under us the flesh which we are bidden to tame; in that very work by which we chastise it, subjected, it is a great thing not to fall in it: but not to slip in it, who can?

**8.** [vv. 8, 9.] Wherefore, because we are both in the flesh, and are not in the flesh (for we are in the flesh by that bond which is not yet loosed, which *to be dissolved and to be with Christ is by far the best*[^19]: but we are not in the flesh in that we gave the firstfruits of the spirit to God, if indeed we can say that *our citizenship is in heaven*[^20], and that as it were with our head we please God, but with our feet as the lowest parts of the soul we still feel slipperiness), hear how to hope pertains what is so sung as if it were already accomplished: *He rescued,* he says, *my eyes from tears, my feet from falling*: and yet he does not say: "I please"; but: *I will please in the sight of the Lord, in the land of the living;* sufficiently showing that from that part he does not yet please in the sight of the Lord, which is in the land of the dead, that is, in mortal flesh. *For those who are in the flesh cannot please God*[^21]. Whence what follows, and the same apostle says: *But you are not in the flesh*[^22]; he says it according to this, that *the body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*[^23]: according to which they pleased the Lord, because according to it they were not in the flesh. But who would please the living God, in a dead body? But what does he say? *But if the Spirit of him who raised Jesus Christ from the dead dwells in you; he who raised Jesus Christ from the dead will give life also to your mortal bodies because of the Spirit who dwells in you*[^24]: Then we will be *in the land of the living,* pleasing in every part in the sight of the Lord, in no part pilgrims from him. *For as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims from our Lord*; and insofar as we are pilgrims from there, so far we are not *in the land of the living.* *But we are confident and think it good rather to be pilgrims from the body, and to be at home with the Lord; and therefore we strive, whether at home or pilgrims, to be pleasing to him*[^25]. *We strive* indeed now, who still await the redemption of our body: but when death has been swallowed up in victory, and this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality; then there will be no weeping, because no falling; and no falling, because no corruption. And therefore we will no longer strive to please, but will altogether please in the sight of the Lord, in the land of the living.
