# Exposition of Psalm 3

**1.** [v 1.] What is said persuades us that this psalm is to be understood from the person of Christ: *I slept and took my rest, and rose up, because the Lord will sustain me*[^1]. For this sounds more fittingly in reference to the Lord's passion and resurrection than to that history in which David is recorded to have fled from the face of his son who was making war against him. And since it is written of the disciples of Christ, *As long as the bridegroom is with them, the sons of the bridegroom do not fast*[^2], it is no wonder if his impious son signifies the impious disciple who betrayed him. From whose face his having fled, though it can be taken historically, when at his departure he withdrew with the others onto the mountain, yet spiritually, when the Son of God, that is, the power and wisdom of God, deserted the mind of Judas, since the devil utterly invaded him, in what is written, *And the devil entered into his heart*[^3], it is well taken that Christ fled from his face; not because Christ yielded to the devil, but because, Christ departing, the devil took possession. This departure I think is called a flight in this psalm because of its swiftness; which is also signified by the Lord's word when he says: *What you do, do quickly*[^4]. We also speak this way in common usage, so that we say: It flees me, of what does not come to mind; and of a most learned man we say: Nothing flees him. Therefore the truth fled the mind of Judas when it ceased to enlighten him. Now Absalom, as some interpret it, is said in the Latin tongue to mean "the father's peace": which may seem strange, whether in the history of Kings, since Absalom waged war against his father; or in the history of the New Testament, since Judas was the betrayer of the Lord, how he can be understood as "the father's peace." But there too those who read diligently see that in that war David was at peace toward his son, who also lamented him with great grief when he was slain, saying: *Absalom my son, who will grant me to die for you?*[^5]; and in the history of the New Testament, the very patience of our Lord, so great and so wonderful, in that he bore with him so long as though he were good, since he was not ignorant of his thoughts, since he admitted him to the banquet at which he commended and handed over to the disciples the figure of his body and blood, and finally that in the very betrayal he received the kiss, it is well understood that Christ showed peace to his betrayer; although that man was laid waste by the inner war of so wicked a thought. And therefore Absalom is called "the father's peace," because the father had the peace that he did not have.

**2.** [vv 2. 3.] *Lord, why are they multiplied who trouble me?* So multiplied indeed, that even from the number of the disciples there was not lacking one who joined the number of persecutors. *Many rise up against me: many say to my soul: There is no salvation for him in his God.* It is manifest that, unless they had despaired of his rising again, they would not have killed him. To this purpose are those words: *Let him come down from the cross, if he is the Son of God*; and: *He saved others, himself he cannot save*[^6]. Therefore Judas too would not have betrayed him, unless he had been of the number of those who despised Christ, saying: *There is no salvation for him in his God.*

**3.** [v 4.] *But you, Lord, are my upholder*: this is said to God according to the man; for the taking up of the man is the Word made flesh. *My glory*: even he whom the Word of God so took up, that together with him he became God, calls God his glory. Let the proud learn, who do not gladly hear when it is said to them: *What do you have that you have not received? But if you have received it, why do you boast as though you had not received it?*[^7] *And the one who lifts up my head*: here I think the human mind itself is to be understood, which is not absurdly called the head of the soul, which so clung and in a manner coalesced with the surpassing supereminence of the Word taking up the man, that it was not cast down by such great humility of the passion.

**4.** [v 5.] *With my voice I have cried out to the Lord*: that is, not with the voice of the body, which is uttered with the noise of beaten air; but with the voice of the heart, which is silent to men, but to God sounds like a cry. By this voice Susanna was heard; and of this voice the Lord himself gives instruction, that we pray in our closed chambers, that is, in the secret places of the heart, without noise. Nor can anyone easily say that one prays less with this voice if no sound of words is uttered from the body; since, even when we are silent and pray in our hearts, if thoughts foreign to the affection of the one praying run in between, it cannot yet be said: *With my voice I have cried out to the Lord.* Nor is this rightly said, except when the soul alone, drawing into the prayer nothing of the flesh and nothing of carnal intentions, speaks to the Lord, where he alone hears; and this too is called a cry, because of the force of the intention itself. *And he heard me from his holy mountain*: we have it said through the Prophet that the Lord himself is the mountain, as it is written that a stone cut without hands grew to the size of a mountain. But this cannot be taken from his own person, unless perhaps he wished to say thus: He heard me from myself, as from his holy mountain, since he dwelt in me, that is, in the mountain itself. But it is plainer and more straightforward, if we take it that God heard from his righteousness: for it was just that he should raise from the dead the innocent one who was slain, and to whom evils were repaid for good, and that he should repay the persecutors with their deserts; for we read: *Your righteousness is like the mountains of God*[^8].

**5.** [v 6.] *I slept and took my rest.* It can not unfittingly be noted that what is put, *I*, is set down to signify that he endured death by his own will, according to that saying: *Therefore the Father loves me, because I lay down my soul, that I may take it again. No one takes it from me: I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again*[^9]: it is not, then, he says, that you took and killed me as though against my will, but *I slept and took my rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me.* Now the Scriptures contain innumerably that sleep is put for death, as the Apostle says: *I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, concerning those who have fallen asleep*[^10]. Nor should it be asked why it was added, *took my rest*, when he had already said, *I slept*; for the Scriptures have repetitions of this kind in customary use, as we showed many in the second psalm. Some copies, however, have *I slept, and was lulled into sleep*, and others otherwise; in the various ways they were able to interpret what was put in Greek, *egō de ekoimēthēn kai hypnōsa.* Unless perhaps the falling asleep may be taken of the one dying, but the sleep of the one dead; so that the falling asleep is that by which one passes into sleep, just as awakening is that by which one passes into being awake. Let us not suppose that these repetitions are in the divine books for the sake of empty ornaments of speech. So it is well taken: *I slept and took my rest*; I gave myself over to the passion, and death followed. *And I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me.* This is the more to be noticed, how in one sentence he put both a verb of past time and one of future: for he said *I rose up*, which is of the past; and *will sustain*, which is of the future; whereas he could not rise again except by that sustaining. But in prophecy future things are well mixed with past, so that both may be signified: because the things prophesied as to come are future according to time; but according to the knowledge of those prophesying, are already to be held as done. Verbs of the present time are also mixed in, which in their place, when they occur, will be treated.

**6.** [v 7.] *I will not fear the thousands of the people surrounding me*: it is written in the Gospel how great a multitude stood around him as he suffered and was crucified. *Rise up, Lord, save me, my God*: it is not to one sleeping or lying down that it is said to God, *rise up*; but it is the custom of the divine Scriptures to attribute to the person of God what he does in us; not indeed everywhere, but where it can fittingly be said, as when he himself is said to speak, when by his gift the Prophets or Apostles or any messengers of truth speak. Hence is that text: *Do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ?*[^11] For he did not say, of him by whose enlightening or command I speak; but he attributed the very speaking to him by whose gift he was speaking.

**7.** [v 8.] *For you have struck all who oppose me without cause*: this is not to be punctuated as though it were one sentence: *Rise up, Lord; save me, my God, for you have struck all who oppose me without cause*: for he does not save him because he struck his enemies; but rather, once he himself was saved, he struck them. Therefore it pertains to what follows; so that this is the sense: *For you have struck all who oppose me without cause, you have broken the teeth of sinners*; that is, from this you broke the teeth of sinners, because you struck all who oppose me. For the punishment of those who oppose is that by which their teeth were broken, that is, the words of the sinners tearing the Son of God with their curses, reduced to nothing, as it were to dust; so that we take "teeth" to be slanderous words, of which teeth the Apostle says: *But if you bite one another, take heed that you are not consumed by one another*[^12]. The teeth of sinners can also be taken as the chief men among sinners, by whose authority anyone is cut off from the society of those who live rightly, and as it were incorporated into those who live badly. Contrary to these teeth are the teeth of the Church, by whose authority believers are cut off from the error of the gentiles and of various doctrines, and transferred into that which is the body of Christ: with these teeth it was said to Peter that he should eat the slaughtered animals; that is, by killing in the gentiles what they were, and changing them into that which he himself was. And of these teeth of the Church it is said: *Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes coming up from the washing, which all bear twins, and there is none barren among them*[^13]. These are they who teach rightly, and live just as they teach; who do what is said: *Let your works shine before men, that they may bless your Father who is in heaven*[^14]: for moved by their authority, men believe in God who speaks and works through them, and, separated from the world to which they were conformed, pass into the members of the Church. And therefore those through whom these things are done are rightly called teeth, like sheep that have been shorn, because they have laid down the burdens of earthly cares, and coming up from the washing, from the cleansing of the filth of the world through the sacrament of baptism, all bear twins. For they fulfill the two precepts, of which it is said: *On these two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets hang*[^15], loving God with their whole heart, and with their whole soul, and with their whole mind; and their neighbor as themselves. Among whom there is none barren, since they render such fruits to God. According to this understanding, then, *you have broken the teeth of sinners* is to be taken thus; that is, you have brought to nothing the chief men among sinners, by striking all who oppose me without cause: for the chief men persecuted him, according to the Gospel history, while the lesser multitude honored him.

**8.** [v 9.] *Salvation is the Lord's, and your blessing is upon your people.* In one sentence he both instructed men in what they should believe, and prayed for believers: for when it is said, *Salvation is the Lord's*, the speech is directed to men; nor does it so follow, And his blessing is upon his people, as though the whole were said to men; but the prayer is turned to God himself, for that very people to whom it was said: *Salvation is the Lord's.* What, then, does he say, but this? Let no one presume upon himself, since it is the Lord's to save from the death of sins; for *Wretched man that I am, who will deliver me from the body of this death? The grace of God, through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^16]; but you, Lord, bless your people who hope for salvation from you.

**9.** This psalm can also be taken of the person of Christ in another way, that is, so that the whole speaks. I say the whole, that is, with his body of which he is the head, according to the Apostle who says: *But you are the body of Christ and its members*[^17]; therefore he is the head of this body. For which reason in another place he says: *But doing the truth in love, let us grow up in him in all things, who is the head, Christ, from whom the whole body is joined and knit together*[^18]. Therefore in the Prophet the Church speaks together with its head, set amid the storms of persecutions throughout the whole world, which we know has already come to pass: *Lord, why are they multiplied who trouble me? Many rise up against me*, desiring to wipe out the Christian name. *Many say to my soul: There is no salvation for him in his God*: for they would not otherwise hope that they could destroy the Church spreading far and wide, unless they did not believe that it pertained to God's care. *But you, Lord, are my upholder*: in Christ surely; for in that man the Church too was taken up by the Word, which was made flesh and dwelt among us; because he also made us sit in the heavenly places together with him: for the head going before, the rest of the members will follow; for who will separate us from the love of Christ? Rightly, then, the Church too says: *You are my upholder, my glory*; for it does not attribute to itself that in which it excels, since it understands by whose grace and mercy it is such. *And the one who lifts up my head*, namely him who, the firstborn from the dead, ascended into heaven. *With my voice I have cried out to the Lord, and he heard me from his holy mountain.* This is the prayer of all the saints, the odor of sweetness that ascends in the sight of the Lord: for now the Church is heard from that mountain, which is also its head; or from that righteousness of God, by which his elect are set free, and their persecutors are punished. Let the people of God say also this: *I slept and took my rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me*, so that it may be joined and cleave to its head; for to this people it was said: *Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead, and Christ will reach you*[^19]; since it was taken from sinners, of whom it was said in general: *But those who sleep, sleep by night*[^20]. Let it say also: *I will not fear the thousands of the people surrounding me*; namely of the surrounding nations, to wipe out the Christian name, if they could, wherever it might be. But how could they be feared, when the ardor of love in Christ was kindled by the blood of the martyrs as though by oil? *Rise up, Lord, save me, my God*: the body can say this to its own head; for at his rising it was saved, who ascended on high, led captivity captive, gave gifts to men: for this is said in predestination by the Prophet, by which our Lord brought down to the earth that ripe harvest of which it is spoken in the Gospel, whose salvation is in his resurrection, who deigned to die for us. *For you have struck all who oppose me without cause, you have broken the teeth of sinners*: now that the Church reigns, the enemies of the Christian name have been struck with confusion, and whether their slanderous words or their authority have been brought to nothing. Believe, then, men, that *Salvation is the Lord's*: and, may your blessing, Lord, be *upon your people.*

**10.** Each one of us can also say, when the multitude of vices and desires leads the resisting mind into the law of sin: *Lord, why are they multiplied who trouble me, many rise up against me?* And since for the most part, by the heaping up of vices, despair of health creeps in, as though the vices themselves were insulting the soul, or even the devil and his angels were working through harmful suggestions that we should despair, most truly it is said: *Many say to my soul: There is no salvation for him in his God. But you, Lord, are my upholder*; for this is the hope, that he deigned to take up human nature in Christ. *My glory*: from that rule, that no one attribute anything to himself. *And the one who lifts up my head*: whether him who is the head of us all, or the spirit of each one of us, which is the head of the soul and the flesh; for *the head of the woman is the man, and the head of the man is Christ*[^21]. Now the mind is lifted up when it can already say: *With the mind I serve the law of God*[^22]; so that the rest of man, being made peaceful, may be subjected, when, at the resurrection of the flesh, death is swallowed up in victory. *With my voice I have cried out to the Lord*: with that inmost and most intent voice. *And he heard me from his holy mountain*: from him through whom he came to our aid, and through whom as mediator he hears us. *I slept and took my rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me*: which of the faithful cannot say this, recalling the death of his sins, and the gift of regeneration? *I will not fear the thousands of the people surrounding me.* Besides those things which the Church has endured and endures universally, each one also has temptations by which he is hemmed in, so that he may say this: *Rise up, Lord, save me, my God*; that is: Make me rise up. *For you have struck all who oppose me without cause*: rightly it is said in predestination of the devil and his angels, who rage not only against the whole body of Christ, but also against each one privately. *You have broken the teeth of sinners*: each one has those who slander him; he also has the authors of vices trying to cut him off from the body of Christ. But *Salvation is the Lord's*; pride is to be guarded against, and it is to be said: *My soul has clung close after you*[^23]. *And your blessing is upon your people*, that is, upon each one of us.
