# On the Same Psalm 18

**1.** Having prayed the Lord to cleanse us from our hidden things, and to spare his servants from alien things, we ought to understand what this is, so that we may sing with human reason, not as it were with the voice of birds. For blackbirds and parrots and crows and magpies and birds of this kind are often taught by men to sound what they do not know. But to sing knowingly was granted to the nature of man by the divine will. And how many evil and dissolute men so sing things worthy of their own ears and hearts, we know and grieve. For they are the worse, in that they cannot be ignorant of what they sing. For they know that they sing shameful things, and yet they sing the more gladly, the more uncleanly: since they think themselves the merrier, the baser they have been. But we who in the Church have learned to sing the divine words, ought at the same time also to strive to be what is written, *Blessed is the people that understands jubilation*[^1]. Accordingly, dearly beloved, what we have sung with harmonious voice, we ought also with a serene heart to know and to see. For each one of us prayed the Lord in this canticle, and said to God: *From my hidden things cleanse me, Lord, and from alien things spare your servant. If they will not have dominated me, then I will be immaculate, and I will be cleansed from a great transgression.* That we may know well what this is, and of what sort, let us briefly run through the text of the psalm itself, as far as the Lord grants.

**2.** For it is sung of Christ; which appears evidently there, because it is there written: *he himself like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber.* For who is the bridegroom, but he to whom that virgin was betrothed by the Apostle, for whom the chaste friend of the chaste bridegroom fears, lest, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, so the senses of this virgin, the bride of Christ, be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ? In this our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, then, there is set great and full grace, of which the apostle John says: *And we saw his glory, glory as of the Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth*[^2]. This *glory the heavens declare*: the heavens are the saints; lifted up from the earth, bearing the Lord. Although heaven too in a manner declared the glory of Christ: when did it declare it? when, at the birth of that same Lord, a new star which was never seen appeared. But yet there are truer and more sublime heavens, of which it is consequently said there: *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard. Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the world their words.* Whose, but the heavens'? whose, then, but the Apostles'? They declare to us the glory of God, set in Christ Jesus, through grace unto the remission of sins. For all have sinned, and lack the glory of God, justified freely by his blood. Because freely, therefore grace; for it is not grace, if it is not gratuitous. Because we had done nothing good before, by which we might deserve such gifts; rather, because punishment would not be inflicted freely, therefore the benefit was bestowed freely: nothing had gone before in our merits, except that for which we deserved to be condemned. But he, not on account of our righteousness, but on account of his mercy, saved us through the washing of regeneration. This, I say, is the glory of God: this the heavens declared. This, I say, is the glory of God, not yours: for you did nothing good, and yet you received so great a good. If, then, you belong to the glory which the heavens declared, say to the Lord your God: *My God, his mercy will go before me*[^3]: for it has gone before you; surely it has gone before, because it found nothing good in you. You went before his punishment by being proud: he went before your punishment by blotting out your sins. For as one justified from a sinner, made pious from impious, taken up into the kingdom from being condemned, say to the Lord your God: *Not to us, Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory*[^4]. Let us say, *Not to us*: for to whom, if as it were to us? Let us say, I say, *Not to us*, because if he did to us as to us, he would inflict nothing but punishments on us. Not to us, but to his name let him give glory: because he did not do to us according to our iniquities. *Not*, then, *to us, Lord, not to us*: the repetition is a confirmation. *Not to us, Lord, but to your name give glory*: this the heavens knew, who declared the glory of God.

**3.** [v 2.] *And the firmament proclaims the works of his hands*: what was said, *the glory of God*, this is repeated, *the works of his hands.* What are the works of his hands? Not as some suppose, that God made all things by word, and as it were made man, as more excellent than the rest, by his own hands. It is not so to be supposed: that opinion is weak, and not sufficiently refined; for he made all things by word. For although the works of God are narrated as diverse, among which he made man to his own image; yet all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. But as to what pertains to the hands of God, both of the heavens it was said, *And the heavens are the works of your hands*[^5]; and lest you suppose that there too the holy ones were called heavens, he added: *They will perish, but you remain*[^6]. Therefore not only men, but even the heavens which will perish, God made with his hands, to whom it was said, *The heavens are the works of your hands;* and of the earth this same thing was said: *For the sea is his, and he made it, and his hands founded the dry land*[^7]. Therefore if he made both the heavens with his hands and the earth with his hands, he made not man alone with his hands: and if the heavens by word, and the earth by word, then man too by word. What he did by word, this by hand; and what by hand, this by word: for the stature of God is not distinguished by human members, who is wholly everywhere, and contained by no place; what, then, he made by word, he made by wisdom, and what he made by hand, he made by power. But Christ is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God: and all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. The heavens declared, declare, and will declare the glory of God: the heavens, I say, that is, the saints, suspended from the earth, will declare the glory of God, bearing God, thundering with precepts, flashing with wisdom; that glory of God, as I said, by which we, unworthy, were saved. This unworthiness, that is, that of which we were not worthy, the younger son, bound by want, recognizes: he recognizes, I say, this unworthiness, the younger son, a wanderer far from the father, a worshiper of demons, as it were a herder of swine; he recognizes the glory of God, but bound by want. And because by that glory of God we have become what we were not worthy to be, he says to his Father: *I am not worthy to be called your son*[^8]: the unhappy one, through humility, obtains happiness; and he shows himself worthy by that very thing by which he confesses himself unworthy. This *glory of God the heavens declare, and the firmament proclaims the works of his hands*: the heaven the firmament, a firm heart, not a fearful heart. For these things were proclaimed among the impious, among those opposed to God, among the lovers of the world and persecutors of the righteous: among the raging world these things were proclaimed. But what could the raging world do, when the firmament proclaimed these things? *The firmament proclaims*: what? *The works of his hands.* What are the works of his hands? That glory of God by which we were saved, by which we were created in good works: for we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus in good works; for not only men, but even the righteous, if indeed we are such, he himself made us, and not we ourselves.

**4.** [v 3.] *Day to day pours forth word, and night to night proclaims knowledge*: what is this? Perhaps it is plain and open, *Day to day pours forth word*; open and plain, as it were by day: but that *night to night proclaims knowledge* is obscure, as it were by night. *Day to day*, holy ones to holy ones, Apostles to the faithful, Christ himself to the Apostles, to whom he said: *You are the light of the world*[^9]. This seems open and easy to know. But how does *night to night proclaim knowledge?* Some have understood these words simply, and perhaps this may be true; estimating that by this sentence it was signified that what the Apostles heard in the time of our Lord Jesus Christ, when he was on earth, was passed on to those who came after, as it were from time to time: *day to day, night to night*, the former day to the later day, and the former night to the later night; because this teaching is preached by days and by nights. Let this simple understanding suffice for whom it suffices. But certain words of the Scriptures have profited by their obscurity, in that they have brought forth many understandings: and so, if this were plain, you would hear some one thing; but because it was said obscurely, you are about to hear many things. There is also another understanding: *day to day, night to night*, that is, spirit to spirit, flesh to flesh. There is another: *day to day*, spiritual ones to spiritual ones; *and night to night*, carnal ones to carnal ones. For both hear, even if both do not savor alike: for the former hear it as a word poured forth; the latter as knowledge proclaimed: for what is poured forth is poured forth to those present; but what is proclaimed is proclaimed to those set far off. Several senses can be found, but a limit is to be set on account of the straits of the present time. But let us say also some one thing, which certain ones, as it were conjecturing, have opened up: when the Lord Christ, they say, spoke to the Apostles, day poured forth word to day; when Judas betrayed the Lord Christ to the Jews, night proclaimed knowledge to night.

**5.** [vv 5.4.] *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard*: whose, but those heavens' who declare the glory of God? *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard.* Read the Acts of the Apostles, how, the Holy Spirit coming upon them, they were all filled with him; and they spoke in the tongues of all nations, as the Spirit gave them to speak. Behold, *there are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard*: but they sounded not only there where they were filled; *Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the world their words.* Therefore we too here speak: for that sound has reached even to us, the sound which has gone out into all the earth, and the heretic does not enter the Church. Therefore the sound has gone out into all the earth, that you may enter heaven. O pestilent, contentious, most wicked one, and still wishing to err; O proud son, hear the testament of your Father. Behold, what plainer, what more open? *Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the world their words.* Is there need of an expositor? Why do you strive against yourself? Why do you wish to retain a part in litigation, who can retain the whole in concord?

**6.** [v 6.] *In the sun he has set his tabernacle*: in manifestation his Church, not in secret, not one that lies hidden, not as if covered; lest perhaps it become as one covered over the flocks of the heretics. It was said also to someone in the holy Scripture, *Because you did it in secret, you will suffer in the sun*[^10]: that is, you did evil secretly, you will suffer punishments in the manifestation of all. *In the sun*, then, *he has set his tabernacle.* Why do you, heretic, flee into the darkness? Are you a Christian? hear Christ. Are you a servant? hear the Lord. Are you a son? hear the Father: be amended, come back to life. Let us say of you too: *He was dead, and has come back to life; he was lost, and is found*[^11]. Do not say to me, Why do you seek me, if I have perished? For I seek you for this reason, because you have perished. Do not, he says, seek me: this indeed the iniquity by which we are divided wishes; but the love by which we are brothers does not wish it. I would not be wicked, if I sought my servant; and am I called wicked, because I seek my brother? So let him think in whom there is no brotherly love, yet I seek my brother. Let him be angry, provided only he be sought, who, when found, is appeased. I seek, I say, my brother, and I intercede not against him, but for him with my Lord: nor will I say, interceding, Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me; but, tell my brother to hold the inheritance with me. Why, then, do you err, brother? why do you flee through the corners? why do you try to lie hidden? *In the sun he has set his tabernacle. And he himself like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber.* I think you recognize him. He like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, *he has exulted like a giant to run his way*; he *in the sun has set his tabernacle*: that is, he like a bridegroom, when the Word was made flesh, found a chamber in the virginal womb; and thence joined to human nature, as it were coming forth from a most chaste couch, humble in mercy below all, strong in majesty above all. For this is, *the giant exulted to run his way*: he was born, grew, taught, suffered, rose again, ascended; he ran his way, he did not stick in the way. The same bridegroom, then, who did these things, himself set *in the sun*, that is, in manifestation, *his tabernacle*, that is, his holy Church.

**7.** [v 7.] But do you wish to hear how quickly he ran his way? *From the height of heaven is his going forth, and his meeting unto the height of heaven.* But after he ran out from there, and by running back returned, he sent his Spirit. There were seen, upon those over whom he came, divided tongues as it were of fire. Like fire the Holy Spirit came, to consume the hay of the flesh, to cook and purify the gold; like fire he came, and therefore it follows, *And there is none who can hide himself from his heat.*

**8.** [v 8.] *The law of the Lord is immaculate, converting souls.* This is the Holy Spirit. *The testimony of the Lord is faithful, giving wisdom to little ones*: not to the proud. This is the Holy Spirit.

**9.** [v 9.] *The acts of righteousness of the Lord are upright*: not terrifying, but *gladdening hearts.* This is the Holy Spirit. *The precept of the Lord is clear, enlightening the eyes*, not dulling them: not the eyes of the flesh, but of the heart; not of the outer man, but of the inner. This is the Holy Spirit.

**10.** [v 10.] *The fear of the Lord*: not servile, but *chaste*; loving freely, fearing not to be punished by him whom it dreads, but to be separated from him whom it loves. This is the chaste fear, not that which perfected love casts out; but *remaining unto the age of the age.* This is the Holy Spirit; that is, this he gives, this he confers, this he implants, the Holy Spirit. *The judgments of the Lord are true, justified in themselves*: not for the quarrels of division, but for the gathering of unity; for this is, *in themselves.* This is the Holy Spirit. Therefore he made those upon whom he first came to speak in the tongues of all; because he announced that he would gather the tongues of all nations into unity. What one man did then, having received the Holy Spirit, so that one man spoke in the tongues of all; this same thing that very unity now does, it speaks in all tongues. And now one man in all nations speaks in all tongues, one man head and body, one man Christ and the Church, a perfect man, that bridegroom, that bride: but *they will be*, he says, *two in one flesh*[^12]. *The judgments of God are true, justified in themselves*: on account of unity.

**11.** [v 11.] *Desirable above gold and a precious stone, much*: either much gold, or much precious, or much desirable; yet much, to the heretic little. They do not love unity with us, and yet with us they confess Christ. The very Christ whom you confess with me, love him with me. And he who does not wish unity refuses, kicks back, spurns: this is not desirable to him above gold and a precious stone, much. Hear another thing: *And sweeter*, he says, *above honey and the honeycomb.* But this is adverse to the one erring: honey is bitter to the feverish, yet sweet and acceptable to the healed, because it is dear to health. *Desirable above gold and a precious stone, much, and sweeter above honey and the honeycomb.*

**12.** [v 12.] *For your servant too keeps them*: how sweet these things are, your servant proves by keeping them, not by speaking. Your servant keeps them, because they are both now sweet, and hereafter wholesome; for *in keeping them is much reward*: but loving his own animosity, the heretic neither sees this splendor, nor feels the sweetness.

**13.** [vv 13.14.] For *who understands transgressions?* Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do. Therefore, he says, this is the servant who keeps this sweetness, the pleasantness of love, the love of unity. I, he says, the very one who keep them, ask you, since *who understands transgressions?* lest certain things creep upon me as a man, and I be taken unawares by certain things as a man, *From my hidden things cleanse me, Lord.* This, then, we have sung; behold, by speaking we have come to this. Let us say it, and with understanding let us sing it, and by singing let us pray, and by praying let us obtain: let us say, *From my hidden things cleanse me, Lord.* For who understands transgressions? If they are seen as darkness, transgressions are understood. In short, when we repent of a transgression, we are in the light. For when anyone is wrapped up in the transgression itself, he does not see the transgression, as it were with his eyes darkened and covered: for if even the eye of your flesh is bound, you neither see anything else, nor do you see that by which it is covered. Therefore let us say to God, who knows how to see what he may purify, knows how to look into what he may heal; let us say to him: *From my hidden things cleanse me, Lord; and from alien things spare your servant.* My transgressions, he says, defile me, the transgressions of others afflict me: from these cleanse me, from those spare. Take from my heart the evil thought, drive from me the evil persuader: this is, *From my hidden things cleanse me, and from alien things spare your servant.* For these two kinds of transgressions, one's own together with alien, were also manifest at the very first beginning: the devil fell by his own transgression, and cast down Adam by an alien one. This same servant of God, keeping the judgments of God, in which is much reward, in another psalm prays thus: *Let not the foot of pride come to me, and let not the hands of sinners move me*[^13]. Let not, he says, the foot of pride come to me, this is, *From my hidden things cleanse me, Lord;* and let not the hands of sinners move me, this is, *From alien things spare your servant.*

**14.** *If they will not have dominated me*: my hidden things and the transgressions of others. *Then I will be immaculate*: this he dares not by his own strength, but prays the Lord to fulfill, to whom it is said in the psalm: *Direct my journeys according to your word, and let no iniquity dominate me*[^14]. Do not fear, if you are a Christian, an outward man as lord: fear always the Lord your God. Fear the evil in you, that is, your own desire, not what God made in you, but what you yourself made for yourself: the Lord made you a good servant; you created for yourself in your heart an evil lord. Deservedly you are subjected to iniquity, deservedly you are subjected to the lord whom you yourself made for yourself; because you refused to be subject to him who made you.

**15.** But *if my hidden things*, he says, *will not have dominated me, then I will be immaculate; and I will be cleansed from a great transgression*: of what transgression do we suppose? what is that great transgression? Perhaps it is other than what I am about to say, yet I will not hide what I think. I judge that the great transgression is pride. This perhaps is also signified in another way in what he says, *And I will be cleansed from a great transgression.* Do you ask how great this transgression is, which cast down an angel, which from an angel made a devil, and shut off from him the kingdom of heaven forever? This is a great transgression, and the head and cause of all transgressions; for it is written: *The beginning of all sin is pride.* And lest you despise it as something light, *The beginning*, he says, *of man's pride is to apostatize from God*[^15]. This vice is no light evil, my brothers: to this vice, in those persons whom you see eminent, Christian humility is displeasing. On account of this vice they disdain to subject their necks to the yoke of Christ, bound the more tightly by the yoke of sin. For it will not befall them not to serve: for they refuse to serve, but it is expedient for them to serve. By refusing to serve they do nothing else but not serve a good Lord; not that they do not serve at all: because whoever refuses to serve love must needs serve iniquity. From this vice, which is the head of all vices, because from it the rest of the vices were born, the apostasy from God came about, the soul going into darkness, and ill using free choice, the rest of the sins also following: so that it both dissipated its substance, living prodigally with harlots, and through want became a herder of swine, who had been a companion of the angels. On account of this vice, on account of this great sin of pride, the humble God came: this cause, this great sin, this huge disease of souls, brought down the almighty physician from heaven, humbled him even to the form of a servant, drove him with insults, hung him on the wood; that through the saving power of so great a medicine this swelling might be cured. Now at last let man be ashamed to be proud, on account of whom God became humble. So, he says, *I will be cleansed from a great transgression*, because God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.

**16.** [v 15.] *And through this, that the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, may be well-pleasing in your sight always*: for if I will not be cleansed from this great transgression, my words will be well-pleasing in the sight of men, not in your sight. A proud soul wishes to be well-pleasing in the sight of men: a humble soul wishes to be well-pleasing in secret, where God sees; so that if it pleases men by a good work, it congratulates those to whom the good work is pleasing, not itself, to whom it ought to be enough that it did the good work: *For our glory*, he says, *is this, the testimony of our conscience*[^16]. And therefore let us say also what follows, *Lord, my helper and my redeemer*: a helper in good things, a redeemer from evils; a helper that I may dwell in your love, a redeemer that you may set me free from my iniquity.
