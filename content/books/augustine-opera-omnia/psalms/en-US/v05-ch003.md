# Exposition of Psalm 101

**1.** [v. 1.] Behold, a poor man prays, and does not pray in silence. It is permitted, then, to hear him, and to see who he is: lest perhaps he be the one of whom the Apostle says, *Who for your sake became poor, though he was rich, that by his poverty you might be enriched*[^1]. If, then, it is he, how is he poor? For how he is rich, who does not see? For from what are men rich? From gold, I suppose, silver, household, land: but *all things were made through him*[^2]. What, then, is richer than he, through whom riches were made—even those that are not true riches? For through him are even those riches, talent, memory, character, life, the very health of the body, the senses, and the conformation of the limbs: for when these are sound, even the poor are rich. Through him are also those greater riches, faith, piety, righteousness, love, chastity, good character: for no one has even these except through him who justifies the impious. Behold how rich. For who is rich—the one who has what he wants, another making it, or the one who makes what he wants, another having it? I suppose the richer is the one who made what you have; because what he has, you do not have. Behold how rich. In one so rich, from what are we to recognize these words: *I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping*[^3]? Have such great riches come to this? That is very lofty, this very abject. What shall we do? how shall we attune these depths to those heights? They are too far from one another. I do not yet recognize this poor man; perhaps it is another: but let us still seek. For from the fact that he himself does not seem to us this poor man, it is a wonder if you inquire and do not tremble at the riches: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^4]. He who said these things, when he said them, was rich; how much more he of whom he spoke: *In the beginning was the Word*: and not just any Word, but *the Word God*; and not anywhere, but *with God*; and not idle, but *all things were made through him?* Did he eat ashes like bread, and mingle his drink with weeping? We must fear lest our poverty do injury to such great riches. Yet still seek whether he is this poor man; for *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^5]. Look too at that voice: *I am your servant and the son of your handmaid*[^6]. Consider that handmaid, chaste, and virgin, and mother: for there he received our poverty, where he was clothed in the form of a servant, emptying himself; lest you should tremble at his riches, and not dare to approach him with your beggary. There, I say, he received the form of a servant, there he was clothed in our poverty; there he made himself poor, there he enriched us. Now, then, we draw near to understanding these things of him: yet still we must not pronounce rashly. The birth of the virgin is the stone cut from the mountain without hands, where no man worked, no desire was poured in, but faith alone was kindled, and the flesh of the Word was conceived. Then he came forth from the womb; the heavens spoke, the angels announced to the shepherds, the star drew the magi to adore the king, Simeon, filled with the Spirit, recognized the infant God in his mother's hands. Age came on—not to the divinity, but to the flesh. The unlearned old men shudder and marvel at the wisdom of the twelve-year-old boy. Or even if they were learned old men, what is their learning to the Word of God? what is their learning to the Wisdom of God? Are not even the learned, unless he comes to their aid, surely going to perish? He still grows in age of body: he comes to the river to be baptized; the baptizer recognizes God, confesses himself unworthy to loose the strap of his sandal. Now from there the blind are enlightened, hearing is opened to the deaf, the mute speak, lepers are cleansed, the paralyzed are braced up, the sick recover, the dead rise.

**2.** Now indeed, in comparison with that Word through whom all things were made, I recognize the poverty of the riches; but how far still from ashes and weeping with drink? Still I fear to say: It is he; and yet I wish to. For there are here things that compel me to wish, and again certain things that compel me to fear. It is he, and it is not he. Now he is in the form of a servant, now he bears mortal and fragile flesh, now he comes about to die, and yet he is not yet understood in this destitution. *I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping.* Let him, then, add poverty to poverty, and transfigure into himself the body of our lowliness: let him be our head, let us be his members, let the two be in one flesh. For now, that he might first be poor, taking the form of a servant, he left the Father: but in that he was born of the virgin, let him leave the mother too, and cleave to his wife, and let the two be in one flesh. For so they will be two even in one voice, and in that one voice we shall no longer wonder at our voice: *I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping.* For he deigned to have us as members. There are penitents too among his members. For they are not shut out and separated from his Church: nor would he at all join a spouse to himself, except by that voice, *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^7]. Now, then, let us hear what the head and the body, the bridegroom and the bride, Christ and the Church, both one, pray: but the Word and the flesh are not both one; the Father and the Word are both one; Christ and the Church are both one, a certain one perfect man in the form of his fullness: *Until we all meet in the unity of faith, in the knowledge of the Son of God, in the perfect man, in the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ*[^8]. But until we meet, here our poverty is carried on, here still labor and groaning are carried on. Thanks to his mercy. Whence labor, whence groaning to the Word, through whom all things were made? If he deigned to have our death, will he not give us his life? He has lifted us into a great hope, with a great hope we groan. Groaning has sadness; but there is a groaning that has joy too. I think Sarah, barren, groaned joyfully when she gave birth: and we, from your fear we have conceived and brought forth the spirit of salvation. Let us hear, then, Christ poor in us and with us, and for our sake. For the very title indicates one who is poor. In short, suppose me to have surmised who this poor man is; let us hear the prayer, and recognize the person: lest you err anywhere, when you hear something that cannot be fitted to his head; therefore I have spoken beforehand, that when you hear such a thing, you may notice that it sounds from the weakness of the body, and recognize the voice of the members in the head. *The prayer of the needy*, this is what the title has, *when he was in distress, and poured out his prayer in the sight of the Lord.* He is himself that poor man, who elsewhere says, *From the ends of the earth I cried to you, when my heart was in distress*[^9]. This is the same poor man, because he is the same Christ; who in the prophet called himself both bridegroom and bride: *As on a bridegroom he set a miter on me; and as a bride he clothed me with ornament*[^10]. He called himself bridegroom, he called himself bride; whence this, except that he is bridegroom because of the head, bride because of the body? The voice, then, is one, because the flesh is one. Let us hear, and rather in these voices let us too recognize ourselves; and if we see ourselves to be outside, let us labor to be there.

**3.** [vv. 2, 3.] *Hear, O Lord, my prayer, and let my cry come to you.* This is, *Hear, O Lord, my prayer*; which is, *let my cry come to you*: in the doubling is the feeling of one who asks. *Do not turn your face away from me.* When does God turn away from the Son? when the Father from Christ? But because of the poverty of the members: *Do not turn your face away from me. On whatever day I am afflicted, incline your ear to me.* For I am afflicted below; but you are above: if I lift myself up, you become far off; if I humble myself, you incline your ear to me. But what is it, *On whatever day I am afflicted?* Is he not now afflicted? or would he say these things unless he were afflicted? It would be enough, then: Incline your ear to me, for I am afflicted. *On whatever day I am afflicted, incline your ear to me*: as the unity of the body; if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it. You are afflicted today, I am afflicted; another is afflicted tomorrow, I am afflicted; after this generation other descendants, who succeed descendants, are afflicted, I am afflicted; even to the end of the age, whoever in my body are afflicted, I am afflicted. *On whatever* day, then, *I am afflicted, incline your ear to me. On whatever day I call upon you, hear me quickly.* This is the same thing. Even now I call upon you; but, *On whatever day I call upon you, hear me quickly.* Peter prayed, Paul prayed, the other Apostles prayed; the faithful prayed in those times, the faithful prayed in the following times, the faithful prayed in the times of the martyrs, the faithful pray in our times, the faithful will pray in the times of those to come: *On whatever day I call upon you, hear me quickly. Hear me quickly*: for now I ask what you wish to give. Not earthly things, like an earthly man, but, redeemed now from the first captivity, I desire the kingdom of heaven; *Hear me quickly*: for it was only to such desire that you said, *While you are still speaking, I will say: Behold, I am here*[^11]. *On whatever day I call upon you, hear me quickly.* Whence do you call? from what affliction? from what destitution? O poor man before the door of God the rich, with what desire do you beg? with what want do you seek? with what destitution do you knock, that it may be opened to you? Speak, let us hear that very want; in it let us find ourselves too, and ask with you. Hear, and recognize, if you can.

**4.** [v. 4.] *For my days have failed like smoke.* O days! if days: for where day is heard, light is understood. *But my days have failed like smoke. My days*, my times: whence *like smoke*, except because of the elation of pride? Such days the proud Adam was worthy to receive, from whom Christ received flesh. So Christ is in Adam, and Adam in Christ. He freed indeed even from the days of smoke, who deigned to have the voice of the days of smoke. *For my days have failed like smoke.* See the smoke, like to pride, rising, swelling, vanishing: rightly, then, failing, certainly not remaining. *For my days have failed like smoke; and my bones are scorched as in a frying pan.* Even my very bones, even my very strength, not without tribulation, not without burning. The bones of the body of Christ, the strength of the body of Christ, where greater than in the holy Apostles? And yet see the bones fried: *Who is made to stumble, and I am not set on fire?*[^12] They are strong, faithful, good understanders and preachers of the word, living as they speak, speaking as they hear: they are plainly strong, but all who suffer scandals are their frying pan. For there is love there, and the more so in the bones. The bones are more inward than all the flesh, and they bear all the flesh. But if anyone suffers some scandal, and is endangered in soul; the bone is fried in proportion as it loves. Let love be absent, no one is fried: let love be present, and if one member suffers along when one member suffers; how are those fried who bear all the members? *My bones are scorched as in a frying pan.*

**5.** [v. 5.] *I am struck like hay, and my heart has withered.* Look back to Adam, whence the human race. For whence, except from him, was misery propagated? whence, except from him, this hereditary poverty? Let him, then, say, now set in the body of Christ with hope, who once in his own body was without hope: *I am struck like hay, and my heart has withered.* Rightly, since all flesh is hay. But yet whence did this befall you? *Because I forgot to eat my bread.* For God had given the bread of the commandment. For what is the bread of the soul, except the word of God? At the serpent's prompting, the woman transgressing, he touched the forbidden thing, he forgot the commandment: rightly he was struck like hay, and his heart withered, because he forgot to eat his bread. Forgetting to eat the bread, he drank poison; his heart was struck, and withered like hay. He is the one struck in Isaiah, of whom it is said, and to whom it is said: *I will not be angry with you for an everlasting time: for the spirit shall go forth from me, and I have made every breath. For a little sin I grieved him, and struck him, and turned my face from him*[^13]. Rightly here, *Do not turn your face away from me*: that is, from the one struck, of whom you said, *I struck him*; of whom you said, *I saw his ways, and I healed him*[^14]. *I am struck like hay, and my heart has withered, because I forgot to eat my bread.* Now eat the one you had forgotten. But the bread itself has come too, in whose body you may recall the voice of your forgetfulness, and cry out from poverty, that you may take up riches. Now eat; for you are in the body of him who says, *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^15]. You had forgotten to eat your bread; but now that he is crucified, *all the ends of the earth shall be reminded and shall be converted to the Lord*[^16]. After forgetfulness let remembrance come, let the bread from heaven be eaten, that one may live; not manna, as they ate and died: bread of which it is said, *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness*[^17].

**6.** [v. 6.] *From the voice of my groaning my bones have clung to my flesh.* From the voice I understand, from the voice I know: *From the voice of my groaning*, not from the voice of the groaning of those with whom I suffer. For many groan, and I groan too; and I groan at this, that they groan badly. He lost a coin, he groans; he lost faith, he does not groan: I weigh the coin and the faith, and I find a greater groaning over the one who groans badly, or does not groan. He commits a fraud, and rejoices. By what gain? by what loss? He acquired money, he lost righteousness. From this groans the one who knows how to groan; the one who is near the head, who rightly clings to the body of Christ, groans from this. But the carnal do not groan from this, and make themselves to be groaned over, because they do not groan from this: nor can we scorn them, whether not groaning or groaning badly. For we wish to correct them, we wish to amend them, we wish to restore them: and when we cannot, we groan; and when we groan, we are not separated from them. For *From the voice of my groaning my bones have clung to my flesh*: the strong clung to the weak, the firm to the infirm. Whence did they cling? From the voice of their own groaning, not from the voice of the groaning of those others. By what law did they cling, except that by which it is said, *For we who are firm ought to bear the weakness of the infirm?*[^18] *My bones have clung to my flesh.*

**7.** [vv. 7, 8.] *I have become like the pelican that dwells in the wilderness; I have become like the night raven in the ruined walls. I kept watch, and I have become like a solitary sparrow on the roof.* Behold three birds, and three places; may the Lord grant that we say what they mean, and that you hear usefully what is said wholesomely. What do the three birds and three places mean? Which three birds? The pelican, the night raven, and the sparrow: and three places, the wilderness, the ruined walls, and the roof. The pelican in the wilderness, the night raven in the ruined walls, the sparrow on the roof. First, what the pelican is must be said. For it is born in that region, so that this bird is unknown to us. It is born in wildernesses, chiefly of the river Nile, in Egypt. Whatever this bird may be, let us look at what the psalm wished to say of it. *It dwells*, it says, *in the wilderness.* Why do you seek its form, its limbs, its voice, its habits? As much as the psalm tells you, it is a bird dwelling in the wilderness. The night raven, a bird that loves night too. Those are called ruined walls which we commonly call ruins, where walls stand without a roof, without inhabitants: there dwells the night raven. And what the sparrow and the roof are, you already know. I find, then, someone of the body of Christ, a preacher of the word, suffering with the weak, seeking the gains of Christ, mindful of his Lord who is to come, lest he should say, *Wicked and lazy servant, you should have given my money to the bankers*[^19]. From the office of this steward let us look at these three. He has come among some where there are no Christians; he is a pelican in the wilderness: he has come to those who once were and have fallen, he is a night raven in the ruined walls; for he does not desert even the darkness of those who dwell in night, and wishes to win these too: he has come to those who are indeed Christians dwelling in a house, not as those who have not believed, or who have abandoned what they believed, but who walk lukewarmly in what they believe; the sparrow cries out to them, not in the wilderness, because they are Christians; nor in the ruined walls, because they have not fallen; but they are on the roof; or rather under the roof, because they are under the flesh. That sparrow cries out above the flesh, he is not silent about the precepts of God, nor does he become carnal, so as to be subjected to the roof. *For he who is on the roof, let him not come down to take anything out of the house*[^20]; and, *What you hear in the ear, preach upon the housetops*[^21]. These three birds and three places; and one man can have the persons of the three birds, and three men can have the persons of the three birds; and three kinds of places, there are three kinds of men; yet the wilderness, the ruined walls, and the roof are nothing but three kinds of men.

**8.** But why so much of these? Let us look at the Lord himself, lest perhaps it be he, and let him be better recognized, both as the pelican in the wilderness, and the night raven in the ruined walls, and the solitary sparrow on the roof. Let this poor man, our head, tell us: poor by will, let him speak to the poor in their need. For what is said, or even read, about this bird, that is, the pelican, let us not pass over in silence; not rashly affirming anything, but yet not being silent about what those who wrote it wished to be read and said. Hear it in such a way that, if it is true, it agrees; if it is false, it does not hold. These birds are said to kill their little ones as it were with blows of their beaks, and to mourn over the same, killed by themselves, in the nest for three days: finally they say that the mother gravely wounds herself, and pours out her own blood over her offspring, and they, drenched with it, come back to life. Perhaps this is true, perhaps it is false: yet if it is true, see how it agrees with him who gave us life by his own blood. It agrees with him that the mother's flesh gives life to her offspring by her own blood; it agrees well enough. For he himself too calls himself a hen over her chicks: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, how often have I wished to gather your children, as a hen her chicks under her wings, and you would not?*[^22] For he has paternal authority, maternal affection: just as Paul too is both father and mother; not through himself, but through the Gospel: father where he says, *Even if you have many tutors in Christ, yet not many fathers; for in Christ Jesus through the Gospel I begot you*[^23]; but mother where he says, *My little children, of whom I am again in labor, until Christ be formed in you*[^24]. This bird, then, if it is truly so, has a great likeness to the flesh of Christ, by whose blood we have been given life. But how does it agree with Christ that the bird itself kills its own offspring? Or does this too not agree with him: *I will kill, and I will make alive; I will strike, and I will heal?*[^25] Or would Saul the persecutor have died, unless he had been struck from heaven; or would the preacher have been roused, unless he had been given life by his blood? But let those who wrote this see to it; we ought not to set our understanding upon what is uncertain. Let us rather recognize this bird in the wilderness; for it is this that the psalm wished to set down: *The pelican in the wilderness.* I think Christ born of the virgin is here understood. For he alone was so born, hence the wilderness: born in the wilderness, because he alone was so born. After the nativity it came to the passion: by whom was he crucified? was it by those who stood firm? was it by those who mourned? Therefore, as in the night of their ignorance, and as in the ruined walls of their downfall. Behold the night raven, both in the ruined walls and loving the night. For unless he loved it, why would he say, *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do?*[^26] Then, born in the wilderness, because he alone was so born; suffering in the darkness of the Jews as in the night, in transgression as in the ruins: what afterward? *I kept watch.* So you had slept in the ruined walls, and had said, *I slept.* What is it, *I slept?* Because I willed, I slept; loving the night I slept: but there follows, *And I rose up*[^27]. So here, *I kept watch.* But after he kept watch, what did he do? He ascended into heaven, became *like a sparrow* by flying, that is, by ascending: *solitary on the roof*, that is, in heaven. So a pelican by being born, a night raven by dying, a sparrow by rising: there in the wilderness, as it were alone; here in the ruined walls, as it were slain by those who could not stand firm in the building; here now watching and flying solitary on the roof, there he intercedes for us. For our head is a sparrow, his body a turtledove. *For the sparrow has found itself a home.* What home? It is in heaven, he intercedes for us. *And the turtledove a nest for itself*, the Church of God a nest from the wood of his cross; *where it may lay its young*[^28], its little ones. *I kept watch, and I have become like a solitary sparrow on the roof.*

**9.** [v. 9.] *All day my enemies reproached me, and those who praised me swore against me.* With the mouth they praised, in the heart they prepared treachery. Hear their praise. *Master, we know that you teach the way of God in truth, and accept no one's person: is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar?*[^29] Whom you praise, you trip up. Why, except because *those who praised me swore against me?* But whence this reproach, except that I came to make sinners my members, that by doing penance they might be in my body? Hence the whole reproach, hence the persecution: *Why does your master eat with sinners and publicans? The physician is not needed by the healthy, but by the sick*[^30]. Would that you knew yourselves to be sick, that you might seek the physician; you would not kill, and through proud madness perish by a false health.

**10.** [v. 10.] But whence did *my enemies reproach me all day?* whence did *those who praised me swear against me? Because I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping.* Because he wished to have in his members these kinds of men to be healed and freed, hence the reproach. Even today, what sort of reproach of the pagans is there against us? what do you think, brothers, what do you think they say to us? You corrupt discipline, and pervert the morals of the human race. What do you inveigh against? say for what reason; what have we done? By giving, he says, men a place for repentance, by promising impunity for all offenses: therefore men do evil, secure that, when they have been converted, all is forgiven them. Hence, then, the reproach: *Because I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping.* O you who insult, I invite you to this bread. For you do not dare to say that you are not a sinner. Examine your conscience, ascend the tribunal of your mind, do not spare yourself, examine yourself, let the marrow of your heart speak to you; see whether you dare to profess innocence. Here, plainly, if he looks back at himself, he will be disturbed; if he does not flatter himself, he will confess. And what will you do, wretched man, if there will be no harbor of impunity? If there was only license for sinning, and there is no indulgence for sins, where will you be? where will you go? Surely it was for your sake too that this poor man ate ashes like bread, and mingled his drink with weeping. Does such a banquet no longer delight you? But still, he says, men increase sins by the hope of pardon. On the contrary, they would increase sins by despair of pardon. Do you not notice with what licentious cruelty gladiators live? Whence this, except that, as if now destined for the sword and the slaughter, they wish to fulfill their lust before they pour out their blood? Would you not say this too to yourself: I am now a sinner, now wicked, now to be condemned, there is no hope of pardon; why should I not now do whatever I please, even if it is not permitted? Why should I not fulfill, as far as I can, whatever desires, if after these nothing remains but torments alone? Would you not say this to yourself, and become worse by that very despair? Rather, then, he corrects you who promises indulgence, and says, *Return, transgressors, to the heart*[^31]. *I do not will the death of the impious, so much as that he turn back and live*[^32]. With this harbor without doubt set before you, you lower the sails of iniquity, you turn the prow, you set sail toward righteousness, and, hoping for life, you do not neglect the medicine. Nor let God displease you in this, as though through that promise of indulgence he had made sinners secure. For indeed, lest men live worse through despair, he promised the harbor of indulgence; again, lest they live worse through the hope of pardon, he made the day of death uncertain: most providently establishing both, both whither those returning may be received, and whence those delaying may be frightened. Eat ashes like bread, and mingle your weeping with your drink; through this banquet you will come to the table of God. Do not despair; indulgence is promised to you. Thanks be to God, he says, that it is promised; I hold God's promise. Then live well now. Tomorrow, he says, I will live well. God has promised you indulgence; no one has promised you tomorrow. If you have lived badly, live well now, today. *Fool, this night your soul will be taken from you.* I do not say, *The things you have prepared, whose will they be?*[^33] but: According to how you have lived, where will you be? Correct yourself, then, that you may be able to have in the body of Christ this voice, which, unless I am mistaken, you gladly recognize: *Because I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping.*

**11.** [v. 11.] *From the face of your wrath and your indignation, because you lifted me up, and dashed me down.* That is that wrath of yours, O Lord, in Adam; the wrath with which we were all born, to which we clung in being born; the wrath from the propagation of iniquity, the wrath from the mass of sin: according to which the Apostle says, *We too were once by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^34]; and whence the Lord says, *The wrath of God remains upon him, because he did not believe in the only-begotten Son of God*[^35]. For he does not say, The wrath of God will come upon him; but, *remains upon him*: because that in which he was born is not taken away. Why, then, and what does this voice mean: *Because you lifted me up, and dashed me down?* For he does not say, Because you lifted me up, and you dashed me down; but, *Because you lifted me up, you dashed me down.* You dashed me down for this reason, because you lifted me up. Whence this? Man, set in honor, was made to the image of God: lifted into this honor, raised from the dust, raised from the earth, he received a rational soul, and was set, by the very liveliness of reason, over all beasts, cattle, birds, fish. For which of these has the reason of understanding? Because none of these was made to the image of God. Just as none of these has this honor, so none of these has this misery. For what beast laments over sin? what bird fears the Gehenna of eternal fire? Because it has no share in the blessed life, it has no goads of miseries. But man, because he was made to be in the blessed life, if he lives well; therefore he will be in a wretched life, because he has lived badly. Therefore: *Because you lifted me up, you dashed me down*: punishment follows me for this reason, because you gave me free choice. For if you had not given me free choice, and through this reason had not made me better than the cattle, just condemnation would not follow me when I sinned. Therefore through free choice you lifted me up, and through the judgment of justice you dashed me down.

**12.** [v. 12.] *My days have declined like a shadow.* For your days could have been days that do not decline, if you had not declined from the true day: you declined, and you received declining days. What wonder if your days have been made like you? For they are themselves declining days, because you went astray; they are days of smoke, because you swelled up. For above he had said, *My days have failed like smoke*; and now he says, *My days have declined like a shadow.* In this shadow the day is to be recognized, in this shadow the light is to be seen; lest afterward, in late and fruitless repentance, it be said, *What did pride profit us? and what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All things have passed away like a shadow*[^36]. Now say: All things will pass away like a shadow, and do not pass away yourself like a shadow. *My days have declined like a shadow; and I have withered like hay.* For above too he had said, *I am struck like hay, and my heart has withered.* But the hay will grow green again, watered by the blood of the Savior. *I have withered like hay*: I, the man, after that transgression; this I by your just judgment: but you, what?

**13.** [v. 13.] *But you, O Lord, abide forever.* My days have declined like a shadow, and you abide forever: let the eternal one save the temporal one. For it is not that, because I have fallen, you too have grown old: for you are vigorous to free me, who were vigorous to humble me. *But you, O Lord, abide forever; and your memorial unto generation and generation. Your memorial*, because you do not forget; *unto generation*, not one, but *generation and generation*: for indeed we have the promise of the present life and of the future.

**14.** [v. 14.] *You, rising up, will have mercy on Zion, for it is time that you have mercy on it.* What time? *But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law.* And where is Zion? *That he might redeem those who were under the Law*[^37]. First, then, the Jews: for from there came the Apostles, from there those more than five hundred brothers, from there next that multitude which had one soul and one heart toward God. Therefore: *You, rising up, will have mercy on Zion, for it is time that you have mercy on it, for the time has come.* What time? *Behold, now is the acceptable time, behold, now is the day of salvation*[^38]. Who says this? The servant of God, the builder, who said, *You are God's building*; who said, *As a wise architect I laid the foundation;* and, *No one can lay another foundation than that which is laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^39].

**15.** [v. 15.] So here too, what does he say? *For your servants have been well pleased with its stones.* With the stones of what? With the stones of Zion. But there are non-stones there too. The non-stones of what? So what follows? *And they will have mercy on its dust.* Let us recognize the stones in Zion, let us recognize the dust in Zion. For he does not say, They will have mercy on its stones; but what does he say? *For your servants have been well pleased with its stones, and they will have mercy on its dust.* They were well pleased with its stones; but they will have mercy on its dust. I understand the stones of Zion to be all the Prophets: there was sent ahead the voice of preaching, from there the office of the Gospel was taken up, through that proclamation Christ was made known. Therefore your servants were well pleased with the stones of Zion. But those transgressors, withdrawing from the Lord, and offending the Creator by their evil deeds, returned to the earth from which they were taken. They became dust, they became impious: of whom it is said, *Not so the impious, not so; but like the dust which the wind drives from the face of the earth*[^40]. But wait, O Lord; bear with them, O Lord; be patient, O Lord: let not the wind rush in, and carry away this dust from the face of the earth. Let them come, let your servants come, let them recognize your words in the stones, let them have mercy on the dust of Zion, let man be formed to your image: let the dust say, that it may not perish, *Remember that we are dust*[^41]. *And they will have mercy on its dust.* This concerning Zion. Was not the dust the one who crucified the Lord? And what is worse, dust from the ruined walls. Dust it was utterly: yet not in vain had it been said of the dust, *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^42]. From that very dust came a wall of so many thousands of believers, laying the prices of their goods at the feet of the Apostles. So from that dust there arose a humanity both formed and shapely. For who among the gentiles did so? How few we marvel did this, in proportion to their many thousands? Suddenly first three, afterward five thousand; all living in unity, all laying the prices of their sold goods at the feet of the Apostles, that it might be distributed to each as each had need, *who had one soul and one heart toward God*[^43]. Who did this even out of that dust, except the one who also made Adam himself out of dust? This, then, concerning Zion, but not only in Zion.

**16.** [v. 16.] What then follows? *And the nations will fear your name, O Lord, and all the kings of the earth your glory.* Now since you have had mercy on Zion, now since your servants have been well pleased with its stones, recognizing the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets; now since they have had mercy on its dust, that man living might be formed, or rather re-formed, out of the dust; from this the preaching grew among the nations: let the nations fear your name, and all the kings of the earth your glory; let another wall come too out of the nations, let the cornerstone be recognized, let the two coming from different sides cling there, but now no longer thinking opposed things.

**17.** [v. 17.] *For the Lord will build up Zion.* This is being done now. Come, living stones, run into the structure, not into the ruin. Zion is being built; beware the ruined walls: the tower is being built, the ark is being built; watch for the flood. This is being done now: *For the Lord will build up Zion.* But when Zion is built, what will happen? *And he will be seen in his glory.* That he might build up Zion, that there might be a foundation in Zion, he was seen by Zion, but not in his glory: *We saw him, and he had neither form nor comeliness*[^44]. But truly, when he comes to judge with his angels, when all the nations will be gathered before him, when the sheep will be separated to the right, the goats to the left, will they not then see him whom they pierced? And they will be confounded too late, who would not be confounded by the first and wholesome repentance. *The Lord will build up Zion, and he will be seen in his glory*: he who in it was first seen in his weakness.

**18.** [v. 18.] *He looked upon the prayer of the humble, and did not despise their petition.* In the building of Zion this is being done now; those building Zion pray, they groan: that one poor man, because the poor are many; because the thousands in so many nations are one, because of the unity of the peace of the Church. He himself is one, he himself is many: one, because of love; many, because of breadth. So now there is prayer, now there is running; now, if anyone was otherwise and held himself otherwise, let him eat ashes like bread, and mingle his drink with weeping. Now is the time, while Zion is being built; now the stones enter into the structure: when the building is finished and the house dedicated, why do you run, about to seek too late, to ask in vain, to knock to no purpose, to remain outside with the five foolish virgins? Now, then, run: for *He looked upon the prayer of the humble, and did not despise their petition.*

**19.** [vv. 19, 20.] *Let these things be written for another generation.* When these things were written, they did not so profit those among whom they were written: for they were written to prophesy the New Testament, among men who lived by the Old Testament. But God had given that Old Testament too, and had settled his people in that land of promise. But since *your memorial is unto generation and generation*, this belongs not to the unjust, but to the just: in one generation it pertains to the Old Testament; in another generation it pertains to the New Testament. And because what is prophesied foretells the New Testament: *Let these things be written for another generation: and the people that will be created will praise the Lord.* Not the people that has been created, but *the people that will be created.* What is more evident, my brothers? Here was foretold that creation of which the Apostle says, *If, then, anyone is a new creation in Christ, the old things have passed away, behold, all things have been made new; but all things are from God*[^45]. What is it, *but all things are from God?* Both the old and the new, because your memorial is unto generation and generation: *And the people that will be created will praise the Lord. For he has looked out from his holy height.* He looked out from on high, that he might come to the humble: from on high he was made humble, that he might exalt the humble.
