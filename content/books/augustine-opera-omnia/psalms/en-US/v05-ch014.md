# Exposition of Psalm 108

**1.** That this psalm has a prophecy concerning Christ, whoever reads the Acts of the Apostles faithfully recognizes; where it evidently appears that of Judas the betrayer of Christ was prophesied what is here written: *Let his days be few, and let another receive his bishopric*, when Matthias, ordained into the place of Judas, was joined as the twelfth to the number of the Apostles. But if we should try to understand of that one man all the things which are here said in an evil sense, the principle of exposition will by no means, or scarcely, be able to apply; but if of such a kind of evil men, that is, of the enemies of Christ and the ungrateful Jews, all things seem to me able to be more clearly opened. For just as certain things are said which seem to pertain properly to the apostle Peter, and yet do not have an illustrious meaning, except when they are referred to the Church, whose person he is recognized to have borne in figure, because of the primacy which he had among the disciples; as is, *To you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven*[^1], and any such things: so Judas in a way sustains the person of the enemies of Christ, the Jews, who both then hated Christ, and now, the lineage of that same impiety persevering by succession, hate him. Of which men and of which people there can not unfittingly be understood, not only those things which we read more openly about them in this psalm, but even those which are said more expressly of Judas himself properly; as is what I commemorated: *Let his days be few, and let another receive his bishopric.* Which will appear, with the Lord helping, when in the order of treating we come to those verses.

**2.** [v. 2.] The Psalm, then, begins thus: *O God, do not pass over my praise in silence; for the mouth of the sinner and the mouth of the deceitful is opened over me.* Whence it appears both that the faultfinding which the sinner and the deceitful do not keep silent is false, and that the praise which God does not keep silent is true. For God is truthful, but every man a liar; because no man is truthful, except him in whom God speaks. But the greatest praise is of the only-begotten Son of God, by which this very thing that he is, the only-begotten Son of God, is proclaimed. But this did not appear, but lay hidden by the appearance of his weakness, when the mouth of the sinner and the mouth of the deceitful was opened over him; and therefore that was opened, because the power of this one was covered. But he says, *The mouth of the deceitful is opened,* because the hatred which was covered by deceit burst out into a voice. This too is said more plainly in the following verses.

**3.** [v. 3.] *They spoke against me with a deceitful tongue*: then certainly when they praised him as it were a good master with captious flattery. Whence elsewhere it is said: *And those who praised me swore against me*[^2]. Then, because they burst out crying, *Crucify, crucify*[^3]; there followed, he added: *And they surrounded me with words of hatred.* Those who with a deceitful tongue spoke as it were words not of hatred, but of love; therefore *against me,* because they did this by lying in wait; afterward *with words,* not of false and deceitful love, but of open *hatred they surrounded me, and warred against me for nothing.* But just as the pious love Christ for nothing, so the impious hate him for nothing; because, just as truth is sought for its own sake by the best, with no advantage proposed outside it, so iniquity by the worst. Whence even among the authors of secular letters it was said of a certain most wicked man: *You were bad and cruel rather for free*[^4].

**4.** [v. 4.] *For that*, he says, *they should love me, they detracted from me.* There are in this kind six distinctions, which, once commemorated, can most easily be noticed: to render good for evil, not to render evil for evil; to render good for good, to render evil for evil; not to render good for good, to render evil for good. Of these the first two are of the good, and of those two the first is better; the last two are of the bad, and of those the latter is worse; the two middle ones in a way of the middling, but of these the former is near to the good, the latter near to the bad. These things in the holy Scriptures one ought to attend to. The Lord himself renders good for evil, *who justifies the impious*[^5], and hanging on the cross said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^6]. Whose footsteps following, holy Stephen, his knee bent, prayed for those stoning him, saying: *Lord, do not lay this offense to their charge*[^7]. To which matter pertains the precept: *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute you*[^8]. That evil is not to be rendered for evil the apostle Paul says: *Rendering to no one evil for evil*[^9]; and the apostle Peter: *Not rendering evil for evil, or curse for curse*[^10]: whence in the Psalms too it is read: *If I have rendered to those who repaid me evils*[^11]. Of the two latter, that milder one pertains to the nine lepers, who, when they had been cleansed by the Lord, did not give thanks: but that last one, than which nothing is worse, pertains to those of whom it is read in this psalm. *For that they should love me, they detracted from me.* For they owed love for the Lord's so great benefits; which they not only by no means rendered, but even for that good inflicted evil. But the two middle ones which we said are in a way of the middling men, are thus: that the former of them, which is to render good for good, both the good have, and the moderately good, or moderately bad. Therefore the Lord does not reprove this, but does not wish his disciples to remain in this alone, whom he wishes to advance to greater things, to whom he says: *If you love those who love you,* that is, if you render good for good; *what reward will you have,* that is, what great thing will you do? *do not even the publicans do this?*[^12] But he wishes them both to do this, and far more; that is, to love not only friends, but even enemies. But the latter, which is to render evil for evil, both the bad have, and the moderately bad, or moderately good; even so far that the Law gave them a measure of avenging: *An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth*[^13]: which, if it can be said, is the righteousness of the unjust. Not because it is wicked that each one receive what he has done; otherwise the Law would by no means have established it: but because the lust of avenging is vicious, and it pertains more to a judge to decree this among others, than for a good man to seek it for himself. Wherefore the impious, fallen from that summit of kindness, where good is rendered for evil, to how great a depth of malignity have they come, by repaying evil for good! by how great a precipice they have passed over so many intervening degrees! Nor should it be thought something small, because he does not say: For that they should love me, they killed me; but, *they detracted from me*: for indeed they killed because they detracted, denying the Son of God, and saying that *he casts out demons by the prince of demons*[^14]; and: *He has a demon, and is mad; why do you hear him?*[^15] and other such things. By which detraction they were turning away from him those whose conversion he was seeking: and therefore he rather said this, to show that those who detract from Christ, and through this kill souls, harm more than those who, by raging, destroyed his mortal flesh, especially soon to rise again.

**5.** But when he had said, *For that they should love me, they detracted from me;* what does he say? *But I prayed.* He did not indeed say what he prayed; but what better do we understand than for those very men? For they detracted most of all from the crucified one, when they mocked him as a man whom they had as it were conquered; of which cross he said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^16]: so that, since they in the depth of malignity rendered evil for good, he in the summit of kindness might render good for evil. Although it is well understood that he prayed too for his disciples, which he said even before his passion, that their faith might not fail, when he himself, hanging on the wood, to commend patience, did not show his power among the words of those detracting, whom he could destroy by divine power. But it was more useful for us that he gave an example of patience, than if, by destroying his enemies without delay, he had built us up to this, that we should impatiently hasten to be avenged on those we suffer as bad: since it is written: *Better is the patient man than the strong*[^17]. The divine eloquences, then, teach us by the Lord's example, when we hear: *For that they should love me, they detracted from me; but I prayed;* that when we perceive any to be ungrateful, not only not rendering good, but even rendering evil for good, we should pray: and he indeed for others either raging, or grieving, and in danger in their faith; but we even for ourselves first, that we may conquer our mind, with God having mercy and helping, by which we are carried into the desire of avenging, when detraction is made whether against us present or absent. Then, when we recall the patience of Christ, as if he himself were roused—as happened when he slept in the ship—who calms the disturbance and tempest of our heart, with mind settled and appeased, let us pray even for our detractors, that we may securely say: *Forgive us, as we too forgive*[^18]. But he forgave, who certainly did not have a sin to be forgiven him.

**6.** [v. 5.] But there follows: *And they set against me evils for goods.* And as if we were asking, "What evils? for what goods?" *And hatred*, he says, *for my love.* This is all and the great guilt of theirs. For what could they harm, persecuting one dying by will, not by necessity? But the hatred itself is the greatest crime of the persecutor, although it is the voluntary punishment of the one suffering. But he sufficiently expounded how above he said, *For that they should love me*, because they owed love not in just any way, but to his love; when here he added, *for my love.* This love he commemorates in the Gospel, where he says: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, how often have I wished to gather your children, as a hen her chicks under her wings, and you would not!*[^19]

**7.** Then he begins to prophesy what they will receive for that very impiety; and he says those things in such a way, as if, from a desire of avenging, he wished that they come to be; whereas they are said to be future with the most certain truth, and to come worthily upon such men through the justice of God. Which manner of foretelling future things, as it were under the appearance of wishing evil, some not understanding suppose that hatred is rendered for hatred, and an evil mind for an evil mind: since indeed it belongs to few to discern how the punishment of the wicked pleases the accuser desiring to glut his enmities, and how in a far other way it pleases the judge punishing sins with a right will. For that one renders evil for evil; but this one, even when he avenges, does not render evil for evil, since he renders the just for the unjust: but what is just, is certainly good. He punishes, then, not from delight in another's misery, which is evil for evil; but from love of justice, which is good for evil. And so let not the blind slander the light of the Scriptures, supposing that God does not punish sins; nor let the unjust flatter themselves as if he rendered evil for evil. Let us hear, then, what the divine discourse weaves; and in the words as it were of one wishing evil, let us understand the foretellings of one prophesying; and let us discern God repaying just things, with our mind lifted up to his eternal law.

**8.** [v. 6.] *Set over him a sinner, and let the devil stand at his right hand.* Since above the complaint was of several, now the Psalm speaks of one. But above he had said: *They spoke against me with a deceitful tongue, and surrounded me with words of hatred, and warred against me for nothing: for that they should love me, they detracted from me; but I prayed: and they set against me evils for goods, and hatred for my love.* All of several. But now, foretelling what they would deserve for these iniquities of theirs, and what would be to them by divine judgment: *Set over him*, he says, *a sinner*: as if directing his attention to the one who handed him over, of such enemies of his as he spoke above. Since, then, he here foretells Judas the betrayer, according to the Scripture of the Acts of the Apostles, as to be punished with the due punishment; what is it, *Set over him a sinner,* except the one whom he indicates in the following verse, when he says, *And let the devil stand at his right hand?* This, then, he deserved, that he should have the devil over him, that is, be subject to the devil, who was unwilling to be subject to Christ. But *let him stand at his right hand* was said, because he set the works of the devil before the works of God. For this is not without reason called each one's right hand, which he prefers; as the right is preferred to the left. Therefore of those too who, preferring the joys of this world to God, called blessed the people for whom these things are, it was most rightly said: *Their right hand is the right hand of iniquity*[^20]. Whence, in that they called blessed the people for whom these things are, their mouth spoke vanity; which was said of them above. But of whom the mouth speaks truth, against that which these men said, that the people for whom these things are is blessed, he too ought to say what follows in the same psalm: *Blessed is the people whose Lord is their God*[^21]: for to this one the devil is not at the right hand, but the Lord: as he says elsewhere too: *I foresaw the Lord in my sight always, for he is at my right hand, that I not be moved*[^22]. The devil, then, stood at his right hand, when he set greed before wisdom, and money before his own salvation, that he might hand him over by whom he ought to be possessed, lest he be possessed by him whose works Christ himself loosed, by whom he was unwilling to be possessed.

**9.** [v. 7.] *When he is judged, let him go out condemned.* For he was unwilling to be such as to whom it would be said: *Enter into the joy of your lord;* but such as of whom it is said: *Cast him into the outer darkness*[^23]. *And let his prayer become sin.* Since there is no just prayer except through Christ, whom he sold by the monstrousness of sin: but a prayer which is not made through Christ, not only cannot blot out sin, but even itself becomes sin. But when Judas could have so prayed that his prayer became sin, it can be asked. I believe, before he handed over the Lord, and when he was already thinking of handing him over: for after that he could no longer pray through Christ. For after he had handed him over, and repented of it, if he had prayed through Christ, he would have asked indulgence; if he had asked indulgence, he would have had hope; if he had had hope, he would have hoped for mercy; if he had hoped for mercy, he would not in despair have bound his neck for himself. Accordingly, when he had said, *When he is judged, let him go out condemned;* lest from the imminent condemnation he be supposed to have been able to free himself by the prayer which he had learned with his fellow-disciples, where it is said, *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors: And let his prayer*, he says, *become sin;* because it is not made through Christ, whom he was unwilling to follow, but to persecute.

**10.** [v. 8.] *Let his days be few. His days* he said, the days of his apostleship; which were few, since before the passion of the Lord they were consumed by his own crime and death. And as if it were said: What, then, will become of the most sacred number twelve, in which not in vain the Lord wished to have twelve first Apostles? he immediately subjoined: *And let another receive his bishopric.* As if saying: Let him both be punished according to his desert, and let that number be filled up. But if anyone desires to know how it was done, let him read the Acts of the Apostles.

**11.** [v. 9.] *Let his sons become orphans, and his wife a widow.* Certainly, he himself dead, both his sons became orphans, and his wife widowed.

**12.** [v. 10.] *Let his sons, wavering, be carried about, and beg. Wavering* was said, uncertain where they go, destitute of all protection. *Let them be cast out of their dwellings.* He expounded what he had said above, *be carried about.* But all this, how it happened to his wife and sons, the following verses indicate.

**13.** [vv. 11, 12.] *Let the moneylender search out all his substance, and let strangers plunder his labors. Let there be no helper for him*: he means, for the protecting of his posterity; therefore there follows: *Nor let there be one who has mercy on his orphans.*

**14.** [v. 13.] But because the orphans too could, even without a helper and without a guardian, yet grow up in hardships and want, and preserve the lineage by propagation; there follows, and he says: *Let his offspring become unto destruction, in one generation let his name be blotted out*: that is, let what is begotten from him beget no more, and quickly pass away.

**15.** [v. 14.] But what is it that he then subjoins? *Let the iniquity of his fathers come back into remembrance in the sight of the Lord, and let the sin of his mother not be blotted out.* Is it to be understood that even the sins of his parents are rendered to him? For they are not rendered to one who has been changed in Christ, and has begun to be not a son of the wicked, not imitating their ways: because both that is most truly written, *I will render the sins of the fathers upon the sons*[^24]; and that which is said through the prophet: *Both the soul of the father is mine, and the soul of the son is mine; the soul that sins, it will die*[^25]. For this was said of those who turn to God, and do not imitate the evils of their parents; which the prophet himself evidently shows: for he says that the iniquities of the parents do not harm those who, by doing righteousness, have been unlike them. But that which was said, *I will render the sins of the fathers upon the sons,* it was added, *who hate me*, that is, as their parents hated me: so that, just as the imitation of the good causes even one's own sins to be blotted out, so the imitation of the bad causes one to share not only one's own, but even the deserts of those whom one has imitated. If, then, Judas had held that to which he was called, in no way would either his own past or his parents' iniquity pertain to him: because, then, he did not hold the adoption in the family of God, but rather chose the iniquity of the old lineage; the iniquity of his fathers came back in the sight of the Lord, that in him it too might be punished; and the sin of his mother was not blotted out in him.

**16.** [v. 15.] *Let them be against the Lord always*: that is, his fathers and his mother *let them be against the Lord always,* not that they may oppose the Lord, but that the Lord may not forget their most wicked deserts in him, when he repays him these too. *Against the Lord* he said: In the sight of the Lord: for other translators too rendered it thus: *Let them be in the sight of the Lord always;* but others: *Let them be before the Lord always*: as it was said elsewhere: *You set our iniquities in your sight*[^26]. But *always* he says, that that crime alone may be without remission, both here, and in the age to come. *Let the memory of them perish from the earth*: namely of his fathers and his mother. He means the memory of them which is kept in the propagation of generation: this he prophesied would perish from the earth, because both Judas himself, and his sons who were as it were the memory of his fathers and his mother, without succession of offspring, as was said above, were extinguished in the brevity of one generation.

**17.** Someone says: Is even this to be believed to pertain to the punishment of Judas, that after his death his wife and sons came to beggary, and were carried about, cast out of their dwellings, the moneylender searching out all his substance, and strangers plundering all his labors, no one helping or having mercy on his orphans, and that they quickly died without posterity? Does any grief touch the dead even concerning the things that happen among their own after each one's death? or are they at least to be thought to know these things, whose perception is elsewhere according to their deserts, whether well or ill? To whom I answer that it is indeed a great question, and not to be discussed at present, since it is a matter of a more lengthy work, whether, or how far, or in what way the spirits of the dead know the things that are done around us. Yet, what can be briefly said, if they had no care about us, the Lord would not say that that rich man who was suffering torments in hell said: *I have there five brothers, lest they too come into this place of torments*[^27]. But however those who try to understand this otherwise may understand it, and since it must be confessed that it is not a consequence that, if the dead know that their own are alive—because they see them neither in the places of punishments, where that rich man was, nor in the rest of the blessed, where he recognized Lazarus and Abraham, though far off—they therefore necessarily know the things, whether glad or sad, that are done around their dear ones: this I say, that there are few men of such a mind as to neglect, and altogether scorn, what good or evil befalls their own after their death, at least while they live; but many, which even the so great care of commending one's last will and of testaments of whatever sort indicates, take pains that, when they are dead, it may be well with their own. But the continuance of their posterity through the successions of generations only those praiseworthily despise who cut themselves off for the kingdom of heaven, and desire their sons to do this, or long to be crowned with martyrdom, so that none of them remains on the earth: but all the rest, or nearly all, wish their own to be happy in this life after their death, and do not wish their lineage to perish. Wherefore, that Judas dying so unhappily, his wife thus remained a widow and his sons orphans, that, the moneylender searching out all his substance, and strangers plundering his labors, they were cast out of their dwellings, nor did his orphans find anyone having mercy on them, and they perished in one generation without posterity; if the dead perceive these things, it is a heap of evils; if they do not perceive these things, it is a dread for the living. But if it moves anyone how he could have a substance which the moneylender searched out, and strangers plundered, when he was already following the Lord with the other eleven; let him believe that he so left all that he had to his sons and wife, that he did not sincerely or perseveringly break off from there the bond of cupidity: who, even if he seemed to sell them as it were to be distributed to the poor, would surely do what Ananias did after the Lord's ascension. For he would not fear that the Lord would by divinity know this, whom he supposed could be deceived, when he took away from the purses the things that were put in.

**18.** [vv. 6-15.] But now let us see, if we can, as far as the Lord helps, how these things can fit also the people of the Jews, whose enmities against the Lord remained in obstinate hatred: of which people we said that Judas bore the person in figure, as the apostle Peter bore that of the Church. *Set over him a sinner, and let the devil stand at his right hand.* Just as in Judas, so in this people it is to be understood; who, having repelled Christ from himself, was made subject to the devil, whose persuasions in all depraved and earthly desires he set before eternal salvation. *When he is judged, let him go out condemned*: because, persisting in wickedness and unbelief, he stores up for himself wrath on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works. *And let his prayer become sin;* because it is not made through the mediator of God and men, the man Jesus Christ, and the priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. *Let his days be few.* It is to be taken according to the kingdom, because the kingdom of the Jews did not long persevere afterward. *And let another receive his bishopric.* I judge that the Lord Christ himself is not unfittingly understood to be the bishopric of the people of the Jews, because he was made according to the flesh of the tribe of Judah: and the Apostle says: *For I say that Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers*[^28]. And he himself says: *I am not sent except to the sheep which have perished of the house of Israel*[^29]; because to them he showed his presence in the flesh. And the Magi who came from the east said this: *Where is he who is born king of the Jews?*[^30] And this was written in the title over the crucified one: whence to those wishing to change it Pilate not in vain answered: *What I have written, I have written*[^31]. This bishopric of the people of the Jews, then, that is, the Lord Christ, another received, that is, the people of the gentiles. *Let his sons become orphans*: of whom it is said: *But the sons of the kingdom will go into the outer darkness*[^32]. But they were made orphans, that kingdom lost, as if their father lost; although they are well understood to have lost God the Father too: *For he who does not have the Son,* says Truth, *does not have the Father either*[^33]. *And his wife a widow.* By the wife of the kingdom the people can be understood, over which kings, made subject to it, have dominion: but she was made a widow, that kingdom lost. *Let his sons, wavering, be carried about, and beg.* They wavered in dangers, with enemies pressing they were carried about, the sons of the kingdom of the Jews were vanquished. But what is it to beg, except to live by the mercy of men, as they live under the kings of those nations among which they were carried? *Let them be cast out of their dwellings.* So it happened. *Let the moneylender search out all his substance*: that is, of his people. Where nothing better is understood than that their debts are not forgiven, because they are forgiven only in Christ, whom they rejected; who also taught us to say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^34]. But *all his substance* was said, all his life; that no debts, that is, no sins, be given to him. *And let strangers plunder his labors*: the devil and his angels; because those do not lay up treasure in heaven who do not have Christ. *Let there be no helper for him.* Who is a helper for one for whom Christ is not? *Nor let there be one who has mercy on his orphans.* Those who, their father lost, that is, the kingdom, so remained, or God lost, whose Son they persecuted and hated, there is no one who has mercy on them, not for the taking up or sustaining of temporal life, but for the true life, that is, eternal. *Let his offspring become unto destruction*: certainly unto everlasting destruction. *In one generation let his name be blotted out.* Because they were begotten, not regenerated, therefore in one generation they are blotted out: for in the other, that is, in regeneration, if they recognized and held it, they would not be blotted out. *Let the iniquity of his fathers come back into remembrance in the sight of the Lord*: that the Lord may render to the same people persevering in malice the iniquity of his fathers too. For so he says to them: *You are witnesses to yourselves, that you are the sons of those who killed the Prophets*: and a little after he says: *That upon you may come all the just blood that has been shed on the earth, from the blood of Abel the just to the blood of Zechariah*[^35]. *And let the sin of his mother not be blotted out.* The sin of Jerusalem, who is in slavery with her children, who kills the Prophets, and stones those sent to her. *Let them be against the Lord always,* their iniquity and sin; that is, that they not be abolished from the sight of the Lord, with God avenging forever. *And let the memory of them perish from the earth.* The earth of God is the field of God; the field of God is the Church of God: from which earth perished the memory of them, who, since they were natural branches, because of unbelief were broken off.

**19.** [vv. 16, 17.] *For that he did not remember,* either that Judas, or that people, *to do mercy.* But it is better taken of the people, what he says, *he did not remember*: for if it killed Christ, it might at least remember by repenting, and do mercy upon his members, which it has most perseveringly persecuted. Therefore he says that *he persecuted the needy and beggar man.* It can indeed be taken of Judas; because the Lord did not disdain to become poor, when he was rich, that by his poverty we might be enriched. But how shall I understand "beggar," except perhaps because he said to the Samaritan woman, *Give me to drink*[^36], and on the cross said, *I thirst*[^37]? But what follows, how it may be taken in our head himself, that is, the Savior of his body, whom Judas persecuted, I do not find. For when he had said, *And he persecuted the needy and beggar man,* he added and said: *And to put to death the one pricked in heart;* that is, that he might put him to death: for some translated it thus too. But one pricked in heart is not wont to be said, except by the goads of sins in the grief of repenting; as it was said of those who, when they had heard the Apostles after the Lord's ascension, *were pricked in heart*[^38], who had killed the Lord. To whom most blessed Peter spoke, saying among other things: *Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, and your sins will be forgiven you*[^39]. But since these very ones were made the members of him whose members they fixed on the wood, the people of the Jews did not remember to do mercy; it persecuted the needy and beggar man, but in his members: of whom he is going to say, as far as concerns the very works of mercy: *When you did it not to one of these least of mine, you did it not to me*[^40]. *And to put to death the one pricked in heart*: plainly *the one pricked in heart,* but in his members. But of those who persecuted, to put to death the one pricked in heart, was Saul too, consenting to the killing of Stephen who was pricked in heart; for Stephen himself too was of those who were pricked in heart. But Saul remembered to do mercy; and he who in the morning was seizing, in the evening divided the spoils: he himself too pricked in heart, so that even in him they persecuted the needy, wishing to put to death the one pricked in heart. For this they hated in the apostle Paul, that, pricked in heart, he preached the one he had before persecuted. For, persecuting the needy and beggar and pricked-in-heart in his members, to put him to death, he himself too heard from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^41] and, made pricked in heart, he began to suffer such things as he was doing to those pricked in heart.

**20.** [v. 18.] Then the Psalm follows: *And he loved the curse, and it will come to him.* Although Judas too loved the curse, both by stealing from the purses, and by selling and handing over the Lord; yet more openly that people loved the curse, when it said: *His blood be upon us and upon our sons*[^42]. *And he was unwilling to have the blessing, and it will be put far from him.* Judas too indeed, because he was unwilling to have Christ, in whom is the eternal blessing; but more openly the people of the Jews was unwilling to have the blessing, to whom that man enlightened by the Lord said: *Do you too wish to become his disciples?* And it was unwilling to have the blessing, and held it for a curse; and answered: *May you be his disciple*[^43]: and the blessing was put far from it, because it passed over to the gentiles. *And he put on the curse as a garment*: whether Judas, or that people. *And it entered like water into his inward parts.* Therefore both outside and inside; outside like a garment, inside like water: since he fell into the judgment of him who can kill both body and soul in Gehenna; the body outside, the soul inside. *And like oil in his bones.* He shows that he did evil with delight, and procured for himself the curse, that is, eternal punishment; because the blessing is eternal life. For now evil deeds delight, like water into the inward parts, and like oil in the bones; but it is for this reason called a curse, because to such men God has foretold torments. But the curse is as it were oil in the bones, when men suppose themselves strong from it, because they are allowed to commit evils as it were with impunity.

**21.** [v. 19.] *Let it become for him like the garment with which he is covered.* Since above he already spoke of the garment, what is it that he repeats? Or, because he had said, *He put on the curse as a garment,* does *the garment with which* one is not put on, but *covered,* differ from it? For each one is put on with a tunic, but covered with a cloak. And what is this, except to glory in iniquity even in the sight of men? *And like a belt*, he says, *with which he is always girded.* Men gird themselves chiefly for this reason, that they may be more fit for working, lest they be hindered by the folds of the garment. Therefore he girds himself with the curse, who undertakes an evil that is not sudden, but planned, and so learns to do evil that he is always ready: whence here too he says, *And like a belt with which he is always girded.*

**22.** [v. 20.] *This is the work of those who detract from me before the Lord.* He did not say, the reward of them; but, *the work of them*: for it is manifest that by the clothing, and the covering, and the water, and the oil, and the belt, he was describing the very works by which eternal cursing is procured. There is not, then, one Judas, but many, of whom it is said, *This is the work of those who detract from me before the Lord.* Although the plural number too could be set for the singular number; as, Herod being dead, it was said by the angel: *Those who sought the soul of the boy are dead*[^44]. But who detract more from Christ before the Lord, except those who detract from the very words of the Lord, saying that he is not the one whom the Law of the Lord and the Prophets foretold? *And who speak,* he says, *evils against my soul*: by denying that he could, when he willed, rise again; whereas he says: *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again*[^45].

**23.** [v. 21.] *And you, O Lord, Lord, act with me.* Some thought *mercy* was to be understood, and some even added it; but the more correct manuscripts have it thus: *And you, O Lord, Lord, act with me, for your name's sake.* Whence a deeper sense is not to be passed over, that the Son so said to the Father, *Act with me,* because the works of the Father and the Son are the same. Where, even if we understand mercy (for there follows: *Because your mercy is sweet*), even this, because he did not say: Act in me; or Act upon me; or anything of the sort; but said, *Act with me;* we well understand that both the Father and the Son together do mercy in the vessels of mercy. This can also be understood thus: *Act with me,* that is, help me. Which we have in the daily custom of speaking, when of some matter that is on our side, we say: "It acts with us." For the Father helps the Son, insofar as the man is God, because of the form of a servant: to which man God, and to which form of a servant the Father is also Lord. For in the form of God, the Son does not need help; for he is equally omnipotent with the Father, from which he himself too is the helper of man. *For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so too the Son gives life to whom he wishes*[^46]: nor does the Father give life to some, the Son to others, or the Father in one way, the Son in another; because he both does the same things, and in like manner. Whence insofar as the Son of God is man, God raised him from the dead, that is, the Father, to whom he says in the Psalms: *Raise me up, and I will repay them*[^47]: but insofar as he is God, he raised himself too; for which he says: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^48]. Which he signified here too, if anyone attends diligently: for he bade the Scriptures, which bear witness of him, be searched, not passed over on the surface. For he did not say only: *You, O Lord, Lord, act with me;* but he said: *And you.* What is it, *And you,* except that I too? But that he said not once *Lord,* but repeating, *Lord, Lord;* he has in this the feeling of one praying: as is, *God, my God*[^49]. But that, when he had said, *Act with me,* he added, *for your name's sake;* he commended grace. For by no preceding merits of works was human nature borne up to so great a loftiness, that the whole at once, the Word and flesh, that is, God and man, should be called the only-begotten Son of God. But this was done, that by him who had created, through that which had not perished, that which had perished might be sought: whence here too there follows: *Because your mercy is sweet.*

**24.** [v. 22.] *Free me, for I am needy and poor.* Need and poverty is the weakness from which he was crucified. *And my heart is troubled within me.* This is referred to that which he said as the passion drew near: *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^50].

**25.** [v. 23.] *Like a shadow when it declines, I was taken away.* This signified death itself. For as from a declining shadow night comes, so from mortal flesh death comes. *I was shaken off like the locusts.* This I judge is more fittingly understood now in his members, that is, his faithful. Which, to set down somewhat more openly, he preferred to say, *like the locusts,* than like a locust: although even in the singular number many could be taken, as that is: *He spoke, and the locust came*[^51]; but it would be more obscure. They were shaken off, then, that is, put to flight by the persecutors, his faithful, of whom he wished to signify either the multitude by the name of locusts, or that they leaped from place to place.

**26.** [v. 24.] *My knees were made weak by fasting.* We read that the Lord Christ had a fast of forty days: but did that abstinence avail so much in him, that his knees were made weak? Or is this too more aptly understood in his members, that is, his holy ones? *And my flesh was changed because of oil*: because of spiritual grace. Whence Christ too is named from the chrism: but chrism is anointing. But the flesh was changed because of oil not for the worse, but for the better, that is, rising from the contempt of death into the glory of immortality. And so, when he had said, *My knees were made weak by fasting,* where I judge it is signified that those who seemed strong in his members, as if the presence of the bread by which they were sustained being withdrawn, failed in his passion even to denial, which appeared in Peter; as if to confirm them, lest by succumbing they should fall utterly: *And my flesh,* he says, *was changed because of oil,* that I might strengthen by my resurrection those failing in my death, and anoint them by sending the Holy Spirit, who would not have come to them unless I had gone away. For he had said this: *He cannot come unless I go away*[^52]: and the Evangelist said: *The Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified*[^53]. His flesh was not yet changed. But whether by water, because of washing or watering, or by oil, because of exultation and the kindling of love, the Holy Spirit is signified; he is not therefore diverse from himself, because the signs are diverse. For even the lion and the lamb are very diverse, and yet by both Christ was signified. The lion for one thing, the lamb for another; yet not another: because the lamb is not strong, nor the lion innocent; but Christ is both innocent as a lamb, and strong as a lion. But Jesus Christ himself says in Isaiah: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he anointed me*[^54].

**27.** [v. 25.] *And I became a reproach to them*: through the death of the cross. *For Christ* indeed *redeemed us from the curse of the Law, being made a curse for us*[^55]. *They saw me, and shook their heads.* Because they saw him hang, they did not see him rise again; they saw when his knees were made weak, they did not see when his flesh was changed.

**28.** [v. 26.] *Help me, O Lord my God, make me safe according to your mercy.* This can be referred to the whole, that is, both to the head, and to the body: to the head, because of the form of a servant; to the body, because of the servants themselves. For in them too he could say to God, *Help me*, and, *make me safe*; in whom he said to Saul, *Why do you persecute me?*[^56] But that he added, *according to your mercy*; free grace is commemorated, not from the debt of works.

**29.** [v. 27.] *And let them know that this is your hand, and you, O Lord, made it.* He said *let them know* of those for whose raging he even prayed; because among those to whom he became a reproach, shaking their heads in mockery of him, were also those who afterward believed in him. But let those learn who attribute the form of a human body to God, how God has a hand. For if what he makes, he makes with a hand, does he then make his hand itself with a hand? How, then, was it said here: *And let them know that this is your hand, and you, O Lord, made it?* Let us understand, then, that the hand of God is Christ: whence elsewhere it is said: *And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?*[^57] This hand both was, and he made it; because: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word became flesh*[^58]: and it was without time according to divinity, and it was made for him from the seed of David according to the flesh.

**30.** [v. 28.] *They will curse, and you will bless.* Vain, then, and false is the curse of the sons of men, loving vanity, and seeking a lie; but God, when he blesses, does what he says. *Let those who rise against me be confounded.* For they suppose that by rising they advance something against me; but when I have been exalted above the heavens, and my glory has begun to be above all the earth, they will be confounded. *But your servant will rejoice*: whether at the right hand of the Father, or in his members rejoicing, both amid temptations in hope, and after temptations forever.

**31.** [v. 29.] *Let those who detract from me be clothed with shame*: that is, let them be ashamed of having detracted from me. But this can be taken in a good sense too, while they are corrected. *And let them be covered with their confusion as with a double cloak.* A "diplois" is a double cloak. For some translated this verse thus too: *And let them be covered with their confusion as with a double cloak.* But it is understood, let them be confounded both inside and outside, that is, both before God and before men.

**32.** [v. 30.] *I will confess to the Lord exceedingly with my mouth.* "Exceedingly" (nimis) is wont to be said in the custom of Latin speech for what is more than it ought to be: to which the contrary is "too little" (parum), which is less than it ought to be. But *nimis* represents a Greek word; but this verse does not have the Greek word for "excessively," but has another, sphodra. Which some of ours translated by sometimes setting *exceedingly,* sometimes *greatly.* But if *exceedingly* is understood for what is *greatly,* it can be set in praise too; for this confession too signifies praise. For so it follows: *And in the midst of many I will praise him.* He says too in another psalm: *In the midst of the Church I will sing to you*[^59]. But when the Church itself sings, which is the body of Christ, how does the Church sing in the midst of the Church? So here too, since many are the members of Christ, if when they themselves praise, he praises, because they are his members; how does he praise in the midst of many, when, those many praising, he himself is said to praise? Or does he praise in the midst of many for this reason, because he is here with his Church to the consummation of the age; so that what he says, *in the midst of many,* we take to mean that he is honored by those same many? For he is said to be in the midst to whom special honor is shown. But if the heart is as it were the middle of a man; nothing better is understood to be said than, in the hearts of many I will praise him. For Christ dwells through faith in our hearts: and therefore he says, *with my mouth,* that is, with the mouth of my body, which is the Church. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness; but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.

**33.** [v. 31.] *For he stood at the right hand of the poor man.* Of Judas it was said: *And let the devil stand at his right hand;* who wished to increase his own riches by selling Christ: but here the Lord *stood at the right hand of the poor man,* that the riches of the poor man might be the Lord himself. *He stood* indeed *at the right hand of the poor man,* not to multiply for him the years of a life at some time to be ended, nor to increase his money, or make him strong in bodily strength, or unharmed for a time; but *to make safe,* he says, *my soul from those who persecute it.* But the soul is made safe from those who persecute it, if it does not consent to them unto evil; but it does not consent to them, when the Lord stands at the right hand of the poor man, lest by that very poverty, that is, weakness, it succumb. This help was bestowed on the body of Christ in all the holy martyrs.
