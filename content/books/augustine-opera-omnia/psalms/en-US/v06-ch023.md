# Exposition of Psalm 143

**1.** Of the psalm which we have sung I will speak to your charity what the Lord will grant. Yesterday a shorter psalm was treated, but the span of time gave us abundance to say even much about a few things: now, since the psalm is longer, we ought not to delay so long on individual verses; lest perhaps the Lord not grant the means for us to be able to go through the whole.

**2.** [v. 1.] The title of the Psalm is: *Of David himself, when his son persecuted him.* In the books of Kings we know that this happened, that Absalom became an enemy to his father, that he waged against him not only a civil, but even a domestic war: but that he, not unjustly cast down, but piously humbled, received discipline from the Lord; that he endured the medicine, not repaying iniquity for iniquity, but had a heart ready to follow the will of the Lord[^1]. That David is so praiseworthy: but the other David, truly Strong in hand, which 'David' is interpreted, our Lord Jesus Christ, is to be recognized by us. For those past deeds were figures of things to come; nor must that be commended long which you have often heard, which you retain very well. Let us, then, seek in this psalm our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ foretelling himself through this prophecy, and, through the things done long ago, foretelling what would be in this time. For he himself preached himself in the Prophets; since he himself is the Word of God; nor did they say any such thing, except as full of the Word of God. So they announced Christ, full of Christ; and they went before him who was to come, whom, going before, he did not desert. Let us, then, recognize how his son too persecuted Christ: for he had sons, of whom he said: *The sons of the bridegroom do not fast, as long as the bridegroom is with them: but when the bridegroom has been taken from them, then the sons of the bridegroom will fast*[^2]. So the sons of the bridegroom are the apostles: and among these the persecutor Judas was the devil. So Christ is about to foretell his passion in this psalm; let us hear.

**3.** But again we make your charity attentive to this, not by teaching what you do not know, but by admonishing what you know: that our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ is the head of his body, that he is the one mediator of God and men[^3], the man Jesus, born of a virgin, as it were in solitude, as we heard in the Apocalypse[^4]. For by solitude I think it means that he was thus born alone. For that woman bore him, who was to rule the people with a rod of iron. But this woman is the ancient city of God, of which it is said in the psalm: *Glorious things are said of you, O city of God*[^5]. This city has its beginning from Abel himself, as the evil city from Cain[^6]. This ancient city of God, then, always enduring the earth, hoping for heaven, which is also called Jerusalem and Zion. Indeed, of a certain one born in Zion, and of the founder of Zion himself, a certain psalm says: *Mother Zion, a man will say.* What man? *And a man was made in her; and the Most High himself founded her*[^7]. Finally, he himself was made man in Zion, but was made a humble man; and that same Most High founded that city in which he was made man. And so that woman too was clothed with the sun[^8], with that sun of justice whom the impious do not know; who will say at the end: *So we have strayed from the way of truth, and the light of justice did not shine for us, and the sun did not rise for us*[^9]. So there is a certain sun of justice which does not rise for the impious. But this sun he makes rise over the good and the evil[^10]. So she was both clothed with the sun, and bore in her womb the male whom she was to bring forth. He himself was both the founder of Zion, and born in Zion: and that woman, the city of God, was protected by his light, with whose flesh she was made pregnant. Rightly too she had the moon under her feet, because she trampled by strength the mortality of the flesh that grows and decreases. So the Lord Jesus Christ himself is head and body: for he willed to speak even in us, who deigned to die for us; he made us his members. And so sometimes he speaks from the person of his members, sometimes from his own person, as our head. He has something to say without us; we can say nothing without him. The apostle says: *That I may supply what is lacking of the pressures of Christ, in my flesh*[^11]. *That I may supply*, he says, *what is lacking of the pressures*, not mine, but *of Christ; in the flesh*, not now of Christ, but *mine.* Christ still suffers pressure, he says; not in his flesh, in which he ascended into heaven, but in my flesh, which still labors on earth. Christ, he says, suffers pressure in my flesh: *For I live, now not I, but Christ lives in me*[^12]. For unless Christ himself too suffered pressure in his members, that is, his faithful, Saul on earth would not persecute Christ sitting in heaven. Finally, plainly expounding this in a certain place: *For as the body is one*, he says, *and has many members; but all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body: so also Christ*[^13]. He did not say: So also Christ and the body; but, *the body one, the members many, so also Christ.* So the whole is Christ: and because the whole is Christ, therefore the head from heaven: *Saul*, he says, *Saul, why do you persecute me*[^14]? Hold this, and commit it altogether fixed to memory, as sons of ecclesiastical learning and of the Catholic faith, that you may recognize Christ head and body, and that same Christ the only-begotten Word of God equal to the Father; and from there may see by how great grace you reach to God, that he himself willed to be one with us, who is one with the Father. How one with the Father? *I and the Father are one*[^15]. How one with us? *He does not say*, he says: *And to seeds, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^16]. But someone says: If Christ is the seed of Abraham, are we too? Remember that the seed of Abraham is Christ; and therefore if we too are the seed of Abraham, then we too are Christ. *As one body has many members, so also Christ*[^17]: and *as many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ*[^18]. Surely the seed of Abraham is Christ; nor can the most plain apostolic words be contradicted: *And to your seed, which is Christ.* See what he says to us: *But if you are Christ's, then you are the seed of Abraham*[^19]. Therefore that sacrament is great: *The two will be in one flesh*[^20]. The apostle says: *This sacrament is great; but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^21]. Christ and the Church, two in one flesh. Refer to the distance of majesty—two. Two plainly. For we too are not the Word, we too are not in the beginning God with God, we too are not he through whom all things were made[^22]. One comes to the flesh, and there are Christ both he and we. Let us not, then, wonder in the Psalms: for he says many things from the person of the head, many from the person of the members; and all this he speaks as though it were one person. Nor wonder that two are in one voice, if two are in one flesh.

**4.** Judas, the son of the bridegroom, persecuting the bridegroom. Did this happen then; or was an example of things to come sent ahead? For the Church was to suffer many false brothers, so that both now and even to the end the son persecutes that bridegroom. *For if*, he says, *an enemy had reproached me, I would surely have borne it; and if he who hated me had spoken great things against me, I would surely have hidden myself from him*[^23]. Who is the enemy? who is he who hated me? He who says: Who is Christ? Christ was a man, and could not live when he wished to live: and he died, they say, unwilling, conquered, crucified, killed. Enemies say these things. This is an open enemy, says Christ, he hates me, he declares open enmities against me; it is easy either to bear or to avoid him: what do I do about Absalom? what do I do about Judas? what about the false brothers? what about the bad sons, and yet sons, who do not blaspheme Christ against us, but adore Christ with us, and in us persecute Christ? Of whom consequently in the same psalm it is said: It was easy to bear that other, the one who hated me; or to hide myself from him. For you hide yourself from a pagan by entering the Church. But when even there you find what you fear, where do you seek to hide yourself? Finally, that same apostle who groans in dangers from false brothers: *Outside*, he says, *fightings, within fears*[^24]. *If*, then, *he who hated me had spoken great things against me, I would surely have hidden myself from him; but you, my one-souled friend*[^25]. He calls him one-souled, as one in Christ. So the Church has what to bear outside, what to groan over within: yet let it reckon its enemies outside and within; outside more easily avoidable, within more difficultly bearable.

**5.** So let our Lord say, let Christ say with us, the whole Christ: *O Lord, hear my prayer, receive in your ears my entreaty*[^26]. This is, *hear;* which is, *receive in your ears*: it is a repetition, a confirmation. *In your truth hear me, in your justice.* Do not take it just any way, when it is said, *in your justice.* For it is a commendation of grace, lest each one of us think it his own justice. For this is the justice of God, which, that you may have it, God gave. For those who wished to glory in their own justice, what does the apostle say of them? *I bear them witness*, he says, *that they have a zeal of God.* Speaking of the Jews: *They have a zeal of God*, he says, *but not according to knowledge*[^27]. What is, *not according to knowledge*? For what useful knowledge do you commend? is it that which, when it is alone, puffs up; which, unless it is accompanied by love, does not edify? Surely not that itself; but that knowledge which is the companion of love, the teacher of humility[^28]. See whether it is that: *They have a zeal of God*, he says, *but not according to knowledge.* Let him expound what knowledge he means: *Being ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not been made subject to the justice of God*[^29]. Who, then, wish to establish their own justice? Those who, what they have done well, impute to themselves, what badly, to God: altogether perverse. Finally, they will be upright, when they turn this very thing around. Perverse for this, because what is bad, to God; what good, to yourself: you will be upright, when what you do badly, to yourself; what you do well, to God. For you would not from being impious live justly, unless made just by him who justifies the impious[^30]. So, he says: *In your truth hear me, in your justice*, not in mine; that *I may be found in him not having my own justice, which is from the law, but that which is from faith*[^31]. Behold: *In your justice hear me.* For when I look back upon myself, I find nothing else mine but sin.

**6.** [v. 2.] *And do not enter into judgment with your servant*[^32]. Who wish to enter into judgment with him, except those who, being ignorant of the justice of God, wish to establish their own? *What is it that we have fasted, and you did not see, that we have afflicted our souls, and you did not know*[^33]? as though saying: We have done what you commanded; why do you not render what you promised? God answers you: That you may receive what I promised, I will give; that you might do that for which you would receive, I gave. Finally, to such proud men the prophet speaks: *Why do you wish to contend with me in judgment? You have all forsaken me, says the Lord*[^34]. Why do you wish to enter into judgment with me, and to mention your justices? Mention your justices; I know your crimes. How shall I approve justice, where I shall condemn pride? Rightly this humble man in the body of Christ, learning from the head that he is meek and humble of heart[^35]: *Do not enter*, he says, *into judgment with your servant.* Let us not quarrel; I do not wish to have a case with you, that I should set forth my justice, you convict my iniquity: *Do not enter into judgment with your servant.* Why this? why does he fear? *For no living man will be justified before you. Every living man*, surely living here, living in the flesh, a living man about to die, a man born, living from men, from Adam, living an Adam; every man living thus can perhaps be justified before himself, not before you. How, before himself? Pleasing himself, displeasing you: *but before you no living man will be justified.* So do not enter into judgment with me, O Lord, my God. However upright I seem to myself, you bring forth from your treasury the rule, you fit me to it, and I am found crooked. *Do not enter into judgment with your servant.* Well, *with your servant.* It is unworthy of you to enter into judgment with your servant, nor indeed with your friend: for: *I say to you, my friends*[^36], you would not say, unless you yourself had made friends out of servants. Though you call me friend, I confess myself a servant: I am in need of mercy, a fugitive I return, I seek peace; I am not worthy to be called your son: *Do not enter into judgment with your servant; for in your sight no living man will be justified.* *Before death praise no man*[^37]: altogether *every living man.* What of the rams themselves, what of the apostles themselves, of whose offspring it is said: *Bring to the Lord the sons of rams*[^38]? Of these is Paul, who says that he is not perfect: *Not that I have already received*, he says, *or am already perfect*[^39]. Finally, brothers, that you may quickly recognize it, they themselves learned to pray what we pray, to them was given the rule of asking, by the heavenly Jurist: *So pray*[^40], he says: And when he had set certain things before, he set this too for our rams to say, the leaders of the sheep, and the chief members of the Shepherd and gatherer of the one flock; they themselves learned to say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^41]. They did not say: Thanks to you, who have forgiven us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors; but: *Forgive, as we forgive.* But surely they were now praying as believers, surely now as apostles: for that Lord's prayer is given rather to believers. If only those debts were meant which are forgiven through Baptism, it would fit catechumens rather to pray: *Forgive us our debts.* So let the apostles say, let them say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.* And when it is said to them: Why do you say this? what are your debts? let them answer: *For in your sight no living man will be justified.*

**7.** [v. 3.] *For the enemy has persecuted my soul, he has humbled my life on the earth*[^42]. See us, see our head for us: *For the enemy has persecuted my soul.* Both the devil persecuted the soul of Christ, and Judas the soul of his master: and now for the persecution of the body of Christ the same devil remains; but a Judas succeeds Judas. So there is no lack of one of whom the body too may say: *For the enemy has persecuted my soul, he has humbled my life on the earth. He has humbled*, he says, *my life on the earth.* In another place it is said: *They have bent down my soul*[^43]. For what does whoever persecutes do with us, except that, the heavenly hope abandoned, we may savor the earth, and, yielding to the persecutor, love earthly things? And they indeed, as far as in them lies, do this: but let this not befall us, to whom it is said: *If you have risen with Christ, savor the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are on earth: for you are dead*[^44]. For no living man will be justified before God. So they, whether by openly raging, or by secretly lying in wait, try to bring our life down to the earth: against these let us watch, that we may be able to say: *For our conversation is in the heavens*[^45]. *He has humbled*, he says, *my life on the earth*, the enemy.

**8.** *They have set me in dark places, like the dead of the age*[^46]. This you hear more readily of the head, and more readily recognize it in the head. For he became dead for us, but not a dead one of the age. For who are the dead of the age? and how was he not a dead one of the age? The dead of the age, deservedly dead, receiving the wages of iniquity, and drawing death from the propagation of sin; from that voice by which it is said: *For I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins my mother nourished me in the womb*[^47]. But he came through a virgin, taking flesh, not the iniquity of the flesh, taking clean flesh, cleansing flesh. But him whom they thought a sinner, they reckoned as a dead one of the age. But he who in another psalm said: *What I did not plunder, I then paid back*[^48]; and who said in the Gospel: *Behold, the prince of the world comes*[^49], the ruler of death, the persuader of the evil work, the exactor of punishment; *He comes*, he says, *and in me he will find nothing*[^50]. What is, *in me he will find nothing*? No fault, nothing for which I ought to die. *But that all may know that I do the will*, he says, *of my Father, arise, let us go hence*[^51]. That I may die, he says, I do the will of my Father, but I am not worthy of death. I have done nothing for which I should die, but I do so that I may die; that they may be freed through the death of the innocent one, who had reason to die. *They have set me in dark places*, as it were in hell, as it were in the tomb, as it were in the very passion; *like the dead of the age*, him who says: *I have become like a man without help, free among the dead*[^52]. What is, *free*? why *free*? Because everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin[^53]. Finally, he would not loose from chains, unless he were free from chains. He, free, killed death, bound the chain, took captive the captivity, and they set him in dark places like the dead of the age.

**9.** [v. 4.] *And*, he says, *my spirit suffered weariness in me*[^54]. Remember: *My soul is sorrowful even to death*[^55]. See the one voice. Does not the very passing from the head to the members, from the members to the head, appear? *My spirit*, he says, *suffered weariness in me.* We recognize it: *My soul is sorrowful even to death.* But there too we were. For he transfigured into himself the body of our humility, conforming it to the body of his glory; and our old man was nailed to the cross with him[^56]. *In me my heart was troubled. In me*, he says, not in others. For they deserted me, and those who had clung withdrew, and, because they saw me dying, they thought me something other; and they were conquered by the robber, who then believed, when they failed[^57].

**10.** [v. 5.] Then to the members. *I remembered the days of old*[^58]. Did he remember the days of old, through whom every day was made? But the body speaks, each one justified by his grace speaks, cleaving to him in love and devout humility; he speaks, and says: *I remembered the days of old, I meditated on all your works.* Since surely you made all things good, and nothing would stand that was not established by you. Your creation has become a spectacle for me: I sought in the work the craftsman, and in all created things the creator. Why this? to what end this, except that he might understand that whatever good was in himself was made by him; lest, being ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish his own, he should not be subject to the justice of God[^59]; that the earlier voice might fit him: *In your truth and in your justice*[^60]? In all the works of God, then, and in the meditation of all the works of God, he instills grace, commends grace, glories that he has found grace, the grace by which we were freely saved: because indeed we were freely saved. Why do you glory in your own justice? why do you exalt yourself, being ignorant of the justice of God? You gave perhaps something, that you might be saved: what did you give, that you might be a man? Look back, then, upon the founder of your life, the author of your substance and justice and salvation. *Meditate on the works of his hands*, because the justice too which is in you, you will find belongs to his hand. Hear the apostle teaching you this: *Not of works*, he says, *lest perhaps anyone be lifted up*[^61]. Do we not have good works? We have plainly; but see what follows: *For we are his handiwork*[^62], he said. *We are his handiwork*: did he perhaps wish to mention the nature by which we are men, calling this handiwork? Not plainly; he was speaking of works: *Not*, he says, *of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up.* But let us not conjecture; let it follow: *For we are his handiwork, created in Christ Jesus in good works*[^63]. Do not, then, think that you work anything, except insofar as you are evil. Turn yourself away from your own work, to the work of him who made you: he forms; and may he reform what he had formed and you destroyed. For that you might be, he himself worked; that you may be good, if you are good, he himself works. *With trembling*, he says, *and fear work out your own salvation*[^64]. If we work out our own salvation, why with fear, why with trembling, since what we work is in our power? Hear why with fear and trembling: *For it is God who works in you both to will and to work, for good will*[^65]. So with fear and trembling, that it may delight our craftsman to work in the valley. For so he works as it were in the cast-down, who judges among the nations, and fills up the ruins. *On the works of your hands I meditated.* So I have seen, and looked upon your works; because nothing good can be in us, unless it be done by you, who made us.

**11.** [v. 6.] And what did I do, when I saw that from you is every best gift, and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no change, nor shadow of a moment[^66]? When I saw this, turned away from the evil work which I had done in myself, *I stretched out my hands to you. I stretched out*, he says, *my hands to you: my soul is like a land without water to you*[^67]. Rain upon me, he says, to make good fruit. For the Lord will give sweetness, that our land may give its fruit[^68]. *I stretched out my hands to you: my soul is like a land without water*, not to myself, but *to you.* I can thirst for you, I cannot water myself. *My soul is like a land without water to you*: because my soul thirsted for the living God[^69]. When shall I come, except when he comes? My soul thirsted for the living God; because *my soul is like a land without water to you.* The sea abounds, it floods, it is plentiful, it surges, but it is bitter. The water was separated, the dry land appeared, my soul[^70]; water it, because it is *like a land without water to you.*

**12.** [v. 7.] *Quickly hear me, O Lord*[^71]. For what delay is there, when I thirst so, to inflame my thirst? You deferred the rain, that, drinking it in, I might soak it up, not reject what you would pour in. If, then, for this you deferred it, now give it; for *my soul is like a land without water to you. Quickly hear me, O Lord; my spirit has failed.* Let your Spirit fill me, because *my spirit has failed*: this is the reason that you should quickly hear me, because *my spirit has failed.* I have now been made poor in spirit; make me blessed in the kingdom of heaven[^72]. For in whom his own spirit lives, he is proud, by his own spirit he is exalted against God. Let there come about in him for good what is written elsewhere: *You will take away their spirit, and they will fail, and will be turned into their dust*[^73]; that, confessing, they may say: *Remember that we are dust*[^74]. But when they have said: *Remember that we are dust;* then let them say: *My soul is like a land without water to you.* For what is so much a land without water as dust? But *quickly hear me, O Lord*: rain upon me, strengthen me, lest I be dust which the wind casts from the face of the earth[^75]. *Quickly hear me, O Lord; my spirit has failed*: let my want not be deferred. You took away my spirit, that I might fail, and be turned into my dust, and say to you: *My soul is like a land without water to you*: do also what follows in that psalm: *You will send forth your Spirit, and they will be created; and you will renew the face of the earth*[^76]. If, then, there is any new creature in Christ, the old things have passed away[^77]. In its own spirit the old things have passed away, in your Spirit the new things have been made.

**13.** *Do not turn your face away from me*[^78]. You turned it away from the proud. For at one time I abounded, and in my abundance I was exalted: *For I said* at one time *in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever. I said: I shall not be moved, in my abundance*, being ignorant of your justice, and establishing my own[^79]; but you: *O Lord, in your will you gave strength to my beauty*[^80]. *I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved;* but in truth from you was for me whatever I abounded in. And to prove that this was from you for me, *you turned your face away from me, and I was made troubled*[^81]. After this trouble in which I was made, because you turned your face away; after the weariness of my spirit, after my heart troubled in me, from this, that you turned your face away; I have now become like a land without water to you: *Do not turn your face away.* You turned it away from the proud, restore it to the humble. *Do not turn your face away from me;* because if you turn it away, *I will be like those who go down into the pit.* What is, *those who go down into the pit*? *The sinner, when he comes into the depth of evils, will despise*[^82]. They go down into the pit, who lose even confession: against which it is said: *Let not the well shut its mouth over me*[^83]. This depth Scripture very often calls a pit, into which depth when a sinner has come, he despises. What is, 'he despises'? He now reckons no providence; or if he reckons it, he does not think himself to belong to it. He sets before himself the license of sinning, without hope of pardon, the reins of iniquity let loose. He does not say: I will return to God, that he may return to me; nor does he hear: *Turn to me, and I will return to you*[^84]: since, coming into the depth of evils, he despises. *For from a dead man*, he says, *as from one who is not, confession perishes*[^85]. *Do not*, then, *turn your face away from me, and I will be like those who go down into the pit.*

**14.** [v. 8.] *Make me hear your mercy in the morning; for in you I have hoped*[^86]. Behold, I am in the night; but in you I have hoped, until the nocturnal iniquity passes by[^87]. *For we have, as Peter says, the surer prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts*[^88]. So he calls *morning*, after the end of the age, when we shall see what in the age we believe. *For in the morning you will hear my voice; in the morning I will stand before you and will contemplate*[^89]. *Make me hear your mercy in the morning, for in you I have hoped.* For if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience[^90]. The night seeks patience, the day will grant gladness. *Make me hear your mercy in the morning; for in you I have hoped.*

**15.** But here what, until the morning comes? For it does not suffice to hope in the morning; something must be done. Why do something? Because he says in another psalm: *I sought out God in the day of my tribulation*[^91]: as it were in the time of my night I sought out God. How did you seek him out? *With my hands at night before him; and I was not deceived.* With the hands God is to be sought in the night. What is, *with the hands*? With good works. *Before him: For when you do alms, do not sound a trumpet; and your Father who sees in secret will repay you*[^92]. Because, then, the morning is so to be hoped for, and this night so to be borne, and in that very patience until the day dawns one must persevere, what meanwhile must be done here? Are you perhaps about to do anything by yourself, that you may deserve to be led to the morning? *Make known to me, O Lord, the way in which I should walk*[^93]. Therefore he kindled the prophetic lamp; therefore he sent the Lord himself as it were in the earthenware of the flesh, who would say even: *My strength has dried up like a potsherd*[^94]. Walk to the prophecy, walk to the lamp of the things foretold, walk to the words of God. You do not yet see the Word in the beginning, God with God[^95]; walk to the form of the servant, you will be led to the form of the Lord. *Make known to me, O Lord, the way in which I should walk; for to you I have lifted up my soul.* *I have lifted it up to you*, not against you. With you is the fountain of life[^96]. *To you I have lifted up my soul*, as it were I brought a vessel to the fountain: fill me, then, *for to you I have lifted up my soul.*

**16.** [v. 9.] *Take me out from my enemies, O Lord; for I have fled to you*[^97]. I who once fled from you, *have fled to you.* For Adam fled from the face of God, and hid himself among the trees of paradise[^98], that of him it might be said in the book of Job: *Like a servant fleeing his master, and reaching a shadow*[^99]. He fled from the face of his master, and reached a shadow: for he fled to the shadow among the trees of paradise. Woe if he remain in the shadow; lest afterward it be said: *All things have passed away like a shadow*[^100]. *Take me out from my enemies.* I do not here think of men as enemies: *We have not a wrestling against flesh and blood*[^101]. But against whom? *Against the principalities and powers, the rulers of the world*[^102]. Of what world? For not of heaven and earth; for they do not rule what they did not make. *The rulers of the world;* but of what world? *Of this darkness*[^103]. Of what darkness? Surely of the wicked. *For you were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^104]. *The rulers of the world, of this darkness*, the rulers of the wicked; against these you have a struggle. A great battle is yours, not to see the enemy and to conquer. *Against the rulers of the world of this darkness*; the devil, namely, and his angels: not the rulers of that world of which it is said: *And the world was made through him*; but of that world of which it is said: *And the world did not know him*[^105]. *Take me out from my enemies, O Lord; for I have fled to you. From my enemies;* not Judas, but him who filled Judas. Him I endure when seen, him I fight against unseen. For Judas received the morsel, and Satan entered into him[^106]: so that this David suffered persecution from his son. How many Judases Satan fills, receiving the morsel unworthily to their own judgment! For he who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself[^107]. What is given is not evil, but the good is given to the evil for judgment. It cannot go well with one who receives badly what is good. So *take me out from my enemies; for I have fled to you.* For where would I flee? *Where shall I go from your Spirit? If I ascend into heaven, you are there: if I descend into hell, you are present*[^108]. What, then, remains? *If I take up my wings as a dove, and fly into the uttermost parts of the sea*; that is, that in hope I may dwell at the end of the age: *for even there your hand will lead me, and your right hand will bring me through*[^109]. *Take me out from my enemies; for I have fled to you, O Lord.*

**17.** [v. 10.] *Teach me to do your will; for you are my God*[^110]. O confession, O directive! *For you are*, he says, *my God.* Let me run to another to be remade, if I was made by another. You are my whole; *because you are my God.* Shall I seek a father for the sake of an inheritance? *You are my God*, not only the giver of the inheritance, but the inheritance itself: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance*[^111]. Shall I seek a master for the sake of redemption? *You are my God.* Shall I seek a patron for the sake of liberation? *You are my God.* Finally, created, do I desire to be recreated? *You are my God*, who are my Creator, who created me through your Word, and recreated me through the Word. But you created me through the Word, God abiding with you; you recreated me through the Word made flesh for us. *Teach*, then, *me to do your will; for you are my God.* If you do not teach me, I will do my own will, and my God will desert me. *Teach me to do your will; for you are my God. Teach me*: for it is not the case that you are my God, and I shall be my own master. See how grace is commended. Hold this; drink this in, let no one shake it from your heart; lest you have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge; lest, being ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish your own, you be not subject to the justice of God[^112]. You surely recognize the words of the apostle. Say, then, these things: *Teach me to do your will; for you are my God.*

**18.** [vv. 10-12.] *Your good Spirit*[^113], not my evil one: *Your good Spirit will lead me into the right land.* Because my evil spirit led me into the perverse land. And what did I deserve? what good works of mine are reckoned without your help, by which I might obtain it and be worthy to be led by your Spirit into the right land? What works of mine, or what merits of mine? *For your name's sake, O Lord, you will give me life*[^114]. Attend, then, as much as you can, to the commendation of grace, by which you were freely saved. *For your name's sake, O Lord, you will give me life. Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory*[^115]. *For your name's sake, O Lord, you will give me life: in your justice*, not in mine, not because I deserved it, but because you have mercy. For if I showed my merit, I would deserve nothing from you but punishment. You tore away my merits, you grafted in your gifts. *For your name's sake, O Lord, you will give me life: in your justice you will lead my soul out of tribulation; and in your mercy you will lead my enemies to destruction. And you will destroy all who trouble my soul; for I am your servant*[^116].
