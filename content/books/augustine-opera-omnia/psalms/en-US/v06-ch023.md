# Exposition of Psalm 144

**1.** [v. 1.] The title of this psalm is brief in the number of words, but weighty in the burden of mysteries: *Of David himself, against Goliath.* This battle, done in the time of our fathers, your charity recalls with me from the holy Scriptures. For when the foreigners fought against the people of God, one challenged one, Goliath David[^1]: in which contest the will of God would be proved for the victory of one side or the other. But why are we busy about the victory, when we behold the challenger and the challenged? Impiety challenged piety, pride challenged humility, finally the devil challenged Christ. Why do you wonder that the devil was conquered? He was great in stature of body; but the other small in stature, great in faith. The holy David took warlike arms, to go forth against Goliath. These arms, on account of his age, and his small stature of body, as we said, he was not able to carry. He cast aside what burdened, not what helped: he took five stones from the river, and put them in his shepherd's vessel. Armed with these bodily, but with the name of God spiritually, he went forth, and conquered. This indeed David did: but let us search out the mysteries. For we had set forth this title as brief in the number of words, but weighty in the burden of mysteries. Let that apostolic sentence come to mind: *All these things happened to them in figure*[^2]; lest we seem shamelessly to seek something hidden, where it could be said that all was said simply without the depth of a mystery. We have, then, an authority making us attentive to seek, watchful to investigate, devout to hear, faithful to believe, eager to do. In David, Christ: but as you are accustomed to understand, learned in his school, Christ head and body. Do not, then, so hear anything from the person of Christ as though it did not pertain to you, who are members of Christ. This as it were foundation laid, see what follows.

**2.** You know that the first people was burdened with many visible and bodily sacraments; with circumcision, with a certain busy priesthood, and a temple full of figures, with manifold kinds of holocausts and sacrifices. These our David, as arms that pressed down, not that helped, laid aside. *For if a law had been given that could give life, truly justice would be from the law*[^3]. Why, then, the Law? It follows: *But Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*[^4]. Finally, this David, that is, Christ head and body, at the time of the revelation of the New Testament, at the time of insinuating and commending the grace of God, what did he do? He laid aside the arms, he took five stones[^5]: he laid aside, as we said, the burdening arms; so the sacraments of the Law, those sacraments of the Law which are not imposed on the Gentiles, he laid aside, which we do not observe. For you remember how many things we read in the Old Law, and do not observe; but yet sent before and set forth for some signification we understand them: not that we cast aside the Law of God, but that, the promise being fulfilled, we do not celebrate the sacraments that promised. For what they promised has come. For the grace of the New Testament was veiled in the Law; in the Gospel it is revealed. We removed the veil, we recognized what was veiled: but we recognized it in the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, our head and Savior, who was crucified for us; at whose crucifixion the veil of the temple too was torn[^6]. Finally, he laid aside the arms, as the burdens of the sacraments of the Old Law; and took the Law itself. For the five stones signify the five books of Moses. So he took those five stones from the river. You know what the river is. For the mortal age slips away, and whatever comes into the world flows past. There were, then, in the river, as it were in that first people, stones; there they were useless, they were idle, profited nothing, passed over the rivers. What did David do, that the Law itself might be useful? He took grace. For the Law cannot be fulfilled without grace: *For the fullness of the Law is love*[^7]. And whence is this love? See whether it is not from grace. *Love*, he says, *of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^8]. Because, then, grace made the Law be fulfilled, but grace is signified by milk; for this is free in the flesh, where the mother does not seek to receive, but is busy to give; this the mother gives freely, and is saddened if there is no one to receive: how, then, did David show that the Law cannot work without grace, except when, wishing to join those five stones, by which the Law in the five books was signified, to grace, he put them in his shepherd's vessel, with which he was accustomed to milk? Armed with these, surely armed with grace, and therefore presuming not on himself, but on his Lord, he went forth against the proud Goliath, boasting of himself, presuming on himself. He took one stone, threw it, struck the enemy on the forehead; he fell from that part of the body where he did not have the sign of Christ. This too you may attend to: he set out five stones, he sent one. The five books were read, but unity conquered. *For the fullness of the Law*, as we mentioned a little before, *is love*: and the apostle says: *Bearing with one another in love, being eager to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace*[^9]. Then, the giant struck and cast down, he took away his sword, and with it cut off his head[^10]. And this our David did, he cast down the devil from his own: but when his great men believe, whom the devil held in his hand, and with whom he slaughtered the other souls, they turn their tongues against the devil; and so Goliath's head is cut off with his own sword. We have treated the mystery of the title, as the brevity of time allows; let us see what the Psalm itself has.

**3.** *Blessed be the Lord my God, who teaches my hands for the fight, my fingers for war*[^11]. It is our voice, if we are the body of Christ. Let us bless the Lord our God who teaches our hands for the fight, our fingers for war. It seems a repetition of the same thing: what is, our hands for the fight; is, our fingers for war. Or is there some difference between hands and fingers? With the fingers surely the hands too work: so not absurdly we take the fingers as set for the hands. Yet in the fingers we recognize the division of the working, and yet the root of unity. See that grace: the apostle says: *To one indeed through the Spirit is given the word of wisdom, to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith in the same Spirit, to another the gifts of healings in one Spirit, to another kinds of tongues, to another prophecy, to another the discerning of spirits. But all these one and the same Spirit works, dividing to each his own as he wills*[^12]. *To one* that, *to another* this; these are divisions of the working: *all these one and the same Spirit works;* it is the root of unity. With these fingers, then, the body of Christ fights, going forth into war, going forth into the fight.

**4.** Now to mention the kinds of fights and wars is perhaps long, and easier to wage than to explain. We have one war which the apostle mentions: *You have not a wrestling against flesh and blood*[^13], that is, against men from whom you seem to suffer troubles; you do not fight against them themselves: *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of the world*[^14]. And lest you understand the world of heaven and earth, he showed what he meant: *Of this darkness*, he says. The world, namely, not that which was made through him; because: *And the world was made through him*: but the world which did not know him; because: *And the world did not know him*[^15]. This darkness is not by nature, but by will. For the soul does not shine by itself: because humbly and truly it sings: *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, illumine my darkness*[^16]: and: *With you*, it says, *is the fountain of life; in your light we shall see light*[^17]; not in our light, but *in your light.* For our eyes too are called lights; and yet if the light from outside is lacking, even healthy and open they will remain in darkness. So we wage war against the rulers of this darkness, namely the rulers of the unbelievers, the devil and his angels, the rulers of his sword by which the devil fights against the faithful. But as, Goliath being laid low, his sword is taken away, that Goliath's own head may be cut off with his own sword[^18]; so when those unbelievers themselves believe, it is said to them: *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^19]. You fought from Goliath's hand, now in the hand of Christ take Goliath's head.

**5.** This is one battle: but the other is for each one in himself. This kind of war was just now read from the apostolic Letter: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; so that you do not do the things that you wish*[^20]. And this is a grave war, and what is more troublesome, an internal one. In which war if each one is victor, those whom he does not see as enemies he will at once overcome. For the devil and his angels tempt only what is fleshly that has dominion in you. For how do we conquer those enemies whom we do not see, except because we feel our inner fleshly motions? With these we struggle, and them we strike. In the love of money avarice has dominion: to you, with avarice having dominion, the devil sets forth from outside gain with fraud. For very often you do not attain gain unless you commit fraud. So he sets forth from outside, to your avarice which you have not conquered within, which you have not tamed, which you have not subjected to yourself; he sets forth, like a bad master of the games to his athlete, fraud and gain, the work and the prize: Do it, and take it. But if you tread down avarice, if it does not have dominion within you, which, feeling, you conquer; for you do not feel the devil lying in wait: if, then, you have tamed avarice, you attend to the other one setting forth the work and the prize. What did that one set forth? Fraud and gain. What does this one set forth? Innocence and a crown. Do it and take it, both that one says, and this one. Now you, the inner fighter, if you are not conquered by avarice, but victor over avarice; you attend to that one, you conquer this one. For you distinguish both, and say: Here I see the work and the prize, but there the bait and the snare. For you say nothing in yourself which does not pertain to you. For from sin you are divided against yourself. You draw the propagation of desire and the offshoot of death: you have what to fight against in yourself, you have what to overcome in yourself. But you have one whom you may call upon, to help you fighting, and to crown you conquering, who made you when you did not exist.

**6.** How, you say, shall I conquer? Behold, the apostle himself sets forth a most difficult battle, and how laborious it is, or perhaps insuperable if I do not understand, he himself shows. *The flesh*, he says, *lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; so that you do not do the things that you wish*[^21]. How do you bid me conquer, when he says: *So that you do not do the things that you wish*? You ask how? Attend to the grace of the shepherd's vessel, put the stone from the river into the milk-vessel. Behold, I too say to you, rather Truth itself says to you. You altogether do not do what you wish, the flesh fighting against your spirit. If for this battle you presume on yourself, you must be admonished, lest you have heard in vain: *Exult to God our helper*[^22]. For if through yourself you fulfilled the whole, a helper would not be necessary. Again, if you yourself by your own will did nothing, a helper would not be called for: for a helper helps one who is doing something. Finally, when he had said: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; so that you do not do the things that you wish;* and had set you before yourself, as it were failing in yourself, he at once sent you to the helper: *But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not now under the Law*[^23]. For he who is under the law does not fulfill the law, but is pressed by the law; just as that David was under the arms. So if you are led by the Spirit, see who will help you, that you may fulfill what you wish. Your helper, your upholder, your hope, who teaches your hands for the fight, and your fingers for war. *For manifest*, he says, *are the works of the flesh, which are fornications, uncleannesses, the service of idols, luxury, sorceries, contentions, enmities, drunkennesses, revelings, and the like; which I foretell to you, as I have foretold, that those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^24]. Not those who fight against such things, but those who do such things. For when you fight, it is one work; when you conquer, it is another work; when you have peace and rest, it is another work. While I show these things by a few examples, take notice. Some gain is suggested, it delights; it has fraud, but the gain is great; it delights, you do not consent: see the battle, still it is urged, still it is pressed, still it is deliberated; therefore he who fights is in peril. We have seen the battle, let us see the rest. He despised justice, that he might commit fraud; he was conquered: he despised the gain, that he might serve justice; he conquered. In these three, I grieve over the conquered, I fear for the one fighting, I rejoice with the conqueror. But even he who conquered—did he altogether bring it about in himself that money does not at all attend him, or excite nothing of delight in him, however conquerable, however contemptible, however not only such that he does not consent to it, but such that he does not even deign to fight with it? Yet there is in it some tickling of delight. This tickling—and that enemy now neither fights nor reigns: yet it is there, and as it were lingers in the mortal flesh, which will not be. For the whole will be led into victory, but afterward: but now: *The body indeed is dead on account of sin*; and therefore in that same body there is sin, even if sin does not reign: *but the spirit is life on account of justice. But if he who raised Christ from the dead dwells in you; he who raised Christ from the dead will give life also to your mortal bodies, on account of his Spirit dwelling in you*[^25]. There now there will be neither what to fight, nor what to tickle; all will yield in peace. For one nature does not fight against another nature, but as in a house husband and wife. If they disagree against each other, troublesome and perilous is the labor; if the husband is conquered and the wife rules, perverse peace: but if the wife is subjected to the ruling husband, upright peace; yet not one nature from another, because the woman was made for the man from the man. Your flesh, your wife, your maidservant: reckon it whatever you will, you must subject it; and if you fight, that the fight may profit. For this is expedient, that the lower be subjected to the higher; that he too who wishes that to be subjected to him which is lower than himself, be subjected to the one higher than himself. Recognize the order, seek peace. You to God, the flesh to you. What more just? what more beautiful? You to the greater, the lesser to you: serve him who made you, that what was made for your sake may serve you. For we do not know this order, nor do we commend this order: The flesh to you and you to God; but, You to God, and the flesh to you. But if you despise You to God, you will never bring it about that the flesh be subject to you. You who do not obey the Lord, are tormented by the servant. Surely, unless first you to God, that then the flesh to you, will you be able to say these words: *Blessed be the Lord my God, who teaches my hands for the fight, and my fingers for war*? You wish to fight untaught, you will be condemned conquered. First, then, subject yourself to God; then, with him teaching and helping you, fight, and say: *Who teaches my hands for the fight, and my fingers for war.*

**7.** [v. 2.] And when you fight, because while you fight, you are in peril, say what follows, set in the peril of fighting. *My mercy*[^26]. I will not be conquered. What is this: *My mercy*? You offer me mercy, and exist merciful in me? or: You have granted me that I too may be merciful? For by nothing is the enemy so conquered, as when we are merciful. He altogether prepares slanders for the judgment, and cannot object false things, because there is no one before whom. For if he dealt with us before a human judge, he could deceive him by lying, and burden us with false accusations: but because our case with him is before such a judge, who cannot be deceived; therefore he goes about to seduce us to sin, that he may have true things to object. And where perhaps human frailty succumbs to some of his frauds, let there follow in confession the work of humility, let it be exercised in works of mercy and devotion. All things are blotted out, when from a true heart and full confidence we say to him who sees: *Forgive us, as we also forgive*[^27]. Say with your whole heart, say with all confidence, say securely: *Forgive us, as we also forgive;* or do not forgive, if we do not forgive. And even if you do not say: Do not forgive, if we do not forgive; he altogether does not forgive, if we do not forgive. For it will not be that, in order that you be an unpunished sinner, he will be a lying promiser. Do you wish, he says, that I forgive? Forgive. There is another work of mercy: Do you wish that I give? Give. It is set in one place in the Gospel: *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given you*[^28]. I hold something, he says, against you, you too hold something against another; forgive, and I forgive. You ask something of me, another asks something of you; give, and I give. And what forgives? what gives? Is it not love? And whence love except through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^29]? If, then, through works of mercy our enemy is conquered, and we could not have works of mercy unless we had love; but there would be no love in us, unless we received it through the Holy Spirit: he teaches our hands for the fight, and our fingers for war; to him rightly we say: *My mercy*, from whom we have even that we may be merciful. *For judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy*[^30].

**8.** Do you think the works of mercy small? Let me say something even about these. Attend first to that sentence drawn from holy Scripture, which I just now mentioned: *For judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy*[^31]. Without mercy will he be judged who did not do mercy before he was judged. What then? what follows? *But mercy is exalted above judgment*[^32]. What is this, brothers: *Mercy is exalted above judgment*? Mercy is set above judgment: in whom the work of mercy has been found, even if he has had perhaps something in judgment by which he may be punished, as it were by the wave of mercy the fire of sin is extinguished. For *mercy is exalted above judgment.* But what? even when he comes to the aid of such, and frees such, and pardons such, is God unjust? Far from it. There too he is just: mercy does not take away justice from him, nor justice mercy. See whether he is not just: Forgive, and I forgive; give, and I give. See whether he is not just: *With what measure you have measured, with it shall it be measured back to you*[^33]. To this end: *With what measure*: for the measure is not of the same kind; but to this end the same measure: Pardon, and I pardon. There is with you the measure of pardon to be given; you will find with me the measure of pardon to be received: there is with you the measure of giving what you have; you will find with me the measure of receiving what you do not have.

**9.** *My mercy and my refuge, my upholder and my rescuer*[^34]. The fighter here labors much, holding the flesh lusting against the spirit. Hold what you hold. Then it will be fully what you wish, when death has been swallowed up in victory[^35]; when this mortal body, raised up, is transferred into the angelic condition, and flies up into the heavenly quality. *The dead*, he says, *in Christ will rise first: then we too, the living who remain, at the coming of the Lord, will together be caught up with them in the clouds to meet Christ in the air; and so we shall always be with the Lord*[^36]. There death will be swallowed up in victory: there it will be said: *Where, O death, is your striving? where, O death, is your sting*[^37]? For there will be left, neither in mind nor in body, anything that rebels against the love of God. Full victory, full peace. Of this, to us fighting, it is said: *Come, sons, hear me; the fear of the Lord I will teach you*[^38]. You are in battle, you struggle in contention; and yet you desire a certain rest. *Who is the man who wishes life, and loves to see good days*[^39]? Who is there who does not say: I? There is life, there are good days, where nothing lusts against the spirit: where it is not said, Fight; but Rejoice. But who is there who wishes these days? Surely every man says: I. Hear what follows. I see that you labor, I see that you dwell in battle, in peril: hear what follows; he teaches the hands for the fight, the fingers for war. *Restrain your tongue from evil, and let your lips not speak guile; turn away from evil, and do good*[^40]. For how will you be able to do good, unless you turn away from evil? Why do I ask you to clothe, when you still strip? why do I ask you to give, when you still plunder? *Turn away from evil, and do good.* Let not the poor man first weep under you, that the poor man may rejoice over you. *Turn away from evil, and do good.* With what reward? For now you fight. *Seek peace, and follow it*[^41]. Learn, say: *My mercy and my refuge, my upholder and my rescuer, my protector.* *Upholder*, that I may not fall; *rescuer*, that I may not stick fast; *protector*, that I may not be struck. *My protector, and in him I have hoped.* In all these, in my whole labor, in all my battles, in all my difficulties, in him I have hoped. *Who subdues my people under me.* Behold, our head speaks with us.

**10.** [v. 3.] *O Lord, what is man, that you have become known to him*[^42]? All that he is, is this *that you have become known to him. What is man, that you have become known to him? or the son of man, that you esteem him?* You esteem him, you reckon him of so much worth, you weigh him at so much: you arrange, you know under what to put him, above what to put him. For esteeming is, of how great a price each thing is. At how much did he esteem man, who for him poured out the blood of his Only-begotten? *What is man, that you have become known to him?* To whom, who became known? What is *the son of man, that you esteem him?* Since you weigh him at so much, esteem him at so much, you show that he is something precious. For God does not so esteem man, as man esteems man: when he finds a slave for sale, he buys a horse more dearly than a man. See at how much that one esteems you, that you may be able to say: *If God is for us, who is against us?* And at how much he esteemed you, *who did not spare his own Son, but for us all handed him over? How has he not also with him given us all things*[^43]? He who gave this provision to the one fighting, what does he keep for the one conquering? *I am*, he says, *the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^44]. This is the provision of those fighting, brought in from the Lord's granaries, with which the angels are fed: because: *Man ate the bread of angels*[^45]. But after the battles and this provision, what does he keep? what will he give to the victors, except what is said in another psalm: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life; that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord, and be protected as his temple*[^46]? *What is man, that you have become known to him? or the son of man, that you esteem him?*

**11.** [v. 4.] *Man has been made like to vanity;* and yet you have become known to him, and you esteem him. *Man has been made like to vanity*[^47]. To what vanity? To the times slipping by and flowing past. For this is called vanity in comparison to the ever-abiding and never-failing truth. For this creation too is of its own place. *For God filled the earth*, as it is written, *with his good things*[^48]. What is, *his*? Things fitting to himself. But all these earthly, fleeting, transitory things, if they are compared to that truth, where it was said: *I am who am*[^49]; all this which passes away is called vanity. For it vanishes through time, as smoke into the air. And what more shall I say, than what the apostle James said, wishing to bring proud men to humility? *For what is*, he says, *your life? It is a vapor appearing for a little; and afterward it will be done away*[^50]. So: *Man has been made like to vanity.* By sinning *he has been made like to vanity.* For when he was first created, he was made like to truth; but because he sinned, because he received what was deserved, *he has been made like to vanity. For iniquity*, he says in another psalm, *you have chastised man, and made his soul waste away like a spider*[^51]. Hence too this: *Man has been made like to vanity.* There what does he say: *Behold, you have set my days as old*[^52]. Here what does he say? *His days pass like a shadow*[^53]. Let man attend to himself in the days of his shadow, that he may do something worthy of his longed-for light; and if he is in the shadow of night, let him seek the day. For the day of this vanity, to a man who knows, is a day of tribulation; whether by some inconvenience and some trouble it harms us, or by some prosperity the world smiles, all is to be feared and groaned over, since human life on earth is a temptation[^54]: whence it is said: *All the day I walked in sadness*[^55]. We have need of consolations, and whatever God now provides for us, when he provides prosperously, it is not the joy of the blessed, but the consolation of the wretched. So let a man do something, I say, worthy of the longed-for light in these days of his shadow, and in the night let him seek out God, as it is written: *In the day of my tribulation I sought out God with my hands at night before him, and I was not deceived*[^56]. What day of tribulation does he mean, except that which he also calls night? *With my hands at night before him.* We are still in the night, and we keep watch by the lamp of prophecy. Something is promised which is still awaited: but what does the apostle Peter say? *We have the surer prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts*[^57]. That very day, our reward, is there. *In the morning you will hear my voice; in the morning I will stand before you, and will contemplate*[^58]. So work, although in the night, with your hands; that is, by good works seek out God, before that day comes which gladdens you, lest that come which saddens you. For see how securely you work, because you are not deserted by him whom you seek: *With my hands*, he says, *I sought out God at night before him*. That your Father who sees in secret may repay you[^59]; therefore, *before him.* Have within mercy, love; lest you do anything as it were out of zeal to please men. *With my hands*, with my works: in the shadow, in this life: where he himself sees, not where I am eager to please men. And what follows? *And I was not deceived. Man has been made like to vanity; his days pass like a shadow*: and yet you have become known to him, and you esteem him.

**12.** [v. 5.] *O Lord, incline your heavens, and come down: touch the mountains, and they will smoke. Flash forth lightning, and you will scatter them; send forth your arrows, and you will trouble them. Send forth your hand from on high; and take me out, and rescue me from many waters*[^60]. The body of Christ, humble David, full of grace, presuming on God, fighting in this world, calls upon the help of God: *Incline your heavens, and come down.* Who are the inclined heavens? The humbled apostles. For these *heavens declare the glory of God*[^61]: and of these heavens declaring the glory of God it is soon said: *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard: into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the world their words*[^62]. So when these heavens sent forth their voices through all lands, and did wonders, with the Lord flashing forth from them and thundering by miracles and precepts, they were thought to be gods come down from heaven to men. For certain of the Gentiles, thinking this, even wished to sacrifice to them. Then they, seeing undue honor offered to them, and being terrified, and detesting it, and correcting those who so erred, that they might show them the movement of their soul, tore their garments, and said: *Why do you do this? We too are men, subject to suffering, as you also*[^63]. And they began under these words to commend the excellence of our Lord Jesus Christ, humbling themselves that God might be commended; because the heavens had been inclined, that God might come down. *Incline*, then, *your heavens, and come down.* It was done. *Touch the mountains, and they will smoke*: the proud mountains, earthly elations, the swollen greatnesses: *Touch*, he says, *touch* these *mountains;* of your grace give to these mountains: *and they will smoke;* because they will confess their sins. The smoke of those confessing their sins will wring out even the tears of the humbled proud. *Touch the mountains, and they will smoke.* As long as untouched, they seem great to themselves. They will say: You are great, O Lord; the mountains too will say: *You alone are the most high over all the earth*[^64].

**13.** [v. 6.] But there are some conspiring, agreeing as one against the Lord, and against his Christ[^65]. They came together, they conspired. *Flash forth lightnings, and you will scatter them.* Grow frequent in your miracles, and their conspiracy will be dissolved. *Flash forth lightnings, and you will scatter them.* Now, terrified by miracles, they will not dare anything against you, and in those very miracles, growing afraid, they will hesitate. Who is this who can do such great things? who is this who is so exalted, whose name avails so much? When they say: Who is this, they will believe: you have flashed forth in miracles, and scattered their evil agreement. *Send forth your arrows, and you will trouble them. The sharp arrows of the mighty one*[^66]; let your precepts, your sayings, strike their heart. *Send forth your arrows, and you will trouble them.* Let the ill-healthy be wounded, that, well wounded, they may be healed; and let them now say, set in the Church and in the body of Christ, let them say with the Church: *For I am wounded by love*[^67]. *Send forth your arrows, and you will trouble them.*

**14.** *Send forth your hand from on high.* What afterward? what at the end? How does the body of Christ conquer? By heavenly help. For *the Lord himself will come in the voice of the archangel, and in the trumpet of God will come down from heaven*[^68]; the Savior himself of the body, the hand of God: *Send forth your hand from on high; and take me out, and rescue me from many waters.* What is, *from many waters*? From many peoples. What peoples? Foreigners, unbelievers, whether assailing from outside, or lying in wait within. *Take me out from many waters*, in which you exercised me, in which you rolled me to strip me of the filth. This is that water of contradiction[^69]. *Take me out, and rescue me from many waters.*

**15.** [vv. 7-8.] Let us now hear about those many waters, from which God will rescue the body of his Christ, from which God will rescue the humility of David. What is, *from many waters*? What did you say, lest the waters be understood otherwise? what did you call many waters? Hear what I said. *From the hand of foreign sons.* Hear, brothers, among whom we are, among whom we live, from whom we desire to be rescued. *Whose mouth has spoken vanity.* Today all of you, if you had not been gathered to these divine spectacles of the word of God, and lived this hour mingled with them, how many vanities you would hear! *Whose mouth has spoken vanity.* Finally, when they were speaking vanity, when would they hear you speaking truth? *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity*[^70].

**16.** [v. 9.] What of you among them, with the shepherd's vessel, having five stones? Tell me in another way: that same Law which you signified by five stones, signify it in another way too. *O God, a new song I will sing to you.* A new song is of grace; a new song is of the new man; a new song is of the New Testament. *I will sing to you*, he says, *a new song.* But lest you think grace departs from the Law, since rather through grace the Law is fulfilled: *On a psaltery of ten strings I will sing to you*[^71]. *On a psaltery of ten strings*, in the Law of the ten precepts: there I will sing to you, there I will rejoice for you, there I will sing to you a new song; because the fullness of the Law is love[^72]. But those who do not have love can carry the psaltery, cannot sing. I, therefore, he says, among the waters of contradiction will sing to you a new song; and never by their noise will the waters of contradiction bring it about that my psaltery be struck dumb: *On a psaltery of ten strings I will sing to you.*

**17.** [vv. 10-11.] *Who gives salvation to kings*[^73]: the mountains now smoking. *Who redeems David his servant.* You recognize David, be David. Whence does he redeem David his servant? whence does he redeem Christ? whence does he redeem the body of Christ? *From the evil sword rescue me.* *From the sword*, it does not suffice; he adds, *evil.* Without doubt there is a kind sword. What is the kind sword? Of which the Lord says: *I have not come to send peace on the earth, but a sword*[^74]. For he was to separate the faithful from the unbelievers, the sons from the parents, and to break up the other ties, with the sword cutting off the rottenness, but healing the members of Christ. So there is a kind sword, twice-sharpened, mighty with each edge, of the Old and the New Testament, by the narration of past things and the promise of things to come. This sword, then, is kind: but that one is evil with which they speak vanity; because that one is kind with which God speaks truth. So: *From the evil sword rescue me. The sons* indeed *of men, their teeth are arms and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword*[^75]: from this evil sword rescue me. What he just now called a sword, he earlier called many waters: *Rescue me from many waters*[^76]. What I called many waters, I call the evil sword. Finally, when he had spoken of many waters, he went on: *From the hand of foreign sons, whose mouth has spoken vanity*[^77]. And that you may know that they are meant; when he had said here too: *From the evil sword rescue me*, he went on: *And take me out from the hand of foreign sons, whose mouth has spoken vanity*[^78]: similarly as there. And what follows: *Their right hand is a right hand of iniquity;* this too he had set above, when he called them many waters. For you, lest you think the many waters good, he expounded them as the evil sword. So now let him expound what he said: *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* What vanity has their mouth spoken? and how is *their right hand a right hand of iniquity*?

**18.** [vv. 12-14.] *Whose sons are like new plants established in their youth*[^79]. He wishes to enumerate their happiness. Attend, sons of light, sons of peace; attend, sons of the Church, members of Christ; attend whom he calls foreigners, whom he calls foreign sons, whom he calls waters of contradiction, whom he calls the evil sword. Attend, I beg; because among these you are in peril, among their tongues you fight against the desires of your flesh: among their tongues, set in the hand of the devil, with which he fights, you have a wrestling, *not against flesh and blood, but against the principalities and powers, and rulers of this world of darkness*[^80], that is, of the wicked. Attend, that you may distinguish yourselves; attend, lest you think it is true happiness, which men either weak or malicious wish for themselves. Behold, brothers, surely he called them foreign sons, surely many waters, surely the evil sword. See the vanity which they speak, and beware lest you speak such things, beware lest, speaking such things, you imitate them. *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* What vanity has their mouth spoken, and what is their right hand of iniquity? Hear: *Whose sons are like new plants established from their youth; their daughters set in order, and adorned like the likeness of a temple: their storerooms full, overflowing from this into that: their sheep fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth; their oxen fat: there is no breach of the fence, nor outgoing; nor outcry in their streets*[^81]. So is this not happiness? I ask the sons of the kingdom of heaven, I ask the offspring of the resurrection unto eternity, I ask the body of Christ, the members of Christ, the temple of God: So is this not happiness, to have sons unharmed, daughters adorned, full storerooms, abundant flocks, no ruin—I do not say of a wall, but not even of a fence; no tumult and outcry in the streets, but quiet, peace, abundance, plenty of things in the homes, in the cities? So is this not happiness? or ought the just to flee it? or do you not find even the house of the just abounding in all these things, full of this happiness? Was not the house of Abraham abounding in gold, silver, sons, household, flocks[^82]? Did not Jacob the holy patriarch, fleeing from the face of his brother Esau into Mesopotamia, serving, made rich, return, and give thanks to the Lord his God, because with his staff he crossed the river, and returned with such great abundance of flocks and sons[^83]? What do we say? is this not happiness? Let it be so, but a left hand. What is, a left hand? Temporal, mortal, bodily. I do not wish you now to flee it, but neither think it a right hand. For these men are not malicious, not vain, because they abounded in these things; but because what should have been a left hand, they set in the right hand. Therefore *their right hand too is a right hand of iniquity;* therefore *their mouth has spoken vanity*: because they set in the right hand what they ought to have had in the left. For what ought they to set in the right hand? God, eternity, the years of God that do not fail, of which it is said: *And your years will not fail*[^84]: there is the right hand, there ought our desire to be. Let us use the left hand for a time, let us desire the right hand for eternity. If riches flow in, do not set your heart on them[^85]. For if you set your heart on flowing riches, what is a left hand you will make a right hand. Correct yourselves, recognize the wisdom embracing you; to whom it was said: *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand will embrace me*[^86]. See the holy love-songs; see the Song of Songs, of the heavenly marriage of Christ and the Church. What does the bride say of the bridegroom? *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand will embrace me.* The left hand under the head, the right hand over the head. For the arm of one embracing from above is over the head, but the left hand under the head. *His left hand*, she says, *is under my head.* For he will not desert me in temporal necessities: but yet that very left hand will be under the head; it will not be set before the head, but will be under the head; that his right hand may embrace me, promising eternal life. For so the left hand is under the head, if the right hand is over the head; and there is fulfilled what is written to Timothy: *Having the promise of the present life and of that to come*[^87]. *Having*, he says, *the promise of the present life and of that to come.* What in the present? *The left hand under the head.* What in the future? *His right hand will embrace me.* Do you seek the things necessary for the time? *Seek first the kingdom of God*, that is, the right hand; *and all these things will be added to you*[^88]. You will have here, he says, both riches and glory, and in the world to come eternal life: and with the left hand I will hold your weakness, and with the right hand I will crown your perfection. Or did the apostles perhaps, leaving all their things, or distributing what they had to the poor, remain without riches in this world? And where is that promise of the left hand: *He will receive in this world seven times as much*[^89]? He promised multiplication. And in truth, what is lacking to a man of God? If anyone is perhaps an unbeliever, he has one house or a few: *The whole world of riches is the faithful man's*[^90]. See his left hand full under the head: *He will receive in this world seven times as much.* See the right hand embracing: *And in the world to come eternal life*[^91]. Rightly in another place too it is said of Wisdom herself: *In her right hand are the years of life, and in her left hand riches and honor*[^92].

**19.** [v. 15.] Whence, then, are these vain talkers? why has their mouth spoken vanity? Because *their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* I do not, then, accuse them; because their sons are like new plants established from their youth, nor because their daughters are adorned like the likeness of a temple, nor because the other abundance and earthly peace was present to them: but why do I accuse them? *They have called blessed the people that has these things*[^93]. O men speaking vanity! *They have called blessed the people that has these things.* They have lost the true right hand; malicious, perverse, they have clothed the benefits of God the wrong way. O malicious, O vain talkers, O foreign sons! *They have called blessed the people that has these things.* What belonged to the left hand, they have set at the right hand: *They have called blessed the people that has these things.* What of you, David? what of you, body of Christ? what of you, members of Christ? what of you, not foreign sons, but sons of God? Since the vain-talking foreign sons *have called blessed the people that has these things*: you, what do you say? *Blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^94]. Have, then, the left hand, but in the left hand: desire the right hand, that you may be set at the right hand. For they had the left hand in the left hand, in whom he hungered, and they gave him to eat; he thirsted, and they gave him drink; he was a stranger, and they took him in; he was naked, and they clothed him[^95]. All this they took away from the left hand, and transferred to the works of the right hand, that they might be set at the right hand. *The vain-talking foreign sons called*, then, *the people that has these things blessed;* you, say with us: *Blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself.*
