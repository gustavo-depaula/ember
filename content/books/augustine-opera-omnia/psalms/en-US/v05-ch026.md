# On the Same Psalm 118

**1.** [v. 3.] It is written, and read, and true, in this psalm: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways.* But we must labor, with God helping, in whose hand are both we and our words, lest what was rightly said, not rightly understood, disturb the reader or hearer. For it must be guarded against, lest all the saints, whose voice is that: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^1]; either be thought not to walk in the ways of the Lord, since *sin is iniquity*[^2], and those who work iniquity surely have not walked in his ways; or, because it is not doubtful that they walk in the ways of the Lord, be believed not to have sin, which without doubt is false. For it was not written, in order to avoid arrogance and pride: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves.* Otherwise it would not be added: *And the truth is not in us*; but it would be said: Humility is not in us: especially since there follows that by which this sense is more clearly illustrated, and removes all the windings of doubt. For when blessed John had said this, he added: *But if we confess our offenses, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all iniquity*[^3]. What does the elation of damnable impiety here say, what does it contradict? For if it is to avoid arrogance, not to confess the truth, that the saints do not say that they have no sin; what is it that they confess, that they may deserve remission and cleansing? Or does this too happen, that arrogance may be avoided? How, then, will the true cleansing of those sins be obtained, whose confession is false? Let the hay-like elation of the proud, then, fall silent and wither, deceiving itself, which, with simulated humility, says in the ears of men that it has sin, but in its heart, with impious elation, says that it has no sin. For those who say this deceive themselves, and the truth is not in them. But when they say this in the ears of men, they deceive not only themselves, but others too by the perversity of insane teaching: but when they say it in their heart, there they deceive themselves, there the truth is not in them; and through this they deceive themselves in their heart, and in their heart lose the light of truth. But let the holy family of Christ, fructifying and growing in the whole world, humbly truthful and truthfully humble, cry out; let it cry out, I say: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. But if we confess our offenses, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all iniquity.* Let these things, as they are said, so be felt. For then there will be true humility, if it is not shown in the tongue alone; that we may be, according to the Apostle, *not minding high things, but consenting to the lowly*[^4]: he does not say, conversing, but, *consenting;* which is done not with the mouth, but with the heart. Hypocrite, if you say that you have sin, when you believe that you have no sin; outwardly you feign humility, inwardly you embrace vanity. Therefore both in mouth and in heart you do not have the truth. What does it profit you to seem to men that what you say is humble, if God sees that what you mind is high? Certainly, if the divine oracle sounded in your ears: Do not speak high things; not even so would you be undeservedly condemned, if you spoke a humble thing in mouth before men, and a high thing in heart before the Lord. But when you hear: *Do not mind high things, but fear*[^5]; why are you not also inwardly humble where you mind? Or is the mind for this reason inflated with height, that the tongue may lie humility? You read, or hear: *Do not mind high things, but fear:* and you so mind high things, that you suppose yourself to have no sin; and through this, because you do not wish to fear, nothing else remains to you but to swell.

**2.** And whence, you say, is it written: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways?* Or do the saints of the Lord not walk in the ways of the Lord? But if they walk, he says, they do not work iniquity: if they do not work iniquity, they have no sin; since *sin is iniquity.* Rise up to help me, Lord Jesus, and against the proud heretic help me through the confessing Apostle. Behold, where is your man, emptying himself, that he may be filled with you? Let us hear him, my brothers; let us question him about this question, if it please, or rather because it pleases. Tell us, most blessed Paul, whether you walked in the ways of the Lord, while you still lived in the flesh? He answers: And whence did I say: *Yet in that to which we have come, in that let us walk*[^6]? Whence did I say: *Did Titus circumvent you? did we not walk in the same spirit? not in the same footsteps?*[^7] Whence did I say: *As long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims from the Lord; for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^8]? What way of the Lord is surer than faith, by which the just lives? On what other way's journey was I tending to the heights, when I said: *But forgetting the one thing that is behind, stretched out to the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the palm of the supernal calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^9]? Finally, on what other way had I run, when I said: *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course*[^10]? Let these answers suffice, by which we have learned that the apostle Paul walked in the ways of the Lord: but let us inquire something else of him too. Tell, I beg, O Apostle, when, still living in the flesh, you walked in the ways of the Lord; did you have sin, or were you without sin? Let us hear whether he deceives himself, or rather minds what blessed John his fellow-apostle minds: since the truth was in them. And here too, then, he answers: Have you not read where I confess, saying: *For I do not do the good that I will, but the evil that I will not, this I do*[^11]? And this we have heard: so now we ask that: How did you walk in the ways of the Lord, if you did the evil that you would not; when the holy Psalm sounds, saying: *For those who work iniquity have not walked in his ways?* Hear him immediately answering through the consequent sentence: *If I do that which I will not,* he says, *it is now no longer I who work it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^12]. Behold how those who walk in the ways of the Lord do not work sin, and yet are not without sin; because now it is not they themselves who work it, but the sin that dwells in them.

**3.** Here someone will say: How did he do the evil that he would not, and how did he himself not do it, but the sin that dwelt in him? Meanwhile this question is now solved, and it has sufficiently appeared by the authority of the canonical Scripture, that it can come about that those walking in the ways of the Lord, although they are not without sin, yet do not themselves work it. *For those who work iniquity,* that is sin, because *sin is iniquity, have not walked in his ways.* But now, by what reasoning it can be understood that he both does it because of the body of this death, in which the law of sin dwells, and does not himself do it because of the ways of the Lord in which he walks; another sermon is necessary, because this one must be closed.
