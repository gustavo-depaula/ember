# Exposition of Psalm 75

**1.** [v. 2.] The Jews, known to all as enemies of the Lord Jesus Christ, are accustomed to boast in this psalm which we have sung, saying: *God is known in Judea; in Israel his name is great*[^1], and to insult the Gentiles to whom God is not known, and to say that God is known to them alone; since the Prophet says: *God is known in Judea*: elsewhere, then, he is unknown. But God is truly known in Judea, if they understand what Judea is. For truly God is not known except in Judea. Behold, we too say this, that unless someone is in Judea, God cannot be known to him. But what does the Apostle say? *He is a Jew who is one inwardly; with the circumcision of the heart, not in the letter, but in the spirit*[^2]: there are, then, Jews in the circumcision of the flesh, and there are Jews in the circumcision of the heart. Our fathers, many of them holy, had both the circumcision of the flesh, on account of the seal of faith, and the circumcision of the heart, on account of faith itself. Degenerating from these fathers, those who now boast in the name and have lost the deeds; degenerating, then, from these fathers, they have remained Jews in the flesh, pagans in the heart. For the Jews themselves, who are from Abraham, of whom Isaac was born, and from him Jacob, and from Jacob the twelve patriarchs, and from the twelve patriarchs the whole people of the Jews. But they were chiefly called Jews because Judah was one of the twelve sons of Jacob, a patriarch among the twelve, and from his stock the kingdom came to the Jews. For all that people, according to the number of the twelve sons of Jacob, had twelve tribes. Tribes are so called as it were curiae and distinct gatherings of peoples. That people, then, had twelve tribes; of which twelve tribes one tribe was that of Judah, from which were the kings, and there was another tribe of Levi, from which were the priests. But since to the priests serving the temple the land was not divided[^3], and yet it was necessary that the whole region of promise be distributed to twelve tribes: excepting, then, the one tribe of greater dignity, the tribe of Levi, which was among the priests, there would remain eleven, unless by the adoption of the two sons of Joseph the twelve were completed. Mark what this is. One of the twelve sons of Jacob was Joseph. This is the Joseph whom his brothers sold into Egypt, and there by the merit of his chastity was brought to a high dignity, and God was with him in all his work; and he received his brothers, by whom he had been sold, and his father, laboring under famine and going down to Egypt for bread. This Joseph had two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh. Jacob, dying, as it were by testament took those grandsons of his into the number of his sons, and said to his son Joseph: *The others who are born shall be yours; but these are mine, and they shall divide the land with their brothers*[^4]. The land of promise had not yet been given, nor divided; but he was speaking, prophesying in the Spirit. The two sons of Joseph, then, being added, the twelve tribes were nonetheless made, because now they are thirteen: for in place of the one tribe of Joseph, two were added, and they were made thirteen. Excepting, then, the tribe of Levi, that tribe of priests which served the temple and lived from the tithes of all the others to whom the land was divided, there remain twelve. Among these twelve was the tribe of Judah, whence were the kings. For first from another tribe a king was given, Saul[^5], and he was rejected as a bad king; afterward there was given from the tribe of Judah King David, and from him, from the tribe of Judah, were the kings[^6]. Now that Jacob had said this, when he blessed his sons: *A prince shall not fail from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until he comes for whom it has been promised*[^7]. And from the tribe of Judah came our Lord Jesus Christ. For he is, as Scripture says and you have just heard, *of the seed of David*[^8], born of Mary. But as for the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, in which he is equal to the Father; he is not only before the Jews, but even before Abraham himself[^9]; and not only before Abraham, but also before Adam; and not only before Adam, but also before heaven and earth, and before the ages: for *all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^10]. Since, then, it was said in the prophecy: *A prince shall not fail from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until he comes for whom it has been promised*: the earlier times are considered, and it is found that the Jews from the tribe of Judah always had kings, whence they were called Jews; and they did not have a foreign king before, except that Herod who lived when the Lord was born[^11]. From him the foreign kings began, from Herod. Before Herod, all were from the tribe of Judah, but until he should come *for whom it has been promised.* And so, when the Lord himself was already coming, the kingdom of the Jews was overthrown, and taken away from the Jews. Now they have no kingdom, because they refuse to acknowledge the true king. See now whether they are to be called Jews. Now you see that they are not to be called Jews. By their own voice they renounced themselves from that name, so that they are not worthy to be called Jews, except only in the flesh. Where, then, did they separate themselves from that name? They were speaking, they were raging against Christ—that is, the line of Judah—they were raging against the seed of David. And Pilate said to them: *Shall I crucify your king?* And they: *We have no king but Caesar*[^12]. O you who are called Jews, and are not; if you have no king but Caesar, the prince has now failed from Judah: he has come, then, *for whom it has been promised.* Those, then, are more truly Jews, who from Jews have been made Christians: the rest of the Jews, who did not believe in Christ, deserved to lose even that name. The true Judea, then, is the Church of Christ, believing in that king who came from the tribe of Judah through the Virgin Mary; believing in him of whom the Apostle just now spoke, writing to Timothy: *Be mindful that Jesus Christ rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel*[^13]. For from Judah was David, and from David the Lord Jesus Christ. We who believe in Christ belong to Judah; and we have known Christ whom we have not seen with our eyes, we hold him by faith. Let not the Jews insult, then, who are no longer Jews: they themselves said: *We have no king but Caesar.* For it were better for them that their king should be Christ, of the seed of David, of the tribe of Judah. Yet, since Christ himself is of the seed of David according to the flesh, but God over all blessed forever, he himself is our king and our God: our king, according to that he was born of the tribe of Judah according to the flesh, Christ the Lord and Savior; but our God, who is before Judah, and before heaven and earth, through whom all things were made, both spiritual and bodily. For if *all things were made through him,* then Mary herself, of whom he was born, was made through him. How, then, would he be born like the rest of men, who made for himself the mother of whom he was born? Therefore he himself is the Lord: as the Apostle says, when he was speaking of the Jews: *Whose are the fathers; and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all God blessed forever*[^14]. Since, then, the Jews saw Christ, and crucified him, they did not see God: but the Gentiles, because they did not see and yet believed, understood God. Therefore, if God became known to them in Christ, reconciling the world to himself[^15], and they crucified him for this reason, because they did not understand God hidden in the flesh, let that which is called Judea, and is not, withdraw; let the true Judea draw near, to which it is said: *Draw near to him, and be enlightened, and your faces shall not be put to shame*[^16]. The faces of the true Judea shall not be confounded. For indeed they heard, and believed; and the Church was made the true Judea, where Christ is known, who is a man of the seed of David, God above David.

**2.** *God is known in Judea; in Israel his name is great.* And of Israel we ought to understand as of Judea: as those are not true Jews, so neither are they true Israel. For who is called Israel? One who sees God. And how did those see God, among whom he walked in the flesh, and whom, while they supposed him a man, they killed? Rising again, he appeared as God to all to whom he willed to show himself. Those, then, are worthy to be called Israel, who deserved to understand Christ as God set in flesh, so that they did not despise what they saw, but adored what they did not see. For the Gentiles, with humble mind, perceived him whom they did not see with their eyes, and held him by faith. Accordingly, those who held him with their hands killed him; those who held him by faith adored him. *In Israel his name is great.* Do you wish to be Israel? Attend to him of whom the Lord says: *Behold, truly an Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^17]. If the true *Israelite is one in whom there is no guile,* the guileful and lying are not true Israelites. Let not those, then, say that God is among them, and that his name is great in Israel: let them prove themselves Israelites, and I grant that *in Israel his name is great.*

**3.** [v. 3.] *And his place is made in peace, and his dwelling in Zion.* Again, Zion is, as it were, the homeland of the Jews: the true Zion is the Church of Christians. Now the interpretation of the Hebrew names is handed down to us thus: Judea is interpreted "confession"; Israel, "seeing God." After Judea, Israel: so it is set here: *God is known in Judea; in Israel his name is great.* Do you wish to see God? First confess, and so in yourself the place of God is made; because *his place is made in peace.* As long, then, as you do not confess your sins, you quarrel in a certain way with God: for how do you not contend with him, when you praise what displeases him? He punishes the thief; you praise theft: he punishes the drunkard; you praise drunkenness. You contend with God; you have not made a place for him in your heart: because *his place is in peace.* And how do you begin to have peace with God? You begin with him in confession. It is the voice of the psalm saying: *Begin to the Lord in confession*[^18]. What is: *Begin to the Lord in confession*? Begin to be joined to God. How? So that this displeases you which displeases him too. Your bad life displeases him: if it please you, you are disjoined from him; if it displease you, by confession you are joined to him. See in how great a part you are unlike, since it is precisely on account of that unlikeness that you are displeasing. For you were made, O man, to the image of God; but by a perverse and evil life you have disturbed in yourself, and laid waste in yourself, the image of your Maker. Made unlike, you attend to yourself, and are displeasing to yourself: now from that very thing you have begun to become like, because this displeases you which displeases God too.

**4.** But how am I like, you say, when I am still displeasing to myself? For this reason it was said: *Begin.* Begin to the Lord in confession; you will be perfected in peace. For you still have a war against yourself. War is declared upon you, not only against the suggestions of the devil, against the prince of the power of this air, who works in the children of unbelief, against the devil and his angels, the spiritual forces of wickedness[^19]; not only, then, against these is war declared upon you, but against yourself. How against yourself? Against your bad habit, against the oldness of your bad life, which drags you toward the accustomed, and reins you back from the new. For a certain new life is declared to you, and you are old: you are held in suspense by the joy of newness, you are weighed down by the burden of oldness; a war begins for you against yourself. But from that part in which you are displeasing to yourself, you are joined to God; and from that part in which you are now joined to God, you will be fit to conquer yourself, because he is with you who overcomes all things. Attend to what the Apostle says: *With the mind I serve the law of God; but with the flesh the law of sin*[^20]. Why *with the mind*? Because your bad life displeases you. Why *with the flesh*? Because there is no lack of suggestions and evil delights; but from that by which you are joined to God in mind, you conquer what in you refuses to follow. For you have gone ahead in part, and in part you are delayed: drag yourself to him who lifts you up. By a certain weight of oldness you are pressed down; cry out and say: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* who will free me from that by which I am weighed down? for the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul[^21]. Who, then, will free me? *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^22]. But why does he permit you to contend against yourself for a long time, until all the evil desires are swallowed up? That you may understand your punishment in yourself: in you, from yourself, is your scourge; let your quarrel be with yourself. So vengeance is taken on one who rebels against God, that he is a war to himself, who refused to have peace with God. But hold your members against your evil concupiscences. Anger has arisen; do you hold your hand, joined to God. It could arise, but it found no weapons. With your anger is the impulse; with you are the weapons: let the impulse be unarmed, and that which has arisen in vain now learns not to arise.

**5.** But this I say, dearly beloved, lest perhaps, because we said: *But with the flesh the law of sin,* you should think that you must consent to your carnal desires. Even though there cannot now but be carnal desires, you must not consent to them. Therefore the Apostle did not say, Let there be no sin in your mortal body; for he knows that as long as it is mortal, there is sin there: but what does he say? *Therefore let not sin reign in your mortal body.* What is *let it not reign*? He himself expounded it: *So as to obey,* he says, *its desires*[^23]. There are desires, they exist; you do not heed your desires, you do not follow those desires, you do not consent to them: you have sin, but it has lost its reign, when sin no longer reigns in you; afterward, death, the last enemy, will be destroyed[^24]. For what is promised to us, since it was said: *With the mind I serve the law of God; but with the flesh the law of sin*[^25]? Hear the promise: that there will not always be unlawful desires in the flesh. For it shall rise, and be changed; and when this mortal flesh has been changed into a spiritual body, then by no worldly concupiscences, by no earthly delights will it titillate the soul, nor turn it away from the contemplation of God. There comes to be in it, then, what the Apostle says: *The body indeed is dead because of sin; but the spirit is life because of righteousness. But if he who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you; he who raised Jesus Christ from the dead will give life also to your mortal bodies, on account of his Spirit who dwells in you*[^26]. When our bodies, then, have been given life, there will be true peace where the place of God is: but let confession go before. *God is known in Judea*: confess, then, first. *In Israel his name is great*: you do not yet see in vision; see by faith, and there will come to be in you what follows: *And his place is made in peace, and his dwelling in Zion.* Zion is interpreted "contemplation." We said already yesterday, and certain brothers heard—whom today too we have seen present—what contemplation is. For we shall contemplate God face to face[^27]. Here it is promised to us, in whom now, not seeing, we believe. How shall we rejoice when we see him? Brothers, if now the promise alone makes such joy in us, how great will the rendering make it? For there will be rendered to us what he promised. And what did he promise? Himself, that in his face, and in the contemplation of him, we may rejoice; and nothing else will delight us, because there is nothing better than he who made all the things that delight. *His place is made in peace, and his dwelling in Zion*: that is, in a certain contemplation and beholding his dwelling is made *in Zion.*

**6.** [v. 4.] *There he broke the powers of the bows, and the shield, and the sword, and war.* Where did he break them? In that eternal peace, in that perfect peace. And now too, my brothers, those who have believed well see that they ought not to presume upon themselves; and all the power of their threats, and whatever in them is sharp for harming, he breaks it: and whatever they hold as something great by which they might protect themselves in temporal matters, and the war which they waged against God by defending their sins—all these things he broke there.

**7.** [v. 5.] *You, enlightening wonderfully from the eternal mountains.* Who are the eternal mountains? Those whom he himself made eternal; who are the great mountains, the preachers of truth. You enlighten; but from the eternal mountains: first the great mountains receive your light, and by your light which the mountains take up, the earth too is clothed. But the apostles received those great mountains; the apostles received, as it were, the first beginnings of the rising light. Did they keep with themselves what they received? No; lest it be said to them: *Wicked and slothful servant, you should have given my money to the bankers*[^28]. If, then, they did not keep with themselves what they received, but preached it to the whole world: *You, enlightening wonderfully from the eternal mountains.* Those whom you made eternal, through them you promised eternal life to the rest as well: *You, enlightening wonderfully from the eternal mountains.* Magnificently and with weight it is said: *You;* lest anyone suppose that the mountains enlighten. For many, supposing that they were enlightened by the mountains themselves, made for themselves parties from the mountains; and they cut down the mountains themselves, and were themselves broken. Some made for themselves a Donatus, some made for themselves a Maximian, some made for themselves this one and that one. Why do they reckon their salvation to be in men, not in God? O man, the light came to you through the mountains; but God enlightens you, not the mountains. *You, enlightening,* he says: *you,* not the mountains. *You, enlightening: from the* eternal *mountains,* indeed; but, *you, enlightening.* Hence too in another place, what does the psalm say? *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help shall come to me*[^29]. What then? Is your hope in the mountains, and from there will help come to you? Have you remained in the mountains? See what you do. There is something above the mountains: above the mountains is he whom the mountains tremble at. *I lifted up,* he says, *my eyes to the mountains, from where help shall come to me.* But what follows? *My help,* he says, *is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^30]. To the mountains, indeed, I lifted my eyes, because through the mountains the Scriptures were shown to me; but I have my heart in him who enlightens all the mountains.

**8.** Therefore, brothers, this was said so that none of you should wish to place his hope in a man. A man is something only so long as he clings to him by whom man was made. For withdrawing from him, man is nothing, even when he clings to those others. Take counsel through a man in such a way that you consider him who enlightens the man. For you too can approach him who speaks to you through a man; for he did not make that man approach himself and then repel you. And whoever has truly approached God in such a way that God dwells in him, all who do not place their hope in him are displeasing to him. For this reason a certain example was given, when they divided the apostles themselves among themselves, and went into schisms, those who said: *I am of Paul, I of Apollos, I of Cephas,* that is, of Peter. These the Apostle bewails, and says to them: *Is Christ divided?* and he chose himself, the one whom among them he might disparage: *Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^31] See the good mountain seeking glory, not for itself, but for him by whom the mountains are enlightened: he did not wish presumption to be placed in himself, but in him in whom he himself too had placed his trust. Whoever, then, wishes so to commend himself to peoples that, if perhaps some tumult should befall him, he breaks the peoples off after himself, and divides the catholic Church on his own account, is not of those mountains whom the Most High enlightens. But what is he? Darkened by himself, not enlightened by the Lord. And how are these mountains tested? If perhaps some tumult should arise against the mountains in the Church—either through popular seditions of carnal men, or through some false suspicions of men—the good mountain repels from itself all who on its account wish to withdraw from unity. For he himself will thus remain in unity, if on his account that unity is not divided. But those others were divided: when the people withdrew from the world and followed their name, they rejoiced; they were lifted up, and were cast down. Let them be humbled, and they would be exalted, as the Apostle was humbled, saying: *Was Paul crucified for you?* and in another place: *I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. Therefore neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase*[^32]. Such mountains are humble in themselves, lofty in God; but those who are lofty in themselves are humbled by God: *For he who exalts himself shall be humbled; and he who humbles himself shall be exalted*[^33]. Therefore such men, who attend to their own prides, embitter the peaceable in the Church. The latter wish to set peace; the former mingle dissension among themselves. And what does another psalm say of them? *Let those who embitter not be exalted in themselves*[^34]. *You, enlightening*—attend here—*you, wonderfully, from the eternal mountains.*

**9.** [v. 6.] *All the foolish of heart were troubled.* The truth was preached, eternal life was declared; another life was declared to exist, one that is not of this earth: men despised the present life, and loved the life to come, enlightened through the enlightened mountains. But *the foolish of heart were troubled.* How *were they troubled*? When the Gospel is preached. And what is eternal life? and who is it who rose from the dead? The Athenians were amazed when the apostle Paul spoke of the resurrection of the dead, and supposed that he was telling some kind of fables[^35]. But because he was declaring that there is another life, which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man[^36], therefore *the foolish of heart were troubled.* But what befell them? *They slept their sleep, and all the men of riches found nothing in their hands.* They loved present things, and they slept among those very present things; and so those very present things became delightful to them: just as one who sees in his sleep that he has found treasures is rich only so long as he does not wake. The dream made him rich; the waking makes him poor. Sleep held him, perhaps, sleeping on the ground and lying on the hard floor, poor and perhaps a beggar; in his dreams he saw himself lying on an ivory or golden bed, and on feathers heaped high: as long as he sleeps, he sleeps well; waking, he finds himself on the hard floor, where his sleep had held him. Such are these too: they came into this life, and through temporal desires they fell asleep here, as it were; and riches and empty fleeting pomps took them up, and passed away: they did not understand how much good could have been made from them. For if they had known another life, they would have stored up there for themselves what here was to perish: as Zacchaeus, the chief of the publicans, saw that good, when he received the Lord Jesus into his lodging, and said: *Half of my goods I give to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore fourfold*[^37]. This man was not in the vanity of those dreaming, but in the faith of those awake. Therefore the Lord, since the physician had entered to the sick man, pronounced his salvation, and said: *Today salvation has come to this house, since he too is a son of Abraham*[^38]: that you may know that we, by imitating faith, are sons of Abraham; but the Jews, who are proud of the flesh, have degenerated from faith. Therefore *the men of riches slept their sleep, and found nothing in their hands.* They slept in their desires; it delights them, this sleep passes, this life passes, and they find nothing in their hands, because they placed nothing in the hand of Christ. Do you wish to find something in your hands afterward? Do not despise the hand of the poor now; and look to the empty hands, if you wish to have full hands. For the Lord said: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in,* and the rest. And they: *When did we see you hungry, thirsty, or a stranger?* And he says to them: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^39]. He willed to hunger in the poor, who is rich in heaven; and do you, man, hesitate to give to a man, when you know that you give to Christ what you give, from whom you received whatever you give! But those men *slept their sleep, and all the men of riches found nothing in their hands.*

**10.** [v. 7.] *At your rebuke, O God of Jacob, all who mounted horses slumbered.* Who are they *who mounted horses*? Those who refused to be humble. It is not a sin to sit on horses; but to raise the neck of one's power against God, and to suppose oneself to be in some honor. Because you are rich, you have mounted up; God rebukes, and you sleep. Great is the anger of the one rebuking, great is the anger! Let Your Charity attend to a fearful thing. A rebuke has a din; a din is wont to make men wake. So great is the weight of God's rebuke that he says: *At your rebuke, O God of Jacob, all who mounted horses slumbered.* Behold with what sleep that Pharaoh slumbered who mounted horses[^40]: for he did not wake in heart, because at the rebuke he had a hard heart. For hardness of heart is a falling asleep. I ask you, my brothers, see how they sleep who, with the Gospel sounding, and the Amen and the Alleluia throughout the whole world, still refuse to condemn the old life and to wake into the new. The Scripture of God was in Judea; now it is sung throughout the whole world: in that one nation it was said that the one God who made all things must be adored and worshiped; now where is it kept silent that Christ has risen? Mocked on the cross, he has now fixed that very cross of his, on which he was mocked, upon the foreheads of kings; and still men sleep! Great is the anger of God, brothers! Better did we hear him who says: *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead; and Christ will enlighten you*[^41]. But who hear him? Those who do not mount horses. Who are they who do not mount horses? Those who do not vaunt and exalt themselves, as it were in their honors and powers. *At your rebuke, O God of Jacob, those who mounted horses slumbered.*

**11.** [v. 8.] *You are terrible; and who shall resist you then because of your anger?* Now they sleep, and do not feel him angry; but he was angry that they might sleep. What, sleeping, they do not now feel, they will feel at the end: for the judge of the living and the dead will appear; *and who shall resist you then because of your anger?* For now they say what they wish, and dispute against God, and say: Who are the Christians? Or who is Christ? Or what fools, who believe what they do not see, and abandon the delights they do see, and follow the faith of things that are not displayed to their eyes? You sleep, and you bleat; you speak against God as much as you can. *How long, O Lord, how long shall sinners glory? shall they answer, and speak iniquity?*[^42] But when will no one answer, and no one speak, except when he turns upon himself? When will they turn upon themselves the teeth with which they now gnaw at us, with which they tear us by mocking Christians and reproaching the life of the saints? Then they will turn upon themselves, when that befalls them which is said in the book of Wisdom: *They shall say among themselves, repenting, and groaning through anguish of spirit*: when they have seen the glory of the saints, then they will say: *These are they whom we once held in derision*[^43]. O you who have slept much! Surely you now wake, and find nothing in your hands. You see how full of the glory of God are the hands of those whom, as it were poor men, you mocked. Say, then, to yourselves then, when you do not resist the anger of God; not with hand, nor with tongue, nor with word, nor with thought: for he will appear manifest to you, whom you thought to be mocked, when it was announced that he would come to you. And what will they say? *Therefore we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shone for us, and the sun has not risen for us*[^44]. How would the sun of righteousness rise for those asleep? But from his anger and rebuke they sleep. Perhaps he will say this: Then let me not mount the horse: and then they will accuse their own horses. Hear them accusing their own horses on which they slept: *Therefore we have erred,* says Scripture, *from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shone for us, and the sun has not risen for us. What did pride profit us? And what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All things have passed away like a shadow*[^45]. So you woke at last. But better that you had not mounted the horse, that you might not then slumber when you ought to have been awake; and that you might hear the voice of Christ, and Christ might enlighten you. *You are terrible; and who shall resist you there then because of your anger?* For what will there be then?

**12.** [v. 9.] *From heaven you hurled judgment: the earth trembled, and was still.* She who now troubles herself, who now speaks, will have to fear at the end and be still. Better that she be still now, that at the end she might rejoice.

**13.** [v. 10.] *The earth trembled, and was still.* When? *When God arose unto judgment, to save all the meek of heart.* Who are *the meek of heart*? Those who did not mount raging horses, but in their humility confessed their sins. *To save all the meek of heart.*

**14.** [v. 11.] *For the thought of man shall confess to you, and the remnants of thought shall keep festival to you.* First, the *thought;* afterward, the *remnants of thought.* What is the first *thought*? That from which we begin: that good *thought* from which you will begin to confess. Confession joins us to Christ. But now the confession itself—that is, the first *thought*—produces in us the remnants of thought; and those very *remnants of thought shall keep festival to you. The thought of man shall confess to you, and the remnants of thought shall keep festival to you.* What is the *thought* that *shall confess*? That which condemns the former life, to which what it was is displeasing, that it may be what it was not, that itself is the first *thought.* But because you ought so to withdraw from sins, having confessed to God by the first thought, that it does not withdraw from your memory that you were a sinner, through this—that you were a sinner—you keep festival to God. There is still something to be understood. The first *thought* has confession, and a withdrawal from the old life: but if you have forgotten from what sins you were freed, you do not give thanks to your liberator, and you do not keep festival to your God. Behold the first *thought*, confessing, of the apostle Saul, now Paul, who was first Saul: when he heard the voice from heaven, while he was persecuting Christ, and raging against Christians, and wished to bring those found anywhere to be killed, he heard the voice from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* and surrounded by light, blindness having been made in his eyes that he might see within, he sent forth the first thought of obedience; when he heard: *I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute, Lord,* he said, *what do you bid me do?*[^46] This is the *thought* that confesses; he now calls Lord the one he was persecuting. How the *remnants of thought keep festival,* you have heard in the case of Paul, when the Apostle himself was being read: *Be mindful that Christ Jesus rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel*[^47]. What is *be mindful*? Let not the *thought* be blotted out from your memory by which you first confessed; let there be the *remnants of thought* in your memory. And see how the same apostle Paul repeats in another place what was granted to him: *I who was formerly,* he says, *a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent*[^48]. He who says: *I was formerly a blasphemer,* is he still a blasphemer? That he might not be a blasphemer, there was the first *thought* confessing; but that he might call to mind what was granted to him, there were the *remnants of thought,* and through those very remnants of thought he kept festival.

**15.** For indeed, my brothers, behold, Christ has renewed us; he has forgiven all our sins, and we have been converted: if we forget what has been forgiven us, and by whom it has been forgiven, we forget the gift of the Savior; but when we do not forget the gift of the Savior, is not Christ daily sacrificed for us? And Christ was sacrificed for us once, when we believed; then there was the *thought*: but now there are the *remnants of thought,* when we remember who came to us, and what he granted us; from those very remnants of thought—that is, from that very memory—he is daily so sacrificed for us, as though he daily renews us, who by his first grace renewed us. For the Lord has already renewed us in baptism, and we have been made new men; rejoicing in hope, indeed, that we may be patient in tribulation[^49]: yet it ought not to withdraw from our memory what has been bestowed on us. And even if your *thought* is not now what it was (for the first *thought* was to withdraw from sin; but now you do not withdraw, but you withdrew then): let there be the *remnants of thought,* lest he who healed you fall from memory. If you have forgotten that you had a wound, there will not be for you the *remnants of thought.* For what do you suppose David said? Behold, he speaks from the person of all. The holy David sinned grievously; Nathan the prophet was sent to him, and reproved him: and he confessed, and said: *I have sinned*[^50]. This was the first *thought* of the one confessing: *The thought of man shall confess to you.* What were the *remnants of thought*? When he says: *And my sin is ever before me*[^51]. What, then, was the first *thought*? That he should withdraw from sin. And if he has now withdrawn from sin, how is his sin ever before him, except because that *thought* passed, but the *remnants of thought* keep festival? Let us remember, then, dearly beloved brothers; we ask you: whoever has been freed from sin, let him remember what he was; let there be in him the *remnants of thought.* For then he bears another to be healed, if he remembers that he himself was healed. Therefore let each one recall what he was, and whether he now is so no longer; and then he will come to the aid of one who still is what he himself now is not. But if he vaunts himself as it were on his own merits, and repels sinners as unworthy, and rages without mercy, he mounts the horse; let him beware that he does not sleep: for *those who mounted horses slumbered*[^52]. Now he has dismissed the horse; he has humbled himself: let him not again mount the horse, that is, let him not again raise himself into pride. From what does this come to him? If the *remnants of thought* keep festival to God.

**16.** [v. 12.] *Make vows, and render them to the Lord our God.* Let each one vow what he can, and render it. Do not vow, and not render; but let each one vow what he can, and render it. Do not be slothful in vowing; for you will not fulfill it by your own strength. You will fail, if you presume upon yourself; but if upon him to whom you vow, vow, and you will render it in security. *Make vows, and render them to the Lord our God.* What should we all in common vow? To believe in him, to hope from him for eternal life, to live well according to the common measure. For there is a certain measure common to all. Not to commit theft is not enjoined on a consecrated woman, and is not enjoined on a married woman: not to commit adultery is enjoined on all: not to love wine-bibbing, by which the soul is engulfed, and corrupts in itself the temple of God, is equally enjoined on all: not to be proud is equally enjoined on all: not to kill a man, not to hate one's brother, not to hold ruin against anyone, is enjoined on all in common. All this we all ought to vow. There are also vows proper to individuals: one vows to God conjugal chastity, that he should know no other woman besides his wife; so too the woman, that she should know no other man besides her husband. Others also vow, even having experienced such a marriage, to suffer nothing more of the kind, to desire or endure nothing more of the kind: and these have vowed something greater than the former. Others vow virginity itself from their earliest age, so that they may not even experience the kind of thing that those others, having experienced, abandoned: and these have vowed the most. Others vow that their house should be hospitable to all the saints who come: they vow a great vow. Another vows to leave all his goods to be distributed to the poor, and to go into the common life, into the fellowship of the saints: he vows a great vow. *Make vows, and render them to the Lord our God.* Let each one vow what he wishes to vow: let him attend to this, that he render what he has vowed. Whatever each one vows to God, if he looks back, it is evil. Some consecrated woman wished to marry: what did she wish? What any virgin too might wish. What did she wish? What her own mother too did. Did she wish something evil? Evil, plainly. Why? Because she had already vowed to the Lord her God. For what did the apostle Paul say of such? Though he says that young widows may marry if they wish[^53], yet he says in a certain place: *But she will be more blessed if she so remains, according to my counsel*[^54]. He shows that she is more blessed if she so remains; yet not to be condemned, if she wishes to marry. But what does he say of certain women who vowed, and did not render? *Having,* he says, *condemnation, because they have made void their first faith*[^55]. What is *they have made void their first faith*? They vowed, and did not render. Let no brother, then, placed in a monastery, say: I will withdraw from the monastery, for it is not only those who are in a monastery who will come to the kingdom of heaven, and those who are not there do not belong to God. It is answered him: But those did not vow; you vowed, you looked back. When the Lord threatened concerning the day of judgment, what did he say? *Remember Lot's wife*[^56]. He said it to all. For what did Lot's wife do? She was freed from Sodom, and, set on the way, she looked back: where she looked back, there she remained. And she was made a pillar of salt[^57], that by gazing upon her men might be seasoned; that they might have a heart, not be foolish, not look back, lest, giving a bad example, they themselves remain and season others. For even now we say that to certain brothers of ours, whom perhaps we have seen as it were grow weak in their good resolve: Do you too wish to be such as that man? We set before them certain ones who looked back. They are foolish in themselves, but they season others, when they are recalled, that those, fearing their example, may not look back. *Make vows, and render them to the Lord our God,* because that wife of Lot pertains to all. A married woman wished to commit adultery; she looked back from her own place to which she had come. A widow who had already vowed so to remain wished to marry; she wished what was lawful for the woman who married; but for her it was not lawful, because she looked back from her own place. A consecrated virgin, now dedicated to God, let her have also the other things which truly adorn that virginity, and without which that virginity is base. For what if she be whole in body, and corrupt in mind? What is it that I have said? What if no one has touched her body, but if perhaps she is a drunkard, is proud, is quarrelsome, is foul-tongued? All these things God condemns. If, before she had vowed, she had married, she would not be condemned: she chose something better, she surpassed what was lawful for her; she is proud, and commits so many unlawful things. This I say: It is lawful to marry before she vows; it is never lawful to be proud. O you, virgin of God, you refused to marry, which is lawful; you exalt yourself, which is not lawful. Better a humble virgin than a humble married woman; but better a humble married woman than a proud virgin. But she who looked back to marriage is not condemned because she wished to marry; but because she had already gone ahead, and becomes Lot's wife by looking back. Do not be slothful, you who are able, to whom God inspires it, to lay hold of better degrees, because we do not say these things in order that you may not vow, but that you may vow, and render: *Make vows, and render them to the Lord our God.* Now that we have treated these things, perhaps you were wishing to vow, and now you do not wish to vow. But attend to what the Psalm has said to you. It did not say: Do not vow; but: *Vow, and render.* Because you heard, *render,* do you not wish to vow? Were you, then, wishing to vow, and not to render? Rather, do both. Let the one be from your profession; the other will be perfected by the help of God. Look to him who leads you, and you will not look back, from where he leads you out. He who leads you walks before you; that from where he leads you out is behind you. Love the one leading, and he does not condemn you for looking back. *Make vows, and render them to the Lord our God.*

**17.** *All who are round about him shall offer gifts.* Who are those round about him? For where is he, that one should say: *All who are round about him*? If you think of God the Father, where is he not, who is everywhere present? If you think of the Son according to the form of his divinity, he too is everywhere with the Father; because he himself is the Wisdom of God, of which it is said: *She reaches everywhere by reason of her purity*[^58]. But if you understand the Son according to the fact that he assumed flesh, and was seen among men, and was crucified, and rose again, we know that he ascended into heaven. Who are those round about him? The angels. Do we, then, not offer gifts, because he said: *All who are round about him shall offer gifts*? If our Lord were buried here on earth, and his body lay, as that of some martyr or apostle, we would attend to those who were round about him—whatever nations were about that place on every side, or who gathered to that burial place with gifts: but now he has ascended; he is on high. What is this: *All who are round about him shall offer gifts*? For now I will tell you what God admonishes—what he himself has deigned to inspire in me from these words: if afterward something better appears, that too is yours, because the truth is common to all. It is neither mine nor yours; it is not this man's or that man's: it is common to all. And perhaps for this reason it is in the middle, so that all who love the truth may be round about it. For whatever is common to all is in the middle. Why is it said to be in the middle? So that it may be just so far from all, and just so near to all. What is not in the middle becomes, as it were, private. What is public is set in the middle, so that all who come may perceive it, may be enlightened. Let no one say: It is mine; lest in his own part he wish to do what is in the middle for all. What, then, is: *All who are round about him shall offer gifts*? All who understand that the truth is common to all, and do not make it as it were their own by being proud of it, they shall offer gifts; because they have humility: but those who make as it were their own what is common to all, as it were set in the middle, and try to seduce it to a faction, these shall not offer gifts; because *All who are round about him shall offer gifts to the terrible one.* Gifts shall be offered to the terrible one: let all, then, who are round about him fear. For they will fear, and praise him with trembling, because they are round about him for this very purpose, that all may attain to him, and that he may flow forth publicly to all, and publicly enlighten: this is to tremble. When you make him as it were your own, and no longer common, you are lifted up into pride; whereas it is written: *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult before him with trembling*[^59]. Therefore those who are round about him shall offer gifts: for they are humble, who know the truth to be common to all.

**18.** [v. 13.] To whom shall they offer gifts? *To the terrible one, and to him who takes away the spirit of princes.* For the spirit of princes is the proud spirit. They, then, are not his spirits; because even if they have known something, they want it to be their own, not public: but he who commends himself as equal to all, who sets himself in the middle, that all may take up as much as they can, whatever they can; not of any man, but of God, and therefore his own, because they themselves have been made his. Therefore it is necessary that all those be humble: they have lost their own spirit, and they have the Spirit of God. By whom have they lost their own spirit? By him *who takes away the spirit of princes;* since it is said to him in another place: *You will take away their spirit, and they shall fail, and shall return to their dust. You will send forth your Spirit, and they shall be created, and you shall renew the face of the earth*[^60]. Someone has understood something; he wants it to be his own, he still has his own spirit: it is good for him to lose his own spirit, and to have the Spirit of God: he is still proud among the princes; it is good that he return to his dust, and say: *Remember, O Lord, that we are dust*[^61]. For if you have confessed yourself to be dust, God makes of dust a man. All who are round about him offer gifts: all the humble confess to him, and adore him. They offer gifts *to the terrible one.* Why *terrible*? *Exult with trembling*[^62], *and to him who takes away the spirit of princes*: that is, he takes away the boldness of the proud. *Terrible among the kings of the earth.* The kings of the earth are terrible; but he is above all, who terrifies the kings of the earth. Be a king of the earth, and God will be terrible to you. How, you say, shall I be a king of the earth? Rule the earth, and you will be a king of the earth. Do not, then, in greed for ruling, set before your eyes the most far-flung provinces, over which to spread your kingdoms; rule the earth which you carry. Hear the Apostle ruling the earth: *I so box, not as one beating the air; but I chastise my body, and bring it into servitude, lest perhaps, preaching to others, I myself be made a castaway*[^63]. Therefore, my brothers, be round about him, so that, through whoever the truth sounds to you, you do not impute it to him through whom it sounds; but let it be from the middle for all, because it is equally present to all. And be humble, lest you usurp it to yourselves too, if perhaps you have understood some good of his: because both what we have understood better is yours, and what you have understood better is ours, so that we may be round about him, and be humble. And so, losing our own spirit, we shall offer gifts to the terrible one above all the kings of the earth—that is, above all who rule their own flesh, but are subject to their Creator.
