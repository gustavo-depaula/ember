# On the Same Psalm 118 (5)

**1.** [v. 13.] The beginning of this sermon for us, in the psalm of which we discourse, is this verse: *With my lips I have announced all the judgments of your mouth.* What is this, dearest ones? what is this? Who can announce all the judgments of God, since he cannot investigate them? Or do we hesitate to exclaim with the Apostle: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out!*[^1] The Lord says: *I have yet many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^2]: and although he immediately promised them all truth through the Holy Spirit; yet blessed Paul cries out: *We know in part*[^3]; that we may understand that by the Holy Spirit indeed, of whom we have received a pledge, we are led to all truth; but when we come into another life, after the mirror and riddle of this life, and see face to face. How, then, does this one say: *With my lips I have announced all the judgments of your mouth?* And he says this who a little before, in the immediately preceding verse, had said: *Teach me your justifications.* By what manner, then, did he announce all the judgments of his mouth, who still wishes to learn his justifications? Or did he indeed already know all the judgments, but still desired to learn the justifications? But this is more wonderful, if he already knew the unsearchable things of God, and did not know the things which he commanded men to do. For justifications are not the words, but the deeds of righteousness, namely the works of the just, which God commands. But they are called God's, although they are done by us, because unless he gives them they are not done. But the judgments of God are those by which the world is judged by him both now and at the end of the age. But since all things are contained in the eloquences of God, both justifications namely and judgments; why does he still seek to learn the justifications, who says that he hid the eloquences of God in his heart? For he says: *In my heart I have hidden your eloquences, that I may not sin against you*[^4]: then going on he added: *Blessed are you, O Lord; teach me your justifications*[^5]; and then: *With my lips,* he says, *I have announced all the judgments of your mouth.* These two things indeed seem not to be contrary to one another, but rather friendly and joined, so that, since he hid the eloquences of God in his heart, he announces his judgments with his lips; *For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^6]: but what he set in the middle between these two: *Blessed are you, O Lord; teach me your justifications,* how it agrees with a man in whose heart the eloquences of God already are, and who has announced with his lips all the judgments of God, that he should still wish to learn the justifications of God, is not found, except that he be understood to wish to learn them by doing, not by holding in memory and speaking; and that he showed that we ought to ask from the Lord that without which we can do nothing. But this we treated in another sermon before this one: but now, how he said that he announced with his lips all the judgments of the mouth of God, when they are said to be unsearchable, and of whose depth it is written elsewhere: *Your judgments are a great deep*[^7]; as far as God grants, we have undertaken to treat.

**2.** What, then, we are to understand here, attend. Does the Church not know the judgments of God? It knows them plainly. For it surely knows to what kind of people the judge of the living and the dead is going to say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*; and to what kind he is going to say: *Go into the eternal fire*[^8]. It knows, I say, that neither fornicators, nor those serving idols, nor those and those whom the apostle Paul there enumerates, will possess the kingdom of God: it knows wrath and indignation, tribulation and distress upon every soul of man working evil, of the Jew first and of the Greek; but glory, and honor, and peace to everyone working good, to the Jew first and to the Greek. These and such judgments of God, evidently expressed, the Church knows: but they are not all; since there are certain unsearchable ones, and like a great deep, profound and hidden. Or are these too known to certain more excellent members of this man, who with his head the Savior is the whole Christ? For unsearchable they are perhaps said to a man, because by his own powers he cannot search them out. But why could he not by the gift of the Holy Spirit, to whomever the Lord deigns to confer this? For so that was said: *God dwells in inaccessible light*[^9]; and yet we hear: *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*[^10]. Which question is solved thus, that he is inaccessible to our powers, but is approached by his gifts. Although, even if to no one at all of the saints, as long as the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, is it granted to know all the judgments of God, because truly it is too much for a man, since indeed (that I may say something for example, whence the immensity of the judgments of God may be conjectured) no one without the judgment of God is slow in mind, or lame in body: yet the Church has whence to say, that is the people of acquisition, and to say truly: *With my lips I have announced all the judgments of your mouth;* that is, I have kept silent none of your judgments, which you wished to become known to me through your eloquences, but I have announced all absolutely with my lips. For this seems to me to be what he wished to signify, in that he does not say, all your judgments; but, *all the judgments of your mouth,* that is, those which you have told me: so that by his mouth we understand his eloquence, which he made to us in the many revelations of the saints, and in the two Testaments; all which judgments the Church does not cease everywhere to announce with its lips.

**3.** [v. 14.] Then he subjoins and says: *In the way of your testimonies I was delighted, as in all riches.* The way of the testimonies of God we understand to be nothing swifter, nothing surer, nothing briefer, and nothing greater than Christ, in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden. Hence this one too says that he was delighted or pleased in this way, *as in all riches.* For they are the testimonies of God, by which he deigns to prove to us how much he loves us. But God commends his love in us, since, while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Since, then, he himself says: *I am the way*[^11], and the lowliness of his carnal nativity and passion are the evident testimonies of the divine love toward us; without doubt the way of the testimonies of God is Christ. For through these testimonies which we see fulfilled in him, we await and hope that the future things too toward us, which are promised eternal, are to be fulfilled. For he who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all, how has he not also with him given us all things?

**4.** [vv. 15, 16.] There follows: *In your commandments I will chatter and I will consider your ways.* What the Greek has as adoleskheso, some Latin translators rendered *I will chatter,* some *I will be exercised;* which two seem to be diverse from one another: but if the exercise of the mind is understood, with a certain delight of discussion, both are joined, and as it were from both something one is tempered, so that chattering is not alien to such an exercise. For the talkative are wont to be called chatterers. So the Church exercises itself in the commandments of God, talkative with copious discussions of teachers against all the enemies of the Christian and catholic faith: which discussions are then fruitful to those discussing, if there is considered there nothing but the *ways of the Lord,* as it is written, *mercy and truth*[^12]; the fullness of which two is found in Christ. Through this sweet exercise comes about too what he adds: *In your justifications I will meditate, I will not forget your words.* Therefore certainly *I will meditate,* that *I may not forget.* Whence that blessed man in the first psalm will meditate in the law of the Lord day and night.

**5.** In all these things which, as we could, we have discussed, let us remember, dearest ones, that he who hides in his heart the eloquences of the Lord, and announces with his lips all the judgments of his mouth, and is delighted in the way of his testimonies as in all riches, and, chattering or exercising himself in his commandments, considers his ways, and meditates in his justifications, lest he forget his words, by all which things he surely appears instructed in the law and teaching of God; yet prays and says: *Blessed are you, O Lord; teach me your justifications.* Where nothing else is understood to be asked, except the help of grace, that what he now knows by word, he may learn by deed too.
