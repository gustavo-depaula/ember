# On the Same Psalm 118 (4)

**1.** [v. 9.] Let us consider these verses, dearest ones, in this psalm, and, as the Lord grants, let us scrutinize his sacred letters: *By what does the younger one correct his way? By keeping your words.* He asks himself, and answers himself. *By what does the younger one correct his way?* thus far is the question: then the answer: *By keeping your words.* But in this place the keeping of the words of God is to be understood as the doing of the precepts: for in vain are they kept in memory, if they are not kept in life too. For some, by holding the words of God, act so that they may not forget them, and do not act by living so that they may be corrected: but this one does not say: By what does the younger one exercise his memory? but: *By what does he correct his way?* and to this he answers: *By keeping your words.* Nor in any way is the way to be called corrected, as long as the life is perverse.

**2.** But what does this "younger one" mean? For he could say: By what does a man correct his way? or: By what does a man (vir) correct his way? which is often set in the Scriptures so that, from the more honored sex, man (homo) is understood, by a manner of expression by which the whole is signified from a part. For neither is the woman not blessed who has not gone in the counsel of the impious: where yet it was said: *Blessed is the man (vir)*[^1]. But here he does not say "Man" (homo), nor "Man" (vir), but "the younger one." Is the older one to be despaired of? or by what other thing does even the older one correct his way, than by keeping the words of God? Or is it perhaps an admonition by what age it most of all ought to be done; according to that which is written elsewhere: *Son, from your youth take up instruction, and even to gray hairs you will find wisdom*[^2]? There is another understanding too, that here is recognized the younger evangelical son, who, having set out from his father into a far country, poured out his substance, living prodigally with harlots; and after he fed swine, having endured want and hunger, returned to himself, and said: *I will arise, and go to my father*[^3]. For by what did he correct his way, except by keeping the words of God, which, hungering, he desired as it were the paternal bread? For his older brother would not correct his way, who said to his father: *Behold, so many years I serve you, and I never passed by your commandment*[^4]. The younger one, then, corrected his way, which he confessed to have so depraved and held perverse, that he said to his father: *I am no longer worthy to be called your son*[^5]. A third understanding too occurs to me, which I indeed, as far as I am wise according to my small measure, prefer to the two above: that the older one be recognized as the old man; and the new, the younger: the older, who bears the image of the earthly man; the younger, who bears the image of the heavenly: because *not first what is spiritual, but what is animal; afterward the spiritual*[^6]. Let anyone, then, be, as far as pertains to the age of the body, decrepit with aged oldness, he will be younger to God, converted, having received the newness of grace; and in this he corrects his way, by keeping his words: this is the word of faith which we preach, and it is the faith which works through love.

**3.** [v. 10.] But this younger people, the son of grace, the new man, the singer of the new song, the heir of the new testament, this younger one not Cain, but Abel; not Ishmael, but Isaac; not Esau, but Israel; not Manasseh, but Ephraim; not Eli, but Samuel; not Saul, but David; attend to what he adds: *With my whole heart,* he says, *I have sought you out; do not repel me from your commandments.* Behold he prays, that he may be helped to keep the words of God, in which he had said the younger one corrects his way. For surely this is: *Do not repel me from your commandments*: for what is it to be repelled by God, except not to be helped? For human weakness is not tempered to his right and arduous commandments, unless his preceding love help. But those whom he does not help, these he is rightly said to repel, as though by a flaming sword the unworthy are kept from extending the hand to the tree of life. But who is worthy, from when through one man sin entered into the world, and through sin death, and so it passed into all men, in whom all sinned? But the undue mercy of God heals our due misery. For this one who speaks and says: *With my whole heart I have sought you out;* and how could he, except that he, turned away, were converted to him by the one to whom it is said: *God, you, turning us, will give us life*[^7], and the one who seeks the lost and recalls the erring, who says: *What had perished I will seek, and what had erred I will recall*[^8]?

**4.** [vv. 11, 12.] Hence it is that he both corrects his way by keeping the words of God, with him governing, with him doing it: for he could not by himself, since the prophet Jeremiah confesses and says: *I know, O Lord, that a man's way is not his own, nor will a man go and correct his way*[^9]. For from the Lord this man too wished it above, where he said: *Would that my ways were directed*: and here, where he added: *In my heart I have hidden your words, that I may not sin against you;* he immediately sought divine help, lest in his heart the words of God should be hidden without fruit, unless works of righteousness followed. For when he had said this, he added: *Blessed are you, O Lord; teach me your justifications. Teach* he said, in the way those learn them who do them; not in the way those do who, only that they may have what to speak, merely remember them. For surely he had already said: *In my heart I have hidden your words, that I may not sin against you*: Why, then, does he still seek to learn the things which, hidden, he already keeps in his heart? which surely he would not have done, unless he had learned them. Why, then, does he add and say: *Teach me your justifications,* except because he wishes to learn them by doing, not by speaking or holding in memory? Since, then, as is read in another psalm: *He will give a blessing who gave the law*[^10]; therefore: *Blessed are you, O Lord; teach me,* he says, *your justifications.* For because in my heart I hid your words, that I may not sin against you, you gave the law; give also the blessing of grace, that I may learn by doing what you bade by intimating. Let these things suffice, that your minds may be nourished without weariness: the things that follow desire another sermon.
