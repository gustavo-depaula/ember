# On the Same Psalm 21

**1.** What God did not wish to be passed over in silence through his Scriptures, neither must it be passed over in silence by us, and it must be heard by you. The passion of the Lord, as we know, was done once; for Christ died once, the just for the unjust. And we know, and hold it certain, and retain it with unshaken faith, *that Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more, and death will no more have dominion over him*[^1]; these are the words of the Apostle: yet, lest we forget what was done once, it is done every year in our memory. As often as the Passover is celebrated, does Christ die so often? But yet the yearly remembrance as it were represents what was once done, and so moves us as though we saw the Lord hanging on the cross; yet not mocking, but believing. For hanging on the wood he was mocked, sitting in heaven he is adored. Or perhaps he is still mocked, and we are not now to be angry at the Jews, who derided him dying, not reigning? And who is there that still mocks Christ? Would that it were one, would that it were two, would that they could be numbered! All the chaff of his threshing floor mocks him, and the wheat groans that the Lord is mocked: this I wish to groan with you; for it is a time for mourning. The passion of the Lord is celebrated: it is a time for groaning, a time for weeping, a time for confessing and praying. And which of us is fit to pour out tears worthy of so great a sorrow? But what does the prophet say? *Who will give water to my head, and to my eyes a fountain of tears?*[^2] if there were truly a fountain of tears in our eyes, it would not itself suffice. That Christ is mocked in a thing that is open, in that thing where no one can say, I did not understand! For to him who possesses the whole world, a part is offered; and it is said to him sitting at the right hand of the Father, Behold what you have here: and for the whole earth Africa alone is shown to him!

**2.** The words which we have just heard, brothers, where do we place them? If they could be written with tears! Who was the woman who entered with the ointment? Whose type did she bear? was it not the Church's? Of what was that ointment a figure? was it not of the good odor, of which the Apostle says: *We are the good odor of Christ in every place?*[^3] For the Apostle too suggested the person of the Church herself: and what he said, *we are*, he said to the faithful. And what did he say? We are the good odor of Christ in every place. In every place Paul said all the faithful are the good odor of Christ, and it is contradicted, and it is said: Africa alone smells well, the whole world stinks. We are the good odor of Christ in every place, who says it? The Church. This good odor that vessel of ointment signified, with which the Lord was anointed. Let us see whether the Lord himself does not bear witness with her. When a certain one, seeking his own things, greedy, a thief, that is, that Judas, said of that ointment: *Why this waste? the precious thing could have been sold, and profit the poor*, for he wished to sell the good odor of Christ, what did the Lord answer? *Why are you troublesome to the woman? She has done a good work upon me.* And what more shall I say, when he himself said: *But wherever this Gospel is preached in the whole world, what this woman has done will also be told*[^4]! Is there anything to add? is there anything to take away? is there any reason to lend an ear to slanderers? Either the Lord lied, or did he deceive him? Let them choose what to say: let them either say that truth lied, or say that truth was deceived. *Wherever this Gospel is preached.* And as though you asked of him: For where will it be preached? *In the whole world*, he says. Let us hear the psalm, let us see whether it says this. Let us hear what is sung in lamentation, and truly a thing worthy of lamentation when it is sung to the deaf. I wonder, brothers, whether today this psalm is read also in the party of Donatus. I beg you, my brothers, I confess to you, the mercy of Christ knows, that I so wonder as though they were stones there, and do not hear. What is said more openly to the deaf? The passion of Christ is recited as evidently as the Gospel, and it was said I know not how many years before the Lord was born of the Virgin Mary: it was a herald announcing the judge to come. Let us read it, as far as the straits of the time permit, not according to the affection of our sorrow, but, as I said, as far as the straits of the time permit.

**3.** [v 2.] *God, my God, look upon me: why have you forsaken me?* This first verse we heard on the cross, where the Lord said: *Eli, Eli;* which is, *My God, my God: Lama sabachthani?* which is, *why have you forsaken me?* The Evangelist interpreted that, and said he had said it in Hebrew: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* What did the Lord wish to say? For God had not forsaken him, since he himself was God; surely the Son of God is God, surely the Word of God is God. Hear that Evangelist from the head of his Gospel, who eructated what he had drunk from the breast of the Lord, let us see whether Christ is God: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^5]. That very Word, then, which was God, *was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^6]. And when the Word, who was God, had been made flesh, he hung on the cross, and said: *My God, my God, look upon me: why have you forsaken me?* Why is it said, but because we were there, but because the body of Christ is the Church? Why did he say, *My God, my God, look upon me: why have you forsaken me?* but as if in a manner making us attentive and saying, this psalm is written of me? *Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions.* Of what transgressions, of him of whom it was said: *Who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth*[^7]. How, then, does he say *of my transgressions;* but because for our transgressions he himself prays, and made our transgressions his own transgressions, that he might make his righteousness our righteousness?

**4.** [v 3.] *My God, I will cry to you by day, and you will not hear; and by night, and not unto folly for me*: he said it surely of me, of you, of him; for he bore his body, that is, the Church. Unless perhaps you suppose, brothers, that when the Lord said, *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me*[^8], he feared to die. The soldier is not braver than the commander. It is enough for the servant to be as his lord. Paul says, a soldier of King Christ: *I am pressed between the two, having a desire to be dissolved, and to be with Christ*[^9]. He longs for death, that he may be with Christ, and does Christ himself fear death? But what was he doing but bearing our weakness, and, for those still set in his body who fear death, saying these things? Thence was that voice, it was the voice of his members, not of the head; so here too, *By day and by night I have cried, and you will not hear.* For many cry in tribulation, and are not heard: but unto salvation, not unto folly. Paul cried that the sting of the flesh might be taken from him, and was not heard so that it should be taken away; and it was said to him: *My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness*[^10]. Therefore he was not heard; but not unto folly, but unto wisdom: that man might understand that God is a physician, and that tribulation is a medicine unto salvation, not a punishment unto damnation. Set under the medicine you are burned, you are cut, you cry out: the physician does not hear unto your will, but hears unto your health.

**5.** [v 4.] *But you dwell in the holy place, the praise of Israel.* You dwell in those whom you have sanctified, and whom you make to understand that for their benefit you do not hear some, and for their damnation you hear some. For his benefit Paul was not heard, for his damnation the devil was heard. Job asked to tempt, and it was granted. The demons asked to go into the swine, and were heard. The demons are heard, the Apostle is not heard: but they are heard unto damnation, the Apostle is not heard unto salvation: *because not unto folly for me. But you dwell in the holy place, the praise of Israel.* Why do you not hear your own too? Why do I say these things? Remember always to say, Thanks be to God. And here there is a great multitude, and those who are not accustomed to come, have come: to all I say, that a Christian set in tribulation is proved, if he has not forsaken his God. For when it is well with a man, the Christian is deserted by himself. Fire enters the furnace, and the furnace of the goldsmith is a thing of great mystery. There is the gold, there is the chaff, there the fire works in a narrow space. That fire is not diverse, and does diverse things; it turns the chaff to ashes, it takes the dross from the gold. But those in whom God dwells become better in tribulation, as gold proved. And if perhaps the enemy the devil should ask, and it should be granted him: whether by some pain of the body, or by some loss, or by the loss of his own, let him have his heart fixed in him who does not withdraw himself; and even if he as it were withdraws his ear from the one weeping, yet he applies his mercy to the one praying. He knows what he does who made us, he knows also how to remake us. He is a good builder who built the house; and if anything falls there, he knows how to repair it.

**6.** [v 5.] And see what he says: *In you our fathers hoped: they hoped, and you rescued them.* And we know, and we read, how many of our fathers hoping in him God rescued. He rescued the very people of Israel from the land of Egypt; he rescued the three young men from the furnace of fire; he rescued Daniel from the den of lions; he rescued Susanna from a false charge. All called upon him, and were rescued. Did he fail toward his Son, so as not to hear him hanging on the cross? But why is he himself not rescued now, who said: *In you our fathers hoped, and you rescued them?*

**7.** [v 7.] *But I am a worm, and not a man. A worm, and not a man*: for man too is a worm; but he, *A worm, and not a man.* Whence not a man? Because God. Why, then, did he so cast himself down as to say, *A worm*? Is it because a worm is born of flesh without intercourse, as Christ was of the Virgin Mary? And a worm, and yet not a man. Why a worm? Because mortal, because born of flesh, because born without intercourse. Why not a man? Because in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

**8.** *The reproach of men and the outcast of the people*: see how great things he suffered! Now, that we may speak of the passion, and come to it with greater groaning, see how great things he now suffers, and then see why! For what fruit? Behold, our fathers hoped, and were rescued from the land of Egypt. And as I said, so many called upon him, and at once for the time, not in the future life, but immediately they were set free. Job himself was granted to the devil asking, rotting with worms: yet in this life he recovered his health, he received double what he had lost: but the Lord was scourged, and no one came to his aid; he was defiled with spittle, and no one came to his aid; he was struck with blows, and no one came to his aid; he was crowned with thorns, no one came to his aid; he was lifted up on the wood, no one rescued him; he cries, *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* no one comes to his aid. Why, my brothers? why? at what price did he suffer such great things? All these things which he suffered are a price. At the price of what thing he suffered such great things, let us recite, let us see what he says. First let us seek what he suffered, then why: and let us see how much they are enemies of Christ, who confess that he suffered such great things, and take away the why. Let us here hear the whole in this psalm, both what he suffered, and why. Hold these two, what and why. Now let me explain the what itself. Let us not linger over it, and the very words of the psalm reach you better. See what the Lord suffers, attend, Christians: *The reproach of men and the outcast of the people.*

**9.** [vv 8.9.] *All who saw me mocked me, they spoke with their lips, and shook the head. He hoped in the Lord, let him rescue him; let him save him, since he wants him*: but why did they say these things? Because he had become man, they spoke as though against a man.

**10.** [v 10.] *Because you are he who drew me out of the womb*: would they say such things against that which in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God? For that Word through which all things were made was not drawn out of a womb, except because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. *Because you drew me out of the womb: my God, from my mother's breasts*: for before the ages my Father, from my mother's breasts my God.

**11.** [v 11.] *Upon you I was cast from the womb*: that is, that you alone might be my hope, now as it were a man, now as it were weak, now the Word made flesh. *From the womb of my mother you are my God*: not from you my God; for from you my Father: but from the womb of my mother, my God.

**12.** [v 12.] *Do not depart from me: because tribulation is near, because there is none who helps*: see one deserted; and woe to us, if he deserts us, *because there is none who helps.*

**13.** [v 13.] *Many calves have surrounded me, fat bulls have beset me*: the people and the chief men; the people, many calves; the chief men, fat bulls.

**14.** [v 14.] *They opened their mouth against me, like a lion seizing and roaring*: let us hear their roaring in the Gospel: *Crucify, crucify!*[^11]

**15.** [v 15.] *Like water I was poured out, and all my bones were scattered*: by his bones he means his firm ones; for the bones are firm in the body. When did he scatter his bones? When he said to them: *Behold, I send you as lambs into the midst of wolves*[^12]. He scattered his firm ones, and was poured out like water: for water, when it is poured out, either washes or waters; Christ was poured out like water, the filthy were washed, minds were watered. *My heart was made like wax melting, in the midst of my belly*: by his belly he means the weak in his Church. How was his heart made like wax? His heart is his Scripture, that is, his wisdom which was in the Scriptures. For the Scripture was closed; no one understood it: the Lord was crucified, and it was melted like wax, that all the weak might understand the Scripture; for thence too the veil of the temple was rent, because what was veiled was revealed.

**16.** [v 16.] *My strength has dried up like a potsherd*: magnificently he said, My name was made firmer by tribulation. For as a potsherd before the fire is soft, after the fire strong; so the Lord's name before the passion was despised, after the passion is honored. *And my tongue has clung to my jaws*: as that member in us avails only for speaking; so he said that his preachers, his tongue, clung to his jaws, that from his inner parts they might take wisdom. *And you have brought me down into the dust of death.*

**17.** [v 17.] *Because many dogs have surrounded me, the council of the malicious has surrounded me*: see the Gospel too. *They have pierced my hands and my feet.* Then were made those wounds, the scars of which the doubting disciple handled, he who said: *Unless I put my fingers into the scars of his wounds, I will not believe.* When he said to him: *Come, put your hand in, unbelieving one;* and he put his hand in, and cried out: *My Lord and my God!* And he: *Because you have seen me, you have believed; blessed are those who do not see and believe*[^13]. *They have pierced my hands and my feet.*

**18.** [v 18.] *They have numbered all my bones*: when, hanging, he was stretched out on the wood. The stretching of the body on the wood could not be better described than by saying: *They have numbered all my bones.*

**19.** [v 19.] *But they themselves considered, and gazed at me*: they considered, and did not understand; they gazed, and did not see. They had eyes as far as the flesh, not a heart as far as the Word. *They divided my garments among themselves*: his garments, his sacraments. Attend, brothers. His garments, his sacraments could be divided through heresies: but there was there a garment which no one divided. *And over my vesture they cast lots. There was there a tunic*, says the Evangelist, *woven from above*[^14]. Therefore from heaven, therefore from the Father, therefore from the Holy Spirit. What is that tunic, but love, which no one can divide? What is that tunic, but unity? Over it the lot is cast, no one divides it. The heretics could divide the sacraments among themselves, they did not divide love. And because they could not divide it, they withdrew: but it remains whole. By lot it falls to certain ones: he who has this is secure; no one moves him from the catholic Church, and if he begins to have it outside, he is brought in, as the olive branch by the dove.

**20.** [v 20.] *But you, Lord, do not put your help far off.* And it was done: after three days he rose again. *Look to my defense.*

**21.** [v 21.] *Rescue my soul from the broadsword*: that is, from death; for the broadsword is a sword, and by the sword he wished death to be understood. *And my only one from the hand of the dog. My soul, my only one*, head and body: *My only one* he said, the Church: *from the hand*, that is, from the power of the dog. Who are the dogs? Those who bark in canine fashion, and do not understand against whom. Nothing is done to them, and they bark. What did the dog do, passing on its way? Yet it barks. Those who bark with blind eyes, not discerning against whom or for whom, are dogs.

**22.** [v 22.] *Save me from the mouth of the lion*: you know who the roaring lion is, going about and seeking whom he may devour. *And my humility from the horns of the unicorns*: he would not say unicorns, but the proud; therefore he added, *my humility.*

**23.** [v 23.] You have heard what he suffered, and what he prayed, that he might be rescued from these: let us now attend to why he suffered. Now see, brothers, he who is not in that lot, for which Christ suffered, why is he a Christian? Behold, we understand what he suffered: his bones were numbered, he was mocked, his garments were divided, moreover lots were cast over his vesture, the raging and furious surrounded him, and all his bones were scattered, and this we both hear and read in the Gospel. Let us see why. O Christ, Son of God, if you had been unwilling, you would not have suffered, show us the fruit of your passion. Hear, he says, the fruit: I am not silent, but men are deaf. Hear, he says, the fruit, why I suffered all these things. *I will tell of your name to my brothers.* Let us see whether in a part he tells the name of God to his brothers. *I will tell of your name to my brothers: in the midst of the Church I will sing of you.* This is done. But let us see what that Church is. For he said: *In the midst of the Church I will sing of you.* Let us see the Church, for which he suffered.

**24.** [v 24.] *You who fear the Lord, praise him*: wherever God is feared and praised, there is the Church of Christ. See, my brothers, whether in these days throughout the whole world it is without cause that it is said: Amen and Alleluia. Is God not feared there? Is God not praised there? Donatus went out, and said: He is not feared at all, the whole world has perished. Without cause you say: The whole world has perished. So a small part remained in Africa? Does Christ, then, not say something, whence he may stop these mouths? does he not say something, whence he may root out the tongues of those saying these things? Let us see, lest perhaps we find it. Still it is said to us: *In the midst of the Church*: he speaks of our Church. *You who fear the Lord, praise him*: let us see whether they praise the Lord, and let us understand whether he speaks of them, and whether in the midst of their Church he is praised. How do they praise Christ, who say: He has lost the whole world, the devil has taken the whole from him, and he himself has remained in a part? But still let us see, let him say more openly, let him speak more openly: let there be nothing to interpret, nothing to suspect. *All the seed of Jacob, magnify him.* Perhaps still they say: We are the seed of Jacob. If it is they, let us see.

**25.** [v 25.] *Let all the seed of Israel fear him.* Still let them say: We are the seed of Israel; let us permit it, let them say. *Because he has not spurned nor despised the prayer of the poor*: of what poor? Of those not presuming upon themselves. Let us see whether they are poor, who say: We are the righteous. Christ cries: *Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions.* But still let them say what they wish. *Nor has he turned away his face from me, and when I cried to him, he heard me.* Why did he hear? to what thing?

**26.** [v 26.] *With you is my praise*: he placed his praise with God; he taught not to presume in man. Still let them say what they wish. Now indeed they have begun to be burned, the fire has begun to draw near; there is none who can hide himself from its heat. But still let them say: We too have placed our praise with him, and we too do not presume in ourselves; let them still say. *In the great Church I will confess to you*: now here I think it has begun to touch the inner parts. What is the great Church, brothers? Is a tiny part of the world the great Church? The great Church is the whole world. Now if anyone wishes to contradict Christ: Tell us, you said: *In the great Church I will confess to you*: what great Church? You have remained in a scrap of Africa, you have lost the whole world: you shed your blood for the whole, but you have suffered an invader. These things we said to the Lord as though asking, yet knowing what he would say. Let us suppose that we do not know what he says: does he not answer us? Be still, I still say something whence no one may doubt. Let us wait, then, for what he is about to say. Now I wished to pronounce it, and not to allow men to interpret anything else, since Christ says: *In the great Church.* And you say that he has remained in an extreme part. And still they dare to say: Our Church too is great; what do Bagai and Thamugadi seem to you? If he does not say something whence they may be struck dumb, still let them say that Numidia alone is the great Church.

**27.** [vv 27.28.] Let us see, let us hear the Lord still: *My vows I will render before those who fear him.* What are his vows? The sacrifice which he offered to God. Do you know what sacrifice? The faithful know the vows which he rendered before those who fear him: for it follows: *The poor will eat, and be satisfied.* Blessed are the poor, because they eat for this purpose, that they may be satisfied: for the poor eat; but those who are rich are not satisfied, because they do not hunger. The poor will eat: thence was that fisherman Peter, thence was the other fisherman John and James his brother, thence was also the tax collector Matthew. Of the poor were they themselves, who ate and were satisfied, suffering such things as they ate. He gave his supper, he gave his passion: he is satisfied who imitates. The poor imitated: for they themselves suffered so as to follow the footsteps of Christ. *The poor will eat.* But why the poor? *And those who seek him will praise the Lord.* The rich praise themselves, the poor praise the Lord. Why are they poor? Because they praise the Lord, and seek the Lord. The Lord is the riches of the poor: therefore the house is empty, that the heart may be full of riches. Let the rich seek wherewith to fill the chest, the poor seek wherewith to fill the heart: and when they have filled it, those who seek him praise the Lord. And see, brothers, those who are truly poor, of what they are rich: because not in the chest, not in the barn, not in the storeroom: *Their hearts will live unto the age of the age.*

**28.** Therefore attend. The Lord suffered; all the things you have heard the Lord suffered. We ask why he suffered, and he has begun to tell: *I will tell of your name to my brothers, in the midst of the Church I will sing of you.* But still they say: This is the Church. *Let all the seed of Israel fear him.* They say: We are the seed of Israel. *Because he has not spurned nor despised the prayer of the poor.* Still they say: We are they. *Nor has he turned away his face from me*: Christ the Lord himself from himself, that is, from his Church, which is his body. *With you is my praise.* You wish to praise yourselves. But they answer: We too altogether praise him. *My vows to the Lord I will render before those who fear him.* The sacrifice of peace, the sacrifice of love, the sacrifice of his body the faithful know: it cannot now be disputed about from there. *My vows I will render before those who fear him.* Let the tax collectors eat, let the fishermen eat, let them eat, let them imitate the Lord, let them suffer, let them be satisfied. The Lord himself died, the poor too die: and the death of the disciples is added to the death of the master. Why? Give me the fruit. *All the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord.* Come, brothers, why do you seek from us what we may answer to the party of Donatus? Behold the psalm, and here it is read today, and there it is read today. Let us write it on our foreheads, let us go forth with it, let not our tongue be still, let it say these things: Behold Christ suffered, behold the merchant shows the price, behold the price which he gave, his blood was shed. In a sack he carried our price: he was struck with a lance, the sack was poured out, and the price of the world flowed forth. What do you say to me, O heretic? Is it not the price of the world? Was Africa alone redeemed? You do not dare to say: The whole world was redeemed, but it has perished. What invader did Christ suffer, that he should lose his own thing? Behold, *all the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord.* Still let it satisfy you, and let it say. If it said the ends of the earth, and did not say *all the ends of the earth;* they would have to say: Behold we have the ends of the earth in Mauretania. *All the ends of the earth* it said: O heretic, *all* it said; by what way will you go out, to escape the question? You have no way to go out, but you have whither to go in.

**29.** [v 29.] I beg you, I do not wish to dispute from it, lest it be said that my discourse avails something: attend to the psalm, read the psalm. Behold Christ suffered, his blood was shed: behold our redeemer, behold our price. What did he buy, let it be told me. Why do we ask? What if someone says to me: O fool, why do you ask? You carry the book, there you have from where he bought, there seek what he bought. Behold, there you have it: *All the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord.* For the ends of the earth will be reminded. But the heretics have forgotten, and therefore they hear it every year. Do you think they put their ears there, when their reader says: *All the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord?* Come, perhaps it is one verse: you were thinking of something else, you were chatting with your brother, when he said that: attend, because he repeats, and beats upon the deaf: *And all the families of the nations will adore in his sight.* Still he is deaf, he does not hear, let him be beaten again: *Because the kingdom is the Lord's, and he himself will rule the nations.* Hold these three verses, brothers. Today they were sung there too, or perhaps they have blotted them out. Believe me, my brothers, I so burn, I so suffer violence, that I wonder at I know not what deafness and hardness of their heart, so that I sometimes doubt whether they have that in their books. Today all run to church, today all attentively hear the psalm, all hear with suspended heart. But suppose they are not attentive: is it one verse, *All the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord?* You awake, but still you rub your eyes: *And all the families of the nations will adore in his sight.* Shake off sleep, still you are heavy, hear: *Because the kingdom is the Lord's, and he himself will rule the nations.*

**30.** If there is still something they may say, I do not know: let them quarrel with the Scriptures, not with us. Behold the book itself, let them contend against it. Where is the tongue: We saved the Scriptures, lest they be burned? They were saved, whence you may be burned. What did you save? Open, read: you saved it, and you assail it. What did you save from the flame, which you wish to blot out with the tongue? I do not believe, I do not believe that you saved it: I altogether do not believe, you did not save it. Most truly our people say, that you handed it over. He is proved a traditor, who, the testament being read, does not follow it. Behold it is read, and I follow it; it is read, and you refuse. Whose hand cast it into the flame? He who believes and follows; or he who grieves because there is something to be read? I do not wish to know who saved it: from wherever the book was found, the testament of our Father has come out from whatever cave: I know not what thieves wished to take it away, I know not what persecutors wished to burn it: from wherever it was brought forth, let it be read. Why do you quarrel? We are brothers, why do we quarrel? Our father did not die intestate. He made a testament, and so died: he died, and rose again. So long is there contention about the inheritance of the dead, until the testament is brought forth in public; and when the testament has been brought forth in public, all are silent, that the tablets may be opened and recited: the judge listens attentively, the advocates are silent, the heralds make silence, the whole people is suspended, that the words of the dead man, not feeling in his tomb, may be read. He lies without feeling in the tomb, and his words avail: Christ sits in heaven, and is his testament contradicted? Open, let us read. We are brothers, why do we contend? Let our soul be appeased, our Father did not leave us without a testament. He who made the testament lives forever: he hears our voices, he recognizes his own. Let us read, why do we quarrel? Where the inheritance itself is found, let us hold it. Open the testament, read in the first chapter of the Psalter itself: *Ask of me*[^15]. But who says it? Perhaps not Christ. There you have it: *The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you*[^16]. Therefore the Son of God says it, or the Father says it to his Son. What, then, does he say to the Son? *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession.* It is wont to happen, brothers, that when there is inquiry about a possession, the adjoining owners are sought. Between this and that adjoining owner, the heir is sought, or to whom it is given, or who bought it. Between which adjoining owners is it sought? Between this and that one possessing. He who left all the ends, left no adjoining owners. Wherever you turn yourself, it is Christ. You have the ends of the earth as inheritance, come here, possess the whole with me. Why, by quarreling, do you call to a part? Come here: you are conquered to your own good, you will have the whole. Or do you still cavil? I have now read the testament, and you cavil. Or do you still cavil, because he said the ends of the earth, he did not say all the ends of the earth? Let us read, then. How was it read? *All the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord. And all the families of the nations will adore in his sight. Because the kingdom is the Lord's, and he himself will rule the nations.* It is his, not yours. Recognize the Lord: recognize the possession of the Lord.

**31.** But you too, because you wish to possess your private things, and not in common unity with Christ; for you wish to dominate on earth, not to reign with him in heaven, possess your houses. And sometimes we come to them saying: Let us seek the truth, let us find the truth. And they: You hold what you hold: you have your sheep, I have my sheep; do not be troublesome to my sheep, because I too am not troublesome to your sheep. Thanks be to God: they are my sheep, they are his sheep: what did Christ buy? Nay, let them be neither mine nor yours: but let them be his who bought them, his who sealed them. Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase. Why do I have mine, do you have yours? If Christ is there, let mine go there, because they are not mine: if Christ is here, let yours come here, because they are not yours. For the sake of possessions let them kiss our head and hands, and let alien sons perish. It is not my possession, he says. What is this? Let us see whether it is not your possession, let us see whether you do not claim it for yourself. I labor for the name of Christ, you for the name of Donatus. For if you attend to Christ, Christ is everywhere. You say: Behold, here is Christ: I say: He is throughout the whole. Praise the Lord, children, praise the name of the Lord. Whence do they praise? How far do they praise? From the rising of the sun even to its setting praise the name of the Lord. Behold what Church I show, behold what Christ bought, behold what he redeemed, behold for whom he gave his blood. But what do you say? I too gather for him. *He who does not gather with me*, he says, *scatters*[^17]. You divide unity, you seek your own possessions. And why do they have the name of Christ? Because for the defense of your possession you have set up the titles of Christ. Do not some do this in their house? Lest some powerful man invade his house, he sets there the titles of a powerful man, lying titles. He himself wishes to be the possessor, and wishes the front of his house to be fortified with an alien title; so that when the title is read, anyone, terrified by the power of the name, may keep himself from invading. They did that, when they condemned the Maximianists. They acted before judges, and recited their council: as if showing titles, that they might seem bishops. Then the judge asked: Who is this other bishop of the party of Donatus? The Office answered: We know none but Aurelius the catholic. They, fearing the laws, did not answer except about one bishop. But that they might be heard by the judge, they put forward the name of Christ: upon their own possession they set up his titles. The Lord is good, who spares them, and where he has found his own titles, claims it for his possession. His mercy is powerful, that he may do this for them, whomever he has found bearing the name of Christ, let him gather them. And see, brothers, when some powerful man has found his own titles, does he not rightly claim the thing for himself, and say: He would not set up my titles, unless the thing were mine? He set up my titles, the thing is mine: where I find my name, it is mine. Does he change the titles? The title that was, is the same: the possessor is changed, the title is not changed. So too those who have the baptism of Christ, if they come to unity, we do not change the titles, or blot out the titles; but we recognize the titles of our king, the titles of our emperor. But what do we say? O wretched house, let him possess you whose titles you have; you have the titles of Christ, do not be the possession of Donatus.

**32.** Many things we have said, brothers; but let that not depart from your memory, which is read today. Behold, again I say, and it must often be said: through this very day, that is, through the sacraments of this day, I bind you, that it may not go out from your hearts. *All the ends of the earth will be reminded, and will be converted to the Lord. And all the families of the nations will adore in his sight. Because the kingdom is the Lord's, and he himself will rule the nations.* Against so open and manifestly demonstrated a possession of Christ, do not hear the words of the slanderer. Whatever they contradict, men say it: but this God says.
