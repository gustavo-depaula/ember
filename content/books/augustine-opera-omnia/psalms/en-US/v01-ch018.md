# Exposition of Psalm 18

**1.** [v 1.] The title is known: nor does the Lord Jesus Christ say these things, but these things are said of him.

**2.** [v 2.] *The heavens declare the glory of God*: the righteous Evangelists, in whom God dwells as in heavens, set forth the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, or the glory by which the Son glorified the Father upon the earth. *And the firmament proclaims the works of his hands*: and the firmament proclaims the deeds of the Lord's mighty works, the firmament made by the confidence of the Holy Spirit even a heaven, which before, through fear, was earth.

**3.** [v 3.] *Day to day pours forth word*: Spirit to spiritual ones brings forth the fullness of the unchangeable Wisdom of God, which Word in the beginning is God with God. *And night to night proclaims knowledge*: and the mortality of the flesh, as to carnal ones set far off, by suggesting faith, proclaims the knowledge to come.

**4.** [v 4.] *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard*: through whom the voices of the Evangelists have not been heard, since the Gospel was preached in all tongues.

**5.** *Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and to the ends of the world their words.*

**6.** [v 6.] *In the sun he has set his tabernacle*: but the Lord, that he might war against the kingdoms of temporal errors, about to send not peace but a sword upon the earth, has set in time, or in manifestation, as it were his military dwelling, that is, the dispensation of his incarnation. *And he himself like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber*: and he himself coming forth from the virginal womb, where God was joined to human nature as a bridegroom to a bride. *He has exulted like a giant to run his way*: he has exulted like one most strong, and surpassing other men with incomparable strength, not to dwell in the way, but to run it. For he did not stand in the way of sinners.

**7.** [v 7.] *From the height of heaven is his going forth*: from the Father is his going forth, not temporal, but eternal, by which he was born of the Father. *And his meeting unto the height of heaven.* And he meets, by the fullness of divinity, unto equality with the Father. *And there is none who can hide himself from his heat*: but when the Word was even made flesh and dwelt among us, taking up our mortality, he permitted no mortal to excuse himself from the shadow of death; for the heat of the Word penetrated even that.

**8.** [v 8.] *The law of the Lord is immaculate, converting souls.* The law of the Lord, then, is he himself, who came to fulfill the law, not to dissolve it: and the immaculate law, who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth; not pressing down souls with the yoke of servitude, but converting them to imitate himself by liberty. *The testimony of the Lord is faithful, giving wisdom to little ones*: *The testimony of the Lord is faithful*, because no one knows the Father but the Son, and he to whom the Son wills to reveal him: which things are hidden from the wise, and revealed to little ones; since God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.

**9.** [v 9.] *The acts of righteousness of the Lord are upright, gladdening the heart*: all the acts of righteousness of the Lord are upright in him, who did not teach what he himself did not do; so that those who imitate them might rejoice in heart, in the things they did freely with love, not servilely with fear. *The precept of the Lord is clear, enlightening the eyes*: the precept of the Lord is clear, without the veil of carnal observances, enlightening the sight of the inner man.

**10.** [v 10.] *The fear of the Lord is chaste, remaining unto the age of the age*: the fear of the Lord, not that penal one under the law, dreading that temporal goods be taken away from it, by love of which the soul commits fornication; but the chaste fear, by which the Church, the more ardently it loves its bridegroom, the more diligently it guards against offending him: and therefore perfected love does not cast out this fear, but it remains unto the age of the age.

**11.** [v 11.] *The judgments of the Lord are true, justified in themselves*: the judgments of him who judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, are truly justified unchangeably; for God neither, in threatening or promising, deceives anyone, nor can anyone snatch from him either punishment from the impious, or reward from the pious. *Desirable above gold and a precious stone, much*: whether much gold and stone, or much precious, or much desirable; yet the judgments of God are desirable above the pomps of this world, by desire of which it comes about that the judgments of God are not desired, but feared, or despised, or not believed. But if anyone is himself gold and a precious stone, so as not to be consumed by fire, but taken up into the treasure of God, he desires the judgments of God more than himself, whose will he prefers to his own. *And sweeter above honey and the honeycomb*: and whether anyone is now honey, who, already loosed from the bonds of this life, awaits the day when he may come to the banquet of God; or is still a honeycomb, so that he is as it were wrapped about with this life as with wax, not bound up with it, but filling it, who has need of some pressing of the hand of God not crushing, but pressing out, by which he may be strained from temporal life into eternal; the judgments of God are sweeter to him than he is to himself, because they are sweeter to him above honey and the honeycomb.

**12.** [v 12.] *For your servant keeps them*: for to one who does not keep them, the day of the Lord is bitter. *In keeping them is much reward*: not in some advantage placed outside, but in this very thing, that the judgments of God are kept, is much reward: it is much, because there is rejoicing in them.

**13.** [v 13.] *Who understands transgressions?* But in transgressions what sweetness can there be, where there is no understanding? since who understands transgressions, which close the very eye to which truth is sweet, to which the judgments of God are desirable and sweet; and as darkness closes the eyes, so transgressions close the mind, and allow it neither to see the light, nor itself?

**14.** [v 14.] *From my hidden things cleanse me, Lord*: from the desires lying hidden in me cleanse me, Lord. *And from alien things spare your servant*: lest I be seduced by others; for he who is clean from his own is not captured by alien things. Spare, then, from alien desires, not the proud man and one desiring to be in his own power, but your servant. *If they will not have dominated me, then I will be immaculate*: if my hidden things, and the sins of others, will not have dominated me, then I will be immaculate; for there is no third origin of sin, besides one's own hidden thing by which the devil fell, and the alien thing by which man was seduced, so that by consenting he made it his own. *And I will be cleansed from a great transgression*: from what other, but of pride? For there is no greater transgression than to apostatize from God, which is the beginning of man's pride: and truly that man is immaculate who lacks even this transgression; because this is the last for those returning to God, which was the first for those withdrawing.

**15.** [v 15.] *And the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, will be such as to be well-pleasing in your sight always*: the meditation of my heart not for the boasting of pleasing men, because now there is no pride; but in your sight always, who behold a pure conscience. *Lord, my helper, and my redeemer*: Lord, my helper, of one tending toward you; since you are my redeemer, that I might tend toward you: lest anyone, attributing either to his own wisdom that he is converted to you, or to his own strength that he comes to you, be the more repelled by you who resist the proud; because he has not been cleansed from a great transgression, nor has he been well-pleasing in your sight, who redeem that we may be converted, and help that we may come to you.
