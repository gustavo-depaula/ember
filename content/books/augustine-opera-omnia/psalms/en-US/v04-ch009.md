# Exposition of Psalm 81

**1.** [v. 1.] A Psalm *of Asaph himself.* To this psalm, as to others which are similarly headed, it gave as a title either the name of that man by whom it was written, or the interpretation of that same name; so that to the synagogue, which is Asaph, its understanding may be referred: especially since its first verse too assigns this. For it begins thus: *God stood in the synagogue of gods.* Which gods far be it that we should understand to be the gods of the Gentiles, whether idols, or some heavenly or earthly creature besides men: since a little after this verse this same psalm asserts, and more openly expresses, what gods it wishes to be understood, in whose synagogue God stood, where it says: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High; but you shall die like men, and fall like one of the princes*[^1]. In the synagogue, then, of these sons of the Most High, of whom that same Most High says through Isaiah: *I have begotten sons and exalted them; but they have despised me*[^2], God stood. *In the synagogue* we take the people of Israel; because their own is properly wont to be called the synagogue, although it has also been called the Church. But ours the Apostles never called a synagogue, but always the Church: whether for the sake of distinguishing, or because between a gathering (congregatio), whence the synagogue, and a calling-together (convocatio), whence the Church received its name, there is some difference; namely, that cattle too are wont to be gathered, and they properly, of whom we also speak properly as flocks; but to be called together is rather of those using reason, such as men are. Whence in the person of that same Asaph it is sung in another psalm: *I have become as a beast before you, and I am always with you*[^3]: when surely, although he seemed bound to the one true God, yet for great and supreme goods they sought from him carnal, earthly, temporal things. We find them often called sons too; not by that grace which pertains to the New Testament, but by that which pertains to the Old: because that too is grace by which he chose Abraham, and from his flesh propagated so great a people; by which, when they were not yet born, he loved Jacob, but hated Esau[^4]; by which he freed them from Egypt, by which he brought them into the land of promise, the nations being cast out. For unless this too were grace, it surely would not be said of us, to whom power has been given to become sons of God, not for taking up an earthly, but the heavenly kingdom, in the same Gospel soon after, that we have received grace for grace[^5]; that is, for the promises of the Old Testament, the promises of the New Testament. It is clear, then, as far as I judge, in what synagogue of gods God stood.

**2.** Next it is to be inquired whether we ought to take the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit, or the Trinity itself to have stood *in the synagogue of gods; and in the midst to distinguish gods*: because both each one singly is God, and the Trinity itself is one God. It is indeed not easy to strain this out; because also the presence of God—not corporeal, but spiritual, which befits his substance—cannot be denied to be present to created things, in a manner surely marvelous and scarcely intelligible to few, to whom it is said: *If I ascend into heaven, you are there; if I descend into hell, you are present*[^6]. Whence God is rightly said to stand in the gathering of men invisibly, as he himself fills heaven and earth, which he himself foretells of himself through the prophet[^7]: nor only is he said, but, according to the capacity of the human mind, he is somehow known to stand in the things he created, if man too stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy on account of his inward voice[^8]. Yet, as I judge, this psalm strives to intimate something which came about from a certain time, that God should stand in the synagogue of gods. For that standing by which he fills heaven and earth neither pertains properly to the synagogue, nor is varied by time. *God,* then, *stood in the synagogue of gods,* surely he who said of himself: *I am not sent except to the sheep that are lost, of the house of Israel*[^9]. The cause too is stated: *And in the midst to distinguish gods.* I recognize, then, that God stood in the synagogue of gods, *whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh*: for that God might stand in the synagogue of gods, to this end he came from them according to the flesh. But what God? For not such as those, in whose synagogue of gods he stood; but, as the Apostle there follows, *who is over all God blessed forever*[^10]. I recognize, I say, that he stood; I recognize God too in the midst as bridegroom, of whom a certain friend of his says: *In your midst stands one whom you do not know*[^11]. Of whom indeed a little after in this psalm it is said: *They did not know, nor did they understand, they walk in darkness*[^12]: the Apostle too testifies: *That blindness in part has happened to Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in*[^13]. For they saw him standing in the midst of them; but they did not see him as God, such as he wished himself to be seen, saying: *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^14]. But he distinguishes gods, not by their merits, but by his own grace; from the same lump making some vessels unto honor, others unto dishonor[^15]. For who distinguishes you? For what have you that you have not received, but if you have received, why do you glory as though you had not received[^16]?

**3.** [vv. 2-3.] Hear too the voice of God distinguishing, hear too the voice of the Lord dividing the flame of fire[^17]: *How long do you judge iniquity, and accept the persons of sinners?* As elsewhere: *How long heavy of heart*[^18]? Is it not even until the coming of him who is the light of the heart? I gave the law; you resisted hard: I sent the Prophets; you afflicted them with injuries, or killed them, or connived at those doing these things. But that it may not be worthy even to speak to those who killed the servants of God sent to them; you who kept silent when these things were being done—that is, you who wished to imitate, as though innocent, those who then kept silent: *How long do you judge iniquity, and accept the persons of sinners?* Is even now the heir himself, coming, to be killed? Did he not for your sake wish to be without a father, as it were an orphan? did he not for your sake hunger and thirst as one in need? did he not cry out to you: *Learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart*[^19]? Was he not made poor, when he was rich, that by his poverty you might be enriched[^20]? *Judge,* then, *for the orphan and the needy; justify the humble and the poor.* Not those proud and rich for their own sake, but this one humble and poor for your sake, believe to be just, preach to be just.

**4.** [v. 4.] But they will envy him, nor will they spare at all, saying: *This is the heir, come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*[^21]. *Take away,* then, *the helpless one, and rescue the poor from the hand of the sinner.* These things were said, that it might be known that in that people in which Christ was born and killed, even those were not free from so great a crime who, although they were of so great a multitude that, as the Gospel says, the Jews feared them, and therefore did not dare to lay hands on Christ[^22], afterward connived, and permitted him to be slain by the malignant and envious princes of the Jews; who, if they had willed, would always be feared, so that never against him would the hands of the wicked prevail. For of these too it is said elsewhere: *The dumb dogs did not know how to bark*[^23]. Of these also that: *Behold how the righteous one has perished, and no one considers*[^24]. He has perished, as far as is in those who wished to destroy him: for how could he perish by dying, who in that very way rather sought back what had perished? Further, if these are justly rebuked, and deservedly accused, who by dissembling allowed so great a crime to be done; how are those to be rebuked, or rather not even rebuked, but with what severity to be condemned, who did this by counsel and malice?

**5.** [v. 5.] But to all, what follows fits most rightly: *They did not know, and did not understand, they walk in darkness.* Because both those, if they had known, would never have crucified the Lord of glory[^25]; and those, if they had known, would never have consented to ask that Barabbas be released, and Christ crucified. But since the above-mentioned *blindness in part has happened to Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles enter in*[^26]; through this blindness of that people, Christ being crucified, *all the foundations of the earth shall be moved.* So they were moved, and shall be moved, until there enter in the predestined fullness of the Gentiles. For even in the very death of the Lord the earth was moved, and the rocks were rent[^27]. And if we understand the foundations of the earth as those happy with an abundance of earthly goods: it was rightly foretold that they would be moved, either by marveling that humility, poverty, death—as it were a great misery of Christ according to them—are so loved, so worshiped; or themselves too, despising the vain felicity of this world, by loving and following that one. So all the foundations of the earth are moved, when partly they marvel, partly they are even changed. For just as we not absurdly speak of the foundations of heaven, upon which the kingdom of heaven is built up in the saints and faithful, whom Scripture calls living stones[^28]: whose foundation is firstly Christ himself from the Virgin; of whom the Apostle says: *Other foundation no one can lay besides that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^29]: then the Apostles themselves and Prophets, by whose authority the heavenly place is chosen, that by following it we may be built together; whence he says to the Ephesians: *Now you are no longer strangers and sojourners; but you are fellow citizens of the saints and members of the household of God, built up upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone, in whom the whole building, fitly framed together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord*[^30]: so not unfittingly are the foundations of the earth understood, by envying whose surpassing and powerful earthly felicity men are drawn by their authority to coveting goods of this kind, and by attaining them are built together as earth upon earth, just as in that supernal building heaven upon heaven; because both to the sinner it was said: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^31]; and: *The heavens declare the glory of God,* when *into all the earth their sound went out, and to the ends of the world their words*[^32].

**6.** [vv. 6-7.] But the kingdom of earthly felicity is pride, against which the humility of Christ comes, reproaching those whom he wishes from humility to make sons of the Most High, and rebuking: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High. But you shall die like men, and fall like one of the princes.* Whether he said to those: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High,* namely to those who are predestined to eternal life; but to others: *But you shall die like men, and fall like one of the princes,* in this way too distinguishing gods: or he rebukes all together, that he may distinguish the obedient and corrected: *I,* he says, *said: You are gods and all sons of the Most High;* that is, to all of you I promised heavenly felicity; *but you,* through the weakness of the flesh, *shall die like men, and,* through the elation of the soul, *like one of the princes,* that is, the devil, shall not be exalted, but *fall.* As if he said: Since the days of your life are so few, that you die quickly like men, it does not profit you unto correction; but like the devil, whose days in this world are many, because he does not die in the flesh, you are exalted, that you may fall. For through diabolical pride it came about that the perverse and blind princes of the Jews envied the glory of Christ: through this vice it came about and comes about that the humility of Christ crucified even unto death becomes cheap to those who love the excellence of this world.

**7.** [v. 8.] Accordingly, that this vice may be healed, it is said in the person of the prophet himself: *Arise, O God, judge the earth.* For the earth swelled, when it crucified you; arise from the dead, and judge the earth. *For you shall destroy among all the nations*: what, except the earth? that is, those who savor earthly things; either consuming the affection of earthly cupidity and elation in believers; or, distinguishing those not believing, the earth to be worn down and destroyed. So through his members, whose conversation is in heaven, he judges the earth, and destroys among all the nations. Nor is it to be passed over, that some codices have: *For you shall inherit among all the nations*: because this too is not unfittingly taken, nor is anything repugnant that both be together. His inheritance, then, comes about through the love, which, mercifully cultivating by his precepts and grace, destroys earthly cupidity.
