# Exposition of Psalm 43

**1.** This psalm is entitled "to the sons of Korah," as its title shows. Now Korah is interpreted "Baldness" or "Calvary," and we find in the Gospel that the Lord Jesus Christ was crucified in the place of Calvary. Therefore it is clear that this psalm is sung to the sons of his passion. And we have on this point a most evident and most firm testimony of the apostle Paul: for at the time when the Church labored in the persecutions of the Gentiles, he took from here a verse, which he might interpose for the exhortation and consolation of patience. For here it is said what he set in his Epistle: *For your sake we are put to death all the day, we are reckoned as sheep for the slaughter*[^1]. Let us hear, then, the voice of the martyrs in the psalm: and see what a good cause the voice of the martyrs has, when it says: *For your sake.* For the Lord too added, on account of justice, when he said: *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of justice*[^2]: lest anyone suffering persecution should seek glory from the punishment itself, not having a good cause. And hence, exhorting his own, he said: Blessed shall you be when men have done or said this and that to you, for my sake. Hence, then, the voice: *For your sake we are put to death all the day.*

**2.** [v. 1] Now it is a counsel of God of great consideration and great depth, what was the cause that, when he had led our fathers the patriarchs, and that whole people of Israel, out of Egypt with a most strong hand, and had drowned their pursuing enemies in the sea, and had led them through the gainsaying nations, and, the enemies being warred down, had set them in the land of promise, and had wrought great victories from the fewness of his own amid the great multitude of enemies: afterward it pleased him as it were to turn himself away from his people, so that his saints suffered a slaughter of killing and of death, and no one resisted, no one defended, no one prohibited; as though he turned his face from their groanings, as though he had forgotten them, as though he himself were not the God who, with a mighty hand and an exalted arm, by most evident power set our fathers, that is, that people, as I said, freed from Egypt, the nations being conquered and driven from their land, in the kingdom, all marveling that often the many were conquered by the few. This, then, in the groaning of confession begins to be sung in this psalm. For these things were not done in vain, except that it might be understood why they were done. Accordingly, because they were done, is manifest: why they were done, must be more deeply sought. Therefore the title does not have only, *to the sons of Korah;* but: *For understanding, to the sons of Korah.* This is also in that psalm whose first verse the Lord himself said from the cross: *My God, my God, look upon me, why have you forsaken me?* For transfiguring us into what he said, and into his body (because we too are his body, he our head), he said the voice from the cross not as his own, but as ours. For God never forsook him, nor did he ever withdraw from the Father; but on account of us he said this: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* For there follows: *Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions.* And he showed from whose person he said this; for in him no transgression could be found. *I will cry to you,* he says in that psalm, *by day, and you will not hear; and by night,* it is of course understood, and you will not hear; but he added: *and not unto folly for me*[^3], that is, this very thing that you will not hear, is not unto folly for me, but unto understanding. What is, unto understanding you will not hear? that is, you will not hear me unto temporal things, that I may understand that eternal things are to be desired from you. God, then, does not forsake, and when he seems to forsake, he takes away what you evilly desired, and teaches what you ought rightly to desire. For if God always favored us in these prosperities, so that all things abounded to us, and we suffered no tribulation in this time of our mortality, no oppressions or straits; we would say only that these are the highest goods which God grants his servants, and we would desire no greater from him. But for this reason he mingles with this evilly-sweet life the bitternesses of tribulations, that another life, which is wholesomely sweet, may be sought; this is: *For understanding, to the sons of Korah.* Finally, let us hear the psalm, and there rather let us see this.

**3.** [vv. 2-3] *O God, with our ears we have heard; our fathers have announced to us, the work which you wrought in their days, and in the days of old*[^4]. Marveling why in these days he had, as it were, deserted those whom he willed to exercise in sufferings, they recall the past things which they heard from their fathers, as though saying: Our fathers did not relate to us the things which we suffer. For in that other psalm too he said this: *In you our fathers hoped, they hoped, and you delivered them: but I am a worm, and not a man, the reproach of men, and the outcast of the people*[^5]. They hoped, and you freed them: but did I hope, and you forsook me; and in vain did I believe in you, and in vain is my name written with you, and your name written in me? These things, then, our fathers told us. *Your hand destroyed the nations, and you planted them; you weakened the peoples, and you cast them out*: that is, you cast out the peoples from their land, that you might bring these in and plant them, and confirm their kingdom by your mercy. These things we have heard from our fathers.

**4.** [v. 4] But perhaps they could do these things for this reason, because they were strong, because warriors, because unconquered, because trained, because warlike? Far be it. Our fathers did not tell us this, Scripture does not have this; but what does it have, except what follows? *For not in their sword did they possess the land by inheritance, and their arm did not save them; but your right hand, and your arm, and the illumination of your countenance. Your right hand,* your power; *your arm,* your Son himself. *And the illumination of your countenance*: what is this? Because you were present to them with such signs, that you were understood to be present. For when God is present to us by some miracle, do we see his face itself with our eyes? But by the effect of the miracle he insinuates his presence to men. Finally all who marvel at deeds of this kind, what do they say? I saw God present. *But your right hand, and your arm, and the illumination of your countenance: because you were well pleased in them:* this is, you so dealt with them, that you were well pleased in them, so that whoever attended how it was dealt with them, would say that truly God is with them, and God acts them.

**5.** [v. 5] What then? Was there one then, and another now? Far be it. For what follows? *You are he, my king and my God.* You are he, for you have not been changed. I see the times changed, the creator of the times is not changed. *You are he, my king and my God.* You are wont to lead me, you are wont to rule me, you are wont to come to my aid. *Who command salvation for Jacob.* What is, *Who command*? Even if you, through your very substance and nature by which you are whatever you are, are hidden, nor through what you are were you present to the fathers, so that they saw you face to face; yet through any creature you command salvation for Jacob. For that vision face to face is reserved for those freed in the resurrection. And those fathers, even of the New Testament, although they saw your revealed mysteries, although they announced revealed secrets; yet they said that they see in a mirror and in an enigma: but that the vision face to face is reserved for the future, when there shall come what the Apostle himself says: *For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God; but when Christ shall appear, your life, then you also shall appear with him in glory*[^6]. Then, therefore, that vision face to face is reserved for us, of which John too says: *Beloved, we are children of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be; we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^7]. Even if, then, at that time our fathers did not see you face to face according to what you are, even if that vision is reserved in the resurrection, yet even if angels were present, you *command salvation for Jacob.* Not only are you present through yourself, but through whatever creature of yours you may be present, you command this for the salvation of your servants which you yourself through yourself do; but this is done for the salvation of your servants which those do to whom you command. Since, then, you yourself are my king and my God, and you command salvation for Jacob, why do we now suffer these things?

**6.** [v. 6] But perhaps only past things have been related to us, but about the future nothing such is to be hoped. Rather indeed it is to be hoped. *In you we will winnow our enemies*[^8]. Therefore our fathers have shown us the work which you wrought in their days, and in the days of old: because your hand destroyed the nations, you cast out the peoples and planted them. These are past things: but about the future what will be? *In you we will winnow our enemies*: there will come a time when all the enemies of Christians, like chaff are winnowed, like dust are winnowed, and cast off the earth. Therefore, if both the past things have been so related to us, and such future things foretold; in the midst of present things why do we labor, except *for understanding, to the sons of Korah? In you we will winnow our enemies: and in your name we will despise those who rise up against us.* This about the future.

**7.** [v. 7] *For I will not hope in my bow*[^9]: as neither did the fathers in their sword. *And my sword will not save me.*

**8.** [v. 8] *For you have saved us from those who afflict us*[^10]. And this too is said in a figure of the past for the future: but for this reason it is said as past, because it is as certain as if it were done. Attend to why the prophets for the most part say things as though they were past, when future things are foretold, not done. For of the Lord himself the future passion was foretold, and yet: *They dug,* he says, *my hands and my feet, they numbered all my bones*: he did not say: They will dig, and will number. *But they themselves considered and gazed upon me*: he did not say: They will consider and will gaze. *They divided my garments among them*[^11]: he did not say, they will divide. All these are said as though past, when they are future; because to God even future things are as certain as if they were past. For to us the things that are past are certain; the things that are future are uncertain. For we know that something has happened, and it cannot come about that what has happened has not happened. Give a prophet to whom the future is as certain as the past is to you: and as for you that which you remember to have been done, it cannot come about that it was not done, so for him that which he knows is future, it cannot come about that it not be done. Therefore from security the things which are still future are said as past. This, then, we hope. *For you have saved us from those who afflict us, and those who hate us you have confounded.*

**9.** [v. 9] *In God we shall be praised all the day*[^12]. See how he mingles also words of future time, that you may understand that the past things which were said too were foretold about the future. *In God we shall be praised all the day: and in your name we will confess forever.* What: *we shall be praised*? What: *we will confess*? Because from all who afflict us you have delivered us, because you will give us a perpetual kingdom, because there shall be fulfilled in us: *Blessed are they who dwell in your house, O Lord, forever and ever they shall praise you*[^13].

**10.** [v. 10] These future things, then, being certain to us, and those past things, since we have heard them from our fathers, what now? *But now you have cast us off and confounded us*[^14]. You have confounded us, not in our conscience, but in the face of men. For there was a time when Christians were afflicted, when everywhere they fled, when everywhere it was said, He is a Christian, as though it pertained to insult and to reproach. Where, then, is that God of ours, our king, who commands salvation for Jacob? Where is he who did all the things which our fathers related to us? Where is he who is to do all the things which he revealed to us through his Spirit? Has he been changed? But these things happen *for understanding, to the sons of Korah.* For we ought to understand something, why he willed us to suffer all these things in the meantime. All which? *But now you have cast us off and confounded us: and you will not go forth, O God, in our powers.* We advance against our enemies, and you do not advance with us: we see them, they prevail, and we are weak. Where is that power of yours? where is your right hand and your might? where is the sea dried up? where the pursuing Egyptians overwhelmed by the waves? where Amalek resisting, overcome by the sign of the cross? *And you will not go forth, O God, in our powers.*

**11.** [v. 11] *But you have turned us back behind, before our enemies*[^15]: so that, as it were, they are in front, we behind; they accounted victors, we conquered. *And those who hate us plundered for themselves*: what, but us?

**12.** [v. 12] *You have given us like sheep for food, and among the nations you have scattered us*[^16]: we have been eaten by the nations. By these are signified those who so suffered, that they were turned into the body of the Gentiles. For the Church bewails them, as her members devoured.

**13.** [v. 13] *You have sold your people without a price*[^17]. For we saw whom you gave, we did not see what you received. *And there was no multitude in their jubilations.* For when Christians fled from their pursuing idolatrous enemies, were there congregations and jubilations to God? Were hymns sung together from the churches of God, which are wont to be sung together in peace, and to resound to the ears of God with the sweet harmony of the brotherhood? *And there was no multitude in their jubilations.*

**14.** [vv. 14-15] *You have set us as a reproach to our neighbors, a mocking and a derision to those who are round about us. You have set us as a likeness among the nations*[^18]. What is, *as a likeness*? When men cursing give a likeness of the one they detest: So may you die, so may you be punished. How many such things were then said? So may you be crucified. And today there are not lacking enemies of Christ, those very Jews, against whom, when we defend Christ, they say to us: So may you die as he did. For they would not have inflicted that death, unless they had vehemently shuddered to die in that manner; or what mystery it would have, if they had been able to understand. When the blind man is anointed, he does not see the eye-salve in the hand of the physician. For the cross itself was made even for those persecutors. From it they were afterward healed, and believed in him whom they themselves killed. *You have set us as a likeness among the nations, a shaking of the head among the peoples*: a shaking of the head for insulting. *And they spoke with their lips, and shook the head*[^19]. This to the Lord, this also to all his saints, whom they could persecute, seize, mock, betray, afflict, kill.

**15.** [vv. 16-17] *All the day my shame is against me, and the confusion of my face has covered me: from the voice of one reproaching and speaking against me*[^20]: that is, from the voice of those insulting and casting a crime at me, because I worship you, because I confess you: and casting a crime at me about that name, from which all my crimes will be blotted out. *From the voice of one reproaching and speaking against me,* that is, speaking against me. *From the face of the enemy and persecutor.* And what is this understanding? The past things which were said, will not happen in us: the future things which are hoped for, do not appear. The past things: in your great glory the people was led out of Egypt, freed from the pursuers, led through the nations, the nations being cast out, set in the kingdom. What future things? The people is to be led out of this Egypt of the world, Christ being the leader, appearing in his glory; the saints to be set at the right, the iniquitous at the left, the iniquitous to be damned with the devil in eternal punishment; the kingdom to be received from Christ with the saints forever. These are the future things, those the past. In the midst what? Tribulations. Why? That it may appear how much the soul worshiping God worships God; that it may be seen whether it worships him freely from whom it freely received salvation. For if God says to you: What did you give me that I should make you? Surely, if, being made, you earned me, you had not earned me before I made you. What shall we say to him, who first freely made us, because he is good, not because we merited anything? Then, about that very repair, about the second birth, what shall we say? That our merits brought it about that that perpetual salvation should be sent us by the Lord? Far be it. If our merits did anything, it would come to our damnation. He did not come to the inspection of merits, but to the remission of sins. You were not, and you were made: what did you give to God? You were evil, and you were freed: what did you give to God? What did you not freely receive from him? Deservedly it is called grace too, because it is given freely. It is required of you, then, that you too worship him freely: not because he gives temporal things, but because he grants eternal things.

**16.** But see lest you think of those eternal things otherwise, and by thinking of eternal things carnally, you nonetheless do not worship God freely. For what? If you worship God for this reason, that he gives you a farm, will you not worship him, because he takes from you the farm? But perhaps you say: I will worship him for this reason, that he will give me an estate, yet not a temporal one. Nonetheless you still bear a corrupt mind: for you do not worship with chaste love, you still demand a reward. For you wish to have in the future world those things which here it is necessary that you leave behind: you wish to change carnal pleasure, not to amputate it. Fasting is not praised in him who keeps his belly for a luxurious supper. For sometimes men are invited to a great supper, and when they wish to come to it avid, they fast: is this fasting to be reckoned to continence, and not rather to luxury? Do not, then, hope that such things are to be given you by God, as he here bids you despise. For the Jews hoped these things, therefore they were troubled in that question. For they too hope for the resurrection, but they hope to rise again to such pleasures of the body as they here love. Therefore when that question was set before them by the Sadducees, who do not believe the resurrection, about that woman who married seven brothers succeeding one another, whose wife she would be in the resurrection, they failed, and could not answer. But when it was set before the Lord, because such a resurrection is promised us, not one in which such pleasures are sought again, but one in which eternal joys about God himself are obtained, the Lord answered, and said: *You err, not knowing the Scriptures, nor the power of God: for in the resurrection they will neither marry, nor take wives: for they will not begin to die.* That is, there a successor is not sought, where there will be no one departing. And what will it be? *But they will be,* he says, *equal to the angels of God*[^21]. Unless perhaps you think that the angels rejoice in daily feasts, and in the wine by which you make yourself drunk, or perhaps suppose that the angels have wives. None of these things is among the angels. Whence do the angels rejoice, except whence the Lord says: *Do you not know that their angels always see the face of the Father?*[^22] If, then, the angels rejoice at the face of the Father, prepare yourself for such joy: or do you find anything better than to see the face of God? Woe to such a love of yours, if you even suspect anything more beautiful than he is from whom is all that is beautiful, which might hold you so that you do not deserve to think of him. The Lord was in the flesh, and appeared a man to men. How did he appear? I have already said, a man to men. What great thing did he appear? Flesh to flesh. What great thing did he appear, of whom it was said: *We saw him, and he had no form nor comeliness*[^23]? Who had no form nor comeliness? He of whom likewise it is said: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^24]. As man, he had no form nor comeliness; but beautiful in form from this, that he is beyond the sons of men. Therefore showing that deformed form of the flesh to the eyes of those beholding, what does he say? *He who loves me, keeps my commandments; and he who loves me, will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will show him myself*[^25]. Him whom they saw, he promised to show them. But what is this? As if he said: You see the form of a servant, the form of God is hidden: by this I coax you, that I reserve for you; with this I nourish you, little ones, with that I feed the grown. Therefore, that this faith of ours by which we are cleansed may be prepared for invisible things, this is, that *for understanding, to the sons of Korah* all these things were done, that there should be taken from the saints the things they had, and that the temporal life itself should be taken from them: that not for the sake of these very temporal things they should worship the eternal one himself, but by chaste love of him should endure all these things which they suffered for a time.

**17.** [vv. 18-19] In short, because the sons of Korah understood this, what do they say? *All these things have come upon us, and we have not forgotten you*[^26]. What is, *and we have not forgotten you*? *And we have not acted iniquitously in your testament: and our heart has not withdrawn behind: and you have turned aside our paths from your way*[^27]. Behold, here is the understanding, that our heart has not withdrawn behind, that we have not forgotten you, that we have not acted iniquitously in your testament, set in great tribulations and persecutions of the nations. *You have turned aside our paths from your way.* For our paths were in the pleasures of the world; our paths were in the prosperities of temporal things: you took away our paths from your way, and showed us how strait and narrow is the way that leads to life. *And you have turned aside our paths from your way.* What is, *you have turned aside our paths from your way*? As though he said to us: You are set in tribulation, you suffer many things, many things which you loved in this world you have lost: but I have not dismissed you from the way, which I teach you, narrow as it is. You were seeking broad paths: what do I say to you? By this one is the going to eternal life: by the one you wish to walk, you go to death. *How broad and spacious is the way that leads to destruction, and many are those who walk by it! How strait and narrow is the way that leads to life, and few are those who walk by it!*[^28] Who are the few? Those who endure tribulations, who endure temptations, who in all these troubles do not fail; who do not rejoice at the word for an hour, and in the time of tribulation, as though the sun being risen, wither away, but have the root of love, as we heard what was just now read in the Gospel. The root, I say, of love have, that when the sun has risen, it may not burn you, but nourish you. *All these things have come upon us, and we have not forgotten you; and we have not acted iniquitously in your testament; and our heart has not withdrawn behind.* But because we do all these things amid tribulations, now walking in the narrow way, *you have turned aside our paths from your way.*

**18.** [v. 20] *For you have humbled us in the place of affliction*[^29]. Therefore you will exalt us in the place of strength. *And the shadow of death has covered us.* For this mortality is the shadow of death. True death is damnation with the devil.

**19.** [v. 21] *If we have forgotten the name of our God*[^30]. This is the understanding of the sons of Korah. *And if we have stretched out our hands to a strange god.*

**20.** [v. 22] *Will not God seek out these things? For he knows the secrets of the heart*[^31]. He knows, and seeks out: if he knows the secrets of the heart, what does he do there? *Will not God seek out these things?* He knows in himself, he seeks out on account of us. For for this reason God sometimes seeks out, and says that it becomes known to him, what he makes become known to you. He tells you his work, not his knowledge. We say for the most part: A glad day, when it is serene: does the day itself rejoice? But we call it glad, because it makes us glad. And we say: A sad heaven. For there is no such sense in the clouds: but because men seeing the face of heaven of this kind are saddened, it is called sad, because it makes them sad. So God too is said to know, when he makes us know. God says to Abraham: *Now I have known that you fear God*[^32]. Did he not know before? But Abraham himself did not know himself: because in that very temptation he became known to himself. For for the most part a man thinks he can do what he cannot, or thinks he cannot do what he can; a questioning comes to him from the divine dispensation, and through the questioning he is made known to himself; and God is said to have known, what he made him know. Did Peter know himself, when he said to the physician: *I am with you even unto death*[^33]? The physician knew, the vein being inspected, what was being transacted within in the sick man; the sick man did not know. The access of temptation came; and the physician proved his judgment, the sick man lost his presumption. So, then, God both knows, and seeks out. He knows. How does he himself seek out? On account of you, that you may find yourself, and give thanks to him who made you. *Will not God seek out these things?*

**21.** *For he knows the secrets of the heart*[^34]. What is, *he knows the secrets*? what secrets? *Because for your sake we are put to death all the day, we are reckoned as sheep for the slaughter*[^35]. For you can see a man put to death; why he is put to death, you do not know: God knows this; the matter is in secret. But someone says to me: Behold, he is held for the name of Christ, he confesses the name of Christ. Why do not heretics too confess the name of Christ, and yet do not die for his sake? Plainly in the Catholic Church itself, I say, do you think there have been lacking, or can be lacking, those who would suffer for the sake of human glory? If such men were lacking, the Apostle would not say: *If I deliver my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^36]. He knew, then, that there could be certain ones who would do this from boasting, not from love. Therefore it is obscure; God alone sees this, we cannot. He alone can judge, who knows the secrets of the heart. *Because for your sake we are put to death all the day, we are reckoned as sheep for the slaughter.* I have already said, that hence too the apostle Paul placed a testimony for exhorting the martyrs, lest they fail in the tribulations undertaken for the name of Christ.

**22.** [v. 23] *Rise up, why do you sleep, O Lord?*[^37] To whom does he say it? and who says it? Will not rather he be said to sleep and snore, who speaks such things, *Rise up, why do you sleep, O Lord?* He answers you: I know what I say: I know that he who guards Israel does not sleep[^38]: but yet the martyrs cry, *Rise up, why do you sleep, O Lord?* O Lord Jesus! you were killed, you slept in the passion, you have now risen again for us. For we know that you have risen again for us. Why have you risen? The nations which persecute us think you dead, do not believe that you have risen. Rise up, then, for them too. Why do you sleep, not to us, but to them? For if they now believed that you had risen, could they persecute those who believe in you? But why do they persecute? Blot out, kill I know not whom, who have believed in you, I know not what man, evilly dead? Still you sleep to them: rise up, that they may understand that you have risen, and may rest. Finally it came about, while the martyrs die, and say these things, they sleep, and rouse Christ, truly dead, by their fallings asleep; Christ rose again in a certain way among the nations, that is, was believed to have risen: so little by little they too, by believing converted to Christ, made a great number, which the persecutors would fear, and the persecutions ceased. Whence? Because Christ rose again among the nations, who slept to them while they did not yet believe. *Rise up, and do not cast us off unto the end.*

**23.** [v. 24] *Why do you turn away your face?*[^39] as though you were not present, as though you had forgotten us. *You forget our want, and our tribulation.*

**24.** [v. 25] *For our soul has been humbled in the dust*[^40]. Where has it been humbled? In the dust, that is, the dust persecutes us. Those persecute us, of whom you said: *Not so the ungodly, not so; but like the dust which the wind drives from the face of the earth*[^41]. *Our soul has been humbled in the dust; our belly has clung to the earth.* He seems to me to have expressed the punishment of excessive humbling, by which, when anyone prostrates himself, his belly clings to the earth. For whoever is so humbled as to bend the knees, still has whither he may be humbled: but whoever is so humbled that his belly clings to the earth, has no further whither he may be humbled. For if he should wish further, it will no longer be to humble, but to overwhelm. Therefore perhaps he means this: We have been too much humbled in this dust, we have no further whither we may be humbled: now the humbling has come even to the utmost, let mercy too come.

**25.** Or perhaps, brothers, the Church bewails by this voice those whom those who persecuted persuaded to ungodliness: so that those who endured say this: *Our soul has been humbled in the dust*? That is, amid the hands of this dust, amid the hands of the ungodly and persecutors: *Our soul has been humbled in the dust,* to this end, that we might call upon you, that you might give aid from tribulation: but *our belly has clung to the earth,* that is, our belly has consented to the ungodliness of this dust; for this is said by *It has clung.* For if, when you love and burn with love, you rightly say to God: *My soul has clung after you*[^42]; and: *For me to cling to God is good*[^43]: but you then cling to God, when you consent to God: not without cause is it said, about that belly which clung to the earth, except that those are signified who, not enduring persecution, consented to the iniquitous: for so they clung to the earth. But why are they called the belly, except because they are carnal: so that the mouth of the Church is in the saints, is in the spiritual; the belly of the Church is in the carnal? And so the mouth of the Church is prominent; the belly is hidden, as softer and weaker. This Scripture signifies in a certain place, where someone says he received a book; *and the book itself was sweet,* he says, *in my mouth, and bitter in my belly*[^44]. What is this, except because the highest precepts which the spiritual grasp, the carnal do not grasp; and the things over which the spiritual rejoice, the carnal are saddened? This book, brothers, what does it have? *Sell all that you have, and give to the poor.* How sweet in the mouth of the Church! It is done by all the spiritual. But say to any carnal one: Do this; he more easily withdraws from you sad, like that rich man from the Lord, than he does what was said[^45]. But why did he withdraw sad, except because that book is sweet in the mouth, and bitter in the belly? You gave some gold and silver; it came to this point, that unless you lose, you perhaps commit some sin, perhaps inflict an injury on the Church, are compelled to blaspheme: and so, set in straits, either of the loss of money, or of the loss of justice, it is said to you: Rather lose the money, lest you lose justice: but you, to whom justice is not sweet in the mouth, but you are still weak in those members which the Church reckons in the belly, saddened, choose sometimes to lose something of justice, rather than even a coin of money; and you strike yourself with a heavier loss, filling your purse, and emptying your heart. Perhaps, then, of these he said: *Our belly has clung to the earth.*

**26.** [v. 26] *Rise up, O Lord, help us*[^46]. And truly, dearly beloved, he rose up and helped. For when he rose up, that is, when he rose again, and became known to the nations, the persecutions ceasing, even those who had clung to the earth were drawn out of the earth, and, doing penance, were restored to the body of Christ, although weak, although imperfect, that there might be fulfilled in them: *Your eyes saw my imperfect thing, and in your book all shall be written*[^47]. *Rise up, O Lord, help us: and redeem us for your name's sake*: this is, freely; for your name's sake, not for my merit; because you deigned to do it, not because I am worthy that you should do it for me. For even this very thing, that we have not forgotten you, and that our heart has not withdrawn behind, that we have not stretched out our hands to a strange god, except by your help, whence could we? unless by your addressing and exhorting within, not deserting, whence should we be able? Therefore, whether suffering in tribulations, or rejoicing in prosperities, redeem us; not for our merit, but for your name's sake.
