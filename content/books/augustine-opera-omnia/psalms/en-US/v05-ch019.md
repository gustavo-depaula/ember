# Exposition of Psalm 113

**1.** [vv. 1-6.] We have read indeed, and most well-known we retain, most beloved brothers, what is narrated in the book of Exodus, that the people of Israel, freed from the unjust dominion of the Egyptians, passed through dry ground between the divided waves of the sea: that the river Jordan too, when they entered through it into the land of promise, touched by the feet of the priests carrying the ark of the Lord, stood above with its flow bridled; but from the lower part flowed away what was running into the sea, until, the priests standing on dry ground, the whole people passed over: we know these things, nor yet ought we to suppose that in this psalm, to which, the Alleluia being pronounced and sung, we now responded, the Holy Spirit does this, that, recalling those past deeds, we should by no means think of such things to come. *For those things,* as the Apostle says, *happened to them in figure; and were written for our correction, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^1]. When, then, we hear in the Psalm: *At the going out of Israel from Egypt, of the house of Jacob from a barbarous people, Judea was made his sanctification, Israel his power; the sea saw and fled, the Jordan was turned back*: do not suppose that past things are narrated to us, but rather that future things are foretold; because those miracles too, when they were done among that people, were done present indeed, but not without the signification of things to come. And therefore, that he who foretold these things by singing might show that he was doing by words the same things which there were done by deeds, one and the same Spirit working both those deeds and these words, so that what was reserved to be manifested at the end of the ages might be announced by figures of things and words running before; he did not say at all the things which were done there, but some things otherwise than we learned them read there: lest he be supposed truly to recall things transacted, rather than to foretell things to come. For first we read that the Jordan itself was not turned back, but stood from that part where the waters flowed in from above, when that people passed over; then we do not read of the leaping mountains and hills: which he so added that he repeated all those things too. For when he had said, *The sea saw and fled, the Jordan was turned back;* he wove on: *The mountains leaped like rams, and the hills like lambs of the flock*: and again he asks the same: *What is it to you, O sea, that you fled; and you, O Jordan, that you were turned back? mountains, that you leaped like rams; and hills, like lambs of the flock?*

**2.** Let us attend, then, to what we are admonished of; because both those deeds were our figures, and these words exhort us to recognize ourselves. For if we hold with a firm heart the grace of God which has been given us, we are Israel the seed of Abraham: to us the Apostle says: *Therefore you are the seed of Abraham*[^2]. For as he says in another place: *Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision, was the faith of Abraham reckoned to him for righteousness, and he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which is in uncircumcision, that it might be reckoned to them too for righteousness; that he might be the father of circumcision to those who are not only of circumcision, but also to those who follow the footsteps of the faith which is in the uncircumcision of our father Abraham*[^3]. For he was made the father not only of the nation carnally circumcised, to whom it was said: *I have set you as the father of many nations.* But *many,* not some, but all; which is openly said, when it is said to him: *And in you all the nations will be blessed*[^4]. Let no Christian, then, suppose himself a stranger to the name of Israel. For to those who believed of the Jews, in whose number we find chiefly the Apostles, we are joined in the cornerstone. Hence the Lord says in another place: *I have other sheep which are not of this fold; I must lead them too, that there may be one flock and one shepherd*[^5]. The Christian people, then, is more Israel, and itself rather the house of Jacob. For the same is Israel who is Jacob. But that crowd of the Jews, which was deservedly reprobated for its perfidy, sold its birthright for carnal pleasure, that it might pertain not to Jacob, but rather to Esau. For you know that it was said in this mystery: *That the elder will serve the younger*[^6].

**3.** But Egypt, since it is interpreted "Affliction," or "Afflicting," or "Pressing," is often set as an image of this world; from which one must spiritually withdraw, lest we be drawing the yoke with unbelievers. For so each one becomes a fit citizen of the heavenly Jerusalem, when first he has renounced this world; just as that people could not be led into the land of promise, unless it first withdrew from Egypt. But just as that people did not depart from there, except freed by divine help; so no one is turned in heart from this world, except helped by the gift of divine mercy. For what was there once prefigured, this in this end of the age, in this, as blessed John writes, last hour, is fulfilled in the daily offspring of the Church in each believer. Hear the Apostle, the teacher of the Gentiles, teaching and instructing: *For I do not wish you,* he says, *to be ignorant, brothers, that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed: but the rock was Christ. But God was not well pleased with all of them: for they were laid low in the desert. But these things were made our figures*[^7]. What more do you wish, most beloved brothers? Surely it is manifest, not by human supposition, but by apostolic, that is, divine and dominical teaching; for God was speaking in them, and although from carnal clouds, yet the Lord was thundering: surely, then, by so great a testimony it is manifest that all those things were done figuratively, and are now fulfilled in our salvation; because then they were foretold as future, now they are read as past, and are recognized as present.

**4.** Hear what is more wonderful, that the sacraments of the old Books, hidden and veiled, are in some part revealed by the old Books. For the prophet Micah speaks thus: *As in the days,* he says, *of their going out from Egypt, I will show them wonders. The nations will see, and be confounded at all their might: with their hands they will stop their mouths, their ears will be deafened, licking the earth, like serpents drawing the earth; they will be disturbed at their enclosures, in the Lord our God they will be out of their mind, and will be terrified by you. Who is like your God, taking away iniquity, and passing over the impiety of the remnants of your inheritance? And he did not hold his wrath as a witness, for he is voluntary and merciful; he himself will turn and have mercy on us, he will sink our offenses, he will sink in the depth of the sea all our faults*[^8]. Surely you notice, brothers, that the most sacred mysteries are here more manifestly opened. In this psalm, then, although the wonderful Spirit of prophecy gazes upon future things, yet it seems to narrate them as it were transacted. *Judea was made,* he says, *his sanctification: the sea saw and fled*: both *was made,* and *fled,* are words of past time; and *the Jordan was turned,* and *the mountains leaped,* and *the earth was moved,* in the same way sound past; yet without prejudice to understanding them as future. Otherwise, against the Gospel testimony, we are compelled to understand even those things not foretold of the future, but commemorated of the past: *They divided my garments among them, and over my garment they cast lots*[^9]. Which, although said in words of past time, yet foretold what so long afterward was to be fulfilled in the passion of the Lord. But yet, most beloved, this prophet whom I commemorated, has also filed the coarse hearts, and unhesitatingly extended them to understanding future things from past deeds; so that we believe not only by apostolic authority that those things were our figures, but that not even by the Prophets themselves was this passed over, that even their eloquence disclosing it, seeing and rejoicing, certain and secure, we may bring forth from the treasure of God both new and old things harmoniously cohering with one another. For when, so long after the going-out of that people from Egypt, and so long before these times of the Church, he sang these things which I have commemorated; yet he attests without any doubt that he foretells future things. *As in the days,* he says, *of their going out from Egypt, I will show them wonders. The nations will see, and be confounded.* This is what was said here: *The sea saw, and fled*: for if here, by words of past time, as is, *saw* and *fled,* future things are hiddenly foretold; *They will see* certainly *and be confounded,* when they are words of future time, who would dare to think of past things? And a little after, that our very enemies, who pursued us fleeing in order to destroy us, that is, our offenses, like the Egyptians overwhelmed in the sea, so in Baptism are sunk and extinguished, he intimates clearer than light, saying: *For he is voluntary and merciful, he himself will turn and have mercy on us; he will sink our offenses, he will sink in the depth of the sea all our faults.*

**5.** What is it, dearest ones? You who recognize yourselves Israelites according to the seed of Abraham, who are the house of Jacob according to the promise heirs, recognize too that you went out from Egypt, you who renounced this world; that you went out from a barbarous people, you who by the confession of piety separated yourselves from the blasphemies of the nations. For your tongue is not barbarous, which does not know how to praise God, to whom you sing Alleluia. For *Judea was made his sanctification* in you: *For he is not a Jew who is one in the open, nor is circumcision that which is in the open in the flesh; but he is a Jew who is one in secret, and circumcision of the heart*[^10]. Question, then, your hearts: if faith has circumcised them, if confession has purged them; in you *Judea was made his sanctification,* in you *Israel his power.* For he gave you power to become the sons of God.

**6.** Now let each one of you recall, when he wished to apply his heart to God, and, withdrawing from the former desires of his ignorance, to subject his devoted mind to his sweet yoke, and to the carnal deeds of this world (in which he was laboring without fruit, as if in Egypt he were making bricks under the hard dominion of the devil), having heard the voice of the Lord saying, *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you*[^11], those things deserted and cast off, to run under the light burden of Christ: let each one of you, then, recall how all worldly impediments yielded, the voices of those dissuading either did not dare to burst out, or, the name of Christ being considered, exalted and honored throughout all lands, trembled and fell silent. Therefore: *The sea saw and fled*: that without contradiction the way to spiritual liberty might be opened to you.

**7.** But how the *Jordan was turned back*, I do not want you to seek outside yourselves, I do not want you to suspect anything evil. For the Lord rebukes some who set their back to him, and not their face. And whoever deserts his beginning, and is turned away from his Creator, like a river into the sea, slips into the embittering malice of this world. It is good for him, then, that he be turned back, and that God become for him before his face, returning, whom he had set behind himself; and that the sea of this world, which he had made for himself before his face, when he was slipping there, become behind him; and so let him forget the things that are behind, that he may be extended to the things that are before: which is now useful to him, converted. For before he is converted, if he forgets the things that are behind, he forgets God; because he had made him behind, to whom he had set his back: and if he is extended to the things that are before, he is extended into the world; because he had set it before his face, into which, greedy, he was rushing. The Jordan, then, signifies those who have received the grace of Baptism; and so the Jordan is turned back, when they are converted to God, so that they no longer have him behind, but, with unveiled face beholding the glory of the Lord, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory.

**8.** *The mountains leaped like rams*: the faithful stewards of the word of truth, the holy Apostles, the holy preachers of the Gospel. *And the hills like lambs of the flock.* These are they to whom it is said: *In Christ Jesus through the Gospel I begot you*[^12]: these are they to whom it is said: *Not to confound you do I write these things, but I admonish you as my dearest sons*[^13]: these are they of whom it is said: *Bring to the Lord the sons of rams*[^14]. Attend, through all lands, you who know how to wonder at these things, and to rejoice and sing to the Lord your God; attend that these things are fulfilled through all the nations, which so long beforehand were figuratively done and foretold.

**9.** Question and say: *What is it to you, O sea, that you fled; and you, O Jordan, that you were turned back? mountains, that you leaped like rams; and hills, like lambs of the flock?* What is it, O world, that your impediments yielded? what is it, O so many thousands of the faithful throughout the whole world, renouncing this world, that you are turned to your Lord? What is it that you rejoice, to whom at the end it will be said: *Well done, good servant, because you were faithful in a few things, I will set you over many*[^15]? what is it that you rejoice, to whom at the end it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the origin of the world*[^16]?

**10.** [v. 7.] All these things will answer you, and you yourselves will answer yourselves: *From the face of the Lord the earth was moved, from the face of the God of Jacob.* What is it, *from the face of the Lord;* except his presence who said: *Behold, I am with you to the consummation of the age*[^17]? For the earth was moved; but because it had remained badly sluggish, it was moved, that it might be more solidly made firm from the face of the Lord.

**11.** [v. 8.] *Who turned the rock into pools of waters, and the cliff into fountains of waters.* For he melted himself and a certain hardness of his to water his faithful, that he might become in them a fountain of water springing up unto eternal life; because before, when he was not known, he seemed hard. Hence those were disturbed, and did not wait until, the Scriptures being opened, he flowed in and flooded over them, who said: *This is a hard saying, who can hear it?*[^18] That rock, that hardness was turned into pools of waters, and that cliff into fountains of waters, when, rising, he expounded to them, beginning from Moses through all the Prophets, that so Christ had to suffer; and sent the Holy Spirit, of whom he was saying: *If anyone thirsts, let him come and drink*[^19].

**12.** [v. 9 (1).] *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory.* For this grace of water bursting forth from the rock (but the rock was Christ), was not given as it were by preceding works, but by him having mercy who justifies the impious. For indeed Christ died for the impious, that men might seek not any glory of their own, but of the name of God.

**13.** [v. 10 (2).] *Over your mercy,* he says, *and your truth.* These two, mercy and truth, notice in the holy Scriptures how often they are joined to one another. For in his mercy he called the impious, and in truth he judges those who, called, were unwilling to come. *Lest at some time the nations say, Where is their God?* For at the last his mercy and truth will appear, when the sign of the Son of man will appear in heaven, and then all the tribes of the earth will mourn, nor will they say: *Where is their God?* when he is preached to them not as one still to be believed, but already shown as one to be feared.

**14.** [v. 11 (3).] *But our God is in heaven above.* Not in the sky, where they see the sun and moon, the works of God which they worship; but *in heaven above,* which transcends all bodies, heavenly and earthly. Nor is our God so in heaven that, the sky being withdrawn, he should dread a fall without a seat. *In the heavens and on earth all things whatsoever he willed he made.* Nor does he need his works, as if he were set in them, that he may abide; but he persists in his own eternity, in which abiding he made all things whatsoever he willed in the heavens and on earth: for they were not yet bearing him, that they might be able to be made by him; since, unless they were made, they could not bear him. Therefore in those things in which he is, he contains as it were one in need, he is not contained by them as one in need. Or it may be understood thus: *In the heavens and on earth all things whatsoever he willed he made,* either in the higher or in the lower he established his voluntary grace for his people, lest anyone glory in the merits of works: because whether the mountains leap like rams, or the hills like lambs of the flock; from the face of the Lord the earth was moved, lest they remain perpetually in earthly filth.
