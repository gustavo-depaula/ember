# Exposition of Psalm 85

**1.** No greater gift could God bestow on men, than that he should make his Word, through which he created all things, their head, and fit them to him as members; that he might be the Son of God and the son of man, one God with the Father, one man with men: so that both, when we speak to God in prayer, we do not separate the Son from there; and when the body of the Son prays, it does not separate its head from itself; and that the one Savior of his body, our Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God, may both pray for us, and pray in us, and be prayed to by us. He prays for us, as our priest; he prays in us, as our head; he is prayed to by us, as our God. Let us therefore recognize both our voices in him, and his voices in us. And when something is said of the Lord Jesus Christ, especially in prophecy, which pertains as it were to a certain humility unworthy of God, let us not hesitate to attribute it to him who did not hesitate to join himself to us. For to him all creation is subject, because through him all creation was made. And therefore, when we behold his sublimity and divinity, when we hear: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. This was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^1]; beholding this most preeminent divinity of the Son of God, exceeding all sublimities of creatures, we hear also in some part of the Scriptures as it were one groaning, praying, confessing; and we hesitate to attribute these words to him, because our thought, from the recent contemplation of him which was in the divinity, is sluggish to descend to his humility; and as if it would do him an injury, if it recognized his words in the man, to whom he directed his words when he prayed to God, it often sticks, and tries to change the sense; and it finds nothing in Scripture, except that which recalls it to him, and does not let it stray from him. Let it therefore rouse itself, and awaken in its faith; and let it see that he whom it contemplated a little before in the form of God, took the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man, humbled himself, made obedient even unto death[^2]; and willed the words of the Psalm to be his, hanging on the cross, and saying: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me*[^3]? He is prayed to, then, in the form of God, he prays in the form of a servant: there the Creator, here the created, taking up the creature to be changed without being changed himself, and making us together with himself one man, head and body. We pray, therefore, to him, through him, in him: and we say with him, and he says with us; we say in him, he says in us the prayer of this psalm, which is entitled: *A Prayer of David.* For our Lord according to the flesh is the son of David: but according to the divinity the Lord of David, and the creator of David; and not only before David, but also before Abraham, from whom David; but also before Adam, from whom all men; but also before heaven and earth, in whom is all creation. Let no one, then, when he hears these words, say: Christ does not say it; or again say: I do not say it: rather, if he recognizes himself in the body of Christ, let him say both, both, Christ says it, and: I say it. Be unwilling to say anything without him, and he says nothing without you. Do we not have it in the Gospel? Where surely it is written: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. All things were made through him;* there surely we have: And Jesus was saddened[^4], and Jesus was wearied[^5], and Jesus slept[^6], and Jesus hungered and thirsted[^7], and Jesus prayed, and Jesus spent the night in prayer. *Jesus,* it says, *spent the night, and continued in prayer*[^8]; and clots of blood ran down through his body[^9]. What did he show, when through the body of the one praying clots of blood dropped down, except that his body, which is the Church, was already flowing with the blood of martyrs?

**2.** *Incline, O Lord, your ear, and hear me.* He says it from the form of a servant; you, servant, in the form of your Lord: *Incline, O Lord, your ear.* He inclines his ear, if you do not lift up your neck: for he draws near to the humbled; from the exalted he withdraws far, except him whom, himself humbled, he has exalted. God therefore inclines his ear to us. For he is above, we below; he in the height, we in lowliness, but not abandoned. *For God shows his love in us: for while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. For scarcely,* he says, *does anyone die for a righteous man; for perhaps for a good man someone may dare to die: but our Lord died for the impious*[^10]. For our merits had not gone before, for which the Son of God should die; but rather because there were no merits, great was the mercy. With how certain, then, with how firm a promise does he keep for the righteous his life, who gave to the unrighteous his death? *Incline, O Lord, your ear, and hear me; for I am needy and poor.* Therefore he does not incline his ear to the rich man: to the needy and poor he inclines his ear, that is, to the humble and the one confessing, to the one in need of mercy: not to the satisfied and the one exalting himself, and boasting, as though he lacked nothing, and saying: *I give you thanks, that I am not as this Publican*[^11]. For the rich Pharisee boasted of his merits: the needy Publican confessed his sins.

**3.** Nor take what I said in this way, brothers: To the rich man he does not incline his ear, as though God did not hear those who have gold and silver, and household and estates: if perhaps they were born so, or hold that place of human affairs; let them only remember what the Apostle says: *Charge the rich of this world not to be high-minded*[^12]. For those who are not high-minded are poor in God; and to the poor and needy and helpless he inclines his ear. For they know that their hope is not in gold and silver, nor in those things with which they seem to flow about for a time. It is enough that the riches do not ruin them; that they do not harm them is sufficient: for they can profit nothing. Plainly the work of mercy profits both in the rich and in the poor: in the rich from the will and the deed, in the poor from the will alone. When, then, one is such, despising in himself whatever there is by which pride is wont to be puffed up, he is the poor man of God: he inclines his ear to him, for he knows his bruised heart. Surely, brothers, that poor man covered with sores who lay before the gate of the rich man was carried by angels into the bosom of Abraham, so we read, so we believe: but that rich man who was clothed in purple and fine linen, and feasted sumptuously every day, was carried to the lower regions to torments[^13]. Was that poor man truly carried by angels by the merit of his want; but that rich man sent to torments by the sin of his riches? In that poor man humility is understood honored, in that rich man pride condemned. Briefly I prove that not riches, but pride was tortured in that rich man. Surely that poor man was carried up into the bosom of Abraham: of Abraham himself Scripture says that he had here very much gold and silver, and was rich on earth[^14]. If whoever is rich is carried off to torments; how had Abraham gone before the poor man, to receive him carried into his bosom? But Abraham was in his riches poor, humble, trembling at all the precepts, and obeying. So much did he hold those riches as nothing, that, bidden by the Lord to sacrifice his son, for whom he was keeping the riches, he did it[^15]. Learn therefore to be needy and poor; whether you who have something in this world, or you who have nothing. For you find a beggar man too being proud, and a man having riches confessing. God resists the proud, both the silk-clad and the ragged: but to the humble he gives grace[^16], both to those having some substance of this world and to those not having it. God is the inward inspector; there he weighs, there he examines: you do not see the balance of God; your thought is lifted into it. See that the merit of his being heard—that is, by which he was heard—he set in this, that he said: *For I am needy and poor.* Take care that you be not without being needy and poor: if you are not, you will not be heard. Whatever there is about you or in you whence you can presume, cast it from you; let your whole presumption be God: be needy of him, that you may be filled with him. For whatever else you have without him, you are the more emptily empty.

**4.** [v. 2.] *Guard my soul, for I am holy.* But this, *for I am holy,* I do not know whether anyone else could perhaps say, except he who was without sin in this world; not the committer, but the forgiver of all sins. We recognize the voice of him saying: *For I am holy, guard my soul*: surely in that form of a servant which he had assumed. For there is flesh, there is also soul. For it was not, as some have said, flesh alone and the Word; but both flesh, and soul, and the Word: and all this one Son of God, one Christ, one Savior; in the form of God equal to the Father, in the form of a servant the head of the Church. Therefore: *For I am holy,* when I hear, I recognize his voice; and here do I separate mine? Surely he speaks inseparably from his body, when he speaks thus. And shall I dare to say: *For I am holy?* If holy as sanctifying, and needing no one to sanctify; proud and lying: but if holy as sanctified, according to that which was said: *Be holy, because I also am holy*[^17]; let the body of Christ too dare, let that one man crying from the ends of the earth[^18], with his head, and under his head, dare to say: *For I am holy.* For he received the grace of holiness, the grace of baptism and of the remission of sins. *And these things indeed you were,* says the Apostle, enumerating many sins, both light and grave, and usual and horrible: *And these things indeed you were; but you have been washed, but you have been sanctified*[^19]. If, then, he says they are sanctified, let each of the faithful too say: *I am holy.* This is not the pride of one puffed up, but the confession of one not ungrateful. For if you say that you are holy of yourself, you are proud: but again, a believer in Christ, and a member of Christ, if you say that you are not holy, you are ungrateful. For the Apostle, reproving pride, does not say: You have not; but says: *For what have you that you have not received*[^20]? You were not reproved because you said that you had what you do not have; but because you wished that what you have should be from yourself. Rather, both acknowledge that you have, and that you have nothing of yourself, that you be neither proud nor ungrateful. Say to your God: I am holy, because you have sanctified me; because I received, not because I had it; because you gave, not because I deserved. For from the other side you begin to do an injury to our Lord Jesus Christ himself. For if all Christians and faithful and baptized have put him on in him, as the Apostle says: *As many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ*[^21]; if they have been made members of his body, and say that they are not holy, they do an injury to the head himself, whose members are not holy. Now see where you are, and from your head take dignity. For you were in darkness, *but now you are light in the Lord. You were,* he says, *once darkness*[^22]; but did you remain darkness? Did the illuminator come to this end, that you should remain darkness, or that you should become light in him? Therefore let each Christian say, rather let the whole body of Christ say, let it cry out everywhere, suffering tribulations, diverse temptations and innumerable scandals; let it say: *Guard my soul, for I am holy: save your servant, O my God, hoping in you.* Behold, that holy one is not proud, because he hopes in the Lord.

**5.** [v. 3.] *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I have cried to you all the day*: not one day. *All the day,* understand as all time: from the time the body of Christ groans in pressures, until the end of the world when the pressures pass, this man groans, and cries to God; and each one of us has in proportion his own cry in this whole body. You cried in your days, and your days passed; another succeeded you, and cried in his days; and you here, that one there, that one elsewhere: the body of Christ cries all the day, with its members departing and succeeding. One man is stretched out unto the end of the world: the same members of Christ cry, and certain members already rest in him, certain ones now cry, certain ones will cry when we ourselves have rested, and after them others will cry. Here he attends to the voice of the whole body of Christ, saying: *I have cried to you all the day.* But our head at the right hand of the Father intercedes for us[^23]: some members he receives, others he scourges, others he cleanses, others he consoles, others he creates, others he calls, others he recalls, others he corrects, others he restores.

**6.** [v. 4.] *Make glad the soul of your servant; for to you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul.* Make it glad, because I have lifted it up to you. For it was on earth, and on earth it felt bitterness: lest it waste away in bitterness, lest it lose all the sweetness of your grace, I lifted it up to you; make it glad with you. For you alone are gladness: the world is full of bitterness. Surely he rightly admonishes his members to have their heart on high. Let them hear, then, and do it; let them lift up to him what is ill on earth. For there the heart does not rot, if it be lifted up to God. If you had grain in lower places, lest it rot, you would lift it up to higher places. You would change the place of the grain, and do you permit the heart to rot on earth? You would lift up the grain to higher places; lift up the heart to heaven. And whence, you say, can I? What ropes, what machines, what ladders are needed? The steps are affections; your journey is your will. By loving you ascend, by neglecting you descend. Standing on earth, you are in heaven, if you love God. For the heart is not lifted up as the body is lifted up: that the body may be lifted up, it changes place; that the heart may be lifted up, it changes will. *For to you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul.*

**7.** [v. 5.] *For you, O Lord, are sweet and gentle.* Therefore make it glad. As if affected with weariness from the bitterness of earthly things, he wished to be sweetened, and sought the fountain of sweetness, and did not find it on earth. For wherever he turned himself, he found scandals, fears, tribulations, temptations. In what man is there security? of whom is there sure joy? Not of oneself surely; how much less of another? Either they are evil, and they must be borne, and one must hope that they can be changed; or they are good, and they must be so loved that we fear (because they can be changed) lest they be evil: there their malice makes bitterness for the soul; here anxiety and fear, lest he who walks well slip. Wherever, then, one turns oneself, one finds bitterness in earthly things; one has nothing whence to be sweetened, unless one lifts oneself to God. *For you, O Lord, are sweet and gentle.* What is *gentle?* Bearing me, until you perfect me. For truly, my brothers, I will speak as a man among men, and from men: let each one bear his own heart, and look at himself without flattery, and without coaxing. For nothing is more foolish than for anyone to coax and seduce himself. Let him attend, then, and see how many things go on in the human heart; how the very prayers are often impeded by vain thoughts, so that the heart scarcely stands to its God: and it wishes to hold itself that it may stand, and in a manner flees from itself, nor finds bars by which to enclose itself, or certain obstacles by which to hold back its flutterings and certain wandering motions, and to stand to be made glad by its God. Scarcely does such a prayer occur among many prayers. Each one would say that this happens to him, and not to another, unless we found in the Scriptures of God David praying in a certain place, and saying: *For I have found, O Lord, my heart, to pray to you*[^24]. He said that he found his heart, as though it were wont to flee from him, and he to follow it as a fugitive, and not to be able to catch it, and to cry to God: *For my heart has forsaken me*[^25]. And so, my brothers, attending to what he here says: *You are sweet and gentle;* I seem to myself to see here what he says, *gentle. Make glad the soul of your servant, for to you I have lifted up my soul; for you are sweet and gentle*: I seem to myself to see that he called God gentle for this reason, that he bears these things of ours, and yet awaits from us a prayer, that he may perfect us; and when we give it to him, he receives it gratefully, and hears it; nor does he remember the so many which we pour out in disorder, and he receives the one which we scarcely find. For what man is there, brothers, with whom, if his friend has begun to converse, and that one has wished to answer his conversation, and has seen him turn away from him, and speak something else to another, who bears this? Or if perhaps you entreat a judge, and set him in a place to hear you, and suddenly, when you speak to him, you leave him, and begin to chat with your friend; how long does he tolerate you? And God tolerates so many hearts of those praying, and thinking of diverse things: I forbear to mention even harmful things, I forbear to mention sometimes perverse things and hostile to God; even the superfluous things are an injury to think of, to him with whom you had begun to speak. Your prayer is a speaking to God: when you read, God speaks to you; when you pray, you speak to God. But what? is the human race to be despaired of, and is it now to be said that every man pertains to damnation upon whom some thought has crept while praying, and has interrupted his prayer? If we say this, brothers, I do not see what hope remains. But since there is some hope toward God, since his mercy is great, let us say to him: *Make glad the soul of your servant, for to you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul.* And how have I lifted it up? As I could, as you gave strength, as I was able to catch it fleeing. And it slipped from you, that, however often I stood before you (suppose God to say it), you thought of so many vain and superfluous things, and scarcely poured out to me a fixed and stable prayer: *Because you are sweet, O Lord, and gentle*: you are gentle, bearing me. From sickness I flow away; cure me, and I will stand: strengthen me, and I will be firm. But until you do it, you bear me: *Because you are sweet, O Lord, and gentle.*

**8.** *And greatly merciful.* For not only merciful, but *greatly merciful*: for our iniquity abounds, your mercy too abounds. *And greatly merciful to all who call upon you.* And what is it that Scripture says in many places, that *they will call, and I will not hear them*[^26] (surely *merciful to all who call upon you*); except that certain who call do not call upon him? of whom it is said: *They did not call upon God*[^27]. They call, but not God. You call upon whatever you love; you call upon whatever you call into yourself, you call upon whatever you wish to come to you. Further, if you call upon God for this, that money may come to you, that an inheritance may come to you, that worldly dignity may come to you; you call upon those things which you wish to come to you: but you set God as a helper of your desires, not as a hearer of your prayers. God is good, if he gives what you wish. What, if you wish ill, will he not be more merciful by not giving? Further, if he does not give, then God is now nothing to you; and you say: How much I asked, how often I asked, and I was not heard! For what did you ask? Perhaps the death of your enemy. What, if he too was asking yours? He who created you created him too; you are a man, he too is a man: but God is the judge; he hears both, and does not hear both. You are sad, because you were not heard against him; rejoice, because he was not heard against you. I, you say, did not ask this; I did not ask the death of my enemy, but I asked the life of my son: what evil did I ask? You asked nothing evil, as you felt it. For what, if he was snatched away, lest malice change his understanding[^28]? But he was a sinner, you say; and therefore I wished him to live, that he might be corrected. You wished him to live, that he might be better: what if God knew that, if he lived, he would be worse? Whence, then, do you know what would profit him, to die or to live? If, then, you do not know, return to your heart, leave to God his own counsel. What then, you say, shall I do? what shall I pray? What shall you pray? What the Lord taught you, what the heavenly master taught you. Call upon God as God, love God as God: nothing is better than he; desire him, long for him. See one calling upon God in another psalm: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek.* What is it he asks? *That I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life.* To what end this? *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*[^29]. If, then, you wish to be a lover of God, love him with the sincerest marrow and chaste sighs, love him, burn for him, gape for him, than whom you find nothing more pleasant, nothing better, nothing more joyful, nothing more lasting. For what is so lasting as that which is everlasting? You do not fear lest he ever perish from you, who acts that you not perish. If, then, you call upon God as God, be secure, you are heard; you belong to this verse: *And greatly merciful to all who call upon you.*

**9.** Do not, then, say: That he did not give me. Return to your conscience; weigh, question, do not spare it. If you have truly called upon God, be certain that perhaps what you wished in temporal terms, he did not give for this reason, because it did not profit you. Let your heart be edified in this, brothers, a Christian heart, a faithful heart; lest, having become sad, as though cheated of your desires, you begin to go into indignation against God: for it is not expedient to kick against the goad[^30]. Run back to the Scriptures. The devil is heard, and the Apostle is not heard: what does it seem to you? How are the demons heard? They asked to go into the swine, and it was granted them[^31]. How was the devil heard? He asked for Job to be tempted, and received it[^32]. How was the Apostle not heard? *In the greatness of the revelations, lest I be exalted, there was given me a thorn of my flesh, an angel of Satan to buffet me: for which I asked the Lord three times to take it from me, and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for power is perfected in weakness*[^33]. He heard him whom he was disposing to condemn, and did not hear him whom he wished to heal. For the sick man too asks many things of the physician, the physician does not give them: he does not hear him according to his will, that he may hear him according to his health. Therefore set God as your physician; ask salvation of him, and your salvation he himself will be: not as salvation from outside, but that he himself be salvation; lest again you love another salvation besides him, but as you have in the psalm: *Say to my soul: I am your salvation*[^34]. What is it to you, what he say to you, that he give himself to you? Do you wish that he give himself to you? What if what you wish to have, he himself wishes you not to have, that he may give himself to you? He removes hindrances, that he may enter to you. Think and consider, brothers, the goods which God gives to sinners; and from this understand what he keeps for his servants. To sinners blaspheming him daily he gives heaven and earth, gives fountains, fruits, health, children, abundance, plenty: all these goods none but God gives. He who gives such things to sinners, what do you suppose he keeps for his faithful? Is this to be thought of him, that he who gives such things to the evil keeps nothing for the good? Rather, he keeps it, not earth, but heaven. Perhaps I say something too cheap, when I say heaven; but himself, who made heaven. Heaven is beautiful, but more beautiful is the maker of heaven. But I see heaven, and I do not see him. For you have eyes to see heaven; you do not yet have a heart to see the maker of heaven: therefore he came from heaven to earth, to cleanse the heart, by which may be seen he who made heaven and earth. But plainly with patience await salvation. With what medicines he may cure you, he knows; with what cuttings, with what burnings, he knows. You acquired sickness for yourself by sinning: he comes not only to soothe, but also to cut and burn. Do you not see how much men suffer under the hands of physicians, with a man promising an uncertain hope? You will be healed, says the physician; you will be healed, if I cut. And a man says it, and to a man; neither is he who says it certain, nor he who hears it, because he says it to a man, who did not make man, and does not perfectly know what goes on in man: and yet at the words of a man, who knows still less what goes on in man, the man believes, submits his members, lets himself be bound, or often even unbound is cut or burned; and receives perhaps the health of a few days, now healed, ignorant when he may die; and perhaps, while he is being cured, he dies; and perhaps he cannot be cured. But to whom has God promised something, and deceived him?

**10.** [v. 6.] *Fix in your ears, O Lord, my prayer.* Great is the affection of the one praying. *Fix in your ears, O Lord, my prayer*: that is, let my prayer not go out from your ears, fix it there in your ears. How did he travail, to fix his prayer in the ears of God? Let God answer, and say to us: Do you wish me to fix your prayer in my ears? Fix in your heart my law. *Fix in your ears, O Lord, my prayer; and attend to the voice of my prayer.*

**11.** [v. 7.] *In the day of my tribulation I cried to you, for you heard me.* The cause that you would hear me, because *in the day of my tribulation I cried to you.* A little before he had said: *All the day I cried*, all the day I was troubled. Let no Christian, then, say that there is a day on which he is not troubled. We have understood the whole day as the whole time: all the day he is troubled. What then, is there tribulation even when it is well? Surely tribulation. Whence tribulation? Because as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord[^35]. However much abounds here, we are not yet in that homeland to which we hasten to return. To whom the pilgrimage is sweet, he does not love the homeland: if the homeland is sweet, the pilgrimage is bitter; if the pilgrimage is bitter, all the day is tribulation. When is there not tribulation? When in the homeland there is delight. *Delights at your right hand even unto the end. You will fill me with joy,* he says, *with your countenance*[^36], *that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*[^37]. There labor and groaning will pass away: there not prayer, but praising; there the Alleluia, the Amen there, a voice harmonious with the angels; there vision without failing, and love without weariness. As long, then, as you are not there, you see that you are not in good. But all things abound? Let all things abound, see whether you are secure that not all things perish. But I have what I did not have; money has accrued which was not there. Perhaps fear too has accrued which was not there; perhaps you were the more secure, the poorer you were. Finally, let there be abundance, let the affluence of this world overflow, let security be given that they do not perish: let God say from above, You will be in these things eternally, these things will be with you eternally, but you will not see my face. Let no one consult the flesh; consult the spirit: let your heart answer you; let hope answer, faith, love, which has begun to be in you. Would we, then, if we received security that we would always be in the affluence of worldly goods, and God said to us: You will not see my face, rejoice in those goods? Someone perhaps would choose to rejoice, and say: These things abound for me, it is well with me, I seek nothing more. He has not yet begun to be a lover of God; he has not yet begun to sigh as a pilgrim. Far be it, far be it: let all seducing things withdraw, let false blandishments withdraw; let those things withdraw which daily say to us: *Where is your God*[^38]? Let us pour out over ourselves our soul, let us confess in tears, let us groan in confessions, let us sigh in our miseries. Whatever is present to us besides our God is not sweet: we want not all the things he has given, if he does not give himself who has given all things. *Fix in your ears, O Lord, my prayer, and attend to the voice of my prayer. In the day of my tribulation I cried to you, for you heard me.*

**12.** [v. 8.] *There is none like you among the gods, O Lord.* And what did he say? *There is none like you among the gods, O Lord.* Let the pagans fashion for themselves what gods they wish; let them bring silversmiths, goldsmiths, polishers, sculptors, let them make gods. What kind of gods? Having eyes, and not seeing[^39]; and the rest which the psalm says consequently. But we do not worship these, he says; we do not worship them, these are signs. And what do you worship? Something else worse: *For the gods of the Gentiles are demons*[^40]. And what? Neither do we worship demons, they say. Plainly you have nothing else in your temples, nothing else fills your soothsayers but a demon. But what do you say? We worship angels, we have angels as gods. You plainly do not know the angels. For the angels worship one God, nor do they favor men who wish to worship the angels, and not God. For we find honored angels prohibiting men from adoring them, but the true God[^41]. But let them say angels, let them say men, because it was said: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High*[^42]: *There is none like you among the gods, O Lord.* Whatever else a man may think, what was made is not like him who made. Excepting God, whatever else is in the nature of things, was made by God. How great a difference there is between him who made and that which was made, who can worthily think? This man, then, said: *There is none like you among the gods, O Lord*: but how greatly unlike God is, he did not say, because it cannot be said. Let Your Charity attend: God is ineffable; we say more easily what he is not, than what he is. You think of earth; this is not God: you think of the sea; this is not God: all things that are on earth, men and animals; this is not God: all things that are in the sea, that fly through the air; this is not God: whatever shines in heaven, the stars, the sun and moon; this is not God: heaven itself; this is not God: think of the angels, the Virtues, Powers, Archangels, Thrones, Seats, Dominations; this is not God. And what is he? This only I could say, what he is not. Do you ask what he is? *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^43]. Why do you seek that it ascend onto the tongue, which has not ascended into the heart? *There is none like you among the gods, O Lord; and there is nothing according to your works.*

**13.** [v. 9.] *All nations, as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord.* He announced the Church: *All nations, as many as you have made.* If there is a nation which God has not made, it will not adore him: but there is no nation which God has not made; because God made Adam and Eve, the fountain of all nations, from there all nations were propagated. All nations, then, God made: *All nations,* then, *as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord.* When was it said? When none adored before him except a few holy ones in the one people of the Hebrews, then this was said; and now is seen what was said: *All nations, as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord.* When these things were said, they were not seen, and were believed; when they are seen, why are they denied? *All nations, as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord, and will glorify your name.*

**14.** [v. 10.] *For you are great, and doing wonders; you are the only great God.* Let no one call himself great. There were to be those who would call themselves great: against these it is said: *You are the only great God.* For what great thing is said to God, that he himself is the only great God? Who does not know this, that he himself is the great God? But because there were to be those who would call themselves great, and make God small; against them it is said, *you are the only great God.* For what you say is fulfilled, not what they say, who call themselves great. What did God say through his Spirit? *All nations, as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord*[^44]. What does I-know-not-who say, who calls himself great? Far be it: God is not adored in all nations; all nations have perished, Africa alone has remained. This you say, who call yourself great; another thing he says who is the only great God. What does he say who is the only great God? *All nations, as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord.* I see what the only great God said; let the man falsely great keep silent; therefore falsely great, because he disdains to be small. Who disdains to be small? This man who says this. *Whoever wishes to be greater among you,* said the Lord, *shall be your servant*[^45]. That man, if he wished to be the servant of his brothers, would not separate them from their mother: but since he wishes to be great, and does not wish to be wholesomely small; God, who resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble[^46], since he alone is great, fulfills all that he foretold, and contradicts those who speak evil. For such men speak evil of Christ, who say that the Church has perished from the whole world, and remained in Africa alone. If you said to him: You will lose your farm; perhaps he would not hold back his hand from you: and he says that Christ has lost his inheritance, redeemed by his blood! But see how great an injury he does, brothers. Scripture says: *In a broad nation is the glory of a king; in the diminishing of the people is the ruin of the prince*[^47]. Therefore you do this injury to Christ, to say that his people is diminished to this smallness. Were you born for this, do you call yourself a Christian for this, that you may envy the glory of Christ, whose sign you assert that you bear on your forehead, and have lost from your heart? *In a broad nation is the glory of a king*: acknowledge your king; give him glory, give him a broad nation. What broad nation shall I give, you say? Do not give from your own heart, and you will give rightly. Whence do I give, you say? Behold, give from here: *All nations, as many as you have made, will come, and will adore before you, O Lord*[^48]. Say this, confess this, and you have given a broad nation: because all nations are one in one; that is unity. For as the Church and the churches, those churches which are the Church; so that nation which is the nations: before, nations, many nations; now, one nation. Why one nation? Because one faith, because one hope, because one love, because one expectation. Finally, why not one nation, if one homeland? The homeland is heavenly, the homeland is Jerusalem: whoever is not a citizen of it does not belong to this nation; but whoever is a citizen of it is in the one nation of God. And this nation is stretched out from east to west, from the north and the sea, through the four parts of the whole world. This God says: From east and west, from north and sea give glory to God. This he foretold, this he fulfilled, who alone is great. Let him cease, then, to say this against the only great one, who refused to be small: because there cannot be two great ones, God and Donatus.

**15.** [v. 11.] *Lead me, O Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth.* Your way, your truth, your life, is Christ. Therefore the body to him, and the body from him. *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^49]. *Lead me, O Lord, in your way.* In what way? *And I will walk in your truth.* It is one thing to lead to the way; another, to lead in the way. See man everywhere poor, everywhere needing help. Those who are outside the way are not Christians, or are not yet catholics, let them be led to the way: but when they have been brought to the way, and made catholics in Christ; let them be led by him in that very way, lest they fall. Surely they now walk in the way. *Lead me, O Lord, in your way*: surely I am now in your way, lead me there. *And I will walk in your truth*: with you leading, I will not err; if you let go, I will err. Pray, then, that he not let go, but lead unto the end. How does he lead? By always admonishing, by always giving you his hand. *And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed*[^50]? For by giving his Christ, he gives his hand; by giving his hand, he gives his Christ. He leads to the way, by bringing to his Christ; he leads in the way, by leading in his Christ: but Christ is the truth. *Lead me,* then, *O Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth*: surely in him who says: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life.* For you who lead in the way and the truth, whither do you bring except to the life? You lead, then, in him, to him. *Lead me, O Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth.*

**16.** *Let my heart be glad, that it may fear your name.* Therefore fear is in gladness. And how is there gladness, if there is fear? Is not fear wont to be bitter? There will sometime be gladness without fear, now gladness with fear: for there is not yet full security, nor perfect gladness. If there is no gladness, we fail; if there is full security, we exult wrongly. Therefore let him both sprinkle gladness, and strike fear, that from the sweetness of gladness he may bring us to the seat of security: by giving fear, let him not make us exult wrongly, and withdraw from the way. Therefore the psalm says: *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult before him with trembling*[^51]: so too the apostle Paul says: *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation; for it is God who works in you*[^52]. Whatever, then, comes prosperous, brothers, is rather to be feared: the things you suppose prosperous are rather temptations. An inheritance comes, an abundance of goods comes, the overflow of I-know-not-what happiness abounds: these are temptations; beware lest these corrupt you. Whatever too is prosperous according to Christ and the genuine love of Christ: if perhaps you have gained your wife who was in the party of Donatus; if perhaps your children who were pagans have believed; if perhaps you have gained your friend who wished to seduce you to the theaters, and you have led him to the church; if perhaps some contradictor, your enemy, raging madly, having laid aside that rage, has become gentle, and acknowledged God, nor barks against you, but cries out with you against evil: these are glad things. For whence do we rejoice, if we do not rejoice in these? or what other joys are ours, except these? But because tribulations too abound, and temptations, and dissensions, and schisms, and the other evils, without which this world cannot be, until iniquity pass; let that exultation not make us secure, but let our heart so be glad, that it fear the name of the Lord, lest it be glad from one thing, and struck from another. Do not expect security in the pilgrimage: when we wish it here, it will be birdlime for the body, not security for the man. *Let my heart be glad, that it may fear your name.*

**17.** [vv. 12-13.] *I will confess to you, O Lord my God, with my whole heart, and I will glorify your name forever; for your mercy is great over me, and you have rescued my soul from the lower hell.* What we say, brothers, if I do not expound it to you as one certain, do not be angry. For I am a man, and as much as is granted from the holy Scriptures, so much I dare to say: nothing of myself. I have not yet experienced hell, nor have you: and perhaps there will be another way, and it will not be through hell. These things are uncertain. But because Scripture says, which cannot be contradicted: *You have rescued my soul from the lower hell;* we understand that there are as it were two hells, a higher, and a lower: for whence the lower hell, except because there is a higher hell? It would not be called another hell, except in comparison with that higher part. It seems, then, brothers, that there is a certain heavenly habitation of the angels: there the life of ineffable joys, there immortality and incorruption, there all things abiding according to God's gift and grace. That part of things is supernal. If, then, that is supernal; this is earthly, where there is flesh and blood, where corruptibility, where birth and mortality, where departing and succeeding, where mutability and inconstancy, where fears, desires, dreads, uncertain gladnesses, fragile hope, falling substance; I think that all this part cannot be compared to that heaven of which I was speaking a little before: if, then, this part is not compared to that part, that is supernal, this infernal. And after death whither from here, unless there be a lower hell than this hell in which we are in the flesh, and this mortality? *For the body,* says the Apostle, *is dead because of sin*[^53]. Therefore here too there are dead; that you may not wonder that it is called hell, if it abounds in the dead. For he did not say: The body is to die; but: *The body is dead.* Our body still surely has life; and yet, compared to that body which is to be such as the bodies of the angels are, the body of man is found dead, although it still has a soul. But again from this hell—that is, from this part of hell—there is another lower, whither the dead go; whence God willed to rescue our souls, sending even there his Son. For, brothers, on account of these two hells the Son of God was sent, freeing on every side. To this hell he was sent by being born, to that one by dying. Therefore his voice is in that psalm, with no man conjecturing, but the Apostle expounding, where he says: *For you will not leave my soul in hell*[^54]. Therefore either it is his voice here too: *You have rescued my soul from the lower hell*: or it is our voice through Christ himself our Lord; because he came even unto hell for this, that we should not remain in hell.

**18.** I will say another opinion too. For perhaps among the lower regions themselves there is some lower part, whither are thrust the impious who have sinned most. For whether in certain places among the lower regions Abraham was not, we cannot sufficiently define. For the Lord had not yet come to hell, to rescue thence the souls of all the saints who went before, and yet Abraham was there in rest. And a certain rich man, when he was tormented in the lower regions, when he saw Abraham, lifted up his eyes. He could not see him with eyes lifted up, unless that one were higher, this one lower. And what did Abraham answer him, when he said: *Father Abraham, send Lazarus, that he may dip his finger, and drop it on my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame? Son,* he says, *remember that you received good things in your life; but Lazarus evil things: now, however, he rests here, but you are tormented. And besides these things,* he says, *between us and you a great chasm is fixed; so that neither can we come to you, nor anyone thence come to us*[^55]. Therefore between these two hells perhaps, in one of which the souls of the righteous rested, in the other the souls of the impious are tormented, a certain man praying here, now set here in the body of Christ, and praying in the voice of Christ, said that God rescued his soul from the lower hell, because he freed himself from such sins through which he could be led to the torments of the lower hell. Just as if a physician should see a sickness threatening you perhaps from some labor, and say: Spare yourself, treat yourself thus, rest, use these foods; for if you do not, you will fall ill: but if you do it and are saved, you rightly say to the physician: You have freed me from a sickness; not in which you already were, but in which you were to be. Some man having a troublesome case was to be sent to prison; another came, defended him: giving thanks, what does he say? You have rescued my soul from prison. A debtor was to be hanged; it was paid for him: the freed man is said to be freed from hanging. In all these they were not in those things; but because by such deserts they were so dealt with, that unless they had been aided, they would be there; thence they rightly say that they are freed, whither they were not permitted to be led through their liberators. Therefore, brothers, whether it be the one or the other; hold me here as a searcher of the word of God, not a rash affirmer. *And you have rescued my soul from the lower hell.*

**19.** [v. 14.] *O God, those passing beyond the law have risen up against me.* Whom does he call those passing beyond the law? Not the pagans, who did not receive the law. For no one passes beyond what he did not receive: the Apostle says definitely: *For where there is no law, neither is there transgression*[^56]. By "those passing beyond the law" he means transgressors of the law. Whom, then, do we understand, brothers? If we take this voice from the Lord himself, those passing beyond the law were the Jews. *Those passing beyond the law have risen up against me*: they did not keep the law, and they accused Christ, as though he were passing beyond the law. *Those passing beyond the law have risen up against me.* And the Lord suffered the things we know. Do you suppose his body suffers nothing of the kind now? Whence can it be otherwise? *If they called the head of the household Beelzebub; how much more those of his household? The disciple is not above the master, nor the servant above his lord*[^57]. The body too suffers those passing beyond the law; and they rise up against the body of Christ. Who are those passing beyond the law? Do the Jews perhaps dare to rise up against Christ? No: for neither do they themselves make much tribulation for us. For they have not yet believed, have not yet acknowledged salvation. Bad Christians rise up against the body of Christ, from whom the body of Christ daily suffers tribulation. All schisms, all heresies, all who live most wickedly within, and impose their own conduct on those living well, and draw them to their own, and corrupt good conduct by evil conversations[^58]; these *passing beyond the law have risen up against me.* Let every pious soul say it, let every Christian soul say it. He who does not suffer this, let him not say it. But if it is a Christian soul, it knows that it suffers evils: if it recognizes in itself its own passion, let it recognize here its own voice; but if it is outside passion, let it be outside the voice too; but that it may not be outside passion, let it walk by the narrow way[^59], and begin to live piously in Christ, it must needs suffer this persecution. *For all,* says the Apostle, *who wish to live piously in Christ suffer persecution*[^60]. *O God, those passing beyond the law have risen up against me; and a synagogue of the powerful have sought my soul.* A synagogue of the powerful is a gathering of the proud. A synagogue of the powerful rose up against the head—that is, our Lord Jesus Christ—of those crying and saying with one mouth: *Crucify, crucify*[^61]: of whom it was said: *The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword*[^62]. They did not strike, but they cried out: by crying out they smote, by crying out they crucified. The will of those crying out was fulfilled, when the Lord was crucified: *And a synagogue of the powerful have sought my soul. And they have not set you before their sight.* How did they not set you? They did not understand God. Let them spare the man: let them walk to what they saw. Suppose that he was not God, he was a man: was he therefore to be killed? Spare the man, and acknowledge God.

**20.** [v. 15.] *And you, O Lord God, pitying and merciful, longsuffering and greatly merciful, and truthful.* Why *longsuffering and greatly merciful, and pitying?* Because hanging on the cross he said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^63]. Whom does he ask? For whom does he ask? who asks? where does he ask? The Son the Father, crucified for the impious, amid those very injuries, not of words, but of inflicted death, hanging on the cross: as though he had his hands stretched out for this, that he might so pray for them, that his prayer might be directed like incense in the sight of the Father, and the lifting up of his hands an evening sacrifice[^64]. *Longsuffering, and greatly merciful, and truthful.*

**21.** [v. 16.] If, then, you are *truthful, look upon me, and have mercy on me; give power to your servant.* Because *truthful, give power to your servant.* Let the time of patience pass, let the time of judgment come. How, *give power to your servant? The Father judges no one; but all judgment he has given to the Son*[^65]. He, rising again, will himself also come to earth to judge; he himself will be seen terrible, who was seen contemptible. He will demonstrate power, who demonstrated patience: on the cross was patience; in the judgment will be power. For a man will appear judging, but in glory: because *as you saw him go,* said the angels, *so he will come*[^66]. That very form will come to judgment; therefore the impious too will see him: for the form of God they will not see. *For blessed are the pure of heart, for they shall see God*[^67]. Appearing in the form of man, he will say: *Go into the eternal fire;* that what Isaiah said may be fulfilled: *Let the impious be taken away, that he may not see the glory of the Lord*[^68]. Let him be taken away that he may not see the form of God. The form of man, then, they will see. *Who, though he was in the form of God, equal to God*[^69]: this the impious will not see. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^70]: this the impious will not see. For if God is the Word, and *blessed are the pure of heart, for they shall see God;* but the impious are impure of heart: without doubt they will not see God. And where is: *They shall see him whom they pierced*[^71]: except that it appears that they will see the form of man, that they may be judged; the form of God they will not see, except those who will be separated to the right? For when they have been separated to the right, this will be said to them: *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom, which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* But to the impious at the left, what? *Go into the eternal fire, which my Father prepared for the devil and his angels.* But the judgment finished, how did he conclude? *So the impious shall go into eternal burning; but the righteous into eternal life*[^72]. Now from the vision of the form of man those go to the vision of the form of God. *For this is,* he says, *eternal life, that they may know you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^73]: you supply: And him too the one true God; because the Father and the Son are one true God: so that this is the sense, that they may know you and Jesus Christ whom you sent the one true God. For those will not go to the vision of the Father, and not there see the Son too. If the Son were not too in the vision of his Father, the Son himself would not say to his disciples that the Son is in the Father, and the Father in the Son. The disciples say to him: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us. He says to them: So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me? Philip, he who has seen me has seen the Father also.* You see that in the vision of the Father there is the vision of the Son too; and in the vision of the Son there is the vision of the Father too. Therefore he consequently subjoined, and said: *Do you not know that I am in the Father, and the Father in me*[^74]? That is, both with me seen the Father is seen, and with the Father seen the Son is seen. The vision of the Father and the Son cannot be separated: where the nature and substance is not separated, the vision cannot be separated. For that you may know that there the heart must be prepared to see the divinity of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, into which, not seen, we believe, and by believing we cleanse the heart whence it can be seen; the Lord himself says in another place: *He who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me: but he who loves me will be loved by my Father; and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him*[^75]. Did they not see him with whom he was speaking? Both they saw, and they did not see: something they saw, something they believed; they saw the man, they believed the God. But in the judgment they will see the same our Lord Jesus as man together with the impious; after the judgment they will see God apart from the impious. *Give power to your servant.*

**22.** *And save the son of your handmaid.* The Lord the son of a handmaid. Of what handmaid? To whom, when it was announced that he would be born, she answered and said: *Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done to me according to your word*[^76]. He saved the son of his handmaid, and his own Son: his own Son, in the form of God; the son of his handmaid, in the form of a servant. Of the handmaid of God, then, the Lord was born in the form of a servant; and he said: *Save the son of your handmaid.* And he was saved from death, as you know, his flesh which was dead being raised up. But that you may see that he is God, and was not so raised up by the Father that he was not raised up by himself; because he himself too raised up his flesh, you have it said in the Gospel: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*: but lest we should suppose otherwise, the Evangelist goes on to say: *But he said this of the temple of his body*[^77]. The son of the handmaid, then, was saved. Let each Christian too, set in the body of Christ, say: *Save the son of your handmaid.* Perhaps he cannot say: *Give power to your servant;* because that Son received power. But why does he not say this too? Was it not said to the servants: *You shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^78]? and do the servants not say: *Do you not know that we shall judge angels*[^79]? Each of the saints, then, receives power too, and each of the saints is the son of his handmaid. But what if he was born of a pagan woman, and made a Christian? how can the son of a pagan woman be the son of his handmaid? He is indeed carnally the son of a pagan woman, but spiritually the son of the Church. *And save the son of your handmaid.*

**23.** [v. 17.] *Make with me a sign for good.* What sign, except of the resurrection? The Lord says: *This perverse and embittering generation seeks a sign; and a sign shall not be given it, except the sign of the prophet Jonah. For as Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights, so shall the Son of man too be in the heart of the earth*[^80]. Therefore with our head a sign for good has already been made: let each of us too say: *Make with me a sign for good;* because at the last trumpet, at the coming of the Lord, both the dead will rise incorrupt, and we shall be changed[^81]. This will be a sign for good. *Make with me a sign for good, that those who hate me may see, and be confounded.* In the judgment they will be confounded ruinously, who now refuse to be confounded wholesomely. Now, then, let them be confounded; let them accuse their bad ways, let them hold the good way: for none of us lives without confusion, unless he first revives, having been confounded. God now offers them an entrance to wholesome confusion, if they do not despise the medicine of confession: but if they now refuse to be confounded, then they will be confounded, when their iniquities will lead them along from the opposite side[^82]. How will they be confounded? When they will say: *These are they whom we once held in derision and as a likeness of reproach. We senseless ones esteemed their life madness: how have they been reckoned among the sons of God? What did pride profit us*[^83]? Then they will say it: now let them say it, and they say it wholesomely. For let each one turn to God humbly, and now say: What did pride profit me? and let him hear from the Apostle: *For what glory did you have in those things in which you now blush*[^84]? You see that there is even now a wholesome confusion in the place of repentance; but then a late, useless, fruitless one. *What did pride profit us? or what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All things have passed away like a shadow*[^85]. For what? when you lived here, did you not see that all these things passed away like a shadow? Then you would leave the shadow, and be in the light; you would not afterward say: *All things have passed away like a shadow,* when you are about to go from shadow into darkness. *Make with me a sign for good, that those who hate me may see, and be confounded.*

**24.** *For you, O Lord, have helped me, and have consoled me. You have helped me,* in the contest; *and have consoled me,* in sadness. For no one seeks consolation, except one who is in misery. Do you not wish to be consoled? Say that you are happy. And you hear: *My people* (now you answer, and I hear the murmur of those who hold the Scriptures well. May God who wrote this in your hearts confirm it in your deeds. You see, brothers, that those who say to you: You are happy, seduce you): *My people, those who call you happy mislead you into error, and disturb the paths of your feet*[^86]. So too from the Epistle of the apostle James: *Be wretched,* he says, *and mourn; let your laughter be turned into grief*[^87]. You see what you have heard: when would these things be said to us in the region of security? Surely this region is one of scandals, and temptations, and all evils, that we may groan here, and deserve to rejoice there; be troubled here, and consoled there, and say: *For you have delivered my eyes from tears, my feet from slipping; I will please the Lord in the region of the living*[^88]. This region is one of the dead. The region of the dead passes, the region of the living comes. In the region of the dead is labor, grief, fear, tribulation, temptation, groaning, sighing: here the falsely happy, the truly unhappy; because false happiness is true misery. But he who acknowledges that he is in true misery, will be also in true happiness: and yet now, because you are wretched, hear the Lord saying: *Blessed are those who mourn*[^89]. O, *blessed are those who mourn!* Nothing is so joined to misery as mourning; nothing so removed and contrary to misery as blessedness: you say "those who mourn," and you say "blessed"! Understand, he says, what I say: I call those who mourn blessed; why blessed? In hope. Why mourning? In reality. For they mourn in this death, in these tribulations, in their pilgrimage; and because they acknowledge that they are in this misery, and groan, they are blessed. Why do they mourn? Blessed Cyprian was saddened in the passion; now he has been consoled in the crown. Now even consoled he is still sad. For our Lord Jesus Christ still intercedes for us[^90]: all the Martyrs who are with him intercede for us. Their intercessions do not pass away, until our groaning passes away: but when our groaning passes away, we shall all be consoled in one voice, in one people, in one homeland, thousands of thousands joined to the singing angels, to the choirs of the heavenly Powers living in one city. Who there groans? who there sighs? who there labors? who there is in want? who there dies? who there offers mercy? who breaks bread for the hungry, where all are fed with the bread of righteousness? No one says to you: Take in the stranger: no one there will be a pilgrim; all live in their own homeland. No one says to you: Reconcile your friends quarreling: in the everlasting peace of God they enjoy his countenance. No one says to you: Visit the sick: health and immortality abide. No one says to you, bury the dead: all will be in eternal life. The works of mercy cease, because misery is not found. And what shall we do there? Shall we perhaps sleep? If now we fight against ourselves, although we bear this flesh, the house of sleep, and keep vigil in these lights, and this festival gives us spirit for vigil; what kind of vigils will that day give us? Therefore we shall keep vigil, we shall not sleep. What shall we do? These works of mercy will not be there, because there will be no misery. Perhaps these will be works of necessity which are here now, of sowing, plowing, cooking, grinding, weaving? None of these, because there will be no necessity. So there will be no works of mercy, because misery passes: where there will be neither necessity nor misery, the works of necessity and mercy will not be. What will there be? what will be our business? what our action? Or no action, because rest? Shall we then sit, and be sluggish, and do nothing? If our love grows cold, our action will grow cold. Love, then, at rest in the countenance of God, whom we now desire, for whom we sigh; when we have come to him, how will it kindle us? Toward whom, not yet seen, we so sigh, when we have come to him, how will it enlighten us? how will it change us? what will it make of us? What, then, shall we do, brothers? Let the psalm tell us: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house.* Whence? *Forever and ever they will praise you*[^91]. This will be our action, the praise of God. You love, and you praise. You will cease to praise, if you cease to love. But you will not cease to love, because such is he whom you see, that he offends you with no weariness: and he satisfies you, and does not satisfy you. It is marvelous what I say. If I say that he satisfies you, I fear lest, as satisfied, you wish to depart; as from a lunch, as from a dinner. What, then, shall I say? He does not satisfy you? Again I fear lest, if I say: He does not satisfy you, you seem needy; and as though emptier you exist, and there be less in you something which ought to be filled. What, then, shall I say, except what can be said, can scarcely be thought? Both he satisfies you, and he does not satisfy you: because I find both in Scripture. For when it said: *Blessed are those who hunger, for they shall be satisfied*[^92]: it is again said of Wisdom: *Those who eat you shall hunger again: and those who drink you shall thirst again.* Rather, it did not say, *again;* but said, *still*: for "shall thirst again" is as though one, first satisfied, departed and digested and returned to drink. Such is: *Those who eat you shall still hunger*[^93]: so when they eat, they shall hunger; and those who drink you, so by drinking, shall thirst. What is it to thirst by drinking? Never to be wearied. If, then, that ineffable and everlasting sweetness will be there; now what does he ask of us, brothers, except an unfeigned faith, a firm hope, a pure love, and that man walk in the way which the Lord gave, bear temptations, and receive consolations?
