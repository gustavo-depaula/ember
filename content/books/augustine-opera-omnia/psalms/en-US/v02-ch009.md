# Exposition of Psalm 37

**1.** Opportunely to what we sang, *I declare my iniquity, and I will take care for my sin*[^1], this woman answered from the Gospel reading. For the Lord, looking on her iniquities, called her a dog, saying, *It is not good to throw the children's bread to dogs*[^2]. But she, who knew how to declare her iniquity, and to take care for her sin, did not deny what the truth said; but, confessing her misery, she rather obtained mercy, taking care for her sin. For she had asked that her daughter be cured, perhaps signifying in the daughter her own life. Attend, then, to the whole psalm, as much as we can, while we consider and treat it. May the Lord be present to our hearts, that we may here wholesomely find our voices, and, as we find them, bring them forth, neither finding them with difficulty, nor bringing them forth unskillfully.

**2.** [v. 1] Its title is, *A psalm of David himself, for a remembrance of the sabbath*[^3]. We seek what is written for us of holy David the prophet, from whose seed is our Lord Jesus Christ according to the flesh: and among the good things known to us about him through the Scriptures, we do not find that he ever remembered the sabbath. For why would he so remember it, according to that observance of the Jews by which they observed the sabbath; why would he so remember what necessarily came among the seven days? It was, then, to be observed, not so to be remembered. But no one remembers except what is not set in his presence. For example: in this city you remember Carthage, where you once were; and on today's day you remember yesterday's day, or that of a former year, or any earlier one, and some deed of yours which you have already done, or some place where you were, or some matter at which you were present. What does this remembrance of the sabbath mean, my brothers? what soul so remembers the sabbath? what is this sabbath? For with groaning he remembers it. And when the psalm was read, you heard, and now when we go over it again, you will hear how great the mourning is, how great the groaning, how great the weeping, what misery. But happy is he who is so wretched. Whence also the Lord in the Gospel called certain mourners blessed. Whence blessed, if mourning? whence blessed, if wretched? Rather he would be wretched if he were not mourning. Such a one, then, let us understand here too as remembering the sabbath, some mourner: and would that we ourselves were that "some one"! For there is a certain one grieving, groaning, mourning, remembering the sabbath. The sabbath is rest. Without doubt this man was in some unrest, who with groaning remembered rest.

**3.** [v. 2] He, accordingly, narrates and commends to God the unrest which he suffered, fearing something more grievous than was where he was. For that he is in evil, he says openly, and there is no need of an interpreter, nor of suspicion, nor of conjecture: in what evil he is, is not doubted from his words, nor is there need that we seek it, but that we understand what he says. And unless he feared something worse than that by which he was held, he would not so begin: *O Lord, rebuke me not in your indignation, nor amend me in your wrath*[^4]. For it will be that some will be amended in the wrath of God, and rebuked in his indignation. And perhaps not all who are rebuked will be amended: but yet some will be saved in amendment. It will be, indeed, because amendment is named: yet so, as it were, through fire. But there will be some who will be rebuked, and not amended. For surely he will rebuke those to whom he will say, *I was hungry, and you gave me not to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me not drink*: and the rest which there he pursues, rebuking a certain inhumanity and barrenness in the wicked set at the left, to whom it is said, *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^5]. Dreading these more grievous things, apart from this life, in whose evils he laments and groans, he asks and says, *O Lord, rebuke me not in your indignation.* Let me not be among those to whom you will say, *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels. Nor amend me in your wrath*: that in this life you may cleanse me, and render me such, that I no longer need the amending fire, on account of those who will be saved, yet so, as it were, through fire. Why? except because here they build upon the foundation wood, hay, stubble? But let them build gold, silver, precious stones, and they would be secure from both fires; not only from that eternal one which will torment the ungodly forever, but also from that which will amend those who will be saved through fire. For it is said, *But he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire*[^6]. And because it is said, *he will be saved,* that fire is despised. So plainly, although saved through fire, yet that fire will be more grievous than whatever a man can suffer in this life. And you know how great things the wicked have suffered here, and can suffer: yet they have suffered as great as the good too were able to suffer. For what did any malefactor, robber, adulterer, criminal, sacrilegious man endure by the laws, which a martyr did not endure in the confession of Christ? These evils, then, which are here, are far easier: and yet see how, that men may not suffer them, they do whatever you command. How much better do they do what God commands, lest they suffer those more grievous things?

**4.** [v. 3] But why does he ask not to be rebuked in indignation, nor amended in wrath? As if saying to God: Since these things which I suffer are now many, are great, I ask that they may suffice. And he begins to enumerate them, satisfying God, offering those things which he suffers, lest he suffer worse: *Because your arrows are fixed in me, and you have confirmed your hand upon me*[^7].

**5.** [v. 4] *There is no health in my flesh from the face of your wrath*[^8]. Now he was speaking of these things which he suffered here: and yet this now is of the wrath of the Lord, because it is of the vengeance of the Lord. Of what vengeance? Which he took from Adam. For it was not that he was not avenged on him, or that God had said in vain, *You shall die the death*[^9]: or that we suffer anything in this life, except from that death which we earned by the first sin. For indeed we carry a mortal body (which surely would not be mortal), full of temptations, full of anxieties, subject to bodily griefs, subject to wants, mutable, languid even when it is healthy, because surely it is not yet fully healthy. For whence did he say, *There is no health in my flesh,* except because that which is called the health of this life, to those who understand well and remember the sabbath, is surely not health? For if you do not eat, hunger disquiets you. This is a kind of natural disease: because nature has been made for us a punishment from the vengeance. What was a punishment to the first man, is nature to us. Whence the Apostle says, *We too were by nature children of wrath, as also the rest*[^10]. *By nature children of wrath,* that is, carrying the vengeance. But why does he say, *We were*? Because in hope we now are not: for in reality we still are. But we say better that which we are in hope, because we are certain of the hope. For our hope is not uncertain, that we should doubt of it. Hear that very glory in hope. *In ourselves,* he says, *we groan, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^11]. What then? Are you not yet redeemed, O Paul? has the price not yet been paid for you? Has that blood not already been shed? is it not the very price of us all? Plainly it is. But see what he says: *For in hope we have been saved: but hope that is seen is not hope. For what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, by patience we await it*[^12]. But what does he await by patience? Salvation. The salvation of what thing? Of the body itself: because he said this, *the redemption of our body.* If he awaited the salvation of the body, that was not the salvation which he had. Hunger, thirst kills, if it is not aided. For the medicine of hunger is food, and the medicine of thirst is drink, and the medicine of weariness is sleep. Take away these medicines, see whether the things that exist do not kill. If, these being set aside, there are no diseases, there is health. But if you have something that can kill you, unless you eat; do not boast of health, but, groaning, await the redemption of your body. Rejoice that you are redeemed; but not yet in reality, in hope you are secure. For indeed, if you do not groan in hope, you will not come to the reality. This, then, is not health, he says: *There is no health in my flesh from the face of your wrath.* Whence, then, the arrows fixed? The very punishment, the very vengeance, and perhaps the griefs which here it is necessary to suffer, both of mind and of body, these he calls the arrows. For of these arrows holy Job too made mention, and when he was in those griefs, he said the arrows of the Lord were fixed in him. Yet we are wont to take the words of God too as arrows: but could this man so grieve to be struck by these? The words of God, like arrows, rouse love, not grief. Or is it that love itself too cannot be without grief? For whatever we love and do not have, we must grieve. For he both loves and does not grieve, who has what he loves: but he who loves, as I said, and does not yet have what he loves, must groan in grief. Hence that in the person of the Church, the bride of Christ in the Song of Songs: *For I am wounded with love*[^13]. She said she was wounded with love: for she loved something, and did not yet hold it; she grieved, because she did not yet have it. Therefore if she grieved, she was wounded: but this wound carried her off to true health. He who has not been wounded with this wound cannot come to true health. Will he, then, always be wounded in the wound? We can, accordingly, take the arrows fixed thus too, that is, your words are fixed in my heart, and from those very words of yours it has come about that I remember the sabbath; and that very remembrance of the sabbath, and not yet the retaining of it, makes me not yet rejoice, and recognize that there is no health even in this flesh, nor ought it to be called so, when I compare this health with that health which I shall have in eternal rest, where this corruptible shall put on incorruption, and this mortal shall put on immortality; and I see that, in comparison with that health, this health is a disease.

**6.** *There is no peace for my bones from the face of my sins*[^14]. It is wont to be asked whose voice it is; and some take it to be Christ's, on account of certain things here said about the passion of Christ, to which we shall come a little later, and we shall recognize that they are said about the passion of Christ. But *There is no peace for my bones from the face of my sins,* how would he say it who had no sin? The necessity of understanding constrains us, then, to recognize, as it were, the full and whole Christ, that is, head and body. For when Christ speaks, sometimes he speaks from the person of the head alone, which is the Savior himself, born of the Virgin Mary; sometimes from the person of his body, which is the holy Church, spread throughout the whole world. And we are in his body, if at least our faith in him is sincere, and our hope sure, and our love kindled: we are in his body, and his members, and we find ourselves speaking there, the Apostle saying, *For we are members of his body*[^15]; and in many places the Apostle says this. For if we say the words are not Christ's, then neither will those words be Christ's: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* For there too you have, *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions*; just as here you have, *from the face of my sins;* so there too you have, *the words of my transgressions*[^16]. And if Christ is surely without sin and without transgression, we begin to suppose that those words are not of that psalm. And it is very hard and contrary that that psalm should not pertain to Christ, where we have his passion so open, as if it were recited from the Gospel. For there we have, *They divided my garments among them, and over my garment they cast lots*[^17]. What of the fact that the Lord himself, hanging on the cross, brought forth with his own mouth the first verse of that very psalm, and said, *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?*[^18] What did he wish to be understood, except that that whole psalm pertained to him, because he pronounced its head himself? But where it follows and says, *The words of my transgressions,* there is no doubt that it is the voice of Christ. Whence, then, the sins, except from the body which is the Church? Because the body of Christ and the head speaks. Why does it speak as one? Because *they shall be,* he says, *two in one flesh. This sacrament is great,* says the Apostle, *but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^19]. Whence also, when he himself was speaking in the Gospel, answering those who had brought a question about putting away a wife, he said, *Have you not read what is written, that God from the beginning made them male and female, and a man shall leave his father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife, and they shall be two in one flesh? Therefore now not two, but one flesh*[^20]. If, then, he himself said, *Now not two, but one flesh;* what wonder if one flesh, one tongue, the same words, as of one flesh, head and body? Let us so hear, as one: but yet head as head, and body as body. The persons are not divided, but the dignity is distinguished: because the head saves, the body is saved. Let the head display mercy, the body weep over its misery. The head is for cleansing, the body for confessing sins: yet one voice, where it is not written when the body speaks, when the head; but we, in hearing, distinguish; while he speaks as one. For why should he not say, *of my sins,* who said, *I was hungry, and you gave me not to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me not drink; I was a stranger, and you took me not in; I was sick, and in prison, and you visited me not*? Surely the Lord was not in prison. Why should he not say this, who, when it was said to him, *When did we see you hungering and thirsting, or in prison, and did not minister to you?* answered, that he had said this from the person of his body: *When you did it not to one of these least of mine, neither did you do it to me*[^21]? Why should he not say, *from the face of my sins,* who said to Saul, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^22]—he who surely in heaven was now suffering no persecutor? But as there the head spoke for the body, so here too the head speaks the voices of the body, even when you hear the voices of the head. But neither, when you hear the voices of the body, separate the head; nor, when you hear the voices of the head, separate the body: because now not two, but one flesh.

**7.** *There is no health in my flesh from the face of your wrath*[^23]. But perhaps God was angry at you unjustly, O Adam, O human race, God was angry unjustly! because you said, now recognizing your very punishment, now a man set in the body of Christ, *There is no health in my flesh from the face of your wrath.* Set forth the justice of the wrath of God: lest you seem to excuse yourself, and accuse him. Follow on, and say whence the wrath of the Lord. *There is no health in my flesh from the face of your wrath: there is no peace for my bones.* He repeated what he said, *There is no health in my flesh*: for this is, *there is no peace for my bones.* But he did not repeat, *from the face of your wrath*: but he stated the cause of the wrath of God: *There is no peace,* he says, *for my bones from the face of my sins.*

**8.** [v. 5] *Because my iniquities have gone over my head, like a heavy burden they have been made heavy upon me*[^24]. And here he put the cause first, and subjoined the effect; he said what, and whence it came: *My iniquities have gone over my head.* For no one is proud, except the unjust, whose head is lifted up. He is lifted up who raises his head against God. You heard, when the reading of the book of Ecclesiasticus was read, *The beginning of a man's pride is to apostatize from God*[^25]. That one who first was unwilling to hear the precept, his iniquity lifted up his head against God. And because his iniquities lifted up his head, what did God do to him? *Like a heavy burden they have been made heavy upon me.* It is of lightness to lift up the head, as though he carries nothing who lifts up the head. Because, then, what can be lifted up is light, he receives a weight by which he can be pressed down. For his labor is turned upon his head, and his iniquity descends upon his crown. *Like a heavy burden they have been made heavy upon me.*

**9.** [v. 6] *My sores have putrefied and stunk*[^26]. Now he who has sores is not healthy. Add that the sores themselves have putrefied and stunk. Whence did they stink? Because they putrefied. Now how this is to be explained in human life, who does not know? Let one have a sound sense of smell of the soul, he perceives how sins stink. To which stench of sins that fragrance was contrary, of which the Apostle says, *We are the good fragrance of Christ to God, in every place, among those who are being saved*[^27]. But whence, except from hope? Whence, except from the remembrance of the sabbath? For one thing we bewail in this life, another we presume on in that life. What is bewailed, stinks: what is presumed on, is fragrant. Therefore, unless there were that fragrance which invited us, we would never remember the sabbath. But because we have through the Spirit that very fragrance, so that we may say to our bridegroom, *After the fragrance of your ointments we shall run*[^28]; we turn our smelling away from our own stenches, and, turning ourselves to him, we breathe again a little. But unless our evils too smell to us, we never confess with these groans: *My sores have putrefied and stunk.* Whence? *From the face of my foolishness.* Whence he said above, *From the face of my sins*: thence now, *From the face of my foolishness.*

**10.** [v. 7] *I have been afflicted with miseries, and bowed down even to the end*[^29]. Whence has he been bowed down? Because he was lifted up. If you are humble, you are raised up; if you are lifted up, you are bowed down: for there will not be lacking to God a weight by which to bow you down. That will be the weight, the burden of your sins: this will be folded back upon your head, and you will be bowed down. But what is it to be bowed down? Not to be able to raise oneself up. Such a woman the Lord found, for eighteen years bent: she could not raise herself up. Such are those who have their heart on the earth. But truly, because that woman found the Lord, and he healed her, let her have her heart above. Yet, insofar as she is bowed down, she still groans. For he is bowed down who says, *For the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that thinks of many things*[^30]. In these things let him groan, that he may receive that other, let him remember the sabbath, that he may deserve to come to the sabbath. For what the Jews celebrated was a sign. A sign of what thing? Of that which this man remembers, who says, *I have been afflicted with miseries, and bowed down even to the end.* What is *even to the end*? Even to death. *All the day I walked saddened. All the day,* without intermission. This he means by *all the day,* all my life. But from when did he know it? From when he began to remember the sabbath. For as long as he remembers what he does not yet have, do you not wish that he walk saddened? *All the day I walked saddened.*

**11.** [v. 8] *Because my soul is filled with illusions, and there is no health in my flesh*[^31]. Where, then, is the whole man, is soul and flesh. The soul is filled with illusions, the flesh has no health: what remains whence there may be gladness? Ought he not to be saddened? *All the day I walked saddened.* Let there be sadness for us, until both our soul be stripped of illusions, and our body be clothed with health. For that is true health, which is immortality. But how great the illusions of the soul are, if I should wish to say it, when is there time enough? For whose soul does not suffer these? It is a brief thing I admonish, how our soul is filled with illusions. From the face of those very illusions, sometimes we are scarcely permitted to pray. We do not know how to think of bodies except images; and often there rush in those we do not seek, and we wish to go from this to that, and to pass from that to the other; and sometimes you wish to return to what you were thinking of, and to let go that from which you are thinking, and something else occurs to you: you wish to remember what you had forgotten; and it does not come into your mind, and rather something else comes which you did not wish. Where was that which you had forgotten? For why did it afterward come into mind, when it was no longer sought? But when it was sought, there occurred for it innumerable things which were not sought. I have spoken a brief matter, brothers: I have sprinkled something on you, by which, having received it, thinking of the rest yourselves, you may find what it is to bewail the illusions of our soul. It received, then, the punishment of illusion, it lost the truth. For as illusion is the punishment of the soul, so truth is the reward of the soul. But while we were set in these illusions, truth came to us, and found us covered with illusions, took up our flesh, or rather from us, that is, from the human race. It appeared to the eyes of the flesh, that through faith it might heal those to whom it was about to show the truth: so that, the eye being healed, the truth might be laid open. For he himself is the truth, which he promised us, when his flesh was seen, that faith might be begun, whose reward would be truth. For Christ did not show himself on earth, but showed his flesh. For if he showed himself, the Jews would see him and recognize him: but if they had recognized him, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. But perhaps the disciples saw him, when they said to him, *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^32]. And he, to show that he had not been seen by them, subjoined, *So long a time am I with you, and you have not known me? Philip, he who sees me sees also the Father*[^33]. If, then, they saw Christ, how did they still seek the Father? For if they saw Christ, they would see also the Father. Not yet, therefore, did they see Christ, who desired the Father to be shown to them. Hear that they did not yet see: he promised that as a reward in another place, saying, *He who loves me keeps my commandments: and he who loves me shall be loved by my Father, and I will love him.* And as if it were said to him: What will you give him, loving him? *And I will show,* he says, *myself to him*[^34]. If, then, he promised this as a reward to those who love him, that he will show himself to them; it is manifest that that vision of truth is promised us such, that, when it is seen, we shall no longer say, *My soul is filled with illusions.*

**12.** [v. 9] *I have been weakened and humbled exceedingly*[^35]. He who remembers the height of the sabbath, he himself sees how much he has been humbled. For he who cannot think what that height of rest is, does not see where he now is. Therefore another psalm said, *I said in my ecstasy: I am cast away from before your eyes*[^36]. For, his mind being taken up, he saw something sublime, I know not what; and what he saw, he was not yet wholly there: and by a certain, if it may be said, as it were flashing of the eternal light made, where he felt that he was not there—which he could in some way understand—he saw where he was, and how, by human evils, he had been weakened and confined: and he said, *I said in my ecstasy: I am cast away from before your eyes.* It is something such, what I saw in ecstasy, that from it I perceive how far off I am, who am not yet there. He was already there who said he was taken up into the third heaven, and there heard ineffable words, which it is not lawful for a man to speak. But he was recalled to us, that he might groan, first to be perfected in weakness, and so afterward be clothed with power: yet encouraged, because he saw something of those things, for the dispensation of his office, he added, saying, *I heard ineffable words, which it is not lawful for a man to speak*[^37]. Now, then, what need is there that you seek from me or from anyone the things which it is not lawful for a man to speak: if it was not lawful for him to speak, who was permitted to hear? Yet let us bewail and groan in confession, let us recognize where we are, let us remember the sabbath, and patiently await what he promised, who showed us in himself too an example of patience. *I have been weakened and humbled exceedingly.*

**13.** *I roared from the groaning of my heart*[^38]. You often notice servants of God interrupting with groans, and the cause is sought: and nothing appears but the groan of some servant of God; if at least it has reached the ears of a man set near him. For there is a hidden groan which is not heard by man: yet if so great a thought of some desire has occupied the heart, that the wound of the inner man is expressed with a clearer voice, the cause is sought; and a man says within himself: Perhaps it is that whence he groans, and perhaps that was done to him. Who can understand, except he in whose eyes and ears he groans? Therefore *I roared,* he says, *from the groaning of my heart*: because men, if ever they hear the groan of a man, often hear the groan of the flesh; they do not hear one groaning from the groaning of the heart. Someone has taken away his goods; he roared, but not from the groaning of the heart: another, because he carried out his son; another, because his wife; another, because his vineyard was hailed on, because his vat soured, because some man plundered his beast of burden; another, because he suffered some loss; another, because he fears a hostile man: all these roar from the groaning of the flesh. But the servant of God, because he roars from the remembrance of the sabbath, where is the kingdom of God, which flesh and blood shall not possess: *I roared,* he says, *from the groaning of my heart.*

**14.** [v. 10] And who recognized whence he was roaring? He subjoined, *And all my desire is before you*[^39]. For not before men, who cannot see the heart: but *all my desire is before you.* Let your desire be before him; and the Father who sees in secret will reward you. For your very desire is your prayer: and if the desire is continual, the prayer is continual. For not in vain did the Apostle say, *Praying without intermission*[^40]. Do we without intermission bend the knee, prostrate the body, or lift up the hands, that he should say, *Pray without intermission*? Or if we say we pray thus, this, I think, we cannot do without intermission. There is another, inner, prayer without intermission, which is desire. Whatever else you do, if you desire that sabbath, you do not intermit praying. If you do not wish to intermit praying, do not intermit desiring. Your continual desire is your continual voice. You will be silent, if you cease to love. Who were silent? Those of whom it was said, *Because iniquity has abounded, the love of many shall grow cold*[^41]. The coldness of love is the silence of the heart: the flagrancy of love is the cry of the heart. If love always remains, you always cry; if you always cry, you always desire; if you desire, you remember rest. And before whom your heart's roaring is, you ought to understand. Now what kind of desire ought to be before the eyes of God, consider. Is it that our enemy should die, which men desire as though justly? For sometimes we pray for what we ought not. Let us see what men pray as though justly. For they pray that someone may die, and that the inheritance may come to them. But even those who pray that their enemies may die, let them hear the Lord saying, *Pray for your enemies*[^42]. Let them not, then, pray this, that their enemies may die: but let them pray this, that they may be corrected; and their enemies will be dead; for, now corrected, they will not be enemies. *And all my desire is before you.* What if the desire is before him, and the groan itself is not before him? How can this be, when the desire itself has the groan as its voice? Therefore it follows: *And my groaning is not hidden from you.* From you it is not hidden, but from many men it is hidden. Sometimes a humble servant of God seems to say, *And my groaning is not hidden from you.* Sometimes a servant of God seems even to laugh: is that desire dead in the heart? But if the desire is within, the groan too is within: it does not always reach the ears of men, but it never withdraws from the ears of God.

**15.** [v. 11] *My heart is troubled*[^43]. Whence is it troubled? *And my strength has forsaken me.* Often something sudden rushes in; there comes a troubling of the heart: the earth trembles, thunder is given from heaven, a horrible onset or crash happens, a lion perhaps is seen in the way; there comes a troubling: robbers lie in wait; there comes a troubling of the heart, there is dread, anxiety is struck in from every side. Whence this? Because *my strength has forsaken me.* For if that strength remained, what would be feared? Whatever were announced, whatever raged, whatever sounded, whatever fell, whatever was dreadful, would not terrify. But whence that perturbation? *My strength has forsaken me.* Whence has strength forsaken? *And the light of my eyes is not with me.* The light of his eyes, then, had hidden itself from Adam. For the light of his eyes was God himself: whom, when he had offended, he fled to the shade, and hid himself among the trees of paradise. He dreaded the face of God, and sought the shade of the trees. Now among the trees he did not have the light of his eyes, in which he was wont to rejoice. If, then, he from the origin, and we from the propagation; and these members return to him, the second or latest Adam, because the latest Adam was made into a life-giving spirit; and they cry from his body in this confession, *And the light of my eyes is not with me*: now confessing, now redeemed, now in the body of Christ he is, and the light of his eyes is not with him? Plainly it is not with him: but it is indeed, as it were, of those still remembering the sabbath, as of those seeing in hope; but it is not yet that light of which it is said, *I will show myself to him*[^44]. There is something of light, because we are children of God, and surely we hold this in faith: but it is not yet that light which we shall see. *For it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^45]. For now it is the light of faith and the light of hope. *For as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord: for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^46]. *And as long as we hope for what we do not see, by patience we await it*[^47]. These, then, are the voices of pilgrims, not yet set in the homeland. And he rightly says, and truly says, and, if he is not deceitful, truthfully confesses: *And the light of my eyes is not with me.* These things a man suffers within, there with himself, in himself, and himself, from no one to no one but himself: these he has deserved to be his own punishment to himself, whatever he enumerated above.

**16.** [v. 12] But is this the only thing a man suffers? For he suffers from himself within, but from without from those among whom he lives: he suffers his own evils, and is forced to suffer the evils of others too. Hence are those two voices: *Cleanse me from my hidden faults, O Lord, and from the faults of others spare your servant*[^48]. Now he has confessed his own hidden things, from which he desires to be cleansed: let him speak also of the faults of others, from which he wishes to be spared. *My friends.* What shall I now say of enemies? *My friends and my neighbors drew near against me, and stood*[^49]. This which he says, *they stood against me,* understand. For if they stood against me, they fell against themselves. *My friends and my neighbors drew near against me, and stood.* Now let us understand the voices of the head, now let our head begin to dawn in the passion. But again, when the head has begun to speak, do not separate the body from it. If the head was unwilling to separate itself from the voices of the body, shall the body dare to separate itself from the passions of the head? Suffer in Christ; because, as it were, Christ sinned in your weakness. For just now he spoke your sins as if from his own mouth, and called them his own. For he said, *From the face of my sins,* which were not his. As, then, he willed our sins to be his on account of his body, so let us too will his passions to be ours on account of our head. For he did not suffer enemies from among friends, and we not. Rather let us too prepare for this, to feast at the same table: let us not refuse such a cup, that we may find, through his humility, the desire of his loftiness. For he answered those who wished to cling to his loftiness, while they did not yet consider his humility, and said to them, *Can you drink the cup which I am to drink?*[^50] Therefore those passions of the Lord too are our passions: and each one, if he serves God well, keeps the faith well, displays what he owes, and conducts himself justly among men, I would see whether he does not suffer even what Christ here enumerates in his passion.

**17.** *My friends and my neighbors drew near against me, and stood: and my neighbors stood far off*[^51]. Which neighbors drew near, and which neighbors stood far off? The Jews were neighbors, because they were kinsmen: they drew near, both when they crucified him. The apostles too were neighbors: and yet they stood far off, lest they suffer with him. This can also be understood thus: *My friends,* that is, those who feigned themselves my friends. For they feigned themselves friends, when they said, *We know that you teach the way of God in truth*: when they wished to tempt him, whether tribute should be paid to Caesar, when he convicted them out of their own mouth, they wished to seem friends: but he did not need that anyone should bear him witness about man; for he himself knew what was in man: so that when they had spoken friendly words, he answered them, *Why do you tempt me, hypocrites?*[^52] Therefore *My friends and my neighbors drew near against me, and stood: and my neighbors stood far off.* You know what I said. I said the neighbors drew near, and yet stood far off. For they drew near in body, but stood far off in heart. Who so near in body as those who lifted him onto the cross? Who so far off in heart as those who blasphemed? Hear that farness from the prophet Isaiah, see that nearness and farness: *This people honors me with their lips*: behold, it draws near in body: *But their heart is far from me*[^53]. The same near, the same far: near in lips, far in heart. Yet because the apostles, fearing, stood far off, we take it more freely and plainly of them, that we understand some to have drawn near, others to have stood far off: since Peter too, who had more boldly followed, was still so far off that, questioned and disturbed, he denied the Lord three times, with whom he had promised he would die. He afterward, that from far off he might become near, heard after the resurrection, *Do you love me?* and said, *I love you*. And by saying it he drew near, who by denying had been made far, until by the threefold voice of love he loosed the threefold voice of denial. *And my neighbors stood far off.*

**18.** [v. 13] *And they did violence who sought my soul*[^54]. Now it is manifest who sought his soul: those who did not have his soul, because they were not in his body. Those who sought his soul were far from his soul: but they sought to kill it. For his soul is sought also for good. For in another place he reproaches certain ones, saying, *And there is no one who seeks my soul*[^55]. He reproaches some not seeking his soul, and again he reproaches others seeking his soul. Who is it that seeks his soul well? He who imitates his passions. Who are they who sought his soul badly? Those who did him violence, and crucified him.

**19.** It follows: *Those who sought my evils spoke vanity*[^56]. What is *Those who sought my evils*? They sought many, and did not find them. Perhaps he says this: They sought my crimes. For they sought what they might say against him, and did not find. For they sought evils from the good, they sought crimes from the innocent: when would they find them in one who had no sin? But because they sought sins in one who had no sin, it remained that they should feign what they did not find. Therefore: *Those who sought my evils spoke vanity,* not truth. *And they meditated deceit all the day*: that is, they meditated falsehood without ceasing. You know how many false witnesses were spoken against the Lord, before he suffered. You know how many false witnesses were spoken, even when he had risen. For those soldiers, guards of the tomb, of whom Isaiah said, *I will set the wicked for his burial*[^57] (for they were wicked, and were unwilling to speak the truth, and, corrupted, sowed a lie): attend to what vanity they spoke. They too were questioned, and said, *While we slept, his disciples came, and took him away*[^58]. This is to speak vanity. For if they were sleeping, whence did they know what had been done?

**20.** [vv. 14-15] Therefore he says: *But I, like a deaf man, was not hearing*[^59]. He who did not answer to the things he heard, was as one not hearing. *But I, like a deaf man, was not hearing: and like a mute man not opening his mouth.* And he repeats the same: *And I became like a man not hearing, and not having in his mouth retorts.* As though there were nothing for him to say to them, as though there were nothing whence he might rebuke them. Had he not already before rebuked many things, said many things, and said, *Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites*[^60], and many such things? Yet when he suffered, he said none of these: not because he had nothing to say, but he awaited that they should fulfill all things, and that all the prophecies about him should be fulfilled, of whom it had been said, *And like a sheep before its shearer without voice, so he opened not his mouth*[^61]. It behooved him, then, to be silent in the passion, who was not to be silent in the judgment. For he had come to be judged, who would afterward come to judge: and therefore to judge with great power, because he was judged with great humility.

**21.** [v. 16] *Because in you, O Lord, I have hoped; you will hear, O Lord my God*[^62]. As if it were said to him: Why did you not open your mouth? why did you not say, Spare me? why, hanging on the cross, did you not rebuke the wicked? It follows and says, *Because in you, O Lord, I have hoped; you will hear, O Lord my God.* He has admonished you what to do, if perhaps tribulation should come upon you. For you seek to defend yourself, and perhaps no one accepts your defense. Now you are perturbed, as though you had lost your cause, because you have no one's defense or testimony. Guard within your innocence, where no one oppresses your cause. False witness has prevailed against you, but among men: will it prevail with God, where your cause is to be pleaded? When God is the judge, there will be no other witness than your conscience. Between the just judge and your conscience fear nothing but your cause: if you have not a bad cause, you will dread no accuser, you will refute no false witness, you will seek no true one. Only bring a good conscience, that you may be able to say, *Because in you, O Lord, I have hoped; you will hear, O Lord my God.*

**22.** [v. 17] *Because I said: Lest at any time my enemies exult over me, and while my feet are shaken, they spoke great things against me*[^63]. Again he returns to the weakness of his body, and again that head attends to his feet: it is not so in heaven, that he forsakes what he has on earth: he attends plainly, and sees us. For sometimes, as this life is, our feet are shaken, and they slip in some sin: there rise up the most worthless tongues of enemies. From this, then, we understand, even when they were silent, what they sought. The unmerciful then speak harshly, rejoicing that they have found what they ought to have grieved over. And *I said: Lest at any time my enemies insult over me.* I said this, and yet perhaps for amendment you made them speak great things about me, *while my feet were shaken,* that is, they were lifted up, they said many evil things, when I was shaken. For they ought to have had mercy on the weak, not to insult; as the Apostle says, *Brothers, if a man be overtaken in some fault, you who are spiritual instruct such a one in the spirit of meekness.* And he embraces why: *looking,* he says, *to yourself, lest you too be tempted*[^64]. These men were not such as those of whom he says, *And while my feet were shaken, they spoke great things against me*: but they were such as those of whom he says elsewhere, *Those who press me will exult if I am moved*[^65].

**23.** [v. 18] *Because I am ready for scourges*[^66]. Altogether magnificently, as though he said: For this I was born, to bear scourges. For he would not be born except of Adam, to whom scourges are owed. But sometimes sinners in this life are either not scourged, or scourged less; because their intention is now despaired of. But those for whom eternal life is prepared, must be scourged here; because that saying is true: *Son, do not fail in the discipline of the Lord, nor be wearied when you are rebuked by him: for whom the Lord loves, he chastises; but he scourges every son whom he receives*[^67]. Therefore let not my enemies insult, let them not speak great things: and if my Father scourges me, *I am ready for scourges;* because an inheritance is being prepared for me. You do not wish the scourge, the inheritance is not given to you. For every son must be scourged. So far is every one scourged, that he spared not even him who had no sin. *Because I am ready for scourges.*

**24.** [vv. 18-19] *And my grief is before me always*[^68]. What grief? Perhaps over the scourge. And truly, my brothers, truly I say to you, men grieve over their scourges; why they are scourged they do not grieve. This man was not so. Hear, my brothers: When someone suffers a loss, he is more inclined to say, "I have suffered undeservedly," than to consider why he has suffered; grieving over the loss of money, not over the loss of justice. If you have sinned, grieve for your inner treasure: you have nothing in the house, but perhaps you are emptier in heart: but if the heart is full of its own good, of your God; why do you not say, The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done; blessed be the name of the Lord? Whence, then, did this man grieve? over the scourge by which he was scourged? Far be it. *And my grief,* he says, *is before me always.* And as if we said, what grief? whence the grief? *Because I declare my iniquity, and I will take care for my sin*[^69]. Behold whence the grief. Not grief over the scourge: over the wound, not over the medicine. For the scourge is a medicine against sins. Hear, brothers: we are Christians; and yet very often, if anyone's son dies, he bewails him; if he sins, he does not bewail him. Then he would bewail, then he would grieve, when he saw him sinning; then he would set a limit, then teach a norm of living, give discipline: or if he did, and the son did not hear, then he was to be bewailed; then worse off, dead, living dissolutely, than ending dissoluteness by dying: then, therefore, when he was doing these things in your house, he was not only dead, but also stank. These things are to be grieved over, those are to be endured: those to be borne, these to be bewailed. And to be bewailed, as you heard this man bewail: *Because I declare my iniquity, and I will take care for my sin.* Do not be secure, when you have confessed your sin, as though always prepared to confess and to commit the sin. So declare your iniquity, that you take care for your sin. What is it, to take care for your sin? To take care for your wound. If you said, "I will take care for my wound"; what would be understood, except, "I will give effort that it be healed"? For this is to take care for a fault, always to strive, always to attend, always studiously and diligently to act so that you heal the sin. Behold, from day to day you bewail your sin, but perhaps the tears run, and the hands are idle. Let alms be given, let sins be redeemed, let the needy rejoice over your gift, that you too may rejoice over the gift of God. He is in need, you too are in need: he is in need toward you, you too are in need toward God. You despise one who is in need of you, will God not despise you who are in need of him? Therefore fill the want of the needy, that God may fill your inner parts. This is: *I will take care for my sin,* I will do all things whatsoever are to be done, to abolish and heal my sin. *And I will take care for my sin.*

**25.** [v. 20] *But my enemies live*[^70]. It is well with them, they rejoice in the happiness of the world, where I labor, and roar from the groaning of my heart. How do his enemies live, since he already said of them that they spoke vanity? Hear too in another psalm: *Whose sons are like new plants established.* But above he had said, *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, their daughters adorned like the likeness of a temple; their storehouses full, overflowing from this into that; their oxen fat; their sheep fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth: there is no breach of the hedge, nor cry in their streets.* Therefore my enemies live: this is life, this they praise, this they love, this they have to their own harm. For what follows? *They called blessed the people that has these things.* But what of you, who take care for your sin? what of you, who declare your iniquity? *Blessed,* he says, *is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^71]. *But my enemies live: and they have been confirmed over me, and those who hate me unjustly have been multiplied.* What is, *who hate me unjustly*? They hate one who wishes them good. Who, if they returned evil for evil, would not be good; who, if they returned not good for good, would be ungrateful: but they return evil for good who hate unjustly. Such were the Jews: Christ came to them with good things, they returned to him evil for good. Beware of this evil, brothers: it creeps in quickly. Because we said, The Jews were such: let not each one of you suppose himself far exempted. Let some brother of yours rebuke you, wishing you good; you hate him, and you are such. And see how quickly it happens, how easily: and avoid so great an evil, so nimble a sin.

**26.** [v. 21] *They slandered me who repay evil for good, because I followed after justice*[^72]. For this reason, evil for good. What is, *I followed after justice*? I did not let go: lest perhaps you should always understand "persecution" in an evil sense, he said *followed after,* followed completely: *Because I followed after justice.* And hear our head wailing in the passion: *And they cast me away, the beloved, like an abominated dead man.* It was too little to say dead, why abominated? Because crucified. For this death of the cross was a great abomination among them, they not understanding that it was said in prophecy, *Cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree*[^73]. For he himself did not bring death, but found it here propagated from the curse of the first man: and, taking up that same death of ours, he hung on the tree the death which had come from sin. Therefore, lest some should think, as certain heretics think, that our Lord Jesus Christ had a false flesh, and did not loose a true death on the cross; the prophet intended this, and said, *Cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree.* He showed, then, that the Son of God too died a true death, which was owed to mortal flesh: lest, it not being a curse, you should think him not truly dead. But because that death was not false, but had descended from that propagation which had come from the curse, when God said, *You shall die the death*[^74]: altogether, because a true death reached even to him, that a true life might reach to us; even the curse of death reached to him, that the blessing of life might reach to us. And they cast me away, the beloved, like an abominated dead man.

**27.** [v. 22] *Forsake me not, O Lord my God; depart not from me*[^75]. Let us say it in him, let us say it through him; for he himself intercedes for us: and let us say, *Forsake me not, O Lord my God.* And yet he had said, *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* and he says, *My God, depart not from me.* If he does not withdraw from the body, did he withdraw from the head? Whose voice, then, was it, except the first man's. Therefore, from him, showing himself to bear true flesh, he says, *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* God did not forsake him. If he does not forsake you believing in him, would the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God, forsake Christ? But he had transfigured in himself the person of the first man. We know, the Apostle saying, *that our old man was crucified with him*[^76]. But we would not be rid of our oldness, unless he were crucified in weakness. For he came for this, that we might be renewed in him: because by desiring him and imitating his passion we are renewed. Therefore it was the voice of weakness, it was our voice, by which it was said, *Why have you forsaken me?* Hence there it was said, *The words of my transgressions*: as though he said: These words from the person of the sinner were transfigured into me. *Depart not from me.*

**28.** [v. 23] *Attend to my help, O Lord of my salvation*[^77]. This is the salvation, brothers, the salvation about which the prophets inquired, as the apostle Peter says: and they did not receive who inquired; but they inquired and foretold, and we came and found what they inquired about. And behold, we too have not yet received; and there will be born after us, and they will find what they too will not receive, and will pass over: that all together, at the end of the day, with the patriarchs, and prophets, and apostles, may receive the denarius of salvation. For you know the hired men or laborers led at different times to the vineyard, yet they received the wage alike[^78]. The prophets, then, and the apostles, and the martyrs, and we, and those who will be after us even to the end of the world, at that very end are to receive everlasting salvation; that, contemplating the glory of God, and beholding his face, we may praise him forever, without failing, without any punishment of iniquity, without any perversity of sin, praising God, and no longer sighing, but cleaving to him for whom even to the end we sighed, and in hope we rejoiced. For in that city we shall be, where our good is God, our light is God, our bread is God, our life is God: whatever is our good, away from which, as pilgrims, we labor, in him we shall find it. In him will be the rest, which now, remembering, we must grieve over. For we remember that sabbath, in whose remembrance so great things have been said, and we ought to say so great things, and, saying them, never to be silent, not with the mouth, but with the heart: because we are so silent with the mouth that we may be able to cry out with the heart.
