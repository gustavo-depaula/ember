# On the Same Psalm 118 (9)

**1.** [v. 25.] There follows in this greater psalm what we ought to consider, and, as the Lord has given, to treat thoroughly: *My soul has clung to the pavement; give me life according to your word.* What is it: *My soul has clung to the pavement?* For when he adds and says: *Give me life according to your word;* why he asks to be given life, he certainly set the cause before, saying: *My soul has clung to the pavement.* Therefore, because he asks to be given life for this reason, that his soul clung to the pavement; it is a wonder if he wishes anything good to be understood from this. For this whole sentence is such, as if he said: I am dead; give me life. What, then, is the pavement? If we wish to take the whole universe as it were one certain great house, we see its vault as it were the heaven: the earth, then, will be the pavement. He wishes, then, to be rescued from earthly things, and to say with the Apostle: *Our citizenship is in heaven*[^1]. Accordingly, to cling to earthly things is the death of the soul; to which evil the contrary life is asked, when it is said: *Give me life.*

**2.** But it must be seen whether these words agree with him who above had said such things, by which he seems rather to have clung to God than to the pavement, so that his citizenship was held not in earthly, but in heavenly things. For how can he be understood to have clung to earthly things who says: *But your servant was exercised in your justifications; for both your testimonies are my meditation, and my counsel is your justifications?* For these are his preceding words, of which preceding words these are the consequents: *My soul has clung to the pavement.* Or ought we from this to understand, that, however much anyone advances in the justifications of the Lord, he has the affect of mortal flesh about these earthly things, in which human life is a temptation on earth; and that from this death, if he perseveringly advances, he daily revives, with him giving life by whose grace our inner man is renewed from day to day? For that too, when the Apostle said: *As long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims from the Lord*[^2], and coveted to be dissolved, and to be with Christ; his soul had clung to the pavement. Whence the body itself too, because it is from the earth, is not absurdly understood to be the pavement; over which, because it is still corruptible and weighs down the soul, it is rightly groaned and said to God: *My soul has clung to the pavement, give me life according to your word.* For we shall not always be with the Lord without our bodies; but then, because they will not be corruptible nor weigh down our souls, if we diligently consider, not we will cling to them, but rather they to us, but we to God. Whence those words of another psalm are: *But for me to cling to God is good*[^3]: so that the bodies may live from us, by clinging to us; but we may live from God, because for us *to cling to God is good.* For this clinging, of which it is said: *My soul has clung to the pavement,* signifies to me not the conjunction of flesh and soul, although some have understood that too; but rather the carnal affect of the soul, by which the flesh covets against the spirit. And if this is rightly taken, surely he who says: *My soul has clung to the pavement, give me life according to your word,* does not pray for this, that he be loosed from the body of this death, the death of the body itself intervening; which the last day of this life, which because of its brevity cannot be long-lasting, will at some time do: but that the coveting by which it covets against the spirit be more and more diminished, and the coveting by which it covets against the flesh be more and more increased; until the former be consumed in us, and the latter be consummated through the Holy Spirit who is given to us. Let the Lord teach us his ways.

**3.** [v. 26.] But well he does not say: *Give me life* according to my merit; but he says, *according to your word*: which is nothing else than, according to your promise? He wishes to be a son of the promise, not a son of elation; that according to grace the promise may be firm to all the seed. For this is the word of the promise: *In Isaac shall your seed be called: that is: Not those who are the sons of the flesh, these are the sons of God, but the sons of the promise are reckoned in the seed*[^4]. For what he was through himself, in what follows, he confesses: *My ways I have announced, and you heard me.* Some manuscripts indeed have, *your ways;* but more, and especially the Greek, *my ways,* that is, bad ones. For this seems to me to say: I confessed my sins, and you heard me, that is, that you might forgive them. *Teach me your justifications.* I confessed my ways, you blotted them out; teach me yours. So teach me, that I may do them; not that I may only know what I ought to do. For just as it was said of the Lord that he *did not know sin*[^5], and it is understood, he had not done it; so he too must truly be said to know righteousness who does it. This is the prayer of one making progress. For surely if he did not do it at all, he would not have said those earlier things: *But your servant was exercised in your justifications.* Therefore not those in which he was exercised does he wish to learn from the Lord; but from these he desires, by advancing, as it were by growing, to arrive at others.

**4.** [vv. 27, 28.] Finally he adds and says: *The way of your justifications make known to me;* or, as some manuscripts have, *instruct me*: which is more expressly said from the Greek, *make me understand.* *And I will be exercised in your wonders.* Those greater justifications, which by advancing he desires to lay hold of, he calls the wonders of God. There are, then, certain justifications of God so wonderful that, by human weakness, those who have not experienced them are believed not to be able to arrive at them. Whence this one, laboring, and in a way wearied by their difficulty, adds: *My soul slumbered for weariness; strengthen me in your words.* What is it, *slumbered,* except that it grew cold from the hope by which it had believed it would lay hold of them? But, *strengthen me,* he says, *in your words,* lest from those too at which I now feel myself to have arrived I fall away by slumbering: strengthen me, then, in those your words which I now hold, which I now do, that from them I may be able to tend by advancing to others.

**5.** [v. 29.] And what impedes one from so walking in the way of the justifications of God, that a man may easily be able to arrive even at those wonders? What do we think, except what he asks to be removed from himself in the following, saying: *The way of iniquity remove from me?* And because *the law* of works *entered in, that the offense might abound*[^6]; there follows, and he says: *And by your law have mercy on me.* By what law, except the law of faith? Hear the Apostle: *Where, then, is your boasting? It is excluded. By what law? of works? No; but by the law of faith*[^7]. This is the law of faith, by which we believe and pray that it be given us by grace, that we may do what by ourselves we cannot fulfill; lest, ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish our own, we be not subject to the righteousness of God. In the law of works, then, is the righteousness of God commanding; but in the law of faith, the mercy of him who comes to aid.

**6.** [vv. 30-32.] But when he had said: *And by your law have mercy on me;* from the benefits which he has already obtained, in a way, if it can be said, he prescribes, that he may obtain the rest which he has not yet obtained. For he says: *The way of truth I have chosen; your judgments I have not forgotten. I have clung to your testimonies; O Lord, do not confound me. The way of truth I have chosen,* where I might run: *your judgments I have not forgotten,* that I might run. *I have clung to your testimonies,* while I ran: *O Lord, do not confound me;* let me tend where I run, let me arrive where I tend. For it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy. Finally there follows: *The way of your commandments I ran, when you enlarged my heart.* I would not run, unless you had enlarged my heart. Plainly by this verse is expounded how it was said: *The way of truth I have chosen, your judgments I have not forgotten, I have clung to your testimonies.* For this is the running of the way of the commandments of God. And because he alleges to the Lord his benefits rather than his own merits; as if it were said to him: How did you run this way, by choosing, and not forgetting the judgments of God, and clinging to his testimonies, could you do these things by yourself? No, he says. What, then? *The way of your commandments,* he says, *I ran when you enlarged my heart.* Not, then, by my own, and as if needing no help of yours, choice; but *when you enlarged my heart.* The enlargement of the heart is the delight in righteousness. This is the gift of God, that in his precepts we be not narrowed by the fear of punishment, but enlarged by love, and the delight of righteousness. For this breadth he promises us, saying: *I will dwell in them, and walk about*[^8]. For how broad it is where God walks about! in this breadth the love is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to us. Whence too it was said: *And in your streets let your waters run*[^9]: for the street (platea) took its name from breadth from the Greek word; since in Greek platos is called broad. These are the waters of which the Lord cries: *Whoever thirsts, let him come to me. He who believes in me, rivers of living water will flow from his belly*: and the Evangelist expounding what he said: *But this,* he says, *he said of the Spirit whom those who would believe in him were to receive*[^10]. Many things could be said of this breadth of heart, but the length of this sermon now forbids it.
