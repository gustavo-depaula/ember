# Exposition of Psalm 62

**1.** On account of those who are perhaps still untaught in the name of Christ, because he gathers from everywhere who gave his blood for all, something must be said in few words that both those who know may gladly hear, and those who do not know may learn. These Psalms that we sing, before our Lord Jesus Christ was born of the virgin Mary, were spoken and written down at the dictation of the Spirit of God. For David was king in the nation of the Jews; which nation alone worshiped the one God, who made heaven and earth, the sea and all that is in them, both the things that are seen and the things that are not seen. But the other nations either worshiped idols which they had made with their own hands, or the creation of God, not the Creator himself, that is, either the sun, or the moon, or the stars, or the sea, or the mountains, or trees. For all these God made; and he wishes to be praised in them, not them to be worshiped in his place. Therefore in that very nation of the Jews David was king, of whose seed our Lord Jesus Christ was born of the virgin Mary; because from him the virgin Mary who bore Christ drew her race: and these Psalms were spoken, and in them Christ was prophesied as to come after many years: and there was said by those Prophets, who lived before our Lord Jesus Christ was born of the virgin Mary, whatever was to be in our times; which we now read, and see: and we greatly rejoice that our hope was foretold by the saints, who did not see it fulfilled, but saw it in the spirit as to come: and we now read and hear it from the readers; we discuss those things; and as they are in the Scriptures, so we see them fulfilled throughout the whole world. Who would not rejoice at this? Who would not also hope that the things that have not yet come are to come, on account of those that have already been so greatly fulfilled? For now, brethren, you see that the whole world, the whole earth, all the nations, all the regions run to the name of Christ, and believe in Christ. Surely you see this, how everywhere the vanities of the pagans are overthrown; you see this, it is manifest to you. Surely we do not read this too to you from a book, and it is not done before your eyes? This whole, then, which you see done before your eyes, was written before immense volumes of years through those whom we now read, when we already see these things being fulfilled. But since those things also have been written which have not yet come, that is, that our Lord Jesus Christ is to come to judge, who first came to be judged; for he first came humble, afterward he is to come exalted; he came to show an example of patience, afterward he is to come to judge all according to their merits, whether good or evil: since, then, that which we hope has not yet come, that Christ is to come as judge of the living and the dead, we ought to believe it. Let us believe the little that remains is to come, when we now see so great things that were then to be, now fulfilled. For he is a fool who is unwilling to believe the few things that remain, when he sees so many fulfilled, which were not when they were foretold.

**2.** This psalm, then, is spoken from the person of our Lord Jesus Christ, both of the head and of the members. For that one who was born of Mary, and suffered, and was buried, and rose again, and ascended into heaven, and now sits at the right hand of the Father and intercedes for us, is our head. If he is the head, we are the members: his whole Church which is spread everywhere is his body, of which he himself is the head. But not only the faithful who are now, but also those who were before us, and those who will be after us, even unto the end of the age, all pertain to his body; of which body he himself is the head, who ascended into heaven. Since, then, we now know head and body, he is the head, we the body. When we hear his voice, we ought to hear it both from the head and from the body; because whatever he suffered, we too suffered in him; because what we too suffer, he himself too suffers in us. As, if the head suffers something, can the hand say that it does not suffer? or if the hand suffers something, can the head say that it does not suffer? or if the foot suffers something, can the head say that it does not suffer? When one of our members suffers something, all the members run to come to the aid of the member that suffers. If, then, when he suffered, we suffered in him, and he has now ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father; whatever his Church suffers in the tribulations of this age, in temptations, in necessities, in straits (because it must be so instructed, that it be purged by fire like gold), he himself suffers. We prove this, that we suffered in him, since the Apostle says: *But if you have died with Christ, why do you still, as though living, make decisions about this world*[^1]? Likewise he says: *Because our old man was crucified together with him, that the body of sin might be done away*[^2]. If, then, in him we have died, in him too we have risen again. For the Apostle himself said: *But if you have risen with Christ, relish the things that are above, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God*[^3]. If, then, in him we have died, and in him we have risen again; and he himself dies in us, and rises again in us (for he himself is the unity of head and body); not without reason is his voice also ours, and our voice also his. Let us hear, then, the Psalm, and in it understand Christ speaking.

**3.** [v. 1] This psalm has the title: *For David himself, when he was in the desert of Idumea.*[^4] By the name of Idumea is understood this age. For Idumea was a certain nation of wandering men, where idols were worshiped. This Idumea is not set in a good sense. If it is not set in a good sense, it is to be understood that this life, where we suffer so great labors and where we are subjected to so great necessities, is signified by the name of Idumea. And this is the desert where there is much thirst, and you are now going to hear the voice of one thirsting in the desert. But if we recognize ourselves thirsting, we shall also recognize ourselves drinking. For he who thirsts in this age will be filled in the age to come, the Lord saying: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled*[^5]. Therefore in this age we ought not, as it were, to love feasting. Here we must thirst; elsewhere we shall be fattened. But now, lest we fail in this desert, he sprinkles upon us the dew of his word, and does not let us altogether dry up, that there be no demanding-back of us, but that we so thirst that we drink. But that we may drink, we are sprinkled with some grace of his; yet we thirst. And what does our soul say to God?

**4.** [v. 2] *O God, my God, to you I keep watch from the light.*[^6] What is it to keep watch? Certainly not to sleep. What is it to sleep? There is a sleep of the soul, there is a sleep of the body. The sleep of the body we all ought to have; because if there be no sleep of the body, a man fails, the body itself fails. For our fragile body cannot long sustain the soul watchful and intent on actions: if the soul is long intent on actions, the fragile and earthly body does not contain it, does not sustain it acting perpetually; and it fails, and gives way. Therefore God gave sleep to the body, by which the members of the body are repaired, that they may be able to sustain the watchful soul. But we ought to beware lest our soul itself sleep; for the sleep of the soul is evil. Good is the sleep of the body, by which the health of the body is repaired. But the sleep of the soul is to forget its God. Whatever soul has forgotten its God, sleeps. Therefore the Apostle says to certain ones who had forgotten their God, and were doing, as if in sleep, the ravings of idol-worship. For so are those who worship idols, as those who see vain things in dreams: but if their soul wakes, it understands by whom it was made, and does not worship what it itself made. The Apostle, then, says to certain ones: *Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead; and Christ will enlighten you*[^7]. Surely the Apostle was not awakening one sleeping in body? But he was awakening the sleeping soul, when he awakened it to this, that it be enlightened by Christ. Therefore, according to that watching, this one says: *O God, my God, to you I keep watch from the light.* For you would not keep watch in yourself, unless your light rose, which awakened you from sleep. For Christ enlightens souls, and makes them keep watch: but if he withdraws his light, they fall asleep. For therefore it is said to him in another psalm: *Enlighten my eyes, lest I ever sleep in death*[^8]. Or if, turned away from him, they fall asleep, his light is present to them, and they cannot see, because they sleep. As also one who sleeps in body during the day; the sun has already risen, the day is already warm, and he is as if in the night, because he does not keep watch to see the day already risen: so for certain ones, with Christ already present, with the truth already preached, there is still in them the sleep of the soul. To them, then, you, if you keep watch, will say daily: *Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead; and Christ will enlighten you.* For your life and your morals ought to keep watch in Christ, that others, sleeping pagans, may perceive it, and at the sound of your watching be awakened, and themselves shake off sleep, and begin to say with you in Christ: *O God, my God, to you I keep watch from the light.*

**5.** *My soul has thirsted for you.*[^9] Behold that desert of Idumea. See how he thirsts here: but see what is good here: *It has thirsted for you.* For there are those who thirst, but not for God. Everyone who wishes something furnished to himself is in the ardor of desire: that very desire is the thirst of the soul. And see how many desires there are in the hearts of men: one desires gold, another desires silver, another desires possessions, another inheritances, another ample money, another many flocks, another a great house, another a wife, another honors, another sons. You see these desires, how they are in the hearts of men; all men burn with desire; and one is scarcely found who says: *My soul has thirsted for you.* For men thirst for the age; and do not understand themselves to be in the desert of Idumea, where their soul ought to thirst for God. Let us, then, say: *My soul has thirsted for you;* let us all say it, because in the concord of Christ we are all one soul: let that very soul thirst in Idumea.

**6.** *My soul has thirsted for you*, he says: *in how manifold a way for you my flesh too.*[^10] It is too little that my soul has thirsted; my flesh too has thirsted. Suppose the soul thirsts for God, how does the flesh too thirst for God? For when the flesh thirsts, it thirsts for water; when the soul thirsts, it thirsts for the fountain of wisdom. From that fountain our souls will be inebriated, as another psalm says: *They will be inebriated from the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink from the torrent of your delights*[^11]. Wisdom, then, is to be thirsted for, righteousness is to be thirsted for. We shall not be satisfied with it, we shall not be filled with it, except when this life is finished, and we have come to that which God has promised. For God promised equality with the angels: and now the angels do not thirst as we do, do not hunger as we do; but they have the feasting of truth, of light, of immortal wisdom. Therefore they are blessed; and out of so great blessedness, because they are in that city, the heavenly Jerusalem, from which we are now on pilgrimage, they attend to us pilgrims, and have mercy on us, and at the Lord's command help us, that we may at some time return to that common homeland, and there with them be at some time satisfied from the Lord's fountain of truth and eternity. Now, then, let our soul thirst: whence does our flesh too thirst, and this in a manifold way? *In a manifold way for you*, he says, *my flesh too.* Because to our flesh too the resurrection is promised. As to our soul blessedness is promised, so to our flesh too the resurrection is promised. Such a resurrection of the flesh is promised to us: hear, and learn, and hold what the hope of Christians is, why we are Christians. For we are not Christians for this, that we should ask for ourselves earthly happiness, which very often even robbers and the wicked have. For another happiness we are Christians, which we shall then receive, when all this life of this age has passed. Therefore the resurrection of the flesh too is promised to us; and such a resurrection of the flesh is promised to us, that this very flesh which we now carry will rise in the end. And let it not seem incredible to you. For if God made us who were not, is it a great thing for him to restore us who were? Therefore let it not seem incredible to you, because you see the dead, as it were, rot, and go into ashes and into dust. Or if someone dead be burned, or if dogs tear him apart, do you think that he is therefore not going to rise from there? All things that are torn apart and rot into certain ashes are whole to God. For they go into those elements of the world whence they first came, when we were made: we do not see them; but yet God will produce them from whence he knows, because even before we were, he produced us from whence he knew. Such a resurrection of the flesh, then, is promised to us, that although it be the very flesh which we now carry that is to rise, yet it will not have the corruption that it now has. For now, from the corruption of frailty, if we do not eat, we fail and hunger; if we do not drink, we fail and thirst; if we keep watch long, we fail and sleep: if we sleep long, we fail; therefore we keep watch: if we eat and drink long; although we eat and drink for refreshment, that very prolonged refreshment is a failing: if we stand long, we are wearied; therefore we sit: and if we sit long, we are wearied there too, and therefore we rise. Then see that there is no settled state of our flesh: since infancy flies away into boyhood; and you seek infancy, and there is no infancy, because now in place of infancy there is boyhood: again that too passes into youth; you seek boyhood, and do not find it: the youth becomes a young man; you seek the youth, and he is not: the young man becomes an old man; you seek the young man, and do not find him: and the old man dies; you seek the old man, and do not find him. Our age, then, does not stand still: everywhere is weariness, everywhere lassitude, everywhere corruption. Attending to what hope of resurrection God promises us, amid all these our manifold failings we thirst for that incorruption; and so our flesh thirsts for God in a manifold way. In this Idumea, in this desert, in how manifold a way it labors, in so manifold a way it thirsts; in how manifold a way it is wearied, in so manifold a way it thirsts for that untiring incorruption.

**7.** Although, my brethren, the flesh of a good and faithful Christian thirsts for God even in this age: because if the flesh has need of bread, if it has need of water, if it has need of wine, if it has need of a coin, if this flesh has need of a beast of burden, it ought to ask from God, not from demons, and idols, and certain powers of this age, I know not what. For there are those who, when they suffer hunger in this age, abandon God, and ask Mercury, or ask Jupiter to give them, or her whom they call the Heavenly One, or some similar demons: their flesh does not thirst for God. But those who thirst for God ought to thirst on every side, both in soul and in flesh: because to the soul God gives his bread, that is, the word of truth; and to the flesh God gives what is necessary, because God made both the soul and the flesh. For the sake of your flesh you ask demons: surely God did not make the soul, and demons made your flesh? He who made the soul, he himself made the flesh too: he who made both things, he himself feeds both things. Let both of us thirst for God, and from manifold labor be satisfied in a simple way.

**8.** [v. 3] But where does our soul thirst, and our flesh in a manifold way, not for anyone, but for you, O Lord, that is, for our God; where does it thirst? *In a deserted land, and without way, and without water.*[^12] We said it was this age; this is Idumea, this is the desert of Idumea, whence the Psalm received its title. *In a deserted land.* It is too little, *deserted*, where no man dwells; in addition, *and without way, and without water.* Would that the desert itself at least had a way! would that a man running there at least knew by what way to go out from there! He sees no man for comfort, he sees no way by which to be free of the desert. So there he turns aside. Would that there were at least water, from which he might be refreshed, who cannot go out from there! An evil desert, horrible and to be feared! and yet God had mercy on us, and made for us a way in the desert, our Lord Jesus Christ himself; and made for us a consolation in the desert, by sending to us the preachers of his word; and gave us water in the desert, by filling his preachers with the Holy Spirit, that there might be made in them a fountain of water springing up unto eternal life. And behold we have here all things; but they are not of the desert. Therefore the Psalm first commended the property of the desert, that you too, when you had heard in what evil you were, if you could have here any consolations, whether of companions, or of a way, or of water, might attribute them not to the desert, but to him who deigned to visit you in the desert.

**9.** *So in the holy place I have appeared to you, that I might see your power and your glory.*[^13] First my soul thirsted, and my flesh in a manifold way for you in the desert, and in a land without way, and without water; and *so in the holy place I have appeared to you, that I might see your power and your glory.* Unless one first thirst in this desert, that is, in the evil in which he is, he never comes to the good which is God. But *I have appeared to you*, he says, *in the holy place.* Now in the holy place there is great consolation. *I have appeared to you*, what is it? That you might see me: and therefore you have seen me that I might see you. *I have appeared to you, that I might see.* He did not say: I have appeared to you, that you might see; but: *I have appeared to you, that I might see your power and your glory.* Whence the Apostle too: *But now*, he says, *knowing God, or rather known by God*[^14]. For first you appeared to God, that God could appear to you. *That I might see your power, and your glory.* Truly in this desert, that is, in this wilderness, if a man should demand, as it were from the wilderness, what is for his salvation, he will never see the power of the Lord, and the glory of the Lord; but will remain about to die of thirst; nor will he find a way, nor consolation, nor water by which to last in the wilderness. But when he has raised himself up to God, so as to say to him from all his inmost marrow: *My soul has thirsted for you: in how manifold a way for you my flesh too*; lest perhaps he ask the necessities of the flesh from others and not from God, or not desire that resurrection of the flesh which God promises us: when, then, he has raised himself up, he will have no small consolations.

**10.** Behold, brethren, how our flesh, as long as it is mortal, as long as it is frail, before that resurrection has these consolations by which we live, bread, water, fruits, wine, oil (if all these consolations and supports forsake us, we certainly cannot last), although this flesh has not yet received that perfect health, where it will suffer no strait, no want: so too our soul, when it is here in this flesh, amid the temptations and perils of this age, is still weak; it too has the consolations of the word, the consolations of prayer, the consolations of discussion: these things are to our soul as those are to our flesh. But when our flesh has risen again, so that we no longer need these things, it will have a certain place and state of its incorruption: so too our soul will have its food, the very Word of God, through which all things were made. Yet thanks be to God, who now in this wilderness does not forsake us; whether by giving us what is necessary for the flesh, or by giving us what is necessary for the soul: and when he instructs us by certain necessities, he wishes us to love him the more; lest perhaps we be corrupted by feasting, and forget him. He withdraws from us sometimes what is necessary, and wears us down; that we may know that he is Father and Lord, not only coaxing, but also scourging. For he prepares us for a certain incorruptible and great inheritance. If you intend to leave to your son one cask, or one cellar of yours, or whatever you have in your house, and lest he lose it, you instruct him, and correct him with scourges unto discipline, lest he lose anything of yours, which he too is here going to leave behind, as you too are; do you not wish that our Father instruct us with the scourges even of necessities or tribulations, who is going to give us such an inheritance, which cannot pass away? For God will give us himself as an inheritance, that we may possess him, and be possessed by him forever.

**11.** Therefore in the holy place let us appear to God, that he may appear to us; let us appear to him in holy desire, that he may appear to us in the power and glory of the Son of God. For to many he has not appeared; let them be in the holy place, that he may appear to them too. For many think he was only a man, because he is preached as born of man, crucified and dead, as having walked on earth, eaten and drunk, and done the other things that are human: and they think him to have been such as the rest of men are. But you heard just now, when the Gospel was read, how he commended his majesty: *I and the Father are one*[^15]. Behold how great a majesty, and how great an equality of the Father descended to the flesh on account of our weakness! Behold how greatly we were loved, before we loved God! If, before we loved God, we were so greatly loved by him that he made his Son, equal to himself, a man for our sake; what does he reserve for us who now love him? Therefore many think that the Son of God appeared on earth as something very small, I know not what; because they are not in the holy place, his power does not appear to them, and his glory: that is, because they do not yet have a sanctified heart, whereby they might understand the eminence of his power, and give thanks to God because for their sake one so great came whither he came, to such a birth, to such a suffering; they cannot see his glory and his power.

**12.** [v. 4] *Because your mercy is better than lives.*[^16] Human lives are many; but God promises one life; and he gives it to us not as it were on account of our merits, but on account of his mercy. For what good have we done, that we should merit it? or what good deeds of ours went before, that God should give us his grace? Did he find righteous acts to crown, and not offenses to forgive? Surely the offenses which he forgave, if he willed to punish them, it would not be unjust. For what is so just, as that the sinner be punished? Since it is just that the sinner be punished, it pertained to his mercy not to punish the sinner, but to justify him, and out of a sinner to make him just, and out of an ungodly one to make him godly. Therefore his *mercy is better than lives.* What lives? Those which men have chosen for themselves. One has chosen for himself the life of trading, another the life of farming, another the life of lending at interest, another the life of soldiering; one this, another that. The lives are diverse, but *your mercy is better than* our *lives.* Better is what you give to the corrected than what the perverse choose. You give one life, which is to be preferred to all our lives, whatever we could have chosen in the world. *Because your mercy is better than lives, my lips will praise you.* My lips would not praise you, unless your mercy went before me. By your gift I praise you, through your mercy I praise you. For I could not praise God, unless he gave me to be able to praise him. *Because your mercy is better than lives, my lips will praise you.*

**13.** [v. 5] *So I will bless you in my life, and in your name I will lift up my hands.*[^17] *So I will bless you in my life*: now in my life which you have given me; not in that which I chose according to the world with the rest, among many lives, but which you gave me through your mercy, that I might praise you. *So I will bless you in my life.* What is, *so*? That I attribute to your mercy my life in which I praise you; not to my merits. *And in your name I will lift up my hands.* Lift up your hands, then, in prayer. Our Lord lifted up his hands for us on the cross, and his hands were stretched out for us. Therefore his hands were stretched out on the cross, that our hands may be stretched out unto good works; because his cross furnished us mercy. Behold, he lifted up his hands, and offered for us himself as a sacrifice to God, and through that sacrifice all our sins were blotted out. Let us too lift up our hands to God in prayer; and our hands lifted up to God will not be confounded, if they are exercised in good works. For what does he do who lifts up his hands? Whence is it commanded that we pray to God with hands lifted up? For the Apostle says: *Lifting up pure hands, without anger and disputing*[^18]. So that when you lift up your hands to God, your works may come to mind. For because these hands are lifted up that you may obtain what you wish, you think to exercise those very hands in good works, lest they blush to be lifted up to God. *In your name I will lift up my hands.* These prayers of ours are in this Idumea, in this desert, in a land without water, and without way, where Christ is for us the way; but not a way of this earth. *In your name I will lift up my hands.*

**14.** [v. 6] And what shall I say, when I lift up my hands in your name? what shall I ask? Come now, brethren, when you lift up your hands, seek what you ask; for you ask from the Almighty. Ask something great; not such things as those ask who have not yet believed. You see what kind of things are given even to the ungodly. Are you going to ask money from your God? Surely he gives it even to the wicked who do not believe in him? What great thing, then, are you going to ask, which he gives even to the wicked? But let it not displease you that the things he gives even to the wicked are so trifling that they are worthy to be given even to the wicked: lest the things that can be given even to the wicked seem to you, as it were, great. All earthly gifts indeed are God's; but see that the things he gives even to the wicked are not to be held for great. There is something else that he reserves for us. But let us consider the things he gives even to the wicked, and from this understand what he reserves for the good. See the things he gives to the wicked: he gives them this light; see that both the good and the evil see it: the rain that descends upon the earth; and from this how great goods are born! And it is furnished thence both to the evil and to the good, the Gospel saying: *Who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil; and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^19]. These gifts, then, that are born either from the rain or from the sun, we ought indeed to ask from our Lord, since they are necessary; but not these alone, because these are given both to the good and to the evil. What, then, ought we to ask, when we lift up our hands? Because the Psalm said it as it could. What is it I said, as it could? As a human mouth could to human ears. Because through human mouths these things were said, and they were said through certain likenesses, such as all the weak, all the little ones could grasp. What did it say? what did it ask? *In your name*, he says, *I will lift up my hands.* What about to receive? *As with fatness and richness let my soul be filled.* Do you think this soul desired some fatness of the flesh, my brethren? For it did not for a great thing desire this, that fat rams, that those fat pigs be slaughtered; or that it might come to some cookshop, where it might find fat viands, whence it might be satisfied. If we believe this, we are worthy to hear these things. Therefore we ought to understand something spiritual. Our soul has a certain fatness. There is a certain fullness of rich wisdom. For souls that lack this wisdom wither away; and become altogether so thin that in all good works they quickly fail. Why do they quickly fail in all good works? Because they do not have the fatness of their fullness. Hear the Apostle speaking of a fat soul, instructing each one to do good. What does he say? *God loves a cheerful giver*[^20]. Whence would a fat soul be fat, unless it were filled by the Lord? And yet however fat it be here, what will it be in that future age, where God will feed us? Meanwhile in this pilgrimage, what we shall be then cannot even be said. And perhaps we desire that very fullness here, when we lift up our hands, where we shall be so filled with fatness that all our want will utterly perish, and we shall desire nothing: because everything will be ready for us, whatever we here desire, whatever we here for a great thing love. Now our fathers are dead; but God lives: we could not have fathers here always; but there we shall always have one living Father, having our homeland: whatever is earthly, we cannot always be there; and it is necessary that others be born, and to this end the children of those citizens are born, that they may shut out from there their parents. For to this end a boy is born, that he may say to his elder: What are you doing here? It is necessary that those who succeed, and those who are born, shut out those who went before them. There we shall all live alike; there will be no successor there because no predecessor. What kind is that homeland? But do you love riches here? God himself will be your riches. But do you love a good fountain? What is more glorious than that wisdom? what more luminous? Whatever can be loved here, he will be to you in place of all things, who made all things. *As with fatness and richness let my soul be filled; and lips of exultation will praise your name.* In this wilderness, in your name I will lift up my hands: *let my soul be filled as with fatness and richness; and lips of exultation will praise your name.* For now there is prayer, as long as thirst lasts; when thirst has passed, prayer passes, and praise succeeds: *And lips of exultation will praise your name.*

**15.** [vv. 7-8] *If I have remembered you upon my bed, in the dawns I meditated on you; because you have become my helper.*[^21] By his bed he means his quiet. When anyone is at quiet, let him be mindful of God; when anyone is at quiet, let him not be dissolved through quiet and forget God; if he is mindful of God when he is at quiet, in his actions he meditates on God. For he called actions "the dawn," because every man at dawn begins to do something. What, then, did he say? *If I was mindful of you on my bed, then in the dawns I meditated on you.* If, then, I was not mindful on my bed, then in the dawn I did not meditate on you. He who, when he is idle, does not think of God, can he think of God in his actions? But he who is mindful of him when he is at quiet, meditates on him when he acts, lest he fail in his action. Therefore what did he add? *And in the dawns I meditated on you; because you have become my helper.* For unless God helps our good works, they cannot be fulfilled by us. And we ought to do worthy things; that is, as it were in the light, when we work with Christ showing the way. Whoever works evil, works in the night, not in the dawn, the Apostle saying: *Those who are drunk, are drunk by night; and those who sleep, sleep by night: but we who are of the day, let us be sober.* He exhorts us to walk honestly according to the day: *As in the day*, he says, *let us walk honestly*[^22]. And again: *You*, he says, *are sons of light, and sons of the day; we are not of the night nor of darkness*[^23]. Who are the sons of the night, and the sons of darkness? Those who do all evil things. So far are they sons of the night, that they fear that what they do be seen: and the evils they do publicly, they do publicly for this reason, that many do them; the things few do, they do in secret. But those who do such things publicly are indeed in the light of the sun, but in the darkness of the heart. No one, then, works in the dawn, except one who works in Christ. But he who, when idle, is mindful of Christ, meditates on him in all his actions; and he is to him a helper in good work, lest through his weakness he fail. *If I have remembered you upon my bed, in the dawns I meditated on you; because you have become my helper.*

**16.** *And in the covering of your wings I will exult.*[^24] I grow cheerful in good works, because over me is the covering of your wings. If you do not protect me, because I am a chick, the kite will snatch me. For the Lord himself says in a certain place to Jerusalem, that certain city where he was crucified: *Jerusalem*, he says, *Jerusalem, how often I have willed to gather your children, as a hen her chicks, and you would not*[^25]! We are little ones: therefore let God protect us under the shade of his wings. What when we have become greater? It is good for us that even then he protect us, that under that greater one we may always be his chicks. For he is always greater, however much we grow. Let no one say: Let him protect me, while I am a little one: as though one could at some time come to so great a greatness that it should suffice for itself. Without the protection of God you are nothing. Let us always wish to be protected by him: then we shall always be able to be great in him, if we are always little under him. *And in the covering of your wings I will exult.*

**17.** [v. 9] *My soul has clung after you.*[^26] See one desiring, see one thirsting, see how he cleaves to God. Let this affection be born in you. If it now sprouts, let it be watered, and grow: let it come to such strength that you too may say from your whole heart: *My soul has clung after you.* Where is the glue itself? The glue itself is love. Have love, by which glue your soul may cling after God. Not with God, but after God; that he may go before, you follow. For he who wishes to go before God wishes to live by his own counsel, and is unwilling to follow the precepts of God. Therefore Peter too was repelled, when he wished to give counsel to Christ who was to suffer for us. For Peter was still weak, and did not know how great would be the benefit to the human race in the blood of Christ: but the Lord, who had come to redeem us, and to give as a price for us his blood, began to preach his suffering. Peter was alarmed at the Lord as though about to die, whom he wished to live here always as he saw him, because, given over to fleshly eyes, he was held by a fleshly affection about the Lord; and he said to him: *Far be it from you, Lord; be merciful to yourself.* And the Lord: *Get behind me, Satan; for you do not relish the things of God, but the things of men*[^27]. Why, *the things of men*? Because you wish to go before me, *get behind me,* that you may follow me: so that, now following Christ, he might say: *My soul has clung after you.* Deservedly he joins: *Your right hand has upheld me. My soul has clung after you; your right hand has upheld me.* Christ said this in us: that is, in the man whom he bore for us, whom he offered for us, he said this. The Church too says this in Christ, says it in its head; because it too suffered here great persecutions, and even now suffers them individually. For who pertaining to Christ is not agitated by various temptations, and daily the devil and his angels deal with him, that he be perverted by some desire, some suggestion, either by the promise of gain, or the terror of loss, or the promise of life, or the terror of death, or the enmities of some powerful one, or the friendships of some powerful one? In all ways the devil presses, how he may cast one down; and we live amid persecutions, and have perpetual enemies, the devil and his angels: but let us not fear. The devil and his angels are as it were kites: we are under the wings of that hen, and he cannot touch us; for the hen who protects us is strong. She is weak for our sake; but strong in himself is our Lord Jesus Christ, the very Wisdom of God. Therefore the Church too says this: *My soul has clung after you; your right hand has upheld me.*

**18.** [v. 10] *But they themselves sought my soul in vain.*[^28] What did they do to me who sought to destroy my soul? Would that they sought my soul, to believe with me! But they sought my soul to destroy me. What about to do? For they were not about to snatch away the glue, by which my soul clung after you. *For who will separate us from the love of Christ? tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or hunger, or nakedness, or the sword*[^29]? *Your right hand has upheld me.* Therefore on account of that glue and on account of your most powerful right hand, *they sought my soul in vain.* Whoever have persecuted, or desire to persecute the Church, of these this can be understood: but chiefly let us take this of the Jews, who sought to destroy the soul of Christ, both in our very head whom they crucified, and in his disciples whom they afterward persecuted. *They sought my soul. They will go into the lower parts of the earth.* They were unwilling to lose the earth, that they might crucify Christ: into the lower parts of the earth they went. What are *the lower parts of the earth*? Earthly desires. It is better to walk in the flesh upon the earth, than to go by desire beneath the earth. For everyone who, against his own salvation, desires earthly things, is beneath the earth: because he set the earth before himself, set the earth above himself, and made himself beneath. They, then, fearing to lose the earth, what did they say of the Lord Jesus Christ, when they saw many crowds going after him, because he did wonders? *If we let him live, the Romans will come, and take from us both our place and our nation*[^30]. They feared to lose the earth, and went beneath the earth: there befell them also what they feared. For therefore they willed to kill Christ, lest they lose the earth; and therefore they lost the earth, because they killed Christ. For Christ being killed, because the Lord himself had said to them: *The kingdom will be taken from you, and given to a nation doing righteousness*[^31], great calamities of persecutions followed them. The Roman emperors and the kings of the nations conquered them: they were shut out from that very place where they crucified Christ; and now that place is full of Christian praisers, has no Jew; it is rid of the enemies of Christ, filled with the praisers of Christ. Behold, they lost the place to the Romans, because they killed Christ, who killed him for this reason, lest they lose the place to the Romans. Therefore, *they will go into the depths of the earth.*

**19.** [v. 11] *They will be handed over into the hands of the sword.*[^32] Truly so it befell them visibly; they were assailed by the enemies rushing in. *They will be the portions of foxes.* By foxes he means the kings of the age, who lived then when Judea was subdued. Hear, that you may know and understand that he calls them foxes. King Herod the Lord himself called a fox: *Go*, he says, *and tell that fox*[^33]. See, and attend, my brethren: they were unwilling to have Christ as king, and were made the portions of foxes. For when Pilate the governor, the legate in Judea, killed Christ at the voices of the Jews, he said to those very Jews: *Shall I crucify your king?* Because he was called king of the Jews, and he himself was the true king. And they, rejecting Christ, said: *We have no king but Caesar*[^34]. They rejected the lamb; they chose the fox; deservedly they were made the portions of foxes.

**20.** [v. 12] *But the king*[^35]: it is set thus for this reason, because they chose the fox, but were unwilling to have the king. *But the king*: that is, the true king, for whom the title was inscribed when he suffered. For Pilate set this title, inscribed above his head, *King of the Jews*, in the Hebrew tongue, the Greek, and the Latin: that all who passed by might read the glory of the king, and the ignominy of the Jews themselves, who, rejecting the true king, chose the fox Caesar. *But the king will rejoice in God.* They were made the portions of foxes: *But the king will rejoice in God.* He whom they seemed to themselves as it were to have overcome when they crucified him, then, crucified, poured out the price by which he bought the world. *But the king will rejoice in God: praised will be everyone who swears by him.* Why, *praised will be everyone who swears by him*? Because he chose for himself Christ, not the fox; because when those Jews insulted him, then he gave that whereby we might be redeemed. To him, then, we pertain who redeemed us, who for us conquered the world, not with an armed soldier, but with a mocked cross. *But the king will rejoice in God: praised will be everyone who swears by him.* Who swears by him? He who promises to him his life, who vows to him and pays it, who becomes a Christian: this is what he says: *Praised will be everyone who swears by him. Because the mouth of those speaking unjust things has been stopped up.* How great unjust things did the Jews speak? How great evils did they say, not only the Jews, but also all who on account of idols persecuted Christians? When they raged against Christians, they thought they could make an end of Christians: when they thought they could make an end of them, the Christians grew, and they themselves were ended. *The mouth of those speaking unjust things has been stopped up.* No one now dares to speak publicly against Christ: now all fear Christ: *Because the mouth of those speaking unjust things has been stopped up.* When the Lamb was in weakness, even foxes dared against the Lamb. The Lion of the tribe of Judah conquered, and the foxes were silent: *Because the mouth of those speaking unjust things has been stopped up.*
