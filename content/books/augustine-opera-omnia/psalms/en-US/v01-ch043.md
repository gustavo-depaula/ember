# On the Same Psalm 33 (32) (2)

**1.** Both in announcing and in hearing the word of truth there is labor. This labor, brothers, we bear with an even mind, if we remember the Lord's sentence and our own condition. For from the very beginning of our race, man heard—not from a deceitful man, nor from the seducing devil, but from Truth itself, out of the mouth of God: *In the sweat of your face you shall eat your bread*[^1]. Accordingly, if our bread is the word of God, let us sweat in hearing, lest we die in fasting. A few verses of the first parts of this psalm were treated in the solemnity of the Vigils lately held: let us hear what remains.

**2.** [vv. 5-6] Here begins the part that remains, which we have just now sung. *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord. By the word of the Lord the heavens were made firm.* For it is the same thing, By the speech of the Lord the heavens were made solid. He had said above: *Sing to him well in jubilation,* that is, sing ineffably: *For the word of the Lord is upright, and all his works are done in faith.* He promises nothing that he does not render: he has been made a faithful debtor, be you a greedy exactor. Then, when he had said: *All his works are done in faith;* he added why: *He loves mercy and judgment.* He, then, who loves mercy, shows mercy. And he who shows mercy, can he promise and not give, who could give even if he did not promise? Therefore, because he loves mercy, he must show what he promises: and because he loves judgment, he must exact what he gave. Hence the Lord himself says to a certain servant: *You should have given out my money, and I, coming, would have exacted it with interest*[^2]. We admonish you of this, so that we may know what we have just now heard. For he himself says in another place in the Gospel: *I judge no one: the word that I have spoken to them, it will judge them on the last day*[^3]. Nor let him excuse himself who does not wish to hear, as if there were nothing to be exacted of him. For this very thing is exacted, that he refused to receive it when it was given. For it is one thing not to be able to receive, another to be unwilling: in the one there is the excuse of necessity, in the other the guilt of will. Therefore, *all his works are done in faith: he loves mercy and judgment.* Receive mercy, and fear judgment; lest, when he comes to exact of us, he so exact of us that he sends us away empty. For he exacts an account; the account rendered, he grants eternity. Receive mercy, then, brothers, let us all receive. Let none of us sleep in receiving, lest he be roused to ill for the rendering. Receive mercy: thus God cries out to us, as if in time of famine it were said: Receive grain. And when you heard this in time of famine, surely, spurred by the very goad of need, you would run, turning yourself this way and that, you would seek whence to receive what was said: Receive. And when you found it, when would you hold yourself back? what delay would you interpose? So now too it is said: Receive mercy. *For he loves mercy and judgment.* When you have received it, use it well; that you may render a good account, when his judgment comes who now grants you mercy beforehand in this famine.

**3.** [v. 5] So do not say to me: Where do I receive it? where do I go? Recall what you sang: *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord.* Where is the Gospel not now preached? where is the word of the Lord silent? where does salvation cease? It is needful that you be willing to receive: the storehouses are full. This very fullness and abundance did not await you as you came, but it itself came to you as you slept. It was not said: Let the nations rise and go to one place: but these things were announced to the nations where they were, so that thence might be fulfilled the prophecy saying: *Each one from his own place shall adore him*[^4].

**4.** [v. 6] *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord.* What of the heavens? Hear what of the heavens. For they do not need mercy, where there is no misery. On earth the misery of man abounds, the mercy of the Lord superabounds: the earth is full of the misery of man, and the earth is full of the mercy of the Lord. The heavens, then, where there is no misery, do they, because they do not need mercy, not need the Lord? All things need the Lord, both the miserable and the happy. Without him the miserable is not lifted up, without him the happy is not governed. Therefore, lest perhaps you should ask about the heavens, when you heard: *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord,* hear that the heavens too need the Lord: *By the speech of the Lord the heavens were made solid.* For they were not a foundation to themselves of themselves, nor did the heavens furnish their own firmness to themselves. *By the word of the Lord the heavens were made firm, and all their power by the Spirit of his mouth.* They had nothing of themselves, and they received, as it were a supplement, from the Lord. For *by the Spirit of his mouth* is not a part, but *all their power.*

**5.** Mark well, brothers, the same works of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. For it is not to be passed over carelessly, because of certain wicked dividers and turbulent confounders. For both are done badly. Those men, by dividing badly, confound the creature with the Creator; and although the Spirit of God is the Creator, they number him among creatures. They divide, and they confound: let them be confounded, that they may be converted. Hear now the one work of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. The Word of God is surely the Son, and the Spirit of his mouth is the Holy Spirit. *By the word of the Lord the heavens were made solid.* But what is it to be made solid, except to have a stable and firm power? *And all their power by the Spirit of his mouth.* It could also be said thus: By the Spirit of his mouth the heavens were made solid, and by the word of the Lord all their power. For what is *all their power,* this is *they were made solid.* This, then, the Son and the Holy Spirit do. Is it without the Father? Who, then, does it through his Word and his Spirit, except he whose the Word is, and whose the Spirit? This Trinity, then, is one God. Him he adores who knows how to adore, him he has everywhere who has turned to him. For he is not sought by those turned away; but he himself calls those turned away, that he may fill those who are converted.

**6.** Truly, brothers, leaving aside those higher heavens unknown to us as we labor on earth and seek after these things as best we can by human conjectures—leaving aside, then, those heavens, how they are set one above another, or how many they are, or in what ways they are distinguished, with what inhabitants they are filled, by what arrangement they are governed, how there one unfailing hymn, sung in concert by all, proclaims God—it is much for us to find these things out, yet we are busy to attain it. For there is our homeland, which perhaps in a long pilgrimage we have forgotten. For ours is the voice in that psalm: *Woe is me, that my pilgrimage has been made long!*[^5] So of those heavens it is hard even for me to discourse, if indeed not impossible, and for you to understand. Surely, let one who outstrips me in understanding these matters enjoy where he has gone before, and pray for me that I may follow. Meanwhile, leaving aside those heavens, I have some of which I may here discourse as best I can—the heavens nearest to us, the holy Apostles of God, preachers of the word of truth, through which heavens we have been rained upon, so that throughout the whole world the crop of the Church might sprout up; though for now, drinking the common rain together with the tares, yet not destined to have the threshing-floor in common.

**7.** So when it had been said: *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord*: as if you asked: From where has the earth been filled with the mercy of the Lord? First the heavens were sent, to scatter the mercy of the Lord over the earth, and this over the whole earth. For see what is said elsewhere of these heavens: *The heavens declare the glory of God, and the firmament announces the works of his hands.* What the heavens, that the firmament. *Day to day pours forth speech, and night to night announces knowledge.* There is no ceasing, there is no silence. But where did they preach, and how far did they reach? *There are no tongues, nor speeches, whose voices are not heard.* But this pertains to the fact that they spoke in the tongues of all in one place. Speaking in the tongues of all, they fulfilled what was said: *There are no tongues nor speeches whose voices are not heard.* But I ask, how far did that voice in all tongues reach, which fulfilled this. Hear, then, what follows: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^6]. Whose, except the heavens that declare the glory of God? Therefore, if their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world, what they preached to us, let him show us who sent them. He shows it plainly, he shows it faithfully: for even before they came to pass, he foretold things to come—he whose works are all done in faith. For he rose from the dead, and, when his members had been handled and he was recognized by his disciples, he said: *It behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name.* From where, and how far? *Among all nations,* he says, *beginning at Jerusalem*[^7]. And what greater mercy do we all await from the Lord, brothers, than that our sins be forgiven? Since, then, this is the great mercy of the Lord, the remission of sins, and this remission of sins the Lord foretold would be preached among all nations. *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord.* From where is the earth full? Of the mercy of the Lord. Why? Because everywhere God forgives sins, because he sent the heavens to rain upon the earth.

**8.** And how did these heavens themselves dare to go with confidence, from weak men to become heavens, except because *by the word of the Lord the heavens were made solid?* For from where would sheep among wolves have so great a power, except because *by the Spirit of his mouth is all their power?* *Behold,* he says, *I send you as sheep into the midst of wolves*[^8]. O most merciful Lord! Surely you do this, that the earth may be full of your mercy. If, then, you are so merciful that you fill the earth with mercy, see whom you send, see where you send them. Where, I say, do you send, and whom do you send? Sheep into the midst of wolves. If one wolf be sent into the midst of innumerable sheep, who resists it? What does it not throw into confusion, unless perhaps because it is quickly sated? For otherwise it would devour all. You send the weak among the savage? I send them, he says, because they become heavens, to rain upon the earth. From where are weak men heavens? But *by the Spirit of his mouth is all their power.* Behold, the wolves will seize you, and hand you over, and bring you before the powers for my name's sake. Now arm yourselves. By your own power? Far be it. *Do not think what you shall speak: for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^9]: because *by the Spirit of his mouth is all their power.*

**9.** These things came to pass; the Apostles were sent, they bore pressures. Do we now bear as great pressures to hear these things, as they bore to sow them? No. Will our labor, then, brothers, be fruitless? No. I see your crowding, but you too see our sweat. If we endure, we shall also reign together. Behold, those things came to pass. From those sheep sent into the midst of wolves we also celebrate the memorials of the martyrs. This place, when the body of the blessed martyr was struck down, was full of wolves: one captured sheep conquered so many wolves, and the slain sheep filled the place with sheep. The sea then raged with the huge waves of persecutors, the sky of God was going forth onto the dry and thirsty land. But now, through the things that those endured who broke the battle-line, the name of Christ has been glorified: he occupied even the powers themselves, walking upon the heads of the swelling floods. And because these things came to pass, now too those who see, not yet believing, our assemblies, celebrations, solemnities, the now manifest and public praises of our God—do you think they do not grieve, do you think they do not rage? But now is fulfilled what was said of them: *The sinner shall see, and be angry.* What then, because he will be angry? Do not fear the wolf, O sheep. Now do not fear their threats and ragings. He is angry: but what follows? *He shall gnash with his teeth, and waste away*[^10].

**10.** [vv. 7-9] Because, then, the salt water of the sea that has remained now does not dare to rage against Christians, but a hidden murmur gnaws within it, and the brine, shut up within its mortal skin, frets; see what follows: *Gathering the waters of the sea as in a wineskin.* So when before the sea raged with free waves, but now is shut bitter within mortal breasts, he did it who conquered in those men, who then set bounds to the sea, that its waves, returning upon themselves, should be broken small. He himself gathered the water of the sea as into a wineskin: the mortal skin covers the bitter thought. For, fearing for their skin, they hold within what they do not dare to send forth. For the bitterness is the same: thus they hate, thus they detest. But what then raged openly, now rages secretly; what else shall I say than what was said: *He gnashes, and wastes away?* Let the Church go forth, then, let it walk: the way has been made, our road has been paved by the emperor. Let us be fervent in the paths of good works, for this is our walking. And if at any time the pressures of temptations arise from where they were not expected, now that the waters of the sea are gathered as into a wineskin, let us understand that the Lord does this for discipline, that he may shake from us a security that wrongly trusts in temporal things, and may direct us into his kingdom with desire well-ordered. This desire is drawn out by tribulations pounding it on this side and that, so that we may be tuneful to the ears of the Lord like ductile trumpets. For this too is said in the Psalms, that we praise God on ductile cymbals[^11]. The ductile trumpet is drawn out by the hammer; thus the Christian heart is stretched toward God by the blows of pressures.

**11.** Let us remember, then, brothers, that even in these times, in which the water of the sea has been gathered as into a wineskin, God does not lack the means to bring forth that whereby he may amend us, when we have need of amendment. For so it follows: *Setting the depths in storehouses.* By the storehouses of God he means the secret of God. He knows the hearts of all, what he should bring forth at the time, from where he should bring it, how great a power he should grant to the evil over the good, to judge the evil indeed, but to instruct the good. He knows how to do these things, who sets the depths in storehouses. So let what follows come to pass: *Let all the earth fear the Lord.* Let it not glory in a proud joy, with reckless exultation, saying: Now the water of the sea has been gathered as into a wineskin; who will do anything to me? who will dare to harm me? Do you not know that he has set the depths in storehouses, do you not know from where he may bring forth what is needful to scourge you, your Father? He who, indeed, for your discipline has the storehouses of the deep, with which to instruct you toward the storehouses of the heavens. Return, then, to fear, you who were going into security. Let the earth exult, but also fear. Let it exult: why? Because the earth is full of the mercy of the Lord. Let it fear: why? Because he so gathered the waters of the sea into a wineskin that he might set the depths in storehouses. So in the earth there comes to pass what is elsewhere said briefly of both: *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling*[^12].

**12.** *Let all the earth fear the Lord: and let all who inhabit the world be moved by him.* Let them not fear another in his stead: *let all who inhabit the world be moved by him.* Does a wild beast rage? Fear God. Does a serpent lie in wait? Fear God. Does a man hate you? Fear God. Does the devil assail you? Fear God. For the whole creation is under him whom you are bidden to fear. *For he spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created.* This follows in the psalm. For when he had said: *Let all who inhabit the world be moved by him*: lest man should turn himself to fearing something else, and, turned away from the fear of God, should fear some creature in his stead, and adore what has been made, having dismissed him who made it; he confirmed us in the fear of God, as it were saying to us and addressing us: Why are you going to dread something from heaven, something from earth, something from the sea? *He spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created.* He who spoke and they were made, commanded and they were created, when he commands they are moved, when he commands they are still. And the malice of men can have its own desire of harming: but power, if he does not give it, it does not have. *For there is no power except from God*[^13]: it is a definitive sentence of the Apostle. He did not say: There is no desire except from God. For there is an evil desire, which is not from God: but because that very evil desire harms no one if he does not permit it: *There is no power,* he says, *except from God.* Hence God, a man standing before a man, says: *You would have no power over me, unless it had been given you from above*[^14]. He was judging, he was teaching: while he was being judged, he was teaching, that he might judge those whom he had taught: *You would have no power over me,* he says, *unless it had been given you from above.* What is this? Does man alone not have power unless he has received it from above? What of the devil himself? Did he dare to take away even one little sheep from the holy man Job, unless he first said: *Put forth your hand,* that is, give power[^15]? The devil wished it, but the other did not permit; when the other permitted, the devil could: so it was not the devil who could, but he who permitted. Therefore Job himself, well instructed, did not say, as we are accustomed to remind you, The Lord gave and the devil took away; but: *The Lord gave, and the Lord has taken away: as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done*[^16]; not, as it pleased the devil. See, then, my brothers, eating with so much labor this wholesome and useful bread, see that you fear no one but the Lord. Besides him, fear no other, Scripture tells you. Accordingly, let all the earth fear the Lord, who has set the depths in his storehouses. And let all who inhabit the world be moved by him. *For he spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created.*

**13.** [v. 10] But now the evil kings have ceased, they have been made good: they too have believed, they now bear the sign of Christ's cross on their forehead, a sign more precious than any gem of the diadem: those who raged have been destroyed. But who did this? Perhaps you, that you should exalt yourself? *The Lord scatters the counsels of the nations, and he reproves the thoughts of the peoples, and he reproves the counsels of princes.* When they said: Let us take them away from the earth, the Christian name will not exist if we do this; let them be killed thus, let them be tortured thus, let such and such be inflicted on them. These things were said, and in the midst of these the Church grew. *He reproves the thoughts of the peoples, and he reproves the counsels of princes.*

**14.** [v. 11] *But the counsel of the Lord remains forever, the thoughts of his heart from generation to generation.* It is a repetition of the sentence. What he called before *counsel,* this he calls afterward *the thoughts of his heart*: and what he said above *remains forever,* this he says afterward *from generation to generation.* The repetition is a confirmation. But do not suppose, brothers, that, because he said *the thoughts of his heart,* God as it were sits and thinks what he should do, and seeks counsel about doing or not doing something. Those slownesses are yours, O man: his word runs even to swiftness. When can there be the delay of thinking in that word which is one, and embraces all things? But the thoughts of God are spoken of, that you may understand, that according to yourself you may dare to raise your heart even to words like to your weakness: for the thing itself is much beyond you. *The thoughts of his heart from generation to generation.* What are the thoughts of his heart, and what is the counsel of the Lord that remains forever? Against what counsel did the nations rage, and the peoples meditate vain things[^17]? Since the Lord reproves the thoughts of the peoples, and reproves the counsels of princes. From where, then, does the counsel of the Lord remain forever, except that he foreknew and predestined us beforehand? Who takes away the predestination of God? Before the foundation of the world he saw us, made us, amended us, sent to us, redeemed us: this his counsel remains forever, this his thought remains from generation to generation. The nations raged then, openly tossing and savaging; let them now waste away as it were shut up and gathered into a wineskin: they had free audacities, let them have fierce and bitter thoughts. When can they destroy what he thought, and which remains forever?

**15.** [v. 12] But what is this? *Blessed is the nation.* Who is there who, on hearing this, does not raise himself up? For all love blessedness: and men are therefore perverse because they wish to be evil but do not wish to be wretched: and since misery is the inseparable companion of malice, these perverse men not only wish to be evil and do not wish to be wretched—which cannot be—but they wish to be evil for this very reason, that they may not be wretched. What is this that I have said: They wish to be evil for this reason, that they may not be wretched? Consider this a little in all men who do evil deeds: they always wish to be blessed. A man commits theft: you ask, why? Because of hunger, because of need. So, that he may not be wretched, he is evil; and therefore the more wretched, because he is evil. So, for the sake of driving off misery, and for the sake of acquiring blessedness, all men do whatever they do, whether good or evil: therefore they always wish to be blessed. Whether living badly or living well, they wish to be blessed: and it does not befall all to be what all wish to be. For all wish to be blessed, but they will not be so except those who have willed to be righteous. And behold, some man or other wishes to be blessed so as to do evil. From where? From money, from silver and gold, from estates, farms, houses, slaves, the pomp of the world, fleeting and perishable honor. By having something they wish to be blessed: so seek what you may have, that you may be blessed. For when you are blessed, you will surely be better than when you are wretched. But it cannot be that a worse thing should make you better. You are a man; whatever you desire, by which you long to be blessed, is worse than you. Gold, silver, any bodies whatever, which you long to acquire, to possess, to enjoy, are lower than you. You are better, you are mightier: and surely you wish to be better than you are, when you wish to be blessed, because you are wretched. For it is surely better to be blessed than wretched. You wish to be better than yourself: and you seek, you search out, the means whereby this may come to pass, things worse than yourself. Whatever you seek on earth is worse than you. This every man wishes for his friend, thus he conjures him: So may you be better, so may we see you better, so may we rejoice over you bettered. What he wishes for his friend, this he himself also wills. Receive, then, faithful counsel. You wish to be better than yourself, I know, we all know, we all will it: seek what is better than you, that thereby you may be made better than yourself.

**16.** Behold now heaven and earth: let not these beautiful bodies so please you that you would be made blessed by them. What you seek is in the soul. For you wish to be blessed: in your very soul seek what is better. For since there are two things, that is, soul and body, and of these two that is better which is called soul, your body can be made better through the better, because the body is subject to the soul. Your body, then, can be made better through your soul: so that when your soul has been made righteous, your body too may afterward be made immortal. For through the illumination of the soul the body merits incorruption, so that the repair of the lower comes about through the better. If, then, the good of your body is your soul, because it is better than your body; when you seek your good, seek that which is better than your soul. But what is your soul? Attend, lest perhaps, despising your soul and supposing it to be some cheap and abject thing, you seek cheaper things by which your soul may be made blessed. In your soul is the image of God; the mind of man receives it. It received it, and by inclining itself to sin, discolored it. He himself came to it as its re-former, who before was its former; because through the Word all things were made, and through the Word this image was impressed. The Word himself came, that we might hear from the Apostle: *Be reformed in the newness of your mind*[^18]. Now, then, it remains that you seek what is better than your soul. What will it be, I beseech you, except your God? You find nothing better than your soul; for when your nature is perfected, it will be made equal to the angels. Now above it there is none but the Creator. Raise yourself to him, do not despair, do not say: It is too much for me. Rather it is too much for you, perhaps, to have the gold you seek. The gold, even if you wish it, perhaps you will not have; God, when you wish him, you will have: because even before you willed it he came to you, and when you were of an averted will he called you, and when you were converted he struck terror into you, and when, struck with terror, you confessed, he consoled you. He who bestowed all things on you, he who made you to be, who together with those who are with you, even the evil, bestows the sun, bestows the rain, bestows fruits, springs, life, health, so great consolations—he keeps something for you which he gives to none but you. And what is it that he keeps for you, except himself? Seek something else, if you find something better: God keeps himself for you. Greedy man, why do you gape after heaven and earth? Better is he who made heaven and earth: him you shall see, him you shall have. Why do you wish that that villa were yours, and, passing through it, you say: Blessed is he whose possession this is? How many say this who pass through it: and yet, when they have said it, and pass through it, they can shake their heads and sigh, but can they possess it? Cupidity sounds, iniquity sounds: but do not covet your neighbor's property. Blessed is he whose that villa is, whose this house is, whose this field is. Restrain your iniquity, hear the truth: *Blessed is the nation whose is*—what? Now you know what I am going to say. Therefore desire to have him, and then at last you will be blessed. By this alone you will be blessed: by a thing better than you are, you will be made better. God is, I say, better than you, who made you. *Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord.* Love this, possess this, this you will have when you wish, this you will have freely.

**17.** *Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord.* Our God! For whose is God not? Yet surely not of all in the same way. Ours the more, ours who live of him as of our bread. Let him be our inheritance, our possession. Or is it perhaps rash that we make God our possession, since he is the Lord, since he is the Creator? It is not rashness: it is the affection of desire, and the sweetness of hope. Let the soul say, let it say altogether secure: You are my God; he who says to our soul: *I am your salvation*[^19]. Let it say, let it say secure: it will do him no wrong when it says this; rather it will do him wrong if it does not say it. Did you wish to have trees by which you might be blessed? Hear Scripture saying of wisdom: *It is a tree of life to all who possess it.* Behold, it has called wisdom our possession. But lest you suppose that wisdom itself, because Scripture has called it your possession, is something lower than you, it follows and adds: *And to those who lean on it as on the Lord, it is safe*[^20]. Behold, your Lord has been made for you as it were a staff: the man leans secure, because the staff does not give way. Say, then, secure, it is your possession, *to those who possess it.* Scripture has said it, confidence has filled up your doubt: say secure, love secure, hope secure. Let those words too be yours in the psalm: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance*[^21].

**18.** So from this we shall be blessed, by possessing God. What then? Shall we possess him, and he not possess us? From where, then, does Isaiah say: *Lord, possess us*[^22]? He possesses, then, and is possessed, and all for our sake. For he is not possessed by us, that we may be blessed of him, in the same way that he possesses us that he may be blessed. He both possesses and is possessed, for no other reason than that we may be blessed. We possess him, and he possesses us; because we worship him and he tends us. We worship him as Lord God, he tends us as his land. That we worship him, no one doubts; but that he tends us, who shows it to us? He who says: *I am the vine, you are the branches, my Father is the husbandman*[^23]. Behold, in this psalm too both are told us, both are shown us. He has already said that we possess him: *Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord.* Whose is that estate? His. Whose is he? His. Whose is this man? Let us speak of God, let us speak whose he is. And as it is wont to be answered to us, when we ask about some estates or farms, broad and most pleasant: There is a certain senator, and it is called this or that, whose this possession is: and we say: Blessed is that man. So if we ask: Whose is this God? There is a certain blessed nation whose he is: for the Lord their God is he. And not as that senator possesses his estate, but is not possessed by his estate, so too the God of this nation. Hence, that we may be his, we ought to labor: but they possess each other in turn. You have heard that the nation possesses him: *Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord.* Hear that he too possesses it: *The people whom the Lord has chosen for his inheritance.* Blessed the nation in its possession, blessed the inheritance in its possessor, *the people whom the Lord has chosen for his inheritance.*

**19.** [v. 13] *From heaven the Lord looked down, he saw all the sons of men.* Take *all* here in such a way that you understand all of that nation, holding that inheritance, or being that inheritance. For all these are themselves God's inheritance. And these very all the Lord looked down upon from heaven, and he saw them who said: *When you were under the fig tree, I saw you*[^24]. For he saw him because he had mercy on him. Hence, often, when asking for mercy, we say to a man: See me. And of him who despises you, what do you say? He does not see me. There is, then, a certain seeing of one who has mercy, not a seeing of one who punishes. That seeing toward sins is a taking notice: those sins he does not wish to be seen who says: *Turn your face from my sins*[^25]. What he wishes to be pardoned, he does not wish to be recognized: *Turn,* he says, *your face from my sins.* When, then, he has turned his face from your sins, will he not see you? And from where does he say elsewhere: *Do not turn your face from me*[^26]? Let him turn it, then, from your sins, let him not turn it from you: let him see you, have mercy on you, come to your aid. *From heaven the Lord looked down, he saw all the sons of men*: belonging to the Son of man.

**20.** [v. 14] *From his prepared dwelling place*: which he prepared for himself. He saw us from the Apostles, he saw us from the preachers of truth, he saw us from the angels whom he sent to us. All these are his house, all these are his dwelling place: for all these are the heavens that declare the glory of God. *He saw all the sons of men; from his prepared dwelling place he looked upon all who inhabit the earth.* They are his own, they are his, that nation is blessed whose God is the Lord; they are that people whom the Lord has chosen for his inheritance: for he is throughout all lands, and is not in a part. *He looked upon all who inhabit the earth.*

**21.** [v. 15] *Who fashioned their hearts one by one.* With the hand of his grace, with the hand of his mercy, he fashioned our hearts, he molded our hearts, he fashioned them one by one, giving us certain single hearts, which yet do not break unity. As all the members are formed one by one, and have their works one by one, and yet live in the unity of the body—the hand does what the eye does not do, the ear avails for what neither the eye nor the hand avails for; yet all work in unity, and the hand and the eye and the ear do different things, and are not opposed to one another—so too in the body of Christ individual men, as it were individual members, rejoice in their own gifts, because he who chose the people for his inheritance fashioned their hearts one by one. *For are all apostles? are all prophets? are all teachers? do all have the gifts of healings? do all speak with tongues? do all interpret? To one indeed, through the Spirit, is given the utterance of wisdom, to another the utterance of knowledge, to another faith in the same Spirit, to another the gifts of healings*[^27]. Why? Because he fashioned their hearts one by one. And as in our members the works are different, but the health is one: so in all the members of Christ the gifts are different, but the love is one. *Who fashioned their hearts one by one.*

**22.** *Who understands all their works.* What is *understands?* He sees more secretly and inwardly. You have it in the psalm: *Understand my cry*[^28]. For there is no need, as it were, of voices, that something may reach the ears of God. The hidden seeing is called understanding. He said it more expressly than if he had said: He sees all their works: lest then you should suppose those works to be seen in the way that you too see the work of a man. A man sees the deed of a man by the movement of his body, but God sees in the heart. Because, then, he sees within, it was said, *he understands all their works.* Two men give to the poor; the one seeks for himself a heavenly reward, the other human praise: you see in the two one thing, God understands two; for he understands within and recognizes within, he sees their ends, he sees their very intentions. *Who understands all their works.*

**23.** [v. 16] *A king is not saved by the abundance of his strength.* All to the Lord, all in God. Let God be your hope, let God be your strength, let God be your firmness, let him be your prayer, let him be your praise, let him be the end in which you rest, let him be your help when you labor. Hear the truth: *A king is not saved by the abundance of his strength: nor will a giant be saved by the abundance of his might.* A giant is some proud man, exalting himself against God, as though he were himself something in himself and through himself. Such a one is not saved by the abundance of his strength.

**24.** [vv. 17-18] But he has a great horse, strong, sturdy, swift; can it, if anything should befall, quickly free him from danger? Let him not be deceived, let him hear what follows: *A horse is a liar for safety.* Have you understood what is said: *A horse is a liar for safety?* Let not your horse promise you safety: if it has promised it to you, it will lie. For if God wills, you will be freed: if God does not will, when the horse falls you will fall the harder. So do not suppose that it was said: *A horse is a liar for safety,* as it were, a righteous man is a liar for safety, as though the righteous lie for the sake of safety. For it is not written *"the equitable man,"* which is said from equity; but *horse,* a four-footed animal. The Greek codex shows this. And evil beasts are reproved—men who seek for themselves occasions of lies: since Scripture says: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^29]; and: *You will destroy all who speak a lie*[^30]. What then is: *A horse is a liar for safety?* The horse lies to you when it promises you safety. Does a horse speak to anyone, and promise safety? But when you see a well-formed horse, of good strength, endowed with a great course, all these things as it were promise you safety from it: but they deceive, if God does not watch over you, because *a horse is a liar for safety.* Take the horse also figuratively for any grandeur of this world, any honor onto which you proudly mount: the higher you go, the more you falsely suppose yourself not only more lofty but also safer. For you do not know how it may cast you down, dashed the more heavily, the more loftily you were borne. *A horse is a liar for safety: and in the abundance of its strength it will not be saved.* And from where will he be saved? Not from strength, not from might, not from honor, not from glory, not from a horse. And from where? Where shall I go? Where shall I find whence I may be saved? Do not seek long, do not seek far. *Behold, the eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear him.* You see that they are the same whom he looked upon from his dwelling place. *Behold, the eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear him, those who hope in his mercy*: not in their own merits, not in strength, not in might, not in a horse, but in his mercy.

**25.** [v. 19] *That he may rescue their souls from death.* He promises eternal life. What in this pilgrimage? Does he abandon us? See what follows: *And feed them in famine.* It is now the time of famine, the time of fullness will be afterward. He who does not abandon us in the famine of this corruption, how will he satisfy us when we have been made immortal? But as long as it is the time of famine, it must be borne, we must hold out, we must persevere to the end. Now all things must be run through, since the way too is level, and we must think what we carry. The spectators in the amphitheater perhaps rave even now, and sit in the sun: and we, if we stand, are nevertheless in the shade, and the things we behold are more useful and more beautiful. Let us behold beautiful things, and be beheld by the Beautiful One. Let us behold with the mind those things that are spoken in the meanings of the divine Scriptures, and let us rejoice in such a spectacle. But who is our spectator? *Behold, the eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear him, who hope in his mercy: that he may rescue their souls from death, and feed them in famine.*

**26.** [v. 20] But because of the endurance of the pilgrimage, as long as it is famine, and we wait to be refreshed on the way lest we faint; what is laid upon us, or what ought we to profess? *Our soul will be patient for the Lord.* Secure, it will await him who promises mercifully, and exhibits mercifully and truly: and until he exhibits, what shall we do? *Our soul will be patient for the Lord.* But what, if in that very patience we shall not endure? Rather, plainly we shall endure: *For he is our helper and protector.* He helps in the battle, he protects from the heat, he does not abandon you; bear, endure. He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved[^31].

**27.** [v. 21] And what, when you have endured, when you have been patient, when you have come to the end, what will be yours? For what reward do you bear it? why do you suffer so many hard things for so long? *For in him our heart will rejoice, and in his holy name we have hoped.* Hope here, that you may rejoice there: hunger and thirst here, that you may feast there.

**28.** [v. 22] He has exhorted us to all things, he has filled us with the joy of hope, he has set before us what we should love, in whom alone and of whom alone we should presume: after these things comes a brief and salutary prayer: *Let your mercy, Lord, be upon us.* And by what merit? *As we have hoped in you.* To some I have been burdensome; I am aware of it: to others, again, I have even ended the sermon quickly; this too I am aware of: let the weak pardon the stronger, and let the stronger entreat for the weak. Let us all be all the members in one body, let us be quickened by our head: and in him is our hope, and in him is our strength. Let us not hesitate to exact mercy from the Lord our God; he altogether wishes to be exacted of. For he will not be troubled when he is exacted of, nor at all straitened, like one of whom you ask what he does not have, or what he has little of, and he fears to give lest he have less. Do you wish to know how God doles out mercy to you? Do you dole out love: let us see whether there is an end, while you dole out. How great, then, is the opulence in the very Highest, if there can be so much in the image?

**29.** Therefore, brothers, to this love above all we exhort you—not only toward your own selves, but also toward those who are outside, whether still pagans, not yet believing in Christ, or divided from us, confessing the head together with us but separated from the body. Let us grieve for them, brothers, as for our brothers. Whether they will or not, they are our brothers. They will cease to be our brothers only when they cease to say, *Our Father.* The prophet said of certain men: *To those who say to you: You are not our brothers; say: You are our brothers*[^32]. Consider of whom he could have said this: surely not of pagans? No, for we do not call them our brothers according to the Scriptures and the ecclesiastical manner of speaking. Surely not of the Jews who did not believe in Christ? Read the Apostle, and see that when the Apostle says "brothers" without any addition, he does not wish any to be understood but Christians: *A brother or sister is not under bondage in such cases,* he says[^33]: when he was speaking of marriage, by "brother" and "sister" he meant a Christian man or woman. Likewise he says: *But you, why do you judge your brother, or you, why do you despise your brother?*[^34] And in another place: *You,* he says, *do wrong and defraud, and that to brothers*[^35]. So those who say: You are not our brothers, call us pagans. For this reason they also wish to rebaptize us, saying that we do not have what they give. Hence their error follows, that they deny us to be their brothers. But why did the prophet say to us: *Say to them: You are our brothers*—except because we recognize in them what we do not repeat? They, then, by not recognizing our baptism, deny us to be brothers: but we, by not repeating theirs, but recognizing our own, say to them: *You are our brothers.* Let them say: Why do you seek us, why do you want us? Let us answer: *You are our brothers.* Let them say: Go from us, we have no dealings with you. We surely have dealings with you: we confess one Christ, we ought to be in one body, under one head. Why, then, he says, do you seek me, if I have perished? I seek you for this reason, that of you it may be said to me: *Your brother was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found*[^36]. So if I have perished, he says, how am I your brother? That it may be said to me of you: Your brother was dead, and is alive again; he had perished, and is found. Great absurdity, great madness. Why do you seek me, if I have perished? Why should I seek you, except because you had perished? If, then, I have perished, he says, how am I your brother? We adjure you, then, brothers, by the very bowels of love by whose milk we are nourished, by whose bread we are made firm, by Christ our Lord, by his meekness, we adjure you (for it is time that we should expend upon them great love, abundant mercy in beseeching God for them, that he may at some time give them a sober sense, that they may come to themselves and see that they have nothing at all to say against the truth: there has remained to them nothing but the mere weakness of contentiousness, which is the more feeble the greater the strength it supposes itself to have)—for the weak, for the carnally wise, for the animal and carnal, yet for our brothers, celebrating the same sacraments, though not with us, yet the same; answering one Amen, though not with us, yet one: pour out the marrow of your love to God for them. For we have done something for their salvation in council, which there is now no time today to set forth to you. Hence we exhort you that, more eagerly and in greater numbers (for our brothers who are not now present will hear it from you), you gather tomorrow at the basilica of the Tricliae.
