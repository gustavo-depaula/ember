# Exposition of Psalm 126

**1.** [v. 1.] As you already remember, this psalm, as we treat them in order, is the one hundred twenty-sixth, which is among those psalms whose title is *A Song of ascents.* It is, as you know, the voice of those who ascend: where to, except to that heavenly Jerusalem, the mother of us all, which is in the heavens[^1]? She who is heavenly is also eternal: but this one which was on earth had a shadow of her. And so this one fell, that one abides: this one fulfilled the time of its preaching, that one holds the eternity of our restoration. From her we are pilgrims in this life, for her return we sigh: wretched and laboring so long, until we return to her. Nor have the angels our fellow citizens abandoned us in our pilgrimage, but they have announced to us that the King himself would come to us. And he came to us, and was held in contempt among us by us, and afterward was with us: and he taught us to be held in contempt, because he was held in contempt; he taught us to endure, because he endured; he taught us to suffer, because he suffered; and he promised that we would rise again, because he rose again, showing in himself what we ought to hope for. If, then, brothers, the ancient prophets, our fathers, before the Lord Jesus Christ came in the flesh, before he had died, risen again, and ascended into heaven, nevertheless sighed for that city; how much ought we to long for that to which he himself has gone before us, and from which he has never departed? For the Lord did not so come to us as to desert the angels: he both remained with them and came to us; he remained with them in majesty, he came to us in the flesh. But where were we? If he is called our redeemer, we were held captive. Where, then, were we held, that he should come to redeem captives? where were we held? Perhaps among barbarians? Worse than barbarians is the devil and his angels. They formerly held the human race: from them he redeemed us, who gave for us not gold nor silver, but his own blood.

**2.** But how man had come into captivity, let us ask the apostle Paul. For he especially groans in this captivity, sighing for the eternal Jerusalem; and he has taught us to groan by that same Spirit by whom he too, filled, was groaning. For he says this: *The whole creation groans together and is in pain until now.* And again: *For the creation was made subject to vanity*, he says, *not of its own will, but because of him who made it subject, in hope*[^2]. He called the whole creation, in men even not yet believing, but who would nevertheless believe, the creation groaning in labors. Is it then only in those who have not yet believed? does not the creation groan and travail in those too who have believed? *And not only that*, he says, *but we ourselves too, having the firstfruits of the spirit*—that is, we who already in spirit serve God, who already in mind have believed in God, and in that very faith have given certain firstfruits, that we may follow our firstfruits—therefore *we ourselves too groan within ourselves, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^3]. So he himself was groaning, and all the faithful groan, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of their body. Where do they groan? In this mortality. What redemption do they await? That of their body, which has gone before in the Lord, who rose from the dead, and ascended into heaven. Before this is rendered to us, we must groan—even the faithful, even those who hope. So it follows there, and he says; when he had said: *And we ourselves too groan within ourselves, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*; as though it were said to him: What good, then, has Christ done you, if you still groan; and how has the Savior saved you? he who groans is still sick: he added and said: *For by hope we have been saved. But hope which is seen is not hope: for what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^4]. Behold why we groan, and how we groan; because what we hope for, we indeed already await, but do not yet hold, and until we hold it, we sigh in time, because we long for what we do not yet hold. Why? Because *by hope we have been saved.* The flesh now taken from us in the Lord has been saved, not in hope, but in reality. For our flesh has risen and ascended saved in our head; in the members it is yet to be saved. Let the members rejoice securely, because they have not been deserted by their head. For he said to the laboring members: *Behold, I am with you, even to the consummation of the world*[^5]. So it came about that we were turned toward God. For we had no hope but toward this world; and from that, wretched slaves, and twice wretched, because we had both placed our hope in this life, and, having our face toward this world, had our back against God. But when the Lord has turned us, so that we now begin to have our face toward God and our back to this world, we who are still on the way nevertheless attend to the homeland: and when we perhaps suffer some tribulation, yet we hold to the way, and are carried by the wood; the wind is indeed rough, but it is a favorable wind; with labor indeed, but it quickly leads, quickly brings through. Because, then, we were groaning over our captivity, and even those who have already believed groan; but we had forgotten how we became captives, and yet are reminded through Scripture: let us ask the apostle Paul himself; for he says: *For we know that the law is spiritual; but I am carnal, sold under sin*[^6]. Behold whence we became captives, because we were sold under sin. Who sold us? We ourselves, who consented to the seducer. We were able to sell ourselves, we are not able to redeem ourselves. We sold ourselves by consenting to sin, we are redeemed by faith in justice. For innocent blood was given for us, that we might be redeemed. Whatever blood that one shed in persecuting the just, what kind of blood did he shed? He shed the blood of the just indeed; the blood of the prophets, of our fathers, of the just he shed, and of the martyrs: yet all of them coming from the stock of sin. He shed the one blood of him who was not justified, but born just: when that blood was shed, he lost those whom he held. For those for whom innocent blood was given have been redeemed; and, turned back from captivity, they sing this psalm.

**3.** *When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion, we were made like those consoled*[^7]. This he meant to say: We were made rejoicing. When? *When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion.* Which Zion? That very Jerusalem, and the eternal Zion. How is Zion eternal, how is Zion captive? In the angels eternal, in men captive. For not all the citizens of that city are captive; but those who are pilgrims away from it, they are captive. Man is a citizen of Jerusalem, but, sold under sin, he became a pilgrim. From his stock was born the human race, and the captivity of Zion filled the lands. And how is this captivity of Zion a shadow of that Jerusalem? The shadow of that Zion which the Jews received in an image, in a figure, was in the captivity in Babylon; and after seventy years that people returned to its city[^8]. The seventy years signify all the time that is rolled around in seven days: but when all time shall have passed, then we too return to our homeland; just as that people, after seventy years, returned from the captivity of Babylon. For Babylon is this world: for 'Babylon' is interpreted 'confusion.' See whether the whole of human life is not confusion. Whatever men do in vain hope, when they have come to know what they do, they blush. Why do they labor? for whom do they labor? For my sons, one says. And they, for whom? For their sons. And they, for whom? For their sons. So no one for himself. From this confusion, then, those had already been turned back to whom the apostle says: *For what glory had you in those things in which you now blush?*[^9] So this whole life of human affairs, not pertaining to God, is confusion. In this confusion, in this Babylon, Zion is held captive: but the Lord turns back the captivity of Zion.

**4.** *And we were made*, he says, *like those consoled*: that is, we rejoiced, as though receiving consolation. Consolation is only for the wretched, consolation is only for those who groan, who mourn. Why *like those consoled*, except because we still groan? We groan in reality, we are consoled in hope: when reality has passed, from groaning there will come eternal joy, where there will be no need of consolation, because we will be wounded by no misery. But why does he say *as though consoled*, and does not say 'consoled'? This word *like* is not always set down to express likeness: when we say *like*, it is sometimes referred to a true property, sometimes to a likeness; here it is referred to a true property. But examples must be given to us even from the common speech of men, so that it may be easily understood. When we say: As the father lived, so too the son; we say this for likeness: and: As the beast dies, so man dies; this is said for likeness. But when we say: He acted like a good man; is he not a good man, but merely has the likeness of a good man? He acted like a just man; this 'like' does not deny that he is just, but shows his property. You acted like a senator: So am I not a senator, if he should say: On the contrary, because you are one, you acted like a senator; and because you are just, you acted like a just man; and because you are good, you acted like a good man. So because these men too were truly consoled, they rejoiced *like those consoled*; that is, their joy was great, as of those consoled, the one who died consoling those who would die. For all of us in dying groan: he who died consoled us, that we might not fear to die. He rose first, that we might have what we could hope for. Because, then, he rose first, he gave us hope: because, set in misery, we were consoled by hope; hence great joy. And the Lord turned back our captivity, so that now from captivity we may hold to the way, and go to the homeland. Now, then, redeemed, let us on the way not fear our enemies lying in wait. For he redeemed us for this, that the enemy might not dare to lie in wait for us, if we do not depart from the way. For Christ himself was made the way[^10]. Do you wish not to suffer robbers? He says to you: I have paved the way for you to the homeland; do not depart from the way. I have fortified the way in such a manner that the robber dare not approach you; do not depart from it, and the robber dare not approach you. Walk, then, in Christ, and sing rejoicing, sing as one consoled; because he has gone before you, who commanded you to follow.

**5.** [v. 2.] *Then our mouth was filled with joy, and our tongue with exultation*[^11]. The mouth, my brothers, which we have in the body, how is it filled with joy? It is not usually filled, except either with food, or drink, or some such thing put into the mouth. Our mouth is sometimes filled: and there is more that we say to your holiness; when we have our mouth full, we cannot speak. But we have an inner mouth, that is, in the heart, whence whatever proceeds, if it is evil, defiles us; if it is good, cleanses us. For about that very mouth you have heard, while the Gospel was being read. For the Jews were exulting over the Lord, because his disciples ate with unwashed hands[^12]. They exulted, who had cleanness outside, and inside were full of stains; they exulted, whose justice was only in the eyes of men. But the Lord sought our inner cleanness; and if this be present, it must be that the things outside too are clean. *Cleanse*, he says, *the things that are within, and the things that are outside will be clean*[^13]. The Lord himself says in another place: *Rather, give alms, and behold, all things are clean to you*[^14]. But whence does almsgiving proceed? From the heart. For if you stretch out your hand, but do not have mercy in your heart, you have done nothing; but if you have mercy in your heart, even if you have nothing to stretch out with your hand, God accepts your almsgiving. But those wicked men sought cleanness outside. Of such was also that Pharisee who had invited the Lord, when there came the woman who was a sinner and notorious in the city, who washed the Lord's feet with her tears, wiped them with her hair, anointed them with ointment. So the Pharisee, who had invited the Lord, and had no cleanness except outwardly in the body, but in his heart was full of iniquity and rapine, said within himself: *If this man were a prophet, he would know what woman has come to him at his feet*[^15]. Whence did he know whether he knew or did not know? But he supposed that he did not know, because he did not repel her from himself. For if such a woman had come to the Pharisee himself, he who held cleanness as it were in the flesh would have blown her away, repelled her, cast her off; lest the unclean should touch the clean, and contaminate his cleanness. Because the Lord did not do this, he therefore believed that he did not know what kind of woman had come to him at his feet; but the Lord not only knew her, but was hearing her thoughts: for even if the touch of the body does something, O unclean Pharisee, could the flesh of the Lord be polluted by the touch of the woman, or rather the woman be cleansed by the touch of the Lord? But the physician was allowing the sick woman to touch the medicine; and she who had come knew the physician; and she who was accustomed perhaps to be brazen in her fornication was made more brazen for her salvation. She burst into the house where she had not been invited; but she had wounds, and she had come there where the physician reclined. But that man who had invited the physician seemed to himself to be healthy; therefore he was not being cured. You now know what follows in the Gospel, how that Pharisee was put to confusion, when he showed him that he both knew that woman, and had heard his thought.

**6.** But let us return to what was just now read from the Gospel, which pertains to the present verse where it was said: *Our mouth was filled with joy, and our tongue with exultation*: for we are asking what mouth, and what tongue. Let your charity attend. The Lord was exulted over, because his disciples were eating with unwashed hands[^16]. The Lord answered them as was fitting, and, calling the crowds, said: *Hear, all of you, and understand. Everything that enters into the mouth does not defile a man, but what comes out*[^17]. What is this? When he said: *What enters into the mouth*, he meant to point to none but the mouth of the body. For foods enter, and foods do not defile men; because: *All things are clean to the clean*[^18]; and: *Every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving*[^19]. Certain things had been set down for the Jews in a figure, and were called unclean[^20]. But after the Light himself came, the shadows were removed, we are not held in the letter, but made alive in the spirit: and the yoke of observance was not imposed on Christians which had been imposed on the Jews; because the Lord said: *For my yoke is sweet, and my burden is light*[^21]: and: *All things*, says the apostle, *are clean to the clean: but to the unclean and the unbelieving nothing is clean; but both their mind and their conscience are defiled*[^22]. What did he wish to be understood? To a clean man both bread and pork are clean, to an unclean man neither bread nor pork. *But to the unclean*, he says, *and the unbelieving nothing is clean.* Why is nothing clean? *But both their mind and their conscience*, he says, *are defiled*: because if what is within is unclean, what is outside can in no way be clean. If, then, for those whose inner parts are unclean, the outer parts cannot be clean; if you wish the outer parts to be clean, cleanse the inner parts. For there is the mouth which will be filled with joy for you, even when you are silent: for when you are silent and rejoice, your mouth cries out to God. But see whence you rejoice. If you rejoice over the world, with unclean joy you cry out to God: but if you rejoice over your redemption, as this psalm said: *When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion, we were made joyful*; then your mouth is filled with true joy, and your tongue with exultation: it is plain that you rejoice in hope, and your joy is accepted with God. In that very joy, or in that very mouth which we have within, we both eat and drink: as from this mouth for the refreshment of the body, so from that mouth for the refreshment of the heart. For hence: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be satisfied*[^23].

**7.** But if nothing makes a man unclean except what comes out of the mouth, and when we hear this in the Gospel we understand only the mouth of the body; it is absurd and exceedingly foolish that we should then think a man is not made unclean when he eats, but think him unclean if he vomits. For the Lord says: *Not what enters into the mouth, but what comes out, defiles*[^24]. When, then, you eat, you do not become unclean; and when you vomit, you are made unclean? When you drink, you are not unclean; and when you spit, you are unclean? For when you spit, something comes out of your mouth; when you drink, something enters into your mouth. What did the Lord mean: *Not what enters into the mouth, but what comes out, defiles*? Going on in the same place, according to another evangelist, he said what the things are that proceed from the mouth; so that you may understand that he was speaking not of the mouth of the body, but of the mouth of the heart. For he says: *For from the heart come forth evil thoughts, fornications, murders, blasphemies; these are the things that defile a man: but to eat with unwashed hands does not defile a man*[^25]. So those things, my brothers, how do they come out of the mouth, except because *they come out of the heart*, as the Lord himself says? For it is not when we speak them that they then defile us. Let no one say: When we speak, they come out of our mouth, because words and sounds come out of our mouth; and when we speak evil things, we are made unclean. What if someone does not speak, but only thinks evil things, is he clean, because nothing has proceeded from the mouth of his body? But God has already heard from the mouth of the heart. Behold, my brothers, attend to what I say. I name theft: I have just now named theft; has theft defiled me because I named it? Behold, it came out of my mouth, and did not make me unclean. But the thief rises in the night, and says nothing with his mouth, and by doing it becomes unclean. Not only does he not speak, but he even presses on the crime in total silence; and he so fears his voice may be heard, that he does not even wish his footsteps to make a sound: is he, then, clean because he is so silent? But I say more, my brothers. Behold, he still lies on his bed, he has not yet risen to commit the theft; he keeps awake, and waits for men to sleep: he already cries out to God, he is already a thief, he is already unclean, the crime has already proceeded from the inner mouth. For when does the crime proceed from the mouth? When the will to do it is resolved upon. You resolved to do it; you said it, you did it. If you have not committed the theft outwardly; perhaps the one from whom you were planning to take it did not deserve to lose it: and he has lost nothing, and you will be condemned for the theft. You resolved to kill a man; you said it in your heart, murder sounded from your inner mouth: the man still lives, and you are punished as a murderer. For what you are before God is the question, not what you have not yet appeared to be before men.

**8.** Surely, then, we know, and we ought to know, and to hold, that there is a mouth of the heart, and there is also a tongue of the heart. That very mouth is filled with joy: in that very mouth within we pray to God, when the lips are closed and the conscience is open. There is silence, and the breast cries out: but to whose ears? Not of man, but of God. Be secure, then; he hears who has mercy. And again, when no man hears the evil things, if they proceed from your mouth, do not be secure, because he hears who condemns. Susanna was not heard by the unjust judges, she was silent and prayed[^26]. Her mouth was not heard by men, her heart cried out to God. Was it because her voice did not proceed from the mouth of the body that she therefore did not deserve to be heard? She was heard; when she prayed, no man knew. So then, brothers, think about what we have in the inner mouth. See there that you say nothing evil within, and you do nothing evil outside: for nothing can be done by a man outside, except what has been said within. Guard the mouth of the heart from evil, and you will be innocent: the tongue of your body will be innocent, your hands will be innocent; your feet too will be innocent, your eyes will be innocent; all your members will serve as soldiers of justice, because a just commander has held the heart.

**9.** [vv. 2-3.] *Then they will say among the nations: The Lord has done great things in dealing with them. The Lord has done great things in dealing with us; we were made joyful*[^27]. See, brothers, whether Zion does not now say that among the nations, throughout the whole world; see whether men do not run to the Church. In the whole world our price is received; 'amen' is answered. So they say among the nations, the captives of Jerusalem, those of Jerusalem who will return, the pilgrims, sighing for their homeland. What do they say? *The Lord has done great things in dealing with us; we were made joyful.* Did they themselves deal with themselves? They dealt badly with themselves, because they sold themselves under sin. The Redeemer came, and dealt well with them: and *the Lord has done great things in dealing with them. The Lord has done great things in dealing with us; we were made joyful.*

**10.** [v. 4.] *Turn back, O Lord, our captivity, like a torrent in the south*[^28]. What this is, let your charity attend. He had already said: *When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion.* He was speaking as though of the past: but it usually happens that, speaking of the past, the Prophet foretells the future. For he was speaking as though of the past, when in another psalm he said: *They have dug my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones*[^29]. He did not say, they will dig; he did not say, they will number; he did not say, they will divide my garments among themselves; he did not say, over my garment they will cast lots: these things were to come, and were sung as though done. For all things that are to come are already done for God. Here, then, when he said: *When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion, we were made like those consoled; then our mouth was filled with joy, and our tongue with exultation*; that he might show that he was thinking of future things under the figure of the past, he said: *Then they will say among the nations.* *They will say* is now of the future. *The Lord has done great things in dealing with us; we were made joyful.* When they were sung, these things were to come, and now are seen as present. So he prays as though of future things, who was foretelling future things as though past: *Turn back, O Lord, our captivity.* So the captivity was not yet turned back, because the Redeemer had not yet come. So then, when the Psalms were sung, what was prayed for then has now been done: *Turn back, O Lord, our captivity, like a torrent in the south.* As torrents are turned back in the south, so turn back our captivity. We were asking what this is; but it will be made plain now, with the Lord helping, by your prayers. In a certain place Scripture, when it was commanding and admonishing about good works, said: *Like ice in fair weather, so will your sins be dissolved*[^30]. So sins were binding us. How? As cold binds water so that it does not flow. And bound by the cold of sins, we had frozen. But the south is a warm wind: when the south wind blows, the ice melts, and the torrents are filled. But torrents are the name for the rivers of winter; for, filled with sudden waters, they flow with great force. So we had frozen in captivity; our sins were constraining us: the south wind blew, the Holy Spirit; our sins were forgiven us, we were loosed from the cold of iniquity; like ice in fair weather, our sins are dissolved. Let us run to the homeland, like torrents in the south. For we have long labored, and we labor even while doing good. For this very human life which we have entered is wretched, full of labors, of sorrows, of perils, of hardships, of temptations. Do not be seduced by the joy of human affairs; notice the things to be wept over in human affairs. The child who is born could have laughed first: why does it begin to live with weeping? It does not yet know how to laugh; why does it already know how to weep? Because it has begun to go into this life. But if it is one of those captives, here it weeps and groans; but joy will come.

**11.** [v. 5.] For it follows: *Those who sow in tears will reap in joy*[^31]. In this life, which is full of tears, let us sow. What shall we sow? Good works. The works of mercy are our seeds: of which seeds the apostle says: *But in doing good let us not grow weary; for in due season we shall reap, never tiring. So then, while we have time, let us do good to all, but especially to those of the household of faith*[^32]. Speaking, then, of those very alms, what does he say? *But this I say: He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly*[^33]. So he who sows much will reap much: *he who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly*; and he who sows nothing will reap nothing. Why do you long for broad estates, where you may sow many seeds? You have no broader place in which to sow than Christ, who willed to be sown in. Your land is the Church; sow as much as you can. But you have little to do it with. Do you have the will? Just as it would be nothing, what you have, if good will were not present; so too, because you do not have, do not be sad, if you have good will. For what do you sow? Mercy. And what will you reap? Peace. But did the angels say: Peace on earth to rich men? Rather: *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^34]. In Zacchaeus there was great good will, in Zacchaeus great love. He received the Lord into his lodging, received him rejoicing, and promised that he would give half of his estate to the poor, and that, if he had taken anything from anyone, he would repay fourfold[^35]; so that you may understand that he kept half for himself for this reason, not that he might have something to possess, but that he might have wherewith to repay debts. Great good will; he gave much, he sowed much. So then that widow who put in two mites, did she sow little[^36]? On the contrary, as much as Zacchaeus. For she had lesser means, but she had an equal will. She put in two mites out of as great a will as Zacchaeus put in half his estate. If you attend to what they gave, you find them different: if you attend to what they gave it from, you find them equal; she gave whatever she had, and he gave what he had.

**12.** Suppose someone does not have even two coins: is there something cheaper that we may sow, so that we may reap that harvest? There is: *Whoever gives a cup of cold water to a disciple will not lose his reward*[^37]. A cup of cold water costs not two coins, but is free; yet sometimes it is free in such a way that one man has it and another does not: if, then, he who has it gives it to him who does not have it, he has given as much—if he gave what he gave out of full love—as much as she in the two mites, as much as Zacchaeus in half of his goods. For not without reason did he add *cold*, in order to point to a poor man. Therefore he said: *A cup of cold water*, lest anyone make an excuse even from this, that he had no wood to heat the water with. *Whoever gives a cup of cold water to one of the least will not lose his reward.* What if he does not have even this? Let him be secure, even if he does not have even this: *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^38]: only let him fear this, lest he have something and not do it. For if he has and does not do it, he has frozen within; his sins have not yet been dissolved like a torrent in the south, because his will is cold. What good are the great goods that we possess? Let a fervent will come, now thawed by the warmth of the south wind; even if he has nothing, all is reckoned to him. How much do beggars provide for themselves! Let your charity attend to how almsgiving is done. Surely those on whom you do almsgiving are beggars; beggars are in need. You attend perhaps to your brothers, if they are in need of anything; you bestow, if Christ is in you, even on strangers. But if they are beggars, who make a profession of asking, in their hardship even they have something to provide for one another. God has not deserted them, that they may be tested, for they do works of mercy. This man cannot walk; he who can walk lends his feet to the lame man; he who sees lends his eyes to the blind man; and he who is young and healthy lends his strength to the old or the sick man, he carries him: the one is in need, the other is rich.

**13.** Sometimes even a rich man is found poor, and something is provided for him by a poor man. Someone or other comes to a river, the more delicate the richer he is; he cannot cross: if he crossed with his limbs bared, he would grow cold, fall sick, die: a poor man, more exercised in body, approaches, and carries the rich man across; he does almsgiving toward the rich man. So do not think those only to be poor who do not have money. In whatever a man is poor, there look at him; for perhaps you are rich in that in which he is poor, and you have something to lend. Perhaps you lend your limbs, and it is more than if you lent money. He is in need of counsel, you are full of counsel; in counsel he is poor, you are rich. Behold, you neither labor nor lose anything; you give counsel, and you have provided almsgiving. Now, my brothers, when we speak, you are toward us as poor men; and because God has deigned to give to us, we give to you from that; and we all receive from him who alone is rich. So, then, the body of Christ holds together, the allied members are so joined and made one in love and in the bond of peace, when each provides what he has to him who does not have it: in what he has, he is rich; in what the other does not have, he is poor. So love one another, so cherish one another. Do not attend to yourselves alone; but attend to those in need around you. But because these things are done with labors and hardships in this life, do not grow weary. In tears you sow, in joy you will reap. For what, my brothers? The farmer himself, when he goes forth with his plow and carries the seed, is not the wind sometimes cold, and the rain a deterrent? He looks at the sky, sees it gloomy, trembles with cold, and yet goes forth and sows. For he fears that while he looks at the gloomy day and waits for a glad day, the season may pass, and he may not find what to reap. Do not delay, my brothers; sow in winter, sow good works even while you weep; because *those who sow in tears will reap in joy.* They cast their seeds, good will and good works.

**14.** [v. 6.] *Going they went, and wept, casting their seeds*[^39]. Why did they weep? Because they were among the wretched, and they were wretched. It is better, my brothers, that no one be wretched, than that you do works of mercy. For he who, in order to do works of mercy, wishes there to be wretched people, has a cruel mercy: just as, if a physician, in order to exercise his art, wished there to be many sick people, it would be a cruel medicine. It is better that all be healthy, than that the physician's art be exercised. So it is better that all, blessed, reign in that homeland, than that there be those on whom mercy is spent. Yet as long as there are those on whom it may be spent, let us not cease, in this hardship, to cast our seeds. Even if we sow with weeping, yet with joy we will reap. For in that resurrection of the dead each one will receive his sheaves, that is, the fruit of the seed, the crown of joys and exultation. Then there will be the triumph of those rejoicing, and exulting over death itself, over which they groaned: then they will say to death: *Where, O death, is your striving? Where, O death, is your sting?*[^40] But why do they already rejoice? Because they carry their sheaves. Because *going they went, and wept, casting their seeds.* Why *casting their seeds*? Because *those who sow in tears will reap in joy.*

**15.** By this psalm we have exhorted you especially to do works of mercy, because by this one ascends; and you see that he sings the song of ascents who ascends. Remember; do not love to descend and not to ascend, but think about ascending: for he who was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho fell among robbers. Had he not gone down, he would not have fallen among robbers[^41]. So now Adam went down, and fell among robbers: for we are all Adam. But a priest passed by and despised him; a Levite passed by and despised him; because the Law could not heal. A certain Samaritan passed by, that is, our Lord Jesus Christ: for it was said to him: *Do we not say truly that you are a Samaritan, and have a demon?*[^42] He did not say: I am not a Samaritan; but he said: *I do not have a demon*[^43]. For 'Samaritan' is interpreted 'guardian.' If he said: I am not a Samaritan; he would be saying: I am not a guardian. And who else would be the guardian? Then, setting forth the parable: *The Samaritan passed by, and did mercy with him*[^44], as you know. He lay wounded on the way, because he went down. The Samaritan, passing by, did not despise us: he cared for us, lifted us onto his beast, into his flesh; brought us to the inn, that is, to the Church; entrusted us to the innkeeper, that is, to the apostle; gave two denarii wherewith to care for us, the love of God and the love of neighbor: for on these two commandments hang the whole Law and the Prophets[^45]. And he said to the innkeeper: *If you spend anything more, on my return I will repay you*[^46]. The apostle spent more; for although it was permitted to all the apostles to receive, as soldiers of Christ, their pay from Christ's provincials, he labored with his own hands, and gave his own rations to the provincials[^47]. All has been done: if we went down, and were wounded; let us ascend, let us sing, and let us make progress, that we may arrive.
