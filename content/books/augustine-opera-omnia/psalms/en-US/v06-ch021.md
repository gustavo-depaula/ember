# Exposition of Psalm 141

**1.** You have heard, brothers, our admonition and request from the mouth of the apostle, when his Letter was just now read. For he says: *Be instant in prayer, watching in it; praying at the same time for us too, that God may open to us a door of the word, to speak his mystery, that I may make it manifest, as it behooves me to speak*[^1]. Deign to count these words as mine too. For there are in the holy Scriptures deep mysteries, which are hidden for this, that they may not become cheap; are sought for this, that they may exercise; are opened for this, that they may feed. The psalm which we have just now sung, in many sentences is somewhat obscure. When, with the Lord helping, the things that have been said begin to be dug out and expounded, you will see that you hear this which you already knew. But for this reason they were said in manifold ways, that the variety of expression might take away weariness of the truth.

**2.** For what more, brothers, or more wholesome, are you about to hear and to know, than: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*[^2]; and: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^3]? But lest you think these two precepts small: *On these two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets hang*[^4]. Whatever, then, is wholesomely conceived in the mind, or brought forth by the mouth, or carved out from any divine page, has no end except love. But this love is not just any kind. For evildoers too are entangled with one another by the fellowship of a lost conscience, and are said to love one another, to be unwilling to depart from one another, to be reconciled by their conversations, to long for one another when absent, to rejoice at one another's presence. This love is hellish: it has birdlime, by which to cast down into the depths; not wings, by which to lift up to heaven. But what is the love by which it is set apart and distinguished from the other things that are called loves? That which is called the true love of Christians has been defined by Paul, and so circumscribed by certain bounds of its own, although it is infinite in its divinity, that it is altogether distinguished from the others. *For the end*, he says, *of the precept is love*[^5]. He could say only this far. For in other places where he was speaking as though to those who knew, he said only this far: *The fullness of the law*, he says, *is love*[^6]; and he did not say what kind of love. He did not say it there, because he said it elsewhere: for not everywhere can or ought all things be said. So here: *The fullness of the law is love.* You were asking perhaps, what love? what kind of love? You hear in another place: *The end of the precept is love from a pure heart*[^7]. Now see whether robbers have among themselves love from a pure heart. A pure heart in love is this, when you love a man according to God; because you ought also to love yourself in such a way that the rule does not err: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^8]. For if you love yourself badly and uselessly; so loving your neighbor too, what do you provide for him? But how do you love yourself badly? As Scripture suggests, which flatters no one, and convicts you that you do not love yourself; rather convicts you that you even hate yourself: *But he who*, he says, *loves iniquity, hates his own soul*[^9]. If, then, you love iniquity, do you think that you love yourself? You err: so, loving your neighbor too, you will lead him to iniquity, and your love will be a snare for the loved one. *Love*, then, *from a pure heart* is that which is according to God, *and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned*[^10]. This love, defined by the apostle, has two precepts; of the love of God, and of the love of neighbor. In no Scriptures seek anything else, let no one teach you anything else. Whatever is obscure in Scripture, this is hidden there: whatever is plain there, this is open there. If it were nowhere open, it would not feed you: if it were nowhere hidden, it would not exercise you. This love cries out from a pure heart, from the heart of such men, in these words, as he here now prays. And who this is, I would quickly say: it is Christ.

**3.** But you are about to hear words which are unworthily taken of our Lord Jesus Christ, and one who understands little will think that I rashly said it is the person of Christ in this psalm. For how can it be of our Lord Jesus Christ, of that spotless lamb, of him in whom alone no sin was found, who alone could most truly say: *Behold the prince of this world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^11], that is, no fault, no guilt; who alone paid back what he did not plunder[^12]; who alone poured out innocent blood, the only Son of God taking flesh, not by which he himself would be diminished, but by which he would increase us: how, then, is it rightly taken of this person: *Set, O Lord, a guard to my mouth, and a door of restraint about my lips, that you may not bend my heart to evil words, to excuse excuses in sins*[^13]? For most plainly this is the sense: Guard, O Lord, my mouth by a certain door and gate of your precept, that my heart may not be bent to evil words. What evil words? Those by which sins are excused. Lest, he says, I prefer to excuse my sins, rather than to accuse them. These words do not fall upon our Lord Jesus Christ himself. For what sins did he commit, which he ought rather to confess than to defend? These are our words. Yet surely Christ speaks. If they are our words, how does Christ speak? And where is the love of which I was speaking? Do you not know that it has made us one in Christ? Love cries out to Christ concerning us, love cries out concerning Christ for us. How does love cry out to Christ concerning us? *And it will be, everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^14]. How does love cry concerning Christ for us? *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^15]? *You*, says the apostle, *are the body of Christ and members*[^16]. If, then, he is the head, we the body, one man speaks; whether the head speaks, or the members, one Christ speaks. And it belongs to the head to speak even in the person of the members. Notice our own custom. First, how only the head can speak in our members: but now notice how our head speaks in the person of all the members. In a tight place someone treads on your foot: You tread on me, says the head. Someone has wounded your hand: You have wounded me, says the head. No one touched your head, but the unity of the framework of your body speaks. The tongue, which is in the head, has taken up the person of all your members, it discharges the word for all. So then let us hear Christ speaking; but let each one recognize there his own voice, as cleaving to the body of Christ. For sometimes he is about to say words in which none of us finds his own person, but they belong to the head alone; yet he does not separate himself from our words and take it to his own proper things, or from his own proper things not return to ours. For of him and of the Church it was said: *The two will be in one flesh*[^17]. Whence he himself too says in the Gospel about this very thing: *So then they are now not two, but one flesh*[^18]. These things are not new; you have always heard them from us: but on occasions they must be recalled, first because the very Scriptures which we treat are so interwoven with one another, that many things are repeated in many places; and it is useful. For the care of the world has thorns, and chokes the seeds; it is needful that what the world forces us to forget be more often recalled by the Lord.

**4.** [v. 1.] *O Lord, I have cried to you, hear me*[^19]. This we can all say. This I do not say, the whole Christ says: but it was said rather from the person of the body; because, when he was here, bearing flesh, he prayed, and from the person of the body he prayed to the Father, and when he prayed drops of blood dripped from his whole body. So it is written in the Gospel: *Jesus prayed with intent prayer, and sweated blood*[^20]. What is the flowing-out of blood from the whole body, except the passion of the whole Church of the martyrs? *O Lord, I have cried to you, hear me: attend to the voice of my supplication, while I cry to you.* You thought the business of crying already finished, when you said: *I have cried to you.* You have cried, now do not be secure. If the tribulation is finished, the cry is finished: but if the tribulation of the Church and of the body of Christ remains until the end of the age, let it not say only: *I have cried to you, hear me;* but: *Attend to the voice of my supplication, while I cry to you.*

**5.** [v. 2.] *Let my prayer be directed as incense in your sight; the lifting up of my hands an evening sacrifice*[^21]. That this is usually understood of the head himself, every Christian recognizes. For with the day now declining toward evening, the Lord on the cross laid down his soul, to take it up again; he did not lose it unwillingly. But yet there too we were figured. For what of his hung on the wood, except what he took from us? And how can it be that God the Father at any time send away and desert the only Son, who is surely one God with him? And yet, fixing our weakness on the cross, where *our old man*, as the apostle says, *was nailed to the cross with him*[^22], from the voice of that man of ours he cried out: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me*[^23]? That, then, is the evening sacrifice, the passion of the Lord, the cross of the Lord, the offering of the saving victim, the holocaust accepted by God. That evening sacrifice made, in the resurrection, a morning gift. So a prayer purely directed from a faithful heart rises as incense from a holy altar. Nothing is more delightful than the fragrance of the Lord: so let all who believe smell.

**6.** [v. 3.] *Our old man*, then—these are the apostle's words—*was nailed to the cross with him; that the body of sin might be emptied*, he says, *that we might no longer serve sin*[^24]. Hence in that very psalm, when it had been said: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me, far from my salvation*; it was at once added: *The words of my sins*[^25]. What sins, if you attend to the head? And yet that it was his voice in the psalm, he himself testified on the cross; he said the very words, he pronounced the very verse. No room is left for human conjecture, no opening for denying to any Christian whatever. What I read in the psalm, this I hear from the Lord. In that very psalm too I recognize what I read in the Gospel: *They have dug my hands and my feet; they have numbered all my bones. But they considered and looked upon me; they divided my garments among themselves, and over my garment they cast lots*[^26]. All these things, as foretold, so done; as we have heard, so we have seen. So if our Lord Jesus Christ, figuring us in the love of his body, although he himself was without sin, said: *The words of my sins;* but said this from the person of his body: who dares, among his members, to say that he has no sin, except one who has dared to lift himself up under the name of false justice, and to accuse Christ of falsehood? Confess, then, O member, what your head pronounced for you. That we may confess it, that we may do it, and not justify ourselves before the sight of him alone who is just, who justifies the impious[^27]; he subjoined now the voice of his body: *Set, O Lord, a guard to my mouth, and a door of restraint about my lips.* He did not say, a bar of restraint; but, *a door.* A door is both opened and closed: so if it is a door, let it both be opened and be closed; let it be opened to the confession of sin, let it be closed to the excuse of sin. For so it will be a door of restraint, not of ruin.

**7.** [v. 4.] For what does this door of restraint profit us? what does Christ pray from the person of the body? *That you may not bend*, he says, *my heart to evil words.* What is, *my heart*? The heart of my Church, the heart surely of my body. Attend to those words where the rule is fixed for us: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^28]? when no one had touched him. *I was hungry, and you fed me; I thirsted, and you gave me drink*: and the rest. And they: *When did we see you hungry or thirsty*? And he: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^29]. These things ought not to be unusual for Christians, especially for those in whom the rules are fixed for understanding the rest; and they will either not be troubled, or be quickly corrected. So as there the just are about to say: Lord, why did you say: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat? when did we see you hungry?* and he will answer: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me;* so here too let us say to Christ within, in our inner man, who deigns to dwell there through faith[^30]. For he is not absent from us, and there is one to whom we may say, since he himself says: *Behold, I am with you, even to the consummation of the age*[^31]. Let us, then, say to him too, since we have taken his voice in this psalm: for it is his voice, which no one denies: *The lifting up of my hands an evening sacrifice.* Say, then, consequently: *Set, O Lord, a guard to my mouth, and a door of restraint about my lips; that you may not bend my heart to evil words, to excuse excuses in sins.* Why do you pray thus, O Lord? what sins of yours are you about to excuse? He answers: When one of my members prays thus, I pray thus: as there he answered: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me.*

**8.** But when your heart has not been bent, O member of Christ, when your heart has not been bent *to evil words, to excuse excuses in sins, with men working iniquity; and you will not combine with their elect.* For this follows: *And I will not combine with their elect.* Who are their elect? Those who justify themselves. Who are their elect? Those who seem just to themselves, and despise the rest, as that Pharisee in the temple said: *God, I give you thanks, that I am not as the rest of men*[^32]. Who are their elect? *This man, if he were a prophet, would know what woman has come to him at his feet*[^33]. Do you recognize the voice of that other Pharisee, who had invited the Lord, when that woman who was a sinner in the city came and approached his feet? That shameless woman, once brazen for fornication, more brazen for salvation, burst into another's house. But he who reclined there was not another's. Not as a stranger did she follow some guest, but as a maidservant her Lord. She approached the feet, because she longed to follow his footsteps; she washed them with tears, wiped them with her hair. What are the feet of Christ, except those by which he traversed the whole world? *How beautiful are the feet of those who announce peace, who announce good things*[^34]! So how many have taken up the feet of the Lord, that by taking up the just man in the name of a just man, they might receive the reward of a just man; and they took up a prophet in the name of a prophet, that they might receive the reward of a prophet! *And whoever gives*, he says, *to one of these least a cup of cold water, only in the name of a disciple, amen I say to you, he will not lose his reward*[^35]. So he who with such kindness took up the feet of the Lord, what did he spend, except what he had superfluous in his house? Rightly, because the hairs are as it were superfluous, with them she wiped the feet of the Lord. Your superfluous things become necessary to you, if with them you have served the feet of the Lord. So she wished to be cured, conscious of a great wound. But is the wound great, and the physician small? But the Pharisees were unwilling to be touched by the unclean, they avoided all contact with sinners; and if ever by necessity they were touched, they washed themselves. And they almost baptized at every hour not only themselves, but also their vessels, their beds, their cups, their dishes, as the Lord mentions in the Gospel. So when that Pharisee knew this woman—who surely, if she approached the feet of that Pharisee, he would repel, lest his holiness be polluted; for he had holiness in body, not in heart[^36]; and because he did not have it in heart, surely he had a false one in body—because, then, he would have repelled her, when the Lord did not do this, he supposed that he did not know who she was, and said within himself: *This man, if he were a prophet, would know what woman has come to him at his feet.* He did not say, he would have repelled her: but, *he would know who* she was; as though it were a consequence that, if he knew, he would repel her. So from this, that he did not repel her, he suspected for certain that he also did not know. But the Lord so had his eyes on that woman, that he had his ears on the heart of the Pharisee. And so, having heard his thought, he set forth the parable which you know. *Two debtors there were to a certain creditor; one owed him five hundred denarii, the other fifty: when both had nothing to repay, he forgave both. I ask of you*, he said, *which of them loved him more? And he answered*, now compelled by the truth to bring forth a sentence against himself: *I believe*, he said, *Lord, that he to whom he forgave more. And turning to the woman, he said to Simon: Do you see this woman? I entered your house: you gave me no kiss; but she has not ceased to kiss my feet: you gave me no water for my feet; but she has washed my feet with her tears: you gave me no oil; but she has anointed me with ointment. For which reason I tell you: many sins are forgiven her, because she has loved much*[^37]. Why? Because she confessed, because she wept, because her heart was not bent to evil words to excuse excuses in sins, she did not combine with their elect, that is, with those who defend themselves.

**9.** For neither would this woman herself, if her heart were bent to evil words, lack a defense of her sins. Do not those equal to her in baseness, but not equal in confession—harlots, adulteresses, the shameless—daily defend their sins? If they have lain hidden, they deny; but if they have either been caught and convicted, or have done it publicly, they defend it. And how easy is their defense, how quick, but how headlong, how daily, and how sacrilegious! Oh, if God did not wish it, I would not do it! God willed this, fortune willed this, fate willed this. She does not say: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me*[^38]; not as that sinful woman coming to the feet of the physician: *Heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.* And this defense, my brothers, is of what kind of people? Not only of the unlearned, but also of the learned. They sit, and reckon the stars; they attend to, describe, conjecture the intervals, courses, revolutions, positions, motions. They seem learned, great. All this learned and great thing is a defense of sin. You will be an adulterer, because you have Venus thus: you will be a murderer, because you have Mars thus. So Mars is the murderer, not you; and Venus the adulteress, not you. See lest both for Mars and for Venus you be condemned. For God who is to condemn, knows that it is you who say: Not I, to the judge who knows. Moreover, that very astrologer who even sells you the tales of your snares, that you may not procure death even for free (for you buy death from the astrologer at a price, you who have despised life from Christ for free): that same astrologer himself, if he sees his wife behaving a little too wantonly, or shamelessly attending to some strangers, or repeatedly going back to the window; does he not seize her, beat her, and give discipline in his own house? Let his wife answer him: If you can, beat Venus, not me. Will he not answer: Fool, one thing is what befits the ruler, another what is set forth for the buyer? So who are their elect? The elect of the evil, the elect of the impious, with whom one must not combine, that is, with whom one must not have fellowship. But who are they? Those who reckon themselves just, and despise the rest as sinners, as those Pharisees were: or, because their very sins are either manifestly caught or done publicly, defending and asserting them, lest any fault be attributed to them; and that they themselves may be judged to have done nothing evil, but all of it God, who, they say, either so created man, or arranged the stars, or neglects human affairs. These are the defenses of the elect of the world. But let the member of Christ say, let the body of Christ say, let Christ say from the person of his body: *You will not bend my heart to evil words, to excuse excuses in sins, with men working iniquity; and I will not combine with their elect.*

**10.** You know, brothers—which must not be passed over—that the more eminent just men, as it were holding in themselves the first degree of justice, are called Elect among the Manichees. Those who know, recognize it; those who did not know, hear it. Indeed all the saints are the elect of God, and we have this in the Scriptures; but they usurped that name for themselves, and applied it as it were more familiarly to themselves, so that they may now be called, as it were properly, the elect. Who are these elect? To whom, if you say: You have sinned; at once they bring forth that impious defense, worse and more sacrilegious than the rest: Not I sinned, but the race of darkness. What is this race of darkness? That which waged war with God. And does it sin, when you sin? It, he says, because I am mingled with it. For God who mingled you, what did he fear? For they say this, that that race of darkness rebelled against God, before the world was made; and he, taking care lest his kingdoms be laid waste by the hostile onslaught of the approaching enemy, sent here his members, his substance, this which he himself is; if gold, gold; if light, light: whatever it is, this he sent, and mingled it, they say, with the inward parts of the race of darkness, and from it fashioned the world. And we souls, they say, are of those very members of God; but we are pressed here by the inward parts of the race of darkness, and whatever we are said to sin, that race sins. They seem indeed to excuse themselves from sin; but they do not excuse their God from the crime of fear, nor the very substance of their God from the crime of corruptibility. For if God is incorruptible, if unchangeable, if uncontaminable, if unstainable, if impenetrable, what was that race going to do to him? Let it have made what onslaught it wished; how would it terrify the impenetrable, the inviolable, the uncontaminable, the unchangeable and the incorruptible? If, then, God is such, he is cruel; because without cause he sent you here, to whom no harm could be done. Why did he send you? Behold, the race of darkness could not harm him; but he gravely harmed you, and he himself, more than it, was your enemy: although it too could harm you. You could be pressed, you could be made captive, you could be defiled, you could be corrupted: so he too could be. For a fragment, as it were, and a small portion of his very nature convicts the mass. Such as that is which he sent here, such is that which remained there. This they themselves say: they confess that there are two substances; one that, and one this. Their books have this; and if they deny it, they are read out, and convicted.

**11.** What then? That in this one beginning I may say nothing more, may say no worse things, may say no more criminal things: in this very beginning by which they bring in war, see how they are warred down; and where they say war was waged against God by the race of darkness, they themselves are caught in the war of their own words. For there is nothing they can say, or where they may flee. But you wish, O wicked man, false elect, to defend your sin, so that when you have done something evil, you may seem not to have done it: you seek into whom you may pour back your sin, and you pour it back into the race of darkness. But attend to God, whether you do not pour it back into him. For if that race of darkness which you imagine could speak, it would say this to you: Why do you accuse me? Could I do anything to your God, or could I not? If I could, I am stronger than he: if I could not, why did he fear me? If he did not fear, why did he send you here, to suffer such great things here, since you are his member, since you are his substance? if he did not fear, he envied; if he did this not from fear, he did it from cruelty. How unjust, then, is he, to whom no harm could be done, and he brought it about that his members should here be so harmed? Or could he be harmed? Then he is not incorruptible. When, then, you wish to defend yourself about your sin, you cannot praise God. In the praise of God you would not be straitened, unless you boasted in your own. Pass over to your own blame, and you will praise God. Return to the words of the Psalms which you abhor, say: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^39]. I said, I sinned; not fortune, not fate, not the race of darkness. If, then, you have sinned, now see how the praise of God lies open, in which you were straitened, when you wished to defend yourself. Better are you straitened in your sins, and widened out in his praise. Now, then, your sin being confessed, see how God is praised: because he is both just, when he punishes you persevering; and merciful, when he frees you confessing. *You will not*, then, he says, *bend my heart to evil words, to excuse excuses in sins;* so that I may say that the race of darkness did what I did.

**12.** *With men working iniquity.* What iniquity? Let us tell some wicked iniquity of theirs. Hear the wicked, public iniquity of the Manichees, which they confess. They say it is more expedient for a man to be a moneylender than a farmer. You ask the reason, and they render an account. See whether that reason is not to be called madness. For he who gives money at interest, they say, does not harm the cross of light: but he who, they say, is a farmer, greatly harms the cross of light. You ask what cross of light? Those members, they say, of God which were captured in that battle, are mingled with the whole world, and are in trees, in herbs, in fruits, in produce. He afflicts the members of God who cuts the earth with the furrow; he afflicts the members of God who pulls up an herb from the earth; he afflicts the members of God who plucks fruit from a tree. Lest he commit these false murders in the field, he commits true murders in moneylending. He does not hold out bread to a beggar. See whether there can be a greater iniquity than this justice. He does not hold out bread to a beggar: you ask, why? Lest the life which is in the bread, which they call a member of God, the divine substance, that beggar receive, and bind it in the flesh. What then of you? what? why do you eat? Do you not have flesh? But we, they say, because we are illumined by the faith of the Manichee, by our prayers and psalms, we who are the elect, purify from it the life which is in that bread, and send it to the treasuries of the heavens. Such are the elect, that they are not to be saved by God, but saviors of God. And it is Christ himself, they say, crucified in the whole world. I in the Gospel had taken Christ as Savior; but you in your books are saviors of Christ. You are plainly blasphemers of Christ, and therefore not to be saved by Christ. So that a morsel may not be given to a beggar, and the member of God may weep in the morsel, the beggar will die of hunger! A false mercy in the morsel commits a true murder in the man. But who are their elect? *You will not bend my heart to evil words, and I will not combine with their elect.*

**13.** [v. 5.] *The just man will correct me in mercy, and will rebuke me*[^40]. See the sinner, the confessor: he wishes to be corrected mercifully, rather than to be praised deceitfully. *The just man will correct me in mercy*: if he is just, if he is merciful, when he sees me sinning. This surely certain members of Christ say, concerning certain members of Christ they say, in one body they say it. The Lord deigns to speak from the person of the corrector, nor does he refuse the person of the corrected or to be corrected: for all the members are in him, and he himself says: *The just man will correct me.* What just man will correct you? The head corrects all the members. *The just man will correct me in mercy, and will rebuke me.* He will rebuke, but in mercy: he will rebuke, but does not hate; and the more he rebukes, because he does not hate. And why does this man give thanks for it? Because: *Rebuke a wise man, and he will love you*[^41]. *The just man will correct me*: because he persecutes? Far from it: this man rather is to be corrected, if he corrects out of hatred. Why, then, does he correct? *In mercy. And he will rebuke me*: in what? In mercy. *But let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head.* What is: *Let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head*? My head will not grow great from adulation. False praise is adulation; false praise of the adulator, this is the oil of the sinner. Therefore men too, when they mock someone with false praise, say even this of him: I have anointed his head. So love to be rebuked by the just man in mercy; do not love to be praised by the sinner with mockery. Have oil with you, and you will not seek the oil of the sinner. For those wise virgins carried oil with them[^42]: the wise virgins carried oil with them, that is, their conscience bore witness to them. Oil is glory, it shines, it glitters on the surface. But the glory ought to be good, and a true glory, that it may be there within in its vessels. Hear what is in the vessels: *But let a man prove himself, and then he will have glory in himself, and not in another*[^43]. What is, 'in his vessels'? Hear the apostle himself: *For this is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^44].

**14.** Finally, because you are in the body of Christ, and still bear a certain mortality; be yourself just to yourself, and in yourself be just. You are a sinner, take vengeance on yourself; return to your conscience, exact punishments of yourself, torment yourself. For so you offer sacrifice to God. *For if you had wished a sacrifice, I would surely have given it*, says the sinner, *in holocausts you will not delight*[^45]. What then? does he accept no sacrifice? *A sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit; a contrite and humbled heart God does not spurn*[^46]. Humble your heart, crush your heart, torment your heart; and you will correct it in mercy: for you do not hate yourself, when you rage against yourself. You will be just in the part that corrects, although you are still a sinner in the part to be corrected: for in that part in which you displease yourself, you are unjust; in that in which what is unjust in you displeases you, you are just. Do you wish to see how just you are? This in you displeases you which also displeases God: now you have joined yourself to the will of God, and you hate in yourself not what he made, but what he hates. From this, that you hate in yourself what you have done, which he too hates who did not do it, you have begun to be severe toward yourself; he will be merciful: he will spare, because you did not spare. So, since you are joined to his eyes, and delight together in his law, and rebuke in yourself what his law rebukes, and this in you displeases you which also displeases the eyes of God, see how just you are: but from this, that, fallen, you have done the things that displease God, and by a certain frailty of human weakness slip into them, and still bear the weakness of the flesh, and groan with the conscience of a certain struggle, in this part you are wicked and a sinner.

**15.** How, you will say, in one part just, in one part a sinner? what is it you say? We labor, we seem to speak contraries, unless the apostolic authority comes to our aid. Hear this from the apostle, lest you accuse me as one who understands evilly: *For I delight*, he says, *in the law of God according to the inner man*[^47]. Behold the just man. Is he not just who delights in the law of God? Whence, then, a sinner? *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin*[^48]. I still wage war against myself, I am not yet wholly restored to the image of my maker; I have begun to be recarved, and in that part in which I am reformed, what is deformed displeases me. So as long as I am thus, what do I hope? *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^49]. The grace of God which has now begun to recarve, the grace of God which pours in sweetness, that you may now through the inner man delight in the law of God: thence the rest will be healed, whence these too were healed. Groan, still wounded, chastise yourself, displease yourself.

**16.** *I do not so fight*, he says, *as one beating the air; but I chastise my body and bring it into servitude, lest perhaps, preaching to others, I myself be found reprobate*[^50]. Does he who chastises the body hate the body? If anyone chastises a slave, does he hate the slave? if anyone beats a son, does he hate the son? And, to say something more closely connected, your flesh is as it were your wife. This the apostle himself says: *No one ever hated his own flesh; but nourishes and cherishes it, as Christ also the Church*[^51]. Surely the flesh is as it were a wife, and no one hates his own flesh. Yet what does he say in another place? *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*[^52]. It lusts against you, as your wife; love and chastise it, until there comes about, in one reformation, one concord. When will this be? Since now you cry out: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* will this body be separated from you, and then will you be secure? And what is: *Within ourselves we groan, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^53]? So it is restored from mortality to immortality, and no longer resists, because there is no mortality to resist. Therefore chastise your body; now tame what you will afterward receive; now let it fail, that then it may suffice. For in this life it cannot be restored, as long as it is borne mortal. Let it not put you down, let it not break you off; bear it, instruct it, chastise it: it will be restored at the end. And because *no one ever hated his own flesh*, the flesh too will rise again. But how? shall I then too struggle? *It is necessary*, he says, *that this corruptible put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality*[^54].

**17.** So when it is said: *He will correct me, and will rebuke me;* whether it be a brother, or a neighbor, or one nearby, or you yourself, you ought to be rebuked and corrected in mercy. *But let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head.* But what shall I do, you say to me? I endure flatterers, they do not cease to make a din; they praise in me things I do not want, they praise in me what I count little, they reprove in me what I hold dear—flatterers, deceivers, beguilers. A great man, for example, is Gaius Seius; great, learned, wise: but why a Christian? For great learning, and great letters, and great wisdom. If great wisdom, approve that he is a Christian: if great learning, he chose learnedly. Finally, what you blame, this pleases him whom you praise. But what? That praise does not sweeten; it is the oil of the sinner. But he does not cease to speak. Let it not fatten your head; that is, do not rejoice at such things; that is, do not nod assent, do not consent, do not be gladdened by it: and if he has brought the oil of adulation, yet let your head remain whole, not puffed up, not swollen. For if it is puffed up and swollen, it makes a weight and casts you down headlong. *But let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head.*

**18.** *Because still even my prayer is in their good pleasure*[^55]. Wait; now they blame me, says Christ. In the first times of the Christians, the Christians were reproved on every side. *Still* wait; *and my prayer will be in their good pleasure.* The time will come, when there will be more than thousands of men beating their breast and saying: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^56]. Now how many have remained who blush to beat their breast for themselves? So let them reprove; let us bear it. Let them reprove, hate, accuse, detract: *Still even my prayer is in their good pleasure;* the time will come, when my prayer will please them. For they will raise themselves up as it were just by their own strength; they will be conquered in the struggle: because they proudly raised themselves up, they will be dashed down, dragged off by their sins, they will see themselves wicked, the things said through the Prophets will be fulfilled; the judgment will begin to be feared, the keenness of the soul will turn itself to the conscience of sins, and that prayer will please them: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.* O wordy defense of iniquity! Surely now the peoples say this, and the thunder of the peoples beating their breasts does not rest. Rightly do the clouds thunder, in which God now dwells. Where is that wordiness, where is that boasting: I am just, I have done nothing evil? Surely, when in the holy Scriptures you have contemplated the rule of justice, however much you have made progress, you will find yourself a sinner. You have made progress, you now worship one God; very well: you do not fornicate from him to idols, to astrologers, to fortune-tellers, to soothsayers, to augurs, to sorcerers; for that is fornication from the Lord God: you are now in some number of the members of Christ. Now begin to see also those sins of human fellowship. You do not kill anyone, you do not commit adultery with anyone's wife, you do no injury to your wife by going to another, you do not contaminate yourself with any worst corruption, you have kept your hands from theft, your tongue from perjury, your heart from coveting your neighbor's property: now you are just. Attend to the rest; do not now grow proud. Do you sin nothing with your tongue? do you not slip into a hard word? But what great thing do you say? What great thing? *He who says to his brother: You fool; will be liable to the fire of hell*[^57]. Now all that pride trembles. Behold, now he does not greatly do that whereby God may be blasphemed by some impiety; he does not rush upon anyone to harm him, he does not do to another what he is unwilling to suffer: what of the tongue? who tames it? But behold, you have now bridled even it: although who is so great as to perfect this in every way? but behold, you have now bridled even it: what do you do about your thoughts? what do you do about the tumult and troop of rebelling desires? You do not give them your members. So I believe, and see: but yet thoughts sometimes incline and carry you away, very often in prayer on bent knees. You prostrate your body, you bend your neck, you confess sins, you adore God: I see where the body lies, I ask where the mind flits. I see the members lying: let us see whether the conscience stands, let us see whether it is fixed on him whom it adores; whether it is not very often, by thoughts, as by the heat of the sea, snatched away, and lifted by a storm into one thing and another. Now if you were speaking with me, and suddenly turned yourself to your slave, and left me—I do not say one from whom you were asking something, but one with whom you were speaking as an equal—would I not reckon an injury done to me? Behold what you do daily to God. Of what kind, did I just now say, brothers? Now even him who worships only God, who confesses Christ, who knows the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit as one God, who does not fornicate after him, who does not adore demons, who does not seek helps for himself from the devil, who holds the Catholic Church; of whose fraud no one complains, over whose pressure the weak neighbor does not groan; who does not tempt another's wife, who is content with his own, or who is content not even with his own, but as is permitted and as the apostolic discipline allows, where there is the consent of both, or where she has not yet been taken to wife. He who is now such, is yet caught in those things which I have said.

**19.** [v. 6.] So the time has come which was said: *Still even my prayer is in their good pleasure;* whether that which he taught, or that by which he intercedes for us. In all these daily sins, then, what hope is there, except that with a humble heart we say in the Lord's prayer, which is now in our good pleasure, of those who do not defend their sins, but confess them: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^58]; and that we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just, that he himself may be the propitiation for our sins[^59]? Now let the proud speak; they are conquered in number, conquered by the peoples, the whole earth from the sun's rising to its setting praising the name of the Lord. What do the few who dispute otherwise accomplish? They are the judges of the impious. But what is it to you? See what follows: *Their judges were swallowed up beside the rock*[^60]. What is: *They were swallowed up beside the rock*? *But the rock was Christ*[^61]. *They were swallowed up beside the rock.* *Beside*, that is, compared—the judges, great, powerful, learned: they are called *their judges*, as it were judging about morals, and bringing forth a sentence. Aristotle said this. Join him to the rock, and he is swallowed up. Who is Aristotle? Let him hear: Christ said it; and among the dead he trembles. Pythagoras said this, Plato said this. Join them to the rock, compare their authority to the evangelical authority, compare the puffed-up to the crucified. Let us say to them: You wrote your letters in the hearts of the proud; he fixed his cross in the hearts of kings. Finally, he died, and rose again: you are dead, and I do not wish to ask how you may rise. So: *Their judges were swallowed up beside* that *rock.* They seem to say something, until they are compared to the rock. Therefore if any of them is found to have said this which Christ also said; we give him congratulation, we do not follow him. But he was before Christ. If anyone speaks true things, is he before the very Truth? O man, attend to Christ, not when he came to you, but when he made you. Even a sick man can say: But I fell into my bed before the physician had come to me. Surely the physician came later for this reason, because you fell first.

**20.** See, then, the text of the Psalm: *Still even my prayer is in their good pleasure.* But there will be many who contradict: *Their judges were swallowed up beside the rock.* What then will there be? *They will hear my words, because they have prevailed*[^62]. My words have prevailed over their words. Certain things were said by them eloquently, but by me truly. One thing is to praise a talkative man, another to praise a truthful one. *They will hear my words, because they have prevailed.* Whence have they prevailed? Which of them was caught in a sacrifice, when by these laws those things were forbidden, and did not deny it? Which of them was caught adoring an idol, and did not cry out: I did not do it, and fear lest he be convicted? Such ministers the devil had. But whence have the words of the Lord prevailed? *Behold, I send you as lambs in the midst of wolves. Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul; but fear him who can send both body and soul into the fire of hell*[^63]. He gave fear, he subjoined hope, he kindled love. Do not, he said, fear death. Do you fear death? I die first. Do you dread lest even a hair of yours perish? I rise first whole in the flesh. Rightly you have heard his words, because they have prevailed. They spoke, and were killed; they fell, and stood. And what came about from so many deaths of the martyrs, except that the very words prevailed, and, the earth being as it were watered by the blood of the witnesses of Christ, the crop of the Church sprouted everywhere? *They will hear*, he says, *my words, because they have prevailed.* Whence have they prevailed? We have already said: when they are preached by those who do not fear. Who do not fear what? Neither exiles, nor losses, nor death, nor the cross. For not death alone; but not even the cross, than which death nothing seemed more execrable. This the Lord himself took up, lest his disciples should not even fear death, but should not even shudder at the kind of death. So when these things are said by those who do not fear, they have prevailed.

**21.** [v. 7.] What then of those deaths of all the martyrs, what did they do? Hear: *As the thickness of the earth is broken up over the earth, our bones have been scattered beside hell*[^64]. Beside hell the bones of the martyrs were scattered, that is, the bodies of the witnesses of Christ. For the martyrs were killed, and as it were those prevailed who killed them. They prevailed by persecuting, that the words of Christ might prevail by being preached. And what came about from the deaths of the saints? *As the thickness of the earth is broken up over the earth, our bones have been scattered beside hell.* What is, *the thickness of the earth is broken up over the earth*? We know that the thickness of the earth is the things that are contemptible. The things that are as it were contemptible to men, make the earth fruitful. For it was said in a certain psalm that the deaths of the saints lay unburied; and there was no one to bury them[^65]. But all those deaths became the thickness of the earth. As the earth takes a certain richness from contemptible and cast-off things; so from what this world despised, the earth took thickness, that from it the crop of the Church might sprout the more fruitfully. You know already, brothers, that the contemptible things of this earth, by which the earth grows fat, I neither wish to name, nor is it fitting to say: there is a certain fattening of the earth, and a certain richness; and it is contemptible to men, and as it were filthy, and is cast away. But what did he do here, that I may now use his words too? *From the earth* he raised up *the needy, and from the dunghill* he exalted *the poor man; that he might set him with the princes, with the princes of his people*[^66]. For he was prostrate on the earth; as the fatness of the earth, he was broken up over the earth: so lay that Lazarus full of sores, but yet was lifted up by the angels into the bosom of Abraham[^67]. *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^68]. As it is contemptible to the world, so it is precious to the farmer. For there he knows the usefulness and the rich sap, and knows what he aims at, what he chooses, from which a fertile crop rises: but this world despises it. Do you not know that *God chose the contemptible things of the world, and the things that are not, as the things that are, that the things that are might be brought to nothing*[^69]? From the dunghill was raised up Peter, and Paul; and when they were killed, they were despised: now, the earth being fattened from them, the crop of the Church rising up, behold what is noble and chief in the world, the emperor comes to Rome; whither does he hasten? to the temple of the Emperor, or to the memorial of the Fisherman? *For as the thickness of the earth is broken up over the earth, our bones have been scattered beside hell.*

**22.** [v. 8.] *Because to you, O Lord, are my eyes: in you I have hoped; do not take away my soul*[^70]. For they were tormented in the persecutions, and many failed. And because he said of the captivity of persecution: *As the thickness of the earth is broken up over the earth, our bones have been scattered beside hell*: it occurs that many failed and many were in peril; and as it were in the midst of the tribulations of the persecutions the voice of one praying was sent forth: *Because to you, O Lord, are my eyes.* I do not care what those who stand around threaten; *to you, O Lord, are my eyes.* I fix my eye more on your promises than on their threats. I know what you suffered for me, what you promised me: *To you, O Lord, are my eyes: in you I have hoped; do not take away my soul.*

**23.** [v. 9.] *Guard me from the trap which they have set for me*[^71]. What was the trap? If you consent, I spare you. In the trap is set the bait of this life: if a bird loves this bait, it falls into the trap: but if the bird is such as to say: *And the day of man I have not coveted, you know*[^72]; its eyes will not withdraw from God, and he himself will pluck its feet from the snare[^73]. *Guard me from the trap which they have set for me, and from the scandals of those who work iniquity.* He said two things, both to be distinguished from each other: the persecutors, he said, set a trap; but the scandals are from those who consented and apostatized; and from both he wishes to be guarded. On this side the threateners rage, on this side the consenters slip: I fear lest this man be such as I should fear; lest that man be such as I should imitate. These things I do to you, if you do not consent: *Guard me from the trap which they have set for me.* Behold, your brother has now consented: *And from the scandals of those who work iniquity.*

**24.** [v. 10.] *The sinners will fall into his nets*[^74]. What is it, then, brothers: *The sinners will fall into his nets*? But not all sinners: certain sinners, who are so far sinners that they so love this life as to prefer it to eternal life, will fall into his trap. But what do you say? Do you suppose that such men as these fall into his nets? But what of your disciples, O Christ? Behold, when the persecution was raging, when they all left you alone, and each one went his own way (you foretold this, because you foresaw it: for not because you foretold it, did you do it, or did you deny yourself in anyone): but behold, those who clung to you, in your temptation and persecution, in which the enemies sought you to be crucified, failed. And that one bold man who had promised you that he would be with you even to death, indeed heard from the physician what was going on in himself the sick man. For, feverish, he had said he was healthy; but the physician was touching the vein of his heart. It came to the temptation, it came to the proving, it came to the accusation; and now he himself, questioned not by some great power, but by the lowest servant, and that a woman, questioned by a maidservant, succumbed. He denied three times. The first denial made, when he was reminded, he denied again: the second denial made, when he was reminded, he denied a third time. This the Lord had foretold; he had not commanded it, had not compelled it. Or if Peter is therefore thought to have acted rightly, because the Lord had foretold this; then Judas too who betrayed him acted rightly, because the Lord had foretold this too. Far from it, my brothers; this is the voice of those elect, who even defend their sins rather than confess them. Let us rather attend to Peter himself. If he sinned nothing, why did he weep? Let us not ask about Peter, except the tears of Peter: we find no more faithful witnesses about him. *He wept bitterly*[^75], he says. He was not yet fit for the passion: *You will follow me afterward*[^76], it was said to him. He was still to be firm, confirmed by the Lord's resurrection.

**25.** So it was not yet the time that those bones should be scattered beside hell. For see how many failed, even those first ones clinging to his mouth; they too failed. Why this? *I am alone, until I pass over.* For this follows in the Psalm. Above he had said: *Guard me, O Lord, from the trap which they have set for me, and from the scandals of those who work iniquity. From the trap, and from the scandals*; both from those who terrify, and from those who have lapsed. But because in his passion even those first ones, who were to be the leaders of the Church and the pillars of the earth, failed; what he says in another psalm had not yet come to pass: *I have confirmed its pillars*[^77]. What does he say: *I am alone, until I pass over*? This voice is from the person of the head: *I am alone, until I pass over.* What is, *alone*? In the passion you alone suffer, you alone are killed by the enemies. *I am alone, until I pass over.* What is, *until I pass over*? The evangelist says: *But when the hour had come that Jesus should pass over from this world to the Father*[^78]. What is, then, *until I pass over*, except, *from this world to the Father*? For then I confirmed its pillars, that is, the pillars of the earth, when in my resurrection they plainly learned that death is not to be feared: *until*, then, *I pass over, I am alone*: when I have passed over, I will be multiplied; many will imitate me, many will suffer for my name. I am one, until I pass over; many in me will be one when I have passed over. *I am alone, until I pass over.* Hear too the mystery of his word. According to the Greek manner of speech, Pascha seems to signify suffering; for 'paschein' is said 'to suffer': but according to the Hebrew tongue, as those have interpreted who knew it, Pascha is interpreted 'Passing-over.' For even if you ask the Greeks well, they deny that Pascha is Greek. There does sound in it 'pascho,' that is, 'I suffer,' but it is not usually inflected thus: for suffering in Greek is called 'pathos,' not 'pascha.' So Pascha, as those say who knew it, and who interpreted for us what we might read, is interpreted 'passing-over.' Therefore, the Lord's passion approaching, as though he spoke with that very word, the evangelist said: *When the hour had come that Jesus should pass over from this world to the Father.* So Pascha sounded here in this verse: *I am alone, until I pass over.* After the Pascha I will no longer be alone, after the passing-over I will not be alone. Many will imitate, many will follow: and if they follow afterward, what will it be? *I am alone, until I pass over.* What is it that the Lord says in this psalm: *I am alone, until I pass over*? what is it we have expounded? If we have understood it, attend to his own words in the Gospel: *Amen, amen I say to you*, he says, *unless a grain of wheat falling into the earth dies, it itself remains alone; but if it dies, it brings forth much fruit*[^79]. He said this there, where he also said: *When I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all things to myself*[^80]. *Unless a grain of wheat*, he says, *falling into the earth dies, it itself remains alone; but if it dies, it brings forth much fruit.* So to that grain a great crop was owed: but wait, let it still be put to death; for a grain, unless it falls into the earth and is put to death, remains alone.

**26.** So he was alone, before he was put to death. Therefore Peter too did not yet have such strength: he was to receive the strength of following, he did not have that of going before[^81]. For on account of Christ, that is, on account of the confession of the name of Christ, by which we are Christians, no one died before Christ: lest perhaps it occur to you. For many died and are martyrs, many prophets suffered such things; yet they did not die because they foretold Christ, but because they spoke the sins of men against them, and resisted their iniquities more freely: and they are reckoned among the martyrs. For justly, if not for the confession of the name of Christ, yet for the truth they were killed. But so far for the name itself, that is, for the confession of the name of Christ, no one died before that grain fell into the earth, from whose person it was said: *I am alone, until I pass over*, that even John himself, who had recently been killed, given by the wicked king to the dancing girl, was not killed for this, because he confessed Christ[^82]. He could surely have been killed for that too, and by many. If he was killed for something else by one man, how much more could he have been killed for Christ by those very men who killed Christ? For John was bearing witness to Christ. Those who heard Christ wished to kill him, and did not kill the one who bore witness. For if an attack were made on John on account of Christ, he would not deny it. (For there was in him great strength, by which he was called the friend of the bridegroom[^83]. Great grace in him, great excellence: *Among those born of women none has risen greater than John the Baptist*[^84].) So an attack was made on the one who did not have such strength: an attack is made on Peter, none is made on John. For Peter received that very strength afterward; but then he was weak. For the name of Christ he is questioned who did not have strength: he who had strength does not suffer persecution for Christ; lest for the name of Christ he should go before Christ. He is not killed by the Jews, who bore free witness to Christ, whom the Jews killed; and he is killed by Herod, because he said to him: *It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife*[^85]. For his brother had not died without offspring. For the law of truth indeed, for equity, for justice: therefore holy, therefore a martyr; but yet not for that name by which we are Christians. Why this, except that it might be fulfilled: *I am alone, until I pass over*?
