# Exposition of Psalm 68

**1.** We have arisen and been gathered into the people of God in this age at that time when now that herb from the grain of mustard has stretched out its branches; when now that leaven, which at first was contemptible, has leavened three measures, that is, the whole world restored from the three sons of Noah: since from east and west and north and south come those who will recline with the Patriarchs, those being cast out who were born of their flesh, and did not imitate their faith. To this glory of the Church of Christ, then, we have opened our eyes; and that barren one, to whom joy was foretold and promised, that she would have more sons than she who had a husband, we have now found such that she had forgotten the reproaches and ignominy of her widowhood: and therefore we can perhaps wonder when we read the voices of the humility of Christ, or of our own, in some prophecy. And it can happen that we are less affected by them, because we did not come at that time when these things were read with relish, amid abounding pressure. But again, if we consider the abundance of tribulations, and recognize on what way we walk (if indeed we walk on that one), how narrow it is, and how it leads through pressures and tribulations to everlasting rest; and how that very thing which in human affairs is called happiness is more to be feared than misery; since misery very often brings from tribulation a good fruit, but happiness corrupts the soul by a perverse security, and gives place to the devil the tempter: when, then, we have considered prudently and rightly, that, like a salted victim, human life upon earth is a temptation, and that no one at all is secure, nor ought to be secure, until one comes to that homeland from which no friend goes out, into which no enemy is admitted, even now in that very glory of the Church we recognize the voices of our tribulation; and as members of Christ, with the joining of love subjected to our head, and holding to one another, we shall say of the Psalms what we find the martyrs to have said here who were before us, because tribulation is common to all, from the beginning even to the end. Yet this psalm which we have undertaken to treat, and of which we have proposed to speak to Your Charity in the name of the Lord, let us recognize in the grain of mustard: let us for a little while call away our thought from the height of the herb and the spreading of the branches, and from that glory where the birds of heaven rest; and let us hear from how small a thing this greatness which delights us in the herb has risen. For Christ speaks here (but we say it to those who already know); Christ, not only the head, but also the body. From the very words we recognize it. For that Christ speaks here we are not at all permitted to doubt. For here are the express words which were fulfilled in his passion: *They gave gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink*[^1]: when these things were fulfilled even to the letter, and rendered exactly as they were foretold. And when Christ himself had said: *I thirst,* hanging on the cross, and at this word vinegar in a sponge was given him, which having received he said: *It is consummated,* and so, his head bowed, gave up the spirit, showing that all these things which were foretold were then consummated in him; it is not permitted here to understand anything else. The Apostles too, speaking of Christ, gave testimonies from this psalm. But who would deviate from their sentences? or what lamb would not follow the rams? Therefore Christ speaks here: and it is rather to be demonstrated by us where his members speak, that we may show that the whole one speaks here, than to be doubted that Christ speaks.

**2.** [v. 1] The title of the Psalm is: *Unto the end, for those who will be changed, for David himself.*[^2] Now hear of the change for the better: for the change is either for the worse, or for the better. Adam and Eve for the worse; those who from Adam and Eve cleaved to Christ were changed for the better. *For as through one man death, so also through one man the resurrection of the dead: and as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*[^3]. From that which God formed, Adam was changed; but for the worse of his iniquity: from that which iniquity worked, the faithful are changed; but for the better through the grace of God. That we were changed for the worse, was our iniquity: that we are changed for the better, not our righteousness, but the grace of God furnishes. That, then, we have been changed for the worse, let us impute to ourselves: that we are changed for the better, let us praise God. *For those,* then, is this psalm, *who will be changed.* But whence was this change made, except from the passion of Christ? Pascha itself is interpreted in Latin "Passage." For Pascha is not a Greek name, but Hebrew. It does indeed resound in the Greek tongue suffering, because *paschein* is "to suffer"; but, the Hebrew speech being consulted, it indicates another thing. Pascha commends a passage. Which John the evangelist too admonished, who, the passion being imminent, when the Lord came to the supper at which he commended the sacrament of his body and blood, so speaks: *But when the hour had come, in which Jesus would pass over from this world to the Father*[^4]. He expressed, then, the passage of the Pascha. But unless he himself passed over from here to the Father, who came for our sake, how could we pass over from here, who descended not to lift anything, but fell? But he did not fall; but descended, that he might lift up him who had fallen. The passage, then, both his, and ours, is from here to the Father; from this world to the kingdom of heaven, from mortal life to eternal life, from earthly life to heavenly life, from corruptible life to incorruptible, from the conduct of tribulations to perpetual security. Therefore: *For those who will be changed,* is the title of the Psalm. Let us, then, mark, recognize, groan together over the cause of our change, that is, the very passion of the Lord, and our voice in tribulations, in the text of the Psalm: and by hearing, recognizing, groaning together, let us be changed, that the title of the Psalm may be fulfilled in us: *For those who will be changed.*

**3.** [v. 2] *Save me, O God, because waters have entered even to my soul.*[^5] That is the grain now despised, which seems to give humble voices. In the garden it is buried, while the world will marvel at the greatness of the herb, of which herb the seed was despised by the Jews. For truly consider the mustard seed, minute, dark, altogether to be scorned; so that there be fulfilled what was said: *We saw him, and he had no form nor comeliness*[^6]. But he says that the waters had entered even to his soul; because those crowds, which he signified by the name of waters, could prevail thus far, that they killed Christ. They prevailed to despise, to hold, to bind, to insult, to buffet, to spit upon. Still how far? Even unto death. Therefore *waters have entered even to my soul.* For he called this life his soul, as far as they could approach by raging. But could they do this, unless they were permitted by him? Whence, then, does he cry out as though he suffered something unwilling, except because the head prefigures his members? For he indeed suffered, because he willed; but the martyrs, even though they were unwilling. For to Peter he so foretold his passion: *When you have grown old,* he says, *another will gird you, and lead you where you do not wish*[^7]. For although we desire to cleave to Christ, yet we are unwilling to die; and therefore we suffer gladly, or rather patiently, because no other passage is given by which we may cleave to Christ. For if we could come to Christ in another way, that is, to eternal life, who would wish to die? For our nature, that is, a certain fellowship of soul and body, and a certain familiarity of gluing and joining in these two, the Apostle, expounding in a certain place, says that we have a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens; that is, an immortality prepared for us, with which we are to be clothed in the end, when we have risen from the dead; and he says: *In which we do not wish to be unclothed, but clothed over, that the mortal may be swallowed up by life*[^8]: If it could be done, we would so wish, he says, to become immortal, that immortality itself would now come, and now change us as we are, that this our mortal might be swallowed up by life, that the body might not be laid aside through death, to be received again in the end. Although, then, we pass over from evils to goods; yet that very passage is somewhat bitter, and has gall, which the Jews gave to the Lord in the passion, it has something sharp to be borne, by which are shown those who gave him vinegar to drink. Prefiguring, then, and transforming ourselves into himself, he says this: *Save me, O God, because waters have entered even to my soul.* Those who persecuted could even kill; but they will not have anything more to do. For the Lord himself sent before an exhortation, saying: *Do not fear those who kill the body, and have nothing more to do; but fear him who has power to kill both body and soul in the gehenna of fire*[^9]. By a greater fear we despise lesser things, and by a greater desire of eternity we disdain all temporal things. For here both temporal delights are sweet, and temporal tribulations are bitter: but who would not drink the cup of temporal tribulation, fearing the fire of gehenna; and who would not despise the sweetness of the age, gaping after the sweetness of eternal life? Hence, then, that we may be freed, let us cry out, lest perhaps in pressures we consent to iniquity, and be truly irreparably swallowed up: *Save me, O God, because waters have entered even to my soul.*

**4.** [v. 3] *I am fixed in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance.*[^10] What does he mean by mire? or those very ones who persecuted? For of mire man was made. But these, by falling away from righteousness, were made the mire of the deep; whom persecuting and desiring to drag to iniquity, whoever does not consent, of his own mire makes gold. For his mire will deserve to be turned into the heavenly condition, and to become a companion of those of whom the title of the Psalm says: *For those who will be changed.* But these, since they were the mire of the deep, I stuck in them; that is, they held me, prevailed against me, killed me. *I am fixed,* then, *in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance.* What is this, *there is no substance*? Is the very mire not a substance? Or, by cleaving, was I made to be no substance? What, then, is, *I am fixed*? Did Christ so stick? Or did he stick, and not, as it was said in the book of Job: *The earth was handed over into the hands of the ungodly*[^11]? Or was he fixed according to the body, because he could be held, and had the very crucifixion? For unless he had been fixed with nails, he would not have been crucified. Whence, then, *there is no substance*? Or is that mire not a substance? We shall understand, then, if we can, what is, *and there is no substance,* if we first understand what substance is. For substance is said also of riches: according to which we say: He has substance, and: He has lost his substance. But shall we think this was here said, *and there is no substance,* that is, there are no riches, as though something about riches were now treated, or some question about riches were had? Or perhaps because that very mire was poverty, and there will be no riches, except when we have been made partakers of eternity? For then are the true riches, when nothing will be lacking to us. According to the understanding of this word too this sense can be taken, that it be said: *I am fixed in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance,* that is, I came to poverty. For here he says: *I am poor and grieving;* the Apostle too says: *That for your sake he became poor, when he was rich, that by his poverty you might be made rich*[^12]. Therefore, wishing to commend to us his poverty, the Lord perhaps said, *there is no substance.* For he came to the highest poverty, when he put on the form of a servant. What are his riches? *Who, being in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^13]. These are great and incomparable riches. Whence, then, this poverty? Because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man; he humbled himself, made obedient unto death*[^14]: so that he said: *Waters have entered even to my soul.* Add above death: what more will you add? The ignominy of death. Therefore there follows: *Even the death of the cross*[^15]. Great poverty! But hence will be great riches; because as his poverty was fulfilled, so will our riches too be fulfilled from his poverty. How great riches he has, that of his poverty he made us rich! How great will he make us of his riches, whom he made rich of his poverty!

**5.** *I am fixed in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance.*[^16] Substance is understood in another way, that which we are, whatever we are. But this is somewhat more difficult to understand, although the things are familiar: but because the word is unfamiliar, it needs commendation and a little exposition; to which, however, if you are attentive, perhaps we shall not labor over it. A man is called, a beast is called, earth is called, heaven is called, the sun, moon, stone, sea, air are called: all these are substances, by the very fact that they are. The natures themselves are called substances. God is a certain substance; for what is no substance is nothing at all. Substance, then, is to be something. Whence also in the catholic Faith, against the poisons of certain heretics, we are so built up that we say the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit are of one substance. What is, of one substance? For example: if the Father is gold, gold is the Son too, gold the Holy Spirit too. Whatever the Father is in that he is God, this the Son, this the Holy Spirit. But when he is the Father, it is not that which he is. For the Father is said not in relation to himself, but to the Son: but in relation to himself he is called God. And so in that he is God, by that very thing he is substance. And because the Son is of the same substance, without doubt the Son too is God. But what the Father is; because it is not the name of a substance, but is referred to the Son, we do not so say that the Son is the Father, as we say that the Son is God. You ask what the Father is: it is answered, God. You ask what the Son is: it is answered, God. You ask what the Father and the Son are: it is answered, God. Asked about the Father alone, answer God: asked about the Son alone, answer God: asked about both, answer not gods, but God. Not as among men. You ask what father Abraham is: it is answered, A man; his substance is answered. You ask what his son Isaac is: it is answered, A man; of the same substance Abraham and Isaac. You ask what Abraham and Isaac are: it is not answered, A man; but, Men. Not so in divine things. For there so great is the fellowship of substance, that it admits equality, but does not admit plurality. If, then, it be said to you: When you tell me the Son is this which the Father is, surely the Son too is the Father; answer: According to substance I told you the Son is this which the Father is, not according to that which is said in relation to another. For in relation to himself he is called God, in relation to the Father he is called Son. And again the Father in relation to himself is called God, in relation to the Son is called Father. What is said the Father in relation to the Son, is not the Son: what is said the Son in relation to the Father, is not the Father: what is said the Father in relation to himself, and the Son in relation to himself, this is the Father and the Son, that is, God. What is, then, *there is no substance*? According to this understanding of substance, how shall we be able to understand this sentence of the Psalm: *I am fixed in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance*? God made man; he made a substance: and would that he remained in that which God made! If man remained in that which God made, he would not be fixed in that which God begot. But further, because through iniquity man slipped from the substance in which he was made (for iniquity itself is not a substance; for iniquity is not a nature which God formed, but iniquity is a perversity which man made): the Son of God came to the mire of the deep, and was fixed; and there was no substance in which he was fixed; because he was fixed in their iniquity. *I am fixed in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance.* All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. All natures were made through him: iniquity was not made through him, because iniquity was not made. Those substances were made through him which praise him. The whole creation praising God is mentioned by the three young men in the furnace; and from earthly things to heavenly, or from heavenly to earthly, the hymn of those praising God passes. Not that all these things have the sense of praising; but because all things, well considered, produce praise, and the heart is filled with the consideration of the creature to burst forth a hymn to the Creator. All things praise God, but those which God made. Did you notice in that hymn that avarice praises God? There the serpent too praises God; avarice does not praise. For all creeping things were named there in the praise of God: all creeping things were named; but no vices were named there. For vices we have from ourselves and from our own will; and vices are not a substance. In these the Lord was fixed, when he suffered persecution; in the vice of the Jews, not in the substance of men which was made through him. *I am fixed,* he says, *in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance.* I am fixed, and I did not find what I made.

**6.** *I came into the depth of the sea, and the storm sank me.*[^17] Thanks to his mercy who came into the depth of the sea, and deigned to be swallowed by the sea-monster: but he was vomited out on the third day. He came into the depth of the sea, in which depth we were sunk, in which depth we had suffered shipwreck: he came there himself, and the storm sank him; because he suffered there the waves, the very men; the storms, the voices of those saying: *Crucify, crucify.* While Pilate said: *I find no cause in this man for which he should be killed,* the voices of those saying: *Crucify, crucify*[^18], grew strong. The storm increased, until he was sunk who had come into the depth of the sea. And the Lord suffered among the hands of the Jews what he did not suffer when he walked upon the waters; what not only he himself had not suffered, but had not even permitted Peter to suffer. *I came into the depth of the sea, and the storm sank me.*

**7.** [v. 4] *I labored crying out; my jaws have become hoarse.*[^19] This where? this when? Let us question the Gospel. For we recognize in this psalm the passion of our Lord. And indeed we know that he suffered; that the waters entered even to his soul, because the peoples prevailed even unto his death, we read, we believe; that he was sunk by the storm, because the sedition prevailed to kill him, we recognize: but that he labored crying out, and that his jaws became hoarse, we not only do not read, but we even read the contrary, that he did not answer them a word, that there might be fulfilled what was said in another psalm: *I became like a man not hearing, and not having in his mouth refutations*[^20]; and what was prophesied in Isaiah: *Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before the shearer, so he did not open his mouth*[^21]. If he became like a man not hearing, and not having in his mouth refutations, how did he labor crying out, and his jaws become hoarse? Or did he then keep silent for this reason, because he was hoarse, who had cried out so much without cause? And that voice of his indeed from a certain psalm on the cross we know: *O God, my God, why have you forsaken me*[^22]? But how great was that voice, or how long-lasting, that in it his jaws became hoarse? He cried out long: *Woe to you scribes, and Pharisees*[^23]: he cried out long: *Woe to the world from scandals*[^24]. And truly how, as it were hoarse, he cried out, and therefore was not understood, when the Jews said: *What is it he says? This word is hard; who can hear it? We do not know what he says*[^25]. He spoke all words; but his jaws were hoarse to those who did not understand his voices. *I labored crying out; my jaws have become hoarse.*

**8.** *My eyes failed from hoping in my God.*[^26] Far be it that this be taken of the person of the head: far be it that his eyes failed from hoping in his God, in whom rather God was reconciling the world to himself, and who was the Word made flesh, and dwelt among us, so that not only God was in him, but he himself too was God. Not so, then; not the eyes of our head failed from hoping in his God; but his eyes failed in his body, that is, in his members. This is the voice of the members, this the voice of the body, not of the head. How, then, do we find it in his body and members? What else shall I say? what else shall I mention? When he suffered, when he died, all the disciples despaired that he was the Christ. By the thief the Apostles were conquered, who then believed, when they failed. See his members despairing; attend to those two whom after the resurrection he found on the way speaking among themselves, of whom one was Cleopas, when their eyes were held lest they recognize him. For how would they recognize with their eyes him from whom they had wavered in mind? Something had been done in their eyes like their mind. For they were speaking among themselves, and when they were addressed by him about what they were speaking of, they answered: *Do you alone sojourn in Jerusalem? Do you not know the things that have been done, how Jesus of Nazareth, mighty in deeds and words, was killed by the elders and chief priests? And we hoped that it was he who would redeem Israel*[^27]. They had hoped, and they did not hope. Their eyes failed from hoping in their God. He transfigured them, then, into himself by saying: *My eyes failed from hoping in my God.* This hope he restored, when he offered his scars to be handled; which, when Thomas had touched, he returned to the hope which he had lost, and cried out: *My Lord, and my God.* Your eyes failed from hoping in your God: you handled the scars, and found your God; you handled the form of the servant, and recognized your Lord. To whom yet the Lord himself says: *Because you have seen, you have believed.* And foretelling us with the voice of his mercy: *Blessed,* he says, *are those who do not see and believe*[^28]. *My eyes failed from hoping in my God.*

**9.** [v. 5] *They were multiplied beyond the hairs of my head, those who hated me without cause.*[^29] How multiplied? That they added to themselves even one of the twelve. *They were multiplied beyond the hairs of my head, those who hated me without cause.* To the hairs of his head he compared his enemies. Deservedly they were shaved off, when in the place of Calvary he was crucified. Let the members receive this voice; let them learn to be hated without cause. For now if it is necessary, Christian, that the world hate you, why do you not make it so that it hate you without cause, that in the body of your Lord, and in this psalm foretold of him, you may recognize your voice? How will it come about that the world hate you without cause? If you harm no one, and are thus hated: for this is without cause, without reason. Let it be too little for you to be hated without cause; furnish in addition that they requite you evil for good. *My enemies who persecute me unjustly have been made strong.* What he said: *They were multiplied beyond the hairs of my head;* this afterward: *My enemies have been made strong*: and what at first, *who hated me without cause;* this afterward, *who persecute me unjustly.* What, then, is *without cause,* this is *unjustly.* This is the voice of the martyrs, not in the punishment, but in the cause. To suffer persecution, to be held, to be scourged, to be shut up, to be proscribed, to be killed, is not praise; but, having a good cause, to suffer these things, this is praise. For praise is in the goodness of the cause, not in the bitterness of the punishment. For however great the sufferings of the martyrs were, are they equaled to the sufferings of all robbers, all sacrilegious men, all the wicked? For what, does the world hate these too? It hates them plainly. For they exceed the middle measure of the world by the greatness of their malice, and are in a manner alien from the very fellowship of worldly men, infesting even earthly peace; and they suffer many evils, but not without cause. Finally, see the voice of that thief hanging with the Lord on the cross, when one of the two thieves insulted the crucified Lord from the other side, and said: *If you are the Son of God, free yourself*: the other restrained him, and said: *Do you not fear God, even because you are set in the same condemnation? And we indeed justly for our deeds*[^30]. Behold, not without cause: but by confession he poured out from himself the corruption, and became fit food for the Lord. He shut out his iniquity, accused it, and was rid of it. Behold there two thieves, there too the Lord; and they crucified, and he crucified: and the world hated them, but not without cause; and it hated him, but without cause. *What I did not plunder, I then paid back.* This is without cause. I did not plunder, and I paid back; I did not sin, and I gave penalties. For he alone is such, he truly plundered nothing. Not only did he plunder nothing, but even what he had not from plunder, he emptied himself of, that he might come to us. *For he did not count it robbery to be equal to God;* and yet *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^31]. He altogether did not plunder. But who plundered? Adam. Who plundered first? That very one who seduced Adam. How did the devil plunder? *I will set my seat to the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^32]. He usurped to himself what he had not received: behold robbery. The devil usurped to himself what he had not received, lost what he had received; and from the very cup of his pride, he offered it to drink to him whom he wished to deceive: *Taste,* he says, *and you will be like gods*[^33]. They wished to plunder divinity, they lost happiness. He, then, plundered, and therefore paid back. But I, he says, *what I did not plunder, I then paid back.* The Lord himself, approaching the passion, so speaks in the Gospel: Behold, *the prince of this world comes,* that is, the devil, *and in me he will find nothing,* that is, he will not find why he should kill me; *but that all may know that I do the will of my Father, rise, let us go*[^34]: and he went to the passion, to pay what he did not plunder. For what is, *in me he will find nothing*? No fault. Did the devil lose anything from his house? Let that one examine plunderers; *he will find nothing in me.* Yet he says he plundered nothing, attending to sin, usurped nothing that was not his; for this is of robbery, this of iniquity: for from the devil himself he wrested those whom that one had plundered. *No one,* he says, *enters into the house of the strong one, and plunders his vessels, unless he first bind the strong one*[^35]. He bound the strong one, and plundered his vessels, certainly he did not plunder; but he answers you: Those vessels had perished from my great house; I did not commit theft, but received back theft.

**10.** [v. 6] *O God, you knew my imprudence.*[^36] Again from the mouth of the body. For what imprudence in Christ? Is he not himself the Power of God and the Wisdom of God? Or does he call that his imprudence, of which the Apostle says: *The foolish thing of God is wiser than men*[^37]? *My imprudence*: this very thing which they mocked in me who seem to themselves to be wise. You knew why it was done: *you knew my imprudence.* For what is so like imprudence, as when, having in his power to lay low his persecutors with one voice, he allowed himself to be held, scourged, spat upon, buffeted, crowned with thorns, fixed to the wood? It is like imprudence; it seems foolish: but this foolish thing surpasses all the wise. It is indeed foolish: but even a grain, when it falls into the earth, if no one knows the custom of farmers, seems foolish. With great labor it is reaped, carried to the threshing floor, threshed, winnowed: after so great perils of sky and storms, and the labors of countrymen and the care of the masters, the cleansed grain is put into the barn. Winter comes; and what had been cleansed is brought forth and cast: it seems imprudence; but, that it be not imprudence, hope makes. He, then, did not spare himself; because the Father too *did not spare him, but handed him over for us all*[^38]. And of himself, *Who loved me,* says the Apostle, *and handed over himself for me*[^39]: because *a grain, unless it falls into the earth that it may die, fruit,* he says, *it will not yield*[^40]. This is the imprudence; but you knew it: but these, if they had known, would never have crucified the Lord of glory. *O God, you knew my imprudence; and my offenses are not hidden from you.* This plainly, lucidly, openly, because it is to be understood from the mouth of the body. Christ had no offenses; he was a taker-up of offenses, but not a committer. *And my offenses are not hidden from you*: that is, I have confessed to you all my offenses; and before my face you saw them in my thought, you saw the wounds that you might heal. But where? Certainly in the body, in the members; in those faithful, whence that member which was confessing its sins already cleaved to him. *And my offenses,* he says, *are not hidden from you.*

**11.** [v. 7] *Let not those who await you be put to shame in me, O Lord, Lord of powers.*[^41] Again the voice of the head: *Let them not be put to shame in me*: let it not be said to them: Where is he in whom you presumed? let it not be said to them: Where is he who said to you: *Believe in God, and believe in me*[^42]? *Let not those who await you be put to shame in me, O Lord, Lord of powers. Let not those who seek you be confounded over me, O God of Israel.* This can be understood also from the body; but if you do not set his body as one man: because truly his body is not one man, but a small member; the body, however, consists of members. His full body, then, is the whole Church. Deservedly, then, the Church says: *Let not those who await you be put to shame in me, O Lord, Lord of powers.* Let me not be so afflicted by the persecutors rising up, let me not be so worn down by my envying enemies, by the barking heretics who went out from me, because they were not of me; for if they had been of me, they would perhaps have remained with me. Let me not be so pressed by their scandals, *that those who await you be put to shame in me, O Lord, Lord of powers. Let not those who seek you be confounded over me, O God of Israel.*

**12.** [v. 8] *Because for your sake I bore reproach, shamelessness covered my face.*[^43] It is nothing great that is said, *I bore;* but what is said, *for your sake I bore.* For if you bear because you have sinned, you bear for your own sake, not for God's. *For what glory is it to you,* says Peter, *if, sinning, you are punished, and bear it*[^44]? But if you bear because you kept God's commandment, you truly bear for God's sake; and your reward remains forever, because for God's sake you bore reproaches. For therefore he first bore, that we might learn to bear. And if he, who had not that which was laid to him; how much more we who, even if we do not have the sin which the enemy lays to us, yet have another which may worthily be scourged in us? Someone, I know not who, calls you a thief, and you are not a thief: you hear reproach; yet you are not so not a thief, that you are not something which displeases God. Further, if he who had plundered nothing at all, who most truly had said: *Behold, the prince of this world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^45], was called a sinner, was called unrighteous, was called Beelzebub, was called mad; do you disdain, servant, to hear for your merits what the Lord heard for none of his merits? He came that he might furnish you an example. As though he did this in vain, so you do not profit. For why did he hear it, except that you, when you heard it, might not fail? Behold, you hear it, and fail: in vain, then, did he hear it; for not for his own sake, but for your sake he heard it. *Because for your sake I bore reproach, shamelessness covered my face. Shamelessness,* he says, *covered my face.* What is shamelessness? Not to be confounded. Finally it seems as it were a vice, when it is said: He is a shameless man. Great shamelessness of a man, not to blush. So shamelessness is as it were impudence. A Christian must have this shamelessness, when he comes among men to whom Christ is displeasing. If he blushes about Christ, he will be blotted out of the book of the living. It is needful, then, that you have shamelessness, when you are insulted about Christ; when it is said: Worshiper of a crucified man, adorer of one badly dead, venerator of one killed: here if you blush, you are dead. For see the sentence of him who deceives no one: *He who blushes at me before men, I too will blush at him before the angels of God*[^46]. Observe, then, you; let there be in you shamelessness, be brazen when you hear reproach about Christ; be altogether brazen. What do you fear for your forehead, which you have armed with the sign of the cross? For this is: *For your sake I bore reproach, shamelessness covered my face. For your sake I bore reproach*: and because I did not blush about you, when I was insulted for your sake, *shamelessness covered my face.*

**13.** [vv. 9-10] *I became estranged to my brothers, and a stranger to the sons of my mother.*[^47] To the sons of the Synagogue he became a stranger. For in his own country it was said: *Do we not know him to be the son of Mary and Joseph*[^48]? And whence elsewhere: *But this man we do not know whence he is*[^49]? *I became,* then, *a stranger to the sons of my mother.* They did not know whence I am, of whom my flesh was: they did not know me born of the loin of Abraham; whence my flesh was hidden, when, setting his hand under that very loin, the servant swore by the God of heaven. *I became a stranger to the sons of my mother.* Why this? why did they not recognize me? why did they call me a stranger? why did they dare to say: *We do not know whence he is*? *Because zeal for your house has consumed me*: that is, because I persecuted in them their iniquities, because I did not bear patiently those whom I rebuked, because I sought your glory in your house, because I scourged in the temple those misbehaving; where also it is set down: *Zeal for your house has consumed me*[^50]. Hence a stranger, hence an alien; hence: *We do not know whence he is.* They would recognize whence I am, if they recognized what you commanded. For if I found them keeping your commandments, zeal for your house would not consume me. *And the reproaches of those reproaching you have fallen upon me.* This testimony the apostle Paul too used (that very reading was just now read), and says: *Whatever things were written before, were written that we might be taught; that through the consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope*[^51]. He said, then, this was the voice of Christ: *The reproaches of those reproaching you have fallen upon me.* Why, *you*? Is it the Father who is reproached, and not Christ himself? Why, *the reproaches of those reproaching you have fallen upon me*? Because he who knew me, knew the Father too: because no one reproached Christ, except by reproaching God: because no one honors the Father, except him who honors the Son too. *The reproaches of those reproaching you have fallen upon me,* because they found me.

**14.** [v. 11] *And I covered my soul in fasting; and it became a reproach to me.*[^52] The fasting of Christ we have already commended to Your Charity spiritually in another psalm. His fasting was, when all who had believed in him failed; because his hunger too was that there might be belief in him: because his thirst too was, when he said to the woman: *I thirst, give me to drink*[^53]; for he thirsted for her faith. And when on the cross he said: *I thirst*[^54], he sought their faith, for whom he had said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^55]. But what did those men offer to him thirsting? Vinegar. Vinegar is also called old. Deservedly from the old man they offered, because they were unwilling to be new. Why were they unwilling to be new? Because to the title of this psalm where it is written: *For those who will be changed,* they did not pertain. Therefore, *I covered my soul in fasting.* Finally he rejected even the gall which they offered: he chose to fast, rather than to take bitterness. For the embittering ones do not enter into his body, of whom in another place the psalm says: *Those who embitter, let them not be exalted in themselves*[^56]. Therefore, *I covered my soul in fasting; and it became a reproach to me.* This very thing became a reproach to me, that I did not consent to them, that is, I fasted from them. For he who does not consent to those badly persuading, fasts from them; and through this fasting he merits reproach, that for this reason he is insulted, because he does not consent to evil.

**15.** [v. 12] *And I made my garment sackcloth.*[^57] We have already said something of the sackcloth too, whence is that: *But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself in haircloth, and humbled my soul in fasting*[^58]. *I made my garment sackcloth*: that is, I set before them, against whom they might rage, my flesh; I hid my divinity. *Sackcloth,* because it was mortal flesh, that from sin he might condemn sin in the flesh. *And I made my garment sackcloth; and I became to them a parable;* that is, a derision. It is called a parable, when a likeness is given of someone, when ill is spoken of him. So, for example: May that one perish like that one, is a parable; that is, a comparison and likeness of a curse. *I became,* then, *to them a parable.*

**16.** [v. 13] *Against me insulted those who sat in the gate. In the gate,*[^59] nothing other than in public. *And they played on me who were drinking wine.* Do you think, brethren, this happened to Christ only? Daily it happens to him in his members: when perhaps it will be necessary for a servant of God to forbid drunkenness and luxury in some estate or town, where the word of God has not been heard, it is too little that they sing, but they even begin to sing against the very one by whom they are forbidden to sing. Compare now his fasting, and their wine. *And they played on me who were drinking wine*: the wine of error, the wine of impiety, the wine of pride.

**17.** [v. 14] *But I, my prayer to you, O Lord.*[^60] But I was toward you. But how? Toward you by praying. For when you are cursed, and have nothing to do; when reproaches are hurled at you, and you find no way to correct him by whom they are hurled, nothing remains to you but to pray. But remember to pray for him too. *But I, my prayer to you, O Lord. A time of good pleasure, O God.* For behold, the grain is buried; the fruit will rise up. *A time of good pleasure, O God.* Of this time the Prophets too said what the Apostle mentions: *Behold, now is the acceptable time, behold, now is the day of salvation*[^61]. *A time of good pleasure, O God. In the multitude of your mercy.* This is the time of good pleasure, *In the multitude of your mercy.* For if there were not a multitude of your mercy, what should we do about the multitude of our iniquity? *In the multitude of your mercy. Hear me in the truth of your salvation.* Because he said, *of your mercy,* he added the truth too; because mercy and truth are all the ways of the Lord. Why mercy? By forgiving sins. Why truth? By rendering what was promised. *Hear me in the truth of your salvation.*

**18.** [v. 15] *Save me from the mire, that I may not stick fast.*[^62] Of that of which he said above: *I am fixed in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance*[^63]. Accordingly, because you have well received that which was expounded, it is not necessary that you hear it here more fully. From here he says he is to be freed, where before he said he was fixed: *Save me from the mire, that I may not stick fast.* And he himself expounds this: *Let me be rescued from those who hate me.* They, then, are the mire where I had stuck. But this is perhaps suggested. A little before he had said: *I am fixed;* now he says: *Save me from the mire, that I may not stick fast;* when, according to the earlier sentence, he ought to say: Save me from the mire where I had stuck, by rescuing me, not by bringing it about that I do not stick. Therefore he had stuck in flesh, but had not stuck in spirit. He says this from the weakness of his members. When perhaps you are caught by one who presses you to iniquity, your body indeed is held; according to the body you are fixed in the mire of the deep: but as long as you do not consent, you have not stuck; but if you consent, you have stuck. Let your prayer, then, be there, that, as your body is now held, your soul too be not held, that you be free in chains. *Let me be rescued from those who hate me, and from the depth of the waters.*

**19.** [v. 16] *Let not the storm of water sink me.*[^64] But he had already been sunk. *I came into the depth of the sea,* you said; *and the storm sank me,* you said. It sank me according to the flesh, let it not sink me according to the spirit. To those to whom it was said: *If they persecute you in one city, flee into another*[^65]; this was said to them, that they should stick neither in flesh nor in spirit. For it is not to be sought that we stick even in flesh; but as much as we can, we ought to avoid it. But if we have stuck, and have come into the hands of sinners, now we have stuck in body; we are fixed in the mire of the deep: it remains to pray for the soul that we not stick fast, that is, not consent; that the storm of water not sink us, so that we go into the depth of the mire. *Nor let the deep swallow me, nor let the pit close its mouth over me.* What is this, brethren? what did he pray? The pit is great, the depth of human iniquity: there if anyone has fallen, he will fall into the deep. But yet, set there, if he confesses his sins to his God, the pit will not close its mouth over him: as it is written in a certain other psalm: *From the depths I cried to you, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice*[^66]. But if there is done in him what another sentence of Scripture says: *The sinner, when he comes into the depth of evils, despises*[^67]; the pit has closed its mouth over him. Why has it closed its mouth? Because it has closed his mouth. For he has lost confession; he is truly dead, and there is fulfilled in him what is said elsewhere: *From a dead man, as from one who is not, confession perishes*[^68]. This thing is vehemently to be feared, brethren. If you see a man who has committed iniquity, he is sunk into the pit: but when you tell him his iniquity, and he says: Truly I have sinned, I confess; the pit has not closed its mouth over him: but when you see him say: For what evil have I done? he has become the defender of his sin; the pit has closed its mouth over him, and he has no means by which to be rescued. Confession being lost, there will be no place for mercy. You have become the defender of your sin; how will God be your deliverer? That he, then, may be the deliverer, do you be the accuser.
