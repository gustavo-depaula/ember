# On the Same Psalm 101

**1.** Yesterday we heard the groaning of a certain poor man in prayer; and we recognized him to be the one who for our sake became poor, though he was rich, and the members clinging to him and speaking through their head. For there we saw ourselves too; if indeed we too are anything by his grace. But the words of groaning were already finished, and those of consolation had begun; but they could not be finished by yesterday's discussion: in what remains, let us today hear the poor man no longer groaning, but rejoicing; rejoicing for this reason, because he hopes; hoping for this reason, because he does not presume on himself. He foretold the happiness of human affairs in the Scriptures of God, and added: *Let these things be written for another generation: and the people that will be created will praise the Lord. For he has looked out from his holy height.* Yesterday's sermon was carried this far; see what follows.

**2.** [vv. 20-22.] *The Lord looked out from heaven to earth, to hear the groaning of those in chains, to set free the sons of those who were put to death.* We find it said in another psalm: *Let the groaning of those in chains enter into your sight*[^1]; and said in that place where the voice of the martyrs was understood. Whence are the martyrs in chains? Were they not in fetters rather than in chains? For we know that the holy martyrs of God were led after the judges, going around through the provinces, and put in fetters; but in chains we do not know. There are recognized also the chains of God's discipline and of his fear, of which it is said, *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^2]. For through this fear the servants of God did not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul; because they feared him who has power to kill both body and soul in the Gehenna of fire. For unless the martyrs had been bound with the chains of this fear, when would they have endured all those hard and grievous things from their persecutors, since they were free to do what they were being compelled to do, and to escape what they were suffering? But God had bound them with these chains, hard indeed and grievous for a time, but to be endured for the sake of his promises, to whom it is said, *Because of the words of your lips, I have kept hard ways*[^3]. One must indeed groan in these chains, to obtain the mercy of God; whence the voice of the martyrs is in another psalm: *Let the groaning of those in chains enter into your sight*: yet such chains are not to be avoided, so that a ruinous liberty be sought, and the brief sweetness of temporal life, which perpetual bitterness follows. Accordingly Scripture, lest we refuse to be in the chains of wisdom, addresses us thus: *Hear, son, and accept my judgment, and do not cast away my counsel; and put your foot into her fetters, and your neck into her collar: bow your shoulder and bear her, and do not hate her bonds. With all your soul draw near to her, and with all your strength keep her ways: search out and seek, and she will become known to you; and having become continent, do not forsake her. For at the last you will find her rest, and she will be turned for you into gladness, and her fetters will be for you a protection of strength, and her collar a robe of glory. For there is a golden adornment upon her, and her bonds are threads of hyacinth: you will put her on as a robe of glory, and place her on yourself as a crown of exultation*[^4]. Let those in chains cry out, then, as long as they are in the bonds of God's discipline, in which the martyrs were trained: the chains will be loosed, and they will fly, and those same chains will afterward be turned into ornament. This happened with the martyrs. For what did the persecutors do by killing them, except that their chains were loosed and turned into crowns?

**3.** *From heaven*, then, *the Lord looked out, to hear the groaning of those in chains, to set free the sons of those who were put to death.* They were put to death; but the sons of those who were put to death, who are they but we? And how are we set free, except when we say to him, *You have burst my bonds; to you I will sacrifice a sacrifice of praise*[^5]? For each one is set free from the bonds of evil desires, or from the knots of his sins. The remission of sins is a setting free. For what would it have profited Lazarus that he came forth from the tomb, unless it were said, *Loose him, and let him go*[^6]? He indeed raised him from the tomb by his voice, he restored the soul by crying out, he overcame the earthly mass placed upon the buried man, and the bound man came forth: not, then, by his own feet, but by the power of the one bringing him out. This happens in the heart of the penitent: when you hear a man repent of his sins, he has now come back to life; when you hear a man, by confessing, bring forth his conscience, he has now been led out of the tomb, but he is not yet loosed. When is he loosed? by whom is he loosed? *Whatever you loose*, he says, *on earth, will be loosed in heaven too*[^7]. Rightly can the loosing of sins be given through the Church: but the dead man himself can be raised only by the Lord crying out within; for this God does inwardly. We speak to your ears; how do we know what is being done in your hearts? But what is done within is done not by us, but by him.

**4.** He looked out, then, *to set free the sons of those who were put to death.* Of which you have heard who those put to death were, and which sons you have heard. For what then? *That the name of the Lord may be announced in Zion.* For first the Church was oppressed, when those in chains were being put to death: after those oppressions the name of the Lord is announced in Zion, with great liberty, in the Church itself. For it is itself Zion: not that one place, first proud, afterward taken captive; but the Zion whose shadow was that Zion, which is interpreted "watching"; for this reason, because, set in the flesh, we see into the things ahead, stretching ourselves not to the present that is, but to that which is to come. Therefore "watching." For every watchman looks out from afar. That is called a watchtower where guards are set: these watchtowers are made on rocks, on mountains, in trees, for this purpose, that from a higher place one may see far. Zion, then, is watching, the Church is watching. Whence watching? To see far, this is watching. For *there is labor before me, until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand the last things*[^8]. What watching is it, to understand in the last things? To cross the sea by seeing, not by sailing, and to dwell at the ends of the sea; that is, to set one's hope there, in that which will be when the age is finished. So if the Church is watching, there already the name of the Lord is announced. Not only is the name of the Lord announced in this Zion, but *also his praise*, he says, *in Jerusalem.*

**5.** [v. 23.] And how is it announced? *In gathering the peoples into one, and kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord.* Whence was this done, except by the blood of those put to death? whence was this done, except by the groanings of those in chains? They, then, were heard, who were in oppression and lowliness; so that in our times the Church might be in such great glory as we see, so that now the kingdoms which used to persecute themselves serve the Lord.

**6.** [v. 24.] *It answered him in the way of his strength.* To whom did it answer, except to the Lord? Who answered, let us see above: *And his praise*, he says, *in Jerusalem; in gathering the peoples into one, and kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord. It answered him in the way of his strength.* What answered him, or who answered him, in the way of his strength? Let us, then, first seek who answered, and so we will seek what the way of his strength is. The earlier words indicate that either his praise, or Jerusalem, answered him: for above he had said, *And his praise in Jerusalem; in gathering the peoples into one, and kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord. It answered him*: we cannot say, The kingdoms; because he would have said, They answered. *It answered him*: we cannot say, The peoples; because here too he would have said, They answered. Because, then, *it answered him*, we seek a singular number above, and find none, except either his praise, or Jerusalem. And because it is ambiguous, whether his praise or Jerusalem, let us treat it according to both. How does his praise too answer him? When they give him thanks, called by him. For he calls, we answer; not by the voice, but by faith; not by the tongue, but by our life. For if God calls you, and commands you to live well, and you live badly, you do not answer his calling, nor does his praise answer him concerning you; because you live in such a way that he is not praised, but rather blasphemed through you: but when we live in such a way that God is praised through us, his praise answers him. Concerning his called and holy ones, Jerusalem also answered. For Jerusalem too was called, and the first Jerusalem refused to hear, and it was said to her, *Behold, your house will be left to you desolate. Jerusalem, Jerusalem* (he cries out, and is not answered), *how often have I wished to gather your children, as a hen her chicks under her wings, and you would not!*[^9] She is not answered: it is rained upon from above, and instead of fruit thorns are brought forth. But truly that Jerusalem, of which it is said, *Rejoice, barren one, who do not bear; break forth and cry out, you who are not in labor, for many are the children of the deserted one, more than of her who has a husband*[^10]; *it answered him.* What is it, *it answered him?* It did not scorn the one who called. What is it, *it answered him?* He rained, she gave fruit.

**7.** *It answered him*, but where? *In the way of his strength.* Was it in itself? For what would it be in itself, or what voice would it have in itself, of itself, except only the voice of sin, the voice of iniquity? Shake out its voice, what do you find except, for the most part, *I said: Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^11]? Further, if it has been justified, *it answered him*, not from its own merits, but from his hands. Where? *In the way of his strength.* It is Christ, it is he: *I am*, he says, *the way, the truth, and the life*[^12]. But before the resurrection he was not recognized by his people; and especially, crucified out of weakness, who he was lay hidden, until, strong by rising again, he should appear. The Church, then, did not answer him in the way of weakness, but *in the way of his strength*; because after the resurrection he called the Church from the whole world, now not weak on the cross, but strong in heaven. For the praise of the faith of Christians is not that they believe Christ died; but that they believe Christ rose again. For even the pagan believes him dead; and casts this at you as a charge, that you have believed in a dead man. What, then, is your praise? To believe that Christ rose again, and to hope that you will rise again through Christ: this is the praise of faith. *For if you believe in your heart that Jesus is Lord, and confess with your mouth that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved*[^13]. He does not say: If you confess that God handed him over to be killed; but, *if you confess that God raised him from the dead*, then *you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness; but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.* But why do we believe him dead too? Because we cannot believe that he rose again, unless we first believe that he was dead. For who rises, unless he has been dead? who wakes, unless he has first slept? But *shall not he who sleeps add that he rise again?*[^14] This is the faith of Christians. In this faith, then, by which the Church was gathered, those *many children of the deserted one, more than of her who has a husband; it answered him*, it spoke its praise to him according to his precepts; *in the way of his strength*, not in the way of his weakness.

**8.** How it answered him you have already heard above: *In gathering the peoples into one, and kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord.* In this, then, it answered him, in unity: but whoever is not in unity does not answer him. For he is one, the Church is unity: nothing answers the One but unity. But there arise some who say: This has already been done; the Church answered him in all the nations, bearing more children than she who had a husband, *it answered in the way of his strength*; for it believed that Christ rose again, all the nations believed in him: but that Church which was of all the nations now is not; it has perished. This those say who are not in it. O shameless voice! Is it not, because you are not in it? See lest you, for that reason, are not; for it will be, even if you are not. This abominable, detestable voice, full of presumption and falsehood, propped up by no truth, illuminated by no wisdom, seasoned by no salt, vain, rash, headlong, ruinous, the Spirit of God foresaw, and, as if against them, when he announced unity: *In gathering the peoples into one, and kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord.* And when he had added, *It answered him*, certainly his praise, certainly Jerusalem our mother to be called back from her pilgrimage, fruitful with many children, more than she who had a husband; since certain men were going to say against it, It was, and is not: *The fewness*, he says, *of my days announce to me.* What is it, that some men, withdrawing from me, murmur against me? what is it, that the lost contend that I have perished? For surely they say this, that I was, and am not: *Announce to me the fewness of my days.* I do not ask of you those eternal days: those are without end, where I will be; those I do not ask: I ask the temporal ones, announce to me the temporal days: *The fewness of my days*, not the eternity of my days, *announce to me.* How long I will be in this age, announce to me; because of those who say, It was, and now is not; because of those who say, The Scriptures are fulfilled, all the nations have believed, but the Church of all the nations has apostatized and perished. What is this, *The fewness of my days announce to me?* And he announced, nor was that voice in vain. Who announced it to me, except the Way itself? How did it announce it? *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age*[^15].

**9.** But here they arise, and say: *I am with you*, he says, *even to the consummation of the age;* because he foresaw us, because the party of Donatus will be in the land. Is it itself the one that said, *The fewness of my days announce to me*; and not rather that one which spoke above, *In gathering the peoples into one, and kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord?* Whence does your heart ache? Because the emperors too set forth laws against heretics: there is fulfilled, *And kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord.* For you are not the sons of those who were put to death, whose voice, the voice of those in chains, was heard by the Lord. Far from it: your deeds do not indicate this, your pride does not indicate this, your vanity does not indicate this: you have no taste, and you are outside; you are salt that has lost its savor, and therefore you are trodden underfoot by men too. Hear what it says: which Church? The one that gathered *the peoples into one.* Which Church? The one that gathered *kingdoms, that they may serve the Lord.* Moved by your voices and your false opinions, it asks of God that he announce to it the fewness of its days, and it finds that the Lord has said, *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age.* Here you say: He spoke of us; we are, we will be, even to the consummation of the age. Let Christ himself be questioned, to whom it was said, *The fewness of my days announce to me. And this Gospel will be preached*, he says, *in the whole world, as a testimony to all the nations; and then the end will come*[^16]. What is it that you were saying: Surely this was, and it perished? Hear the Lord announcing the fewness of my days. *This Gospel will be preached*, he says. Where? *In the whole world.* To whom? *As a testimony to all the nations.* What afterward? *And then the end will come.* Do you not see that there are still nations among whom the Gospel has not yet been preached? Since, then, it is necessary that what the Lord said be fulfilled, announcing the fewness of my days to the Church, that this Gospel be preached among all the nations, and then the end come; what is it that you say that the Church of all the nations has already perished, when the Gospel is preached for this very purpose, that it may be among all the nations? Therefore even to the end of the age the Church is in all the nations; and this very thing is the fewness of days, because everything that is finished is small; so that one may now pass from this fewness into eternity. Let the heretics perish, let them perish from what they are, and be found so as to be what they are not. The fewness of days will last to the end of the age: small for this reason, because this whole time—I do not say from today to the end of the age, but from Adam to the end of the age—is a tiny drop compared to eternity.

**10.** [v. 25.] Let the heretics, then, not flatter themselves against me, because I said, *The fewness of my days*, as though it would not remain to the end of the age. For what did he add? *Do not call me back in the half of my days.* Do not, as the heretics speak, so deal with me. Lead me through to the end of the age, not to the half of my days; and perfect for me my few days, that you may afterward give me eternal days. Why, then, did you ask about the fewness of days? Why? Do you wish to hear? *Your years are unto generation of generations.* For this reason I asked about the few days, because, although these days last with me to the end of the age, they are few in comparison with your days: for *your years are unto generation of generations.* Why does he not say, Your years are unto ages of ages; for so eternity is more usually signified in the holy Scriptures: but says, *Your years are unto generation of generations?* But what are *your years?* What, except those that do not come and pass away? what, except those that do not come in order not to be? For every day in this time comes for this purpose, that it may not be; every hour, every month, every year: none of these stands; before it comes, it will be; when it has come, it will not be. Those years of yours, then, the eternal ones, your years that are not changed, will be *unto generation of generations.* There is a certain generation of generations; in it your years will be. What is it? There is a certain one, and, if we recognize it well, we will be in it, and the years of God will be in us. How will they be in us? As God himself will be in us: whence it is said, *That God may be all in all*[^17]. For the years of God are not one thing and he himself another: but the years of God are the eternity of God: eternity is the very substance of God, which has nothing changeable; there nothing is past, as if it now were not; nothing is future, as if it were not yet. There is nothing there but: It is; there is not there: It was and It will be; because what was, now is not; and what will be, is not yet: but whatever is there, simply is. Rightly so God sent his servant Moses. For he asked the name of the one sending him; he asked, and he heard, nor was the longing of his good desire deserted. He asked, however, not as if out of the curiosity of presuming, but out of the necessity of ministering. *What*, he says, *shall I say to the sons of Israel, if they say to me: Who sent you to us?* And he, indicating himself to the creature as Creator, to man as God, to the mortal as immortal, to the temporal as eternal: *I am*, he says, *who I am.* You would say: I am. Who? Gaius; another, Lucius; another, Marcus. Would you say anything except your name? This was awaited of God. For this was what was asked. What are you called? By whom shall I answer that I am sent, to those who ask? *I am.* Who? *Who I am.* Is this your name? is this the whole of what you are called? Would to be itself be your name, unless whatever else is, compared to you, were found not truly to be? This is your name: express this same thing better. *Go*, he says, *and say to the sons of Israel: He who is sent me to you. I am who I am: he who is sent me to you.* Behold a great *Is*, a great *Is!* Compared to this, what is man? Compared to that so great *Is*, what is man, whatever he is? Who can grasp that being? who can become a partaker of it? who can pant for it? who can aspire to it? who can presume that he can be there? Do not despair, human frailty. *I am*, he says: *the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^18]. You have heard what I am in myself, hear too what I am for your sake. This eternity, then, called us, and the Word burst forth out of eternity. Already eternity, already the Word, and not yet time. Why not yet time? Because time too was made. How was time too made? *All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^19]. O Word before the times, through whom the times were made, born in time too, though he is eternal life, calling the temporal, making them eternal! This is the generation of generations. For a generation goes, and a generation comes. And you see that the generations of men are on the earth as leaves on a tree; but on a tree of the olive, or laurel, or any other that is clothed in foliage all the time. So the earth bears the human race as it were as leaves: it is full of men, but while some die others succeed by being born. For always that tree is adorned with a green garment: but look beneath, how many dry leaves you tread on.

**11.** So there was a generation under Adam; it passed. From there were born some who would be partakers of God's eternity even in that time: for from there came Abel, from there Seth, from there Enoch. That generation passed, the flood came, one house remained. And that generation gave some, Noah himself, and his three sons, and his three daughters-in-law: for in that whole house of eight only one sinner was found. The number was added to the earlier generation. Then from the three sons of Noah, as from three measures of flour, the world was filled. Abraham was chosen, Isaac, and Jacob; holy men, patriarchs, pleased God. That generation too gave those who followed, who also begot, they gave the Prophets, they gave the heralds of God. Afterward came our Lord Jesus Christ himself, he put leaven into three measures of flour, until the whole was leavened. In the times of his flesh on earth there were the Apostles, there were holy men; after them other holy men: and now in the name of Christ whoever are holy, and after us whoever will be, and to the end of the age whoever are holy. Out of so many generations you will gather all the holy offspring of all the generations, and make of them one generation: *In* this *generation of generations are your years*, that is, that eternity will be in that generation which is gathered from all the generations, and reduced into one; it will itself be a partaker of your eternity. The other generations are begotten to fill up the times, out of which that one is regenerated unto eternity; changed, it will be made alive, it will be fit to bear you, receiving strength from you. *Unto generation of generations are your years.*

**12.** [vv. 26-28.] *In the beginning you founded the earth, O Lord.* I have come to know your eternity, by which you precede all the things you have made. *In the beginning you founded the earth, O Lord; and the heavens are the works of your hands. They will perish; but you abide: and all will grow old like a garment, and like a covering you will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the very same.* Who are you? *You are the very same.* You who said, *I am who I am*, you are the very same. And although even these things would not be except from you, and through you, and in you, yet they are not what you yourself are: for *you are the very same. And your years will not fail.* Those years of yours will not fail, those years of yours which will be unto generation of generations will not fail. Knowing this, then, I would have asked you about the fewness of my days, unless I knew that all the days of the age, from the beginning to the end, are few in comparison with your eternity. I have come to know, then, from what I asked. Let the heretics not exalt themselves, as though the days of the Church, spread throughout the whole world, were few; for even though they last to the end, they are few. Why are they few? Because at some time they are to be finished. Those years which will be *unto generation of generations*, these are to be loved, these to be desired, for these one must sigh; for these one must remain in unity, for these one must shun whatever evil of the heretics there is, for these one must answer the lost, for these one must win those who had erred, and call back those who had perished: there our desire ought to be. But yet, that I may answer the verbose, the badly garrulous, the slanderous, the whisperers, the detractors, for this reason *announce to me the fewness of my days; and do not call me back in the half of my days*, so as to take me away from the earth before the whole world is filled with the Gospel, against the answer of my Lord, who says, *This Gospel must be preached in the whole world, as a testimony to all the nations; and then the end will come*[^20]. What of this, brothers? These things are plain, they are manifest: God founded the earth, we know; the heavens are the works of his hands. For do not suppose that God makes one thing as it were by hand, another by word. What he makes by word, this he makes by hand; for he is not distinguished by bodily members, who said, *I am who I am.* And perhaps his word is his hand. Certainly his hand is his power. For because it is said, *Let there be a firmament*, and the firmament was made; he is understood to have made it by his word: but because he said, *Let us make man to our image and likeness*[^21]; he is seen to have made it as it were by hand. Hear, then: *The heavens are the works of your hands.* Behold, what he made by word, he surely made also by hands; because he made it by his power, by his might. Attend rather to what he made, and do not ask how he made it. It is too much for you to grasp how he made it, since he so made you that you should first be an obedient servant, and afterward perhaps an understanding friend. Therefore: *The heavens are the works of your hands.*

**13.** *They will perish; but you abide.* The apostle Peter said this openly: *The heavens existed of old, constituted of water and through water by the word of God; through which the world that then was, deluged with water, perished: but the earth and the heavens that now are, by the same word are stored up, reserved for fire*[^22]. He has already said, then, that the heavens perished through the flood: and we know that the heavens perished according to the extent and the spaces of this air. For the water swelled up, and occupied all this capacity where the birds fly; and so certainly the heavens near the earth perished: the heavens according to which the birds are called the birds of heaven. But there are also the higher heavens of heavens in the firmament: but whether they too are to perish by fire, or only these heavens which also perished by the flood, is a somewhat thornier dispute among the learned, nor can it easily be unfolded, especially in the narrowness of time. Let us, then, dismiss it, or defer it; let us know, however, that these things perish, that God abides. And if certain things abide with God, which were made by God, they do not abide in themselves, but in God, by not withdrawing from God. For what? are we going to say, brothers, that the angels are to perish by the fire with which the world will be burned? Far from it. But what? shall we say that God did not make the angels? Far from it. But what shall we say? And whence would they be, if they had not been made by him? *He spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created*[^23]. For this was said when the commemoration of his works was made, among which the angels too were named. The angels, then, will be with him, even while the world blazes with fire; and there will be a burning of the world, not burning the holy ones of God: what the furnace of the king was to the three boys, that the burning world will be to the just signed in the Trinity.

**14.** Perhaps here too we may not inopportunely understand the heavens to be the just themselves, the holy ones of God, in whom God, abiding, thundered with precepts, flashed with miracles, rained upon the earth with the wisdom of truth; for the heavens declared the glory of God. But will they too perish? Or will they perish in a certain manner? In what manner? According to the garment. What is it, according to the garment? According to the body. For the body is the garment of the soul; for the Lord named it a garment, where he said, *Is not the soul more than food, and the body more than the garment?*[^24] How, then, does the garment perish? *Even if our outer man is corrupted, yet the inner is renewed from day to day*[^25]. They will perish, then, but according to the body: *but you abide.* If, then, they will perish according to the body, where then is the resurrection of the flesh? where is the example of the members that went before in the head? where? Do you wish to hear? It will be changed; it will not be such as it was. Hear the Apostle saying, *And the dead will rise incorruptible, and we will be changed.* How will we be changed? *A natural body is sown, a spiritual body will rise*[^26]. The mortal, then, is sown, the immortal will rise; the corruptible is sown, the incorruptible will rise. We await, therefore, a change: so the heavens will perish, and the heavens will be changed. But perhaps the bodies of the saints are not rightly called heavens? If they do not bear God, let them not be heavens. And whence, you say, do you prove to me that they bear God? Has it so far slipped your mind: *Glorify and bear God in your body*[^27]? Such heavens, then, will perish, but not forever; they will perish in order to be changed. Or does the Psalm not say this? Read what follows: *And all will grow old like a garment, and like a covering you will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the very same, and your years will not fail.* You hear a garment, you hear a covering, and do you understand something other than the body? Let us hope, then, also for the change of our bodies, but yet from him who was both before us, and abides after us; from whom we are what we are, to whom we will come when we have been changed; changing, not changed; making, not made; and moving, but abiding; and—how can it be understood by flesh and blood?—*I am who I am: but you are the very same, and your years will not fail.* But we, compared to those years, with these tattered years of ours, what are we? And those, what are they? And yet we ought not to despair. For already by a certain greatness and excellence of wisdom he had said, *I am who I am;* and yet to console us: *I am*, he says, *the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^28]: and we are the seed of Abraham: and we, although abject, although earth and ashes, hope in him. We are servants; but for our sake our Lord *took the form of a servant*[^29]: for our sake the immortal willed to die for mortals, for our sake he showed this example of resurrection. Let us hope, then, that we will come standing to those years, in which the days are not carried on by the circuit of the sun, but that which is abides as it is, because this alone truly is.

**15.** [v. 29.] But tell whether we can ever be there. Hear, and see whether you ought to despair; hear what follows: *The sons of your servants will dwell there.* Where, except in the years that do not fail? *The sons of your servants will dwell there; and their seed will be directed unto the age*: unto the age of the age, unto the eternal age, unto the abiding age. But *the sons*, he says, *of your servants*: is it to be feared that we are the servants of God, and our sons will be there, not we? Or if we are sons of servants, because we are sons of the Apostles, what shall we say? What audacity, then, so unhappy, of subsequent sons, glorying in their recent succession, would dare to say: We will be there; the Apostles will not be there? Far be this from the piety of sons, far from the faith of little ones, far from the understanding of the great. The Apostles too will be there: the rams go before, the lambs follow. Why, then, *The sons of your servants*, and not, more compactly, Your servants? Both they are your servants, and their sons are your servants, and the sons of these, the grandsons of those, what are they, except your servants? You would include all compactly, if you said: Your servants will dwell there. Let us see what he wished to admonish us. For there is a certain thing done in former ages. For forty years the sons of Israel were worn down in the desert: none of them entered into the land of promise, but their sons; they indeed entered, for so we recall, unless I am mistaken, two, the rest did not. Of so many thousands two entered. So much was labored with them: but God does not labor; certainly his servants labored. How much did Moses bear, how much did he hear, for the men who would not enter into the land of promise? Their sons entered, what does it signify? New men entered, the old did not enter. Yet from there two entered, one and unity, as it were head and body, Christ and the Church, with all that newness, that is, of the sons. Therefore: *The sons of your servants will dwell there. The sons of the servants* are the works of the servants; no one will dwell there except through his own works. What, then, is it, And the sons will dwell there? Let no one glory that he will dwell there, if he says he is a servant of God, and does not have works; for none will dwell there but the sons. What, then, is it, *The sons of your servants will dwell there?* The servants will dwell there through their works, the servants will dwell there through their sons. Do not, then, be barren, if you wish to dwell there; send ahead the offspring you will follow, by sending them ahead, not by burying them. Let your sons lead you to the land of promise, the land of the living, not of the dying: while you live here in this pilgrimage, let them go before, let them receive you. For the sake of carnal refreshment, the son went before Jacob into Egypt, and said to his father and his brothers, *I have gone ahead to prepare food for you*[^30]. Let your sons, then, go before, let your works go before you: such sons as you have sent ahead, such you will follow.
