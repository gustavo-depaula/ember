# Exposition of Psalm 57

**1.** [vv. 1-2] The voice that we have sung is to be heard by us, rather than cried out. For to all, as in an assembly of the human race, truth cries out: *If indeed you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.*[^1] For to what unjust man is it not easy to speak righteousness? or who, asked about righteousness, when he has no case at stake, does not easily answer what is just? Since by the hand of our Fashioner truth wrote in our very hearts: *What you do not wish done to you, do not do to another*[^2]. This, even before the Law was given, no one was permitted not to know, that there might be a means by which even those to whom the Law had not been given might be judged. But lest men should complain that something was lacking to them, there was written also on tablets what they were not reading in their hearts. For it was not that they did not have it written, but that they were unwilling to read it. There was set before their eyes what they were forced to see in their conscience; and, as it were, with the voice of God applied from outside, man was driven to his interior, Scripture saying: *For in the thoughts of the ungodly there will be questioning*[^3]. Where there is questioning, there is law. But because men, craving the things that are outside, were made exiles even from themselves, a written law was also given: not because it was not written in their hearts; but because you were a fugitive from your own heart, you are caught by him who is everywhere, and called back within to yourself. Therefore what does the written law cry to those who forsook the law written in their hearts? *Return, transgressors, to the heart*[^4]. For who taught you to be unwilling that another approach your wife? who taught you to be unwilling that theft be done to you? who taught you to be unwilling to suffer injury, and whatever else can be said either generally or particularly? For there are many things about which men, asked about each, would answer with a clear voice that they are unwilling to suffer them. Come, if you are unwilling to suffer these things, are you the only man? are you not living in the fellowship of the human race? He who was made with you is your companion; and all were made in the image of God, unless by earthly desires they wear away what he formed. *What*, then, *you do not wish done to you, do not do to another.* For you judge it to be evil in that which you are unwilling to suffer: and this the inmost law, written in your very heart, compels you to know. You were doing it, and the cry was raised amid your hands: how are you compelled to return to your heart, when you suffer this amid another's hands? Is theft good? No. I ask: Is adultery good? All cry out: No. Is murder good? All cry out that they detest it. Is it good to covet a neighbor's property? No, is the voice of all. Or if you do not yet confess it, let someone come who covets your property: let it please you, and answer what you will. All, then, asked about these things, cry out that they are not good. Again, about benefits, not only about doing no harm, but also about furnishing and bestowing. Every hungry soul is asked: You suffer hunger; another has bread, and it overflows for him beyond sufficiency, he knows you are in need, he does not give: it displeases you, hungry; let it displease the one who is filled too, when you have come to know that another is hungry. A stranger, needing shelter, comes into your country, and is not received: he then cries out that that city is inhuman, that he could easily have found refuge among barbarians. He feels the iniquity, because he suffers it: you perhaps do not feel it; but you ought to think of yourself too as a stranger, and see how one might be displeased with you who did not furnish you what you, in your own country, are unwilling to furnish a stranger. I ask all: Are these things true? True. Are these things just? Just.

**2.** But hear the Psalm: *If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.*[^5] Let there be righteousness not of the lips, but also of deeds. For if you act otherwise than you speak, you speak good things, and judge badly. For if you act—how do you judge? Asked what is better, gold or faith, you are not so perverse and deviant from all truth as to answer that gold is better: you set faith, then, when asked, before gold; you have spoken righteousness. Have you heard the Psalm? *If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* And where shall I prove that you do not judge as you have spoken? I now hold your answer setting faith before gold. Behold, from somewhere a friend comes, and with no witness entrusted gold to you; he alone knew it, and you, as far as concerns men. There is there another witness, who is not seen and sees: and he entrusted gold to you in secret and in your chamber, perhaps with all witnesses removed; a witness who is present, not present in the chamber of walls, but in the chambers of your consciences. He entrusted it and departed, made it known to none of his own, you scorn that he would return and recover from his friend what he had given: as human things are, he died, he has an heir, he left a son; the son does not know what the father had, what he entrusted to you. Come now, return, return, transgressor, to your heart, there the law is written: *What you do not wish done to you, do not do to another.* Think that you yourself entrusted it, said it to none of your own, died, left a son: what would you wish furnished to him by your friend? Answer, judge the case: the judge's tribunal is in your mind; God sits there, the accuser conscience is present, the torturer fear. You are among human things, you are held in human fellowship: think what you would wish furnished to your son by your friend. I know what your thought answers you: judge as you hear. Judge, there will be a voice. The voice of truth is not silent; it does not cry with the lips, but cries out from the heart: apply your ear; there be with the son of your friend. You see perhaps even him wandering in need, not knowing what his father had, where he put it, to whom he entrusted it: think of your son too, make him live whom you despise as dead, think yourself dead that you may live. But greed commands one thing, commands against God; God one thing, greed another; one thing our founder in paradise, another the crosswise seducing serpent. Let your former fall come to mind: therefore mortal, therefore laboring, therefore eating bread in the sweat of your brow, therefore the earth produces thorns and thistles for you: learn by experience what you were unwilling to learn by precept. But greed conquers: why not rather truth? And where is what you were saying? Behold, you think of denying the gold, behold, you think of hiding it entirely from your friend's heir. I had asked a little before what is dearer, and what is better, gold or faith. Why do you say one thing, do another? Do you not fear this voice: *If you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men*? Behold, you spoke to me that faith is better, and in your judgment you held gold better. You did not judge as you spoke: you spoke true things, and judged false things. Therefore even when you were speaking righteousness, you were not speaking truly: *For if you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* When you answered me about righteousness, you spoke from shame, not from conviction.

**3.** But let us now come to the present matter, if it please. For that is the sweet voice, known to the ears of the Church, the voice of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the voice of his body, the voice of the laboring Church, sojourning on earth, living amid the dangers of those who curse and those who flatter. You will not fear the threatener, if you do not love the flatterer. So he whose voice this is attended, and saw all men speaking righteousness. For who dares not to speak it, lest he be called unjust? When, then, he heard, as it were, the voices of all, and gave heed to the lips of all, he cried out to them: *If indeed you truly speak righteousness*, if you do not falsely speak righteousness, if one thing does not clamor on the lips while another is hidden in the heart, *judge rightly, you sons of men.*[^6] Hear from the Gospel his own voice, the very same that is in this psalm. *Hypocrites*, the Lord says to the Pharisees, *how can you speak good things, when you are evil? Either make the tree good, and its fruit good; or make the tree bad, and its fruit bad*[^7]. Why do you wish to whitewash yourself, you wall of mud? I know your inner parts, I am not deceived by your plastering: I know what you put forward, I know what you cover. *For he had no need*, says the evangelist, *that anyone should bear him witness about man: for he himself knew what was in man*[^8]. *He knew what was in man*, who had made man, and had become man to seek man. See, then, whether these voices do not follow one another: *Hypocrites, how can you speak good things, when you are evil? If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* Did you not speak righteousness, when you said: *Master, we know that you are just, and accept no one's person*[^9]? Why did you cover deceit in your heart? why did you show the image of Caesar to your Creator, and erase his image in your hearts? Was it not heard what you spoke, and tested how you judged? Did you not crucify him whom you had said to be just? *If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* Why do I hear your speech: *We know that you are just;* when I foresee your judgment: *Crucify, crucify*[^10]? *If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* For what did you accomplish by raging against God who was man, and by killing your king? For he was not therefore not going to be king, because he was killed by you, going to rise again. In the title where it was written, *King of the Jews*, set over the Lord's cross, in three tongues, Hebrew, Greek, Latin, the judge, a man, knew how to say: *What I have written, I have written*[^11]: and did God not know how to say: What I have written, I have written? He is altogether your king: living, he is your king; killed, he is your king. Behold, he has risen, and in heaven he is your king: behold, he is to come; woe to you, for he is your king. Go now, and speak righteousness, and refuse to judge rightly, you sons of men. You are unwilling to judge rightly, you will be rightly judged. For that your king lives, and now dies no more, and death will no more have dominion over him. Behold, he comes; *return, transgressors, to the heart*[^12]. Behold, he will come, be corrected before he comes, anticipate his face in confession. Behold, he will come; he is your king. Remember the title over the cross. If you do not see it written, yet it remains: on earth it is not read, but in heaven it is kept. For do you think that inscription was corrupted? And what does the title of this psalm say? *Unto the end, do not corrupt, for David himself, for the inscription of a title.* That inscription of the title, then, is not corrupted. Christ is your king, because Christ is the king of all: *for his is the kingdom, and he will have dominion over the nations*[^13]. If, then, he is king, behold, before he comes, he says to you: I still speak, he says, I do not yet judge; I who so cry out in threatening, am unwilling to strike in judging. *If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.*

**4.** [v. 3] But now, what do you do? Why do I say these things to you? *For in your heart you work iniquities on the earth.*[^14] Iniquities perhaps in the heart alone? Hear what follows: and the hands follow their heart, and the hands serve their heart, it is thought and it is done; or it is therefore not done, not because we are unwilling, but because we cannot. Whatever you wish, and cannot do, God reckons as done. *For in your heart you work iniquities on the earth.* What then? *Your hands knit together iniquities.* What is, *knit together*? Sin upon sin, and to sin sin, for the sake of sin. What is this? He committed theft, it is a sin: he was seen, he seeks to kill the one by whom he was seen; sin is knit to sin. God may have permitted, by hidden judgment, that he kill the one he wished to kill; he feels it is known, he seeks to kill another too; he knits a third. While he contrives these things, perhaps lest he be found out, or because he has acted lest he be convicted, he consults an astrologer; a fourth is added. The astrologer answers perhaps something harsh and evil; one runs to the soothsayer to be expiated: the soothsayer answers that he cannot expiate; a sorcerer is sought. And who could count all the sins that are knit to sins? *Your hands knit together iniquities.* As long as you knit, you bind sin to sin: loose yourself from sins. But I cannot, you say. Cry to him: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death*[^15]. For the grace of God will come, so that righteousness may delight you, as iniquity used to delight; and you, a man freed from chains, will cry out to God: *You have burst my bonds*[^16]. What is, *you have burst my bonds*, except, you have forgiven my sins? Hear that they are bonds: Scripture answers: *Each one is bound fast with the cords of his own sins*[^17]. Not only bonds, but cords too. They are cords, which are made by twisting: this is that you were knitting sins to sins. *Woe to those who draw sins like a long rope*, Isaiah cries. *Woe to those who draw sins like a long garment*[^18]: what else is it than woe to those whose hands knit iniquity? And because each one is bound fast with his own sins, as also he is beaten with his own sins, the Lord drove out those misbehaving in the temple with a whip made of little cords. But you do not wish your bonds to be burst now, because you do not feel your bonds; they even delight you, and are a pleasure: you will feel them in the end, when it will be said: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^19]. You shudder, you fear, you beat your breast; you say sins are evil, righteousness is good. *If, then, you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* Let your words be found in your life; in your deeds let your lips be recognized. Do not, then, knit iniquity; because whatever you knit will be applied to your own binding. They do not hear; not all, however: and those who do not hear were foreknown.

**5.** [v. 4] *Sinners are estranged from the womb, they went astray from the belly, they have spoken false things.*[^20] Both when they speak iniquity, they speak false things; because iniquity is fallacious: and when they speak righteousness, they speak false things; because they bring forth one thing with the mouth, and cover another in the heart. *Sinners are estranged from the womb*: What is this? Let us seek more diligently: for perhaps it says this, that God foreknew men as sinners even in their mothers' wombs. For whence, when Rebecca was still pregnant and carried twins in her womb, was it said: *Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*[^21]? For it was said: *The elder will serve the younger*[^22]. There is a hidden judgment of God there; but yet *from the womb*, that is, from their very origin, *sinners are estranged.* Whence estranged? From the truth. Whence estranged? From the blessed homeland, from the blessed life. Or perhaps estranged from the very womb? And who are the sinners estranged from the womb? For who would be born, if they were not held there? Or who would live today, so as to hear these things without cause, unless they were born? Perhaps, then, sinners are estranged from a certain womb, in which love was suffering pains, saying through the Apostle: *With whom I am again in travail, until Christ be formed in you*[^23]. Wait, then; be formed. Do not assume for yourself a judgment which perhaps you do not know. You are still carnal, you have been conceived: by the very fact that you have received the name of Christ, by a certain sacrament you have been born in the inmost parts of your mother. For a man is not born only from the inmost parts, but also in the inmost parts. He is first born in the inmost parts, that he may be able to be born from the inmost parts. Therefore it was said to Mary too: *For what is born in her is of the Holy Spirit*[^24]. He had not yet been born of her, but he had already been born in her. So certain little ones are born within the inmost parts of the Church; and it is good that they come out formed, lest they slip out by miscarriage. Let your mother bring you forth, not miscarry you. If you are patient, until you are formed, until in you the teaching of truth is sure, the maternal inmost parts ought to contain you. But if by your impatience you shake the sides of your mother, she casts you out with grief indeed, but more to your harm than her own.

**6.** *Sinners are estranged from the womb, they went astray from the belly, they have spoken false things.*[^25] Did they, then, go astray from the belly because they spoke false things? Or rather did they speak false things because they went astray from the belly? For in the belly of the Church, truth abides. Whoever is separated from this belly of the Church must speak false things: he must, I say, speak false things, who either was unwilling to be conceived, or whom, conceived, his mother cast out. Hence the heretics cry out against the Gospel: that we may rather speak of these whom we grieve to be cast out. We recite to them: Behold, Christ said: *It behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day*[^26]. I recognize there our head, I recognize there our bridegroom: do you recognize with me the bride, see what follows: *That repentance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^27]. Come here, come here. Behold the Church *through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*: I do not say: Come here; she herself comes to you. But they, deaf against the Gospel, and not permitting us to read the words of God, which they boast they have guarded from the flame and wish to blot out with their tongue, speak their own things, speak empty things: That one betrayed, and that one betrayed. Indeed I too say: That one betrayed, and that one betrayed: and I say true. But what is that to me? Neither do you read to me from the Gospel those whom you name, nor do I read from the Gospel those whom I name. Let our papers be removed from the midst, let the Codex of God come forth into the midst. Hear Christ speaking, hear truth speaking: *That repentance*, he says, *and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem.* No, they say; but hear what we say: what the Gospel says we are unwilling to hear. *Sinners are estranged from the womb, they went astray from the belly, they have spoken false things.* Let us speak true things, because we have heard true things; what the Lord says, not what man says. It can happen that a man lie: it cannot happen that truth lie. From the mouth of truth I recognize Christ, truth itself: from the mouth of truth I recognize the Church, partaker of truth. Let no one speak false things to me, who has gone astray from the womb in the inmost parts of the Church: first I would see what he wishes to teach me. I see one estranged from the womb, I see one who has gone astray from the belly, and am I to hear from him anything but false things? *They went astray from the belly, they have spoken false things.*

**7.** [vv. 5-6] *Indignation is to them according to the likeness of a serpent.*[^28] You are going to hear a great thing. *Indignation is to them according to the likeness of a serpent.* As though we had said, what is it you have said? there follows: *Like a deaf asp.* Whence deaf? *And stopping up its ears.* Therefore deaf, because it stops up its ears. *And stopping up its ears, which will not hear the voice of the charmers, and of the medicine medicated by the wise one.* As we have heard, what men too say who learned these things with what understanding they could, but yet what the Spirit of God knows much better than all men. For he did not say this in vain, except because it can be that even what we have heard about the asp is true. The asp, when it begins to suffer the Marsian charmer, who calls it out with certain proper charms, as there are also many magical ones, hear what it does. But meanwhile attend to this, brethren: for this must be said beforehand, lest anyone hear it as though hesitating: not, from whatever a likeness is drawn, is the thing itself praised by the Scriptures, but only a likeness is drawn from it. For he did not praise the unjust judge who was unwilling to hear that widow, and neither feared God nor reverenced men; and yet the Lord drew a likeness from it: nor did he praise that lazy man who gave three loaves to the one asking, not on account of friendship, but overcome by weariness; and yet from it he gave a likeness. From things not to be praised, then, in a certain manner some likenesses are drawn. Or if you think one must go to the Marsians for this reason, because you have heard this in the Scripture of God; one must go also to the spectacles of the theater, because the Apostle says: *I do not so box, as one beating the air*[^29]; for to box is to do the pancratium. Now, because a likeness is drawn from here, ought these spectacles to delight us? or because he said: *He who contends in the contest is abstinent from all things*[^30]; ought a Christian therefore to crave these vain and playful athletics? Attend to what is said to you for a likeness, what is admonished for a prohibition. So, then, here too a certain likeness is drawn from the Marsian, who charms in order to draw the asp out of its dark cave: he certainly wishes to draw it into the light; but it, loving its own darkness in which it hides, wrapping itself, is said, when it is unwilling to come out, yet refusing to hear those voices by which it feels itself compelled, to dash one ear to the earth, and stop up the other with its tail, and so, avoiding those voices as much as it can, it does not come out to the charmer. To these the Spirit of God called like certain men who do not hear the word of God, and not only do not do it, but are altogether unwilling to hear it lest they do it.

**8.** This happened in the first times of the faith too. Stephen the martyr was preaching the truth, and was charming, as it were, the dark minds, that he might draw them into the light: when he came to the mention of Christ, whom they were altogether unwilling to hear, what does Scripture say of them? what does it recount of them? *They closed*, it says, *their ears*[^31]. But what they did afterward, the narrated passion of Stephen declares. They were not deaf, but they made themselves deaf. For because they did not have open ears in the heart, yet the violence of the word, rushing in through the ears of the flesh, was doing violence even to those ears of the heart: they closed even the ears of the body, and went for stones. Behold, deaf asps, harder than the stones with which they stoned their charmer: they did not hear the voice of the charmer, *and of the medicine medicated by the wise one.* What is the medicine medicated by the wise one? Perhaps a medicine compounded, let it say medicine medicated. Or do we seek something there, how, if it is already a medicine, it is also medicated? There were medicines in the Prophets, there were medicines in the Law, all the precepts themselves were medicines; and this medicine was not yet medicated: by the coming of the Lord the medicine was medicated; this they could not bear. For because they were not cured by the medicine, the very medicine was medicated by the coming of the Lord. Now Stephen was charming with the medicated medicine; this they were unwilling to hear: whence the medicine was medicated, against this they closed their ears. For there they did this, where Christ was named. Their indignation, like the indignation of a serpent. Why do you close your ears? Wait, hear; and if you can, rage. Because they wished only to rage, they were unwilling to hear. But if they heard, perhaps they would cease to rage. Their indignation, like the indignation of a serpent.

**9.** Such men too we suffer. At first they seemed to themselves to hold the truth: God did not cease, did not rest. In his Church the truth was preached, in the inmost parts of the mother their lies were laid open: what shines was made plain; the city set on a mountain was shown, which cannot be hidden, and the lamp was set on a lampstand, which shines for all who are in the house. For where does the Church of Christ lie hidden? where does the truth of Christ lie hidden? Is it not the very mountain that grew from the least stone, and filled the whole face of the earth? They are convicted from this, they have nothing to say against the Church. And what remained to them? Why do you seek us? what do you want with us? they say; depart from us. And they say to their own too: Let no one speak with them, let no one join them, let no one hear them. Their indignation, the indignation of serpents: *like a deaf asp and one stopping up its ears, which will not hear the voice of the charmers, and of the medicine*, that is, the voice of the medicine, *medicated by the wise one.* Is it not from this now understood what sort of medicine, when it says voice? Surely a medicine does not have a voice? There is a certain medicine that has a voice. We carry a medicine; hear its voice, not like deaf asps. *If indeed you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* It is the voice *of the medicine*, and this *medicated by the wise one.* For now Christ has come who would fulfill the Law and the Prophets, who would establish truth itself: on which two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets hang.

**10.** Or perhaps do we seek something also in that it is said the asp so closes its ears that it presses one of them to the earth, and stops up the other with its tail? What does this mean? By the tail, the hinder parts are certainly understood; therefore things past, on which we ought now to set our back, so that we may be intent on those things that are promised to us: therefore we ought to be delighted neither by our past life nor by our present. For this the Apostle admonishes, saying: *What fruit, then, had you once in those things of which you are now ashamed*[^32]? He calls us back from recalling things past with delight, and with a certain craving to enjoy them, lest we return in heart to Egypt. What of present things? how does he bid even these to be despised? *Not looking*, he says, *at the things that are seen, but the things that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporal: but the things that are not seen are eternal*[^33]. Again he says of the present life: *If in this life only we are hoping in Christ, we are more wretched than all men*[^34]. Forget, then, things past, in which you lived badly; despise present things, in which you live temporally, lest by binding you, present things not permit you to reach the future. For if the present life delights you, you have set your ear on the earth: if you are delighted even by your past things now slipping behind you, you have stopped up your ear with the tail. You ought, then, to go into the light, to come out of the darkness, having heard the voice of the medicine medicated by the wise one; so that now, walking in the light, and exulting, you may say: *Forgetting the things behind, stretching forth to the things that are before*[^35]. He did not say: Forgetting the things behind, and delighted by present things. When he says: *Forgetting the things behind*, he did not stop up his ear with the tail; when he says: *Stretching forth to the things that are before*, he was not deaf to these present things: deservedly hearing, deservedly preaching, deservedly his tongue exults, preaching the truth in the new light, the old tunic laid aside. For to this too avails the cunning of the serpent, to imitate which the Lord exhorts us. For he says: *Be cunning as serpents*[^36]. What is, *cunning as serpents*? Offer all your members to the striker, provided you keep the head whole. *The head of the man is Christ*[^37]. But it weighs, as it were a burden of a certain skin and as the old age of the old man. Hear the Apostle saying: *Putting off the old man, and putting on the new*[^38]. And how do I put off, you say, the old man? Imitate the cunning of the serpent. For what does the serpent do, to put off its old tunic? It squeezes itself through a narrow hole. And where, you say, do I find this narrow hole? Hear: *Narrow and strait is the way that leads to life, and few are those who enter by it*[^39]. You dread it, and are unwilling to walk, because they are few? There the old tunic is to be laid aside, and it cannot be laid aside elsewhere. Or if you wish to be hindered, weighed down, pressed by the old age, do not go through the narrow way. But if you are weighed down by a certain old age of your sin and of your past life, you cannot pass through. Because, then, the corruptible body weighs down the soul; either let bodily desires not press, or let the concupiscences of the flesh be put off. Whence are they put off, unless you go through the narrow way, unless you are cunning as a serpent?

**11.** [v. 7] *God has crushed their teeth in their mouth.*[^40] Whose? Those whose indignation is like the likeness of a serpent, and of an asp stopping up its ears, that it may not hear the voice of the charmers, and of the medicine medicated by the wise one. What did the Lord do to them? *He crushed their teeth in their mouth.* It was done; this was first done, and is done now. But it would suffice, my brethren, that it be said: *God has crushed their teeth.* Why, *in their mouth*? The Pharisees were unwilling to hear the law, unwilling to hear the precepts of truth from Christ, like that serpent and asp. For they were delighted by their past sins, and were unwilling to lose the present life, that is, earthly joys for eternal joys. They closed one ear from the delight of things past, the other from the delight of things present: therefore they were unwilling to hear. For whence is that: *If we let him go, the Romans will come, and take from us both our place and our nation*[^41]? They certainly were unwilling to lose the place, they had dashed their ear to the earth; and therefore they were unwilling to hear those words medicated by the wise one. It is said of them too that they were greedy and lovers of money: and all their life, even the past, was described by the Lord in the Gospel. Whoever reads the Gospel diligently finds whence they stopped up both ears. Let Your Charity attend: what did the Lord do? *He crushed their teeth in their mouth.* What is, *in their mouth*? That with their own mouth they pronounced against themselves: he compelled them to speak the sentence against themselves with their own mouth. They wished to slander him about the tribute: he did not say: It is lawful to render tribute, or, it is not lawful to render. And he wished to crush their teeth, with which they gaped to bite; but he wished it in their own mouth. If he said: Let tribute be paid to Caesar; they would slander him as having cursed the nation of the Jews, by making it tributary. For from sin they paid tribute, humbled, as had been foretold to them in the Law. We hold him, they say, a curser of our nation, if he bids us pay tribute: but if he says: Do not pay, we hold him as having forbidden us to be devoted to Caesar. Such a two-headed snare they set, as if to catch the Lord. But to whom had they come? To one who knew how to crush their teeth in their own mouth. *Show me a coin*, he says. *Why do you tempt me, hypocrites?*[^42] Do you think about paying tribute? do you wish to do righteousness? do you seek counsel of righteousness? *If you truly speak righteousness, judge rightly, you sons of men.* But now, because you speak one thing, judge another, you are hypocrites: *why do you tempt me?* Now I will crush your teeth in your mouth: *show me a coin.* And they showed it to him. And he does not say: It is Caesar's; but asks: *Whose is it?* that their teeth may be crushed in their own mouth. For when he asked whose image and inscription it had, they said Caesar's. Now then the Lord will crush their teeth in their own mouth. Now you have answered, now your teeth are crushed in your mouth. *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's*[^43]. Caesar seeks his image; render it: God seeks his image; render it. Let Caesar not lose his coin from you: let God not lose his coin in you. And they found nothing to answer. For they had been sent to slander him: and they returned saying that no one could answer him. Whence? Because their teeth were crushed in their own mouth.

**12.** Hence too is that: *By what authority do you do these things? And I will ask you one word; answer me.* And he asked about John, whence was the baptism of John, from heaven, or from men; so that whatever they answered would be against them. They were unwilling to say: From men, fearing men lest they be stoned by them, because they held John for a prophet: to say: From heaven, they more dreaded, lest they confess Christ himself, because John had preached Christ. Pressed on both sides, on this side and on that, they who were preparing to bring a charge answered ignorance; they said: *We do not know*[^44]. For they were preparing a slander, when they said: *By what authority do you do these things?* so that if he said: I am Christ, they might assail him as arrogant, proud, sacrilegious. He was unwilling to say: I am Christ; but asked about John, who had said that he himself was the Christ. They did not dare to reprehend John, fearing lest they be killed by the people: they did not dare to say: John spoke truly, lest it be said to them: Believe him. They fell silent, they said they did not know: now they could not bite. Whence could they not? It already occurs to you; their teeth were crushed in their own mouth.

**13.** The Lord displeased that Pharisee who had invited him to dinner, because a sinful woman came to him at his feet; and he murmured against him, saying: *If this man were a prophet, he would know what woman has come to him at his feet.* O you, no prophet, whence do you know that he did not know what woman had come to him at his feet? Because, namely, he did not keep the cleanness of the Jews, which was guarded, as it were, outwardly in the flesh, and was an exile from the heart, he suspected this of the Lord. But the Lord, who knew the woman's sins, also heard the thoughts of his host, and answered what you know. And not to speak long of this, yet in his mouth he wished to crush his teeth. For he set forth: *Two debtors owed a certain moneylender: one owed five hundred denarii, the other fifty. Neither had the means to repay; he forgave both. Which loved him more?* To this end the Lord asks, that the other may answer: to this end the other answers, that his teeth may be crushed in his mouth. He answered confounded, he was shut out: she was admitted to receive mercy, who had burst into another's dwelling, but had not come to another's God: *The Lord crushed their teeth in their mouth*[^45].

**14.** *The Lord has broken the molars of the lions.*[^46] Not only of the asps. What of the asps? The asps, lying in wait, wish to inject poison, and scatter it, and hiss it in. Most openly the nations raged, and roared like lions. *Why have the nations raged, and the peoples meditated empty things*[^47]? When they lay in wait for the Lord: *Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or is it not lawful?* they were asps, they were serpents: their teeth were crushed in their mouth. Afterward they cried out: *Crucify, crucify*[^48]: now it is not the tongue of an asp, but the roar of a lion. But *the Lord has broken the molars of the lions too.* Perhaps here it is not without point that he did not add, in their mouth. For those lying in wait with captious questions were compelled to be conquered by their own answer: but those who openly raged—were they to be convicted by questions? Yet their molars too were crushed: crucified, he rose again, ascended into heaven, Christ was glorified, is adored by all nations, is adored by all kings. Let the Jews rage now, if they can. They do not rage. *The Lord has broken the molars of the lions.*

**15.** We have this lesson and experience in the heretics too, because we find them too to be serpents deafened by indignation, unwilling to hear the medicine medicated by the wise one: and in their own mouth the Lord has crushed their teeth. How they raged against us, reprehending us as though we were persecutors, for shutting them out of the basilicas? Now ask them: Ought heretics to be shut out of the basilicas, or ought they not? Let them answer now. If they say they ought not: the Maximianists reclaim the basilicas. But lest the Maximianists reclaim the basilicas, they say they ought. What is it, then, that you were saying against us? Or are your teeth crushed in your mouth? What have we to do with kings, they say? what have we to do with emperors? You presume on the emperors. I too ask: What have you to do with the proconsuls, whom the emperors sent? what have you to do with the laws, which the emperors gave against you? The emperors of our communion gave laws against all heretics: they certainly call those heretics who are not of their communion, among whom certainly you too are. If the laws are true, let them avail against you heretics too; if the laws are false, why do they avail against your heretics? Brethren, attend a little, and understand what we have said: when they pleaded their cases against the Maximianists, so as to cast them, condemned by themselves and schismatics, out of their places, which those men held from of old, and had succeeded the bishops their predecessors; wishing to shut them out from there, they proceeded by public laws, came to the judges, said they were Catholics, that they might be able to shut out heretics. Why do you call yourself a Catholic, that a heretic may be shut out, and are you not rather a Catholic, that you may not be a shut-out heretic? Now you are a Catholic, that you may avail to shut out a heretic. For the judge could not judge except by his own laws. They said they were Catholics; they were admitted to plead: they said those men were heretics; he asked whence it might be proved; the Council of Bagai was read, where the Maximianists were condemned; it was inserted into the proconsular Acts, it was proved that those condemned ought not to hold the basilicas, and the proconsul pronounced from the law. From what law? The one passed against heretics. If against heretics, then against you too. Why, he says, against me? for I am not a heretic. If you are not a heretic, those laws are false: for they were passed by those emperors who are not of your communion; all who are not of their communion they call heretics by their laws. I do not ask whether true or false; let that question be set aside, if it is still a question: meanwhile, according to you I now ask: Are the laws true, or are the laws false? If they are true, let them be believed: if they are false, why do you use them? You said to the proconsul: I am a Catholic, shut out the heretic. He asked whence it might be proved a heretic: you brought forth your council, you showed that you had condemned him. He, either conniving, or not understanding, yet used the law as a judge: and you made of the judge what you do not wish to be made of yourself. For if the judge used the emperor's law at your suggestion, why do you not use it for your own correction? Behold, he cast out your heretic by his emperor's law: why are you unwilling that he cast you out by the same law? We repeat what you did: behold, the basilicas were held by the Maximianists, now they are held by you; the Maximianists were shut out of them: there exist the orders of the proconsuls, there exist the records of the proceedings; officers are received, cities are stirred up, men are driven from their places. Why? Because they are heretics. By what law are they driven out? Answer: let us see whether your teeth are not yet crushed in your mouth. Is the law false? Let it not avail against your heretic. Is the law true? Let it avail against you too. They have nothing to answer; *God has crushed their teeth in their mouth.* And therefore, where they cannot creep with slippery deceit like asps, they roar with open violence like lions. The armed throngs of Circumcellions leap out and rage; they make as much slaughter as they can, as much as they can. But *the Lord has broken the molars of the lions too.*

**16.** [v. 8] *They will be despised like running water.*[^49] Let not certain rivers that are called torrents terrify you, brethren; they are filled with winter waters: do not fear; after a little while it passes, the water runs off, it makes a din for a time, soon it will cease; they cannot stand long. Many heresies are already dead: they ran in their channels as much as they could, they ran off, the channels dried up, scarcely is their memory found, or that they ever were. *They will be despised like running water.* But not only they: this whole age makes a din for a time, and seeks whom to drag along. All the ungodly, all the proud, sounding against the rocks of their pride as with rushing and confluent waters, let them not terrify you; they are winter waters, they cannot always flow: they must run down into their place, into their end. And yet of this torrent of the age the Lord drank. For he suffered here, he drank the very torrent; but he drank on the way, but in passing, because he stood not in the way of sinners. But what does Scripture say of him? *He will drink of the torrent on the way, therefore he will lift up his head*[^50]: that is, therefore was he glorified, because he died; therefore he rose again, because he suffered. If he were unwilling to drink on the way of the torrent, he would not die; if he did not die, he would not rise again; if he did not rise again, he would not be glorified. Therefore, *he will drink of the torrent on the way, therefore he will lift up his head.* Our head is already exalted: let his members follow him. *They will be despised like running water. He bent his bow, until they are weakened.* The threats of God do not cease: the bow of God, the threats of God. The bow is bent, it does not yet strike. *He bent his bow, until they are weakened.* And many have been weakened, terrified by the bending of his bow. For hence was that one weakened who said: *What do you bid me do? I am*, he says, *Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute*[^51]. He who cried out from heaven was bending the bow. Many, then, who were enemies, were weakened, and, converted, were unwilling for long to raise their necks against the perseverance of the stretched bow. For so was that one too weakened who says, lest we fear to be weakened: *When I am weak, then I am strong.* And when he prayed that the thorn of the flesh be taken from him, what was answered him? *Power is made perfect in weakness*[^52]. *He bent his bow, until they are weakened.*

**17.** [v. 9] *Like melted wax they will be taken away.*[^53] For you were going to say: Not all are so weakened as I, so that they believe; many persevere in their evil and in their malice. And of these fear nothing: *Like melted wax they will be taken away.* They will not stand against you, they will not endure: by a certain fire of their own desires they will perish. For there is here a certain hidden punishment: of it the Psalm is now going to speak unto the end. The verses are few: be attentive. There is a certain future punishment, the fire of Gehenna, eternal fire. For future punishment has two kinds. Either it is of hell, where that rich man was burning, who wished a drop of water to be dropped onto his tongue from the finger of the poor man whom he had despised before his gate, when he said: *Because I am tormented in this flame*[^54]. And the other is that at the end, of which those are to hear who are to be set on the left: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^55]. Those punishments will be manifest at the time when one has gone out from this life, or, the age ended, one has come to the resurrection of the dead. Now, then, is there no punishment, and does God leave sins altogether unpunished until that day? There is here too a certain hidden punishment; of it the discussion is now. This the Spirit of God wishes to commend: let us understand it, beware of it, avoid it, and we shall not fall into those much more terrible ones. Perhaps someone is going to say to me: There are punishments here too, prisons, exiles, torments, deaths, various kinds of griefs and tribulations. There are indeed these too, and they are dispensed by the judgment of God; but to many for proving, to many for damnation. Yet we see sometimes the just afflicted in these punishments, and the unjust to be free from these punishments: whence the feet of that one tottered, who afterward, giving thanks, said: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart! But my feet were almost moved, because I was zealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^56]. For he had seen the happiness of the wicked, and was delighted to be wicked, seeing the wicked reign, that it was well with them, that they abounded in all plenty of temporal things, such as he himself, still a little one, was still desiring from the Lord: and his feet tottered, until he saw what in the end is to be hoped for or feared. For he says in the same psalm: *This is a labor before me, until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand concerning the last things*[^57]. So not the punishments of hell, not the punishments of that eternal fire after the resurrection, not those punishments which in this age are still mixed for the just and the unjust, and very often heavier for the just than for the unjust: but some punishment, I know not what, of the present life, the Spirit of God wishes to commend. Attend, hear me about to say what you knew: but it is sweeter when it is shown in a Psalm, which before it was shown was thought obscure. For behold, I bring forth things you already knew: but because they are brought forth from a place where you did not yet see them, it comes about that even things known delight as new. Hear the punishment of the ungodly: *Like wax*, he says, *melted they will be taken away.* I said this comes about for them through their own desires. Evil desire is, as it were, a burning and a fire. Fire consumes a garment, does the lust of adultery not consume the soul? When Scripture was speaking of adultery thought of, it said: *Will anyone bind fire in his bosom, and not burn his garments*[^58]? You carry coals in your bosom, the tunic is pierced through: you carry adultery in your thought, and is the soul whole?

**18.** But few see these punishments: therefore the Spirit of God especially commends them. Hear the Apostle saying: *God handed them over to the desires of their heart*[^59]. Behold the fire, before whose face they melt like wax: for they are loosed from a certain constancy of chastity; therefore they too, going into their lusts, are called loosed and fluid. Whence fluid? whence loosed? From the fire of desires. *God handed them over to the desires of their heart, so that they do what is not fitting, filled with all iniquity.* And he enumerates many things that are sins, and says they are the punishments of sins. For he says the first punishment is pride: nay, not the punishment, but the first sin is pride. The first sin is pride: the last punishment is eternal fire, or the fire of hell; for that is now of the damned. Between that first sin and this last punishment, the things that are in the middle are both sins and punishments. For the Apostle says so many things are done by them, which are detestable sins; and yet he says they are punishments. *For this reason*, he says, *God handed them over to the desires of their heart, to uncleanness, so that they do what is not fitting.* And lest anyone think he is afflicted only by those punishments by which he is now delighted, and not fear what is to come in the end, he mentioned the last punishment: *Who, although they had known the righteousness of God, did not understand*, he says, *that those who do these things are worthy of death; not only those who do them, but also those who consent to those who do them*[^60]. *Those who do these things are worthy of death*: which things? Those he enumerated above to be among the punishments: for he said, *God handed them over to the desires of their heart, so that they do what is not fitting.* That one is an adulterer, is already a punishment; that he is a liar, that he is greedy, that he is fraudulent, that he is a murderer, are already punishments. Punishments of what sin? Of the prior apostasy, of that pride: *The beginning of man's sin is to apostatize from God;* and *the beginning of all sin is pride*[^61]. Therefore he stated the sin itself first: *Who, although they knew God, did not glorify him as God, or give thanks; but became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^62]. This is already a punishment, the darkening of the heart. But whence did it befall? *For saying they were wise, they became fools*[^63]. Because they said they had of themselves what they had received from God; or, if they knew from whom they received it, they did not glorify him from whom they knew they had received it; this is: *Saying they were wise.* And there at once the punishment followed: *They became fools, and their foolish heart was darkened. Saying they were wise, they became fools.* Is this a small punishment? To speak of this alone, is the darkening of the heart, the blinding of the mind, a small punishment? If someone committing theft at once lost an eye, all would say that God present had taken vengeance. He lost the eye of the heart, and God is thought to have spared him! *Like melted wax they will be taken away.*

**19.** *Fire fell upon them, and they did not see the sun.*[^64] You see how he speaks of a certain punishment of darkening. *Fire fell upon them*: the fire of pride, the smoky fire, the fire of desire, the fire of anger. How great a fire is it? Upon whom it falls, he will not see the sun. Therefore it was said: *Let not the sun set upon your anger*[^65]. Therefore, brethren, fear the fire of evil desire, if you do not wish to be melted like wax, and to perish from the face of God. For this fire falls upon them, and you will not see the sun. What sun? Not this one which the cattle too see with you, and the flies, both good and evil; because he makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil. But there is another sun, of which those are to say: *And the sun did not rise for us; all those things passed away like a shadow. Therefore we erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness did not shine for us, and the sun did not rise for us*[^66]. Why, except because *fire fell upon them, and they did not see the sun*? The concupiscence of the flesh conquered them. And whence does this concupiscence come? Attend. You were born from the stock with that which you must conquer: do not add enemies to yourself, conquer the one with whom you were born. To the stadium of this life you came with it, grapple with the one who came forth with you. With that one unconquered, why do you provoke the troops of desires? For carnal delight, brethren, is born with the man. But he who is well instructed quickly sees his enemy, and attacks and wrestles, and quickly conquers: for he is a match for enemies not yet grown. But he who despises to conquer that concupiscence with which he was born from the propagation of sin, and still rouses and brings forth many lusts, conquers them with difficulty, and, divided against himself, is consumed by his own fire. So do not hope that those punishments alone are to come; see the present ones. *Fire fell upon them, and they did not see the sun.*

**20.** [v. 10] *Before your thorns bring forth the bramble, as living, as in anger he will drink them down.*[^67] What is the bramble? It is a kind of thorn; certain very dense thorns are said to be such. First it is an herb; and when it is an herb, it is soft and beautiful: yet there are in it the thorns that are afterward to come forth. So now sins delight, and as it were do not prick. The bramble is an herb, and yet is already a thorn. *Before the bramble brings forth thorns*: before the manifest torments of wretched delights and pleasures come forth. Let those question themselves who love something, and cannot attain it; let them see whether they are not tormented by desire: and when they have attained what they unlawfully desire, let them attend whether they are not tormented by fear. Let them see, then, here their punishments, before that resurrection comes, when those rising again in the flesh will not be changed. For we shall all rise again, but we shall not all be changed. For they will have a corruption of the flesh in which they may feel pain, not in which they may die; otherwise those griefs too would be ended. Then the thorns of that bramble, that is, all the griefs and prickings of torments, will be brought forth. What thorns will those suffer who are to say: *These are they whom we once held in derision*[^68]? The thorns of the pricking of repentance, but late and unfruitful, like the barrenness of thorns. The repentance of this time is a medicinal grief: the repentance of that time is a penal grief. Do you not wish to suffer those thorns? Here be pricked with the thorns of repentance, that you may do what was said: *I was turned in distress, while the thorn was being fixed. My sin I acknowledged, and my iniquity I did not cover. I said: I will pronounce against myself my offense to the Lord; and you forgave the impiety of my heart*[^69]. Do it now, be pricked now; let it not come about in you what was said of certain detestable ones: *They were rent, and were not pricked*[^70]. Attend to those who *were rent, and were not pricked.* You see them rent, and you do not see them pricked. Behold, they are outside the Church, and do not repent, so as to laugh at that from which they were rent. The bramble will afterward bring forth their thorns. They do not wish now to have the medicinal pricking; they will have the penal afterward. But even now, before the bramble brings forth thorns, fire has fallen upon them which does not permit them to see the sun, which drinks them down, still living, in the anger of God: the fire of evil desires, of vain honors, of pride, of their own greed; and to what does it press them? that they not know the truth, that they not seem conquered, that the truth itself not subject them. For what is more glorious, brethren, than to be subjected and conquered by the truth? Let the truth overcome you willing; for even unwilling it will overcome you. So then that fire of evil desires, which fell upon them so that they do not see the sun, drinks down the bramble, before it brings forth their thorns: that is, it hides their evil life, before that same life brings forth in the end manifest torments; but in the anger of God this fire hides the bramble. For it is no small punishment that they now do not see the sun, nor believe that the thorns of punishments are afterward to come forth for them from this evil life. For you, he says, are the bramble; which bramble, that is, you yourselves, living, that is, still set in this life, before it brings forth in the future judgment the manifest thorns of your punishments, now in anger he drinks down, that is, as by swallowing it he does not allow it to appear. So this order of words, as I think, is more plainly connected thus: *Fire fell upon them, and they did not see the sun*: which fire *as in anger, as living, drinks you down, the bramble, before it brings forth your thorns*: that is, you yourselves, whom he finds as a bramble, he drinks down before death, before that same bramble brings forth your thorns after death in that penal resurrection. But why did he not say, living, but, *as living*, except because this life of the ungodly is false? For they do not live, but seem to themselves to live. And why not, in anger, but, *as in anger*, except because God does this tranquilly? For this too is written: *But you, O Lord of hosts, judge with tranquility*[^71]. He, then, even when he threatens, is not angry. For he is not disturbed, but as it were is angry, because he punishes and avenges. And those who are unwilling to be corrected, as it were live, but do not live. For the vengeance of the first sin, and of those they added, remains upon them: and it is called the anger of God, because it comes from the judgment of God. Whence the Lord says of the unbeliever: *But the anger of God abides upon him*[^72]. For we are born mortal with the anger of God. Whence the Apostle says: *We too were once by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^73]. What is, *by nature children of wrath*, except because we carry with us the punishment of the first sin? But if we are converted, the anger is taken away, grace is furnished. You are unwilling to be converted, you add even upon what you were born: as in anger you will be drunk down in the present time.

**21.** [v. 11] Recognize, then, this punishment, and rejoice that you are not in this punishment, all you who make progress, all you who understand and love the truth, all you who wish in yourselves the victory of truth more than your own, who do not close your ears against the truth, from the delight of present things and the recollection of things past, lest you be a dog returned to its own vomit. All you who are such, see the punishments of those who are not such, and rejoice. As yet the punishments of hell have not come, as yet the eternal fire has not come: let him who makes progress in God compare himself now with the ungodly, the blind heart with the enlightened heart; compare the two, the one who sees and the one who does not see in the flesh. And what great thing is the sight of the flesh? Did Tobit have eyes of flesh? His son had them, and he did not; and, blind, he was showing the way of life to one who saw. Therefore, when you see this punishment, rejoice that you are not in it. Therefore Scripture says: *The just man will rejoice, when he sees vengeance.*[^74] Not the future vengeance; for see what follows: *He will wash his hands in the blood of the sinner.* What is this? Let Your Charity attend. Surely, when murderers are struck, ought the innocent to go there, and wash their hands? But what is, *he will wash his hands in the blood of the sinner*? The just man, when he sees the punishment of the sinner, himself makes progress: and the death of one avails for the life of another. For if spiritually the blood runs from those who die within, you, seeing such vengeance, wash your hands there; henceforth live more cleanly. And how will he wash his hands, if he is just? For what has he in his hands that should be washed, if he is just? But the just man lives by faith. He called the faithful, then, just: and from the time you have believed, you already begin to be called just. For there has been a remission of sins. And even if some of your sins are from this remaining life, which cannot but flow, as it were like water from the sea into the bilge: yet because you have believed; when you see one who is altogether turned away from God killed in that blindness, with that fire falling upon him not seeing the sun, you who now through faith see Christ, that you may see through vision, because the just lives by faith, attend to the ungodly one dying, and purge yourself from sins. So you will, in a manner, wash your hands in the blood of the sinner. *He will*, then, *wash his hands in the blood of the sinner.*

**22.** [v. 12] *And man will say: If, then, there is fruit for the just.*[^75] Behold, before what is promised comes, before eternal life is given, before the ungodly are cast into eternal fire, here in this life there is fruit for the just. What fruit? *Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation*[^76]. What fruit for the just? *We glory in tribulations: knowing that tribulation works patience, and patience proving, and proving hope, and hope does not confound; because the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^77]. Does the drunkard rejoice, and the just man not rejoice? In love is the fruit for the just. Wretched is the former, even when he intoxicates himself: blessed is the latter, even when he hungers and thirsts. The former gorges himself with wine; hope feeds the latter. Let him see, then, the punishment of the one, his own joy, and think of God. He who has now given such joy from faith, from hope, from love, from the truth of his Scriptures, what does he prepare in the end! On the way he so feeds us, in the homeland how will he fatten us! *And man will say: If, then, there is fruit for the just.* Let those who see believe, and let them see and understand. *The just man will rejoice, when he sees vengeance.* But if he does not have eyes whereby to see vengeance, he will be saddened, nor will he be corrected by it. But if he sees it, he sees what difference there is between the darkened eye of the heart and the enlightened eye of the heart; between the refreshment of chastity and the flame of lust; between the security of hope and the fear of crime. When he sees this, let him distinguish himself, and wash his hands in the blood of that one. Let him make progress from the comparison, and say: *Therefore there is fruit for the just: therefore there is a God judging them on the earth*[^78]: not yet in that life, not yet in the eternal fire, not yet in hell, but here on earth. Behold, that rich man is still clothed in purple and fine linen, and still feasts splendidly every day. The bramble has not yet brought forth thorns; he does not yet say: *I am tormented in this flame*[^79]: but already there is blindness of mind, already the eye of the mind is extinguished. If, blind in the eyes of the flesh, he reclined at his table, however rich, you would call him wretched: blind within, he does not see the bread, Christ, and is he blessed? No one says this but one equally blind. *Therefore there is fruit for the just: therefore there is a God judging them on the earth.*

**23.** If we have been somewhat lengthy, pardon us. We exhort you in the name of Christ, that you think upon these things you have heard unto fruit. Because both to preach the truth is nothing, if the heart dissents from the tongue; and to hear the truth profits nothing, if a man does not build upon the rock. He who builds upon the rock is he who hears and does: but he who hears and does not do, builds upon the sand: he who neither hears nor does, builds nothing. But as he who builds upon the sand builds a ruin for himself; so he who does not build upon the rock, when the flood comes, is swept away without a house. There is nothing to do but both to build and to build upon the rock; that is, both to hear and to do. Nor let anyone say: Why do I go to church? behold, those who go to church daily do not do what they hear. Yet they do go in order to hear; so they can come to hear and to do: but you, how far are you from doing, who so flee from hearing? But I, he says, do not build upon the sand. The flood will find you naked: will it therefore not sweep you away? will the rain therefore not kill you? will the winds therefore not carry you off? Therefore I will come and hear. But when you have heard, do. For if you hear, and do not do, you have indeed built, but upon the sand. Since, then, set without a building, we are naked, but set in a building placed upon the sand, we are under a ruin; it remains that we build upon the rock, and do what we have heard.
