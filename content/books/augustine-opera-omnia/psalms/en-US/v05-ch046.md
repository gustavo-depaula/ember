# On the Same Psalm 118 (21)

**1.** [v. 97.] We have often advised that the praiseworthy breadth, in which, when we do the commandments of God, we suffer no straits, is to be understood as love. On account of which, in this great psalm too, when he had said above, *Your commandment is exceedingly broad,* in this following verse he shows whence it is broad, saying: *How I have loved your law, O Lord!* Love, then, is the breadth of the commandment. For whence can it come about that what God commands to be loved is loved, and the command itself is not loved? For that is the law. *All day,* he says, *it is my meditation.* Behold how I have loved it, that all day it should be my meditation: or rather, as the Greek has, *the whole day,* where the continuance of the one meditating is more expressed. But this is understood as through all time, which is "always." By such love is cupidity overcome, which often contradicts the doing of the law's commands, the flesh lusting against the spirit: against which the spirit, lusting, ought so to love the law of God that all day it be its meditation. But the Apostle says: *Where then is your boasting? It is excluded. By what law? Of works? No, but by the law of faith*[^1]. This is the faith that works through love; since by seeking, asking, knocking, it obtains the good Spirit, through whom that very love is poured out in our hearts. For whoever are led by this Spirit of God, these are the sons of God[^2]: who are received that they may recline with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven; the servant being cast out, who does not remain in the house for ever; that is, Israel according to the flesh, to whom it was said, *When you see Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and all the Prophets in the kingdom of God, but you yourselves thrust out. And they will come from the east, and the west, and the north, and the south, and will recline in the kingdom of God. And behold, those are last who were first, and those are first who were last*[^3]. But the *Gentiles,* as the Vessel of election says, *who did not pursue righteousness, have attained righteousness; the righteousness, however, that is of faith: but Israel, pursuing the law of righteousness, did not arrive at the law of righteousness. Why? Because not of faith, but as of works, they stumbled at the stone of stumbling*[^4]. So they were made the enemies of this man who here speaks in prophecy. None can relish the commandments of God except those who are imbued with the Spirit of wisdom.

**2.** [v. 98.] And then he joins: *Above my enemies you have made me to relish your commandment, for it is mine for ever.* For those indeed have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge. For, ignorant of the righteousness of God, and seeking to establish their own, they are not subject to the righteousness of God[^5]. But this man, who relishes the commandment of God above these his enemies, wishes to be found with the Apostle, not having his own righteousness, which is of the law, but the righteousness through faith in Christ, which is of God[^6]: not because the law which his enemies read is not of God, but because they do not relish it, as this man does who relishes it above his enemies, by clinging to the stone at which they stumbled. For Christ is the end of the law, unto righteousness to everyone who believes[^7], that they may be justified freely by his grace: not like those who suppose they make the law by their own strength, and therefore seek, out of the law of God indeed, yet to establish their own righteousness; but like the son of the promise, who, hungering and thirsting for it, by asking, seeking, knocking, in a way begs from the Father, that, adopted through the Only-begotten, he may receive. But how would he relish the commandment of God thus, unless he himself had made him so to relish it, to whom he says, *Above my enemies you have made me to relish your commandment?* For his enemies, as it were born of Hagar unto slavery, sought temporal rewards out of the same commandment; and therefore it was not theirs for ever, as it is this man's. For those rendered it better who interpreted *for ever* than those who interpreted *for the age,* as though, this age being ended, there could now be no commandment of the law. So indeed there will be none written on visible tablets and in books: but on the tablets of the heart, the love of God and neighbor will remain for ever; on which twofold commandment the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets: and the reward of this commandment kept will be the commander himself, and the reward of love the very Beloved, when God will be all in all[^8].

**3.** [v. 99.] But what is this that follows: *Above all who teach me I have understood?* Who is this who has understood above all who teach him? Who, I say, is he who dares to set himself, by understanding, above all the Prophets—who taught with so excellent an authority not only those who lived with them, by speaking, but also posterity, by writing? To Solomon indeed so great wisdom was given that he seems to have been preferred even to all who were before him[^9]: but it is not to be believed that he could here have been prophesied by his father David; especially because what is here said could not be said in the person of Solomon: *From every evil way I have restrained my feet.* Further, if—which is more acceptable—this prophet foretells Christ, now from the Head, who is the Savior himself, now from his body, which is the Church, arranging the prophetic words, and making as it were one to speak, on account of that great mystery where it was said, *The two shall be in one flesh*[^10]; I plainly recognize him who understood above all who teach him, when, being twelve years old, the boy Jesus remained in Jerusalem, and was found by his parents after three days there in the temple, sitting among the teachers, hearing them and asking them questions; where all who heard him were amazed at his prudence and his answers[^11]. And not without reason, since through this prophecy he had said so long before, *Above all who teach me I have understood.* For he surely wishes all men to be understood, not God the Father; of whom the Son himself says, *As the Father taught me, these things I speak*[^12]. Which is most difficultly understood in the person of the Word; unless one can somehow grasp that for the Son to be taught by the Father is to be begotten. For one for whom it is not one thing to be and another to be taught, but for whom to be is the same as to be taught, surely from him from whom he has his being, from him at the same time he has his being taught. But in the person of the man, where he took the form of a servant, it is more easily understood that he learned from the Father what he said: in which form of a servant, set especially as a boy, men of greater age could think he must be taught; but he whom the Father taught understood above all who taught him. *Because your testimonies,* he says, *are my meditation.* Therefore he understood above all who teach him, because he meditated on the testimonies of God: which he knew about himself better than they did, who said, *You sent to John, and he bore witness to the truth: but I do not receive testimony from man, but I say these things that you may be saved. He was a burning and shining lamp; but you were willing to exult for an hour in his light: but I have testimony greater than John's*[^13]. Such testimonies he meditated, when he understood above all who teach him.

**4.** [v. 100.] But those teachers are not absurdly understood to be also the elders, of whom he says immediately: *Above the elders I have understood.* Which seems to me to be repeated in that manner so that, to us reading these things, that age of his might come to mind which became known to us from the Gospel, in which boyish age he sat among those of greater age—that is, younger among elders—and understood above all who teach him. For "younger" and "elder" are wont to be said of one another as lesser and greater, even though neither of them has come to or approached an aged time of life: although, if we wish to inquire also for the express name of elders in the Gospel, above whom he understood, we find it when the Scribes and Pharisees said to him, *Why do your disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? For they do not wash their hands when they eat bread*[^14]. Behold, the transgression of the tradition of the elders is alleged against him. But let us hear what he, who understood above the elders, answered them: *Why,* he says, *do you also transgress the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition*[^15]? Then a little later, that not only he who is the Head of the body, but also the body itself and its members might understand above those elders, whose tradition about washing hands was borne about; having called the crowds to himself, he said to them, *Hear, and understand*[^16]. As if he said: Understand above those elders, you also, that of you too that prophecy may shine forth to have been sent before: *Above the elders I have understood;* and may fit not only the Head, but also the body, and so the whole Christ. *Not what enters into the mouth defiles a man; but what proceeds out of the mouth, this defiles a man*[^17]. Those elders did not understand this, who had handed down their precepts about washing hands as though great commandments. The very members too of this Head, who understands above the elders, had not yet understood what was said by him. Finally, after a little, Peter answered and said to him, *Explain to us this parable*[^18]. He still thought it was a parable, what the Lord had spoken without figures. But he said: *Are you also still without understanding? Do you not understand that everything that enters the mouth goes into the belly, and is cast out into the privy; but the things that proceed out of the mouth come out of the heart, and these defile a man*[^19]? Are you also still without understanding, and do not understand above those elders? But plainly now, having heard such a master, our Head, each one of us can say: *Above the elders I have understood.* For what he went on and added fits the body too: *Because I have sought out your commandments. Your commandments,* not the commandments of men; *your commandments,* not the commandments of the elders, who, wishing to be teachers of the law, understand neither what they say, nor the things about which they make assertions[^20]. Rightly, concerning the commandments of God, which are to be sought out, that they may be understood above those elders, it was answered to those who preferred their authority to the truth, and it was said: *Why do you also transgress the commandment of God, to establish your traditions*[^21]?

**5.** [v. 101.] But now what is added seems to fit not the Head, but the body: *From every evil way I have restrained my feet, that I may keep your words.* For that Head of ours, the Savior himself of the body, would not be carried into any evil way by carnal cupidity, so that it would be necessary for him to restrain his feet from it, as though they were going there by their own motions: which we do, when we restrain our depraved desires—which he did not have—lest they hold the evil ways. For thus we can keep the words of God, if we do not go after our evil concupiscences, so that they reach the evil things desired; but rather, the spirit lusting against the flesh, bridle them, lest they drag us, seized and overthrown, through evil ways.

**6.** [v. 102.] *From your judgments,* he says, *I have not turned aside, for you have laid down a law for me.* He said what he feared, so as to restrain his feet from every evil way. For what is, *From your judgments I have not turned aside,* except what he says in another place, *But I feared your judgments?* Perseveringly I believed them, *because you have laid down a law for me.* You, more inward than my inmost parts, you within, in the heart, laid down a law for me by your spirit, as by your finger; that I might not fear it, like a servant, without love, but love it, like a son, with chaste fear, and with chaste love fear it.

**7.** [v. 103.] And therefore see what follows: *How sweet are your words to my throat!* or, what is more expressly from the Greek, *your utterances: above honey and the honeycomb to my mouth.* This is that sweetness which the Lord gives, that our land may give its fruit; that we may do the good truly well—that is, not from dread of carnal evil, but from delight in spiritual good. Some manuscripts indeed do not have *honeycomb,* but more have it. The open teaching of wisdom is like honey; but like the honeycomb is that which is pressed out from the more hidden mysteries, as from waxen cells, by the mouth of the one discoursing, as of one chewing: but it is sweet to the mouth of the heart, not of the flesh.

**8.** [v. 104.] But what is it that he says: *From your commandments I have understood?* For one thing is "I have understood your commandments;" another is, *From your commandments I have understood.* He signifies, then, that he understood I know not what else from the commandments of God: that is, as far as it seems to me, he says that by doing the commandments of God he arrived at the understanding of those things which he had desired to know. On account of which it is written: *You have desired wisdom; keep the commandments, and the Lord will furnish it to you*[^22]: lest anyone, going about it backward, before he has the humility of obedience, should wish to arrive at the height of wisdom, which he cannot grasp unless he comes in order. Let him hear, then: *Seek not the things that are too high for you, and search not the things that are too strong for you; but the things which the Lord has commanded you, think on these always*[^23]. Thus a man arrives at the wisdom of hidden things through the obedience of the commandments. But when he had said, *The things which the Lord has commanded you, think on these,* he added *always,* because both obedience is to be kept, that wisdom may be received, and, wisdom received, obedience is not to be forsaken. So this is the voice of the spiritual members of Christ: *From your commandments I have understood.* For the body of Christ rightly says this in those to whom, keeping the commandments, on account of the very keeping of the commandments, a richer teaching of wisdom is furnished: *Therefore,* he says, *I have hated every way of iniquity.* For the love of righteousness must hate every iniquity: which love is so much the greater, the more it is inflamed by the sweetness of a fuller wisdom, which is furnished to him who obeys God, and from his commandments understands.
