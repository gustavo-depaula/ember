# Exposition of Psalm 128

**1.** [vv. 1-4.] As the apostle says, dearest brothers: *We compare spiritual things with spiritual; but the natural man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God*[^1]; care must be taken lest natural men, not perceiving the things that are of the Spirit of God, be scandalized rather than edified by this psalm. For briefly (though we heard it when it was sung), because it is brief, I run through it, not treating it, but reading it. And see that if anyone covets, as a great thing from God, such things as this psalm contains; and perhaps, not because he has been deserted by him, but because he is the more loved, has not received them; and sees that those things which he here hears to be the rewards of those who fear God abound to those who do not fear God: his steps totter, and his footsteps are poured out in a fall, and he says in his heart that he has feared God without cause, when he did not deserve those gifts which he promised to those who fear him; and besides, even those received them who not only did not fear him, but even blasphemed him. For see what it says: *Blessed are all who fear the Lord, who walk in his ways*[^2]. *You will eat the labors of your fruits; you are blessed, and it will be well with you.* So far we can here, although natural men, think of the blessedness of the world to come; but see what follows: *Your wife is like a fruitful vine, on the sides of your house. Your sons are like new olive plantings, around your table. Behold, thus will the man be blessed who fears the Lord*[^3]. How? That his wife may be like a fruitful vine on the sides of his house, and his sons may surround his table, like a planting of olives. So then, have those lost their reward who, for God's sake, refused even to take wives? But he who refused to take a wife says: God will bless me in another way. On the contrary, either he will bless you thus, or he will not bless you: the sentence is plainly stated: *Behold, thus will the man be blessed who fears the Lord.*

**2.** What, then, does this mean, brothers? Lest, by desiring temporal and earthly happiness, we lose the heavenly, the Prophet has set up for us, as it were, a wrapping. This wrapping has something or other inside. But your charity remembers that, when I was treating for you the earlier psalm, the one before this, we ran into a certain obscure verse, where it was said: *As arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out*[^4]; and when we asked who the *sons of those who have been shaken out* are, it seemed to us, with the Lord prompting, as we believe, that the sons of those who have been shaken out are called the apostles, the sons of the prophets: because the prophets spoke in riddles, and covered the meaning with figures of things, as with wrappings of mysteries; which meaning could not come forth to men unless those wrappings were shaken out: whence those were called *sons of those who have been shaken out*, who came forth from the prophets when they were shaken out. So let us too shake out this psalm, lest we be deceived by the wrappings, and, touching what is inside and not seeing it, perhaps say 'wood' for 'gold,' or 'earthenware' for 'silver.' Let us shake it out, if it seems good to your charity: the Lord will be present, that what is inside may come forth; especially, my brothers, because we are celebrating the birthdays of the martyrs. How many evils the martyrs suffered, how many ruins, how many torments, the squalor of prisons, the tightness of chains, the savagery of beasts, the burning of flames, the goads of insults! Would they have suffered all these things, unless they saw something or other toward which they were stretching, which does not belong to the happiness of this world? But it is shameful that we should celebrate the birthdays of the martyrs—of these servants of God, that is, who despised this world for the sake of everlasting happiness—and take what is here written for present happiness, so that if it happens to some faithful man of God, a citizen of that Jerusalem, even after taking a wife to have no sons, we should say: This man does not fear the Lord; for if he feared the Lord, his wife would be like a fruitful vine in his house, not barren, so as to bear none; and if this man feared the Lord, his sons would surround his table, like olive trees. For if we say such things, we are natural men, not perceiving the things that are of the Spirit of God. Let us too begin to shake out, so that we too may be sons of those who have been shaken out. For if we are sons of those who have been shaken out, we will be in the hand of the mighty one like arrows, and he will hurl us, by his command, into the hearts of men who do not yet love, so that, struck by the arrows of the words of God, they may love. For if we begin to preach such things to them: Sons, or my brothers, fear the Lord, that you may have sons and grandsons, that your house may rejoice; we do not shoot so that that eternal Jerusalem may be loved: they will remain in the love of earthly things, and, seeing these things abound to the impious, even if they do not dare to us, they say in their heart: 'Why does the man who does not fear God have a house full of sons?' And perhaps another says to him: 'You do not yet know what may happen to him; what if he buries them, because he does not fear God, and more were born to him for this, that he may suffer greater grief at their death?' But if you say such things, he will answer you: 'I have known an impious man, a pagan, sacrilegious, a worshipper of idols (and perhaps he has known one and speaks truly, and has known not one, nor two or three), whom, an old man, decrepit, dead on his bed, a crowd of sons and grandsons carried to the grave. Behold, he did not fear the Lord, and the most fertile offspring of his house closed his eyes.' What shall we say to this? Nothing evil could happen to him, that he should bury his sons while living, since now, dead, he has been carried by his sons to a glorious tomb.

**3.** Let us shake it out, then, let us shake it out, if we wish to be sons of those who have been shaken out: let something come forth here. For there is a certain man who is thus blessed; and no one fears the Lord except one who is in the members of that man: and there are many men, and there is one man; for there are many Christians, and one Christ. The Christians themselves, with their head, which has ascended into heaven, are one Christ: not he one and we many, but we many too in that one are one. So Christ is one man, head and body. What is his body? His Church, the apostle saying: *Because we are members of his body*[^5]; and: *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^6]. Let us understand, then, the voice of this man, in whose body we are one man; and there we will see those true goods of Jerusalem. For he said thus at the end: *And may you see the goods that are of Jerusalem*[^7]. But if you attend to these goods with the earthly eye—an abundance of sons and grandsons, and the fertility and fecundity of a spouse—these are not the goods of that Jerusalem; because these goods are in the land of the dying, but that is the land of the living. Do not hold it as a great thing if you have sons who will die, even if not before you, certainly after you. Do you wish to have sons who will never die, and will always live with you? Be in the body of him of whom it was said: *But you are the body of Christ and members.*

**4.** For that the Psalm itself might show this—that it is so obscure as to warn that it be knocked upon, so covered as to wish to be shaken out—it began from the plural: *Blessed are all who fear the Lord, who walk in his ways.* He speaks to the many; but because those many are one in Christ, it goes on and now says in the singular: *You will eat the labors of your fruits.* Above he had said: *Blessed are all who fear the Lord, who walk in his ways*: why now: *You will eat the labors of your fruits*; and not, you all will eat? and why: *the labors of your* [singular] *fruits*; and not: the labors of your [plural] fruits? Has he so quickly forgotten that he was speaking to the many? But if you have already shaken it out, what has it answered you? When I address many Christians, I understand one in one Christ. So you are many, and you are one; we are many, and we are one. How are we many, and one? Because we cleave to him whose members we are; and whose head is in heaven, that the members may follow.

**5.** So let him now describe; since it is plain whom he describes. Thus all that follows will be plain: only fear the Lord, and walk in his ways, and do not be jealous of those who do not walk in his ways, when you see them to be unhappily happy. For the men of this world are unhappily happy: but the martyrs were happily unhappy. For they were unhappy for a time, but happy for eternity; and in that very thing, that they were unhappy for a time, they were thought more unhappy than they were. For what does the apostle say? *As though sorrowful, yet always rejoicing*[^8]. Why *always*? Both here and there: indeed both here and there. For here, whence do we rejoice? From hope. There, whence will we rejoice? From the reality. Great joy has the hope of one rejoicing. And if *rejoicing in hope*, see what follows: *patient in tribulation*[^9]. So the martyrs were patient in tribulation, because they rejoiced in hope. But because what is promised was not yet present, what does the apostle say? *For hope which is seen is not hope: but if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^10]. Behold why the martyrs bore all things: because what they did not see, they awaited through patience. Those who killed them loved what they saw: those who were killed sighed for the things they did not see, and hastened to lay hold of the things they did not see; and that they were killed too slowly, they reckoned to be delays for themselves.

**6.** So then, brothers, Felix the martyr, both truly happy in name and in crown, whose day is today, despised the world. Or was it perhaps because he feared the Lord that he was happy on this account, that he was blessed on this account, because his wife was like a fruitful vine in the land, and his sons surrounded his table? He has all these things perfectly, but in the body of him who is here described: and because he so understood it, he despised present things, that he might receive future things. But you should know, brothers, that he did not suffer the death that the other martyrs suffered. For he confessed, he was put off to torments; on another day his body was found lifeless. For they had shut him in prison, but the body, not the spirit. Him whom the executioners were preparing to torture there, they found absent; they lost their savagery. He lay lifeless, without feeling toward them, that he might not be tortured; with feeling toward God, that he might be crowned. Whence he too is happy, brothers, not only in name, but also in the reward of eternal life, if he loved these things.

**7.** So let us hear this psalm as though it were said of Christ: and let us all, cleaving to the body of Christ and made members of Christ, walk in the ways of the Lord; and let us fear the Lord with a chaste fear, a fear that abides forever and ever. For there is another fear which love casts out, John saying: *Fear is not in love; but perfect love casts out fear*[^11]. He does not say of every fear that it is cast out by love; for you have a psalm saying: *The fear of the Lord is chaste, abiding forever and ever*[^12]. So one fear abides, another is cast out. The fear that is cast out is not chaste; but that which abides is chaste. What is the fear that is cast out? Deign to take notice. Some fear only for this, lest they suffer something evil on earth, lest sickness befall them, lest loss, lest bereavement, lest the loss of someone dear, lest exile, lest condemnation, lest prison, lest some tribulation; for this they fear and tremble: this fear is not yet chaste. Hear further. Another fears not to suffer on this earth, but fears hell, with which the Lord too frightened. You heard when the Gospel was read: *Where their worm will not die, and their fire will not be quenched*[^13]. Men hear these things; and because they are truly to come for the impious, they fear, and restrain themselves from sin. They have fear, and through fear they restrain themselves from sin. They fear indeed, but they do not love justice. But when through fear they restrain themselves from sin, a habit of justice comes about, and what was hard begins to be loved, and God grows sweet: and the man now begins to live justly for this reason, not because he fears punishments, but because he loves eternity. Fear, then, has been cast out by love; but a chaste fear has succeeded it.

**8.** What is this chaste fear? According to which we ought, my brothers, to understand what was said: *Blessed are all who fear the Lord, who walk in his ways.* If I can speak worthily of this chaste fear, with the Lord our God helping, many perhaps will be set aflame by this chaste fear into a chaste love. Nor can I perhaps explain it, except by setting forth some likeness. Suppose some chaste woman, fearing her husband: suppose another, an adulteress; and she too fears her husband. The chaste woman fears lest her husband depart; the adulteress, lest he come. What if both are absent? The one fears lest he come; the other, lest he delay. Absent in a certain way is he to whom we are betrothed, absent is he who gave us the pledge, the Holy Spirit, absent is he who redeemed us by his blood; that bridegroom, than whom nothing is more beautiful, who appeared as it were unsightly in the hands of his persecutors, of whom Isaiah said a little before: *And we saw him, and he had no form nor comeliness*[^14]. So is our bridegroom unsightly? Far from it: for how would the virgins love him, who did not seek husbands on earth? So to his persecutors he appeared unsightly; and unless they thought him unsightly, they would not leap upon him, would not beat him with scourges, would not crown him with thorns, would not dishonor him with spittle: but because he appeared unsightly to them, they did these things to him; for they had not the eyes by which Christ might be seen as beautiful. To what kind of eyes did Christ appear beautiful? What kind of eyes was Christ himself seeking, when he said to Philip: *So long a time I have been with you, and you have not seen me?*[^15] These eyes are to be cleansed, that they may be able to see that light; and yet, even lightly grazed by its splendor, they are kindled with love, so that they wish to be healed, and to be made enlightened. For that you may know that Christ who is loved is beautiful, the Prophet says: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^16]. His beauty surpasses all men. What is it we love in Christ? The crucified members, the pierced side, or love? When we hear that he suffered for us, what do we love? Love is loved. He loved us, that we might love him in return; and that we might be able to love him in return, he visited us with his Spirit. He is beautiful, and he is absent. Let the bride ask herself whether she is chaste. We are all among the members, my brothers; we are among his members, therefore we are one man. Let each one see what kind of fear he has, whether the one which love casts out, or a chaste fear abiding forever and ever. He has just now proved it; I speak, and he will prove it. Our bridegroom is absent; ask your conscience: do you wish him to come, or do you still wish him to delay? See, brothers: I have knocked at the doors of your breasts; the voice of those who dwell within he has heard. What the consciences of each one have just now said could not reach my ears, because I am a man: he who is absent in bodily presence, but present in the vigor of his majesty, has heard you. How many, if it is said to them: Behold, now Christ, tomorrow the day of judgment; do not say: 'Would that he might come!' For those who do say it love much: and if it is said to them: He will delay; they fear lest he delay, because their fear is chaste. But as now it is feared that he may delay, so when he comes it will be feared that he may depart. But this fear will be chaste, because tranquil and secure. For we will not be deserted by him, when he has found us, since he sought us before we sought him. So a chaste fear, my brothers, has this: it comes from love. But that fear which is not yet chaste fears his presence and punishments. By fear it does whatever good it does; not by fear of losing that good, but by fear of suffering that evil. It does not fear lest it lose the embraces of the most beautiful bridegroom, but it fears lest it be cast into hell. This fear is good, it is useful; it will not indeed abide forever and ever: but it is not yet that chaste fear abiding forever and ever.

**9.** In what is it chaste? Now again I ask something, that you may ask yourselves. If God, coming, were to speak to us with his own voice (although he does not cease to speak through his Letters), and were to say to a man: You wish to sin, sin; do whatever delights you; whatever you have loved on earth, let it become yours; whoever you are angry at, let him perish; whoever you wish to plunder, let him be plundered; whoever to strike, let him be struck; whoever to condemn, let him be condemned; whoever to possess, may you possess: let no one resist you, let no one say to you: What are you doing? no one: Do not do it; no one: Why have you done it? Let all those earthly things which you have coveted abound to you, and live in them, not for a time, but always: only my face you will never see. My brothers, whence did you groan, except because a chaste fear, abiding forever and ever, has now been born? Why was your heart struck? If God were to say: You will never see my face: behold, you will abound in all this earthly happiness, you will have an abundance of all things, temporal goods will flow around you; you do not lose them, you do not desert them; what more do you want? A chaste fear would indeed weep and groan, and say: On the contrary, let all things be taken away, and let me see your face. A chaste fear would cry out from the psalm, and say: *O God of hosts, convert us; and show your face, and we shall be saved*[^17]. A chaste fear would cry out from the psalm, and say: *One thing I have asked of the Lord.* See how this chaste fear burns, this true love, this sincere love. *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek.* What? *That I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life.* What if for the sake of earthly happiness? Hear what follows: *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord, and be protected as his temple*[^18]; that is, to be his temple, and to be protected by him, this one thing I have asked of the Lord. If you ask this one thing, if you exercise your heart toward this one thing, and fear to lose only this, you will neither envy earthly happinesses, and you will hope for that true happiness, and you will be in the body of him to whom it is sung: *Blessed are all who fear the Lord, who walk in his ways.*

**10.** *You will eat the labors of your fruits.* O you, O you—you many who are one—*you will eat the labors of your fruits.* It seems, as it were, to speak perversely to those who do not understand: for it ought to have said: You will eat the fruit of your labors. For many eat the fruit of their labors. They labor in a vineyard, they do not eat the labor itself; but they eat what is born of their labor. They labor about fruit-bearing trees; who eats labors? But what those trees have brought forth, the fruit of the labor, this gladdens the farmer. What does it mean: *You will eat the labors of your fruits*? Now we have labors, the fruit will come later. But because the labors themselves are not without joy, on account of the hope of which we spoke a little before: *rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation*[^19]; now our very labors gladden us, and make us glad through hope. If, then, our labor could be eaten, and could gladden; the fruit of that labor, when eaten, what will it be like? They ate their labors, who *going went and wept, casting their seeds*: how much more gladly will they eat the fruits of their labors, who *coming will come with exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^20]? And that you may know, brothers, that this labor is eaten, in the earlier psalm you heard it said to the proud, who wished to rise before the light, that is, before Christ, not through the humility by which Christ rose; it was said to them: *Rise after you have sat down*[^21]: that is, be humbled, and rise from there; because he too came to be humbled, who was exalted for your sake. And what was said? *You who eat the bread of sorrow.* This is the labor of the fruits, the bread of sorrow. For unless it were eaten, it would not be called bread: but unless this bread had some sweetness, no one would eat it. With how great a sweetness he weeps in groaning, who prays! The tears of those who pray are sweeter than the joys of the theaters. And hear the flame of desire by which that bread is eaten, of which he here says: *You who eat the bread of sorrow.* In another place this lover, whose voice in the Psalm we very often recognize, says: *My tears have been my bread day and night*[^22]. Whence have tears become bread? *While it is said to me daily: Where is your God?* For before we see him who loved us, who gave us a pledge, to whom we are betrothed, the pagans exult over us, and say: 'Where is what the Christians worship? Let them show us what they worship. Behold, I show them my god, and let them show me their God.' When the pagan says this to you, you find nothing to show him; because there is nothing to show. So you turn back, and weep to God; because you sigh for him before you see him, and groan with longing for him; and because you weep in longing for him, those very tears are sweet, and will be for food for you, because they too have become for you bread day and night, while it is said to you daily: *Where is your God?* But your God will come, of whom it is said: *Where is he?* and will wipe away the tears, and he himself will succeed in place of the bread of tears, and will fatten you forever; because the Word of God will be with us, on whom the angels feed[^23]. Meanwhile, now the labors of the fruits, afterward the fruit of the labor. *You will eat the labors of your fruits; you are blessed, and it will be well with you.* *You are blessed* is of the present; *it will be well with you* is of the future: when you eat the labors of your fruits, *you are blessed*; when you have come to the fruit of your labors, *it will be well with you.* What did he say? For if it will be well with you, surely you will be blessed; and if you will be blessed, surely it will be well with you. But there is a difference between hope and reality. If hope is so sweet, how much sweeter will the reality be!

**11.** Now let us come to this: *Your wife*: it is said to Christ. So his wife is his Church; his Church, his wife, is we ourselves. *Like a fruitful vine.* But in whom is it a fruitful vine? For we see many who enter these walls who are barren; we see that many enter these walls who are drunkards, usurers, slave-dealers, who seek out fortune-tellers, who go to enchanters and enchantresses when their head aches. Is this the fertility of the vine? is this the fruitfulness of the wife? It is not. These are thorns, but it is not everywhere thorny. It has a certain fertility, and it is a fruitful vine; but in whom? *On the sides of your house.* Not all are called the sides of the house. For I ask what the sides are: what shall I say? Are they walls, like strong stones? If he were speaking of this bodily dwelling, perhaps we would understand these as the sides. We call the sides of the house those who cleave to Christ. For not without cause, even in daily speech, do we say of someone who perhaps acts badly from the counsels of evil friends; of him we say: 'He has bad sides.' What does it mean, 'He has bad sides'? Evil men cleave to him. So too of another: 'He has good sides: he lives by good counsels.' What is this? He is governed by good counsels. So the sides of the house are those who cleave to Christ. Nor without cause was the spouse herself made from the side[^24]. When the man slept, Eve was made; when Christ died, the Church was made: and she from the side of the man, when the rib was drawn out; and this one from the side of the man, when the side was struck with a lance, and the Sacraments flowed out[^25]. So *your wife is like a fruitful vine.* But in whom? *On the sides of your house.* In others who do not cleave to Christ, it is barren. But neither will I count those in the vine.

**12.** Your sons. Who is the wife, they themselves are also the sons. In these fleshly marriages and unions, the wife is one and the sons are others: in the Church, who is the wife, they themselves are the sons. For the apostles belonged to the Church, and were among the members of the Church. So they were in his spouse, and were the spouse according to the portion which they held among the members. Why, then, is it said of them: *When the bridegroom has departed from them, then the sons of the bridegroom will fast*[^26]? So they are both the spouse and the sons. I say a wonderful thing, my brothers. In the words of the Lord we find that the Church is both his brothers, and his sisters, and his mother. For when his mother and brothers were announced to him standing outside; because they stood outside, they bore a type. What is the type of the mother? The synagogue. What is the type of the fleshly brothers? The Jews standing outside. And the synagogue stands outside. For Mary is among the sides of his house, and his kinsmen coming from the blood relationship of the Virgin Mary, who believed in him, are among the sides of his house; not insofar as they were joined by fleshly blood relationship, but insofar as they heard the word of God and did it. For this the Lord answered and said: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?*[^27] Whence some have tried to say that Christ did not have a mother, because he said: *Who is my mother?* Why? So then Peter and John and James, and the other apostles, did not have fathers on earth? And yet what does he say to them? *Call no one your father on earth; for one is your Father who is in heaven*[^28]. What, then, he was teaching the disciples about a father, this he himself showed about a mother. For the Lord wishes that we set God before earthly blood relationships. Defer to your father, because he is your father; defer to God, because he is God. Your father begot you by lending his flesh; God created you by applying his power. Let not the father be angry when God is set before him; rather let him rejoice that so much is deferred to himself, that one was found who ought to be set before him. So what shall I say? The Lord, what does he say? *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers? And he stretched out his hands toward his disciples, and said: Behold my mother and my brothers.* They were brothers; how were they mother? He added: *And whoever does the will of my Father, he is my brother, and sister, and mother*[^29]. Take it: brother, on account of the male sex which the Church has; sister, on account of the women whom Christ has here among his members: but how mother, except because Christ himself is in Christians, whom Christians the Church daily brings forth through Baptism? So in whom you understand the spouse, in them the mother, in them the sons.

**13.** So let it be said what kind the sons ought to be. What kind? Peaceable. Why peaceable? Because: *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God*[^30]. Because, then, in the olive there is the fruit of peace: for oil signifies peace, because it signifies love; without love there is no peace: and it is plain that those who divided peace did not have love. Whence I have already expounded to your charity why the dove brought leaves with fruit to the ark[^31]; to signify that those too who are baptized outside, just as that wood was baptized outside the ark, if they have not leaves only, that is, words only, but have also fruit, which is love, the dove itself carries them back to the ark, and they come to unity. Such, then, the sons ought to be, *around the table* of the Lord, *like a planting of olives*. It is a perfect thing, a great blessedness: now who would not wish to be there? When you see someone who is a blasphemer have a wife, sons, grandsons, and you perhaps not have them, do not be jealous; see that it is fulfilled in you too, but spiritually. Or are you perhaps not among the members? If you are not there, lament that you are neither here nor there. But if you are there, be secure; for even if there and not here, it is more fruitful there than here.

**14.** If, then, we have, why do we have? Because we fear the Lord. *Behold, thus will the man be blessed who fears the Lord.* That is the *man* who is men; and the men themselves are the *man*: because the many are one, because Christ is one.

**15.** [vv. 5-6.] *May the Lord bless you out of Zion*[^32]. For you had begun to take notice: *Behold, thus will the man be blessed who fears the Lord*: now perhaps your eyes were going among those who do not fear the Lord, and were seeing there fruitful wives, sons in abundance going around their father's table; I know not where you were going: *May the Lord bless you*, but *out of Zion.* Do not seek those blessings which are not out of Zion. Did the Lord not bless such men, my brothers? This is a blessing of the Lord: or if it is not from the Lord, who will take a wife, if the Lord does not will it? who could be healthy, if the Lord does not will it? or who could be rich, if the Lord does not will it? He gives those things: but do you not see that he gave them to the cattle too? So that blessing is not out of Zion. *May the Lord bless you out of Zion; and may you see the goods that are of Jerusalem.* For those goods are not of Jerusalem. Do you wish to see that they are not the goods of Jerusalem? It was said to the birds too: *Increase and multiply*[^33]. Do you wish to hold as a great thing what was given to the birds? It was given indeed by the voice of God—who would not know it? But use these goods, if you receive them; and think rather how to nourish those who have been born, than that they be born. For it is not now happiness to have sons, but to have good ones. Labor over nourishing them, if they have been born; but if not born, give thanks to God. Perhaps you will be less anxious, and yet you have not remained barren of that mother. Perhaps through you are born spiritually, of that mother, those who are like new plantings of olives going around the table of the Lord. So may the Lord console you, that *you may see the goods of Jerusalem.* For those goods *are*. Why do they *are*? Because they are eternal. Why do they *are*? Because there is the king: *I am who am*[^34]. But these things are goods, and are not: for they do not stand still; they slip away, they flow. Sons are little ones; you fondle the little ones, the little ones fondle you: do they stand still in that? But you wish that they grow, you wish that age be added. But see that when one is added, another dies. When boyhood comes, infancy dies; when youth comes, boyhood dies; when manhood comes, youth dies; when old age comes, manhood dies; when death comes, every age dies. As many stages of age as you wish, so many deaths of ages you wish at once. So these things are not. Then are sons born to you on earth to live with you, or rather to shut you out and to succeed you? Do you rejoice over those who are born, that you may be shut out? For the boys, once born, say this, as it were, to their parents: Come, think of going hence, let us too perform our mime. For the whole life of temptation of the human race is a mime; because it was said: *Every living man is utter vanity*[^35]. And yet, if there is rejoicing over sons who will succeed; how much should there be rejoicing over sons with whom we will abide, and over that father to whom we are born, who will not die, but that we may live forever with him? These are the goods of Jerusalem, because they *are*. *May the Lord*, then, *bless you out of Zion*; *and may you see the goods that are of Jerusalem.* For those goods which you attend to, you see as a blind man. *May you see*, but those goods which are seen by the heart. And how long shall I see the goods of Jerusalem? *All the days of your life.* But if your life be eternal, you will see the goods of Jerusalem forever. But if, my brothers, these are goods, you do not see them all the days of your life: for when you go out of the body, you do not die. Your life perseveres: the body dies, but the life of the spirit perseveres. The eyes do not see, because he has departed who through the eyes was seeing. Wherever is he who through the eyes was seeing, he sees something. For that rich man was not dead who on earth was clothed in purple and fine linen; if he were dead, he would not be tormented among the dead. It would perhaps have been desirable for him to die, but to his own harm he lived among the dead. For he was tormented, and did not see those goods which he had left on earth: behold, his life was such, and he did not see those goods[^36]. So then desire such goods as you may see *all the days of your life*, that is, that you may live forever with those goods.

**16.** What, then, are those goods, brothers, take notice. Can these be called goods: there is gold, there is silver, there is a pleasant estate, there are marbled walls, there are paneled ceilings? Far from it. These the poor have more abundantly in this life. For it is more for a poor man to see the starry sky than for a rich man to see a gilded ceiling. Brothers, then, what is that good with which we are kindled, for which we sigh, with which we are inflamed, for the sake of obtaining and seeing which good we sustain such great labors—as you heard when the apostle was read, that *all who wish to live devoutly in Christ Jesus suffer persecution*[^37]? For it is not the case that, because the devil does not now rage through kings, Christians therefore do not suffer persecution. If the devil is dead, the persecutions are dead; but if that adversary of ours lives, whence does he not suggest temptations? whence does he not rage? whence does he not procure threats or scandals? Oh, if you begin to live devoutly, you will see that everyone who wishes to live devoutly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution. For the sake of what, then, do we suffer such great persecutions? *For if in this life only we have hope in Christ*, says the apostle, *we are more wretched than all men*[^38]. For the sake of what were the martyrs condemned to the beasts? what is that good, can it be told? whence, or what tongue may tell it? or what ears may hear it? And indeed, *neither has the ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^39]. Let us only love, let us only make progress: for you see that battles are not lacking, and we battle with our own desires. We battle outside with unbelieving and disobedient men; we battle within with suggestions, with fleshly disturbances: everywhere we still battle, because the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul[^40]; we still battle, since *if the spirit is life*, yet *the body is dead on account of sin*. But what is to come? *But if the Spirit of him who raised Christ from the dead dwells in you, he will give life also to your mortal bodies, on account of his Spirit who dwells in you*[^41]. So when our mortal members have been given life, then nothing will resist our spirit. There will be no hunger, there will be no thirst, because these arise from the corruption of the body. You refresh yourself because something fails from you. The desires of fleshly delights battle against us. We carry death in the weakness of the body: but when death itself has been turned into that unchangeableness, and this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality, what will death then hear? *Where, O death, is your striving? where, O death, is your sting?*[^42] And perhaps it dies, and it will be said: Do any enemies remain? No; it follows: *the last enemy, death*. When this has been destroyed, immortality will succeed. If there will be no enemy, death will be destroyed as it were last[^43]. Our good, for which we sigh, will be peace. Behold the good, brothers: a great good is called peace. You were asking what it was called: is it gold, is it silver, or an estate, or a garment? It is peace: not such peace as men have among themselves, faithless, unstable, changeable, uncertain; nor such peace as one single man has within himself. For we said that a man battles even with himself; until he tames all his desires, he still battles. What kind of peace, then? Which *the eye has not seen, nor the ear heard*[^44]. What kind of peace? Of Jerusalem, because Jerusalem is interpreted 'vision of peace.' So then *may the Lord bless you out of Zion; and may you see the goods that are of Jerusalem, and may you see them all the days of your life.* *And may you see*, not your sons only, but *the sons of your sons*[^45]. What does it mean, your sons? Your works which you do here. Who are the sons of your sons? The fruits of your works. You do alms, they are your sons: on account of the alms you receive eternal life, they are the sons of your sons. *May you see the sons of your sons*; and there will be what follows, with which it is concluded: *Peace upon Israel.* This peace is preached to you by us, it is loved by us, and we desire that it be loved by you. To that peace come those who have here too been peaceable. Those are peaceable here too, who are so there too; who go around the table of the Lord like a new planting of olives, that the tree may not be barren, such as that fig tree was where the hungry Lord found no fruit[^46]. And you see what happened to it. It had leaves only, it had no fruit: such are those who have words, and do not have deeds. The Lord, coming hungry, found there nothing to eat; because the Lord hungers for our faith and good works. Let us feed him by living well, and he will feed us by granting that we live forever.
