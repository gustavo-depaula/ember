# On the Same Psalm 70

**1.** That the grace of God, by which we have been saved freely, with no merits of ours going before, except those for which punishment was owed, is commended in this psalm, yesterday we conveyed to Your Charity: and since by treating it we could not finish it, we deferred its last part to today, promising in the name of the Lord that we would pay the debt. And since it is time to pay it, be present in mind, like a fertile field, where you both multiply the seed, and are not ungrateful for the rains. We commended yesterday its title; but to renew your attention, and to make it known to those who were absent yesterday, we briefly recall what those may recollect who heard it, and those may hear who did not know it. It is the Psalm of the sons of Jonadab; which name is interpreted "The Lord's Voluntary One": since God is to be served with a voluntary, that is, a good, pure, sincere, and perfect will, not with a feigned heart; which also that indicates where it was said: *Willingly I will sacrifice to you*[^1]. To the sons of this one, that is, to the sons of obedience, the Psalm is sung, and to those who were first led into captivity, that this groaning of ours may be recognized, and the day's own malice suffice for it. For now, if proud we have forsaken, or wearied let us return. Nor can one return except through grace. Grace is given freely: for unless it were free, it would not be grace. Further, if it is grace for this reason, that it is free, nothing of yours went before, that you should receive. For if any good works of yours went before, you received a price, not freely: but the price that was owed us is punishment. That we are freed, then, is not from our merits, but from his grace. Let us praise him, then; let us owe him the whole of what we are, and that we are made safe. To which he concluded, when he had said many things, saying: *O Lord, I will remember your righteousness alone.* At this verse yesterday's treatment was concluded. The first captives, then, that is, those pertaining to the first man: for this reason captives, on account of the first man in whom we all die; because *not first that which is spiritual, but that which is animal, afterward the spiritual*[^2]. On account of the first man the first captives: on account of the second man the second redeemed. For the very redemption cries out our captivity. For whence redeemed, if not first captive? And that captivity, more expressly conveyed by the Apostle, mentioning certain words from his Epistle, we conveyed, and repeat: *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^3]. That is our first captivity, by which the flesh lusts against the spirit. But this came from the punishment of sin, that man should be divided against himself, who was unwilling to be subject to the One. For nothing is so expedient for the soul as to obey. And if it is expedient for the soul to obey, in a servant that he obey his master, in a son that he obey his father, in a wife that she obey her husband; how much more in a man that he obey God? Adam, then, experienced evil: but every man is Adam; as in those who believed every man is Christ, because they are the members of Christ: he experienced, then, the evil which he ought not to have experienced, if he believed him saying: *Do not touch*[^4]; having experienced the evil, let him afterward at least obey the precepts of the physician that he may rise, who was unwilling to believe the physician that he might not be sick. For to a healthy man too a good and faithful physician gives a precept, lest he become necessary to him. For the healthy do not need a physician, but those who are ill. But the good physicians who are friends, who are unwilling to sell their craft, and rejoice more over the healthy than over the sick, give certain precepts to the healthy, by observing which they may not fall into sickness. But further, if they despise the precepts, and fall into sickness, they beseech the physician: whom healthy they despised, sick they ask. Would that they at least asked! lest, the mind lost through fever, they even strike the physician. You heard just now, when the Gospel was read, how the parable was spoken against them. Were they of sound mind who said: *This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*[^5]? Surely not: suppose those who killed the son, were also going to kill the father; this is not to have a sound mind. Finally, behold, because they killed the son: the son rose again, and the stone reprobated by the builders became the head of the corner. They stumbled at him, and were shattered: he will come upon them, and crush them. But not so this one who sings in the Psalm, and says: *I will enter into the power of the Lord;* not mine, but *the Lord's. O Lord, I will remember your righteousness alone.* I recognize none of mine; of your righteousness alone I will remember. From you I have whatever good I have; but whatever I have from myself, I have of evil. You did not render punishment for my merits; but gave grace freely: *Of your* therefore *righteousness alone I will remember.*

**2.** [v. 17] *O God, you have taught me from my youth.*[^6] What did you teach me? That I ought to remember your righteousness alone. For considering my past life, I see what was owed me, and what I have received for that which was owed me. Punishment was owed; grace was rendered: gehenna was owed; eternal life was given. *O God, you have taught me from my youth.* From the very beginning of my faith, by which you renewed me, you taught me that nothing in me went before, whence I might say that what you gave was owed me. For who is converted to God, except from iniquity? who is redeemed, except from captivity? But who can say that his captivity was unjust, when he forsook the Emperor, and deserted to the deserter? For the Emperor is God, the deserter the devil: the Emperor gave a precept, the deserter suggested a fraud. Where were your ears between the precept and the deception? Is the devil better than God? is he who deserted better than he who made you? You believed what the devil promised, and found what God threatened. Now, then, freed from captivity, yet still in hope, not yet in reality, walking by faith, not yet by vision: *O God,* he says, *you have taught me from my youth.* From when I was converted to you, renewed by you who was made by you, recreated who was created, reformed who was formed: from when I was converted, I learned that my merits went nowise before; but that your grace came to me freely, that I might remember your righteousness alone.

**3.** What then, after the youth? since *you have taught me,* he says, *from my youth;* what after the youth? For in that very first conversion of yours you learned that before conversion you were not just, but iniquity went before, that, iniquity being expelled, love might succeed; and, renewed into the new man, in hope only, not yet in reality, you learned that nothing of your good went before, and by the grace of God you were converted to God. Now perhaps from when you were converted, you will have something of your own, and ought to presume on your own powers? As men are wont to say: Now let go; it was needful that you show the way, it is enough, I will walk the way. And he who showed you the way: Perhaps you wish me to lead you? But you, if you are proud: Far be it, it is enough, I will walk the way. You are let go, and through weakness you will again stray. It was good for you that he lead you, who first set you on the way. But unless he himself also lead, you will again err: say to him, then: *Lead me, O Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth*[^7]. But that you have entered the way is the youth, that very renewal, and the beginning of faith. For before you walked by your own ways wandering; erring through woody and rough places, torn in all your limbs: you sought the homeland, that is, a certain stability of your spirit, where you might say: It is well; and say it secure, at rest from all trouble, from all temptation, from all captivity finally; and you did not find it. What shall I say? Did one come to you who would show you the way? The way itself came to you, and you were set on it with no merits of yours going before, since certainly you were erring. What, from when you entered it, do you now rule yourself? does he who taught you the way now let you go? No, he says: *You have taught me from my youth; and until now I will announce your wonders.* For it is a wonder that you still do, that you rule me, who set me on the way; and these are your wonders. What, do you think, are the wonders of God? What more wonderful among the wonders of God, than to raise the dead? Surely I am dead? you say. If you were not dead, it would not be said to you: *Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead; and Christ will enlighten you*[^8]. All the unbelievers are dead, all the unjust; they live in body, but in heart they are extinct. But he who raises a dead man according to the body, restores him to this light to be seen, and to this air to be breathed: but his raiser is not himself to him the light and the air; he begins to see, as he saw before. The soul is not so raised. For the soul is raised by God; although the body too is raised by God: but God, when he raises the body, restores it to the world; when he raises the soul, restores it to himself. If the air of this world withdraws, the body dies; if God withdraws, the soul dies. When, then, God raises a soul, unless he be present who raised it, the one raised does not live. For he does not raise it, and let it go to live to itself: as Lazarus, when he was raised, four days dead, was raised in body through the Lord's bodily presence. For he drew near bodily to the tomb; he cried out: *Lazarus, come forth*: and Lazarus rose, came forth from the tomb bound; then, loosed, he departed. He was raised with the Lord present, but lived also with the Lord absent. Although the Lord had raised him bodily, as far as concerns visible things: yet by the presence of his majesty he raised him; by which presence he nowhere withdraws. Meanwhile yet to the visible presence the Lord raised Lazarus; the Lord departed from that very city, or from that place: surely Lazarus did not not live? Not so is the soul raised: God raises it; it dies if God withdraws. For I will say boldly, brethren, but yet truly. There are two lives, one of the body, the other of the soul: as the life of the body is the soul, so the life of the soul is God; as, if the soul forsakes it, the body dies; so the soul dies, if God forsakes it. This, then, is his grace, that he raise us, and be with us. That, then, he raises us from our past death, and in a manner renews our life, we say to him: *O God, you have taught me from my youth.* But because he does not withdraw from those whom he raises, lest, when he has withdrawn, they die, we say to him: *And until now I will announce your wonders;* because, when you are with me, I live, and you are the life of my soul, which will die if it be let go to itself. Therefore, as long as my life is present, that is, my God, *until now*: what afterward?

**4.** [v. 18] *And even unto old age and gray years.*[^9] These two are names of old age, and are distinguished by the Greeks. For gravity after youth has one name among the Greeks, and the last age coming after that very gravity has another name: for it is called grave, and aged. But because in the Latin tongue the distinction of these two names is lacking, both are set down for old age, *senecta* and *senium* (old age and gray years): but you know that there are two ages. *You have taught me* your grace *from my youth; and until now,* after my youth, *I will announce your wonders,* because you are with me that I may not die, who came that I might rise; *and even unto old age and gray years,* that is, even unto my end, unless you are with me, there will be nothing of my merit: let your grace always persevere with me. This too one man might say, you, that one, I: but because this voice is the voice of a certain great man, that is, of that very unity; for it is the voice of the Church; let us seek the youth of the Church. When Christ came, was crucified, died, rose again, called the nations; they began to be converted, the strong martyrs in Christ were made, the faithful blood was poured out, the crop of the Church rose up: this is the youth. But as the times advance, let the Church confess; let it say: *Until now I will announce your wonders.* Not only in the youth, when Paul, when Peter, when the first Apostles announced; but as the age also advances, I myself, that is, your unity, your members, your body, *will announce your wonders.* What afterward? *And even unto old age and gray years* I will announce your wonders: unto the end of the age the Church will be here. For if it is not to be here unto the end of the age; to whom did the Lord say: *Behold, I am with you all the days, unto the consummation of the age*[^10]? Why was it needful that these things be said in the Scriptures? Because there were going to be enemies of the Christian faith who would say: For a short time are the Christians, afterward they will perish, and the idols will return, and what was before will return. How long will the Christians be? *Even unto old age and gray years;* that is, unto the end of the age. You, while you wait, O wretched unbeliever, for the Christians to pass away, pass away yourself without the Christians: and the Christians will remain unto the end of the age; and you, through your unbelief, when you have ended your brief life, with what face will you go out to the judge whom, while you lived, you blasphemed? Therefore *from my youth, and until now, and even unto old age and gray years, O Lord, do not forsake me.* Not, as my enemies say, will it be unto a certain time. *Do not forsake me, until I announce your arm to every coming generation.* And the arm of the Lord, to whom has it been revealed? The arm of the Lord is Christ. Do not, then, forsake me; let not those rejoice who say: Unto a certain time will the Christians be. Let there be those who announce your arm. To whom? *To every coming generation.* If, then, to every coming generation; unto the end of the age: for the age being ended, no generation will come after.

**5.** [v. 19] *Your power, and your righteousness*[^11]: that is, that I may announce to every coming generation your arm. And what did your arm furnish? Our free liberation. This, then, may I announce, that very grace to every coming generation; may I say to every man to be born: You are nothing through yourself, call upon God; your sins are yours, the merits are God's; punishment is owed you, and when the reward comes, he will crown his own gifts, not your merits. May I say to every coming generation: You came from captivity; you pertained to Adam. May I say this to every coming generation, no powers of mine, no righteousness of mine; but *your power and your righteousness, O God, even unto the highest great things which you have done. Your power and your righteousness,* how far? even unto flesh and blood? Nay rather, *even unto the highest great things which you have done.* In the heights are the heavens; in the heights are the Angels, the Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, Powers: they owe you that they are, they owe you that they live, they owe you that they live justly, they owe you that they live blessedly: *Your power and your righteousness,* how far? *Even unto the highest great things which you have done.* Do not think that man alone pertains to the grace of God. What was the Angel before he was made? what is the Angel, if he forsakes him who created him? Therefore, *your power and your righteousness, even unto the highest great things which you have done.*

**6.** And man exalts himself! and that he may pertain to the first captivity, he hears the serpent suggesting: *Taste, and you will be like gods*[^12]! Men like gods! *O God, who is like you?* Not in the abyss, not in hell, not on earth, not in heaven; for you made all things. Why does the work contend with the craftsman? *O God, who is like you?* But I, says wretched Adam, and in Adam every man, when I wish to be perversely like you, behold what I have become, that from captivity I cry to you: I, for whom it was well under a good king, have been made captive under my seducer; and I cry to you, because I fell away from you. And whence did I fall away from you? When I seek to be perversely like you. For what? Does not God call us to his likeness? Is he not himself who says: *Love your enemies; pray for those who persecute you; do good to those who hate you*? Saying these things, he exhorts us to the likeness of God. Finally, what does he add? *That you may be,* he says, *sons of your Father who is in heaven.* For what does he himself do? This he certainly does: *Who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^13]. He, then, who wishes well to his enemy, is like God: nor is this pride, but obedience. Why? Because we were made in the image of God: *Let us make,* he says, *man in our image and likeness*[^14]. It is not, then, something alien, if we hold the image of God in us: would that we lose it not through pride! But what is it, through pride to wish to be like God? what do we think, that the captive should cry out: *O Lord, who is like you?* what is that perverse likeness? Hear, and understand, if you can: but we believe that he who set us to say these things to you, will give you also to be able to understand. God needs no good, and he himself is the highest good, and from him is every good. That we, then, may be good, we need God: that God be good, he does not need us, nor us only, but *even unto the highest great things which he made,* neither the heavenly beings themselves, nor the supercelestial, nor the heaven of heaven as it is called, does God need, that he be either better, or more powerful, or more blessed. For what would whatever else there is be, unless he himself had made it? What, then, does he who was before you need from you, and was so powerful, that, when you were not, he made you? Surely not as parents make sons? By a certain carnal concupiscence they beget rather than create; for, while these beget, God creates. For if you so create, say what your wife is going to bear. Why do I say: You, say? let her say, who does not know what she carries. Yet men beget sons, both for their solace, and for the support of their old age. Did God therefore create all these things, by which an old man might be helped? God, then, knows what he creates, and what sort it is by his goodness, and what sort it will be by its own will; God knew and ordained all things. But that man may be something, he turns himself to him by whom he was created. For by withdrawing he grows cold; by drawing near he grows warm: by withdrawing he grows dark; by drawing near he grows bright. For from him from whom he has being, with him he has being well. Finally the younger son, who wished to have his substance in his own power, which was kept best for him with his father, made his own master, set out into a far country, clung to an evil prince, fed swine; he was corrected by hunger who had departed proud through fullness. Therefore whoever so wishes to be like God, that he stand toward him, and keep his strength, as it is written, toward him, not withdraw from him: by cleaving to him let him be stamped as wax from a ring, fixed to him let him have his image, doing what was said: *It is good for me to cleave to God*[^15]; he truly keeps the likeness and image to which he was made. But further, if he perversely wish to imitate God, that, as God has no one by whom he is formed, has no one by whom he is ruled, so he himself wish to use his own power, that, as God, with no one forming, no one ruling, he may live; what remains, brethren, except that, withdrawing from his warmth, he grow numb, withdrawing from the truth he grow empty, withdrawing from that which supremely and unchangeably is, changed for the worse, he fail.

**7.** This the devil did: he wished to imitate God, but perversely; not to be under his power, but to have power against him. But man, set under a precept, heard from the Lord God: *Do not touch.* What? This tree. For what is that tree? If it is good, why do I not touch it? if it is evil, what does it do in paradise? Plainly therefore it is in paradise, because it is good; but I do not wish you to touch it. Why do I not touch it? Because I wish you obedient, not gainsaying. Serve to this end, O servant; but do not serve badly, O servant. O servant, hear first the Lord's command, and then learn the counsel of the commander. The tree is good: I do not wish you to touch it. Why? Because I am the Lord, and you are a servant. This is the whole cause. If it is small, do you disdain to be a servant? But what is expedient for you, except to be under the Lord? How will you be under the Lord, unless you are under a precept? Further, if it is expedient for you to be under the Lord, and under a precept, what was God going to command you? For does he seek anything from you? Offer me a sacrifice, is he going to say to you? Did he not himself make all things, in which he made you too? Obey me, is he going to say to you; whether to my bed, when I rest; or to my table, when I refresh myself; or to my bath, when I wash? Because, then, God needs nothing from you, ought he to have commanded you nothing? But if he ought to command something, that you, what is expedient for you, might feel yourself to be under the Lord, you were to be forbidden from some thing; not on account of the malice of that tree, but on account of your obedience. God could not more perfectly show how great a good obedience is, than when he forbade from that thing which was not evil. There obedience alone holds the palm; there disobedience alone finds punishment. It is good; I do not wish you to touch it. For it is not by not touching that you will die. Surely he who forbade from this, withdrew other things? Is not paradise full of fruitful trees? What is lacking to you? This I do not wish you to touch; from this I do not wish you to taste. It is good, but obedience is better. Accordingly, when you have touched it, surely that tree will not be evil, so that you die? But disobedience subjected you to death, because you touched the forbidden. Therefore that tree was called the tree of the knowledge of discerning good and evil, not because such fruits hung from it; but whatever that tree was, of whatever fruit, of whatever produce it was, it was so called for this reason, because the man who was unwilling to discern good from evil by the precept, would discern it by experience; so that by touching the forbidden, he would find punishment. But why did he touch it, my brethren? What was lacking to him? Let it be told me what was lacking to him, set in paradise, in the midst of opulence, and in the midst of delights; to whom the very vision of God was a great delight, whose face he feared as it were an enemy's after sin? What was lacking to him, that he touched it, except that he wished to use his own power, it delighted him to break the precept; that, with no one lording over him, he might become like God, since over God certainly no one lords? Wretchedly wandering, wretchedly presuming, going to be dead, by withdrawing from the way of righteousness! behold, he loosed the precept, shook off from his neck the yoke of discipline, broke the reins of rule with exalting spirit; where is he now? Surely, a captive, he cries out: *O Lord, who is like you?* I wished to be perversely like you, and I have been made like a beast. Under your dominion, under your precept I was truly like you: but man, set in honor, did not understand; he was compared to the senseless beasts, and made like them. Now from the likeness of beasts cry out late, and say: *O God, who is like you?*

**8.** [v. 20] *How great tribulations, many and evil, you have shown me!*[^16] Deservedly, O proud servant: for you wished to be perversely like your Lord, who had been made in the image of your Lord. Would you wish it to be well with you, withdrawing from that good? Plainly God says to you: If you withdraw from me, and it is well with you, I am not your good. Accordingly, if he is good, and supremely good, and good to himself of himself, and good with no alien good, and himself our highest good; by withdrawing from there, what will you be, except evil? Likewise, if he himself is our blessedness, what will there be for one withdrawing, except misery? Return, then, after misery, and say: *O Lord, who is like you? How great tribulations, many and evil, you have shown me!*

**9.** [vv. 20-21] But it was discipline; admonition, not desertion. Finally, giving thanks, what does he say? *And being turned back you gave me life, and from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.*[^17] For when before? what is this, *again*? You fell from the height, O man, disobedient bondservant, proud against the Lord, you fell! There came about in you: *Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled*: let there come about in you: *Everyone who humbles himself will be exalted*[^18]. Return from the depth. I return, he says; I return, I acknowledge: *O God, who is like you? How great tribulations, many and evil, you have shown me! And being turned back you gave me life, and from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.* We understand, I hear. For you brought me back from the abysses of the earth; you brought me back from the depth and drowning of sin. But why, *again*? When was it already done? Let us follow on, lest perhaps the later parts of this very psalm expound to us what here we do not yet understand, what he meant by *again.* Therefore let us hear: *How great tribulations, many and evil, you have shown me! and being turned back you gave me life, and from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.* What then? *You have multiplied your righteousness; and being turned back you have consoled me, and from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.* Behold another *again.* If we labor to solve this *again* set down once, who will be able to solve it doubled? For now this very *again* is a doubling, and again is set down *again.* Let him be present from whom is grace: let the arm be present too which we announce to every coming generation: let him himself be present, and as with the key of his cross open the closed sacrament. For not in vain, when he was crucified, was the veil of the temple rent in the middle, except because through his suffering the secrets of all the mysteries were laid open. Let him himself, then, be present to those passing over to him; let the veil be taken away: let our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ say to us, why such a voice of the Prophet was set before: *You have shown me tribulations many and evil; and being turned back you gave me life, and from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.* Behold, here this *again* is set down first: let us see what this is, and we shall find why the other *again.*

**10.** What is Christ? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^19]. Great this! magnificent this! You, what, O captive? where do you lie? In the flesh, under death. Who, then, is he? who are you? and what is he afterward? For whose sake? Who is he, except what was said, the *Word*? What Word? does it perhaps sound and pass away? The Word, God with God; the Word through which all things were made. What for your sake? *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^20]. *He who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all; how has he not with him given us all things too*[^21]? Behold what, who, for whose sake. The Son of God *flesh* for the sake of the sinner, for the sake of the unjust, for the sake of the deserter, for the sake of the proud, for the sake of the perverse imitator of his God! He made what you are, son of man, that we might be made sons of God! Made flesh: whence flesh? From the virgin Mary. Whence the virgin Mary? From Adam. Therefore from that first captive; and flesh in Christ from the mass of captivity. To what end this? For an example. He took from you that in which he might die for you; he took from you that which he might offer for you, by which example he might teach you. What would he teach you? That you are going to rise again. For whence would you believe, unless the example of flesh taken from the mass of your death went before? Therefore in him first we rose again; because when Christ rose, we rose. For the Word did not die, and rise again; but in the Word the flesh died, and rose again. There Christ died, where you are going to die; and there Christ rose again, where you are going to rise. By his example he taught what you should not fear, what you should hope. You feared death; he died: you despaired of resurrection; he rose again. But you say to me: He rose again; surely I? But he rose again in that which he took from you for your sake. Therefore your nature went before you in him; and what was taken from you, ascended before you: there, then, you too ascended. He ascended, then, first, and in him we; because that flesh is of the human race. Therefore, with him rising too, we were brought back from the abysses of the earth. So, when Christ rose, *from the abysses of the earth you brought me back.* But when we believe in Christ, *from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.* Behold one *again.* Hear it fulfilled by the Apostle: *If, then, you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; relish the things that are above, not the things upon the earth*[^22]. He went before, then; now we too have risen, but still in hope. Hear this same apostle Paul saying: *And we ourselves groan within ourselves, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body.* You still groan, you still await. What, then, is it that Christ furnished you? Hear what follows: *For by hope we have been saved; but hope which is seen is not hope. For what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^23]. We were brought back, then, *again* from the abysses by hope. Why *again*? Because Christ had already gone before. But because we shall rise in reality: for now we live by hope, now we walk according to faith: we were brought back from the abysses of the earth, by believing in him who rose before us from the abysses of the earth; our soul was raised up from the iniquity of unbelief, and there was made in us as it were a first resurrection through faith. But if it will be the only one, where is what the Apostle says: *Awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*? where is what he said in that place: *The body is dead because of sin; the spirit is life because of righteousness. But if he who raised Christ from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Jesus Christ from the dead will give life also to your mortal bodies, through his Spirit dwelling in you*[^24]? Therefore we have already risen in mind, faith, hope, love; but it remains that we rise in body. You have heard one *again;* you have heard another *again*: one *again,* on account of Christ going before; and another, but still in hope, which remains in reality. *You have multiplied your righteousness*: now in believers, now in those who first rose in hope. *You have multiplied your righteousness.* To this very righteousness pertains also the scourge; *because it is time that judgment begin from the house of God*[^25], says Peter; that is, from his saints. For he scourges every son whom he receives. *You have multiplied your righteousness*: because you did not spare even the sons; but those for whom you kept the eternal inheritance, you did not forsake by discipline. *You have multiplied your righteousness; and being turned back you have consoled me*: and on account of the body to rise again in the end, *and from the abysses of the earth again you brought me back.*

**11.** [v. 22] *For I too will confess to you your truth on the instruments of the psaltery.*[^26] The instruments of the psaltery, the psaltery. But what is the psaltery? A wooden instrument with strings. What does it signify? There is some difference between it and the harp: those who know say there is a difference, in that that hollow wood over which the strings are stretched out that they may resound, the psaltery has in the upper part, the harp in the lower. And because the spirit is from above, the flesh from earth, the spirit seems to be signified by the psaltery, the flesh by the harp. And because he had spoken of two bringings-back of ours from the abysses of the earth; one according to the spirit in hope, the other according to the body in reality; hear these two: *For I too will confess to you your truth on the instruments of the psaltery.* This according to the spirit: what of the body? *I will play to you on the harp, O Holy One of Israel.*

**12.** [vv. 23-24] Hear this again, on account of that very *again* and *again. My lips will exult, when I play to you.*[^27] Because the lips are wont to be spoken of both of the inner man and of the outer, it is uncertain how the lips are set down: there follows, then: *And my soul which you have redeemed.* Therefore, of the inner lips, saved in hope, brought back from the abysses of the earth in faith and love, yet *awaiting* still *the redemption of our body,* what do we say? He already said: *And my soul which you have redeemed.* But lest you think the soul alone redeemed, in which now you heard one *again*: *But still,* he says: what still? *But still even my tongue*: therefore now the tongue of the body *will meditate your righteousness all the day long*: that is, in eternity without end. But when this? Now in the end of the age by the resurrection of the body, and the change into the angelic state. Whence is this proved, that it is said of the end: *But still even my tongue will meditate your righteousness all the day long*? *When those who seek evils for me have been confounded, and put to shame.*[^28] When will they be confounded, when will they be put to shame, except in the end of the age? For in two ways they will be confounded; either when they believe in Christ, or when Christ comes. For as long as the Church is here; as long as the wheat groans among the chaff, as long as the ears groan among the tares, as long as the vessels of mercy groan among the vessels of wrath made unto dishonor, as long as the lily groans among the thorns, there will not be lacking enemies who say: *When will he die, and his name perish*[^29]; that is: Behold, the time will come that they be ended, and there be no Christians: as they began at some time, so unto a certain time they will be. But when they say these things; both they die without end, and the Church remains, preaching the arm of the Lord to every coming generation. He himself too will come at last in his brightness; all the dead will rise, each with his own cause: the good will be separated to the right, but the evil to the left; and those who insulted will be confounded, those who chattered will be put to shame: and so my tongue after the resurrection will meditate your righteousness, all the day long your praise, *when those who seek evils for me have been confounded, and put to shame.*
