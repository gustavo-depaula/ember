# Exposition of Psalm 9

**1.** [v 1.] The inscription of this psalm is: *To the end, for the hidden things of the Son, a psalm of David himself.* Concerning the hidden things of the Son it can be asked: but since he did not add whose, the only-begotten Son of God himself is to be understood. For where a psalm is inscribed concerning the son of David, it says: *When he fled from the face of Absalom his son*[^1]; when his name had been stated, and on this account it could not be hidden of whom it spoke, yet it was not said only, from the face of Absalom the son, but there was added, *his.* But here, both because *his* is not added, and because he says many things about the Gentiles, Absalom cannot rightly be taken: for the war which that lost man waged with his father in no way pertains to the Gentiles, since there only the people of Israel was divided against itself. And so this psalm is sung for the hidden things of the only-begotten Son of God. For the Lord himself too, when he puts Son without addition, wishes himself, the only-begotten, to be understood, where he says: *If the Son sets you free, then you will be truly free*[^2]. For he did not say, the Son of God; but only by saying *Son*, he gives to understand whose son he is. Which expression admits nothing but the excellence of him of whom we so speak, that even if we do not name him, he can be understood: for so we say: It rains, it clears, it thunders, and such things, and we do not add who does it; because to the minds of all the excellence of the one doing it offers itself spontaneously, and desires no words. What, then, are the hidden things of the Son? In which word it is first to be understood that there are some manifest things of the Son, from which these are distinguished which are called hidden. For which reason, since we believe in two comings of the Lord, one past, which the Jews did not understand, the other future, which both hope for; and since this one which the Jews did not understand profited the Gentiles, it is not unfittingly taken that it is said of this coming, *for the hidden things of the Son*, where blindness came in part upon Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in. Two judgments too are suggested through the Scriptures, if one observes; one hidden, the other manifest. The hidden is now carried out, of which the apostle Peter says: *It is time that judgment begin from the house of the Lord*[^3]. The hidden judgment, then, is the punishment by which now each one of men is either exercised toward purification, or admonished toward conversion, or, if he despises the calling and discipline of God, is blinded toward damnation. But the manifest judgment is that by which the Lord, when he comes, will judge the living and the dead, all confessing him to be the one by whom rewards will be given to the good, and punishments to the evil. But then that confession will avail not for the remedy of evils, but for the heaping up of damnation. Of these two judgments, one hidden, the other manifest, the Lord seems to me to have spoken, where he says: *He who believes in me has passed from death to life, and will not come into judgment*[^4], into the manifest judgment, namely: for that he passes from death to life through some affliction, by which he scourges every son whom he receives, is the hidden judgment; *But he who does not believe*, he says, *is already judged*[^5]; that is, by this hidden judgment he is already prepared for that manifest one. These two judgments we read also in Wisdom, where it is written: *For this, as to senseless children, you gave judgment for derision; but they, not corrected by this judgment, experienced the worthy judgment of God*[^6]. Those, then, who are not corrected by this hidden judgment of God will most deservedly be punished by that manifest one. Wherefore in this psalm the hidden things of the Son are to be observed, that is, both his humble coming by which he profited the Gentiles with the blindness of the Jews, and the punishment which is now carried out in hidden manner, not yet by the damnation of sinners, but either by the exercise of the converted, or by the admonition that they may be converted, or by the blinding that those who have refused to be converted may be prepared for damnation.

**2.** [v 2.] *I will confess to you, Lord, with my whole heart.* He does not confess to God with his whole heart who doubts in anything about his providence: but he who now discerns the hidden things of God's wisdom, how great is the invisible reward of him who says: *We rejoice in tribulations*[^7]; and how all the torments which are inflicted bodily either exercise those converted to God, or admonish that they be converted, or finally prepare the hardened for just damnation, and so all things are referred to the governance of divine providence, which fools think happen as it were by chance and at random and by no divine administration. *I will tell of all your wonders.* He tells of all God's wonders who sees them done not only openly in bodies, but in souls invisibly indeed, yet far more sublimely and excellently. For earthly men, given over to hidden things, marvel more that the dead Lazarus rose again in the body than that the persecutor Paul rose again in the soul. But since the visible miracle calls the soul to illumination, and the invisible illumines the soul that, called, has come; he tells of all God's wonders who, believing visible things, makes a passage to the understanding of invisible things.

**3.** [v 3.] *I will be glad and exult in you.* No longer in this world; not in the pleasure of the touching of bodies, nor in the flavors of palate and tongue, nor in the sweetness of odors, nor in the pleasantness of passing sounds, nor in the variously colored forms of bodies, nor in the vanities of human praise, nor in marriage and offspring that will die, nor in the superfluities of temporal riches, nor in the acquisition of this world, whether that which is stretched out over spaces of places, or that which rolls on by the succession of time: but *I will be glad and exult in you*, namely in the hidden things of the Son, where the light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord; for *You will hide them*, he says, *in the secret of your countenance*[^8]. He, then, will be glad and exult in you who tells of all your wonders. And he will tell of all your wonders—since now it is said by prophecy—who comes not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him.

**4.** [v 4.] For now the person of the Lord begins to appear speaking in this psalm. For there follows: *I will sing to your name, Most High, when my enemy is turned back.* When, then, was this one's enemy turned back? Was it when it was said to him: *Get back, Satan*[^9]? for then he who by tempting desired to set himself before was made to be behind, by not deceiving the one tempted and prevailing nothing against him. For earthly men are behind; but the heavenly man was made before, although he came after: for the first man, from the earth, earthly; the second man, from heaven, heavenly. But he came from the very stock from which it was said: *He who comes after me was made before me*[^10]. And the Apostle forgets the things that are behind, and stretches forward to the things that are before. The enemy, then, was turned back, after he could not deceive the heavenly man tempted, and turned himself to earthly men where he can dominate. For which reason no man goes before him and makes him to be behind, except one who, laying aside the image of the earthly man, has borne the image of the heavenly. But now if we should prefer to take what was said, *my enemy*, generally as either the sinner or the gentile man, it will not be absurd. Nor will what was said be a punishment: *When my enemy is turned back*; but a benefit, and such a benefit that nothing can be compared to it. For what is more blessed than to lay aside pride, and not to wish to go before Christ, as though one were healthy who has no need of a physician; but to prefer to go behind, after Christ, who, calling a disciple that he may be perfected, says: *Follow me*[^11]? But yet it is more fittingly understood as said of the devil: *When my enemy is turned back.* For the devil is turned back even in the persecution of the righteous, and is a far more useful persecutor than if he went before as leader and prince. We must sing, then, to the name of the Most High when the enemy is turned back; since we ought to prefer to flee him persecuting than to follow him leading: for we have whither to flee and where to hide, in the hidden things of the Son, because the Lord has become a refuge for us.

**5.** [v 5.] *They will be weakened, and will perish before your face.* Who will be weakened and perish, but the unjust and the impious? *They will be weakened*, while they prevail nothing; *and they will perish*, because they will not be impious; *before* the face of God, that is, from the knowledge of God, as he perished who said: *But now I live, no longer I, but Christ lives in me*[^12]. But why will the impious *be weakened and perish before your face? Because you have made my judgment*, he says, *and my cause*: that is, that judgment in which I seemed to be judged, you have made mine; and that cause in which men condemned me, just and innocent, you have made mine. For these things served him for our liberation: just as sailors too call the wind their own, which they use for good sailing.

**6.** *You have sat upon the throne, you who judge equity.* Whether the Son says it to the Father, who also said that: *You would have no power over me, unless it had been given you from above*[^13], referring this very thing to the equity of the Father and to his own hidden things, that he, the judge of men, was judged for the benefit of men: or whether man says to God: *You have sat upon the throne, you who judge equity*, calling his own soul his throne, so that the body is perhaps the earth, which was called the footstool of his feet (for God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself): or whether the soul of the Church, now perfect and without spot or wrinkle, worthy namely of the hidden things of the Son, because the king has brought her into his chamber, says to her bridegroom: *You have sat upon the throne, you who judge equity*, because you rose from the dead, and ascended into heaven, and sit at the right hand of the Father: whichever of these sentences pleases, to which this verse may be referred, it does not exceed the rule of faith.

**7.** [v 6.] *You have rebuked the nations, and the impious one has perished.* We take it that this is more fittingly said of the Lord Jesus Christ than said by him. For who else rebuked the nations, and the impious one perished, but he who, after he ascended into heaven, sent the Holy Spirit, filled with whom the Apostles preached the word of God with confidence, and freely reproved the sins of men? by which rebuke the impious one perished; because the impious one was justified, and made pious. *You have blotted out their name forever, and unto the age of the age.* The name of the impious has been blotted out: for those who believe in the true God are not called impious. But their name is blotted out *forever*, that is, as long as the temporal age rolls on. *And unto the age of the age.* What is *the age of the age*, but that whose effigy and as it were shadow this age has? For the alternation of times succeeding one another, while the moon wanes and again is filled, while the sun every year returns to its place, while spring, or summer, or autumn, or winter so passes that it returns, is a kind of imitation of eternity. But the age of this age is that which stands in unchangeable eternity. As a verse in the mind, and a verse in the voice: the one is understood, the other heard; and the one regulates the other: and therefore the one works in the art and remains, the other sounds in the air and passes. So the manner of this changeable age is defined by that unchangeable age which is called the age of the age: and therefore that remains in the art of God, that is, in his Wisdom and Power; but this is carried out in the administration of the creature. If, that is, it is not a repetition, so that after it was said *forever*, lest this be taken as what passes, there was added *unto the age of the age.* For in the Greek copies it is thus: *eis ton aiōna, kai eis ton aiōna tou aiōnos.* Which most Latins have interpreted, not *forever, and unto the age of the age*, but, *into eternity, and unto the age of the age*: so that in what was said *unto the age of the age*, that might be expounded which was said *into eternity.* *The name*, then, *of the impious you have blotted out into eternity*, because henceforth they will never be impious. And if their name does not extend into this age, much less into the age of the age.

**8.** [v 7.] *The broadswords of the enemy have failed unto the end.* Not in the plural, enemies, but in the singular, of this enemy. And whose enemy's broadswords have failed, but the devil's? Now these are understood as the various opinions of error, by which he, as with swords, slays souls. To overcome these swords and bring them to failure, that sword presses on, of which it is said in the seventh psalm: *Unless you turn back, he will brandish his sword*[^14]. And perhaps this is the end unto which the broadswords of the enemy fail, because up to it they avail something; it now works in hidden manner, but at the last judgment it will be openly brandished. By this the cities are destroyed; for so it follows: *The broadswords of the enemy have failed unto the end; and you have destroyed the cities*: but the cities in which the devil reigns, where deceitful and fraudulent counsels hold the place as it were of a senate-house, to which principality there are present, as it were satellites and ministers, the offices of the several members, the eyes for curiosity, the ears for wantonness, or whatever else is gladly heard for an evil purpose, the hands for robbery or any other crime or outrage, and the rest of the members in this manner serving the tyrannical principality, that is, the perverse counsels. The common people, as it were, of this city are all the delicate affections and turbulent movements of the soul, stirring up daily seditions in man. Where, then, a king, where a senate-house, where ministers, where common people are found, there is a city: for such things would not be in evil cities, unless they were first in individual men, who are as it were the elements and seeds of cities. These cities he destroys, when, the prince being shut out from there, of whom it was said: *The prince of this world has been cast out*[^15], these kingdoms are laid waste by the word of truth, the malign counsels are lulled to sleep, the base affections are tamed, the ministries of the members and senses are taken captive, and transferred to the warfare of righteousness and good works: so that now, as the Apostle says, *sin does not reign in our mortal body*[^16], and the rest of that place. Then the soul is pacified, and man is set in order to seize rest and blessedness. *Their memory has perished with a noise*: namely the impious. But *with a noise*, either because a noise is made, it was said, when impiety is overthrown; for none passes to the highest peace, where there is the highest silence, except one who has first warred with great noise against his own vices: or *with a noise* was said, so that the memory of the impious may perish, that very noise also perishing in which impiety is in tumult.

**9.** [vv 8.9.] *And the Lord remains forever.* Why, then, have the nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things, against the Lord and against his Christ? for *the Lord remains forever. He has prepared his seat in judgment, and he himself will judge the world in equity.* He prepared his seat when he was judged: for by that patience man acquired heaven, and God in man profited believers; and this is the hidden judgment of the Son. But because he is also to come openly and manifestly to judge the living and the dead, he prepared his seat in the hidden judgment; *and he himself* likewise openly *will judge the world in equity*, that is, he will distribute what is worthy to merits, setting the lambs at the right hand, the goats at the left. *He will judge the peoples with justice.* This is what was said above: *He will judge the world in equity*: not as men judge, who do not see hearts, by whom for the most part worse men are absolved than are condemned; but in equity and with justice the Lord will judge, the conscience bearing witness, and the thoughts accusing or defending.

**10.** [v 10.] *And the Lord has become a refuge for the poor.* However much that enemy persecutes, who was turned back, what will he harm those whose refuge the Lord has become? But this will come about, if in this world, of which he is the magistrate, they have chosen to be poor, loving nothing which either deserts the one living or loving here, or is deserted by the one dying; for to such a poor man the Lord has become a refuge: *A helper in opportune times, in tribulation.* So he makes them poor, since he scourges every son whom he receives. For what *a helper in opportune times* is, he explained when he added, *in tribulation*: for the soul is not converted to God, except while it is turned away from this world; nor is it more opportunely turned away from this world, except when to its trifling and harmful and destructive pleasures labors and pains are mixed.

**11.** [v 11.] *And let those who know your name hope in you*: when they have ceased to hope in riches and in the other enticements of this world. For the soul seeking where to fix its hope, when it is torn from this world, is opportunely received by the knowledge of God's name: for the name itself of God is now everywhere widely known; but the knowledge of the name is when he is known whose the name is: for the name is not a name for its own sake, but for the sake of what it signifies. Now it was said: *The Lord is his name*[^17]. For which reason he who gladly subjects himself as a servant to God has known this name. *And let those who know your name hope in you.* Likewise the Lord says to Moses: *I am who I am; and you will say to the sons of Israel: He who is has sent me*[^18]. *Let those*, then, *who know your name hope in you*: let them not hope in those things which pass away with the rolling of time, having nothing but, it will be, and it was; since what in them is future, when it comes, becomes at once past; it is awaited with desire, lost with sorrow. But in the nature of God there will be nothing as though not yet being; or was, as though now not being: but only is that which is, and this is eternity itself. Let them, then, cease to hope for and love temporal things, and betake themselves to eternal hope, who know the name of him who said: *I am who I am*, and of whom it was said: *He who is has sent me. For you have not forsaken those who seek you, Lord.* Those who seek him no longer seek the things that pass away and die: for no one can serve two masters.

**12.** [v 12.] *Sing to the Lord, who dwells in Zion*: this is said to those whom the Lord does not forsake when they seek him. He himself dwells in Zion, which is interpreted "Contemplation," and bears the image of the Church which now is: as Jerusalem bears the image of the Church which is to be, that is, of the city of the saints already enjoying the angelic life; for Jerusalem is interpreted "Vision of peace." Now contemplation goes before vision, just as this Church goes before that which is promised, the immortal and eternal city. But it goes before in time, not in dignity: because more honorable is that which we strive to reach than that which we do, that we may deserve to reach it; and we engage in contemplation, that we may reach vision. But even this Church which now is, unless the Lord dwelt in it, would go into error, however most zealous its contemplation: and to this Church it was said: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^19]; and: *That Christ may dwell through faith in your hearts in the inner man*[^20]. We are commanded, then, to sing to the Lord, who dwells in Zion, that we may with one accord praise the Lord, the indweller of the Church. *Proclaim his wonders among the nations*: and it has been done, and will not cease to be done.

**13.** [v 13.] *Because, seeking their blood, he has remembered.* As though it were answered by those who are sent to evangelize, to that precept which was said: *Proclaim his wonders among the nations*; and it were said: *Lord, who has believed our report?*[^21] and: *For your sake we are killed all the day long*[^22], fittingly there follows, saying that the Christians who die in persecution will not die without great fruit of eternity: *Because, seeking their blood, he has remembered.* But why did he prefer to say *their blood*? Is it as though some other, more inexperienced and of less faith, were to ask, saying: How will they proclaim, when the unbelief of the nations is to rage against them; to whom it is answered: *Because, seeking their blood, he has remembered*; that is, the last judgment will come, where both the glory of the slain and the punishment of the slayers will be manifest? But *he has remembered*—let no one think it is so put as though forgetfulness befalls God; but because after a long time the judgment is to come, it is put according to the affection of weak men, who think God as it were forgetful, because he does not act as quickly as they themselves wish. To these is said also what follows: *He has not forgotten the cry of the poor*; that is, he is not, as you think, forgetful: as though they said, after they heard, *He has remembered.* Then he was forgetful; *He has not forgotten*, he says, *the cry of the poor.*

**14.** [vv 14.15.] But I ask what is the cry of the poor which God does not forget. Is it this cry, whose words are these: *Have mercy on me, Lord, see my humiliation from my enemies?* Why, then, did he not say, Have mercy on us, Lord, see our humiliation from our enemies, as though many poor were crying out; but as though one, *Have mercy on me, Lord?* Is it because one intercedes for the saints, who first was made poor for us, when he was rich, and he himself says: *You who lift me up from the gates of death, that I may proclaim all your praises in the gates of the daughter of Zion?* For man is lifted up in him not only whom he bears, who is the head of the Church, but also whoever of us is in the rest of the members; and he is lifted up from all the depraved desires, which are the gates of death, because through them one goes into death. Now death is the very gladness in enjoyment, when anyone obtains what he has wickedly desired: for the root of all evils is desire; and therefore it is the gate of death, because the widow who lives in delights is dead: to which delights one comes through desires as through the gates of death. But the gates of the daughter of Zion are all the best pursuits, through which one comes to the vision of peace in the holy Church. In these gates, then, all the praises of God are well proclaimed, so that the holy thing is not given to dogs, nor the pearls cast before swine; who prefer stubbornly to bark, rather than zealously to seek, or who neither bark nor seek, but wallow in the mire of their own pleasures. But when in good pursuits the praises of God are proclaimed, it is given to those who ask, and made manifest to those who seek, and opened to those who knock. Or perhaps the gates of death are the bodily senses and the eyes, which were opened to man when he had tasted of the forbidden tree, from which they are lifted up, to whom it is said that they should seek not the things that are seen, but the things that are not seen; because the things that are seen are temporal, but the things that are not seen are eternal: and the gates of the daughter of Zion are the sacraments and beginnings of faith, which are opened to those who knock, that one may come to the hidden things of the Son? For eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love him. Thus far is the cry of the poor, which the Lord has not forgotten.

**15.** [v 16.] Then there follows, *I will exult in your salvation*: that is, with blessedness I will be held by your salvation, which is our Lord Jesus Christ, the Power and Wisdom of God. The Church, then, which is here afflicted, and is saved by hope, speaks: as long as the judgment of the Son is hidden, it says by that very hope, *I will exult in your salvation*; because now it is worn down by the surrounding clamor, whether of the violence or of the error of the gentiles. *The nations are fixed fast in the corruption which they made*: notice how punishment is reserved for the sinner out of his own works; and how those who wished to persecute the Church are fixed fast in that corruption which they thought to bring upon it: for they desired to slay bodies, while they themselves were dying in the soul. *In this trap, which they hid, their own foot has been caught.* The hidden trap is the deceitful thought. The foot of the soul is rightly understood as love: which, when it is depraved, is called desire or lust; but when it is upright, affection or love. For by love it is moved as it were to the place to which it tends. But the place of the soul is not in any space which the form of the body occupies, but in delight, where it rejoices to have come through love: but destructive delight follows desire, fruitful delight follows love. Whence desire too is called a root. Now a root is understood as it were the foot of a tree. Love too is called a root, where the Lord speaks of the seeds which in stony places wither under the burning sun, because they do not have a deep root. Whence he signifies those who rejoice in receiving the word of truth, but yield to persecutions, which are resisted by love alone. And the Apostle says: *That, rooted and grounded in love, you may be able to comprehend*[^23]. The foot of sinners, then, that is, love, is caught in the trap which they hide; because when delight has followed the fraudulent action, when God has handed them over to the desire of their heart, that delight now binds them, so that they dare not tear away their love from there and turn it to useful things; because when they have tried, they will grieve in soul, as though wishing to draw a foot out of a fetter; and succumbing to this grief, they refuse to withdraw from the destructive delights. *In the trap*, then, *which they hid*, that is, in the fraudulent counsel, *their foot has been caught*, namely the love which through fraud comes to a vain gladness, by which sorrow is procured.

**16.** [v 17.] *The Lord is known executing judgments.* These are the judgments of God: not from that tranquillity of his blessedness, nor from the secret places of wisdom, in which the blessed souls are received, is brought forth sword, or fire, or beast, or anything of the kind by which sinners are tormented. But how are they tormented, and how does the Lord execute judgment? *In the works*, he says, *of his own hands the sinner is caught.*

**17.** [vv 18-21.] Here is interposed, *A canticle of the diapsalma*: as though a hidden gladness, as far as we can estimate, of the separation which now takes place, not by places, but by the affections of souls, between sinners and the righteous, like that of the grains from the chaff while still on the threshing floor. And there follows, *Let sinners be turned back into hell*; that is, let them be given into their own hands, when they are spared, and be ensnared by deadly delight. *All the nations that forget God*: because, when they did not approve of having God in their knowledge, God gave them up to a reprobate sense.

**18.** *Because the poor man will not be forgotten unto the end*: who now seems to be in forgetfulness, when sinners are thought to flourish in the felicity of this world, and the righteous to labor; but *the patience*, he says, *of the poor will not perish forever.* For which reason now there is need of patience to bear with the evil, who are already separated in their wills, until they be separated also at the last judgment.

**19.** *Rise up, Lord, let not man prevail.* The future judgment is implored: but before it comes, *Let the nations*, he says, *be judged in your sight*: that is, in hidden manner, which is said to be before God, with few saints and righteous understanding. *Set, Lord, a lawgiver over them.* He seems to me to signify Antichrist, of whom the Apostle says: *When the man of sin will be revealed*[^24]. *Let the nations know that they are men.* So that those who refuse to be set free by the Son of God, and to belong to the son of man, and to be sons of men, that is, new men, may serve a man, that is, the old sinful man, *because they are men.*

**20.** [vv 1-3.] And because that one is believed to be about to come to so great a height of empty glory, and it will be permitted him to do such great things, both upon all men and upon the saints of God, that then truly some weak ones will think God neglects human affairs; the diapsalma being interposed, he adds as it were the voice of those groaning, and asking why the judgment is deferred: *Why, Lord*, he says, *have you withdrawn far off?* Then he who so asked, as though he had suddenly understood, or as though knowing he had questioned in order to teach, adds, saying: *You despise in opportune times, in tribulations*; that is, opportunely you despise, and make tribulations, to inflame souls with desire for your coming: for to these that fountain of life is more delightful who have thirsted much; and so he suggests the counsel of the delay, saying: *while the impious is proud, the poor is set on fire.* It is wonderful and true, with how great zeal the little ones of good hope are kindled to live rightly, by comparison with sinners. By which mystery it comes about that even heresies are permitted to be: not because the heretics themselves wish this; but because divine Providence works this from their sins, which both makes and orders light, but only orders darkness, that by comparison with it the light may be more pleasing, just as by comparison with heretics the finding of truth is more delightful: for by that comparison the approved are made manifest among men who are known to God.

**21.** *They are caught in the thoughts which they think*: that is, their evil thoughts become bonds to them. But why do they become bonds? *Because the sinner is praised*, he says, *in the desires of his soul.* The tongues of flatterers bind souls in sins: for it delights to do those things in which not only is no reprover feared, but even a praiser is heard. *And he who does iniquities is blessed*: hence they are caught in the thoughts which they think.

**22.** [v 4.] *The sinner has provoked the Lord.* Let no one congratulate the man who prospers in his way, whose sins lack an avenger, and have a praiser: this is the greater anger of the Lord. For the sinner has provoked the Lord, that he should suffer these things, that is, that he should not suffer the scourges of correction. *The sinner has provoked the Lord: according to the multitude of his anger he will not seek out.* Much is he angry, while he does not seek out, while he as it were forgets and does not attend to sins, and through frauds and crimes riches and honors are reached: which will above all come about in that Antichrist, who will seem so blessed to men that he will even be thought God. But how great this anger of God is, the following teaches.

**23.** [v 5.] *God is not in his sight, his ways are defiled at all times.* He who knows either what he rejoices in, or what he is glad of in the soul, knows how great an evil it is to be deserted by the light of truth; whereas men think the blindness of the bodily eyes, by which this light is withdrawn, a great evil. How great a punishment, then, does he suffer who, by the prosperity of his sins, is brought to this, that God is not in his sight, and his ways are defiled at all times, that is, his thoughts and counsels are unclean! *Your judgments are taken away from before his face.* For the soul, evilly conscious to itself, while it seems to itself to suffer no punishment, believes that God does not judge, and so the judgments of God are taken away from before its face, when this very thing is the great damnation. *And he will dominate all his enemies.* For so it is handed down, that he is to overcome all kings, and alone to obtain the kingdom; when, according to the Apostle too, who proclaims of him, *He will sit in the temple of God, exalting himself above all that is worshiped and that is called God*[^25].

**24.** [v 6.] And since, handed over to the desire of his heart and destined to the last damnation, he is to come through wicked arts to that vain and empty height and dominion, therefore there follows, *For he has said in his heart: I will not be moved from generation to generation without evil*; that is, my fame and my name will not pass from this generation to the generation of those who come after, unless by evil arts I obtain so exalted a principality, about which those who come after cannot be silent. For a lost soul, devoid of good arts and estranged from the light of righteousness, contrives for itself by evil arts an entry to so long-lasting a fame, that it may even be celebrated among posterity. And those who cannot become well known desire that men should speak even ill of them, provided the name spread far and wide. Which I think is here said: *I will not be moved from generation to generation without evil.* There is also another understanding. If the vain soul, full of error, thinks it cannot come from the mortal generation to the generation of eternity, except by evil arts; which indeed was rumored even of Simon, when he thought he would obtain heaven by wicked arts, and pass from human generation into divine generation by magical means: what wonder, then, if that man of sin too, who is to fulfill all the wickedness and impiety which all the false prophets began, and to do such great signs as to deceive, if it could be, even the elect, is to say in his heart: *I will not be moved from generation to generation without evil?*

**25.** [v 7.] *Whose mouth is full of cursing, and bitterness, and deceit.* For it is a great curse, to seek heaven by such wicked arts, and to procure such merits for the seizing of an eternal seat. But of this his curse his mouth is full: for that desire will not have effect, but only within his mouth will it avail to destroy him who dared to promise these things to himself with bitterness and deceit, that is, with anger and the snares by which he is to turn the multitude to his side. *Under his tongue toil and pain.* Nothing is more toilsome than iniquity and impiety: which toil pain follows; because it labors not only without fruit, but even unto destruction. Which toil and pain is referred to that which he said in his heart, *I will not be moved from generation to generation without evil*: and therefore, *under his tongue*; not, on his tongue; since he is to think these things silently, but to speak other things to men, that he may seem good and just and a son of God.

**26.** [v 8.] *He sits in ambush with the rich.* With what rich, but those whom he will heap with the gifts of this world? And therefore he is said to sit in ambush with these, since he will display their false felicity for the deceiving of men. Who, with a depraved will, while they desire to be such, and do not seek the eternal goods, will fall into his snares. *In hidden places, that he may slay the innocent. In hidden places* I think it was said, where it is not easily understood what is to be sought, or what avoided: but to slay the innocent is from an innocent to make a guilty one.

**27.** [v 9.] *His eyes look upon the poor.* For he will especially persecute the righteous, of whom it was said: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^26]. *He lies in ambush in secret, like a lion in his den.* He calls him a lion in the den, in whom he will work both force and deceit. For the first persecution of the Church was violent, when by proscriptions, torments, slaughters, the Christians were forced to sacrifice: the second persecution is fraudulent, which is now carried out through heretics of every kind and false brothers: the third remains, to come through Antichrist, than which nothing is more dangerous, since it will be both violent and fraudulent. It will have force in its empire, deceit in its miracles: to force is referred what was said, *lion*; to deceit, what was said, *in his den.* And again these very things are repeated in reversed order. *He lies in ambush*, he says, *to snatch the poor*; this pertains to deceit: but what follows, *To snatch the poor while he draws him in*, is assigned to violence; for *draws him in* signifies that by afflicting with whatever torments he can, he brings him to himself.

**28.** [v 10.] Likewise the two that follow are the same. *In his trap he will humble him*, is deceit. *He will be bowed down, and fall, while he dominates the poor*, is force: for the trap well signifies the snares; but domination most openly suggests terror. And well he says, *He will humble him in his trap*: for when he begins to do those signs, the more wonderful they seem to men, the more those saints, who will then be, will be despised, and held as it were for nothing; whom he, to whom they resist by righteousness and innocence, will seem to overcome by wondrous deeds. But *he will be bowed down, and fall, while he dominates the poor*, that is, while he inflicts whatever punishments upon the servants of God resisting him.

**29.** [vv 11.12.] But whence will he be bowed down, and fall? *For he has said in his heart: God has forgotten, he has turned away his face, that he may not see unto the end.* This is the most wretched bowing down and fall, while the human soul as it were prospers in its iniquities, and thinks itself spared; when it is blinded, and reserved for the last and opportune vengeance, of which he now speaks, *Rise up, Lord God, let your hand be exalted*; that is, let your power be made manifest. But above he had said, *Rise up, Lord, let not man prevail, let the nations be judged in your sight*; that is, in hidden manner, where God alone sees. This was done, when the impious came to the great felicity which seems so to men; over whom is set a lawgiver, such as they deserved to have, of whom it is said: *Set, Lord, a lawgiver over them, let the nations know that they are men.* But now, after that hidden punishment and vengeance, it is said, *Rise up, Lord God, let your hand be exalted*: not, that is, in hidden manner, but now in most manifest glory. *Do not forget the poor unto the end*; that is, as the impious think, who say: *God has forgotten, he has turned away his face that he may not see unto the end.* Now they deny that God sees unto the end who say that he does not care for human and earthly affairs; since the earth is as it were the end of things, because it is the last element in which men labor in a most ordered way, but cannot see the order of their labors, which especially pertains to the hidden things of the Son. The Church, then, laboring in those times, as a ship in great waves and storms, as it were rouses the sleeping Lord, that he may command the winds, and tranquillity return: it says, then, *Rise up, Lord God, let your hand be exalted, do not forget the poor unto the end.*

**30.** [vv 13.14.] And so now, understanding the manifest judgment, and exulting, they say, *Why has the impious provoked God?* that is, what did it profit to do such great evils? *For he has said in his heart: He will not seek out.* Then there follows: *You see, because you consider toil and anger, that you may hand them over into your hands.* This sense requires the right delivery, in which, if error is made, it is obscured. For thus the impious said in his heart: God will not seek out, as though God considered toil and anger, that he might hand them over into his own hands; that is, as though he feared to labor and be angry, and therefore spared them, lest their punishment be burdensome to him, or lest he be disturbed by the storm of wrath, as for the most part men do, who dissemble vengeance, lest they labor or be angry.

**31.** *To you the poor man has been left.* For he is poor for this reason, that is, he has despised all the temporal goods of this world, that you alone may be his hope. *You will be a helper to the orphan*; that is, to him for whom this world dies as a father, by whom he was carnally begotten, and who can already say: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^27]. For to such orphans God becomes a father. For the Lord teaches his disciples to become orphans, to whom he says: *Do not call yourselves father on earth*[^28]. Of which thing he himself first gave an example by saying: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?*[^29] Whence certain most pernicious heretics wish to assert that he had no mother: nor do they see what follows, if they attend to these words, that neither did his disciples have fathers; because as he himself said, *Who is my mother*, so he taught them, when he said: *Do not call yourselves father on earth.*

**32.** [vv 15.16.] *Break the arm of the sinner and the malicious one*; namely of that one of whom it was said above: *He will dominate all his enemies.* His arm, then, he called his power: to which is contrary the power of Christ, of which it is said: *Rise up, Lord God, let your hand be exalted. His sin will be sought out, and will not be found on account of it*, that is, he will be judged for his sin, and he himself will perish for his sin. Then what wonder if there follows, *The Lord will reign forever and unto the age of the age, you will perish, O nations, from his earth?* By nations he put sinners and the impious.

**33.** [v 17.] *The desire of the poor the Lord has heard*: that desire with which they burned, when, in the straits and tribulations of this world, they longed for the day of the Lord. *The preparation of their heart your ear has heard*: this is the preparation of the heart, of which it is sung in another psalm, *My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready*[^30]; of which the Apostle says: *But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^31]. Now the ear of God, not a bodily member, but the power by which he hears, we ought to take as a rule; and so, whatever members of his are named, which in us are visible and bodily, we ought to understand the powers of his workings, lest these be often repeated: for it is not right to think corporeal that the Lord God hears not the sounding voice, but the preparation of the heart.

**34.** [v 18.] *To judge for the orphan and the humble*: that is, not for him who is conformed to this world, nor for the proud. For it is one thing to judge the orphan, another to judge for the orphan: he judges the orphan who even condemns; but he judges for the orphan who pronounces sentence on his behalf. *That man may not henceforth add to magnify himself upon the earth.* For they are men, of whom it was said: *Set, Lord, a lawgiver over them, let the nations know that they are men.* But even that one who is understood in the same place to be set over them will be a man, of whom it is now said, *That man may not henceforth add to magnify himself upon the earth*: when, namely, the Son of man comes to judge for the orphan, who has put off the old man, and in this way as it were exalted the Father.

**35.** After the hidden things of the Son, then, of which many things were said in this psalm, there will be the manifest things of the Son, of which a few have now been said at the end of the same psalm: but the title was made from those things which here hold the greater part. The very day of the Lord's coming too can rightly be numbered among the hidden things of the Son, although the Lord's very presence will be manifest; for of that day it was said that no one knew it, neither the angels, nor the Virtues, nor the Son of man: what, then, is so hidden as that which was said to be hidden even to the judge himself, not for the knowing, but for the disclosing? But concerning the hidden things of the Son, even if anyone should wish to understand not the Son of God, but David himself, to whose name the whole Psalter is attributed (for they are certainly called the psalms of David), let him hear those voices by which it is said to the Lord, *Have mercy on us, Son of David*[^32]; and so even in this way let him understand the same Lord Christ, of whose hidden things the very psalm is inscribed. For so it is also said by the angel: *God will give him the seat of David his father*[^33]. Nor is that sentence contrary to this understanding, by which the same Lord asks of the Jews, *If Christ is the son of David, how does he in the spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I put your enemies under your feet?*[^34] For it was said to the inexperienced, who, although they hoped Christ would come, yet awaited him according to the man, not according to what is the Power and Wisdom of God. He teaches there, then, the truest and sincerest faith, that he is both the Lord of King David, according to what is the Word in the beginning, God with God, through whom all things were made; and his son, according to what was made to him from the seed of David according to the flesh. For he does not say, Christ is not the son of David; but, If you now hold that he is his son, learn how he is his Lord; and do not hold in Christ that he is the son of man, for so he is the son of David; do not relinquish that he is the Son of God, for so he is his Lord.
