# Exposition of Psalm 70

**1.** In all the holy Scriptures, the grace of God which frees us commends itself to us, that it may hold us commended to it. This is sung in this psalm, of which we have undertaken to speak with Your Charity. The Lord will be present, that I may so conceive it in heart as is worthy; and so bring it forth as is expedient for you. For the love and fear of God moves much: the fear of God, because he is just; the love, because he is merciful. *For who would say to him: What have you done*[^1], if he condemned the unjust? How great, then, his mercy, that he justifies the unjust? From this, the Apostle too, read to us beforehand, we heard commending especially that same grace: from the commending of which he had the Jews as enemies, presuming as it were on the letter of the Law, and as it were loving and boasting of their own righteousness; of whom he says: *I bear them witness, that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge.* And as though it were said to him: For what is it to have a zeal for God not according to knowledge? he at once added: *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they were not subject to the righteousness of God*[^2]. Glorying, he says, as it were of works, they shut out from themselves grace; and as it were presuming on their own false health, they reject the medicine. For against such the Lord too had said: *I came not to call the just, but sinners to repentance; the healthy do not need a physician, but those who are ill*[^3]. This, then, is the whole great knowledge, that a man know that he himself through himself is nothing; and that whatever he is, is from God, and for the sake of God. *For what have you,* he says, *that you did not receive? But if you have received, why do you glory as though you had not received*[^4]? This grace the Apostle commends; hence he merited to have the Jews as enemies, glorying of the letter of the Law and of their own righteousness. Commending it, then, in the reading which was read, he says thus: *For I am the least of the Apostles, who am not worthy to be called an Apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God.* *But for this reason,* he says, *I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief.* Then a little after: *Faithful is the saying,* he says, *and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the first.* Surely before him there were no sinners? What is, then, *I am the first*? Going before all, not in time, but in malignity. *But for this reason,* he says, *I obtained mercy, that in me Christ Jesus might show all longsuffering, for the instruction of those who will believe in him unto eternal life*[^5]: that is, that every sinner and unjust man now despairing of himself, now having as it were the mind of a gladiator, so as therefore to do whatever he wishes, because he must necessarily be condemned, may yet look to the apostle Paul, to whom so great cruelty, and so great malice was forgiven by God; and, by not despairing of himself, may be converted to God. This grace, then, God commends to us in this psalm too; let us behold it, and see whether it is so, or whether perhaps I suspect otherwise. For I reckon that it has this disposition, and that this resounds in almost all its syllables: that is, that it commends to us the free grace of God, which frees us unworthy, not for our sake, but for his own; so that even if I did not say this, nor had said it beforehand, any man of a moderate heart, hearing attentively the words of the same psalm, would relish this; and perhaps by the very words, if he had something else in his heart, he would be changed, and there would come about what this resounds. What is this? That all our hope be in God, and that we presume nothing of ourselves as of our own powers; lest, making our own what is from him, we lose what we have received.

**2.** [v. 1] The title, then, of this psalm, as a title is wont, indicating at the threshold what is done in the house: *For David himself, of the sons of Jonadab, and of those who were first led into captivity.*[^6] Jonadab was a certain man, commended to us from the prophecy of Jeremiah, who had commanded his sons that they should not drink wine, nor dwell in houses, but in tents. But the sons held and kept the commandment of their father, and from this merited to be blessed by the Lord. But the Lord had not commanded this, but their father. Yet they so received it, as a commandment from the Lord their God: because, although the Lord had not commanded that they should not drink wine, and should dwell in tents; yet the Lord had commanded that sons should obey their father. In that matter alone, then, a son ought not to obey his father, if his father commands something against the Lord his God. For the father ought not to be angry, when God is preferred to him. But where the father commands what is not against God, he is to be obeyed as God; because God commanded to obey the father. God blessed, then, the sons of Jonadab on account of obedience, and set them against his disobedient people, reproaching them that, while the sons of Jonadab were obedient to their father, they were not obedient to their God. But when Jeremiah did these things, he did this with the people of Israel, that they should prepare themselves to be led into captivity into Babylon, and should not resist the will of God, nor hope for anything else, except that they would be captives. The title of this psalm, then, seems to be colored from there, so that, when he had said, *of the sons of Jonadab,* he added, *and of those who were first led into captivity*: not because the sons of Jonadab were led into captivity, but because to those who were to be led into captivity the sons of Jonadab were set in opposition, because they were obedient to their father; that those might understand that they were therefore made captives, because they were not obedient to God. There is added too that Jonadab is interpreted "The Lord's Voluntary One." What is this, the Lord's voluntary one? Serving God willingly with the will. What is, the Lord's voluntary one? *In me, O God, are your vows, which I will repay as praises to you*[^7]. What is, the Lord's voluntary one? *Willingly I will sacrifice to you*[^8]. For if apostolic discipline admonishes a servant to serve a man, his lord, not as it were of necessity, but of will; and by serving willingly to make himself free in heart; how much more is God to be served with the whole, and full, and willing will, who sees the very will of you? For if a servant does not serve you from the soul, you can see his hands, his face, his presence; his heart you cannot: and yet the Apostle says to them: *Not serving to the eye.* And what is, *Not to the eye*? What then, will my lord see how I serve him, that you say to me: *Not serving to the eye*? he added: *But as servants of Christ.* The lord, a man, does not see; but the Lord Christ sees. *From the soul,* he says, *with good will*[^9]. Such was Jonadab; that is, this his name is interpreted. Who are, then, *those who were first led into captivity*? The sons of Israel were led into captivity first, and second, and third; but for these the Psalm, or of these the Psalm, who were led first, does not mean this: that is, the psalm itself, examined, searched out, questioned in all its verses, tells you another thing, that it does not speak of those, certain men, I know not who, I know not when, with enemies rushing in, who were led captive into Babylon from Jerusalem. But what does the Psalm say, except what you heard from the Apostle? It commends to us the grace of God; it commends to us, that we through ourselves are nothing: it commends to us, that whatever we are, we are by his mercy; but whatever we are of ourselves, we are evil. Why, then, *captives*? and why is the grace of the liberator commended by the name of captivity? He opens it, the Apostle himself says: *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man: but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members.* Behold, you have being led captive. What, then, does this psalm commend? What follows there in the Apostle: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^10]. Why, then, *first*? For it has been said why *captives.* As far as I reckon, this too is clear. Since in comparison with the sons of Jonadab all disobedience is blamed. But through disobedience we were made captives, because Adam himself by not obeying sinned. And it was said by the same Apostle, what is also true, that *all in Adam die, in whom all sinned*[^11]. Deservedly too *they were first led captive*: because *the first man of earth is earthly, the second man of heaven is heavenly; such as the earthly, such also the earthly; such as the heavenly, such also the heavenly. As we have borne the image of the earthly, let us bear also the image of him who is from heaven*[^12]. The first man made us captives; the second man freed us from captivity. *For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*[^13]. But in Adam they die through the birth of the flesh; in Christ they are freed through the faith of the heart. It was not in your power that you should not be born of Adam; it is in your power that you believe in Christ. However much, then, you wish to pertain to the first man, you will pertain to captivity. And what is, you wish to pertain? or what is, you will pertain? You already pertain: cry out: *Who will free me from the body of this death*[^14]? Let us hear, then, this one crying out this.

**3.** *O God, in you I have hoped; O Lord, let me not be confounded forever.*[^15] Now I am confounded; but let it not be forever! For how is he not confounded, to whom it is said: *What fruit had you in those things of which you are now ashamed*[^16]? What, then, will be done, lest we be confounded forever? *Draw near to him, and be enlightened, and your faces will not blush*[^17]. You were confounded in Adam: withdraw from Adam, draw near to Christ, and you will no longer be confounded. *In you I have hoped, O Lord; let me not be confounded forever.* If in myself I am confounded, in you let me not be confounded forever.

**4.** [v. 2] *In your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me.*[^18] Not in mine, but in yours; for if in mine, I shall be of those of whom he says: *Being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own righteousness, they were not subject to the righteousness of God*[^19]. Therefore *in your righteousness,* not in mine. For mine—what? Iniquity went before. And when I am just, it will be your righteousness: because by the righteousness given me by you I shall be just; and so it will be mine, that it may be yours, that is, given me by you. For I believe in him who justifies the ungodly, that my faith may be reckoned unto righteousness. It will be, then, so my righteousness too; yet not as my own property, not as given me by myself: as those thought who gloried through the letter, and rejected grace. For it was said in another place: *Judge me, O Lord, according to my righteousness*[^20]. Certainly he did not glory in his own righteousness. But recall the Apostle to me: *For what have you that you did not receive*[^21]? and so call it your righteousness, that you remember that you received it, and do not envy it to those receiving it. For that Pharisee too, as though he had received from God, said: *I give you thanks, that I am not as the rest of men. I give you thanks*: well. *That I am not as the rest of men*: why? For does it delight you that you are good, because another is evil? Finally, what did he add? *Unjust, robbers, adulterers, like this publican too.* This is now not to exult, but to insult. But that captive *did not dare even to lift his eyes to heaven; but struck his breast, saying: Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^22]. It is too little, then, that you recognize that good which is in you to be from God, unless you also do not exalt yourself over him who does not yet have it; who perhaps, when he has received it, will pass you. For when Saul was the stoner of Stephen, how many Christians there were whose persecutor he was! Yet when he was converted, he surpassed all who had gone before him. Therefore say to God what you hear in the Psalm: *In you I have hoped, O Lord; let me not be confounded forever: in your righteousness,* not in mine, *rescue me, and deliver me. Incline your ear to me.* And this too is a confession of humility. He who says: *Incline to me,* confesses that he lies as it were a sick man prostrate before the physician standing. Finally, see that a sick man speaks: *Incline your ear to me, and save me.*

**5.** [v. 3] *Be to me a God a protector.*[^23] Let not the darts of the enemy reach me, for I cannot protect myself. And it is too little, *a protector;* he added, *and a fortified place, that you may save me. A fortified place* be to me; you be my fortified place. Where is that which you fled from him, O Adam, and hid yourself among the trees of paradise? Where is that you dreaded his face, to whom you had been wont to rejoice? You went away, and perished: you were made captive, and behold you are visited, and behold you are not abandoned; and behold the ninety-nine sheep are left on the mountains, and the lost sheep is sought; and behold it is said of the found sheep: *It was dead, and is alive again; it had perished, and is found*[^24]. Behold, God himself has become the place of your refuge, who at first was the dread of your flight. *A fortified place,* he says, be to me, *that you may save me.* I shall not be safe except in you: unless you are my rest, my sickness cannot be healed. Lift me from the earth; let me lie in you, that I may rise into a fortified place. What is more fortified? When you have fled to that place, tell me what adversaries you will dread? who will lie in wait, and reach you? Someone, I know not who, is said to have cried out from the top of a mountain, when the emperor passed by: I do not speak of you: he is said to have looked back and said: Nor I of you. He had despised the emperor with his distinguished arms, with a powerful army. Whence? From a fortified place. If he was secure on high earth; how much more you in him by whom heaven and earth were made? *Be,* then, *to me a God a protector, and a fortified place, that you may save me.* If I choose for myself another place, I cannot be safe. Choose, certainly, O man, if you find one, a more fortified place. There is, then, nowhere to flee from him, except to him. If you wish to escape him angry, flee to him appeased. *Because you are my firmament, and my refuge. My firmament,* what is it? Through you I am firm, and from you I am firm. *Because you are my firmament, and my refuge*: that I may become firm from you, and if anywhere I am made weak in myself, I may flee back to you. For the grace of Christ makes you firm, and immovable against all the temptations of the enemy. But there too is human frailty, there too is still the first captivity, there too is the law in the members fighting against the law of the mind, and wishing to lead captive in the law of sin: still the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul. However firm you are from the grace of God; as long as you still carry the earthen vessel, in which is the treasure of God, something even of the very earthen vessel is to be dreaded. Therefore *you are my firmament,* that I may be firm in this age against all temptations. But if they are many, and trouble me, *you are my refuge.* For this I will confess my weakness, that I may be timid like a hare, because full of thorns like a hedgehog. And, as is said in another psalm: *The rock is a refuge for the hedgehogs and the hares*[^25]: but the rock was Christ.

**6.** [v. 4] *My God, rescue me from the hand of the sinner.*[^26] Sinners in general, among whom this one labors who is now to be freed from captivity; this one who now cries out: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^27]? Within is an enemy, that law in the members; there are also enemies outside: to whom do you cry out? To him, to whom it was cried out: *From my hidden things cleanse me, O Lord; and from those of others spare your servant*[^28]. Therefore: *Save me* when it is said, it is said of your inner sickness, that is, of your iniquity; of that by which you are captive, of that by which you pertain to the first man, that you may cry among the first captives. But, made safe from your iniquity, see now also concerning the iniquities of others, among whom you must necessarily live, until this life is ended. And how long? Behold, it is ended for you: surely it is not ended for the Church, except at the end of the age? But this one man, the unity of Christ, cries out these voices. It is necessary, then, that many faithful, going out from this body, are now in that rest which God furnishes to the spirits of the faithful: but here are still the members of Christ; in those who pass this life, in those who will afterward be born. Therefore unto the end of the age there will be here this man, who cries out to be freed from his sins, and from the law of the members fighting against the law of the mind. Then, amid the sins of others, among whom it is necessary that he live unto the end. But sinners have two kinds: some who received the Law, some who did not receive it; all the pagans did not receive the Law, all the Jews and Christians received the Law. Therefore "sinner" is a general name; either a transgressor of the Law, if he received the Law; or only an unjust man without the Law, if he did not receive the Law. Of each kind the Apostle speaks, and says: *Those who sinned without the Law will perish without the Law; and those who sinned in the Law will be judged by the Law*[^29]. But you who groan between each kind, say to God what you hear in the Psalm: *My God, rescue me from the hand of the sinner.* Of what sinner? *From the hand of the transgressor of the Law and the unjust.* There is indeed also an unjust man transgressing the Law, for he is not not unjust who transgresses the Law: but every transgressor of the Law is unjust, not every unjust man transgresses the Law. *For where there is no Law,* says the Apostle, *there is no transgression*[^30]. Those, then, who did not receive the Law, can be called unjust, transgressors they cannot. Both are judged according to their merits. But I who wish to be freed from captivity through your grace, cry out to you: *Free me from the hand of the sinner.* What is, from his hand? From his power; lest, when he rages, he draw me to consent; lest, when he lies in wait, he persuade iniquity. *From the hand of the transgressor of the Law and the unjust.* Answer him: Why do you seek to be freed from the hand of the transgressor of the Law and the unjust? Do not consent; but if he rages, be patient, endure. But who endures, if he forsakes who becomes the fortified place? Why, then, do I say: *Free me from the hand of the transgressor of the Law and the unjust*? Because it is not in me to be patient, but in you who give patience.

**7.** [vv. 5-6] Finally there follows why I say this: *Because you are my patience.*[^31] Now if my patience, rightly also what follows: *O Lord, my hope from my youth.* Therefore my patience, because my hope: or rather therefore my hope, because my patience? *Tribulation,* says the Apostle, *works patience, patience proving, but proving hope, and hope does not confound*[^32]. Deservedly *in you I have hoped, O Lord; let me not be confounded forever. O Lord, my hope from my youth.* From your youth, O God, is your hope? Was it not also from your boyhood, and from your infancy? Plainly, he says. For see what follows, lest you should think I so said: *My hope from my youth,* as though God profited nothing for my infancy or boyhood; hear what follows: *In you I have been confirmed from the womb.* Hear still: *From my mother's belly you are my protector.* Why, then, *from my youth,* except from when I began to hope in you? For before I did not hope in you; although you were my protector, who led me safe to the time when I learned to hope in you. But from my youth I began to hope in you; from when you armed me against the devil, that, in the array of your army armed with your faith, love, hope, and your other gifts, I might fight against your invisible enemies, and hear from the Apostle: *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood; but against the principalities, and powers, and rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spiritual forces of wickedness*[^33]. He, then, is a young man who fights against these; but although he is a young man, he falls, unless he be the hope of him who cries out: *O Lord, my hope from my youth.*

**8.** *In you is my singing always.*[^34] Is it from when I began to hope in you until now? But, *always.* What is, *always*? Not only in the time of faith, but also in the time of vision. For now, as long as we are in the body, we are on pilgrimage from the Lord; for we walk by faith, not by vision: the time will come when we shall see what, unseen, we believe; and, when we have seen what we believe, we shall rejoice: but when the ungodly have seen what they did not believe, they will be confounded. Then will come the reality of which now there is the hope. For hope which is seen is not hope. But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience. Now, then, you groan, now you run to the place of refuge, that you may be safe; now, set in weakness, you ask the physician: what, when you have received also perfect health? what, when you have been made equal to the angels of God? will you then perhaps forget that grace by which you were freed? Far be it. *In you is my singing always.*

**9.** [v. 7] *I have become as a prodigy to many.*[^35] Here in the time of hope, in the time of groaning, in the time of humility, in the time of grief, in the time of weakness, in the time of the voice from the chains, here, then, what? *I have become as a prodigy to many.* Whence, *as a prodigy*? whence do they insult me who think me a *prodigy*? Because I believe what I do not see. For they, blessed in the things they see, exult in drink, in luxury, in fornications, in avarice, in riches, in plunderings, in worldly dignities, in the whitewashing of the muddy wall; in these they exult: but I walk on a dissimilar way, despising the things that are present, and fearing even the prosperities of the age, nor secure anywhere except in the promises of God. And they: *Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we die*[^36]. What do you say? repeat. *Let us eat,* he says, *and drink.* Go on; what did you say afterward? *For tomorrow we die.* You terrified; you did not seduce. Plainly by this very thing which you said afterward, you struck fear into me lest I consent to you. For tomorrow we die, you said; and there went before: *Let us eat and drink.* For when you had said: *Let us eat and drink;* you added: *For tomorrow we die.* Hear against this from me: Rather let us fast and pray; for tomorrow we die. Holding this strait and narrow way, *I have become as a prodigy to many; but you are a strong helper.* Be present, O Lord Jesus, who may say to me: Do not fail on the narrow way; I passed over first, I am the way itself, I lead, in myself I lead, to myself I bring through. Therefore although *I have become a prodigy to many;* yet I will not fear, because *you are a strong helper.*

**10.** [v. 8] *Let my mouth be filled with praise, that I may tell your glory with a hymn; all the day long your magnificence.*[^37] What is, *all the day long*? Without intermission. In prosperity, because you console; in adversity, because you correct; before I was, because you made me; when I was, because you gave salvation: when I had sinned, because you forgave; when I was converted, because you helped; when I had persevered, because you crowned. So plainly *let my mouth be filled with praise, that I may tell your glory with a hymn; all the day long your magnificence.*

**11.** [v. 9] *Do not cast me off in the time of old age.*[^38] *My hope from my youth, do not cast me off in the time of old age.* What is this time of old age? *When my strength fails, do not forsake me.* God answers you here: Nay rather, let your strength fail, that mine may remain in you; that you may say with the Apostle: *When I am weak, then I am strong*[^39]. Do not fear lest you be cast off in weakness, in this old age. For what, was not your Lord made weak on the cross? did not the strongest, and the fat bulls, before him, as before a man of no avail, captive and oppressed, wag their heads, and say: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^40]? Surely he, because he was weak, was forsaken; he who preferred not to come down from the cross, lest he seem not to have shown power, but to have yielded to those insulting? What did he teach you, hanging, who was unwilling to come down, except patience among those insulting, except that you be strong in your God? Perhaps this too is said from his person: *I have become as a prodigy to many, and you are a strong helper*: from his person according to weakness, not according to power; according to that whereby he had transfigured us into himself, not according to that whereby he himself had descended. For he became a prodigy to many. And perhaps that was his old age; because from oldness, old age is not incongruously named, and the Apostle says: *Our old man was crucified together with him*[^41]. If there our old man was, old age was there; because oldness, old age. Yet because it is true: *Your youth will be renewed like the eagle's*[^42], he himself rose again on the third day, and promised the resurrection at the end of the age. Now the head has gone before; the members will follow. Why do you fear lest he forsake you, lest he cast you off in the time of old age, when your strength has failed? Nay rather, then will his strength be in you, when your strength has failed.

**12.** [vv. 10-11] Why do I say this? *Because my enemies have spoken against me, and those who guarded my soul have taken counsel together, saying: God has forsaken him; pursue and seize him, because there is no one to rescue him.*[^43] This was said of Christ: for he who by the great power of divinity, by which he is equal to the Father, had raised the dead, suddenly among the hands of the enemies was made weak, and as one of no avail was seized. When would he be seized, unless they first said in their heart: *God has forsaken him*? Whence is that voice on the cross: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me*[^44]? Did God, then, forsake Christ, when God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, when Christ too was God, of the Jews indeed according to the flesh, who is over all things God blessed forever? Did God forsake him? Far be it. But in our old man was our voice, because our old man was crucified together with him; and from that our old man he had taken his body, because Mary was of Adam. Therefore what they thought, this he said from the cross: *Why have you forsaken me*? Why do these men think me forsaken, to their own harm? What is, think me forsaken to their own harm? For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. *Pursue, and seize him.* Yet let us take this more familiarly, brethren, from the members of Christ, and recognize our voice in these words: because he too had such voices from our person, not from his power and majesty; but from that which he was made for our sake, not according to that which was he who made us.

**13.** [v. 12] *O Lord my God, do not go far from me.*[^45] So it comes about, and he does not go far at all. For the Lord is near to those who have bruised their heart. *My God, look to my help.*

**14.** [v. 13] *Let those who set my soul against itself be confounded and fail.*[^46] What did he wish? *Let them be confounded and fail.* Why did he wish it? *Those who set my soul against itself.* What is, *those who set my soul against itself*? Setting it as it were into some quarrel. For those are said to be set against, who are provoked to quarrel. If, then, it is so, let us beware of those who set our soul against itself. What is, setting our soul against itself? First, provoking us to resist God, so that in our evils God may displease us. For when are you upright, that the God of Israel may be good to you, good to the upright of heart? when are you upright? do you wish to hear? When in the good that you do, God pleases you; but in the evil that you suffer, God does not displease you. See what I have said, brethren, and be cautious against those who set your souls against. For all who deal with you so that you be wearied in griefs and tribulations, do this, that in that which you suffer God may displease you, and there may go out of your mouth: What is this? what indeed have I done? Now, then, you have done no evil, and you are just, and he unjust? I am a sinner, you say, I confess; I do not call myself just. But what, a sinner? surely as great as he with whom it is well? as great as Gaius Seius? I know his evils, I know his iniquities, from which I, even though a sinner, am far away; and yet I see him flourishing in all goods, and I suffer so much! Therefore I do not say: God, what have I done to you, because I have done no evil at all; but because I have not done so much, that I deserve to suffer these things. Again you are just, and he unjust? Wake up, wretched man; your soul has been set against. No, he says, I did not call myself just. What, then, do you say? I am a sinner, but I have not committed so much as to deserve to suffer these things. You do not say to God, then: I am just, but you are unjust; but you say: I am unjust, but you are more unjust. Behold, your soul has been set against, behold, your soul now wages war. What? against whom? Your soul, against God; which was made, against him by whom it was made. Or even because you are one who cries against him, you are ungrateful. Return, then, to the confession of your weakness; implore the hand of the physician. Let not those seem to you happy who flourish for a time. You are chastised; they are spared: perhaps for you, a chastised and amended son, an inheritance is reserved. Return, then, return, transgressor, to the heart; let not your soul be set against. Far more powerful is he on whom you have declared war. The greater stones you have hurled into heaven, the stronger the ruin that will press you. Return rather; recognize yourself. It is God who displeases you: blush; displease yourself. You would do no good, unless he were good; and you would suffer nothing from me, unless he were just. Wake up, then, into this voice: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^47]. Unjust were those who sat healthy beside Job rotting; and yet he, to be received back, was scourged, they, to be punished, were spared. Whatever tribulation, then, befalls you, whatever insult, let not your soul be set against; let it not be set against, not only against God, but not even against those very ones who do these things to you. For if you hold even them in hatred; your soul has been set against them too. Rather give him thanks; pray for these. For perhaps this prayer for them too is that which you heard: *Let those who set my soul against itself be confounded and fail. Let them be confounded and fail*: for they presume much on their own righteousness; therefore let them be confounded. This is expedient for them, that they recognize their own sins, whence they may be confounded and fail, for they presumed wrongly on their own powers; and they, having failed, may say: *When I am weak, then I am strong*[^48]; and they, having failed, may say: *Do not cast me off in the time of old age.* He wished them good, then, that they may be confounded over their evils, and fail of their perverse powers; and then, now having failed and confounded, may seek the enlightener from confusion, and the restorer from failure. Finally, see what follows: *Let them be clothed with confusion and shame, who think evils for me. Confusion and shame*: confusion from an evil conscience; shame on account of modesty. Let this come about in them, and they will be good. Let it not seem to you that he rages: would that they be heard for! For Stephen too seemed to rage, when with flaming mouth he hurled those words: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit*[^49]. How kindled with anger, how vehement against the enemies! His soul seems to you to have been set against. Far be it. He sought their salvation; he bound with words the frenzied raging wrongly. For see that his soul had not been set against, not only against God, but not even against them: *Lord Jesus,* he says, *receive my spirit*[^50]. Jesus did not displease him, because he had suffered stoning for his word; therefore his soul had not been set against God. Likewise he said: *Lord, do not lay to them this offense*[^51]: therefore his soul had not been set against even his enemies. *Let those be clothed with confusion and shame, who think evils for me.* For this all who trouble me seek; they seek evils for me. Which evils that woman too sought, when she suggested: *Say something against God, and die*[^52]. And that wife of Tobit, who said to her husband: *Where are your righteous deeds*[^53]? She said this to this end, that God might displease him, who had made him blind; and when God displeased him, his soul might be set against.

**15.** [v. 14] If, then, no one by troubling you has persuaded, no one extorted, that God displease you in the things you suffer, or that you hate the men through whom you suffer, your soul is not set against; secure you say what follows: *But I will always hope in you, and will add upon all your praise.*[^54] What is this? It ought to move us, *I will add upon all your praise.* Are you going to make the praise of God more perfect? is there something to be superadded? if it is already all praise, what are you going to add? God has been praised in all his good deeds, in all his creation, in all the establishing of all things, in the governance and rule of the ages, in the order of the times, in the eminence of heaven, in the fruitfulness of the lands, in the surrounding of the sea, in all the excellence of the creature being born everywhere, in the very sons of men, in the giving of the Law, in freeing his own from the captivity of the Egyptians, and all his other wonders: he had not yet been praised in that he raised up the flesh unto eternal life. Let this praise, then, be added through the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ; that here we understand his voice over all past praise: so this too we rightly understand. What, you perhaps a sinner, who feared your soul being set against, who hoped in him alone, that you might be freed from that first captivity, who did not presume on your own righteousness, but on his grace which this psalm commends; what, are you going to add something upon all the praise of God? I will add, he says. Let us see what he adds. All your praise could be, and nothing at all would seem to be lacking to your praise, because nothing would be lacking, if you condemned all the unjust. For it would be no small praise of God, that very righteousness by which the unjust are condemned: it would be great praise. You made man, you gave him free choice, you set him in paradise, you imposed a precept, you announced a most just death if he should violate the precept; you left nothing undone, there is no one who can demand more of you. He sinned; the human race became as it were a mass of sinners, flowing forth from sinners; what then, if you condemn this mass of iniquity, will anyone say to you: You have done unjustly? You would plainly be just even so, and all this would be your praise; but because you freed even the sinner himself by justifying the ungodly, *I will add upon all your praise.*

**16.** [v. 15] *My mouth will tell your righteousness*[^55]: not mine. From this I will add upon all your praise, because even that I am just, if I am just, is your righteousness in me, not mine; for you justify the ungodly. *My mouth will tell your righteousness; all the day long your salvation.* What is, *your salvation*? Salvation is the Lord's. Let no one usurp to himself that he makes himself safe: salvation is the Lord's. No one makes himself safe through himself: salvation is the Lord's, vain is the salvation of man. *All the day long your salvation*: all the time. There is something adverse; preach the salvation of the Lord: there is something prosperous; preach the salvation of the Lord. Do not preach in prosperity, and grow silent in adversity; otherwise it will not be what was said, *all the day long.* For a whole day is with its night. Surely when we say: Thirty days, for instance, have passed, do we say also the nights? do we not in that very name of days include the nights? In Genesis what was said? *Evening came, and morning came, one day*[^56]. Therefore a whole day is with its night; for the night serves the day, not the day the night. Whatever you do in the mortal flesh, ought to serve righteousness; whatever you do by the precept of God, let it not be done for the emoluments of the flesh, lest the day serve the night. Therefore all the day long say the praise of God; namely in prosperity and in adversity: in prosperity, as in the daytime; in adversity, as in the nighttime: yet all the day long say the praise of God, that you may not have sung in vain: *I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise will always be in my mouth*[^57]. With his sons safe, his cattle, his household, all his property, Job praised God; this as it were in the daytime: losses came, bereavement rushed in, what was being kept perished, those for whom it was kept died; this as it were in the nighttime. See, yet, him praising all the day long. Surely after that daytime where he rejoiced, because there came about the setting as it were of that light, that is, of prosperity, did he fail from the praises of God? Was it not day in his heart, whence it shone forth: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^58]? And this was as it were still the evening time: there came also a thicker night, deeper darkness, the grief of the body, the rotting of worms; nor so, in that very rotting, did he fail from the praises of God outside in the night, who within exulted in the day. For to his wife persuading him to blasphemy, and setting his soul against itself, badly persuading the wretched woman as it were a nighttime shadow: *You have spoken,* he says, *like one of the foolish women.* Truly a daughter of night! *If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not bear evil things*[^59]? Have we praised in the day, and shall we fail in the night? *All the day long,* that is, with its night, *your salvation.*

**17.** *Because I have not known tradings.*[^60] Therefore, he says, *all the day long your salvation, because I have not known tradings.* What are these tradings? Let the traders hear, and change their life; and if they have been such, let them not be; let them not recognize what they were, let them forget it: finally, let them not approve, not praise; let them disapprove, condemn, be changed, if trading is a sin. For hence, by a certain greed, I know not what, of acquiring, O trader, when you have suffered loss, you will blaspheme; and there will not be in you what was said: *All the day long your praise.* But when, for the prices of the things you sell, you not only lie, but even swear falsely; how is the praise of God in your mouth all the day long? when, if you are a Christian, even from your mouth the name of God is blasphemed, so that it is said: Behold what sort the Christians are! Therefore, if for this reason this one says the praise of God all the day long, because he has not known tradings, let the Christians correct themselves; let them not trade. But the trader says to me: Behold, I indeed bring from afar wares to those places in which there are not the things I have brought, whence I may live; I seek as it were the reward of my labor, that I may sell dearer than I bought. For whence shall I live, since it is written: *The laborer is worthy of his wages*[^61]? But the matter concerns lying, perjury. This is my vice, not of trading: for surely, if I wished, I could carry on without this vice. I do not, then, as the agent transfer my own fault to the business: but if I lie, I lie, not the business. For I could say: I bought for so much, but I will sell for so much; if it pleases, buy. For the buyer, hearing this truth, would not be repelled, but rather all would run to it; because they would love faithfulness more than the wares. This, then, he says, admonish me not to lie, not to perjure; not that I cast away the business by which I support myself. For to what do you call me, when you call me back from this? Perhaps to some craft? I will be a cobbler; I will make shoes for men. For are they too not liars? are they too not perjurers? Do they not, when shoes have been hired from another, when they have received the price from another, leave what they were doing, and undertake to make for another, and deceive him to whom they had promised they would quickly do it? do they not often say: Today I do it, today I finish it? Then in the very stitching do they not commit such frauds? They do these things, and say these things; but they themselves are evil, not the craft which they profess. All evil craftsmen, then, not fearing God, whether for gain, or for fear of loss or want, lie, perjure; there is not continual praise of God in them. How, then, do you call me back from trading? Or that I should be a farmer, and murmur against God when he thunders, that, fearing the hail, I consult a diviner, that I seek what I may do against the sky; that I wish hunger on the poor, that I may be able to sell what I have kept? to this do you lead me? But the good farmers, you say, do not do these things. Nor those things the good traders. For what, is it also an evil to have children, because, when their head aches, evil and faithless mothers seek sacrilegious bindings and incantations? These are the sins of men, not of things. The trader can say this to me. Seek, then, O Bishop, how you may understand the tradings which you read in the Psalter; lest perhaps you do not understand, and forbid me trading. Admonish me, then, how I should live: if well, it will be well with me; yet one thing I know, that if I am evil, it is not the trading that does it to me, but my own iniquity. When the truth is spoken, there is nothing to gainsay.

**18.** Let us ask, then, what tradings he meant, which whoever truly has not known, all the day long praises God. Trading in the Greek tongue too is named from action, and in the Latin from negated leisure (*negotium*, from *negato otio*): but whether from action, or from negated leisure, let it be examined what it is. For those active traders, as though presuming from that which they do, praise their own works, and do not come to the grace of God. They are, then, against that grace, traders, which this psalm commends. For it commends that grace, that no one glory of his own works. As in a certain place it is said, *The physicians will not raise them up*[^62]; ought men to forsake medicine? But what is this? Under this name are understood the proud, promising salvation to men, when salvation is the Lord's. As, then, against the physicians, that is, the proud promisers of salvation, this watches which was said: *All the day long your salvation*: so against the traders, that is, those who rejoice in their own action and works, this watches which was said: *My mouth will tell your righteousness,* that is, not mine. Who are the traders, that is, as it were pleasing themselves about their own action? Those who, being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, were not subject to the righteousness of God. Deservedly it was called *negotium* too, because it negates leisure. How great an evil is it, that negates leisure? Deservedly the Lord cast out of the temple those to whom he said: *It is written, My house will be called a house of prayer; but you have made it a house of trading*[^63]; that is, glorying of your own works, not seeking leisure, nor hearing Scripture saying against your restlessness and trading: *Be at leisure, and see that I am the Lord*[^64]. What is, *Be at leisure, and see that I am the Lord;* except, that you may know that it is God who works in you, and that you not exalt yourselves of your own works? Do you not hear the voice of him saying: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, that I am meek and humble of heart; and you will find rest for your souls*[^65]? This rest is preached against the traders, this rest is preached against those who hate leisure, by acting and boasting of their own works, so that they do not rest in God; and they leap back so much the farther from grace, the more they exalt themselves of their own works.

**19.** But in some copies it is: *Because I have not known letters.*[^66] Where other codices have, *trading;* there others, *letters*: how they agree is difficult to find; and yet the diversity of the translators perhaps shows the sense, does not introduce an error. Let us ask, then, also how we may understand *letters,* lest we offend the grammarians as, a little before, the traders: because the grammarian too can live honestly in his craft; not perjure, not lie. Let us ask, then, what letters this one has not known, in whose mouth is the praise of God all the day long. There is a certain letter-learning of the Jews: for to them let us refer this too; there we shall find what was said. As, when we asked about the traders, on account of their action and works, we found that trading to be called detestable, which the Apostle noted, saying: *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they were not subject to the righteousness of God*[^67]. Against which the same Apostle says: *Not of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up.* How, then? Shall we not work good? We shall work it; but with him himself working in us: *For we are his handiwork, created in Christ Jesus unto good works*[^68]. As, then, we found this against the traders, that is, those glorying of their action, exalting themselves about the business that negates leisure, restless rather than good workers; because the good workers are those in whom God works: so we find a certain letter-learning, I know not what, among the Jews. Let the Lord be present that I may unfold in words what he deigned to give me to behold in heart. The pride of the Jews, presuming as it were on their own powers and on the righteousness of their works, gloried in the Law, that they themselves had received the Law, the other nations had not received it; in which Law they gloried not of grace, but of the letter. For the Law without grace is the letter alone: it remains to convict iniquity, not to give salvation. For what does the Apostle say? *For if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would altogether be from the law: but Scripture shut up all under sin, that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those believing*[^69]. Of this letter he says in another place: *The letter kills; but the spirit gives life*[^70]. You have the letter, if you are a transgressor of the law: *You who through the letter,* he says, *and circumcision are a transgressor of the law*[^71]. Is it not well sung and said: *Rescue me from the hand of the transgressor of the law and the unjust*[^72]? You have the letter; but you do not fulfill the letter. Whence do you not fulfill the letter? Since *you who preach not to steal, steal; you who say not to commit adultery, commit adultery; you who abominate idols, commit sacrilege. For the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you, as it is written*[^73]. What, then, did the letter profit you, which you do not fulfill? But why do you not fulfill it? Because you presume on yourself. Why do you not fulfill it? Because you are a trader; you exalt your own works: you do not know that the grace of the helper is necessary, that the precept of the commander may be fulfilled. Behold, God commanded; do what he commanded. You begin to do it as it were from your own powers, and you fall; and the letter remains over you punishing, not saving. Deservedly *the Law was given through Moses; Grace and truth came about through Jesus Christ*[^74]. Moses wrote five books: but in the five porticoes surrounding the pool the sick lay; but they could not be cured. See how the letter remains, convicting the guilty, not saving the unjust. For in those five porticoes, in the figure of the five books, the sick were betrayed rather than healed. What, then, healed the sick man there? The movement of the water. When the pool was moved, the sick man went down, and one was healed, because of unity: whoever else went down to that movement was not healed. How was the unity of the body commended, crying out from the ends of the earth? Another was not healed, unless the pool was again moved. So the movement of the pool signified the disturbance of the people of the Jews, when the Lord Jesus Christ came. For by the coming of an angel the water in the pool was understood to be moved. The water, then, surrounded by five porticoes, was the Jewish nation surrounded by the Law. And in the porticoes the sick lay, and only in the troubled and moved water were they healed. The Lord came, the water was troubled, he was crucified; let the sick man go down that he may be healed. What is, let him go down? Let him humble himself. Therefore whoever of you love the letter without grace, you will remain in the porticoes; you will be sick, lying down, not recovering: for you presume on the letter. *For if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would altogether be from the law*[^75]. But the law was given, that it might make you guilty, that, made guilty, you might fear, that, fearing, you might ask pardon, that you might no longer presume on your own powers, and not exalt yourself about the letter. For there is a figure too in this, that Elisha first sent a staff by a servant to raise the dead. The son of his hostess widow was dead; it was announced to him, he gave his staff to his servant: *Go,* he says, *set it upon the dead one*[^76]. Did the Prophet not know what he was doing? The servant went ahead, set the staff upon the dead one, the dead one did not rise. *For if a law had been given which could give life, truly righteousness would be from the law.* The law sent by a servant did not give life; and yet he sent his staff by a servant, who afterward, following, gave life. For when that boy had not risen, Elisha himself came, now bearing the figure of the Lord, who had sent his servant ahead with the staff, as with the Law; he came to the dead one lying, he set his own members upon him. That one was a boy; he was a grown man: he in a manner contracted and shortened the greatness of his manhood, made a little one to fit the dead one. Therefore the dead one rose, when the living one fitted himself to the dead one: and the Lord did what the staff did not do; grace did what the letter did not do. Those, then, who have remained in the staff, glory in the letter, and therefore are not given life. But I wish to glory of your grace. *But far be it from me,* says the Apostle, *to glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^77], except in him who fitted himself, living, to me dead, that I might rise again; that now I might not live, but Christ live in me. Glorying in that very grace, *I have not known letters*: that is, men presuming on the letter, and leaping back from grace, I have reprobated with my whole heart.

**20.** [v. 16] Deservedly there follows: *I will enter into the power of the Lord*[^78]: not mine, but *the Lord's.* For they in their own power gloried of the letter; therefore they did not know the grace joined to the letter. For *the Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came about through Jesus Christ.* For he came that he might fulfill the Law, when he gave love, through which the Law could be fulfilled. *For the fullness of the Law is love*[^79]. They, not having love, that is, not having the Spirit of grace, *because the love of God is poured out in our hearts, through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^80], remained glorying in the letter. But because *the letter kills; but the spirit gives life; I have not known letters,* and *I will enter into the power of the Lord.* Therefore this following verse confirms and perfects the sentence, so as to fix it in the hearts of men, and not permit any other understanding at all to creep in from any side. *O Lord, I will remember your righteousness alone.* O *alone*! What did he add, *alone*? I ask you. It would suffice, *I will remember your righteousness. Alone,* he says, altogether: there I do not think of mine: *For what have you that you did not receive? But if you have received, why do you glory as though you had not received*[^81]? Your righteousness alone frees me; mine alone are nothing but sins. Let me not, then, glory of my own powers; let me not remain in the letter: let me reprobate letters, that is, men glorying of the letter, and presuming perversely, like frenzied men, on their own powers; let me reprobate such, let me enter into the power of the Lord, that when I am weak, then I may be strong; that you may be strong in me, since *I will remember your righteousness alone.*
