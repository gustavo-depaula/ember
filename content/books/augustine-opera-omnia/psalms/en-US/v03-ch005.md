# Exposition of Psalm 55

**1.** [v. 1] As when about to enter some house, we look at the title to see whose it is and to whom it belongs, lest perhaps we rush in unseasonably where we ought not, or again be called back by timidity from that into which we ought to enter: so, as if we were reading, "This is the estate of so-and-so," we have inscribed over the lintel of this psalm: *Unto the end, for the people who were made far from the saints, for David himself, for the inscription of a title, when the foreigners held him in Gath.*[^1] Let us recognize, then, the people *who were made far from the saints, for the inscription of a title.* For this pertains to David himself, whom you now know to understand spiritually: for none is commended to us but the one of whom it was said: *Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^2]. So when you hear *Unto the end*, look toward Christ, lest by remaining on the road you fail to reach the end. For whatever it is, wherever you stand below, before you reach Christ, the divine word says to you nothing else but: Come on, this is not yet the place where there is security. There is a certain place where the most faithful station is set; there is a certain rock where the secure house rises up, so that it does not fear the rain of the storm. For the floods beat against that house, and it did not fall; for it was founded upon the rock: *And the rock was Christ*[^3]. Under the name of David, Christ is figured; because of him it was said: *who was made of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^4].

**2.** Who, then, is the people *who were made far from the saints, for the inscription of a title*? Let the title itself show us this people. For a certain title was written in the Lord's passion, when the Lord was crucified: there was a title inscribed there, in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, *King of the Jews*; the title approved by three tongues as by three witnesses, because in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word will stand. When the Jews had read this title, they were indignant, and said to Pilate: *Do not write, King of the Jews; but that he himself said he was king of the Jews.* Write that he himself said it, they said; not that it is what he said. But because it is true in another psalm, *For the inscription of a title, do not corrupt*[^5], Pilate answered: *What I have written, I have written*[^6]: as though saying, I do not corrupt the truth, even if you love falsehood. Since, then, in that curse the Jews were indignant, saying: *We have no king but Caesar alone*[^7], by the offense of the title they were made far from the saints. Let those draw near to the saints, and be joined to the Holy One, who recognize Christ as king and desire to have him: let those be made far from the saints who, contradicting the title, rejected God as king, and chose a man as king. So every people delighted by a human kingdom, rejecting the Lord's reign in itself—under whose reign each one is so made subject that he himself reigns over his own desires—every such people, then, is far from the saints. So do not, brethren, note this in the Jews alone. In them indeed were given, as it were, first examples, so that in that people might shine forth what every man should beware of. Openly they refused Christ as king, and chose Caesar as king. Caesar indeed is a king, a man to men for human things, but there is another king for divine things; one king for the temporal life, another for the eternal; one earthly king, another heavenly king: the earthly king under the heavenly king, the heavenly king over all things. Not, then, because they said they had Caesar as king did they sin; but because they were unwilling to have Christ as king. And now many are unwilling to have Christ as king, sitting in heaven and reigning everywhere: and these are the ones who trouble us. Against such this psalm strengthens us. For we must suffer such men unto the end: whom we would not suffer, unless it were expedient for us. For every temptation is a testing, and the effect of every testing has its own fruit. For a man is very often unknown even to himself: what he can bear, or what he cannot bear, he does not know; and sometimes he presumes that he can bear what he cannot, and sometimes despairs that he can bear what he can: temptation comes, as it were a questioning, and the man is found out by himself; because he lay hidden even from himself, but did not lie hidden from his maker. Accordingly Peter presumed something, I know not what, that was not yet in him, that he would persevere with the Lord Jesus Christ unto death: Peter did not know his own strength, but the Lord knew it. He who had fashioned him, who was also going to give to the thing fashioned by him the fitting strength, answered that he was less fit; he knew what he had not yet given; the one who had not yet received it did not know: temptation came; he denied, he wept, he received. Since, then, we do not know what we ask, as not having, and from what we should give thanks, as receiving, it is necessary always to be instructed by temptations and tribulations in this age; but we cannot be troubled except by those who are made far from the saints. This farness, brethren, understand to be of the heart, not of the body. For it very often happens that one who sojourns far from you in body is joined to you, because he loves what you love: and it very often happens that one standing beside you, because he loves the world, while you love God, is far from you.

**3.** What, then, does it mean, which still pertains to the title itself, that *the foreigners held him in Gath*? Gath was a certain city of the foreigners, that is, of the aliens, a people far from the saints. For from the fact that they are aliens, they certainly do not draw near to the saints, but are far from the saints. All who refuse Christ as king become aliens. Why do they become aliens? Because that vine too, though planted by him, when it became bitter, what did it hear? *Why have you turned into bitterness, alien vine?*[^8] It did not say: My vine: because if mine, sweet; if bitter, not mine; if not mine, certainly alien. *The foreigners*, then, *held him in Gath.* We find indeed, brethren, that David himself, son of Jesse, king of Israel, sojourned among the foreigners, when he was sought by Saul; and he was in that city and with the king of that city: but we do not read that he was held there. So our David, the Lord Jesus Christ, born of the seed of that David, the foreigners not only held but still hold in Gath. We said that Gath is a city. But the interpretation of this name, when asked, indicates "Winepress." Christ, according as he is the head, the Savior of the body—he who, born of a virgin, was crucified, who has now shown us the example of our resurrection in the resurrection of his flesh, who sits at the right hand of the Father and intercedes for us—is here too, but in his body, which is the Church. The body is joined to its head, the head cries out for the body: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^9] And the body is in its head, according to the Apostle, who says: *And he raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places*[^10]. And we sit there, and he himself labors here: we sit there according to hope, and he is here with us according to love. This conjoining, as of one man, makes two in one flesh, bridegroom and bride. Hence the Lord himself too says: *Therefore now they are no longer two, but one flesh*[^11]. How, then, is he held here in Gath? His body, that is, his Church, is held in the winepress. What is it, in the winepress? In pressures. But in the winepress the pressure is fruitful. The grape on the vine feels no pressure, it seems whole, but nothing flows from it: it is put into the winepress, it is trodden, it is pressed; injury seems to be done to the grape, but this injury is not barren; indeed, if no injury were applied, it would remain barren.

**4.** [v. 2] Whatever saints, then, suffer pressure from those who were made far from the saints, let them attend to this psalm, recognize themselves here, say what is said here, they who suffer what is said here. Surely let him who does not suffer not say: I do not bind myself to a voice, whom I see outside suffering. But let him see lest, while he wishes to be far from suffering, he be made far from the saints. Let each one, then, think of his enemy; if he is a Christian, the world itself is his enemy. Let no one, then, think of private enmities, who is about to hear the words of this psalm: let us know that our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers, and the spiritual forces of wickedness, that is, against the devil and his angels; because even when we suffer troublesome men, he instigates, he inflames, he moves them as his vessels. Let us attend, then, to two enemies: the one we see, and the one we do not see: we see the man, we do not see the devil; let us love the man, beware of the devil; let us pray for the man, pray against the devil, and say to God: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for man has trodden me down.* Do not fear, because man has trodden you down; have wine ready, you have been made a grape so that you may be trodden. *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for man has trodden me down: warring all the day long he has troubled me*—everyone who was made far from the saints. But why should not the devil himself too be understood here? Or perhaps because he was not called "man"? Then the Gospel errs, because it said: *An enemy, a man, did this*[^12]. But under a certain figure he too can be called a man, though not be a man. So whether the one who said these things had in view that devil himself, or the people and each one made far from the saints, through whom the devil troubles the people of God clinging to the saints, clinging to the Holy One, clinging to the king, at whose title those men, indignant, as if struck back, were made far—let him say: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for man has trodden me down*: and let him not fail in this treading down, knowing whom he calls upon, and by what example he has been made strong. The first cluster pressed in the winepress was Christ. When that cluster was pressed out by suffering, there flowed that whence *how glorious is the inebriating cup!*[^13] Let his body too, then, say, gazing on its head: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for man has trodden me down: warring all the day long he has troubled me. All the day long*, all the time. Let no one say to himself: There were tribulations among our fathers, with us there are none. If you think you have no tribulations, you have not yet begun to be a Christian. And where is the voice of the Apostle: *But all who wish to live godly in Christ will suffer persecution*[^14]? If, then, you suffer no persecution for Christ, see whether you have not yet begun to live godly in Christ. But when you have begun to live godly in Christ, you have entered the winepress; prepare yourself for pressures; but do not be dry, lest nothing come out of the pressure.

**5.** [vv. 3-4] *My enemies have trodden me down all the day long.*[^15] Those made far from the saints, they are my enemies. *All the day long*: it has already been said. *From the height of the day.* What does this mean: *From the height of the day*? Perhaps it is high for the understanding. And no wonder, because it is the height of the day. For perhaps they were made far from the saints for this reason, that they could not penetrate the height of the day, whose twelve hours are the shining Apostles. So those who crucified him as a man erred in the day. But why did they suffer darkness, so as to be made far from the saints? Because the day was shining on high, and they did not recognize him lying hidden on high: for if they had recognized him, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. Struck back, then, by this height of the day, and made far from the saints, they became enemies who trouble and tread down, as a grape in the winepress. There is also another understanding: *From the height of the day my enemies have trodden me down all the day long*, that is, all the time: *From the height of the day*, that is, from temporal pride. For when they tread down, they are on high: those who are trodden down are humble, those who tread down are on high. But do not fear the height of those who tread down; it is the height of the day, it is temporal, not eternal.

**6.** *For the many who war against me will fear.*[^16] When will they fear? When the day on which they are on high has passed. For they are on high for a time; when the time of their height is ended, they will fear. *But I will hope in you, O Lord.* He does not say: But I will not fear; but: *The many who war against me will fear.* When that day of judgment comes, then all the tribes of the earth will lament themselves. When the sign of the Son of man appears in heaven, then all the saints will be secure. For that will come which they hoped for, which they desired, which they prayed might come: but for those others no place of repenting will remain, because at the time when repentance could be fruitful, they hardened their heart against the admonishing Lord. Surely they will not also raise a wall against the judging God? Recognize indeed this man's godliness, and, if you are in that body, imitate it. When he had said: *The many who war against me will fear*, he did not reply: But I will not fear; lest, by assigning to his own strength the fact that he does not fear, he too should be among the temporal heights, and through temporal pride should not deserve to come to eternal rest: rather he made you understand whence he will not fear; *But I*, he says, *will hope in you, O Lord*: he did not state his presumption, but the cause of his presumption. For if I will not fear, I can also fail to fear through hardness of heart; for many fear nothing through excessive pride. Let Your Charity attend: one thing is health of body, another is numbness of body, another is immortality of body. Perfect health, indeed, is immortality; but there is also called health, in a certain manner, that which we have in this life. When one is not ill, each is called healthy; and when the physician has examined him, he pronounces him healthy; and when one has begun to be sick, that very health is disturbed; and when he is cured, it returns to that health. Notice, then, and consider three certain conditions of the body: health, numbness, immortality. Health has no sickness; but yet when it is touched and troubled, it feels pain. But numbness feels no pain; it has lost the sense of pain, the more insensible, the worse. Again, immortality feels no pain; for all corruption is consumed, and this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality. There is, then, no pain in the immortal body, no pain in the numb body. Let not the numb man think himself already immortal: nearer to immortality is the health of one who feels pain than the numbness of one who does not feel. You find, then, a proud man, most arrogant in his swelling, who has persuaded himself that he fears nothing: do you think him stronger than the one who says: *Fights outside, fears within*[^17]—stronger than our very head, the Lord our God, who said: *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^18]? This man is not stronger: let not his numbness delight you; he is not clothed with immortality, but stripped of feeling. But do you have a soul not without affection, for those who are without affection are reproached, and say from the sense of health: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I do not burn?*[^19] If the scandal, the perdition of any weak person, did not pertain to this man, would he, as rigid and without pain, seem better? Far from it: it would be numbness, not tranquility. Plainly, brethren, when we have come to that place, to that seat, to that blessedness, to the heavenly homeland, where our soul is filled with security, filled with quiet and everlasting happiness, there will be no pain there: because there will be nothing to feel pain over. *The many*, he says, *who war against me will fear.* And those very numb ones who now fear nothing will fear at some time: for so great a terror will come as will break and crush all hardness. *The many who war against me will fear. But I will hope in you, O Lord.*

**7.** [v. 5] *In God I will praise my words, in God I have hoped: I will not fear what flesh may do to me.*[^20] Why? Because in God I will hope. Why? Because in God I will praise my words. If in yourself you praise your words, I do not say that you will not fear; it is impossible that you should not fear. For you will either have your words as lying, and therefore yours because lying: or, if your words are truthful, and you think you have them not from God, but speak them from yourself; they will be truthful, but you will be a liar: but if you have recognized that you can say nothing true in the wisdom of God, in the faith of the truth, except what you have received from him, of whom it is said: *For what have you that you did not receive?*[^21]—then in God you praise your words, so that in God you may be praised by the words of God. For if what is of God in you is honored by you; you too, made by God, will be honored in God: but if you honor what is of God in you as your own, not God's; then, as that people was made far from the saints, so are you far from the Holy One. Therefore: *In God I will praise my words*: if *in God*, why *my*? Both *in God*, and *my. In God*, because from him: *my*, because I have received them. He himself willed them to be mine, who gave them, by loving him whose they are: because they are mine from him, they have been made mine. For whence is it: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^22]? How *our*? How *give*? By asking it from him you will not be empty, by confessing it yours you will not be ungrateful. For if you do not say it is yours, you have not received it: again, if you say it is yours as though what you call yours were from yourself, you lose what you had received, because you are ungrateful to the one from whom you had received it. *In God*, then, *I will praise my words*, because there he himself is the fountain of true words: *my*, because thirsting I came and drank. *In God I will praise my words, in God I have hoped: I will not fear what flesh may do to me.* Were you not the one who a little before was saying: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for man has trodden me down: warring all the day long he has troubled me?* How, then, here: *I will not fear what flesh may do to me*? What will it do to you? You yourself a little before said: *He has trodden me down, he has troubled me*: will he do nothing when he does these things? He looked to the wine that flows from the treading, and answered: Plainly he has trodden me down, plainly he has troubled me; but what will he do to me? I was a grape, I will be wine: *In God I have hoped, I will not fear what flesh may do to me.*

**8.** [v. 6] *All the day long they abominated my words.*[^23] So it is, you know. Speak the truth, preach the truth, announce Christ to the pagans, announce the Church to the heretics, announce salvation to all: they contradict, they abominate my words. But when they abominate my words, whom do we think they abominate, except him in whom *I will praise my words? All the day long they abominated my words.* Let it at least suffice that they abominate the words, that they go no further, that they reprehend, that they reject. Far from it. Why do I say this? When they reject the words, when they abominate the words which flow from the fountain of truth, what will they do to the one through whom those very words are spoken? What, except what follows: *All their counsels against me are for evil*? If they abominate the very bread, how do they spare the vessel in which it is served? *All their counsels against me are for evil.* If so even against the Lord himself, let not the body, then, disdain what went before in the head, so that the body may cleave to the head. Your Lord was despised, and do you wish to be honored by those who were made far from the saints? Do not arrogate to yourself the wish for what did not go before in him. The disciple is not greater than his master, the servant is not greater than his lord. If they have called the master of the house Beelzebub, how much more those of his household? *All their counsels against me are for evil.*

**9.** [v. 7] *They will sojourn, and hide themselves.*[^24] To sojourn is to be a pilgrim. Those are called sojourners who dwell in a country not their own. Every man in this life is a pilgrim: in which life you see that we are covered round with flesh, through which flesh the heart cannot be seen. Therefore the Apostle says: *Do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, and will illuminate the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart, and then each will have praise from God*[^25]. Before this comes about, in this pilgrimage of carnal life each carries his own heart, and every heart is closed to every heart. Accordingly those whose counsels against this man are for evil, *will sojourn, and hide themselves*; because they are in this pilgrimage and carry flesh, they cover deceit in the heart, they hide whatever evil they think. Why? Because this life is still a pilgrimage. Let them hide; what they hide will appear, and they themselves will not be hidden. There is also in this hiding another understanding, which perhaps will please more. For from among those who were made far from the saints, certain feigned ones creep in, and cause worse tribulations to the body of Christ, because they are not avoided as utterly alien. Mentioning the graver dangers from these, the Apostle, when he enumerated his many sufferings, and said: *In perils of rivers, in perils of robbers, in perils from my own race, in perils from the Gentiles, in perils in the city, in perils in the desert, in perils in the sea: In perils*, he says, *among false brethren*[^26]. These are exceedingly dangerous, of whom it is said in another psalm: *And they entered to see*[^27]. They entered to see, and no one says: Do not enter to see. For he enters as one of yours, he is not guarded against as alien. These, then, *will sojourn, and hide themselves.* Because they so enter the great house, not going to persevere there; therefore *they will sojourn.* For wishing such sinners to be understood as servants, according to that evangelical understanding by which *everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*, the Lord says: *The servant does not abide in the house forever, but the son abides forever*[^28]. He who enters as a son will not merely sojourn, because he will persevere unto the end: he who enters as a servant, crafty, a sinner, attending to the eye; seeking what to snatch, seeking what to accuse or what to disparage, enters to sojourn, not to dwell and persevere. Yet let us not fear these either, brethren: *In God I have hoped, I will not fear what flesh may do to me.* Even if they sojourn, even if they enter, even if they feign, even if they hide themselves, they are flesh: do you hope in the Lord, flesh will do nothing to you. But it brings tribulation, it brings treading down. The wine comes, because the grape is pressed. Your tribulation will not be unfruitful: another sees you, imitates you; because you too, to learn to bear such a one, contemplated your head, that first cluster, to whom the man entered to see, sojourned, and hid himself—the traitor Judas. So do not fear all who enter with feigned mind, sojourning and hiding themselves. Their father Judas was with your Lord, and he indeed knew him: although Judas the traitor sojourned and hid himself, yet his heart lay open to the Lord of all things; he, knowing it, chose one, whence he might make consolation for you who would not know whom to avoid. For he could have not chosen Judas, because he knew Judas; for he says to the disciples: *Have I not chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil?*[^29] Was the devil, then, also chosen? Or if he was not chosen, how did he choose twelve, and not rather eleven? He too was chosen, but for something else. The eleven were chosen for the work of proving, the one was chosen for the work of tempting. Whence could he give you an example, who will not know whom to avoid as wicked, whom to beware as false and feigned, sojourning and hiding themselves, except that he should say to you: Behold, I myself had one of them with me? An example went before, I bore with him, I willed to endure what I knew, that I might furnish consolation to you who do not know. What he did to me, will he not do to you too? Though he may have much power, though he may rage much, he will accuse, he will speak false charges. That falsehoods may prevail—will they prevail against you, and did they not prevail against me? Against me they certainly prevailed, but they did not take heaven from me. His flesh, already buried, endured false witnesses: it was too little to endure them in judgment, he endured them in the tomb. They received money to lie, they said: *While we were sleeping, his disciples came, and took him away*[^30]. But the Jews were so blind as to believe a saying utterly incredible: they believed sleeping witnesses! Either it was false that they slept, and they ought not to have believed liars: or it was true that they slept, and they did not know what was done. *They will sojourn, and hide themselves.* Let them sojourn, and let them hide themselves: what will they do? *In God I have hoped, I will not fear what flesh may do to me.*

**10.** *They will watch my heel.*[^31] For they will so sojourn and hide themselves as to watch where a man slips. They are intent on the heel, watching when a fall happens, so as to hold the foot for a fall, or to set a foot for a stumble, or at least to find what to accuse. And who walks so as never to slip? Surely that which quickly happens even on the tongue. For it is written: *If anyone does not offend in the tongue, he is a perfect man*[^32]. Who at last would dare to call or think himself perfect? Therefore it is necessary that someone slip in the tongue. But those who will sojourn and hide themselves catch at all words, seeking somewhere to make snares and knotty slanders, by which they themselves are first entangled before those whom they strive to entangle; so that they themselves are first caught and perish, before they catch others to destroy them. For a man runs back into his own heart, and from there runs back to God, and knows how to say: *In God I will praise my words.* Whatever good I have said, whatever true I have said, I have said of God, and said about God: whatever else perhaps I have said that I ought not to have said, I have said as a man, but said under God. He who strengthens the one walking, threatens the one erring, pardons the one acknowledging, calls back the tongue, calls back the slip. For the just man will fall seven times and rise again, but the ungodly will be weakened in evils. Let none of us, then, fear cunning persecutors, catchers of words, counters almost of syllables, and transgressors of the precepts. He attends to what he may charge against you; he does not attend to believing in Christ through you. Attend to the words of him whom you reprehend, lest perhaps he teach you something wholesomely. And what, he says, will he be able to teach me wholesomely, who has so slipped in a word? This very thing perhaps he teaches you wholesomely, that you should not be a catcher of words, but a gatherer of precepts. *They will watch my heel.*

**11.** *As my soul has endured.*[^33] This I say, that I have endured. He spoke as one who had experienced it: *As my soul has endured. They will sojourn and hide themselves.* Let my soul endure all of them; let it endure those barking outside, those hiding within. Temptation, coming from outside, comes as it were a river: let it find you on the rock, let it beat against you, not cast you down; the house is founded upon the rock. It is within, it will sojourn and hide itself: that the chaff may be near you, let the treading of oxen come in, let the threshing-sledge of temptations come in; you are purged, it is broken to pieces.

**12.** [v. 8] *As my soul has endured, for nothing you will save them.*[^34] He has taught us to pray for them too. To be sure, *they will sojourn and hide themselves*, to be sure they are deceitful, to be sure they are dissemblers and liers-in-wait: do you pray for them, and do not say: Surely God is not going to correct even such a man, so wicked, so perverse? Do not despair: attend to whom you ask, not for whom you ask. You see the greatness of the disease, do you not see the power of the physician? *They will sojourn and hide themselves: as my soul has endured.* Endure, pray: and what comes about? *For nothing you will save them.* You will so save them that it is nothing to you, that is, that it is no labor to you. To men they are despaired of, but you cure them with a word: you will not labor in curing, although we are amazed in beholding it. There is another sense in this verse, *For nothing you will save them*: with no merits of theirs going before, you will save them. *I who was formerly a blasphemer*, he says, *and a persecutor and insolent*[^35]. He received letters from the priests, that wherever he found Christians, he might bind them and bring them in. Surely, to bind them and bring them in, he first sojourned and hid himself. No good merits of his, then, went before; rather, such things went before as he would be condemned for; he brought nothing good, and was saved. *For nothing you will save them.* They will not bring you goats, rams, bulls, they will not bring gifts and spices into your temple, they do not pour over you anything from a good conscience as a libation; in them all is harsh, all foul, all detestable: and since they bring to you nothing whereby they may be saved, *For nothing you will save them*, that is, your grace given freely. What had that thief brought to the cross? From the gallows to judgment, from judgment to the wood, from the wood to paradise. He believed, for which reason he spoke. But the faith itself, who gave it, except he who hung beside him? *For nothing you will save them.*

**13.** *In anger you will lead down the peoples.*[^36] You are angry and you lead down, you rage and you save, you terrify and you call. For what is: *In anger you will lead down the peoples*? You fill all things with tribulations, so that men, set in tribulations, may run back to you, lest they be seduced by delights and a perverse security. From you it seems to be anger, but fatherly anger. The father is angry with a son who despises his precepts: angry with him, he buffets him, beats him, pulls his ear, drags him by the hand, leads him to school. *In anger you will lead down the peoples.* How many have entered, how many have filled the house of the Lord, led down in his anger, that is, terrified by tribulations, and filled with faith! For to this end tribulation drives, that it may empty out the vessel that is full of wickedness, so that it may be filled with grace. *In anger you will lead down the peoples.*

**14.** [v. 9] *O God, I have declared my life to you.*[^37] For you made me to live, and for this I declare my life to you. Did God, then, not know what he had given? What is it that you declare to him? Do you wish to teach God? Far from it. Why, then, does he say: *I have declared to you*? Or perhaps: Because it profits you that I have declared my life? And how does it profit God? It profits the gains of God. I have declared my life to God, because God made me to live. As the apostle Paul declared his life, saying: *I who was formerly a blasphemer and a persecutor and insolent!* Let him declare his life: *But I obtained mercy*[^38]. He declared his life, not to himself, but to him: because he so declared it that he might be believed; not for his own gains, but for his gains. For what does Paul himself say? *Therefore Christ died and rose again, that he who lives may now live no longer to himself, but to him who died for all*[^39]. If, then, you live, and do not live of yourself, because he furnished that you should live, declare your life, not to yourself, but to him; not seeking your own, not living to yourself, but to him who died for all. For of certain reprobates what does the same Apostle say? *For all seek their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's*[^40]. If you declare your life so that it may profit you, and not profit others, you declare it to yourself, not to God: but if you so declare your life that you also invite others to receive the life that you too have received, you declare your life to him from whom you received it, and you will have a fuller reward, because even from what you received you have not proved ungrateful. *O God, I have declared my life to you. You have placed my tears in your sight.* You have heard me beseeching you. *As also in your promise.* Because you had so promised, you did this. You said you would hear the weeping one: I believed, I wept, I was heard; I found you merciful in promising, truthful in repaying. *As also in your promise.*

**15.** [v. 10] *Let my enemies be turned backward.*[^41] This profits them, he does not wish them ill: for they wish to go before, therefore they are unwilling to be corrected. You admonish your enemy to live well, to correct himself: he despises, he rejects your word: Look at the one who admonishes me, look at the one from whom I am to hear precepts to live by! He wishes to go before you, and by going before is not corrected. He does not attend to the fact that your words are not yours; he does not attend to the fact that you declare your life to God, not to yourself. By going before, then, he is not corrected: it is good for him to be turned backward, and to follow the one he wished to go before. The Lord was speaking to his disciples about his coming passion; Peter shuddered, and said: *Far be it, Lord, this shall not happen*: he who a little before had said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*, having confessed him God, feared lest he die as a man. But the Lord, who had so come as to suffer—for neither could we be saved unless we were redeemed by his blood—a little before had praised Peter's confession, and had said: *For flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but my Father who is in heaven. Therefore you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell will not prevail against it; and to you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven.* See how he carried forward the true confession, godly, full of confidence, because he said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.* But at once, when the Lord began to speak of his passion, that one feared lest he perish by dying, when we ourselves would perish unless he died; and said: *Far be it, Lord, this shall not happen.* And the Lord to him, to whom a little before he had said: *You are blessed, and upon this rock I will build my Church: Get behind me*, he says, *Satan, you are a scandal to me*: Why, then, "Satan," who a little before was "blessed" and "rock"? *For you do not relish the things that are mine*, he says, *but the things that are of man*[^42]. A little before, the things of God: *because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* When in God he praised the word, not Satan, but Peter from the rock (*petra*): but when from himself, and out of human weakness, out of a carnal love of the man, that which would be a hindrance to his salvation and that of others, he was called Satan. Why? Because he wished to go before the Lord, and to give earthly counsel to the heavenly Leader. *Far be it, Lord, this shall not happen.* You say: *Far be it*; and you say: *Lord*: surely if he is Lord, he acts by power; if he is master, he knows what he does, he knows what he teaches. But you wish to lead the leader, to teach the master, to command the Lord, to dictate to God: you go too far before, *get behind.* Surely this profited even those enemies? *Let my enemies be turned backward*: but let them not remain backward. Let them be turned backward for this reason, that they should not go before; but that they should follow, not that they should remain. *Let my enemies be turned backward.*

**16.** *On whatever day I call upon you, behold, I have known that you are my God.*[^43] Great knowledge! He does not say: I have known that you are God; but, *that you are my God.* For he is yours, when he comes to your aid; he is yours, when you are not alien from him. Hence it is said: *Blessed is the people whose Lord is its God.* Why, *whose is*? for whose is he not? He is indeed the God of all: but he is properly called the God of those who love him, who hold him, who possess him, who worship him; as if of his own household, they are his great family, redeemed by the great blood of his only Son. How much God has given us, that we might be his and he might be ours! But the aliens, made far from the saints, are alien sons. See what is said of them in another psalm: *O Lord, free me*, he says, *from the hand of alien sons, whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* And see their height, but the height of the day, that is, temporal pride. *Whose sons*, he says, *are like new plants established, their daughters adorned like the likeness of a temple.* He describes the happiness of the present age, in which men erring, and holding it for a great thing, do not seek the true and everlasting happiness. From this, then, these are alien sons, not sons of God: *Whose sons*, he says, *are like new plants established, their daughters adorned like the likeness of a temple: their storehouses full, overflowing from this into that: their oxen fat, their sheep fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth: there is no breach of the wall nor passage, nor cry in their streets.* And what follows? *They called the people blessed that has these things.* But who said this? Alien sons, *whose mouth has spoken vanity.* You, what do you say? *Blessed is the people whose Lord is its God*[^44]. He took away from the midst all the other things that God gives, and gave God himself. For all those things, brethren, which the alien sons mentioned, God gives; but he gives them also to aliens, gives them also to the wicked, gives them also to blasphemers, he who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust. Sometimes he gives these things to the good, sometimes he does not give them; and to the wicked sometimes he gives, sometimes he does not give: but to the good he reserves himself, and to the wicked eternal fire. There is, then, an evil that he does not give to the good, and there is a good that he does not give to the wicked: there are certain middle things, both good and evil, which he gives both to the good and to the evil.

**17.** Let us, then, love God, brethren, purely and chastely. The heart is not chaste if it worships God for a reward. What then? shall we have no reward from the worship of God? We shall have one indeed, but God himself whom we worship. He himself will be our reward, because we shall see him as he is. Attend to the fact that you obtain a reward. To his lovers what does our Lord Jesus Christ say? *He who loves me keeps my commandments; and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him.* What, then, will you give him? *And I will show myself to him*[^45]. If you do not love, it is too little: if you love, if you sigh, if you worship freely the one by whom you were freely bought—for you had not merited that he should redeem you—if, considering his benefits in you, you sigh, and have your heart restless with desire of him; do not seek from him anything outside of him, he himself suffices you. However greedy you may be, God suffices you. For greed sought to possess the whole earth; add even heaven: greater is he who made heaven and earth. I will say it, brethren: in these human marriages consider what a chaste heart toward God is like. Surely there are human marriages: he does not love his wife who loves her for the sake of the dowry; she does not chastely love her husband who loves him for this reason, that he has given her something, or that he has given her much. The husband is both rich and made poor. How many, proscribed, have been more loved by chaste wives? Many chaste marriages have been proved by the calamities of husbands: lest they should be thought to love anything other than the husband, they not only did not desert them, but obeyed them the more. If, then, the carnal husband is loved freely, if he is loved chastely; and the carnal wife is loved freely, if she is loved chastely: how is God to be loved, the true and truthful husband of the soul, making it fruitful unto the offspring of everlasting life, and not permitting us to be barren? Let us, then, so love him that nothing besides himself be loved; and there comes about in us what we said, what we sang, because here too it is our voice: *On whatever day I call upon you, behold, I have known that you are my God.* This is to call upon God, to call upon him freely. Accordingly of certain men what is said? *They did not call upon the Lord.* As though they seemed to call upon the Lord for themselves; and they asked him for inheritances, for the increase of money, for the prolonging of this life, for the other temporal things: and what does Scripture say of them? *They did not call upon the Lord.* Therefore what follows? *There they feared with fear, where there was no fear*[^46]. What is, *where there was no fear*? Lest money be taken from them, lest something in their house be diminished; finally, lest they have fewer years in this life than they hoped for themselves: but *there they trembled with fear, where there was no fear.* Such were those Jews: *If we let him live, the Romans will come and take from us both our place and our nation*[^47]. *There they feared with fear where there was no fear. Behold, I have known that you are my God.* Great riches of the heart, great light of the inner eye, great confidence of security! *Behold, I have known that you are my God.*

**18.** [v. 11] *In God I will praise the word, in the Lord I will praise the saying: in God I have hoped, I will not fear what man may do to me.*[^48] This is now the same sense that was repeated above.

**19.** [v. 12] *In me, O God, are your vows, which I will repay as praises to you.*[^49] Vow, and repay to the Lord your God. What should you vow, what should you repay? Perhaps those animals that were once offered at the altars? Offer nothing such: in you is what you should vow and repay. From the ark of the heart bring forth the incense of praise, from the storehouse of a good conscience bring forth the sacrifice of faith. Whatever you bring forth, kindle it with love. In you let there be vows, which you may repay as praises to God. For what praise? For what has he furnished you? *Because you have rescued my soul from death.* This is that life which he declares to him: *O God, I have declared my life to you.* For what was I? Dead. Through myself I was dead: through you what am I? Alive. Therefore *in me, O God, are your vows, which I will repay as praises to you.* Behold, I love my God; no one snatches him from me: what I give him no one snatches from me, because it is shut up in the heart. Deservedly is it said in that earlier confidence: *What can man do to me?* Let man rage, let him be permitted to rage, let him be permitted to accomplish what he attempts: what is he going to take away? Gold, silver, cattle, slaves, maidservants, estates, houses; let him take away everything: surely he does not take away the vows that are in me, which I will repay as praises to God? The tempter was permitted to tempt the holy man Job: in one point of time he took away everything, he removed whatever of means he had had, he took away the inheritance, he killed the heirs; and this not gradually, but in a mass, at one stroke, in one onslaught, so that everything was announced at once. With everything taken away, Job alone remained; but in him were the vows of praise which he would repay to God, in him they plainly were: the thief, the devil, had not invaded the ark of his holy breast, he was full of that whence he might sacrifice. Hear what he had, hear what he brought forth: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^50]. O inner riches, where the thief does not come near! God himself had given that whence he received; he himself had enriched him, whence he offered him what he loved. God seeks praise from you, God seeks your confession. But will you give something from your field? He rained that you might have it. Will you give something from the ark? He himself established what you will give. What will you give that you did not receive from him? For what have you that you did not receive? Will you give from the heart? He himself gave faith, hope, and love: this you will bring forth, this you will sacrifice. But plainly all those other things the enemy can take from you against your will; this he cannot take except from one who is willing. Those things one will lose even unwillingly; and, wishing to have gold, will lose gold, and, wishing to have a house, will lose a house: faith no one will lose except one who has spurned it.

**20.** [v. 13] *In me, O God, are your vows, which I will repay as praises to you: because you have rescued my soul from death, my eyes from tears, and my feet from falling; that I may please their God in the light of the living.*[^51] Deservedly he does not please the alien sons made far from the saints, because they do not have the light of the living, whereby they might see what pleases God. The light of the living is the light of the immortals, the light of the saints. He who is not in darkness pleases in the light of the living. A man and the things that are his are looked at; no one knows what sort he is: God sees what sort he is. Sometimes he lies hidden even from the devil himself; unless he tempts, he does not find out, as in the case of this man whom I just mentioned. God knew him, and bore witness to him: the devil did not know him, and therefore had said: *Does Job worship God for nothing?* See to what the enemy provokes: there is perfection. See what the enemy objects. He saw a man serving God, in all things obedient, doing all things well; and because he was rich and had a most happy household, he objects this, that he worships God for this reason, that he gave him all these things: *Does Job worship God for nothing?*[^52] For this was the true light, this the light of the living, that he worshiped God for nothing. God saw in the heart of his servant his free worship. For that heart pleased in the sight of the Lord, in the light of the living: it lay hidden from the devil, because he was in darkness. God admitted the tempter, not that he himself might know what he knew, but that he might furnish it to us to know and to imitate. For if the tempter had not been admitted, would we ourselves see in Job what we ought and would wish to imitate? The tempter was admitted, he took away everything, that man remained alone, bereft of means, alone, bereft of household, alone, bereft of sons, full of God. The wife indeed was left. Do you think the devil merciful, who left him his wife? He knew through whom he had deceived Adam. He had left his own helper, not the consoler of the husband. That man, then, full of God, in whom were the vows that he would repay as praises, that he might show that he worshiped God for nothing, not for this reason that he had received so much; and with everything lost he is such, because he did not lose the one who had given everything: *The Lord gave*, he says: *the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord.* Struck even with a wound from head to foot, yet whole within, he answered the temptress, from the light of the living, from the light of his heart: *You have spoken like one of the foolish women*[^53], that is, like one who does not have the light of the living. For the light of the living is wisdom, and the darkness of the foolish is folly. *You have spoken like one of the foolish women*: you see my flesh, you do not see the light of my heart. For she could then have loved her husband the more, if she had known his inner beauty, and beheld where he was beautiful before the eyes of God: because in him were the vows that he would repay as praises to God. How the enemy had not invaded that patrimony! How whole was what he possessed, and on account of which he hoped to possess more, going to go from virtues to virtue! Therefore, brethren, let all these things avail us for this, that we love God freely, hope in him always, and fear neither man nor devil. Neither the one nor the other does anything, except when it is permitted: it can be permitted only to that which profits us. Let us bear with the wicked, let us be good, because we too were wicked. For nothing God will save all those of whom we dare to despair. Therefore let us despair of no one, let us pray for all whom we suffer, let us never withdraw from God. Let him himself be our patrimony, let him be our hope, let him be our salvation. He himself is here our consoler, there our rewarder, everywhere our life-giver and giver of life; not of another life, but of that of which it was said: *I am the way and the truth and the life*[^54]: so that both here in the light of faith, and there in the light of vision, as in the light of the living, we may please in the sight of the Lord.
