# Exposition of Psalm 58

**1.** [v. 1] As the Scripture of the Psalms is wont to set mysteries in the titles, and to adorn the front of the Psalm with the sublimity of a sacrament, that we may know who are about to enter, when we have read, as it were over the doorpost, what is done within, or whose house it is, or who is the possessor of that estate: so in this psalm too a title was written about a title. For it has, *Unto the end, do not corrupt, for David himself, for the inscription of a title.*[^1] This is what I said, a title about a title. For what the inscription of this title is, which he forbids to be corrupted, the Gospel indicates to us. For when the Lord was being crucified, a title was inscribed by Pilate and set up, *King of the Jews*, in three tongues, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin: which tongues most excel throughout the whole world. Therefore, if the king of the Jews was crucified, and the Jews crucified their king; by crucifying him they made him also king of the Gentiles, rather than killed him. And indeed, as far as was in them, they lost Christ, but to themselves, not to us; and he died for us, and by his blood redeemed us. And now the title is not corrupted: because he is king, not only of the Gentiles, but also of the Jews themselves. For what? Because they contradicted, were they therefore able to overturn the dominion of their king? He is king even over them. For that king carries an iron rod, with which he both rules and breaks. *I*, he says, *have been set up by him as king over Zion his holy mountain, preaching the precept of the Lord. The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth. You will rule them with an iron rod, and like a potter's vessel you will crush them*[^2]. Whom will he rule? whom will he crush? He will rule the obedient, he will crush the resisting. Therefore, *Do not corrupt*, excellently and prophetically: since those Jews too then suggested to Pilate, and said: *Do not write King of the Jews, but* write, *that he himself said he was king of the Jews*[^3]; for that title, they say, has confirmed him as king to us. And Pilate: *What I have written, I have written*[^4]. And it was fulfilled: *Do not corrupt.*

**2.** Nor is this the only psalm to have an inscription of this kind, that the title not be corrupted. Several psalms are thus marked, but yet in all the Lord's passion is foretold. Therefore here too let us understand the Lord's passion, and let Christ, head and body, speak to us. Thus always, or almost always, let us so hear the voices of Christ from the Psalm, that we not only behold that head, the one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus; who also according to divinity in the beginning was the Word, God with God, which Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, flesh from the seed of Abraham, from the seed of David, of the virgin Mary: let us not, then, think of him alone who is our head, when we hear Christ speak; but let us think of Christ, head and body, a certain whole and entire man. For it is said to us: *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^5], by the apostle Paul. And of him it is said by the same Apostle that he is the head of the Church. If, then, he is the head, we the body; the whole Christ is head and body. For sometimes you find words that do not suit the head, and unless you fit them to the body, your understanding will totter: again you find words that are not apt for the body, and yet Christ speaks. There it is not to be feared that anyone err; for quickly he proceeds to fit to the head what he sees does not suit the body. He himself, finally, hanging on the cross, spoke from the person of the body: *My God, my God, look upon me, why have you forsaken me*[^6]? For he had not forsaken Christ, by whom he was not forsaken. Or did he come to us so as to forsake him; or send him so as to depart from him? But because man was forsaken by God, that Adam sinning, who, while he was wont to rejoice before the face of God, terrified by the conscience of sin fled from his joy; and truly God forsook him, because he himself forsook God: from which Adam, when Christ had taken on flesh, he said this from the person of that very flesh, because then our old man was crucified together with him.

**3.** Let us hear, then, what follows: *When Saul sent, and guarded his house, to kill him.*[^7] This pertains not to the Lord's cross, but yet to the Lord's passion. For Christ was crucified, and died, and was buried. That burial, then, was as it were a house: to guard which the kingdom of the Jews sent, when guards were applied to the tomb of Christ. There is indeed the history in the Scripture of Kings, when Saul sent to guard the house, to kill David: but as much as the one who wrote the Psalm took from there, so much ought we to discuss when we treat the title of the Psalm. Did he wish to signify to us only this, that someone was sent to the house that it might be guarded, and that one killed? How, then, was the house guarded for this, if David figured Christ, that Christ might be killed; when Christ was placed in the burial only after being killed on the cross? Refer this, then, to the body of Christ; because to kill Christ was to take away the name of Christ, lest there be belief in Christ, while the lie of the guards prevailed, who were corrupted to say that, while they slept, his disciples came and took him away. This is truly to wish to kill Christ, to extinguish the name of his resurrection, that the lie be preferred to the Gospel. But as Saul did not accomplish it, to kill David; so neither could the kingdom of the Jews accomplish this, that the testimony of sleeping guards should avail more than that of the watching Apostles. For what were the guards taught to say? We give you, they say, as much money as you wish; and say that, while you were sleeping, his disciples came and took him away. Behold, what kind of witnesses of a lie against the truth and resurrection of Christ his enemies, figured by Saul, brought forth. Question, O unbelief, the sleeping witnesses; let them answer you what was done at the tomb. If they were sleeping, whence did they know? If they were awake, why did they not hold the thieves? Let him say, then, what follows.

**4.** [v. 2] *Rescue me from my enemies, O my God, and from those rising up against me redeem me.*[^8] This was done in the flesh of Christ, it is done also in us. For our enemies, the devil and his angels, do not cease to rise up against us daily, and to wish to mock our weakness and frailty, and to ensnare us with deceptions, suggestions, temptations, and whatever snares, while we still live on earth. But let our voice be watchful to God, and let it cry out in the members of Christ, under the head set in heaven: *Rescue me from my enemies, O my God, and from those rising up against me redeem me.*

**5.** [v. 3] *Rescue me from those who work iniquity, and from men of blood save me.*[^9] They indeed were men of blood, who killed the just one, in whom they found no fault: they were men of blood, because when the foreigner wished to release Christ with washed hands, they cried out: *Crucify, crucify*: they were men of blood, who, when the crime of Christ's blood was already laid to them, answered, pledging it to their posterity: *His blood be upon us, and upon our children*[^10]. But neither did the men of blood cease to rise up against his body, for even after the resurrection and ascension of Christ the Church suffered persecutions; and that one indeed first which flowered from the nation of the Jews, of which our Apostles too were. There first Stephen was stoned, and received what he was called. For Stephen means "Crown." Humbly stoned, but sublimely crowned. Then among the Gentiles the kingdoms of the Gentiles rose up, before there was fulfilled in them what had been foretold: *All the kings of the earth will adore him; all nations will serve him*[^11]: and the onslaught of that kingdom raged against the witnesses of Christ; great and much blood of the martyrs was poured out: which being poured out, the crop of the Church, as it were sown, sprouted more fruitfully, and occupied the whole world, as we now behold. From these men of blood, then, Christ is rescued, not only the head, but also the body. From men of blood Christ is rescued, both for those who were, and for those who are, and for those who will be, Christ is rescued, both he who went before, and he who is, and he who is to come. For Christ is the whole body of Christ: and whoever are now good Christians, and those before us, and those who will be after us, the whole Christ is rescued from men of blood; nor is this voice empty: *And from men of blood save me.*

**6.** [v. 4] *Because behold, they have hunted my soul.*[^12] They could hold, they could kill, *they have hunted my soul.* But where is, *You have burst my bonds*[^13]? Where is, *The trap is broken to pieces, and we are rescued*[^14]? Where is that we bless God, *who has not given us as a prey to their teeth*[^15]? They indeed hunted, but he who keeps Israel does not leave us in the hands of the hunters. *Because behold, they have hunted my soul: strong ones rushed in upon me.* These strong ones are not to be passed by carelessly: it must be diligently shown who are these strong ones rising up. Strong over whom, except over the weak, over the feeble, over the not-strong? And yet the weak are praised, and the strong condemned. If we are to understand who are the strong, first the Lord called the devil himself strong: *No one*, he says, *can enter the house of the strong one, and snatch his vessels, unless he first bind the strong one*[^16]. He bound, then, the strong one with the chains of his dominion; and snatched his vessels, and made them his own vessels. For all the unrighteous were vessels of the devil, who, made believers, became vessels of Christ: to whom the Apostle says: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^17], *who makes known his riches in vessels of mercy*[^18]. These, then, can be understood as the strong. But there are among men certain strong ones with a reprehensible and damnable strength, who presume indeed, but on temporal happiness. Does it not seem to you that the rich man was strong, who was just now recited from the Gospel, since his region brought forth in opulence of fruits? Troubled, he found a counsel of storing, that, the old storehouses destroyed, he might build new and larger ones, and, these being filled, he might say to his soul: *You have many goods, soul; feast, be merry, be filled*[^19]. What kind of strong one do you see? *Behold the man who did not make God his helper, but hoped in the abundance of his riches.* See how strong he is: *And he prevailed*, he says, *in his vanity*[^20].

**7.** There are also other strong ones, not by riches, not by strength of body, not by some power of dignity excelling in time, but presuming on their own righteousness. This kind of strong ones is to be guarded against, dreaded, shunned, not imitated: presuming, I say, not on the body, not on wealth, not on family, not on honor; for who does not see that all these are temporal, fleeting, perishable, flighty? but presuming on their own righteousness. Such strength hindered the Jews from entering through the eye of the needle. For when they presume of themselves that they are just, and seem to themselves as it were healthy, they refused the medicine, and killed the very physician. Such strong ones, then, not the weak, he did not come to call who said: *The healthy do not need a physician, but those who are ill. I came not to call the just, but sinners to repentance*[^21]. These were the strong ones who insulted the disciples of Christ, because their master went in to the weak, and dined with the weak. *Why*, they say, *does your master eat with publicans and sinners*[^22]? O strong ones who do not need a physician! That strength is not of health, but of madness. For nothing is stronger than the frenzied, they are more vigorous than the healthy: but the greater their strength, the nearer death. May God, then, turn away from our imitation these strong ones. For it is to be feared lest anyone wish to imitate them. But the teacher of humility, the partaker of our weakness, granting participation in his divinity, descending to this end that he might teach the way and become the way, deigned especially to commend his humility to us; and therefore he did not disdain to be baptized by a servant, that he might teach us to confess our sins, and to be weak that we might be strong, to have rather the voice of the Apostle saying: *When I am weak, then I am strong*[^23]. So he was unwilling to be strong. But these who wished to be strong, that is, who wished to presume on their own power as just, stumbled at the stone of stumbling: and the Lamb seemed to them a kid, and because they killed him as it were a kid, they did not deserve to be redeemed by the Lamb. They themselves, then, are the strong ones who rushed in upon Christ, commending their own righteousness. Hear these strong ones: When certain men of Jerusalem said—those sent by them to seize Christ, and not daring to seize him (because when he willed, then he was seized, who was truly strong): *Why, then*, they say, *could you not seize him? And they answered: No man ever spoke as that man speaks.* And those strong ones: *Has any of the Pharisees believed in him, or any of the scribes, except this people who does not know the Law*[^24]? They set themselves before the weak crowd running to the physician: whence, except because they themselves were strong? and by their strength, what is graver, they also dragged the whole crowd over to themselves, and killed the physician of all. But he too, by being killed, made of his blood a medicine for the sick. *Strong ones rushed in upon me.* Attend especially to these strong ones; and see whether a man should presume on anything, when he should not presume even on righteousness. Now see where those lie who presume on riches, on strength of body, on nobility of family, on dignity of the age, if one who presumes on righteousness itself as his own, falls. *Strong ones rushed in upon me.* Of those strong ones was that one boasting his powers: *I give you thanks*, he says, *that I am not like the rest of men, unjust, robbers, adulterers, like this publican too: I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all I possess*[^25]. See the strong one boasting his powers: attend, on the contrary, to the weak one standing far off, and drawing near by humility. *But the publican*, he says, *stood at a distance, and did not dare even to lift his eyes to heaven, but struck his breast; saying: God, be merciful to me a sinner. Amen I say to you, that publican went down justified rather than that Pharisee.* And see righteousness: *For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled; and he who humbles himself will be exalted*[^26]. These strong ones rushed in, that is, the proud, who, being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own righteousness, were not subject to the righteousness of God.

**8.** [v. 5] What then? *Neither my iniquity, nor my sin, O Lord.*[^27] Strong ones indeed rushed in, presuming on their own righteousness, they rushed in, but found no sin in me. For surely those strong ones, that is, as it were just ones—why could they persecute Christ, except as a sinner? But yet let them see how strong they are, by the savagery of fever, not the firmness of health: let them see how strong they are, who, as it were just, raged against an unjust one. But yet *neither my iniquity, nor my sin, O Lord. Without iniquity I ran, and was directing my course.* Those strong ones, then, could not follow me running: therefore they thought me a sinner, because they did not see my footsteps.

**9.** [v. 6] *Without iniquity I ran, and was directing my course: arise to meet me, and see.*[^28] It is said to God: *Without iniquity I ran, and was directing my course: arise to meet me, and see.* For what? If he does not come to meet, can he not see? As if you were walking a road, and could not be recognized from afar by someone, you would cry out to him and say: Come to meet me, and see how I walk; for when you regard me from afar, you cannot see my step. So then would God, unless he came to meet, not see how without iniquity this man was directing his course, and how without sin he was running? We can indeed take this too: *Arise to meet me*, help me. But what he added, *and see*, is to be understood: make it to be seen that I run, make it to be seen that I direct my course; from that figure by which it was also said to Abraham: *Now I have known that you fear God*[^29]. God says: *Now I have known*: whence, except because now I have made you to know? For each one is unknown to himself before the questioning of temptation, as Peter was unknown to himself by presuming, and by denying learned what strength he had; in his very wavering he understood that he had falsely presumed: he wept; and by weeping deserved fruitfully to know what he was, and to be what he was not. So Abraham, being proved, was made known to himself; and it was said by God: *Now I have known*, that is, now I have made you to know. As a glad day, because it makes glad; and a sad bitterness, because it saddens the one tasting: so God sees, by making to see. *Arise*, then, he says, *to meet me, and see.* What is, *and see*? And help me, that is, in them, so that they may see my course, and follow me, that what is right may not seem perverse to them, that what holds the rule of truth may not seem distorted to them: *For without iniquity I ran, and was directing my course: arise to meet me, and see.*

**10.** The sublimity of our very head admonishes me to say something in this place too; since he was made weak unto death, and took on the flesh of weakness, that he might gather the chicks of Jerusalem under his wings as a hen made weak with her little ones. For in no bird have we ever seen this, even of those that nest before our eyes, like the sparrows of the walls, like the swallows our yearly guests, as it were, like the storks, like other and other birds that nest before our eyes, and sit on their eggs, and feed their chicks, like the very doves we see daily; we have not known, not noticed, not seen any bird made weak with its little ones. How does the hen have this? Certainly I speak of a known thing, which is daily before our sight: how the voice becomes hoarse, how the whole body becomes bristling? the wings droop, the feathers loosen, and you see about the chicks something, I know not what, sickly, and that is the maternal love which is found to be weakness. Why, then, did the Lord, except for this reason, wish to be a hen in the holy Scripture, saying: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, how often I have willed to gather your children, as a hen her chicks under her wings, and you would not*[^30]! And he gathered all the nations, as a hen her chicks, who was made weak for our sake, taking flesh from us, that is, from the human race; crucified, despised, struck with slaps, scourged, hung on the wood, wounded with a lance. This, then, is of maternal weakness, not of lost majesty. Since, then, Christ was such, and therefore despised, and therefore a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal, and therefore many stumbled at him: since, then, he was such, and yet had taken on flesh without sin, he was made a partaker of our weakness, not of iniquity; that from the fact that he shared weakness with us, he might loose our iniquity. Therefore: *Without iniquity I ran, and was directing my course.* What then, is he not according to that which is God to be recognized, and is only that to be considered in him which he was made for our sake, not that whereby he made us? Plainly that too is to be considered; because it is a great sign of godliness to know who endured what for you. Not any small one for you, but for you weak, that highest one. What? Made a little one: *He humbled himself, made obedient unto death.* Who? hear above: *Who, being in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God.* Therefore, equal to God, *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man*[^31]: and so he emptied himself that he took on what he was not, not that he lost what he was. How, then, did he empty himself? Because he appeared to you such; because he did not show you the dignity which he has with the Father; because he now offered you weakness, reserving the brightness for you afterward purified. He, then, equal to the Father, was made such: and yet in that very weakness he is to be recognized, not by vision, but by faith; that what we cannot yet see, we may at least believe, and by believing what we do not see, may deserve also to see. Deservedly, after he rose again, he said to Mary Magdalene, to whom he deigned first to appear: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^32]. What is this? A little after, women touched him. For returning from the tomb, they adored him when he met them, and held his feet: the disciples too handled his scars. What is, then: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father;* except, do not believe me to be only this which you see, lest your gaze here too remain, as it were, your touch? For I seem to you humble, *I have not yet ascended to the Father*, from whom I both descended to you, and from there did not withdraw, I have not yet ascended there, since I did not forsake you. He came not withdrawing, he ascended not forsaking. But what is it that he ascends to the Father? When he becomes known to us as equal to the Father. For we ascend by making progress, that we may be able to see this, to understand this, to grasp this in some measure. Therefore he deferred the touch, did not take it away, did not repel, did not deny. *For I have not yet*, he says, *ascended to the Father. From the highest heaven is his going forth*, says another Psalm, *and his meeting even to the highest of heaven*[^33]. *The highest of heaven*, that is, the highest in all spiritual beings, is the Father: thence his coming forth, and his meeting even to the highest of heaven. He meets even to the highest, which is said only of an equal. Finally, when we compare some unequal things, and apply something small to something great, to see how great is in relation to how great, if we find it unequal, we are wont to speak thus: It does not meet: but if it is equal: It meets. Therefore *his meeting even to the highest of heaven*, because he is equal to the Father. Such he wished to become known to his faithful, who had said: *Do not touch me.* This he wished to be furnished by the Father to his faithful, who said: *Arise to meet me, and see;* make known that I am equal to you. *And see*: what is, *And see*? Make it to be seen that I am equal to you. How long does Philip say to me: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*? How long shall I say: *So long a time I am with you, and you do not know the Father? Philip, he who sees me, sees the Father also: do you not believe that I am in the Father, and the Father in me*[^34]? and still perhaps he does not believe him equal. *Arise to meet me, and see.* Make me to be seen, make yourself to be seen, make our equality known to men. Let not the Jews think they crucified a man. Although in him nothing was crucified except in so far as he was man: yet they did not know whom they crucified. For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. That my faithful may know this Lord of glory, *Arise to meet me, and see.*

**11.** *And you, O Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel.*[^35] You, the God of Israel, who are thought to be the God of Israel alone, who are thought to be the God of one nation that worships you, while all nations worship idols, you, the God of Israel, *attend to visit all the nations.* Let that prophecy be fulfilled, in which Isaiah from your person addresses your Church, your holy city, that barren one whose *children of the deserted are many, more than of her who has a husband.* For to her it was said: *Rejoice, barren one who do not bear, break forth and cry out, you who do not travail; because the children of the deserted are many, more than of her who has a husband*[^36]: more than of the Jewish nation that has a husband, that received the Law; more than of that nation which had an evident king. For your king lies hidden, and you have more children from a hidden bridegroom. Therefore to her it is said: *The children of the deserted are many, more than of her who has a husband.* Then the Prophet adds: *Enlarge the place of your tent, and fix your courts; there is no reason to spare: extend your cords farther, and set up strong stakes, again and again to the right and to the left*[^37]. On the right hold the good, on the left hold the evil, until the winnowing-fan comes: yet possess all the nations. Let the good and the evil be invited to the wedding, let the wedding be filled with those reclining: it is for the servants to invite, for the Lord to separate. *Again and again to the right and to the left extend. For your seed will inherit the nations, and the cities that were deserted you will inhabit*[^38]: deserted by God, deserted by Prophets, deserted by Apostles, deserted by the Gospel, full of demons. *The cities that were deserted you will inhabit. There is no reason for you to fear; for you will prevail: nor be ashamed that you were detestable.* Therefore, because *strong ones rose up against me, do not be ashamed;* when against the Christian name laws were passed, when it was ignominy and infamy to be a Christian. *Do not be ashamed that you were detestable: for you will forget the confusion forever; you will not remember the ignominy of your widowhood. Because I am the Lord who make you: the Lord is his name; and he who rescues you, he himself, the Lord God of Israel, will be called the God of the whole earth*[^39]. *And you, O Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel, attend to visit all the nations*: attend, I say, to visit all the nations.

**12.** *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity.*[^40] Here plainly he terrified. Whom would it not terrify? Who, returning to his own conscience, would not tremble? For even if it is conscious to itself of godliness, it is a wonder if it is not also conscious of some iniquity. For whoever commits sin, also commits iniquity. For if you mark iniquities, O Lord, Lord, who will endure? And yet it is true, nor said in vain, nor can it at all be empty or be able to be: *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity.* But he had mercy even on Paul, who first, as Saul, was working iniquity: for what good did he do, whence he might merit God? Was he not dragging his saints to death? was he not carrying letters from the chiefs of the priests, that wherever he found Christians, he might haul them off to punishment? was he not, doing this, tending to this, breathing and panting slaughter, as Scripture testifies of him, called from heaven by a sublime voice, cast down, raised up, blinded, enlightened, killed, made alive, lost, restored? By what merit? Let us say nothing; let us rather hear him: *I who was formerly*, he says, *a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent; but I obtained mercy*[^41]. Surely: *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity*, this can be understood in two ways: either because God leaves no sins at all unpunished; or because there is a certain iniquity, on those who work which God has no mercy at all. According to these two ways, let us say something briefly to Your Charity, as much as the time suffices.

**13.** All iniquity, whether small or great, must necessarily be punished, either by the man himself repenting, or by God avenging. For even he whom it repents punishes himself. Therefore, brethren, let us punish our sins, if we seek the mercy of God. God cannot have mercy on all who work iniquity as though flattering sins, or not uprooting sins. Plainly either you punish, or he punishes. Do you wish him not to punish? Punish, you. For that too you did which cannot be unpunished: but let it be punished rather by you, that you may do what is written in that psalm: *Let us anticipate his face in confession*[^42]. What is: *Let us anticipate his face*? Before he attends to punish, do you anticipate by confessing and punish. Let him not find what to punish. Because when you punish iniquity, you do equity. And therefore God will have mercy on you, because God now finds you working equity. What is, working equity? Because you have hated in yourself what he too hates; so that you begin to please God, while you punish in yourself what displeases God. For sin cannot be left unpunished: since it is true: *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity.*

**14.** But let us now see the other way in which this sentence can be understood. There is a certain iniquity, which whoever works, it cannot be that God have mercy on him. Do you perhaps ask what that is? The very defense of sins. When anyone defends his own sins, he works a great iniquity: he defends what God hates. And see how perversely, how iniquitously. If he does anything good, he wishes it imputed to himself: if anything evil, to God. For in this way men defend their sins from the person of God, which is worse. What is this? There is no one who would dare to say: Adultery is good, murder is good, fraud is good, perjury is good: no man at all; for even those who do these things cry out when they suffer them. So you find no soul so perverse, so estranged from the fellowship of the human race and from participation in the common blood from Adam, to whom adultery, as I said, fraud, robbery, perjury would seem good. But how do they defend these things? If God had not willed it, I would not have done it. What do you want me to do against my fate? Now you ask what fate is: you run to the stars. You ask who made and ordered the stars: it is God. Therefore to this you defend your sin, that you may accuse God. So the accused is excused, that the judge may be blamed. Plainly God has no mercy on those working such iniquity. *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity.* Pursue, he says, their sins, punish, prick, convert them before themselves, who set themselves behind themselves; and let them be ashamed of themselves, that they may rejoice in you. *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity.*

**15.** [v. 7] *Let them be converted toward evening.*[^43] He speaks of certain ones, I know not who, once workers of iniquity, and once darkness, being converted toward evening. What is, *toward evening*? Afterward. What is, *toward evening*? Later. For before they ought to have recognized the physician, before they crucified Christ. Why, when he was already crucified, rising, ascending into heaven, after he sent his Holy Spirit, with which were filled those who were in one house, and they began to speak in the tongues of all nations, did the crucifiers of Christ become terrified: they were pricked in their conscience, they sought counsel of salvation from the Apostles; they heard: *Repent, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, and your sins will be forgiven you*[^44]. After Christ was killed, after the blood of Christ was poured out, your sins are forgiven. He so willed to die that by his blood he might redeem even the shedders of that same blood. You poured it out raging, drink it confessing. Deservedly *let them be converted toward evening; and let them suffer hunger like dogs.* The Jews called the Gentiles dogs, as unclean. For hence the Lord himself, when a certain Canaanite woman, not a Jewess, cried after him, wishing to turn his mercy to heal her daughter; he, foreseeing all things, knowing all things, but wishing to show her faith, deferred the benefit, and kept her in suspense. And how did he defer? *I am not*, he says, *sent except to the sheep that have perished of the house of Israel*[^45]. Israel, the sheep: the Gentiles, what? *It is not good to send the children's bread to the dogs*[^46]. The Gentiles, then, he called dogs, on account of uncleanness. But what did that hungry woman do? This rather did not repel her: she humbly received the reproach, and merited the benefit. For that was not even to be called a reproach, which had been said by the Lord. A servant, if he says something such to his lord, it is a reproach: a lord even when he says something such to a servant, it can rather be called condescension. *Yes*, she says, *Lord.* What is, *yes*? You speak truly, you speak plainly truly; a dog I am. *But even the dogs*, she says, *eat of the crumbs that fall from their masters' table*[^47]. And at once the Lord: *O woman, great is your faith*[^48]! Just now a dog, now a woman. Whence now a woman who a little before was a dog? By confessing humbly, by not repelling what had been said by the Lord. Therefore the Gentiles are dogs, and therefore hungry. It is good for the Jews too, that they recognize themselves as sinners; and although *toward evening, let them be converted; and let them suffer hunger like dogs.* For he was badly filled who said: *I fast twice in the week*[^49]. But that publican was a dog, suffering hunger; and thence he hungered for the benefit of the Lord, who said: *Be merciful to me a sinner*[^50]. *Let them*, then, *also be converted toward evening: and suffer hunger like dogs.* Let them desire the grace of God, let them understand themselves to be sinners: let those strong ones become weak, those rich ones become poor, those just ones recognize themselves as sinners, those lions become dogs. *Let them be converted toward evening; and suffer hunger like dogs, and they will go round the city.* What city? This world, which in certain places Scripture calls the city of encirclement: that is, because in all the nations on every side the world had surrounded one nation of the Jews, where such things were said, and it was called the city of encirclement. This city those now made hungry dogs will go round. How will they go round it? By evangelizing. Saul, from a wolf made a dog toward evening, that is, converted late, from the crumbs of his Lord ran in his grace, and went round the city.

**16.** [v. 8] *Behold, they themselves will speak with their mouth, and a sword is on their lips.*[^51] Now that sword sharp on both sides, of which the Apostle says: *And the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God*[^52]. Why sharp on both sides? Why, except because it strikes from each Testament? With this sword were slaughtered those of whom it was said to Peter: *Slaughter, and eat*[^53]. *And a sword is on their lips: because who has heard?* They will speak with their mouth: *Who has heard?* that is, they will be vexed against those slow to faith. Those who a little before were themselves unwilling to believe, suffer weariness over those not believing. And truly, brethren, it happens. You see a man lazy before he is a Christian; you cry out to him daily, he is scarcely converted: let him be converted, and now he wishes all to be Christians, and wonders that they are not yet. He has forgotten that he was converted toward evening: but because he has been made hungry like a dog, he has the sword too on his lips; he says: *Who has heard?* What is: *Who has heard?* Who has believed our report, and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed? *Because who has heard?* The Jews do not believe: they were converted to the Gentiles and announced. The Jews did not believe: and yet through believing Jews the Gospel went round the city, and they said: *Because who has heard?*

**17.** [v. 9] *And you, O Lord, will deride them.*[^54] *Who has heard?* All the nations are to be Christian, and you say: *Who has heard?* What is, *you will deride them*? *You will hold all the nations as nothing*: nothing will it be before you; because it will be most easy that all the nations believe in you.

**18.** [v. 10] *My strength I will keep toward you.*[^55] For those strong ones fell for this reason, that they did not keep their strength toward you: that is, those who from above rose up and rushed in presumed of themselves. But I *will keep my strength toward you*: because if I withdraw, I fall; if I draw near, I become stronger. For see what it is, brethren, in the human soul. It does not have light of itself, it does not have strength of itself: but all that is beautiful in the soul is virtue and wisdom; but it is neither wise of itself, nor strong of itself, nor is it light to itself, nor is it strength to itself. There is a certain origin and fountain of virtue, there is a certain root of wisdom, there is a certain—so to speak, if this too is to be said—region of unchangeable truth: from this the soul withdrawing is darkened, drawing near is enlightened. Draw near to him, and be enlightened; because by withdrawing you are darkened. Therefore *my strength I will keep toward you*: I will not withdraw from you, I will not presume of myself. *My strength I will keep toward you, because God is my upholder.* For where was I, and where am I? whence did you take me up? what iniquities of mine did you forgive? where was I lying? whither was I raised? Therefore I ought to remember this; which is said in another psalm. *For my father and my mother forsook me; but the Lord took me up*[^56]? *My strength I will keep toward you, because God is my upholder.*

**19.** [v. 11] *My God, his mercy will anticipate me.*[^57] Behold what it is, *My strength I will keep toward you*: of myself I will presume nothing at all. For what good did I bring, that you should have mercy on me and justify me? What did you find in me, except sins alone? Nothing yours except the nature you created: the rest, my evils which you blotted out. I did not first rise up to you, but you came to rouse me; for *his mercy will anticipate me.* Before I do anything good, *his mercy will anticipate me.* What will the wretched Pelagius answer here?

**20.** [v. 12] *My God showed me concerning my enemies.*[^58] What does he say? How great a mercy he has shown toward me, he showed me in my enemies. Let the one gathered compare himself with those left behind, and the one chosen with those rejected: let the vessel of mercy compare itself with the vessels of wrath; and let it see that out of one mass God made *one vessel for honor, another for dishonor*[^59]. What is, *he showed me concerning my enemies*? *For if God, willing to show wrath, and to demonstrate his power, brought, in much patience, vessels of wrath, which are perfected for perdition,* and for what reason this? *That he might make known his riches in vessels of mercy*[^60]: if, then, he brought vessels of wrath, in which he might make known his riches in vessels of mercy, most rightly was it said: *His mercy will anticipate me. My God showed me concerning my enemies*: that is, how great a mercy he had toward me, he showed me in those toward whom he had none. For unless the debtor is held in suspense, he gives less thanks to whom the debt is remitted. *My God showed me concerning my enemies.*

**21.** But of those very enemies, what? *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law.*[^61] He prays for his enemies, he fulfills the precept. What is: *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity;* and: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law*? How does he not have mercy on all who work iniquity, and how does he not kill them lest they forget your law? But here he spoke of his enemies. What then? Do his enemies work equity? If those who are his enemies work equity; then he himself works iniquity. But because he himself works equity, and further he who works equity certainly suffers iniquity from his enemies; it remains that those who are enemies to the just man work iniquity. Whence, then, a little above: *Have no mercy on all who work iniquity;* and now of his enemies: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law*? Have no mercy on them, then, so as to kill their sins: do not kill them, whose sins you kill. But what is it to be killed? To forget the law of the Lord. That is the true death, to go into the depth of sin: this can indeed be understood also of the Jews. What of the Jews: *Do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law*? These my enemies, the very ones who killed me, do not you kill. Let the nation of the Jews remain: surely it was conquered by the Romans, surely their city was destroyed; the Jews are not admitted to their city, and yet they are Jews. For all those provinces were subjugated by the Romans. Who now recognizes the nations in the Roman empire, what each was, when all have been made Romans, and all are called Romans? Yet the Jews remain with a sign; nor were they so conquered as to be absorbed by the conquerors. Not without cause is that Cain, who, when he had killed his brother, God set a sign upon him, lest anyone kill him. This is the sign that the Jews have: they hold altogether the remnants of their law; they are circumcised, they keep the sabbaths, they sacrifice the Passover, they eat unleavened bread. They are, then, Jews, they are not killed, they are necessary for the believing Gentiles. Why this? That he may demonstrate to us in our enemies his mercy. *My God showed me concerning my enemies.* In the proud branches cut off he demonstrates his mercy to the grafted-in wild olive. Behold where those lie who were proud, behold where you are grafted in who were lying: and do you not be proud, lest you deserve to be cut off. *My God, do not kill them, lest at some time they forget your law.*

**22.** *Scatter them in your power.*[^62] It is already done: through all the nations the Jews have been scattered, witnesses of their own iniquity and of our truth. They themselves have the codices, in which Christ was prophesied, and we hold Christ. And if perhaps sometime someone, a pagan, doubts, when we tell him the prophecies about Christ, at whose evidence he is amazed, and, wondering, thinks them written by us; from the codices of the Jews we prove that this was foretold so long before. See how from our enemies we confound other enemies. *Scatter them in your power*: take from them their power, take from them their strength. *And lead them down, my protector, O Lord. The offenses of their mouth, the word of their lips: and let them be caught in their pride. And from cursing and lying their consummations will be announced, in the wrath of consummation, and they will not be.*[^63] These things are obscure, and I fear lest they be not well conveyed. You are already wearied with hearing: and so, if it please Your Charity, let us defer the rest to tomorrow. The Lord will help, that we may pay you the debt; because we promise concerning him rather than concerning ourselves.
