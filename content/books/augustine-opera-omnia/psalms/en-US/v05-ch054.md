# On the Same Psalm 118 (29)

**1.** [v. 153.] Let no one set in the body of Christ judge this voice to be alien from himself (since in truth the whole body of Christ, set in this humiliation, says it), with which the reading of this psalm begins, about which we have now undertaken to discourse: *See my humiliation, and rescue me; for I have not forgotten your law.* In this place we more fittingly understand no other law of God than that by which it is immovably fixed that everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and everyone who humbles himself will be exalted[^1]. The proud, then, is entangled by evils, that he may be humbled; the humble is rescued from evils, that he may be exalted.

**2.** [v. 154.] *Judge,* he says, *my judgment, and redeem me.* The earlier sentence is in a way repeated. For what he says, *See my humiliation,* is this, *Judge my judgment:* and what he says, *and rescue me,* is this, *and redeem me.* But what was said above, *for I have not forgotten your law,* agrees with what is below, *for the sake of your utterance give me life.* For the utterance itself is the law of God, which he did not forget, that, to be exalted, he might humble himself. But to that very exaltation pertains what he says, *Give me life;* because the exaltation of the saints is eternal life.

**3.** [v. 155.] *Far,* he says, *from sinners is salvation; for they have not sought out your statutes.* For who distinguishes you, O you who said, *Far from sinners is salvation?* Who distinguishes you from sinners, so that salvation is not far from you, but with you? This, surely, distinguishes you: that what those did not do, you yourself did—that is, you sought out the statutes of God. But what do you have that you did not receive[^2]? Are you not the one who a little before was saying, *I have cried with my whole heart; hear me, O Lord: I will search out your statutes?* Therefore, from him to whom you cried, that you might search them out, you received. He himself, then, distinguishes you from those from whom, for that reason, salvation is far, because they did not seek out the statutes of God.

**4.** [v. 156.] This man too saw this. For neither would I see it, unless I saw it in him, unless I were in him. For these are the words of the body of Christ, of which we are members. He saw this, I say, and immediately added: *Your tender mercies are many, O Lord.* This too, then, that we search out your statutes, pertains to your tender mercies. *According to your judgment give me life.* For I know that even your judgment will not be over me without your tender mercy.

**5.** [v. 157.] *Many are they who persecute me and trouble me; from your testimonies I have not turned aside.* It is done, we know, we recall, we recognize. The whole earth is empurpled with the blood of the martyrs; heaven flowers with the crowns of the martyrs; the churches are adorned with the memorials of the martyrs; the seasons are marked with the birthdays of the martyrs; healings increase by the merits of the martyrs. Whence is this, except that what was foretold of this man, spread through the whole world, has been fulfilled: *Many are they who persecute me and trouble me; from your testimonies I have not turned aside?* We recognize it, and give thanks to the Lord our God. For you, the man, you in another psalm, you yourself said: *If the Lord had not been among us, perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive*[^3]. Behold why you did not turn aside from his testimonies, and came to the palm of the heavenly calling amid the hands of many persecuting and troubling you.

**6.** [v. 158.] *I saw,* he says, *the senseless, and I wasted away:* or, as other manuscripts have, *I saw those not keeping the covenant;* and this more have. But who are they who did not keep the covenant, except those who turned aside from the testimonies of God, not bearing the tribulations of many persecuting them? But this is the covenant, that whoever conquers be crowned. This covenant they did not keep who, not enduring persecution, turned aside from the testimonies of God by denying. These, then, this man saw, and was wasting away, because he loved. For that is good zeal, coming from love, not from envy. For wherein they did not keep the covenant, he went on and added: *Because they did not keep your utterances.* For those they denied in tribulations.

**7.** [v. 159.] And this man commends himself as differing from them, and says: *See that I have loved your commandments.* He does not say: I have not denied your utterances or testimonies, which the martyrs were compelled to do, and, not doing, suffered intolerable things: but he said this, where is the fruit of all sufferings; because *if I deliver my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^4]. Commending this, he says: *See that I have loved your commandments.* Then he asked for the reward: *O Lord, in your mercy give me life.* They put to death; do you give life. But if a reward is asked in mercy, which righteousness ought to render; how much more was mercy expended, that there might be obtained the very victory to which the reward was owed?

**8.** [v. 160.] *The beginning,* he says, *of your words is truth, and for ever are all the judgments of your righteousness.* From your truth, he says, your words proceed, and therefore they are truthful, and deceive no one, by which life is foretold to the righteous, punishment to the ungodly. For these are for ever the judgments of God's righteousness.

[^1]: Luke 14:11
[^2]: 1 Cor 4:7
[^3]: Ps 124:1-3 (123:1-3)
[^4]: 1 Cor 13:3
