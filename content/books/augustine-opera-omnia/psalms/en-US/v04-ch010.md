# Exposition of Psalm 82

**1.** [v. 1.] The title of this psalm is: *A Song of a Psalm of Asaph.* We have already often said what Asaph is interpreted, that is, "gathering." That man, then, who was called Asaph, is set as a figure of the gathering of the people of God in the titles of many psalms. But in Greek a gathering is called synagogue, which the people of the Jews held as a kind of proper name, so that they were called the Synagogue; just as the Christian people is more usually called the Church, since it too is surely gathered.

**2.** [v. 2.] And so the people of God in this psalm says: *O God, who shall be like you?* Which I judge is more fittingly taken in Christ, because, made in the likeness of men, he was supposed by those by whom he was despised to be comparable to the rest of men[^1]: for he was even reckoned among the wicked[^2]; but for this, that he might be judged. But when he will come to judge, then will come about what is here said: *O God, who shall be like you?* For if the Psalms were not wont to speak to the Lord Christ, neither would that be said, which no one of the faithful can doubt was said to him: *Your throne, O God, is forever and ever, a rod of direction is the rod of your kingdom: you have loved righteousness, and hated iniquity; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows*[^3]. To him, then, it is now too said: *O God, who shall be like you?* For to many you willed to be like in humility, even to the thieves who were crucified with you[^4]; but in glory when you come, *who shall be like you?* For what great thing is said, when it is said to God: *Who shall be like you;* unless it be said to him who willed to be like men, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man[^5]? And therefore he did not say: Who is like you? which surely would rightly be said, if this were referred to the divinity. But because it was referred to the form of a servant, then his unlikeness from the rest of men will appear, when he will appear in glory. Therefore it follows: *Do not keep silent, nor be restrained, O God*: because first he kept silent, that he might be judged; when, as a lamb before its shearer was without voice, so he did not open his mouth[^6], and restrained his own power. Which, to show that he restrained himself, at that voice of his by which he said: *I am,* those who sought to seize him went back, and fell[^7]. When, then, would he be held and suffer, unless he himself held in and restrained himself, and in a manner softened himself? For thus too some have interpreted what is here set: *Nor be restrained, O God,* so as to say: *Nor grow gentle, O God.* He himself says elsewhere: *I have kept silent; shall I keep silent forever*[^8]? To whom it is here said, *do not keep silent;* of whom it is said elsewhere: *God will come manifest, our God, and will not be silent*[^9]. It is said here: *Do not keep silent*: for he kept silent, that he might be judged, when he came hidden; but he will not keep silent, that he may judge, when he will come manifest.

**3.** [v. 3.] *For behold, your enemies have made a din, and those who hate you have lifted up the head.* It seems to me to signify the last days, when these things which are now repressed by fear are to burst out into a free voice, but plainly an irrational one, so that it is to be called a din rather than speech or discourse. Therefore they will not then begin to hate, but those who hate you will then lift up the head. And not "heads," but *the head;* when they will come to this, that they have even that head which is exalted above all that is called God and that is worshiped[^10]: that in him especially may be fulfilled: *he who exalts himself shall be humbled*[^11]; and when he whom he, to whom it is said: *Do not keep silent, nor grow gentle, O God,* will slay with the spirit of his mouth, and will make void by the enlightenment of his presence[^12].

**4.** [v. 4.] *Against your people they have maliciously taken counsel;* or, as other codices have, *they have craftily thought out a counsel, and have plotted against your saints.* These things are said mockingly: for when would they avail to harm the people or the folk of God, or his saints, who know how to say: *If God is for us, who is against us*[^13]?

**5.** [v. 5.] *They said: Come, and let us destroy them from the nation.* He set the singular number for the plural; as it is said: Whose is this beast, even if it is asked of the flock, and beasts are understood: finally, other codices have, *from the nations;* where the interpreters followed the sense rather than the word. *Come, and let us destroy them from the nation.* This is that din with which they made a din rather than spoke, since vainly they made a vain noise. *And let the name of Israel be remembered no more.* This others said more plainly: *And let there be no memory of the name of Israel anymore.* Because *be remembered of the name* is an unusual locution in the Latin tongue; for one is rather wont to say, let the name be remembered: but the meaning is the same; for he who said, *be remembered of the name,* translated a Greek locution. But *Israel* here ought surely to be understood as the seed of Abraham, to whom the Apostle says: *Then you are Abraham's seed, heirs according to the promise*[^14]: not Israel according to the flesh, of whom he says: *See Israel according to the flesh*[^15].

**6.** [v. 6.] *For they have thought with one mind, together they have made a testament against you*: as though they could be stronger. A testament in the Scriptures is indeed not only that which avails only when the testators are dead; but they called every pact and agreement a testament. For Laban and Jacob made a testament[^16], which surely availed even among the living: and innumerable such things are read in the divine utterances.

**7.** [vv. 7-8.] Then he begins to recall the enemies of Christ under certain names of nations: the interpretation of which names sufficiently indicates what he wishes to be understood. For by such names the enemies of truth are most fittingly figured. For the Edomites are interpreted, either "bloody," or "earthly": the Ishmaelites, "obeying themselves"; surely not God: but themselves. Moab, "from a father": which in the bad sense is not better understood, unless the history itself be considered, that the father, Lot, mingling with his daughter who used him unlawfully, begot him; because from that very thing it received such a name[^17]. But the father is good, yet as the Law, if one use it lawfully[^18]; not incestuously and unlawfully. The Hagarenes, "proselytes," that is, newcomers: by which name are signified among the enemies of the people of God, not those who become citizens; but those who persevere in an alien and adventitious mind, and, an occasion of harming being found, show themselves. Gebal, "vain valley," that is, falsely humble: Ammon, "turbulent people," or "people of grief": Amalek, "people that licks"; whence it is said elsewhere: *And his enemies shall lick the earth*[^19]. The foreigners, although by the very Latin name they indicate themselves aliens, and on this account consequently enemies, yet in Hebrew are called Philistines; which is interpreted "falling by drink," like those whom worldly luxury has made drunk. Tyre in the Hebrew tongue is called Sor; which, whether it be interpreted "narrowness" or "tribulation," is to be taken in these enemies of the people of God according to that which the Apostle says: *Tribulation and anguish upon every soul of man that works evil*[^20]. All these, then, are thus enumerated in this psalm: *The tents of the Edomites and the Ishmaelites, Moab and the Hagarenes, Gebal and Ammon and Amalek, and the foreigners with those dwelling in Tyre.*

**8.** [v. 9.] And as if indicating the cause why they are enemies to the people of God, he adds and says: *For Assyria too has come with them.* But Assyria is wont to be understood figuratively for the devil himself, who works in the children of unbelief[^21], as in his own vessels, that they may attack the people of God. *They have become,* he says, *a help to the children of Lot*: because all the enemies, with their prince the devil working in them, *have become a help to the children of Lot,* which is interpreted "turning aside." But the apostate angels are well understood as it were children of turning aside; for by turning aside from the truth, they departed into the service of the devil. These are they of whom the Apostle says: *Yours is not a wrestling against flesh and blood; but against the principalities and powers and rulers of the world of these shadows, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places*[^22]. Wherefore these invisible enemies are helped by unbelieving men, in whom they work to attack the people of God.

**9.** [vv. 10-13.] Now then let us see what the prophetic spirit imprecates, foretelling rather than cursing. *Do to them,* he says, *as to Midian and Sisera, as to Jabin at the torrent of Kishon. They perished at Endor, they became as the dung of the earth.* All these, according to the history, the people of Israel, who then were the people of God, fought down and conquered; as also those whom he next recalls, saying: *Set their princes as Oreb, and Zeeb, and Zebah, and Zalmunna.* But the interpretations of these names are these: Midian is interpreted "turning aside judgment"; Sisera, "exclusion of joy"; Jabin, "wise." But in these enemies overcome by the people of God, that one is to be understood as wise, of whom the Apostle says: *Where is the wise? where is the scribe? where is the disputer of this world*[^23]? Oreb, "drought": Zeeb, "wolf": Zebah, "victim," but of the wolf; for he too has his victims: Zalmunna, "shadow of commotion." All these fit the evil, whom the people of God conquers in good. Further, Kishon, in which torrent they were conquered, is interpreted "their hardness." Endor, where they perished, is interpreted "the fountain of generation," but surely carnal, to which, being given over, they perished: not caring for regeneration, which leads to the life where they will not marry, nor take wives; for they will not begin to die[^24]. Rightly, then, was it said of them: *They became as the dung of the earth;* for from them nothing was propagated, except earthly fruitfulness. As, then, all those were overcome in figure by the people of God; so he prays that these enemies be overcome in truth.

**10.** *All,* he says, *their princes, who said: Let us possess by inheritance the sanctuary of God.* This is that vain din with which, as said above, *your enemies have made a din*[^25]. But what is to be understood by the sanctuary of God, except the temple of God, of which the Apostle says: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^26]? For what else do the enemies strive but to possess the people of God—that is, to subjugate them, that they may yield to their impious wills?

**11.** [v. 14.] But what follows? *My God, set them as a wheel.* It is fittingly taken, that they may not be stable in what they think; yet I judge that it can rightly be understood thus too: *Set them as a wheel,* in that a wheel is raised up from the parts that are behind, and cast down from the parts that are before: so all the enemies of the people of God become. For this is not a wish, but a prophesying. He adds also: *As stubble before the face of the wind.* By "face" he means presence: for what face is there of the wind, which has no lineaments of body, since it is a motion, that is, as it were a wave of air? But it is set for the temptation by which light and empty hearts are swept away.

**12.** [vv. 15-16.] Now lightness, by which one easily consents to evils, a grievous torment follows; whence next it is said: *As fire that burns up a wood, as a flame burning up the mountains; so you will pursue them in your tempest, and in your anger you will disturb them.* He said "wood" on account of barrenness, "mountains" on account of elation: for such are the enemies of the people of God; empty of righteousness, full of pride. But saying "fire" and "flame," he repeated this same thing by another name, by which he wished God judging and punishing to be understood. But as for what he says, *in your tempest;* this same thing he consequently expounded saying, *in your anger*: and what he said above, *you will pursue them;* this afterward, *you will disturb them.* Let us surely remember to understand the anger of God without any turbulent affection: for his anger means the just reason of vengeance; as if a law were said to be angry, when its ministers, moved according to it, take vengeance.

**13.** [vv. 17-19.] *Fill,* he says, *their faces with ignominy, and they will seek your name, O Lord.* This indeed is prophesied to them as good and desirable: nor would it be prophesied, unless there were in that fellowship of the enemies of the people of God even such men, to whom this would be granted before the last judgment; because they are even now the same, and theirs is the body of the enemies according to the envy by which they emulate the people of God. And now, where they can, they both make a din and lift the head; but in particular, not universally, as at the end of the world, with the last judgment imminent. Yet that body is both in those who from there are to believe, and to pass over into another body (for their faces are wholesomely filled with ignominy, that they may seek the name of the Lord); and in those who persevere unto the end in the same malice, who are set as stubble before the face of the wind, and are burned up like woods and barren mountains. To whom he again returns, saying: *Let them be ashamed, and disturbed forever and ever.* For those who seek the name of the Lord are not disturbed forever and ever; but, looking upon the ignominy of their sins, they are disturbed to this end, that they may seek the name of the Lord, through which they are not disturbed.

**14.** And again he returns to those who, in the same fellowship of the enemies, are to be confounded to this end, that they may not be confounded forever; and to be destroyed in as much as they are evil, that, made good, they may be found forever. For of these, when he had said: *And let them be confounded, and perish;* he straightway subjoined: *And let them know that your name is the Lord; you alone are the Most High in all the earth*: let them, coming to this knowledge, be so confounded that they may be pleasing; so perish, that they may remain. *Let them know,* he says, *that your name is the Lord*: as though not by a true name nor their own are any others called who are named lords; since they rule in a servile way, and compared to the true Lord are not even lords: as it was said: *I am who I am*[^27]; as though the things that have been made are not, if they be compared to him by whom they were made. But as for what he added, *you alone are the Most High in all the earth,* or, as other codices have, *over all the earth;* surely also in all heaven, or over all heaven: but he preferred to say this, by which earthly pride might be pressed down. For the earth—that is, man—ceases to be proud, to whom it was said: *You are earth*[^28]; and: *Why is earth and ashes proud*[^29]? when it knows the Lord to be the Most High over all the earth—that is, that the thoughts of no man avail against those who are called according to the purpose, and of whom it is said: *If God is for us, who is against us*[^30]?
