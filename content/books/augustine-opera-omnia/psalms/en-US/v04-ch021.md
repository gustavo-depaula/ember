# Exposition of Psalm 91

**1.** Attend to the Psalm: may the Lord grant us to open the mysteries which are here contained; since, on account of the weariness of minds, the same things are treated diversely and variously. For God teaches us no other song than that of faith, hope, and love: that our faith be firm in him, as long as we do not see him, believing in him whom we do not see, that we may rejoice when we shall see, and to our faith may succeed the vision of his light, where it will no longer be said to us: Believe what you do not see; but, rejoice because you see. Let our hope too be unchangeable, and be fixed in him, and not waver and fluctuate, not be agitated; as God himself, in whom it is fixed, cannot be agitated. For it is now called hope; then it will be not hope, but reality. For it is called hope so long as that which is hoped for is not seen, the Apostle saying: *But hope that is seen is not hope: for what one sees, why does he hope for? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it with patience*[^1]. Now, then, patience is necessary, until what was promised comes. But no one is patient in good things. When patience is demanded of a man, he is in evils: when it is said: Be patient, bear, endure, there is a trouble, under which God wishes you to be strong, enduring, longsuffering, patient. But does he deceive who promised? The physician draws his iron to cut the wounds, and says to the one he is about to cut: Be patient, endure, bear: in the pains he demands patience, but after the pains he promises health. And he who endures the pains under the physician's iron, unless he sets before himself the health which he does not yet have, fails in the pain which he suffers. There are, then, many evils in this world, within, without; they altogether do not cease, scandals abound: no one feels them, except him who walks the way of God. To him it is said in all the divine pages, that he endure present things, hope for future things, love him whom he does not see, that he may embrace him when he shall see. For love, which is joined to us as the third with faith and hope, is greater above faith and hope: because faith is of things which are not seen; but there will be vision when they have been seen: and hope is of a thing which is not held; which, the thing itself coming, will no longer be hope, because we shall hold, not hope: but love knows only to grow more and more[^2]. For if we love him whom we do not see, how shall we love when we see? Therefore let our desire grow. We are not Christians except for the sake of the world to come: let no one hope for present goods, let no one promise himself the happiness of the world, because he is a Christian; but let him use present happiness as he can, how he can, when he can, as much as he can. When it is present, let him give thanks to the consolation of God: when it is absent, let him give thanks to the justice of God. Everywhere let him be grateful, nowhere ungrateful: and to the Father consoling and caressing let him be grateful; and to the Father amending and scourging and giving discipline let him be grateful; for he always loves, whether he caress, or threaten: and let him say what you heard in the Psalm: *It is good to confess to the Lord, and to sing to your name, O Most High.*

**2.** [v. 1.] The title of the Psalm has: *A Psalm of a Song for the sabbath day.* Behold today too is the sabbath day: this in the present time the Jews celebrate by a certain bodily leisure, languid and loose and luxurious. For they are at leisure for trifles; and although God commanded the sabbath, they exercise the sabbath in the things which God forbids[^3]. Our leisure is from evil works, their leisure is from good works. For it is better to plow than to dance. They are at leisure from good work; from trifling work they are not at leisure. To us God enjoins a sabbath. Of what kind? First see where it is. It is within, in the heart is our sabbath. For many are at leisure in their members, and are in tumult in their conscience. Every evil man cannot have a sabbath: for nowhere does his conscience rest for him; he must needs live in perturbations. But he who has a good conscience is tranquil; and that very tranquility is the sabbath of the heart. For he attends to the Lord who promises; and if he labors in the present, he is stretched out by the hope of the future, and all the cloud of sadness is cleared away; as the Apostle says: *Rejoicing in hope*[^4]. And that very joy in the tranquility of our hope is our sabbath. This is commended, this is sung in this psalm, how a Christian man may be in the sabbath of his heart, that is, in the leisure and tranquility and serenity of his conscience, undisturbed. Hence he says here, whence men are wont to be disturbed, and teaches you to keep the sabbath in your heart.

**3.** [v. 2.] The first thing is, that you yourself, if you have made some progress, confess to God from that in which you have made progress, because they are his gifts, not your merits. From this begin the sabbath, by not attributing to yourself as though you had not received what you received[^5]; nor excusing yourself from the evil that you do, because that is your own. For perverse and disturbed men, who do not keep the sabbath, attribute their evils to God, their goods to themselves. If he has done any good: I did it, he says: if he has done any evil, he seeks whom to accuse, that he may not confess to God. And what is it: He seeks whom to accuse? If he is not very impious, he has at hand Satan to accuse: Satan did it, he says, he himself persuaded me: as though Satan had the power of compelling. He has the cunning of persuading. But if Satan were speaking, and God were silent, you would have whence to excuse yourself: but now your ears are set between God admonishing, and the serpent suggesting. Why are they turned hither, turned away from here? Satan does not cease to persuade evil; but neither does God cease to admonish good. But Satan does not compel the unwilling: it is in your power to consent, or not to consent. If you have done some evil with Satan persuading; dismiss Satan, accuse yourself, that by your accusation you may deserve the mercy of God. Do you seek to accuse him who has no pardon? Accuse yourself, and you receive indulgence. Then many do not accuse Satan, but accuse fate. My fate led me, he says. When you say to him: Why did you do it? why did you sin? And he: By my evil fate. Lest he say: I did it, now he stretches his hands to God; his tongue blasphemes. He does not indeed say this openly, but yet attend, and see that he says this. You ask of him what fate is; and he says: Evil stars. You ask of him who made the stars, who ordained the stars; he has nothing to answer you, except God: It remains, then, that whether through a lattice, or through a long reed, or through a neighbor, he accuses God; and when God punishes sins, he makes God the author of his sins. For it cannot be that he punishes what he made: he punishes what you do, that he may free what he made. But sometimes, all things being dismissed, they go altogether directly against God; and when they sin, they say: God willed this; if God did not will it, I would not sin. To this end does he admonish, that not only it not be heeded that you sin not, but that it be accused that you sin? What, then, does this psalm teach us? *It is good to confess to the Lord.* What is, *to confess to the Lord?* In both things, both in your sin, because you did it, and in the good deed, confess to the Lord, because he did it. Then you will sing to the name of God the Most High: seeking the glory of God, not your own; his name, not yours. For if you seek the name of God, he too seeks your name: but if you neglect the name of God, he too blots out your name. But how did I say: He seeks your name? As he said to his disciples coming, after he sent them to evangelize. When they had done many miracles, and in the name of Christ had cast out demons, returning they said: *Lord, behold the demons are subject to us.* They said indeed, *in your name;* but he saw in them that they rejoiced at that very glorification, and exalted themselves, and were going thence into pride, because it was permitted them to cast out demons. He saw them seeking their own glory, and said to them, seeking himself, rather preserving their names with himself: *Do not rejoice in this; but rejoice that your names are written in heaven*[^6]. Behold where you have your name, if you do not neglect the name of God. Sing, then, to the name of God, that your name may be fixed with God. But what is it to sing (psallere), brothers? The psaltery is a kind of instrument; it has strings. Our work is our psaltery: whoever works good works with his hands sings (psallit) to God; whoever confesses with his mouth sings to God. Sing with the mouth, make music (psalle) with works. To what thing?

**4.** [v. 3.] *To announce your mercy in the morning, and your truth through the night.* What does it mean that in the morning the mercy of God is to be announced, and through the night the truth of God? Morning is said, when it is well with us: night is said, when there is the sadness of tribulation. What, then, did he say briefly? When it is well with you, rejoice in God, because it is his mercy. Now perhaps you would say: If, then, I rejoice in God when it is well with me, because it is his mercy; when I am in sadness, in tribulation, what do I do? It is his mercy, when it is well with me; is it, then, his cruelty, when it is ill? If I praise the mercy, when it is well; shall I, then, reprove the cruelty, when it is ill? No. But when it is well, praise the mercy; when ill, praise the truth: because he scourges sins, he is not unjust. Daniel was in the night, when he prayed: for he was in the captivity of Jerusalem, he was in the power of the enemies. Then the saints were suffering many evils: then he himself was cast into the lions' den, then the three boys were cast into the fire[^7]. These things the people of Israel was suffering in the captivity: it was night. Through the night Daniel was confessing the truth of God; he said in prayer: *We have sinned, we have done impiously, we have committed iniquity: to you, O Lord, glory, to us confusion*[^8]. He was announcing the truth of God through the night. What is it to announce the truth of God through the night? Not to accuse God, because you suffer some evil; but to attribute it to your sins, to his amending: *To announce his mercy in the morning, and his truth through the night.* When you announce mercy in the morning, and truth through the night, you always praise God, you always confess to God, and sing to his name.

**5.** [v. 4.] *On the ten-stringed psaltery, with a song on the harp.* The ten-stringed psaltery you heard not long ago. The ten-stringed psaltery signifies the ten precepts of the Law. But it is needful to sing on it, not to carry the psaltery. For the Jews too have the Law; they carry it, they do not make music. Who are they who make music? Those who work. It is little: those who work with sadness do not yet make music. Who are they who make music? Those who do good with cheerfulness. For in music-making there is cheerfulness. And what does the Apostle say? *For God loves a cheerful giver*[^9]. Whatever you do, do with cheerfulness: then you do good and well. But if you do with sadness, it is done of you, you do not do it; and you rather carry the psaltery, you do not sing. *On the ten-stringed psaltery, with a song on the harp*: that is, in word and in deed. *With a song,* in word; *on the harp,* in deed. If you say words alone, you have as it were a song alone, you do not have a harp: if you work, and do not speak, you have as it were a harp alone. On account of this both speak well, and do well, if you wish to have a song with a harp.

**6.** [v. 5.] *For you have made me glad, O Lord, in your making, and in the works of your hands I will exult.* You see what he says. You made me living well, you formed me: if perhaps I do any good, in the making of your hands I will exult: as the Apostle says: *For we are his workmanship, created in good works*[^10]. For unless he formed you for good works, you would know nothing but your own evil works. For *he who speaks a lie, speaks of his own*[^11]: this the Gospel says. Every sin is a lie. For whatever is against the law and against the truth is called a lie. Therefore what does he say? *He who speaks a lie, speaks of his own;* that is, he who sins, sins of his own. Attend to the sentence on the contrary side. For if he who speaks a lie speaks of his own; it remains that he who speaks the truth speaks of God's. Therefore it is said in another place: *God alone is truthful; but every man is a liar*[^12]. It is not said to you in this sentence: Go, lie securely, because you are a man: rather see yourself a man, because you are a liar; and that you may be truthful, drink the truth, that you may belch forth of God, that you may be truthful. Because of your own you cannot have it, it remains that you drink it from there whence it flows. As if you withdraw from the light, you are in darkness: as a stone is not hot of itself, but either from the sun or from the fire, if you withdraw it from the heat, it grows cold; there it appears that what was hot was not its own; but it was hot either from the sun or from the fire: so you too, if you withdraw from God, will grow cold; if you approach God, you will grow hot; as the Apostle says: *Fervent in spirit*[^13]. Likewise of the light what does he say? If you approach him, you will be in the light; therefore the psalm says: *Draw near to him, and be enlightened; and your faces shall not be ashamed*[^14]. Since, then, you can work no good, except enlightened by the light of God, and made fervent by the Spirit of God; when you see yourself working well, confess to God, and say what the Apostle says, say to yourself lest you be exalted: *For what have you that you have not received? But if you have received, why do you glory as though you had not received*[^15]? Here, then, he confesses to God and teaches us a good confession, because he says: *You have made me glad, O Lord, in your making, and in the works of your hands I will exult.*

**7.** What do we do about those who live ill, and flourish? For hence he has a disturbed soul who loses the sabbath: he sees himself daily engaged in good works, and laboring in straitness of affairs, perhaps in want of family means, perhaps in hunger and thirst and nakedness, perhaps doing good things in prison, and that man by whom he was sent to prison doing evil things and exulting; and there creeps into the heart a most evil thought against God, and he says: God, why do I serve you? why do I obey your words? I have not seized the goods of others, I have not committed theft, I have not killed a man, I have not coveted anyone's property, I have not said false testimony against anyone, I have not done injury to father or mother, I have not bowed to idols, I have not taken the name of the Lord God in vain: I have kept myself from sin. He enumerates the ten strings, that is, the ten precepts of the law, and questions himself according to each, and sees that he has not sinned, not even in one; and he is saddened because he suffers such evils. And those who, I do not say touch some strings, but do not even touch the psaltery itself, and work no good, consult idols: and perhaps they then seem Christians, when their house suffers no evil; but when there is some tribulation there, they run to the soothsayer, or the diviner, or the astrologer. The name of Christ is said to him; he sneers, he twists his mouth. It is said to him: Faithful man, do you consult the astrologer? And he: Depart from me: he himself disclosed to me my goods; for I would have lost them, and would have remained in mourning. Good man, do you not sign yourself with the sign of the cross of Christ? And the Law forbids all these things. You rejoice because you found your goods; are you not sad because you yourself have perished? How much better that your tunic should perish than your soul? He sneers at all these things; he is insulting to his parents, he hates his enemy, he pursues him even to death, he steals where he finds the occasion, he does not cease from false testimony, he plots against another's marriage, he covets his neighbor's property: he does all these things, and flourishes in riches, in honors, in the excellence of this world. That wretched man, doing good and suffering evil, sees him; he is disturbed and says: God, I think, the evil please you, and you hate the good, you love those who do iniquity. If he is moved, and consents to that thought, he will lose the sabbath from his heart: now he begins not to attend to this psaltery; he has deviated from it, he sings without cause: *It is good to confess to the Lord, and to sing to your name, O Most High.* Now, the sabbath being lost from the inner man and the rest of the heart shut out, and the good thought repelled, he begins now to imitate the man whom he sees flourishing in evils; and he himself too turns to doing evil things. But God is patient, because he is eternal, and knows the day of his judgment, where he examines all things.

**8.** [v. 6.] Teaching us this, what does he say? *How magnified are your works, O Lord! Exceedingly deep are your thoughts.* Truly, my brothers, no sea is so deep as is this thought of God, that the evil flourish, and the good labor: nothing so deep, nothing so high; there every unbeliever is shipwrecked, in that deep, in that depth. Do you wish to cross this depth? Do not spring back from the wood of Christ; you will not be drowned: hold yourself to Christ. What is it that I say, hold yourself to Christ? For this he himself willed to labor on earth. You heard, when the prophet was read, that he did not turn his back away from the scourges, that he did not turn his face away from the spittle of men, that he did not turn his cheek from their blows[^16]: why did he will to suffer all these things, except that he might console those who suffer? And he could even have raised his flesh at the end; but you who had not seen, would have nothing to hope: he did not defer his resurrection, lest you still doubt. To this end, then, suffer and endure tribulations in the world, which you attended to in Christ; and let not those move you who do evil things, and flourish in this world. *Exceedingly deep are the thoughts of God.* Where is the thought of God? For the present he loosens the reins; but afterward he will tighten them. Do not rejoice at the fish that exults in its bait; the fisherman has not yet drawn the hook: for he already has the hook in its jaws. And what seems long to you is short: all these things quickly pass. What is the long life of man to the eternity of God? Do you wish to be longsuffering? See the eternity of God. For you attend to your few days, and in your few days you wish all things to be fulfilled. What all things? That all the impious be condemned, and all the good crowned. In those your days do you wish these things to be fulfilled? God fulfills them in his own time. Why do you suffer and make weariness? He is eternal; he delays, he is longsuffering: but you say: Therefore I am not longsuffering, because I am temporal. But it is in your power: join your heart to the eternity of God, and with him you will be eternal. For what was said of temporal things? *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of the flesh as the flower of grass: the grass has dried up, and the flower has fallen.* All things, then, dry up and fall; not that word: *The word of the Lord abides forever*[^17]. The grass passes, the glory of the grass passes; but you have whereby to hold yourself: *The word of the Lord abides forever.* Say, then, to him: *Exceedingly deep are your thoughts.* You have held the wood, you cross through that depth. Do you see anything there? do you understand anything there? I understand, you say. If you are now a Christian, and well instructed, you say: God reserves all things for his own judgment. The good labor, because they are scourged as sons: the evil exult, because they are condemned as aliens. A man has two sons; one he chastises, the other he dismisses: the one does evil, and is not rebuked by the father; the other, as soon as he stirs, is struck with blows, scourged. Whence is that one dismissed, this one struck, except because for this one struck the inheritance is kept, but that one dismissed is disinherited? He sees that he has no hope, and dismisses him to do what he wishes. But the boy who is scourged, if he has no heart, and is imprudent and foolish, congratulates his brother who is not beaten; and groans about himself, and says in his heart: My brother does such great evils, does whatever he wishes against the precepts of my father, and no one says a hard word to him; I, as soon as I stir, am beaten. He is foolish, he is imprudent; he attends to what he suffers, he does not attend to what is kept for him.

**9.** [vv. 7-8.] Therefore, when he had said: *Exceedingly deep are your thoughts;* he straightway subjoined: *The imprudent man shall not know, and the fool shall not understand these things.* What are the things which the fool shall not understand, and the imprudent shall not know? *When sinners spring up like grass.* What is, *like grass?* They are green when it is winter; but they will dry up in summer. You attend to the flower of the grass. What passes more quickly? What is more shining? what greener? Let not its greenness delight you, but fear its drying up. You heard *sinners like grass,* hear too of the righteous: *For behold.* Meanwhile see the sinners; like grass they flourish, well: but who are they who do not know? The fools and the imprudent. *When the sinners spring up like grass, and all who work iniquity look forth.* All who in their heart do not think rightly of God, have looked forth at the sinners springing up like grass, that is, flourishing for a time. Why do they look forth at them? *That they may perish forever and ever.* For they attend to their temporal flower, they imitate them, and wishing to flourish with them for a time, they perish forever: this is: *That they may perish forever and ever.*

**10.** [vv. 9-10.] *But you, O Lord, are the Most High forever.* Awaiting from above, from your eternity, when the time of the unjust may pass, and the time of the just may come. *For behold.* Attend, brothers. Now he himself who speaks (for he speaks from our person, he speaks from the person of the body of Christ: for Christ speaks in his body, that is, in his Church), now he has joined himself to the eternity of God: as I said to you a little before, God is longsuffering and patient, all these evils which he sees done by the evil he endures. Why? Because he is eternal, and sees what he keeps for them. Do you too wish to be longsuffering and patient? Join yourself to the eternity of God; with him await those things which are beneath you: for when your heart has clung to the Most High, all mortal things will be beneath you; and say what follows: *For behold, your enemies shall perish.* Those who now flourish, afterward shall perish. Who are the enemies of God? Brothers, do you perhaps suppose those alone to be enemies of God who blaspheme? They indeed are, and those savage ones who spare not the injuries of God either with tongue or with evil thoughts. And what do they do to God exalted, eternal? If you strike a column with your fist, you are hurt: and do you suppose that blasphemy, striking God, does not break you, since you do nothing to God? But the enemies of God are openly blasphemers, and daily the hidden ones are found. Beware of such enmities of God. For Scripture discloses certain hidden enemies of God; that, since you cannot know them with your heart, you may know them in the Scripture of God, and beware lest you be found with them. Openly James says in his Epistle: *Do you not know that the friend of this world is constituted an enemy of God*[^18]? You have heard. Do you not wish to be an enemy of God? Do not be a friend of this world: for if you are a friend of this world, you will be an enemy of God. For as a married woman cannot become an adulteress, unless she is an enemy of her husband: so the soul, an adulteress by the love of worldly things, cannot but be an enemy of God. It fears, but does not love: it fears the punishment, it is not delighted by righteousness. Therefore enemies of God are all the lovers of the world, all the seekers of trifles, all the consulters of soothsayers, astrologers, diviners. Let them enter the churches, let them not enter the churches; they are enemies of God. For a time they can flourish like grass; but they will perish, when he begins to look forth, and brings his judgment upon all flesh. Join yourself to the Scripture of God, and say it with this psalm: *For behold, your enemies shall perish.* Be not found there, where they shall perish. *And all who work iniquity shall be scattered.*

**11.** [v. 11.] What of you who labor now, if the enemies of God shall perish, and all who work iniquity shall be scattered? you who amid these scandals, amid human iniquities groan, who are troubled in the flesh, but rejoice in heart, what of you? what hope have you, O body of Christ? O Christ, who sit in heaven at the right hand of the Father, but with your feet and your members labor on earth, and say: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^19]? what hope will you have, if the enemies of God shall perish, and all who work iniquity shall be scattered? what will there be for you? *And my horn shall be exalted like that of a unicorn.* Why did he say, *like that of a unicorn?* Sometimes a unicorn signifies pride, sometimes a unicorn signifies the exaltation of unity: because unity is exalted, all heresies with the enemies of God shall perish. *And my horn shall be exalted like that of a unicorn.* And when will it be? *And my old age in rich mercy.* What did he say, *my old age?* My last things. As in our ages the last is old age; so all this which the body of Christ now suffers in labors, in troubles, in vigils, in hunger, in thirst, in scandals, in iniquities, in pressures, is its youth: its old age, that is, its last things, will be in gladness. And let Your Charity attend, because he said old age, lest you suppose death too: for a man in the flesh grows old for this end, that he may die. But the old age of the Church will be white with right deeds, but it will not be corrupted by death. What the head of an old man is, this will be our works. You see how the head grows white, and whitens, the more old age comes on. He who grows old well in his order, you sometimes seek for him a black hair on the head, and do not find it: so when our life is such, that the blackness of sins is sought, and not found; this old age is youthful, this old age is green, it will always be green. You heard of the grass of sinners, hear of the old age of the righteous: *My old age in rich mercy.*

**12.** [v. 12.] *And my eye has looked forth upon my enemies.* Whom does he call his enemies? All who work iniquity. Do not attend to the fact that the iniquitous is your friend: let a matter arise, and there you test him. You begin to come against his iniquity, and there you will see, that when he was caressing you, he was an enemy; but you had not yet struck, not that there might be set up in the heart what was not, but that what was might burst forth. *And my eye has looked forth upon my enemies; and my ear shall hear of those rising against me, the malignant.* When? In old age. What is, in old age? In the last things. And what shall our ear hear? Standing at the right hand we shall hear what shall be said to those who are at the left: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^20]. From this evil hearing the righteous shall not fear. You know that it was said in a certain psalm: *The righteous shall be in everlasting memory; he shall not fear from an evil hearing*[^21]. From what evil hearing? Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels. *And my ear shall hear of those rising against me, the malignant.*

**13.** [v. 13.] The grass passes, the flower of the sinners passes: what of the righteous? *The righteous shall flourish like a palm.* Those spring up like grass; *the righteous shall flourish like a palm.* By the palm he signified height. Perhaps he also signified this by the palm, that in its last parts it is beautiful; so that you draw its beginning from the earth, its end at the top, where it has all its beauty: a rough root is seen on the earth, the beautiful crown is under heaven. Therefore your beauty too will be at the end. Let your root be fixed: but we have our root turned upward. For our root is Christ, who ascended into heaven. Humbled, he will be exalted. *Like a cedar in Lebanon, he shall be multiplied.* See what trees he named: *The righteous shall flourish like a palm; like a cedar in Lebanon, he shall be multiplied.* When the sun comes out, does the palm dry up? does the cedar dry up? But when the sun has at some time grown hot, the grass dries up. The judgment, then, will come, that the sinners may dry up, and the faithful grow green. *Like a cedar in Lebanon, he shall be multiplied.*

**14.** [vv. 14-16.] *Planted in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God they shall flourish: They shall still be multiplied in a rich old age: and they shall be tranquil, that they may announce.* This is the sabbath which I commended to you a little before, whence the Psalm has its title. *They shall be tranquil, that they may announce.* Why do they announce tranquilly? The grass of the sinners does not move them: the cedar and the palm are not bent even in tempests. Therefore let them be tranquil, that they may announce: and truly, because it must now be announced even to men who sneer. O wretched men, lovers of the world! they announce to you who are planted in the house of the Lord; who confess to the Lord in song and on the harp, in word and in deed, they announce to you, and say to you: Do not be seduced by the happiness of the wicked, do not attend to the flower of the grass; do not attend to those happy for a time, wretched forever. Neither is that happiness which now appears outwardly true; nor are they happy in heart, because they are tormented by an evil conscience. But do you be tranquil, hoping in the promises of the Lord your God. For what will you announce in tranquility? *That the Lord God is upright, and there is no iniquity in him.* Attend, brothers, if you are planted in the house of the Lord, if you wish to flourish like a palm, and to be multiplied like a cedar of Lebanon, and not to dry up like grass when the sun grows hot; like those who seem to flourish when the sun is absent. If, then, you do not wish to be grass, but a palm and a cedar, what will you announce? *That the Lord God is upright, and there is no iniquity in him.* How is there no iniquity? He does so great evils; he is healthy, has children, a full house, abounds in glory, is exalted by honors, is avenged on his enemies, and commits all evils: another, innocent in his own affair, not seizing the goods of others, not acting against anyone, labors in chains, in prisons, in want he burns and sighs. How is there *no iniquity in him?* Be tranquil, and you will understand; for you are disturbed, and in your chamber you darken the light for yourself. The eternal God wishes to shine on you; do not make a cloud for yourself from the disturbance. Be tranquil in yourself, and see what I say to you. Because God is eternal, because he now spares the evil, leading them to repentance; he scourges the good, instructing them for the kingdom of heaven; *there is no iniquity in him*: do not fear. Behold, I have been scourged so much, it is manifest, I confess, I have sinned—for I do not say that I am righteous. For this most people say. When perhaps someone is in some misery, in pains, you enter to console him: and he: I have sinned, I confess, my sins are mine, I acknowledge them; but have I sinned as much as that man? I know how much that man did, I know how much he committed: my sins are mine, I confess them to God, but they are less than his; and behold, he suffers no evil. Do not be disturbed, be tranquil, that you may know *that the Lord is upright, and there is no iniquity in him.* What if he scourges you now for this reason, that he does not keep for you the everlasting fire? What if he dismisses that man now for this reason, that he is to hear: *Go into the eternal fire*? But when? When you have been set at the right hand, then it will be said to those set at the left: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^22]. Let not these things, then, move you: be tranquil, keep the sabbath, and announce *that the Lord God is upright, and there is no iniquity in him.*
