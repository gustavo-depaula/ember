# Exposition of Psalm 110

**1.** The days have come that we should sing Alleluia: be present in mind, brothers, to receive what the Lord suggests for our exhortation, and for the nourishing of love, by which *it is good for us to cling to God*[^1]. Be present in mind, good singers, sons of the praise and everlasting glory of the true and incorrupt God. Be present, intent, who know how to *sing and sing psalms in your hearts to the Lord, always giving thanks in all things*[^2]: and praise God; for this is Alleluia. And these days indeed come, about to pass, and pass, about to come, and they signify the day which does not come and pass, because it is neither preceded by a yesterday so that it may come, nor pressed by a tomorrow so that it may pass. To which indeed when we have come, clinging to it, we too will not pass: and as it is sung in a certain place to God, *Blessed are those who dwell in your house: for ages of ages they will praise you*[^3]; this will be the business of those at leisure, this the work of those at rest, this the action of the quiet, this the care of the secure. For just as these days solemnly succeed with welcome cheerfulness to the past days of Lent, by which the sorrow of this life before the resurrection of the Lord's body is signified: so that day which will be given after the resurrection to the full body of the Lord, that is, the holy Church, all the hardships and griefs of this life being shut out, will succeed with perpetual blessedness. But this life demands continence of us, that even with labor and struggle groaning, weighed down, and desiring our dwelling which is from heaven to be put on over us; we should temper ourselves from worldly delights: and this is signified by the number forty, by which both Moses and Elijah and the Lord himself fasted. For it is commanded us both from the Law, and from the Prophets, and from the Gospel itself (which has its testimony from the Law and the Prophets; whence too on the mountain the Savior shone in the midst between the two persons), that we restrain our eagerness as it were by the fast of temperance, from all the allurements of the world, by which captive men forget God; as long as the perfection of the Decalogue of the Law, as it were a psaltery of ten strings, is preached through the four parts of the same world, that is, in the whole world, so that ten taken four times signify the number forty. But by the number fifty after the resurrection of the Lord, in which we sing Alleluia, not the end and passing of any time, but that blessed eternity is signified; because, ten being added to forty, to the faithful laboring in this life that reward is paid which the householder prepared equal both for the first and for the last. Let us hear, then, the breast of the people of God full of divine praise. Behold, there sounds in this psalm a certain one exulting with happy exultation, prefiguring too a people gushing in heart in the love of God, namely the body of Christ, freed from all evil.

**2.** [vv. 1, 2.] *I will confess to you,* he says, *O Lord, with my whole heart.* Confession is not always of sins, but the praise of God too is brought forth by the devotion of confession. The former mourns, the latter rejoices; the former shows the wound to the physician, the latter gives thanks for health. This confession signifies a certain one, not only freed from all evil, but even separated from all the malign. And therefore let us see where he confesses to the Lord with his whole heart. *In the council,* he says, *of the upright, and in the congregation*: I believe, those who will sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel. For now no wicked one is among them, the thefts of no Judas are tolerated, no Simon Magus is baptized, wishing to buy the Spirit, while he thinks to sell it; no Alexander the coppersmith shows many evils, no one covered with a sheep's skin creeps in with false brotherhood: among such as now the Church must groan, and such as it then must shut out, when all the just will be gathered. These are the *great works of the Lord, sought out into all his wills*: through which his mercy deserts no one confessing, no one's iniquity is unpunished; since he scourges even every son whom he receives. But if the just is scarcely saved, where will the sinner and the impious appear? Let a man choose for himself whatever he wills: the works of the Lord are not so constituted that the creature, set in free choice, should overcome the will of the Creator, even if it acts against his will. God does not will that you sin; for he forbids it: yet if you have sinned, do not suppose that the man has done what he willed, and that to God has befallen what he did not will. For just as he wills that man not sin, so he wills to spare the sinner, that he turn back and live; so finally he wills to punish the one persevering in sin, that the obstinate one not escape the power of justice. So whatever you choose, the Almighty will not lack a means by which to fulfill his will concerning you. *For great are the works of the Lord, sought out into all his wills.*

**3.** [v. 3.] *Confession and magnificence is his work.* What is more magnificent than to justify the impious? But perhaps the work of man comes before this magnificence of God, so that, when he has confessed his sins, he may deserve to be justified. For *the Publican went down from the temple justified rather than the Pharisee;* because *he did not dare even to lift his eyes to heaven, but struck his breast, saying: God, be merciful to me a sinner*[^4]. This is the magnificence of the Lord, the justification of the sinner: *since he who humbles himself will be exalted; and he who exalts himself will be humbled*[^5]. This is the magnificence of the Lord; since to whom much is forgiven, he loves much. This the magnificence of the Lord; since *where sin abounded, grace too superabounded*[^6]. But perhaps from works. *Not,* he says, *from works, lest perhaps anyone be exalted. For we are his formation, created in Christ in good works*[^7]. For man does not work righteousness except being justified: but *believing in him who justifies the impious*[^8], he begins from faith; so that good works, not preceding what he deserved, but following what he received, may show it. Whence, then, that confession? It is not yet indeed a work of righteousness, but the disapproval of an offense: but however it may be, do not even in that, O man, glory in yourself, *that he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^9]. *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^10] Not, then, only the magnificence by which the impious is justified, but both *confession and magnificence is his work. What, then, do we say?* that *he has mercy on whom he wills, and hardens whom he wills; is there iniquity with God? Far from it*[^11]. *For his righteousness abides for the age of the age. But you, O man* of this age, *who are you who answer God?*[^12]

**4.** [vv. 4, 5.] *He made a memorial of his wonders*: humbling this one, and exalting that one. *He made a memorial of his wonders*: reserving opportunely unusual prodigies, which the weakness of man, intent on novelty, may remember, although his daily miracles are greater. He creates so many trees throughout the whole earth, and no one wonders: he dried up one by a word, and the hearts of mortals were stupefied; but *he made a memorial of his wonders.* For this miracle will most cling to attentive hearts, which constancy has not made cheap.

**5.** But what did the miracles profit, except that he might be feared? And further, what would fear profit, unless *the merciful and compassionate Lord* gave *food to those who fear him?* food which is not corrupted, bread which came down from heaven, which he gave by no merits. For indeed Christ died for the impious. No one, then, would give such food, except the merciful and compassionate Lord. But if he gave so much to this life, if the sinner to be justified received the Word made flesh; what will he, glorified, receive in the age to come? *For he will be mindful for the age of his covenant.* Nor did he give the whole who gave a pledge.

**6.** [vv. 6-9.] *He will announce to his people the strength of his works.* Let not the holy Israelites be saddened, who left all their things and followed him; let them not be saddened, saying: *Who, then, will be able to be saved?* Because *it is easier for a camel to enter through the eye of a needle, than a rich man into the kingdom of heaven*[^13]. For he announced to them the strength of his works; since *the things that are difficult for men are easy for God*[^14]. *That he may give them the inheritance of the nations.* For there was a going to the nations too, and it was commanded to the rich of this age not to be high-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches; but in the living God, to whom is easy what is difficult for men. For so many were called, so the inheritance of the nations was occupied, so it came to be that even very many who did not leave all their things in this life in order to follow him, even scorned that very life for the confession of his name; and as it were camels, humbling themselves to bear the burdens of afflictions, entered even as through the eye of a needle, through the pricking narrows of passion. He himself did these things, to whom all things are possible.

**7.** *The works of his hands are truth and judgment.* Let truth be held by those who are judged here. The martyrs are judged here, and are brought to the judgment, by which they may judge not only those by whom they were judged, but even the angels, against whom they had a wrestling, even when they seemed to be judged by men. Let not tribulation, distress, hunger, nakedness, the sword separate from Christ. *For all his commandments are faithful*: he does not deceive, he shows forth what he promised. Yet what he promised is not to be awaited here, not to be hoped for here; but *they are confirmed for the age of the age, made in truth and justice.* This is true and just, that here one labor, there one rest: because: *He sent redemption to his people.* But whence are they redeemed, except from the captivity of this pilgrimage? Therefore let rest be sought only in the heavenly homeland.

**8.** God indeed gave to the carnal Israelites the earthly Jerusalem, *which is in slavery with her children*[^15]: but this is the Old Testament, pertaining to the old man. But those who there understood the figure, stood forth even then as heirs of the New Testament; since *the Jerusalem which is above is free, which is our mother*[^16], eternal in the heavens. But by that old one, it was proved by the thing itself that he promised transitory things: *For he commanded* indeed *his covenant for the age.* But what, except the New? Of which, whoever you wish to be heir, I do not want you to deceive yourself, nor think carnally of a land flowing with milk and honey, do not meditate to attain pleasant estates, fruitful and shady gardens, or any such thing, such as the eye of greed is wont here to covet. For since cupidity is the root of all evils, it must be killed so that it be consumed here, not deferred so that it be satisfied there. First flee the punishments, avoid the Gehennas: before you desire God promising, beware of him threatening. *For holy and terrible is his name.*

**9.** [v. 10.] But instead of all the delights of this age, such as you have either experienced, or can increase and multiply by thinking, covet wisdom, the mother of immortal delights: but: *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord.* She will delight, and without doubt will ineffably delight with the chaste and eternal embraces of truth: but first your debts must be granted, before the rewards are demanded. *The beginning*, then, *of wisdom is the fear of the Lord. Good is understanding.* Who denies it? But to understand and not to do is dangerous. *Good*, then, *for those who do it.* Nor let it lift up the mind into pride. For of him whose fear is the beginning of wisdom: *His praise abides for the age of the age*: and this will be the reward, this the end, this the perpetual station and seat. There are found the faithful commandments, confirmed for the age of the age; this is the inheritance of the New Testament, the commandments for the age. *One thing I asked,* he says, *of the Lord, this I will seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life*[^17]. For blessed are those who dwell in the house of the Lord; for ages of ages they will praise him: since *his praise abides for the age of the age.*
