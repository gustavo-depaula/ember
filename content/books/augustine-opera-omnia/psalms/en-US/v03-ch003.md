# Exposition of Psalm 53

**1.** [vv. 1-2] The title of this psalm has the fruit of its own length, if it is understood; and because the psalm is brief, let us make up in the delay over the title what we shall not linger over in the psalm. For on it depends every verse that is sung. So if anyone recognizes what is set up over the front of the house, he will enter secure; nor, when he has entered, will he go astray. For this is noted beforehand on the very doorpost, how one may not go astray within. Its title runs thus: *Unto the end, in hymns, the understanding of David himself, when the Ziphites came and said to Saul: Behold, is not David hidden among us?*[^1] That Saul was the persecutor of the holy man David, we know very well; that Saul bore the figure of the temporal kingdom, pertaining not to life but to death, this too we remember to have commended to Your Charity. Likewise that David himself bore the figure of Christ, or of the body of Christ, this you who already know ought both to know and to recall. What, then, of the Ziphites? Ziph was a certain village whose inhabitants were the Ziphites, in whose region David had hidden himself, when Saul wished to find and kill him. These Ziphites, then, when they had learned this, betrayed him to the persecuting king, saying: *Behold, is not David hidden among us?* Their betrayal indeed profited them nothing, and harmed David nothing. For their malign mind was shown forth: but Saul, even after their betrayal, could not seize David; rather, in a certain cave of that very region, when Saul had been given over to David's hands to be killed, David spared him, and did not do what was in his power. But that other one sought to do what was not in his power. So let the Ziphites themselves see who they were; let us see whom the psalm, on the occasion of them, offers us to understand.

**2.** If, then, we ask what the Ziphites mean, we find "Flourishing ones." Flourishing ones, then, were certain enemies of the holy David, flourishing while he lay hidden. Let us find them in the human race, if we wish to understand the psalm. Let us find here first David hidden, and we shall find against him flourishing adversaries. Attend to David hidden: *For you are dead*, says the Apostle to the members of Christ, *and your life is hidden with Christ in God.*[^2] These hidden ones, then, when will they be flourishing? *When Christ*, he says, *your life appears, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^3]. When these will be flourishing, then those Ziphites will be withering. For to what flower of theirs may glory be compared, attend: *All flesh is grass, and all the honor of the flesh is like the flower of grass.* What is the end? *The grass has withered, and the flower has fallen.* Where then will David be? See what follows: *But the word of the Lord abides forever*[^4]. These, then, are two kinds of men, which you ought both to distinguish and, of them, to choose the one. For what does it profit you to know, if you are too lazy to choose? And indeed now the power of choosing lies at hand: a time will come when the power of choosing will be lacking to you, when God will no longer defer the sentence of judging. For who are these flourishing Ziphites, except that body of Doeg the Edomite, of whom we have already spoken to Your Charity a few days ago: of whom it was said: *Behold the man who did not make God his helper, but hoped in the abundance of his riches, and prevailed in his vanity*[^5]? These are the flourishing sons of the age, who, as you just now heard from the Gospel, are in their generation more shrewd than the sons of light. For they themselves seem to look ahead to the future, whither they do not know whether they are going. You heard what that steward did to his lord, doing good for himself out of his lord's property, and bestowing it on his lord's debtors, so that when he was removed from the stewardship, he might be received by them. And although he committed a fraud against his lord, yet his lord praised his heart, attending not to his own loss, but to the man's cleverness. How much more ought we, with our Lord Jesus Christ himself admonishing us, to make ourselves friends out of the mammon of iniquity? For mammon means "riches." But our riches are there, where our eternal home is in the heavens. Accordingly, those who cannot flourish except temporally, and who do not wish to make themselves friends for eternity out of these, call temporal money "riches," because they have not known true riches. These riches, then, only iniquity reckons such, flourishing for a time like grass. These are the Ziphites, the enemies of David, flourishing in the age.

**3.** These, at times, even the weak sons of light attend to, and their feet totter at them, when they see the wicked flourishing in good fortune; and they say within themselves: What does my innocence profit me? What does it bring me that I serve God, that I keep his commandments, that I oppress no one, that I rob nothing from anyone, that I harm no one, that I help whom I can? Behold, I do all these things, and they flourish, while I labor. What of it? Would you too wish to be a Ziphite? They flourish in the age, they will wither in the judgment, and after the withering they will be cast into the eternal fire: would you too wish this? Do you not know what he who came to you has promised you, what he showed here in himself? If the flower of the Ziphites were to be desired, would not your very Lord have flourished even in this age? Or indeed, was the power to flourish here lacking to him? But he chose rather to lie hidden here among the Ziphites, and to say to Pontius Pilate, who questioned him as though he too were of the flower of the Ziphites and suspicious of his kingdom: *My kingdom is not of this world*[^6]. So here he lay hidden: and all the good lie hidden here, because their good is within, hidden, in the heart, where faith is, where love is, where hope is, where their treasure is. Surely these goods do not appear in the age? Both these goods lie hidden, and the reward of these goods lies hidden. But the dignity of the age, how white it is? It shines for a time: will it always shine? It is a winter herb, green until summer. Let there not, then, come about in the mind what we find in another psalm. For someone there confesses that he nearly fell while tottering, and that his steps slipped as he walked in the way of God, while he looked at a certain flower and the happiness of the unrighteous: and after he came to know what God reserved for the unrighteous in the end, and what he promises to the laboring righteous, who cannot deceive, giving thanks for this knowledge, he says: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart!* Why do you say this? *But my feet*, he says, *were almost moved.* Whence? *Because I was zealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners.* But his steps were strengthened, after he understood concerning the last things. For what he says a little after in the same psalm: *This is a labor before me*: that is, a great question arose in my heart, why men do evil and flourish in the age; while many do well and labor on this earth. Since, then, this question was great before my eyes, and laborious to investigate, *It is a labor*, he says, *before me, until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand concerning the last things*[^7]. What are these last things? What, except those we already know foretold in the Gospel? *For when the Son of man comes, all the nations will be gathered before him; and he will separate them, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats: the sheep he will set on the right, the goats on the left*[^8]. Behold, now those Ziphites will be separated: flame follows the separation. Where is the flower of those who will then stand on the left? Will they not then groan? will they not then be tormented by a late repentance, and say: *What has pride profited us, and what has the boasting of riches brought us? All those things have passed away like a shadow*[^9]? O Ziphites standing on the left, too late do you repent of having flourished in the shadow! Why did you not recognize David, whom you betrayed as hiding among you? For if you were then corrected, this grief would not be fruitless. For there is a fruitful grief, and a fruitless one; the fruitful grief is now, when you accuse yourself, when you reproach in yourself your evil ways, when, having reproached them, you persecute them, when, having persecuted them, you shut them out, and, having shut them out, you are changed, putting off the old man and putting on the new, choosing rather the reproach of Christ than the flower of the Ziphites. But if it should befall you, who have your good in secret and lie hidden among the Ziphites, who have also in secret the promise of your reward, to come to some height of the age, do not be lifted up: for if you are lifted up by it, you will fall into the flower of the Ziphites. For so a certain holy woman, Esther, in that people of the Jews of that time, being the wife of a foreign king, when the danger of her fellow citizens came about, so that she should plead with the king for her fellow citizens, began to pray, and in that very prayer confessed all those royal insignia to be to her like the rag of a menstruous woman. If these women can do this, can men not? And if this Jewish woman could, will the Christian Church not be able? This, then, let me say to Your Charity: *If riches flow in, do not set your heart on them*[^10]. Though they abound, and the prosperity of the age follow you, do not trust the sea, nor the smiling one. If riches flow in, if they abound, tread them underfoot, and hang from your God. For when you have them beneath you, and hang from him, when they are withdrawn, you will not fall. Lest perhaps, through an evil and by no means Christian thought, there come about in you what is said in another psalm, when the flower of these Ziphites is mentioned: *Too deep*, it says, *have your thoughts become.* I say it is said: *Too deep have your thoughts become: the imprudent man will not know, and the fool will not understand these things.* What will he not understand? *When sinners spring up like grass, and all who work iniquity look forth, that they may perish unto the age of the age*[^11]. The flower of the wicked delighted them: they said within themselves: Behold, the wicked flourish; I think God loves the wicked: and, delighted by the temporal flower of the unrighteous, they turned to iniquity, so as to perish. Not for a time, as those flourish, but unto the age of the age. Whence this? Because *the imprudent man will not know, and the fool will not understand these things*, not entering the sanctuary of God so as to understand concerning the last things. And because the understanding here is somewhat difficult, this psalm therefore began thus, that David should lie hidden among the Ziphites, and not be delighted by the flower of the Ziphites; but should rather choose among them humility, so as to have with God a hidden brightness. What, then, is attributed to him in this title? *Unto the end, in hymns*: that is, in praises. By what praises? *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^12]. As though he had seemed to wither, having lost all his substance? Far from it. The leaves had fallen, the root was alive. Therefore: *Unto the end, in hymns.* What is: *The understanding of David himself*? *Understanding*, against that: *The imprudent man will not know, and the fool will not understand these things. The understanding of David himself, when the Ziphites came and said to Saul: Behold, is not David hidden among us?* And let him be hidden among you, provided he does not flourish like you. Hear, then, his voice.

**4.** [v. 3] *O God, save me in your name, and judge me in your power.*[^13] Let the Church say this, lying hidden among the Ziphites. Let the Christian body say this, having in secret the good of its ways, hoping from the secret the reward of its merits; let it say this: *O God, save me in your name, and judge me in your power.* You came, O Christ, you appeared humble, you were despised, you were scourged, you were crucified, you were killed: but on the third day you rose, on the fortieth day you ascended into heaven, you sit at the right hand of the Father, and no one sees it. From there you sent your Spirit, whom the worthy received; filled with your love, they preached throughout the world and the nations the praise of that very humility of yours: I see your name excelling among the human race, but yet you were preached to us as weak. For neither did that teacher of the Gentiles say that he knew anything among us, except Christ Jesus, and him crucified, that we might choose his reproach rather than the glory of the flourishing Ziphites. But what does he say of him? *Although he died of weakness, yet he lives of the power of God*[^14]. He came, then, to die of weakness; he is to come to judge in the power of God: but through the weakness of the cross his name was glorified. Whoever has not believed in the name glorified through weakness will be terrified at the judge when he comes in power. But lest he who was once weak, when he comes strong, cast us with that winnowing-fan to the left, let him save us in his name, and judge us in his power. For who would be so rash as to wish to say to God: *Judge me*? Is it not usually said to men as a curse: May God judge you? It is plainly a curse, if he judges you in his power, but does not save you in his name: but when he has first saved you in his name, he will, to your good, judge you in the consequent power. Be secure: that judgment will not be punishment to you, but discernment. For in a certain psalm it is also said thus: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^15]. What is: *Judge me*? Distinguish me from the Ziphites among whom I lie hidden: I have borne their flower, now let my flower too come. And theirs indeed was a temporal flower, and with the withering of the grass it fell: but what will my flower be? *Planted in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God they will flourish*[^16]. There remains, then, a flower for us too, but one that does not fall, like the leaf of that tree planted beside the waters, of which it was said: *And its leaf will not fall*[^17]. *O God*, then, *save me in your name, and judge me in your power.*

**5.** [v. 4] *O God, hear my prayer; give ear to the words of my mouth.*[^18] Let the words of my mouth reach your ears, because it is not the flower of the Ziphites that I desire from you. *Give ear to the words of my mouth*: do you give ear; for to the Ziphites, even though my prayer sounds, they do not hear, because they do not understand. For they rejoice in temporal things, and do not know how to desire eternal goods. Let my prayer reach you, shaken out and hurled from the desire of your eternal benefits: to your ears I send it forth; help it to reach you, lest it fail midway on the road, and, as if collapsed, fall down. But even if the goods I ask do not now come to me, I am nonetheless secure, because they will come afterward. For someone, even amid sins, is said to have asked God, and not to have been heard, to his own good. For worldly desires had incited him to pray; and, set amid temporal tribulations, he wished that the temporal tribulations might pass and the flower of the grass return, and he says: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* It is the very voice of Christ, but for his members. *The words*, he says, *of my sins: I cried to you by day, and you did not hear; and by night, and not unto folly for me*[^19]: that is, by night too I cried, and you did not hear; and yet this very thing, that you did not hear, you did not hear me to my folly, but rather you did not hear me to my wisdom, that I might understand what I ought to ask of you. For I was asking those things which perhaps I would receive to my harm. You ask for riches, O man! How many have been ruined through their riches! How do you know whether riches are going to profit you? Were not many of the poor more safely hidden; and, made rich, as soon as they began to shine, became a prey to the stronger? How much better were they hidden, how much better unknown, who began to be sought, not for what they were, but for what they had! In these temporal things, then, brethren, we admonish and exhort you in the Lord, that you ask for nothing as though fixed, but what God knows to be expedient for you. For what is expedient for you, you do not know at all. Sometimes what you think profits, harms; and what you think harms, this profits. For you are sick; do not dictate to the physician what medicines he should apply to you. If the teacher of the Gentiles, the apostle Paul, says: *For we do not know what we should pray for as we ought*[^20], how much more we? Yet he, when he seemed to himself to be praying prudently, that the thorn of the flesh might be taken from him, the angel of Satan that buffeted him, lest he be exalted by the greatness of the revelations, what did he hear from the Lord? Was what he wished done? No; but rather that what was expedient might be done. What, then, did he hear from the Lord? *Three times*, he says, *I asked the Lord that he might take it from me; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you: for power is made perfect in weakness*[^21]. I have placed the medicine on the wound: when I placed it, I know; when it is to be removed, I know. Let not the sick man withdraw from the physician's hands, let him not give counsel to the physician. So are all these temporal things. They are tribulations; if you worship God well, you will know that he knows what is expedient for each one: they are prosperities; rather beware lest these corrupt your mind, so that it withdraw from him who gave these things. So this man, understanding, what does he say? *O God, hear my prayer; give ear to the words of my mouth.*

**6.** [v. 5] *For strangers have risen up against me.*[^22] What strangers? Was not David himself a Jew of the tribe of Judah? And the place Ziph itself belonged to the tribe of Judah, it was of the Jews. How, then, strangers? Not by city, not by tribe, not by kinship, but by flower. For do you wish to know these strangers? In another psalm they are called alien sons, *whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* And he enumerates the flower of the Ziphites: *Whose sons are like new plants established in their youth; their daughters arranged and adorned like the likeness of a temple; their storehouses full, overflowing from this into that; their sheep fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth; their oxen fat: there is no breach of the wall, no going out, nor cry in their streets.* But see the Ziphites, see them flourishing for a time. *They called the people blessed that has these things.* Deservedly alien sons. But you, what, lying hidden among the Ziphites? *Blessed is the people whose Lord is its God*[^23]. From this disposition this prayer is sent to the ears of the Lord, when it is said: *Give ear to the words of my mouth; for strangers have risen up against me.*

**7.** *And the mighty have sought my soul.*[^24] For in a new way, my brethren, all who have their hope in this age wish to destroy the kind of men who are holy and who abstain from the hope of this age. Surely they are grown together, surely they live together. These two kinds are vehemently opposed to each other: one of those who place their hope only in worldly things and temporal happiness, and the other of those who firmly place their hope in the Lord their God. And when these Ziphites are at peace with you, do not trust much in their peace; temptations are lacking: when some temptation comes, so that someone is reproved for the flower of the age, I do not say he will take offense at the bishop, but he is unwilling even to approach the Church itself, lest some grass fall away. Why have I said these things, brethren? Because now you all gladly hear in the name of Christ, and as you understand, so you cry out at the word; you would certainly not acclaim unless you understood. This understanding of yours ought to be fruitful. But whether it is fruitful, temptation asks; lest suddenly, when you are called ours, you be found through temptation to be strangers, and it be said: *Strangers have risen up against me, and the mighty have sought my soul*; lest what follows be said: *They have not set God before their sight.* For when will he set God before his sight, before whose sight there is nothing but the age?—how to pile coin upon coin, how to increase the flocks, how to fill the storehouses, how to say to the soul: You have many goods; be merry, feast, be filled. Surely he does not set before his sight the one who says to him who glories thus and shines so in the flower of the Ziphites: *Fool*, that is, not understanding, imprudent man, *this night your soul will be taken from you; whose will these things be that you have prepared?*[^25] *They have not set God before their sight.*

**8.** [v. 6] *For behold, God helps me.*[^26] And they themselves, among whom I lie hidden, do not know it. But if they too set God before their sight, they would find how God helps me. For all the saints are helped by God, but within, where no one sees. For as conscience is a great punishment to the ungodly, so conscience itself is a great joy to the godly. *For this is our glory*, says the Apostle, *the testimony of our conscience*[^27]. In this that man glories within, not in the flower of the Ziphites outside, who now says: *For behold, God helps me.* Indeed, though the things he promises are far off in the future, today his help is sweet and present to me; today, in the joy of my heart, I find that without cause certain men say: *Who will show us good things? For the light of your countenance is sealed upon us, O Lord; you have given gladness in my heart*[^28]: not in my vineyard, not in my flock, not in my cask, not in my table, but *in my heart. For behold, God helps me.* How does he help you? *And the Lord is the upholder of my soul.*

**9.** [v. 7] *Turn back evils upon my enemies.*[^29] As they are green, as they flourish, so they are kept for the fire. *In your power destroy them.* Suppose they flourish now, suppose they spring up like grass: do not you be an imprudent and foolish man, so as to perish unto the age of the age by attending to these things. For *turn back evils upon my enemies.* For if you are in the body of David himself, in his power he will destroy them. These flourish in the happiness of the age, they perish in the power of God. They do not perish as they flourish: for they flourish for a time, they perish forever; they flourish with false goods, they perish with true torments. *In your power destroy them*: whom in your weakness you have borne with.

**10.** [v. 8] *Willingly I will sacrifice to you.*[^30] Who can even understand this good of the heart when another speaks of it, unless he has tasted it in himself? What is: *Willingly I will sacrifice to you*? Yet I will say it; let him grasp it who can, as he can: let him believe who cannot, and pray that he may be able. Yet ought we to pass over this verse so as not to commend it to you? I tell Your Charity, the love of it itself invites me to speak something of it; and I give thanks to God that you listen attentively. But if I saw you weary of listening, I would unwillingly be silent on this verse; and yet in my heart, as far as the Lord would deign to grant, I would not be silent. So let what was conceived in the heart come to the tongue; let what is kept in the mind be brought forth by the voice: let us say, as we can, what it is: *Willingly I will sacrifice to you.* For what sacrifice shall I take here, brethren? or what shall I worthily offer to the Lord for his mercy? Shall I seek victims from the flock of sheep, shall I choose a ram, look out for some bull among the herds, certainly bring incense from the land of the Sabaeans? What shall I do? What shall I offer, except what he says: *The sacrifice of praise will honor me*[^31]? Why, then, *willingly*? Because I love freely what I praise. I praise God, and in the very praise I rejoice: I rejoice in his praise, by which, when he is praised, I do not blush. For it is not as a charioteer, or a hunter, or any actor, is praised by the enthusiasts of theatrical trifles, and others are invited by his praisers, urged to cry out together; and when all have cried out, often, when he is beaten, all blush: our God is not so. Let him be praised by the will, loved with love; let what is loved be freely given, and what is praised. What is "freely given"? He himself for his own sake, not for the sake of something else. For if you praise God so that he may give you something else, you no longer love God freely. You would blush if your wife loved you for the sake of riches; and perhaps if poverty befell you, she would think of adultery. Since, then, you wish to be loved freely by your spouse, will you love God for the sake of something else? What reward are you to receive from God, O greedy one? He keeps for you not the earth, but himself, who made heaven and earth. *Willingly I will sacrifice to you*: not from necessity. For if you praise God for the sake of something else, you praise from necessity. If what you love were at hand for you, you would not praise God. See what I am saying: you praise God, for example, that he may give you ample money; if you had ample money from elsewhere, not from God, would you praise God? If, then, you praise God for the sake of money, you do not willingly sacrifice to God, but sacrifice from necessity: because besides him you love something else, I know not what. Hence it was said: *Willingly I will sacrifice to you.* Despise all things, attend to him. And these things he has given are good for the sake of the giver. For he does indeed give, he gives these temporal things; and to some for their good, to some for their harm, according to the height and depth of his judgments. In the abyss of which judgments the Apostle was awed, saying: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God, how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! For who shall search out his ways, or who shall comprehend his counsels?*[^32] He knows when to give, to whom to give; when to take away, and from whom to take away. Ask in this time what may profit you hereafter, ask what may help you for eternity. But love him freely: because you find nothing better that he can give than himself; or if you find anything better, ask for that. *Willingly I will sacrifice to you.* Why *willingly*? Because freely. What is "freely"? *And I will confess to your name, O Lord, because it is good*: for no other reason, except that it is good. Surely it does not say: I will confess to your name, O Lord, because you give me fruitful estates, because you give me gold and silver, because you give me broad riches, ample money, the most excellent dignity? No. But what? *Because it is good.* I find nothing better than your name: therefore *I will confess to your name, O Lord, because it is good.*

**11.** [v. 9] *Because you have rescued me from all tribulation.*[^33] Therefore I understood that your name is good: for if I could have recognized this before my tribulations, perhaps they would not have been necessary for me. But tribulation was applied for admonition, admonition was made for your praise. For I would not understand where I was, unless I had been admonished by my weakness. From all tribulations, then, you have rescued me. *And my eye has looked down upon my enemies*: my eye has looked down upon those Ziphites. For I passed beyond their flower by the height of my heart, I reached you, and from there I looked down upon them, and saw that *all flesh is grass, and all the splendor of man is like the flower of grass*[^34]: as it is likewise said in a certain place: *I saw the ungodly exalted on high and lifted up like the cedars of Lebanon: I passed by, and behold, he was not.* Why *was he not*? Because you passed by? What does it mean, "because you passed by"? Because it was not without cause: Lift up your heart, you heard, because you did not remain on the earth where you would rot, because you raised your soul to God, and surpassed the cedars of Lebanon, and from that height attended: *and behold, he was not*, and you sought him, *and his place was not found*[^35]. Now there is no labor before you; because you have entered into the sanctuary of God, and understood concerning the last things. So here too it concludes thus: *And my eye has looked down upon my enemies.* Do this, then, brethren, with your souls: lift up your hearts, polish the edge of your mind, learn to love God freely, learn to despise the present age, learn willingly to sacrifice the victim of praise; so that, surpassing the flower of grass, you may look down upon your enemies.
