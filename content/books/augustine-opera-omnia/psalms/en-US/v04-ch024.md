# Exposition of Psalm 94

**1.** [v. 1.] I would wish, brothers, that we should rather hear our father; but this too is good, that we obey our father. Since, then, he bade us, he who deigns to pray for us, I will speak to Your Charity concerning the present psalm what the Lord God of us all will deign to grant. But the title of the Psalm is: *The praise of a song to David himself. The praise of a song* signifies both cheerfulness, because it is a song; and devotion, because it is praise. For what ought a man more to praise, than that which so pleases, that it cannot displease him? The security of praise, then, is in the praise of God. There the praiser is secure, where he does not fear lest he blush at the one praised. Let us, then, both praise, and sing; that is, let us praise with cheerfulness and with gladness. But what we are to praise, the Psalm itself in the following verses indicates to us.

**2.** *Come, let us exult to the Lord.* He invites to a great banquet of exulting, not in the world, but in the Lord. For unless there were in this world an evil exultation, which is to be distinguished from the good exultation, it would suffice to say: *Come, let us exult*: but he distinguished it briefly. What is it to exult well? To exult to the Lord. Therefore the evil exultation is, to exult in the world; the good exultation is, to exult in the Lord. You ought piously to exult to the Lord, if you wish securely to insult the world. But what is: *Come?* Whence does he call those to come, with whom he wishes to exult to the Lord; except because they are far off, that by coming they may draw near, by drawing near they may approach, by approaching they may exult? But whence are they far off? Can a man be far in places from him who is everywhere? Do you wish to be far from him? Whither will you go, that you may be far? For a certain sinner indeed, but yet with hope of salvation, repenting and sorrowing for his sins, and fearing the anger of God, and wishing to appease God, so speaks in another psalm: *Whither shall I go from your spirit? and from your face whither shall I flee? If I ascend into heaven, you are there.* What, then, remains? Because if he ascends into heaven, there he finds God: that he may flee far from God, whither will he go? See what he says: *If I descend into hell, you are present*[^1]. If, then, by ascending into heaven, he there finds God; by descending into hell, he does not flee God: whither will he go, whither will he flee from him angry, except to him appeased? And yet, since absolutely no one can flee from him who is everywhere, unless some were far from God, it would not be said: *This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me*[^2]. Therefore no one is far from God in place, but by unlikeness. What is, by unlikeness? By an evil life, by evil conduct. For if by good conduct one draws near to God; by evil conduct one withdraws from God. One and the same man, then, standing in body in one place, both by loving God draws near to God, and by loving iniquity withdraws from God: he nowhere moves his feet, and yet he can both draw near and withdraw. For our feet in this journey are our affections. As each one has affection, as each one has love, so he draws near to or withdraws from God. Do we not often say, when we find some things unlike: This is far from that? When we compare perhaps two men, two horses, two garments, and someone says: This garment is like, it is such as that one; or: This man is such as that one: what does another say, who contradicts? Far be it; this is far from that. What is, this is far from that? It is unlike it. And they stand next to each other, and yet this is far from that. But two iniquitous men, equal in life and conduct, if one is in the East, the other in the West, are next to each other. And two righteous likewise, let one be in the East, the other in the West, they are together, because they are in God. On the contrary, one righteous, the other iniquitous, even if they be bound by one chain, are far separated from each other. Therefore if by unlikeness we withdraw from God, by likeness we draw near to God. By what likeness? To which we were made, which we had corrupted in ourselves by sinning, which we received by the remission of sins, which is renewed in us within in the mind, that as it were the image of our God may be re-sculpted in the coin, that is, in our soul, and we may return to his treasures. For whence, brothers, did our Lord Jesus Christ wish from a coin to show his tempters what God seeks? For when, concerning the tribute of Caesar, seeking an occasion of calumny, they wished to consult the master of truth, and by consulting to tempt whether it was lawful to give tribute to Caesar; what did he say? *Why do you tempt me, hypocrites?* He asked a coin to be brought to him, and it was brought. *Whose image,* he said, *does it have?* They answered: *Caesar's.* And he: *Render, then, to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's*[^3]. As if he said: If Caesar seeks in a coin his image, does not God seek in man his image? To this likeness our Lord Jesus Christ, inviting us, commands us to love even our enemies; and gives the example from God himself: *As your Father,* he says, *who is in heaven, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust. Be, then, perfect as your Father*[^4]. When he says: *Be perfect as he,* he invites us to likeness. If, then, he invites us to likeness, it is established that by being unlike we had withdrawn from God, and had been made far by unlikeness, and are made near by likeness, that now there may come about in us what is written: *Draw near to God, and be enlightened*[^5]. Therefore to certain ones set far off and living evilly this psalm says: *Come, let us exult to the Lord.* Whither do you go? whither do you withdraw? whither do you depart? whither do you flee, by exulting in the world? *Come, let us exult to the Lord.* Whither do you go to exult, where you fail? Come, let us exult in him by whom we were made. *Come, let us exult to the Lord.*

**3.** *Let us make a joyful noise to God our salvation.* What is it to make a joyful noise (iubilare)? Not to be able to explain in words the joy, and yet by the voice to testify what is conceived within and cannot be explained in words: this is to make a joyful noise. For let Your Charity consider those who make a joyful noise in certain songs, and as it were in a certain contest of worldly gladness; and you see them, as it were amid songs expressed in words, overflowing with a gladness for which the tongue does not suffice in speaking, how they make a joyful noise, that through that voice the affection of the soul may be indicated, unable to explain in words what is conceived in the heart. If, then, they make a joyful noise out of earthly joy; ought we not to make a joyful noise out of heavenly joy, which truly we cannot explain in words?

**4.** [v. 2.] *Let us anticipate his face in confession.* Confession indeed is taken in two ways in the Scriptures. There is the confession of one praising, there is the confession of one groaning. The confession of one praising pertains to the honor of him who is praised: the confession of one groaning pertains to the penitence of him who confesses. For men confess, when they praise God: they confess, when they accuse themselves; and the tongue does nothing more worthy. Truly I think that these are the vows of which he speaks in another psalm: *I will render to you my vows, which my lips have distinguished*[^6]. Nothing is more sublime than this distinction, nothing so necessary both to understand and to do. How, then, do you distinguish the vows which you render to God? That you praise him, accuse yourself; because his is the mercy, to forgive our sins. For if he wished to act according to merits, he would find none but those whom he would condemn. *Come,* then, he said, that we may now withdraw from our sins, and that he may not lead the reckoning with us about past things; but that as it were new tablets be made, all the bonds of our debts being burned. How great, then, his praise, how great his mercy, let us confess, surely praising him. For if confession were always of the penitent, it would not be said in the Gospel of the Lord himself: *In that hour Jesus exulted in the Holy Spirit, and said: I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones*[^7]. Was Christ a penitent because he confessed? He could repent of nothing, because he had done nothing blameworthy; but he confessed in the praise of the Father. Therefore, because here too there is a place of exultation, perhaps we ought to understand that confession which is in the praise of God: whence too it is *the praise of a song,* that here we should understand not the confession of penitents, but of those praising. And what is it that immediately he admonishes us about a certain confession when he says: *Let us anticipate his face in confession?* What is: *Let us anticipate his face in confession?* He is to come; *Let us anticipate his face in confession,* first: before he comes, let us, by confessing, condemn what we have done, that he may find what to crown, not what to condemn. But does this too not pertain to the praise of God, when you confess your sins? Rather, it pertains most to the praise of God. Why does it pertain most to the praise of God? Because the physician is so much the more praised, the more the sick man was despaired of. Confess, then, your sins, the more you despaired of yourself on account of your iniquities. For the praise of the one forgiving is so much the greater, the greater the exaggeration of the one confessing his sins. Let us not, then, suppose that we have withdrawn from the praise of the song, if we here understand this confession, by which we confess our sins: this too pertains to the praise of the song; because when we recognize our sins, we commend the glory of God. *Let us anticipate his face in confession.*

**5.** [vv. 3-5.] *And in psalms let us make a joyful noise to him.* We have already said what it is to make a joyful noise; it is repeated, that it may be confirmed in doing: the very repetition is an exhortation. For we have not now forgotten, so that we should now wish to be reminded, what was said above, that we should make a joyful noise: but often in the affection of the soul a word that was known is repeated, not that it may become known, but that the very repetition may make confirmation; for it is repeated to understand the affection of the speaker. Hence it is that the Lord says: *Amen, amen I say to you*[^8]; one *Amen* would suffice; why: *Amen, amen,* except because the very repetition is a confirmation? *In psalms,* then, he says, *let us make a joyful noise to him.* And what shall we say in psalms? what shall we say, or rather feel in the joyful noise? what are the things that pertain to the praise of this song? Now hear: *For God is a great Lord, and a great king above all gods*: therefore, then, let us make a joyful noise to him. *For the Lord will not cast off his people*: let us make a joyful noise to him. *For in his hand are the ends of the earth, and the heights of the mountains are his*: in all these let us make a joyful noise to him. *For his is the sea, and he made it, and the dry land his hands formed*: let us make a joyful noise to him. But what all these things mean, if they be fittingly discussed, perhaps the time does not suffice: but again, if they are utterly neglected, we shall remain debtors. Briefly, then, for the time, as far as we have been able to touch on it, receive it; because even a few seeds bring back a most abundant harvest, if the soil is fruitful.

**6.** First, why we should make a joyful noise, why we should praise, he set this: *For God is a great Lord, and a great king above all gods.* There are, then, gods above whom our God is great, to whom we make a joyful noise, to whom we exult, to whom we say the praises of the song; there are, but not for us. For the Apostle says: *For even if there are those who are called gods, whether in heaven or on earth, as there are gods many and lords many: yet to us there is one God, from whom are all things, and we through him, and one Lord of ours Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we in him*[^9]. If, then, not for us, for whom? Hear from another psalm: *For the gods of the Gentiles are demons; but the Lord made the heavens*[^10]. More magnificently and more briefly the Holy Spirit could not commend to you your God and Lord through the Prophet. For it was little that God is terrible above all the demons: what great thing, to be above all the demons? *For the gods of the Gentiles are demons.* But where is your God? *But the Lord made the heavens.* Your Lord made that, where the demons cannot dwell: for from the heavens the demons were cast down. The heavens are set before the demons, your Lord is above the heavens too; because your Lord made the heavens too. How much higher, then, than the demons, the gods of the Gentiles, is he who is higher than the heavens, whence the angels fell, that they might become demons? And yet all the nations were under the demons: temples were built for the demons, altars constructed for the demons, priests instituted for the demons, sacrifices offered to the demons, the possessed as it were soothsayers brought in for the demons. All these things the nations displayed for the demons; all these things truly are owed to none but the one great God: the nations made a temple for the demons; God has a temple: the nations made priests for the demons; God has a priest: the nations displayed a sacrifice for the demons; God has a sacrifice. For those demons, wishing to seem gods, would not exact these things for themselves to deceive, except because they know that they are owed to the true God. For it is the custom that what is owed to the true God, the false god exacts for himself. Therefore we recognize the true temple of God. *For the temple of God is holy,* he says, *which you are*[^11]. If, then, we are the temple of God; the altar of God is our soul. What is the sacrifice of God? Perhaps we are now doing this, we set on the altar a sacrifice, when we praise God; for the psalm teaches us, saying: *A sacrifice of praise will glorify me, and there is the way, where I will show him the salvation of God*[^12]. But if you seek the priest, he is above the heavens; he intercedes for you, who on earth died for you[^13]. Therefore *God is a great Lord, and a great king above all gods.* Here take men as *gods*: for the Lord is not king above the demons. And of this we have the testimony of Scripture: *God stood in the synagogue of gods; and in the midst he distinguishes gods*[^14]. He called them *gods* by participation, not by nature; by grace, by which he willed to make gods. How great is the God who makes gods? Or what kind are the gods which a man makes? As great as he in making gods, so great a nothing are these who are made by a man. The true God makes those believing in him gods, to whom he gave power to become sons of God[^15]. And therefore he is the true God, because God was not made: but we, made, not true gods, yet better than those whom a man makes. Since the idols of the Gentiles are silver and gold, the work of men's hands: they have a mouth, and will not speak; they have eyes, and will not see[^16]. But our God made for us seeing eyes. Nor yet in that did he make us gods, because he made for us seeing eyes; for he did this for the cattle too: but therefore he made us gods, because he enlightened our inner eyes. Therefore let there be praise to him, confession to him, let there be a joyful noise to him: *For God is a great Lord, and a great king above all gods.*

**7.** *For the Lord will not cast off his people*: praise to him, a joyful noise to him: What people will he not cast off? Here it is not lawful for us to interpret anything: we have it prescribed by the Apostle, the Apostle expounded whence this was said[^17]. For there was the Jewish people, the people where the Prophets were, the people where the Patriarchs were, the people propagated even according to the flesh from the seed of Abraham; the people in which all the sacraments went before promising our Savior; the people where the temple was instituted, the anointing, the priest, as a figure, that when all those very shadows passed, the light itself might come: this, then, was the people of God; to it the Prophets were sent, in it were born those who were sent; to it the utterances of God were handed over and entrusted. What, then? was all that condemned? Far be it. The olive tree itself was spoken of by the Apostle, for this tree began from the Patriarchs: but there were dry branches there, because too high through pride; cut off, then, on account of barrenness, the wild olive grafted in on account of humility. But yet, dearly beloved, lest the grafted wild olive should be proud, what does the Apostle say? *If you, cut from a natural wild olive, and against nature grafted into a good olive; how much more shall those who are according to nature be grafted into their own olive*[^18]? That, as you, not remaining in unbelief, deserved to be grafted into the olive, when you were a wild olive; so those, corrected, may the more easily be grafted naturally into their own olive: this the Apostle says of them. Therefore it is the same tree; even if some of the branches were broken off, not all. For if all the branches were broken off, whence Peter? whence John? whence Thomas? whence Matthew? whence Andrew? whence all those Apostles? whence the apostle Paul himself who was speaking, and by his fruit testified to the olive? Are not all these from there? Whence too those five hundred brethren to whom the Lord appeared after the resurrection[^19]? whence so many thousands at the voice of Peter, when, filled with the Holy Spirit, the Apostles spoke in the tongues of all nations, with so great an eagerness for the praise of God and their own accusation were converted, that those who first shed the blood of the Lord raging, learned to drink it now believing[^20]? But all those thousands of men were so converted, that they sold their goods, and laid the prices of their goods at the feet of the Apostles[^21]. What one rich man did not do, when he heard from the mouth of the Lord, and withdrew sad from the Lord[^22], this so many thousands of those men did suddenly, by whose hands Christ had been crucified. The greater the wound was in their heart, the more eagerly they sought the physician. Since, then, all those were from there, of them the Psalm now says: *For the Lord will not cast off his people.* For this testimony of the Psalm, when he spoke of this, the Apostle used, and said: *What, then, shall we say, brothers? Has God cast off his people; whom he foreknew? Far be it: for I too am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin. God has not cast off his people, whom he foreknew*[^23]. Surely if the Lord cast off his people, there would be no source whence the Apostle himself might be: but whence he himself, thence others too. In these is the people of the Lord, not in all; but, as it is written: *The remnant shall be saved*[^24]. Not in all; but the threshing-floor being winnowed, the heap was put within, the chaff lies[^25]. Whatever you see of the reprobate Jews, you see chaff. Whence you see this chaff, thence the heap has already gone out, it has already been stored in the granary. Let us see both things, let us distinguish both.

**8.** [v. 4.] What does the Psalm add? *For in his hand are the ends of the earth.* We recognize the cornerstone; the cornerstone is Christ. There cannot be a corner, unless it has joined to itself two walls: they come to the corner from different sides, but in the corner they do not oppose each other. From one side came the circumcision, from another side came the uncircumcision: in Christ both peoples agreed; because he was made the stone, of which it is written: *The stone which the builders rejected, this was made the head of the corner*[^26]. If, then, Christ is the head of the corner, let us not attend to the diversity of those coming from afar, but to the nearness of those cohering in Christ. There it is enacted, there let us see: *For the Lord will not cast off his people.* Behold one wall, in which now, as we said, *the Lord did not cast off his people.* Thence the Apostles, thence all the Israelites who believed, and laid the prices of their sold goods at the feet of the Apostles[^27]; poor by will, rich in God. We have recognized one wall; that what we said was fulfilled there: *For the Lord will not cast off his people*: let us see the other wall too. *For in his hand are the ends of the earth.* Behold the other wall in all the nations: *In his hand are the ends of the earth.* All the nations too came to the cornerstone, where they recognized the kiss of peace; into that one who made of two one: not as the heretics, who made of one two. For this same thing the Apostle says of the Lord Christ: *For he is,* he says, *our peace, who made both one*[^28]. Therefore let us make a joyful noise to him. Why? *Because the Lord will not cast off his people.* Again why? *For in his hand are the ends of the earth, and the heights of the mountains are his.* The heights of the mountains, the loftinesses of the lands. Sometimes these loftinesses, that is, the earthly powers themselves, opposed the Church; they promulgated laws against the Church, they tried to blot out the Christian name from the earth: but after it was fulfilled which was prophesied: *All the kings of the earth shall adore him*[^29]; it came about which is here said: *The heights of the mountains are his.*

**9.** [v. 5.] But perhaps you are busy about temptations, so that, set in so great a grace of the promises of God, you are troubled on account of the scandals of the world? Not even those scandals do anything to you; since they have received a measure from the Lord: *For his is the sea.* For this world is a sea, but the sea too God made: nor can the waves rage except up to the shore, where he himself set a boundary. There is, then, no temptation, unless it has received a measure from the Lord. Let there be temptations, then, let there be tribulations; you are perfected by them, you are not consumed. See whether those very temptations do not profit. Attend to the Apostle: *God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you can bear; but will make with the temptation also a way of escape, that you may be able to endure*[^30]. He did not say: He will not allow you to be tempted at all. For if you refused temptation, you would refuse refreshment. Therefore you are refreshed; and if you are refreshed, you are in the hands of the craftsman. He takes something away from you, he corrects something, he smooths something, he cleanses something: he works with certain tools of his; these are the scandals of this world: only do not fall from the hand of the craftsman. No temptation will come beyond your strength. This God permits for your profit, whence you can make progress. Finally hear the Apostle himself joining this: *But will make with the temptation also a way of escape, that you may be able to endure.* Was the sea, then, perhaps to be feared by you? Do not fear: *For his is the sea, and he made it.* Do you fear scandals from the nations? The nations themselves too he made; he will not permit them to rage beyond that measure which he knows, from which you may profit. Does not another psalm say: *All nations, as many as you have made, will come and will adore before you, O Lord*[^31]? If all nations, as many as you have made, it is manifest that he made the nations too: therefore: *His is the sea, and he made it, and the dry land his hands formed.* Be you the dry land, thirst for the grace of God, that there may come upon you the sweet rain, and find in you fruit. He does not permit the waves to cover what he has sown: *And the dry land his hands formed.* Therefore from this too let us make a joyful noise to him.

**10.** [v. 6.] For since these things are so, since we have explained so great things pertaining to the praise of God, return to that whence he had begun: *Come, let us adore, and fall down before him, and let us weep before the Lord who made us.* For let us exult for this reason, because he made this and that. And he recalled many things, and now he repeats the exhortation: *Come, let us adore, and fall down before him, and let us weep before the Lord who made us.* Now, with the praises of God recalled by me, do not be slothful, and stand far off in life and conduct: *Come, let us adore, and fall down before him.* But perhaps you are anxious about your sins, which had made you far from God; let us do what follows: *And let us weep before the Lord, who made us.* Perhaps you burn with the conscience of an offense; with tears extinguish the flame of sin, weep before the Lord: weep secure before God, who made you; for he does not despise the work of his hands in you. Do not suppose that you can be refreshed by yourself. By yourself you can fail, you cannot refresh yourself: he refreshes who made you. *Let us weep before the Lord who made us*: weep before him, confess to him, anticipate his face in confession. For who are you who weep to him, and confess to him, except one whom he made? It is no small confidence for a thing made toward its maker, and not made anyhow, but made to his image and likeness: *Come, let us adore, and fall down before him, and let us weep before the Lord, who made us.*

**11.** [v. 7.] *For he is the Lord our God.* But that we may securely fall down before him and weep, what are we? *But we are the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hands.* See how elegantly he changed the order of the words, and as it were rendered things not their own; that we should understand them to be the very sheep, who are also the peoples. He did not say: The sheep of his pasture and the people of his hands; which would rather be thought able to fit, because sheep pertain to pasture: but he said, *the people of pasture.* Therefore the people are sheep, because he says, *the people of his pasture;* the people themselves are sheep. But again, because we have sheep which we have bought, not which we have made; but he had said above: *Let us fall down before him who made us*: it was rightly said, *the sheep of his hands.* No man makes sheep for himself: he can buy them, they can be given, he can find them, he can gather them, finally he can steal them; he cannot make sheep. But our Lord made us; therefore *the people of his pasture and the sheep of his hands,* they are those whom he himself deigned to make for himself by his grace. For these sheep he praises also in the Song of Songs, calling certain perfect ones as it were the teeth of the Church, his holy bride: *Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes coming up from the washing, which bear twins, and there is none barren among them*[^32]. What is: *Your teeth?* Those through whom you speak: for the teeth of the Church are those through whom the Church speaks. What kind are your teeth? *Like a flock of shorn ewes.* Why *shorn?* Because they laid down the burdens of the world. Were they not shorn, those sheep of whom I was speaking a little before, whom the precept of God had shorn, saying: *Go, sell all your goods, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^33]? They did this precept; shorn they came. And because they were baptized, believing in Christ, what is said there? *Coming up from the washing;* that is, coming up from cleansing. *And all bring forth twins*: what twins? Those two precepts, on which the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets[^34]. *We,* then, are *the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hands.*

**12.** [v. 8.] Therefore: *Today if you hear his voice.* O my people, people of God! God addresses his people, not only that his people whom he will not cast off, but also all his people. For he speaks in the corner to both walls; that is, in Christ the prophecy speaks, both to the people of the Jews, and to the people of the Gentiles. *Today if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.* For once you heard his voice through Moses, and you hardened your hearts. He spoke through a herald, when you hardened your hearts; through himself he now speaks, let your hearts grow soft. He who sent heralds before himself, himself deigned to come; with his own mouth he speaks here, who spoke through the mouths of the Prophets. *Today,* then, *if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.*

**13.** [v. 9.] Why did you say: *Do not harden your hearts?* Because you remember what your fathers were wont to do. *Do not harden your hearts: as in that embittering, according to the day of temptation in the desert.* You surely recall, brothers, that that people tempted God, received discipline, and were guided in the desert as by the best rider, with the reins of laws, with the reins of precepts; nor were they yet deserted by God even though untamed, not only by present benefits, but also by the rod of amendment that did not cease. *Do not,* then, *harden your hearts: as in the embittering, according to the day of temptation in the desert, where your fathers tempted me.* Now let your fathers not be such; do not imitate them. They were your fathers; but if you do not imitate them, they will not be your fathers: yet because you were born of them, they were your fathers. And if the Gentiles coming from the ends of the earth, as Jeremiah says: *To you the Gentiles will come from the ends of the earth, and will say: Truly our fathers worshiped lies, idols in which there is no profit*[^35]: if the Gentiles abandoned their idols, that they might come to the God of Israel; should those whom the God of Israel himself led out of Egypt through the Red Sea, in which he covered their pursuing enemies[^36]; whom he led out into the desert, fed with manna, never took away his rod from their discipline, never withdrew the benefits of his mercy; should they desert their God, when the Gentiles have come to their God? *Where your fathers tempted me; they tested me, and saw my works.* For forty years they saw my works, for forty years they embittered me: before them I did miracles by the hand of Moses, and they more and more hardened their hearts[^37].

**14.** [v. 10.] *For forty years I was near to this generation.* What is, *I was near?* I presented myself in my signs and powers: not for one day, not for two; but: *For forty years I was near to this generation; and I said: These always err in heart.* The forty years signified this, what also *always.* For this number forty indicates the entirety of the ages, as though the ages are completed by this number. Therefore the Lord fasted forty days, was tempted forty days in the wilderness[^38], and was with the disciples forty days after the resurrection[^39]. In the first forty days he showed temptation, in the latter forty days consolation; because when we are tempted, without doubt we are consoled. For his body, that is, the Church, must needs suffer temptations in this world; but that consoler is not lacking who said: *Behold, I am with you even unto the consummation of the world*[^40]. *And I said: These always err in heart.* For this I was with them forty years, that I might show such a kind of men, which always embitters me even unto the end of the world: because by those forty years he wished to signify the whole age.

**15.** [v. 11.] What then? in place of those will there not be others who enter into the rest of God? Those were disapproved to whom the mercy of God displeased, who resisted God with a hardened heart; those being disapproved, has God lost his people? It will not be true: *God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*[^41]? Therefore, *I said: These always err in heart. And these did not know my ways, to whom I swore in my anger: If they shall enter into my rest. To whom I swore in my anger, if they shall enter into my rest*: a great terror. We began from exultation, but this psalm concluded with a great fear: *To whom I swore in my anger: If they shall enter into my rest.* For the Lord to speak is a great thing; how much more for God to swear? You ought to fear a man swearing, lest on account of the oath he do what is against his will; how much more God, who can swear nothing rashly? He willed the oath to be for confirmation. And by whom does God swear? By himself: for he has no greater by whom to swear[^42]. By himself he confirms his promises, by himself he confirms his threats. Let no one say in his heart: It is true what he promises; it is false what he threatens. As it is true what he promises, so it is certain what he threatens. You ought to be as certain of rest, of happiness, of eternity, of immortality, if you do his precepts, as you ought to be certain of destruction, of the burning of the eternal fire, of damnation with the devil, if you despise his precepts. He swore to them in his anger, then, that they should not enter into his rest: and yet some must needs enter into his rest; for his rest will be given not to none. Those, then, being reprobated, we shall enter: because even if some of the branches were broken off on account of unlikeness and unbelief, we, on account of faith and humility, will be grafted in[^43]. Let us, then, enter into his rest. But whence did those enter who entered, those who were chosen, who did not resist with a hardened heart? Because it is true that *the Lord will not cast off his people.*
