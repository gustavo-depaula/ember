# Humility excludes pride, not wisdom.

**12.** There was seen here, indeed, by those who treated these things before us, another opinion too, and another understanding in these words, which I will not pass over in silence to your charity. For they said this: Every proud man displeases God, and the human soul ought to humble itself, lest it displease God, and with its whole heart to look upon what was said: *The greater you are, the more humble yourself in all things, and before God you will find grace*[^1]. But again there are certain men who, when they have heard that they ought to be humble, let themselves go, wish to learn nothing, thinking that if they learn anything they will be proud; and they remain in milk alone. These Scripture reproves, saying: *And you have become such as need milk, not solid food*[^2]. For God wishes us to be so nourished by milk that we do not remain there; but, growing through milk, that we may come to solid food. So a man ought not to lift up his heart into pride, but ought to raise it into the doctrine of the word of God. For if the soul were not to be raised, it would not be said in another voice of a psalm: *To you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul*[^3]. And unless the soul pours itself out above itself, it does not come to the vision of God, and to the knowledge of his unchangeable substance. For now, while it is still in the flesh, it is said to it: *Where is your God?*[^4] But its God is within, and is spiritually within, and is spiritually on high; not as it were by intervals of place, as by intervals certain places are higher. For if such a height is to be sought, the birds surpass us toward God. So God is high within, and spiritually high; nor does the soul come to touch him, unless it passes beyond itself. For whatever you think of God according to the body, you greatly err. You are much an infant, if you think of God, even according to the human soul, that God either forgets, or is so wise as to become foolish, or does something and repents of it: for all these things are set down in the Scriptures, that God might be commended to us who are sucklings; not that we should hear these things of him in the proper sense, and understand as though God repents, and now learns something he did not know, and understands what he did not understand, and remembers what he had forgotten. For such things belong to the soul, not to God. Unless, then, it passes beyond even the measure of its own soul, it will not see that God is what is; who said: *I am who am*[^5]. And so that man to whom it was said: *Where is your God*, what did he say? *My tears have been my bread day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God?* But that he might find his God, what did he do? *These things I meditated*, he says, *and I poured out my soul above myself*[^6]. That he might find God, he poured out his soul above himself. So it is not said to you thus: Be humble, so that you may not be wise. Be humble, on account of pride; be high, on account of wisdom. Hear a clear sentence on this matter: *Do not be made children in your minds; but in malice be infants, that in your minds you may be perfect*[^7]. Surely it is explained, my brothers, where God wished us to be humble, where high: humble, for the avoiding of pride; high, for the grasping of wisdom. Take milk, that you may be nourished; be so nourished, that you may grow; grow so, that you may eat bread. For when you have begun to eat bread, you will be weaned, that is, you will no longer need milk, but solid food. And he seems to have said this: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul*; that is, if I was not in mind, but in malice, a little child. Signifying this, he said the foregoing words: *O Lord, my heart is not exalted, nor are my eyes lifted up on high; nor have I walked in great things, nor in wonders above me.* Behold, that in malice I was a little child. But because in understanding I was not a little child: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul;* let there be recompensed to me what is recompensed to the little child who is weaned from his mother, that I may now be fit to eat bread.

**13.** And this opinion too, then, brothers, does not displease, because it is not against the faith. Yet it moves me, that it was said not only: *As one weaned from milk, so be the recompense in my soul*; but it was added: *As one weaned from milk upon his mother, so be the recompense in my soul.* Here something or other of this kind occurs to me, so that I see that it is a curse. For he is weaned not as an infant, but as one already somewhat grown: but he who is weak in earliest infancy, which is true infancy, is upon his mother; if perhaps he is weaned from the milk, he is destroyed. So not in vain was it added, *upon his mother.* For all can be weaned by growing. He who grows, and so is weaned, it is good for him; but he who is still upon his mother, it is destructive. So, brothers, we must take care and fear lest anyone be weaned before the time. For every grown child is separated from milk. But let no one be separated from milk then, when he is still upon his mother. But when he is carried in his mother's hands, who was carried in the womb (for he was carried in the womb, that he might be born; he is carried in the hands, that he might grow), he needs milk; he is still upon his mother. So let him not wish then to exalt his soul, when perhaps he is less fit to grasp food, but let him fulfill the precepts of humility. He has where to exercise himself: let him believe in Christ, that he may be able to understand Christ. He cannot see the Word, he cannot grasp the equality of the Word with the Father, the equality of the Holy Spirit with the Father and the Word he cannot yet see; let him believe this, and suck. He is secure, because when he has grown, he will eat what he could not before he grew by sucking: and he has where to stretch himself. *Do not seek things higher than you, and do not search out things stronger than you*[^8]; that is, things which you are less fit to grasp. And what shall I do, you say? shall I remain thus? *But the things that the Lord has commanded you, think on those always*[^9]. What has the Lord commanded you? Do mercy, do not abandon the peace of the Church, do not put hope in man, do not tempt God by desiring miracles. If there is already fruit in you, you recognize that with the good you endure the weeds until the harvest[^10]; that with the evil you can be for a time, not forever. The chaff is here mingled with you for this time on the threshing floor; in the barn it will not be with you. These things *which the Lord has commanded you, think on those always.* You will not be separated from milk as long as you are upon your mother; lest you die of hunger before you are fit to eat bread. Grow; your strength will be strong, and you will see what you could not, and grasp what you did not grasp.

**14.** What then? when I have seen what I could not see, and grasped what I could not grasp, will I now be secure? will I be perfect? No, as long as you live here. This is our perfection, humility. You heard with what the apostolic reading just now concluded, if it stuck in your memory; how that man who received the buffet, lest he be exalted in the revelations on account of the very greatness of the revelations, because he could be exalted unless he received an angel of Satan: and yet he to whom such great things were revealed, what does he say? *Brothers, I do not consider that I myself have laid hold*[^11]. Paul says: *Brothers, I do not consider that I myself have laid hold*; he who for this received the angel of Satan buffeting him, lest he be exalted in the greatness of his revelations[^12]. Who dares to say that he has comprehended? Behold, Paul has not laid hold, and says: *I do not consider that I have laid hold.* And what do you say, O Paul? I still run, he says, *that I may lay hold*. Is Paul still on the way, and do you think yourself in the homeland? *But one thing*, he says, *forgetting the things that are behind*. Do this too, and forget your past evil life. If vanity once delighted you, let it not delight you. *Forgetting the things that are behind*, he says, *stretched out toward the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the palm of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^13]. I hear the voice of God from above, and I run that I may lay hold. For he did not let me remain on the way, because he does not cease to address me. So, brothers, God does not cease to address us. For if he ceases, what is it that we are doing? what is it that the divine readings and the divine songs are doing? So forget the things that are behind, and stretch yourselves out toward the things that are before. Suck the milk in such a way that you grow toward food. When, then, you have come to the homeland, you will rejoice. And still attend to the apostle following toward the palm of the heavenly calling. For he says: *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind*. I do not speak, he says, to the imperfect, to whom I cannot yet speak wisdom, who are still given milk to drink, not fed with solid food; but I speak to those who already eat solid food. They now seem to be perfect, because they understand the equality of the Word with the Father: yet they do not yet see as it is to be seen, face to face; still in part, in a riddle[^14]. So let them run, because when the way is finished, then we return to the homeland: let them run, let them stretch themselves out. *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind; and if you are of any other mind in anything, this too God will reveal to you*[^15]. If perhaps you err in something, why do you not return to the milk of your mother? Because if you are not exalted, if you do not exalt your heart, if you do not walk in wonders above you, but keep humility, God will reveal to you that in which you are of another mind. But if this very thing in which you are of another mind you wish to defend, and obstinately to maintain, and against the peace of the Church; there comes upon you this curse which he spoke: when you are upon your mother and are separated from the milk outside, you will die of hunger outside the womb of your mother. But if you persevere in Catholic peace, if perhaps you are of another mind than you ought to be, God will reveal it to you who are humble. Why? Because God resists the proud, but to the humble he gives grace[^16].

**15**. [v. 3.] Therefore for this the psalm concluded: *Let Israel hope in the Lord, from this time now and forevermore*[^17]. For what is written in Greek, *heos tou aionos*, is rendered, *from this time now and forevermore.* But the word 'age' (saeculum) does not always signify this present age, but sometimes eternity; because 'eternal' is understood in two ways; 'unto eternity,' that is, either always without end, or until we come to eternity. So how is it to be understood here? Until we come to eternity, let us hope in the Lord God; because when we have come to eternity, there will no longer be hope, but the reality itself will be.
