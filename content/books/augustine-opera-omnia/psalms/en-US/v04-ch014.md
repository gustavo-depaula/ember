# Exposition of Psalm 86

**1.** The psalm which has just been sung is brief in the number of words, great in the weight of its meanings. For the whole was read, and you see in how short a time it was brought to its end. This is now set before us by our most blessed father present, to be treated with Your Charity, as far as the Lord deigns to grant. The sudden setting-forth would burden me, were I not at once relieved by the prayer of the one setting it forth. Let Your Charity attend, then. A certain city is sung and commended in this psalm: of which we are citizens, in so far as we are Christians; and from which we are pilgrims, as long as we are mortal; and toward which we tend, by whose way, which was altogether not found, as it were shut off by thickets and brambles, the king of that city himself made himself the way, that we might arrive at the city. Walking, then, in Christ, and still pilgrims until we arrive, and sighing with desire of a certain ineffable rest which dwells in that city, of which rest it was said that this was promised to us, *which eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^1]; walking, then, let us so sing, that we may desire. For he who desires, even if his tongue is silent, sings in heart: but he who does not desire, however much he strikes the ears of men with a cry, is mute to God. See how ardent were the lovers of this city, those very men through whom these things were said, through whom these things were commended to us, with how great an affection these things were sung by them. This affection the love of the city generated in them; but the love of the city the Spirit of God had poured in: *Love,* he says, *of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^2]. Fervent, then, with this Spirit, let us hear what was said of this city.

**2.** [vv. 1-2.] *Its foundations are in the holy mountains.* The Psalm had said nothing of it yet; from here it begins, and says: *Its foundations are in the holy mountains.* Whose foundations? It is not doubtful that foundations, especially on mountains, are of some city. Filled, then, with the Holy Spirit, this citizen, and turning over with himself many things about the love and desire of this city, as it were having meditated many things within himself, burst forth into this: *Its foundations are in the holy mountains;* as though he had already said something of it. How had he said nothing of it, who had never been silent of it in heart? For how is it called *its,* of which nothing has been said? But, as I said, travailing within himself in silence with much about that city, crying to God, he burst forth even into the ears of men: *Its foundations are in the holy mountains.* And as if men hearing should ask: Whose? *The Lord loves,* he says, *the gates of Zion.* Behold whose foundations are in the holy mountains, of a certain city Zion, whose gates the Lord loves, as he consequently says, *above all the tabernacles of Jacob.* But what is: *Its foundations are in the holy mountains?* who are the holy mountains upon which this city is founded? Another citizen said this more plainly, the apostle Paul. A citizen thence the Prophet, a citizen thence the Apostle: and these spoke for this reason, that they might exhort the other citizens. But how are these, that is, the Prophets and Apostles, citizens? Perhaps so that they themselves too are mountains, upon which are the foundations of this city, whose gates the Lord loves. Let another citizen, then, say this openly, lest we seem to be conjecturing. Speaking to the Gentiles, and recalling them that they might return, and as it were be constructed into a holy building: *Built up,* he says, *upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets*[^3]. And because the Apostles or Prophets themselves, in whom is the foundation of the city, would not hold themselves in themselves; he went on there: *Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone.* Lest, therefore, the Gentiles should suppose that they did not belong to Zion: for there was a certain earthly city Zion, which through a shadow bore the image of a certain Zion of which it is now spoken, that heavenly Jerusalem of which the Apostle says: *Which is the mother of us all*[^4]: lest, therefore, they be said not to belong to Zion, because they were not of the people of the Jews, he said this to them: *Therefore now you are no longer pilgrims and sojourners; but you are citizens of the saints, and members of the household of God, built up upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets.* You have the construction of so great a city. But on what does all this framework rest, on what does it lean, that it nowhere fall? *Christ Jesus himself,* he says, *being the chief cornerstone*[^5].

**3.** Someone will perhaps say: If Christ Jesus is the cornerstone, in him indeed two walls are joined together. For two walls do not make a corner, except coming into one from different sides: so too two peoples, from the circumcision and from the uncircumcision, connected to one another into Christian peace in one faith, one hope, one love. But if Christ Jesus is the top corner, the foundations seem as it were first, and the cornerstone last. Someone can therefore say that Christ rather rests upon the Prophets and Apostles, not they upon him, if they are in the foundation, he in the corner. But let him who says this think that the corner too is in the foundation. For the corner is not only there where it is seen, that it may rise to the apex: for it begins from the foundation. For that you may know that Christ is both the foundation and the first and greatest: *Foundation,* says the Apostle, *no one can lay besides that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^6]. How, then, are the Prophets and Apostles foundations, and how is Christ Jesus the foundation, beyond which there is nothing further? How do we suppose, except as openly is said the holy of holies, so figuratively the foundation of foundations? If, then, you think of the sacraments, Christ is the holy of holies: if you think of the flock under a shepherd, Christ is the shepherd of shepherds: if you think of the building, Christ is the foundation of foundations. In these buildings the same stone cannot be both at the bottom and at the top: if it is at the bottom, it will not be at the top; if it is at the top, it will not be at the bottom. For nearly all bodies suffer narrownesses; they can neither be everywhere, nor always: but the divinity, which is present everywhere, the likeness can be drawn to it from every side; and the whole can be in likenesses, because none of these is in proper terms. Is Christ a door, as we see doors made by a carpenter? Surely not: and yet he said: *I am the door.* Or is he a shepherd as we see those shepherds set over cattle? But he said: *I am the shepherd.* And in one place he said both things. In the Gospel he said that the shepherd enters through the door: and there he said: *I am the good shepherd;* and there he said: *I am the door*[^7]. The shepherd enters through the door: and who is the shepherd who enters through the door? *I am the good shepherd.* And what, then, is the door through which you enter, good shepherd? *I am the door.* How, then, are you all things? As, through me, all things. For example, when Paul enters through the door, does not Christ enter through the door? Why? Not because Paul is Christ; but because in Paul is Christ, and through Christ is Paul. He himself said: *Do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ*[^8]? When his saints and his faithful enter through the door, does not Christ enter through the door? How do we prove it? Because Saul, not yet Paul, was persecuting his very saints, when he cried to him from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^9]? Therefore he himself is both the foundation and the cornerstone, rising from the bottom; if indeed from the bottom. For the origin of this foundation holds the summit: and as the foundation of a bodily building is at the bottom; so the foundation of a spiritual building is at the top. If we were built toward the earth, the foundation would have to be set at the bottom for us: because the building is heavenly, our foundation went before toward the heavens. Therefore Christ himself the cornerstone, and the mountains the Apostles, the great Prophets, bearing the framework of the city, make a certain living building. Does this building now cry out from your hearts? this the skilled hand of God does even through our tongue, that you may be squared for the framework of that building. For not in vain was the ark of Noah built even of squared timbers[^10], which nonetheless bore a figure of the Church. For what is it to be squared? Attend to the likeness of a squared stone: such ought a Christian to be. In every temptation of his the Christian does not fall; even if he is pushed, and as it were turned, he does not fall: for a squared stone, wherever you turn it, stands. The Martyrs seemed to fall, when they were struck; but what did a certain voice of the Canticle say? *When the righteous falls he shall not be troubled; for the Lord upholds his hand*[^11]. So, then, be squared, ready for all temptations; whatever pushes, let it not overturn you. Let every fall find you standing. You are raised, then, into this building by pious affection, sincere religion, faith, hope, love; and this very being built up is to walk. In these cities the framework of the buildings is one thing, the citizens dwelling in the framework are another: that city is built of its own citizens; they themselves are the stones who are the citizens; for they are living stones. *And you,* he says, *as living stones are built up into a spiritual house*[^12]. The voice is directed to us. Therefore let us follow on concerning this city.

**4.** *Its foundations are in the holy mountains: the Lord loves the gates of Zion.* I have already spoken beforehand, lest you suppose the foundations to be one thing, the gates another. Why are the Apostles and Prophets foundations? Because their authority bears our weakness. Why are they gates? Because through them we enter into the kingdom of God: for they preach to us. And when we enter through them, we enter through Christ: for he is the door[^13]. And the twelve gates of Jerusalem are spoken of[^14], and one gate is Christ, and the twelve gates are Christ; because in the twelve gates is Christ: and therefore the twelvefold number of the Apostles. A great sacrament of this twelvefold number. *You shall sit,* he says, *upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^15]. If twelve chairs are there; there is no place where the thirteenth apostle Paul may sit, and there will be no way for him to judge: and he said that he would judge, not only men, but also angels. What angels, except the apostate angels? *Do you not know,* he says, *that we shall judge angels*[^16]? Let the crowd answer, then: Why do you boast that you will judge? Where will you sit? Twelve seats the Lord said to the twelve Apostles; one fell, Judas, in his place Matthias was ordained, the twelvefold number of seats was filled[^17]: first find a place where you may sit, and so threaten that you will judge. Let us see, then, what the twelve seats mean. It is a sacrament of a certain universality; because throughout the whole world the Church was to be, whence this building is called to the framework of Christ: and therefore, because from every side one comes to judge, there are twelve seats; just as, because from every side one enters into that city, there are twelve gates. Therefore not only those twelve and the apostle Paul, but as many as are to judge, on account of the signification of universality, belong to the twelve seats; just as as many as will enter, belong to the twelve gates. For the parts of the world are four, East, West, North and South. These four parts are constantly named in the Scriptures. From all these four winds, as the Lord says in the Gospel that he will gather his elect from the four winds[^18]; from all these four winds, then, the Church is called. How is it called? It is called everywhere in the Trinity: it is not called except by baptism in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Four, then, drawn three times, are found twelve.

**5.** Knock, then, with affection at these gates, and let Christ cry out in you: *Open to me the gates of righteousness*[^19]. For he went before in the head; he follows himself in the body. See what the Apostle said, that in him Christ was suffering: *That I may fill up,* he says, *what is lacking of the pressures of Christ in my flesh*[^20]. *That I may fill up*: what? *What is lacking.* To whom is it lacking? *Of the pressures of Christ.* And where is it lacking? *In my flesh.* Was anything of the pressures lacking in that man, which became the Word of God, born of the Virgin Mary? For he suffered whatever he ought to suffer, by his own will not by the necessity of sin; and it seems that all: for set on the cross he received the last vinegar, and said: *It is finished; and bowing his head he gave up the spirit*[^21]. What is: *It is finished?* Now of the measure of sufferings nothing is lacking to me; all that was foretold of me has been completed: as though he were waiting for it to be completed. Who is there who so departs, as he went out of the body? But who is there who could do this? He who first had said: *I have power to lay down my soul, and power to take it up again: no one takes it from me: but I lay it down of myself, and take it up again*[^22]. He laid it down when he willed; he took it up when he willed: no one took it away, no one wrested it. Therefore all the sufferings were fulfilled, but in the head: there still remained the sufferings of Christ in the body. *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^23]. In these members, then, since the Apostle was, he said: *That I may fill up what is lacking of the pressures of Christ in my flesh.* Therefore we go thither whither Christ went before, and still Christ goes thither whither he went before: for Christ went before in the head, he follows in the body. And still Christ labors here; and Christ was suffering here from Saul, when Saul heard: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^24]? As the tongue is wont to say, the foot being trodden: You tread on me. No one touched the tongue; it cries out from compassion, not from being bruised. Still Christ is here in want, Christ is a pilgrim here, Christ is sick here, Christ is shut up in prison here. We do him an injury, if he himself did not say: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; a stranger, and you took me in; naked, and you clothed me; sick, and you visited me. And they: When did we see you suffering these things, and ministered to you? And he: When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^25]. Therefore let us be built up into Christ upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets, he himself being the chief cornerstone[^26]; because *the Lord loves the gates of Zion above all the tabernacles of Jacob.* As though indeed Zion itself were not among the tabernacles of Jacob. And where was Zion, except in the people of Jacob? For Jacob, the grandson of Abraham, whence the people of the Jews was born, is called the people of Israel, because Jacob himself was called also Israel[^27]. These things Your Holiness knows well. But because there were certain tabernacles temporal and imaginary; and he speaks of a certain city which he understands spiritually, of which that earthly one was the shadow and figure: he says: *The Lord loves the gates of Zion above all the tabernacles of Jacob.* He loves that spiritual city above all the figured things by which that city was intimated, always abiding, and always heavenly in peace.

**6.** [vv. 3-4.] *Glorious things have been said of you, O city of God.* As though he was gazing at that city Jerusalem on earth. For see what city he means, of which certain most glorious things have been said. For that one was destroyed on earth; suffering enemies it fell to the ground, it is no longer what it was: it expressed an image, the shadow has passed. Whence, then: *Glorious things have been said of you, O city of God?* Hear whence: *I will be mindful of Rahab and of Babylon, to those who know me.* In that city, he says now from the person of God, I will be mindful of Rahab, and I will be mindful of Babylon. Rahab does not belong to the people of the Jews; Babylon does not belong to the people of the Jews. For it follows: *For the foreigners, and Tyre, and the people of the Ethiopians, these were there.* Rightly *glorious things have been said of you, O city of God*: where not only is that people of the Jews born of the flesh of Abraham; but there all the nations, certain of which are named, that all may be understood. *I will be mindful,* he says, *of Rahab*: who is this harlot, that harlot in Jericho who received the messengers and sent them out by another way; who presumed upon the promise, who feared God, to whom it was said that she should hang scarlet from the window—that is, that she should have on her forehead the sign of the blood of Christ[^28]. She was saved there, and signified the Church of the Gentiles. Whence the Lord to the proud Pharisees: *Amen I say to you, the publicans and harlots go before you into the kingdom of heaven*[^29]. They go before, because they use force: they push in by believing, and faith yields, nor can anyone resist; because those who use force seize it. For there it is set: *The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and those who use force seize it*[^30]. This that thief did, stronger on the cross than in the gullet[^31]. *I will be mindful of Rahab and of Babylon.* Babylon is called a city according to the world. As there is one holy city, Jerusalem; one iniquitous city, Babylon: all the iniquitous belong to Babylon; just as all the saints to Jerusalem. But one slips down from Babylon into Jerusalem. Whence, except through him who justifies the impious[^32]? The city of the pious is Jerusalem; the city of the impious, Babylon. But he came who justifies the impious; because: *I will be mindful,* he says, not only *of Rahab,* but also *of Babylon.* But of whom will he be mindful of Rahab and Babylon? *Of those who know me.* Therefore in a certain place Scripture says: *Pour out your anger upon the nations that have not known you*[^33]. Here it says: *Pour out your anger upon the nations that have not known you;* and elsewhere: *Extend your mercy to those who know you*[^34]. And that you may know that in Rahab and Babylon he signified the Gentiles; as though it were said: What is it that you said: *I will be mindful of Rahab and Babylon, to those who know me?* why did you say this? *For the foreigners,* he says, that is, those belonging to Rahab, those belonging to Babylon, *and Tyre.* But how far the Gentiles? Even to the ends of the earth. For he chose the people which is at the end of the earth: *And the people,* he says, *of the Ethiopians, these were there.* If, then, there is Rahab, there is one from Babylon, because there are the foreigners, there is Tyre, there is the people of the Ethiopians; rightly, *most glorious things have been said of you, O city of God.*

**7.** [v. 5.] Now attend to a great sacrament. Through him there Rahab, through whom there Babylon, now not Babylon; for it lacks Babylon, and begins to be Jerusalem. The daughter is divided against her mother, and will be among the members of that queen to whom it is said: *Forget your people and the house of your father; the king has desired your beauty*[^35]. For whence would Babylon aspire to Jerusalem? whence would Rahab arrive at those foundations? whence the foreigners, whence Tyre, whence the people of the Ethiopians? Hear whence: *Mother Zion, a man will say.* There is a certain man who says: *Mother Zion;* and through this man all these come. But who is this man? He says, if we hear, if we grasp it: *Mother Zion, a man will say.* There follows, as though you asked through whom Rahab came, Babylon, the foreigners, Tyre, the Ethiopians. Behold through whom they came: *Mother Zion, a man will say; and a man was made in her, and he himself, the Most High, founded her.* What is more open, brothers? Truly because *most glorious things have been said of you, O city of God.* Behold: *Mother Zion, a man will say.* What man? He who *was made man in her.* In her a man was made, and he himself founded her. How was he made in her, and himself founded her? That a man might be made in her, she was already founded. So understand, if you can. For: *Mother Zion, will say;* but, *a man, Mother Zion, will say; a man* however *was made in her: he himself* however *founded her,* not a man, but *the Most High.* So he founded the city in which he would be born, as he created the mother of whom he would be born. What is this, brothers? What promises, how great a hope do we hold! Behold, for our sake the Most High who founded the city: *Mother,* he says to the city itself; and *a man was made in her, and the Most High founded her.*

**8.** [v. 6.] As though it were said: Whence do you know these things? We have all sung these things, and Christ sings in all: a man for our sake, God before us. But what great thing before us? Before earth and heaven, before the ages. This man, then, made in her for our sake, the same one, the Most High, founded her. Whence do we know these things? *The Lord will recount in the Scripture of the peoples.* For this follows in the Psalm: *Mother Zion, a man will say; and a man was made in her, and he himself, the Most High, founded her. The Lord will recount in the Scripture of the peoples and of princes.* Of what princes? Of these who were made in her. The princes who were made in her, in her were made princes: for before they were made princes in her, God chose the abject things of the world, to confound the strong. Was the fisherman a prince? was the publican a prince? Plainly princes; but because they were made in her. What kind of princes are these? Princes came from Babylon, believers from the world; princes came to the city of Rome, as it were the head of Babylon; they went not to the temple of the Emperor, but to the memorial of the Fisherman. For whence are these princes? The weak things of the world God chose, to confound the strong: and the ignoble things God chose, and the things that are not as though they were, that the things that are might be made void[^36]. This he does who raises up the needy from the earth, and exalts the poor from the dunghill. Why does he raise him up? That he may set him with princes, with the princes of his people[^37]. A great thing, a great joy, a great gladness. Afterward orators too came into this city; but they would not come, unless the fishermen went before. Great are these things; but where are these, except in that city of God, of which most glorious things have been said.

**9.** [v. 7.] Therefore, all the joys being gathered together and fused, how did he conclude? *As of all who rejoice the dwelling is in you.* As of those who rejoice the dwelling, of all who rejoice in this city. In this pilgrimage we are worn down: our dwelling will be joy alone. Labor and groaning will perish; prayers pass away, praises succeed. There, then, the dwelling of those who rejoice, there will not be the groaning of those desiring, but the gladness of those enjoying. For there will be present he for whom we now sigh; we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is[^38]: there our whole business will be nothing but to praise God, and to enjoy God. And what else shall we seek, where he alone suffices, through whom all things were made? We shall be dwelt in and shall dwell: all things will be subjected to him, that God may be all in all[^39]. *Blessed,* then, *are those who dwell in your house.* Whence blessed? By having gold, by having silver, a numerous household, manifold offspring? Whence blessed? *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^40]. By this one leisured business they are blessed. This one thing, then, let us desire, brothers, when we come to this; let us prepare ourselves to rejoice in God, to praise God. The good works which now bring us through will not be there. We said yesterday too, as far as we could; the works of mercy will not be there, where there will be no misery: you will not find one in need, you will not find one naked, no one thirsting will meet you, no one will be a pilgrim, no one sick whom you may visit, no one dead whom you may bury, none of the litigants among whom you may make peace. What will you do? Or shall we perhaps, on account of the necessities of our body, plant vines, and plow, and trade, and travel? Great rest is there: for there all the works which necessity demands will be withdrawn; necessity being dead, the works of necessity will perish. What, then, will there be? As it could, the human tongue has said: *As of all who rejoice the dwelling is in you.* What is, *as?* why, *as?* Because such joy will be there as we do not know here. I see here many joys, and many rejoice in the world, some from this, some from that; and there is nothing to which I may compare that joy, but it will be *as* joy. For if I say: Joy; there begins to occur to a man such joy as he is wont to have in drinking-cups, in feasts, in avarice, in the honors of the world. For men are lifted up, and rave with a certain gladness: but *there is no rejoicing for the impious,* says the Lord. There is a certain joy which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man[^41]. *As of all who rejoice the dwelling is in you.* Let us prepare ourselves for another joy; because here we find as it were the like, and it is not this: lest we as it were prepare ourselves to enjoy there such things as we rejoice in here; otherwise our continence will be avarice. For there are men who are invited to a rich dinner, where many and precious things are to be set out; they do not lunch: now if you ask them why they do not lunch: We are fasting. A great work, a Christian work, fasting. Do not praise too quickly: seek the cause; the business of the belly is being done, not of religion. Why do they fast? Lest cheap things forestall the belly, and it be unable to admit precious things. Therefore he conducts the business of the gullet by fasting. A great thing surely is fasting: it fights against the belly and gullet; sometimes it soldiers for them. And so, my brothers, if you suppose we shall have anything of the kind in that homeland, to which the heavenly trumpet exhorts us, and therefore you abstain from present things, that there you may receive such things more copiously; you are like those who fast for the sake of greater feasts, and restrain themselves by a greater incontinence. Do not, then, be so: prepare yourselves for something ineffable; cleanse the heart from all your earthly and worldly affections. We shall see something, which seen we shall be blessed, and this alone will suffice us. And what? shall we not eat? Rather we shall eat; and he himself will be our food, who both refreshes and does not fail. *As of all who rejoice the dwelling is in you.* It has already been said whence we shall rejoice: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you.* Let us praise the Lord now too, as far as we can, with groanings mixed in; because by praising him we desire him, and do not yet hold him: when we have held him, all groaning will be withdrawn, and there will remain praise alone, and pure, and eternal. Turned to the Lord.
