# Exposition of Psalm 64

**1.** [v. 1] The voice of holy prophecy is to be recognized from the very title of this psalm. It is inscribed: *Unto the end, a Psalm of David, a Canticle of Jeremiah and Ezekiel from the people of the migration, when they were beginning to go out.*[^1] How the matter happened among our fathers in the time of the Babylonian migration is not known to all, but to those who diligently attend to the holy Scriptures, whether by hearing or reading. For the people of Israel, taken captive, was led from the city of Jerusalem into the servitude of Babylon. But the holy Jeremiah prophesied that after seventy years the people would return from captivity, and would restore that very city of Jerusalem, which, conquered by the enemies, he had lamented. But at that time there were prophets, set in that captivity of the people in Babylon; among whom was also the prophet Ezekiel. But that people was waiting until the times of the seventy years should be fulfilled, according to the prophecy of Jeremiah. It came about that, the seventy years being completed, the temple which had been cast down was restored; and a great part of that people returned from captivity. But since the Apostle says: *These things happened in figure among them; but they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^2]; we too ought to know first our captivity, then our liberation; we ought to know the Babylon in which we are captive, and the Jerusalem for return to which we sigh. These two cities indeed, according to the letter, are truly two cities. And that Jerusalem indeed is now not inhabited by the Jews. For after the Lord was crucified, it was avenged on them with a great scourge; and, uprooted from that place, where with impious freedom they had raged frenzied against the physician, they were scattered among all the nations, and that land was given to Christians: and there was fulfilled what the Lord had said to them: *Therefore the kingdom will be taken from you, and given to a nation doing righteousness*[^3]. But when they then saw many crowds going after the Lord, preaching the kingdom of heaven, and doing wonders, the leaders of that city said: *If we let him go, all will go after him; and the Romans will come, and take from us both our place, and our nation*[^4]. Lest they lose the place, they killed the Lord; and therefore they lost it, because they killed him. A certain earthly city, then, bore the shadow of a certain eternal city in the heavens; but when that which was signified began to be preached more evidently; the shadow by which it was signified was cast down: therefore there is now no temple there, which had been built in the image of the future body of the Lord. We hold the light; the shadow has passed: yet we are still in a certain captivity: *As long as we are*, he says, *in the body, we are on pilgrimage from the Lord*[^5].

**2.** [v. 2] And see the names of these two cities, Babylon and Jerusalem. Babylon is interpreted "Confusion," Jerusalem "Vision of peace." Attend now to the city of confusion, that you may understand the vision of peace: bear with the former, sigh for the latter. Whence can these two cities be discerned? Can we now separate them from one another? They are mingled, and from the very beginning of the human race they run mingled even unto the end of the age. Jerusalem took its beginning through Abel; Babylon through Cain: for the buildings of the cities were made afterward. That Jerusalem in the land of the Jebusites; for first it was called Jebus: thence the nation of the Jebusites was cast out, when the people of God was freed from Egypt, and led into the land of promise. But Babylon was founded in the inmost regions of Persia, which for a long time lifted its head above the other nations. These two cities, then, were founded at fixed times, that there might be made manifest the figure of the two cities begun of old, and about to remain even unto the end in this age, but in the end to be separated. Whence, then, can we now show those which are mingled? The Lord will show them then, when he sets some at the right hand, others at the left. Jerusalem will be at the right hand; Babylon at the left. Jerusalem is to hear: *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* Babylon is to hear: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^6]. Yet we can also bring something forward, as far as the Lord grants, whereby the godly faithful, even in this time, the citizens of Jerusalem, may be distinguished from the citizens of Babylon. These two cities two loves make: love of God makes Jerusalem; love of the age makes Babylon. Let each one, then, question himself what he loves, and he will find of which he is a citizen: and if he find himself a citizen of Babylon, let him root out greed, let him plant love; but if he find himself a citizen of Jerusalem, let him bear with captivity, let him hope for liberty. For many citizens of holy mother Jerusalem were held corrupted by the greeds of Babylon, and by that very corruption of greeds had become as it were citizens of it; and many are still so, and many after us in this land will be so: but the Lord, the founder of Jerusalem, knows whom he has predestined as her citizens, whom he sees still under the dominion of the devil, to be redeemed by the blood of Christ; he himself knows them before they know themselves. Under this figure, then, this psalm is sung. In whose title are set also two prophets, who at that time were in captivity, Jeremiah and Ezekiel, and they sang certain things, *when they were beginning to go out.* He begins to go out who begins to love. For many go out secretly, and the feet of those going out are the affections of the heart: but they go out from Babylon. What is from Babylon? From confusion. How does one go out from Babylon, that is, from confusion? Those who first were confused by like greeds, begin to be distinguished by love; now distinguished, they are not confused. Even if they are still mingled in body, yet by holy desire they are separated; and on account of bodily mingling they have not yet gone out: on account of the affection of the heart they have begun to go out. Now then let us hear, brethren; let us hear, and sing, and desire that of which we are citizens. And what joys are sung to us? How is the love of our city reformed in us, which by a long pilgrimage we had forgotten? But our Father has sent us letters from there, God has supplied us with the Scriptures, by which letters there might be made in us a desire of returning; because, by loving our pilgrimage, we had set our face to the enemies, and our back to the homeland. What, then, is sung here?

**3.** [v. 2] *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion.*[^7] That homeland is Zion: it is the Jerusalem which is Zion; and you ought to know the interpretation of this name. As Jerusalem is interpreted "Vision of peace," so Zion "Watching," that is, sight and contemplation. Some great spectacle, I know not what, is promised to us; and this is God himself who founded the city. A beautiful and comely city, how much more beautiful a founder it has! *A hymn befits you, O God*, he says. But where? *In Zion*: in Babylon it does not befit. For everyone, when he begins to be renewed, now sings in heart in Jerusalem, the Apostle saying: *Our citizenship is in heaven*[^8]. *For walking in the flesh*, he says, *we do not war according to the flesh*[^9]. Now by desire we are there, now we have sent our hope ahead into that land, as an anchor, lest, troubled in this sea, we suffer shipwreck. As, then, of a ship that is at anchor, we rightly say that it is now on land; for it still tosses, but in a manner has been drawn out onto land against the winds, and against the storms: so against the temptations of this our pilgrimage, our hope, founded in that city of Jerusalem, keeps us from being dashed onto the rocks. He, then, who sings according to this hope, sings there: therefore let him say: *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion. In Zion,* not in Babylon. But are you now still there in Babylon? There, he says, I am, this lover, this citizen: there I am; but in flesh, not in heart. But since I have said two things, that I am there in flesh, not in heart; whence I sing, I am not there: for I sing not in flesh, but in heart. The sounding flesh indeed even the citizens of Babylon hear; but the sound of the heart the founder of Jerusalem hears. Whence the Apostle says, exhorting those very citizens to certain love-songs, and desires of returning to that most beautiful city, the vision of peace: *Singing*, he says, *and playing in your hearts to the Lord*[^10]. What is, *singing in your hearts*? Do not sing from there whence you are in Babylon; but sing from there whence you dwell above. Therefore: *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion.* In Zion a hymn befits you, not in Babylon. Those who sing in Babylon, citizens of Babylon, even a hymn of God they do not sing fittingly. Hear the voice of Scripture: *Praise is not comely in the mouth of a sinner*[^11]. *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion.*

**4.** *And to you a vow will be paid in Jerusalem.*[^12] Here we vow, and it is good that we pay there. But who are they who here vow, and do not pay? Those who do not persevere unto the end in what they vowed. Whence another psalm says: *Vow, and pay to the Lord your God*[^13]. *And to you a vow will be paid in Jerusalem.* For there we shall be whole, that is, entire in the resurrection of the just: there our whole vow will be paid; not the soul alone, but the very flesh too, now no longer corruptible, because now not in Babylon, but now a heavenly body changed. What kind of change is promised? *For we shall all rise again,* says the Apostle, *but we shall not all be changed.* But who will be changed he himself said: *In the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet: for the trumpet will sound, and the dead will rise incorrupt,* that is, whole, *and we shall be changed.* But what that change will be, it follows and says: *For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality: but when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality, then will come to pass the saying that is written: Death has been swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your sting*[^14]? For now, the firstfruits of the mind having begun in us, whence we desire Jerusalem, many things from the corruptible flesh strive against us, which will not strive, when death has been swallowed up in victory. Peace will conquer, and war will be ended. But when peace conquers, that city will conquer which is called "Vision of peace." There will, then, be no strife from death. Now how greatly we strive with death! Hence are carnal delights, which suggest to us many even unlawful things: to which we do not consent, but yet by not consenting we strive. First, then, the concupiscence of the flesh led us as we followed, afterward dragged us resisting; then, grace having been received, it began neither to lead nor to drag us, but still to strive with us: after the striving there will be victory too. Now even if it assails you, let it not overcome you: afterward, when death has been swallowed up in victory, it will cease even to fight. What was said? *The last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^15]. I will pay my vow. What vow? As it were a holocaust. For it is called a holocaust when the fire consumes the whole: a holocaust is a sacrifice where the whole is consumed; for "whole" is called *holon*, and burning *causis*. A holocaust is a whole burnt-offering. Let the fire seize us, then, the divine fire in Jerusalem: let us begin to burn with love, until all that is mortal be consumed, and what was against us go into a sacrifice to the Lord. Whence elsewhere it is said: *Do good, O Lord, in your good will to Zion, that the walls of Jerusalem may be built: then you will accept the sacrifice of righteousness, offerings and holocausts*[^16]. A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion; and to you a vow will be paid in Jerusalem. We seek here, lest perhaps the King of that city be commended to us, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ: let us sing, then, until we come to more evident things. For I could now say that it is he himself to whom it is said: *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion; and to you a vow will be paid in Jerusalem.* But if I said it, I would be believed rather than the Scripture; and therefore perhaps I would not be believed. Let us hear what follows.

**5.** [v. 3] *Hear*, he says, *my prayer: to you all flesh will come.*[^17] And we have the Lord saying that power over all flesh was given to him. That King, then, began now to appear, when it was said, *to you all flesh will come. To you*, he says, *all flesh will come.* Why will all flesh come to him? Because he took on flesh. Whither will all flesh come? He took from there the firstfruits from the virginal womb: the firstfruits having been taken, the rest will follow, that the holocaust may be completed. Whence, then, *all flesh*? Every man. And whence, every man? Surely not all were foretold as going to believe in Christ? Are there not many ungodly who will even be damned? Are there not daily many not believing, who die in their unbelief? In what respect, then, do we understand, *to you all flesh will come*? *All flesh* he said, flesh of every kind: from every kind of flesh there will be a coming to you. What is it, from every kind of flesh? Surely the poor came, and the rich did not come? surely the humble came, and the lofty did not come? surely the unlearned came, and the learned did not come? surely men came, and women did not come? surely masters came, and servants did not come? surely the old came, and the young did not come? or the young came, and the youths did not come? or the youths came, and the boys did not come? or the boys came, and infants were not brought? Finally, surely the Jews came (for from there were the Apostles, from there the many thousands first of those betraying, afterward of those believing); and the Greeks did not come? or the Greeks came, and the Romans did not come? or the Romans came, and the barbarians did not come? And who could count all the nations coming to him to whom it was said: *To you all flesh will come*? *Hear my prayer: to you all flesh will come.*

**6.** [v. 4] *The words of the unrighteous have prevailed over us; and our impieties you will forgive.*[^18] What is *The words of the unrighteous have prevailed over us; and our impieties you will forgive*? That, being born in this land, we found unrighteous men whom we heard speaking. If I can unfold what I feel, let the attention of Your Charity help me. Every man, wherever he is born, learns the language of that very land, or region, or city; he is imbued with its morals and life. What could a boy born among pagans do, so as not to worship a stone, when his parents instilled that worship? Thence he heard his first words; that error he sucked in with milk: and because those who spoke were elders, and the boy who was learning to speak was an infant, whence could a little one but follow the authority of his elders, and reckon that good for himself which they praised? Therefore the nations, afterward converted to Christ, and recalling the impieties of their parents, and saying now what the prophet Jeremiah himself said: *Truly our fathers worshiped a lie; a vanity which did not profit them*[^19]: when, then, they say this, they renounce the opinions and sacrileges of their unrighteous parents. But because, that they might be implanted in such opinions and sacrileges, the persuasions of those who, the more they went before in age, the more they were thought to ought to go before in authority, brought it about; one now wishing to return to Jerusalem from Babylon confesses, and says: *The words of the unrighteous have prevailed against us.* Those teaching evil led us; they made us citizens of Babylon: we left the Creator, we adored the creature; we left him by whom we were made, we adored what we ourselves made. *For the words of the unrighteous have prevailed over us*: but yet they did not oppress us. Why? *Our impieties you will forgive.* Let the attention of Your Charity attend. *You will forgive our impieties*: it is said only to a certain priest offering something, whereby impiety may be expiated and propitiated. For impiety is then said to be propitiated, when God becomes propitious to impiety. What is it, God becoming propitious to impiety? that is, pardoning and giving forgiveness. But that God's pardon may be obtained, propitiation is made through some sacrifice. There arose, then, a certain priest of ours, sent by the Lord God; he took from us what he might offer to the Lord, the very holy firstfruits of flesh, as we said, from the womb of the virgin. This holocaust he offered to God: he stretched out his hands on the cross, that he might say: *Let my prayer be directed as incense in your sight; the lifting up of my hands an evening sacrifice*[^20]. As you know, the Lord hung on the cross toward evening: and our impieties were propitiated; otherwise they had swallowed us up: the words of the unrighteous had prevailed over us; the preachers of Jupiter, and Saturn, and Mercury had led us: *The words of the unrighteous have prevailed over us.* But what will you do? *Our impieties, you will forgive.* You the priest, you the victim; you the offerer, you the offering. He himself is the priest, who, having now entered the inner part of the veil, alone there of those who bore flesh, intercedes for us. In figure of which thing, in that first people, and in that first temple, one priest entered into the Holy of Holies; all the people stood outside: and he who alone entered into the inner part of the veil offered a sacrifice for the people standing outside. If it is well understood, the spirit gives life: if it is not understood, the letter kills. Just now, when the Apostle was read, you heard: *The letter kills; but the spirit gives life*[^21]. For what was being done in that people, the Jews did not know; but neither do they know now. For of them it was said: *As long as Moses is read, a veil is set over their heart*[^22]. There the veil is a figure: but the figure will be taken away, and the truth will appear in those very ones. But when will the veil be taken away? Hear the Apostle: *But when you have crossed over to the Lord, the veil will be taken away*[^23]. As long, then, as they do not cross over to the Lord, as long as they read Moses, they have a veil over the heart. For this sacrament the face of Moses then shone, *so that the sons of Israel could not gaze at his face* (you heard just now when it was read); and a veil was between the face of Moses speaking, and the people hearing the words. Through the veil they heard the words; his face they did not see. And what does the Apostle say? *So that the sons of Israel could not gaze at the face of Moses. They did not gaze,* he says, *unto the end*[^24]. What is, *unto the end*? Until they understood Christ. For the Apostle says: *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^25]. There is indeed a splendor on the face of Moses, as on a carnal and mortal face: could it be lasting or eternal? For death succeeding, it would certainly be taken away. But the splendor of the glory of our blessed Lord Jesus Christ is everlasting. But that was a temporal figure, while this, which that figure signified, is the truth. So they read, and do not understand Christ: they do not carry their attention unto the end, because the opposed veil denies them the sight of the inner splendor. And see there Christ under the veil. Our Lord Jesus Christ himself says: *If you believed Moses, you would believe me too; for he wrote of me*[^26]. But our sins and impieties being propitiated through that evening sacrifice, we cross over to the Lord, and the veil is taken away: therefore, when the Lord too was crucified, the veil of the temple was rent. *Hear my prayer: to you all flesh will come. The words of the unrighteous have prevailed over us; and our impieties you will forgive.*

**7.** [vv. 5-6] *Blessed is he whom you have chosen, and taken up.*[^27] Who is chosen by him, and taken up? Someone chosen by our Savior Jesus Christ? Or did he himself, according to the flesh, in that he is man, be chosen and taken up? so that it might be said, as it were, to the Word of God who was in the beginning, as the evangelist says: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God;* because he himself too is the Son of God, the Word of God, of whom also he says: *All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^28]: so that it might be said to that Son of God, that he himself is our priest, flesh having afterward been taken on: *Blessed is he whom you have chosen, and taken up*: that is, that man with whom you were clothed, who began in time, born of a woman, in a manner the temple of him who always is forever, and forever was. Or rather did Christ himself take up a certain blessed one, and is not he himself whom he took up spoken of as in the plural number, but in the singular? For he takes up one, because he takes up unity. Schisms he does not take up, heresies he does not take up: they have made of themselves a multitude; there is not one who may be taken up. But those who remain in the joining of Christ, and are his members, make in a manner one man, of whom the Apostle says: *Until we all come to the knowledge of the Son of God, to the perfect man, to the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ*[^29]. And so one man is taken up, whose head is Christ; because *the head of the man is Christ*[^30]. He is that *blessed man who has not gone away in the counsel of the ungodly*[^31], and the other things that are said there: he is the one who is taken up. He is not outside us; we are in his members, we are ruled under one head, we all live by one spirit, we all desire one homeland. Let us see, then, whether what is said to Christ pertains to us, and is said of us: let us question our consciences, let us search out that love; and if it is still small, and this love but newly born (for perhaps it has just now sprouted in someone), let it diligently root out the thorns sprouting beside it, that is, worldly cares, lest by growing they choke the holy shoot. *Blessed is he whom you have chosen and taken up.* Let us be in him, and we shall be taken up; let us be in him, and we shall be chosen.

**8.** And what will he give us? *He will dwell*, he says, *in your courts.*[^32] That is Jerusalem, to which those sing who begin to go out from Babylon: *He will dwell in your courts. We shall be filled with the good things of your house.* What are the good things of the house of God? Brethren, let us set before ourselves some rich house; with how great goods it is stuffed, how copious it is, how many vessels there are of gold, but also of silver; how much household, how many beasts of burden and animals; finally, the house itself, how it delights with paintings, marble, paneled ceilings, columns, spaces, chambers; and such things are desired, but still from the confusion of Babylon. Cut off all these desires, O citizen of Jerusalem; cut them off! if you wish to return, let captivity not delight you. But you have already begun to go out: do not look back, do not remain on the way. There are still no lack of enemies who would persuade you to captivity and pilgrimage: let the words of the unrighteous no longer prevail over you. Desire the house of God, and desire the good things of his house; but do not such as you are wont to desire either in your own house, or in your neighbor's house, or in your patron's house. Something else is the good of that house. What need is there for us to say what the good things of that house are? Let him himself indicate it who sings going out from Babylon: *We shall be filled*, he says, *with the good things of your house.* What are these good things? We had perhaps lifted up our heart to gold, to silver, and other precious things: do not seek such things; they press down, they do not lift up. Here then let us now meditate on those good things of Jerusalem, those good things of the house of the Lord, those good things of the temple of the Lord: because what is the house of the Lord, this same is the temple of the Lord. *We shall be filled with the good things of your house: your holy temple, wonderful in righteousness.* These are the good things of that house. He did not say: Your holy temple, wonderful in columns, wonderful in marbles, wonderful in gilded roofs; but *wonderful in righteousness.* You have eyes outside whereby you may see marbles and gold: within is the eye whereby the beauty of righteousness may be seen. Within, I say, is the eye whereby the beauty of righteousness may be seen. If there is no beauty of righteousness, whence is a just old man loved? What does he bring in his body that delights the eyes? Bent limbs, a wrinkled brow, a head whitened with gray hairs, weakness everywhere full of complaints. But perhaps because this decrepit old man does not delight your eyes, he delights your ears: by what voices? by what song? even if perhaps as a young man he sang well, all has failed with age. Or perhaps the sound of his words delights your ears, who scarcely utters full words, his teeth having fallen out? Yet if he is just, if he does not covet another's, if he gives of what he has to the needy, if he admonishes well, and relishes what is right, if he believes soundly, if he is ready for the faith of the truth to spend even those very broken limbs, for many martyrs are old men too; whence do we love him? what good do we see in him with the eyes of the flesh? Nothing. There is, then, a certain beauty of righteousness, which we see with the eye of the heart, and love, and burn for; how greatly did men love it in the martyrs themselves, when the beasts tore their limbs. Surely, when blood befouled everything, when the bowels were poured out by bestial bites, did the eyes have anything but what they shuddered at? What was there to be loved, except that in that foulness of the torn limbs there was the whole beauty of righteousness? These are the good things of the house of God; prepare yourself to be satisfied with these. But that you may be satisfied with them, when you arrive; you must hunger and thirst for this, while you are on pilgrimage: thirst for this, hunger for this; because these will be the good things of God. Hear that King to whom these things are said, who came to lead you back, and made himself a way for you. What does he say? *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled*[^33]. *Your holy temple, wonderful in righteousness.* And that very temple, brethren, do not think of it outside yourselves. Love righteousness, and you are the temple of God.

**9.** *Hear us, O God, our savior.*[^34] He has now disclosed which God he means. The Savior is properly the Lord Jesus Christ. He has now appeared more openly, of whom he had said: *To you all flesh will come. Hear us, O God, our savior.* That one man who is taken up into the temple of God, is both many, and is one. From the person of one he said, *Hear, O God, my prayer*; and because he himself, the one, consists of many, he now says, *Hear us, O God, our savior.* Hear him now more openly preached: *Hear us, O God, our savior; the hope of all the ends of the earth, and in the sea afar.* Behold whence it was said: *To you all flesh will come.* From everywhere there is a coming. *The hope of all the ends of the earth*: not the hope of one corner, not the hope of Judea alone, not the hope of Africa alone, not the hope of Pannonia, not the hope of the East, or the West; but *the hope of all the ends of the earth, and in the sea afar;* of the very ends of the earth. *And in the sea afar*: and because in the sea, therefore afar. For the sea in figure means this age, bitter with saltiness, turbulent with storms; where men by perverse and crooked greeds have become like fishes devouring one another. Attend to the evil sea, the bitter sea, savage with waves; attend with what kind of men it is full. Who desires an inheritance, except by the death of another? who desires gain, except by the loss of another? How many desire to be exalted by the falling of others! how many, that they may buy, desire others to sell their goods! how they oppress one another, and those who can, devour! and when one bigger fish has devoured the smaller, it too is devoured by a bigger one. O evil fish, you want prey from the small; you are made prey for the great. These things happen daily, they are before us; we see them, let us shudder at them. Let us not do them, brethren, because he is the hope of the ends of the earth. He, were he not the hope, also in the sea afar, would not say to his disciples: *I will make you fishers of men*[^35]. Now caught in the sea by the nets of faith, let us rejoice that we still swim there within the nets; because this sea still rages with storms, but the nets that caught us will be brought to the shore. The shore is the end of the sea: therefore the arrival at the end of the age. Meanwhile within these very nets, brethren, let us live well: let us not go outside by breaking the nets. For many have broken the nets, and made schisms, and gone outside, because they said they were unwilling to bear the evil fish caught within the nets; they themselves were made worse rather than those whom they said they could not bear. For those nets caught fish both good and bad. The Lord says: *The kingdom of heaven is like a net cast into the sea, which gathers from every kind; which, when it was filled, they drew out, and sitting on the shore, chose the good into vessels, but cast the bad outside: so it will be*, he says, *at the consummation of the age.* He shows the shore, he shows the end of the sea. *The angels will go out, and separate the bad from the midst of the just, and cast them into the furnace of fire: there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^36]. Come now, citizens of Jerusalem, you who are within the nets, and are good fish, bear with the bad; do not break the nets: you are with them in the sea; you will not be with them in the vessels. For he is the hope of the ends of the earth, he is the hope, and in the sea afar. *Afar*, because also in the sea.

**10.** [v. 7] *Preparing the mountains in his strength*[^37]: not in their strength. For he prepared great preachers, and called them mountains; humble in themselves, lofty in him. *Preparing the mountains in his strength.* What does one of those mountains say? *We ourselves had the answer of death in ourselves, that we might not trust in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead*[^38]. He who trusts in himself, and does not trust in Christ, is not of these mountains which he prepares in his strength. *Preparing the mountains in his strength, girt about with might.* Might I understand: *girt about,* what is it? Those who set Christ in the midst, make him girt about, that is, surrounded on every side. We all have him in common; therefore he is in the midst: we all who believe in him surround him about; and because our faith is not from our own powers, but from his might, therefore he is *girt about with might,* his own, not by our strength.

**11.** [vv. 8-9] *You who trouble the bottom of the sea.*[^39] He did this: it is seen what he did. For he prepared the mountains in his strength; he sent them to preach: he was girt about by those believing in his might; and the sea was stirred up; the age was stirred up, and began to persecute his saints. *Girt about with might: you who trouble the bottom of the sea.* He did not say: You who trouble the sea; but, *the bottom of the sea. The bottom of the sea* is the heart of the ungodly. For as from the bottom all things are more vehemently moved, and the bottom contains all things; so whatever proceeded through the tongue, through the hands, through various powers to the persecution of the Church, proceeded from the bottom. For if there were not a root of iniquity in the heart, all those things would not proceed against Christ. He troubled the bottom, perhaps that he might also empty out the bottom; for in certain evil ones he emptied the sea from the bottom, and made the sea a desert. Another psalm says it: *Who turns the sea into dry land*[^40]. All the ungodly and pagans who believed, were a sea; they were made earth: barren first with salt waves; afterward fruitful with the fruit of righteousness. *You who trouble the bottom of the sea: the sound of its waves, who will endure?* What is, *who will endure?* What man will endure the sound of the waves of the sea, the commands of the lofty ones of the age? But whence are they endured? Because he prepares the mountains in his strength. What he said, then, *who will endure?* this he says: We ourselves of ourselves could not endure those persecutions, unless he gave strength. *You who trouble the bottom of the sea: the sound of its waves, who will endure?*

**12.** *The nations will be troubled.*[^41] First they will be troubled: but those mountains prepared in the strength of Christ, were they troubled? The sea was troubled; it was dashed against the mountains: the sea was broken; the mountains remained unshaken. *The nations will be troubled: and all will fear.* Behold, now all fear: before, those who were troubled, now all fear. The Christians did not fear, and now the Christians are feared. All who persecuted now fear. For he conquered who is girt about with might; so all flesh comes to him, that the rest by their very fewness now fear. *And all who dwell in the ends of the earth will fear, at your signs.* For the Apostles did miracles; and thence all the ends of the earth feared, and believed.

**13.** *The goings out of morning and evening you will delight*[^42]: that is, you make delightful. Now in this life what is promised us? *The goings out you will delight, of morning and evening.* There are goings out of morning; there are goings out of evening. *Morning* signifies the prosperity of the age; *evening* signifies the tribulation of the age. Let the attention of Your Charity attend (for in both the human soul is tempted); both lest it be corrupted by prosperity, and lest it be broken by adversity. But morning signifies prosperity for this reason, because morning is glad, the sadness, as it were, of night being over. But the darkness is sad, evening coming: therefore, the evening of the world, as it were, coming, he offered the evening sacrifice. Let no one, then, fear the evening, nor be corrupted in the morning. Behold, someone, I know not who, that you may do some evil, has promised gain; it is morning; ample money smiles upon you; it becomes morning for you. Do not be corrupted, and you will have a going-out in the morning. For if you have a going-out, you will not be caught. For the promise of gain is like bait in a trap: you are hemmed in, and there is no way out; you are caught in the trap. But the Lord your God has given you a going-out, lest you be caught by gain, when he says to you in the heart: I am your riches. Do not attend to what the world promises, but what the Founder of the world promises; attend to what God has promised you who do righteousness, despise what man promises, to lead you away from righteousness. Do not, then, attend to what the world promises, but what the Founder of the world; and you will have a going-out in the morning through the word of the Lord, who says: *What does it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, but suffer the loss of his own soul*[^43]? But he who could not corrupt you by the promised gain, and lead you to iniquity, will threaten punishments, and turn himself to enmities, and begin to say to you: If you do not do this, I will reveal it, I will do it; you will have me as an enemy. First, when he promised gain, it was morning for you: now it grows toward evening; you have become sad. But he who gave you a going-out in the morning, will give you one in the evening too. As you despised the morning of the age by the light of the Lord, so despise also the evening by the sufferings of the Lord, that you may say to your soul: What more will this man do to me than my Lord suffered for me? Let me hold righteousness, let me not consent to iniquity. Let him rage against the flesh: the trap will be broken, and I will fly to my Lord who says to me: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^44]. And of the body itself he gave security, saying: *A hair of your head will not perish*[^45]. Magnificently he set it here: *The goings out of morning and evening you will delight.* For if the going-out itself did not delight you, you would not labor to go out from there. You put your head into promised gain, if the promise of the Savior does not delight you. And again, you yield to the tempter and the terrifier, if he does not delight you who first suffered, to make a going-out for you. *The goings out of morning and evening you will delight.*

**14.** [v. 10] *You have visited the earth, and inebriated it.*[^46] Whence did he inebriate the earth? *How glorious is your inebriating cup*[^47]! *You have visited the earth, and inebriated it*: you sent your clouds, they rained the preaching of truth, the earth was inebriated. *You have multiplied to enrich it.* Whence have *you multiplied to enrich it*? *The river of God has been filled with water.* What is *the river of God*? The people of God. The first people was filled, whence the rest of the earth might be watered. Hear him promising water: *If anyone thirsts, let him come and drink. He who believes in me, rivers of living water will flow from his belly*[^48]. If rivers, then one river too; because on account of unity many are one. Many Churches, and one Church; many faithful, and one bride of Christ: so many rivers, and one river. Many Israelites believed, and were filled with the Holy Spirit: thence they were spread among the Gentiles; they began to preach the truth, and from the river of God which was filled with water, the whole earth was watered. *You have prepared their food; because so is your preparation.* Not because they merited you, to whom you forgave sins: their merits were evil; but you on account of your mercy, *because so is your preparation,* so *have you prepared their food.*

**15.** [v. 11] *Inebriate its furrows.*[^49] Let there first, then, be made furrows which may be inebriated: let the hardness of our breast be opened by the plowshare of the word of God: *Inebriate its furrows; multiply its generations.* We see it: they believe, and from those believing others believe, and from those others believe; and it does not suffice for one man, made faithful himself, to gain one. So the seed too is multiplied: few grains are sown, and crops rise up. *Inebriate its furrows; multiply its generations: in its drops it will rejoice, when it springs up.* That is, before it is perhaps fit for the capacity of the river, *when it springs up, in its drops,* that is, in those suited to it, *it will rejoice.* For to the little ones still, and the weak, certain drops are dripped of the sacraments, because they cannot take the fullness of truth. Hear how it drips for the little ones while they spring up, that is, in their recent rising less capable. The Apostle says: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal, as to little ones in Christ*[^50]. When he says, *little ones in Christ,* he now says they have sprung up, but are not yet fit to take that rich wisdom, of which he says: *We speak wisdom among the perfect*[^51]. Let it rejoice in its drops, when it springs up and grows; strengthened, it will take wisdom too: as an infant is nourished with milk, and becomes fit for food; yet first, of that very food for which it was less fit, milk was made for it. *In its drops it will rejoice, when it springs up.*

**16.** [v. 12] *You will bless the crown of the year of your kindness.*[^52] It is now sown, what is sown grows, there will also be a harvest. And now upon the seed the enemy has oversown tares; and there have sprung up the evil among the good, false Christians, having a like blade, but not an equal fruit. For tares are properly called those which are born in the likeness of the grain; as is darnel, as is wild oats, and other such, which have the first blade altogether alike. Therefore of the sowing of tares the Lord says this: *The enemy came, and oversowed tares; but when the blade had grown, and made fruit, then the tares appeared*[^53]. Therefore *the enemy came, and oversowed tares*: but what did he do to the wheat? The grain is not oppressed by the tares; rather, by the bearing of the tares the fruit of the grain grows. For the Lord himself said to certain laborers wishing to root out the tares: *Let both grow until the harvest, lest perhaps, when you wish to root out the tares, you root out the wheat together with them; but in the time of harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather first the tares, and bind them in bundles to be burned; but the wheat store in the barn*[^54]. That is the end of the year, the harvest of the age. *You will bless the crown of the year of your kindness.* When you hear "crown," the glory of victory is signified. Conquer the devil, and you will have a crown. *You will bless the crown of the year of your kindness.* Again he commends the kindness of God, lest anyone glory in his own merits.

**17.** [v. 13] *And your fields will be filled with abundance. The ends of the desert will grow fat, and the hills will be girt with exultation.*[^55] The fields, the hills, the ends of the desert, are the same men. Fields on account of levelness: therefore on account of levelness, the just peoples were thence called fields. Hills on account of being lifted up; because God lifts up in himself those who humble themselves. The ends of the desert, all the Gentiles. Why the ends of the desert? They were a desert; no prophet had been sent to them: they were as a wilderness, through which no man passes. No word of God was sent to the Gentiles: only to the people of Israel did the prophets preach. There came a coming to the Lord; the wheat in that very people of the Jews believed. For he then said to his disciples: *You say that the harvest is far off; look, and see that the regions are white for the harvest*[^56]. There was, then, a first harvest; there will be a second in the last age. The first harvest from the Jews; because to them were sent the prophets announcing the Savior to come. Therefore the Lord said to his disciples: *See that the regions are white for the harvest*: the regions, namely, of Judea. *Others*, he says, *labored, and you have entered into their labors*[^57]. The prophets labored to sow, and you with the sickle entered into their labors. There was made, then, a first harvest; and from there, from that very wheat which was then purged, the whole world was sown, that there might rise another harvest which is to be reaped in the end. In the second harvest tares were oversown; now here there is labor. As in that first the prophets labored, until the Lord came; so in this second the Apostles labored, and all the preachers of truth labor, until in the end the Lord sends his angels to the harvest. Before, then, it was a desert; but *the ends of the desert will grow fat.* Behold, where the prophets had not sounded, the Lord of the prophets has been received. *The ends of the desert will grow fat, and the hills will be girt with exultation.*

**18.** [v. 14] *The rams of the sheep are clothed*[^58]: understand, *with exultation.* For with what exultation the hills will be girt, with this the rams of the sheep are clothed. The rams themselves, who are the hills. The hills, by their more eminent grace; the rams, because they are the leaders of the flocks. The rams, then, the Apostles, are clothed with exultation, they rejoice in their fruits; not without cause did they labor, not without cause did they preach. *The rams of the sheep are clothed; and the valleys will abound with grain*: and the humble peoples will bring much fruit. *They will cry out*: thence they will abound with grain, because they will cry out. What will they cry out? *For they will say a hymn.* For it is one thing to cry out against God, another to say a hymn; one thing to cry out sacrileges, another to cry out the praises of God. If you cry out in blasphemy, you have brought forth thorns; if you cry out in a hymn, you abound with grain.
