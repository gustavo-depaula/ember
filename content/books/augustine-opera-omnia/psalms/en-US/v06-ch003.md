# Exposition of Psalm 124

**1.** [v. 1.] You now know well, dearest brothers, that a song of ascents is a song of our ascension, and that this same ascension is made not by the feet of the body but by the affections of the heart. This we have very often impressed upon you; nor must the same things be repeated too often, so that there may be room to say things not yet said. This psalm too, then, which you have now heard sung to you, is headed *A Song of ascents.* That is its title. Sing, then, as you ascend: and sometimes it is as if one sings, sometimes as if many; because the many are also one, since Christ too is one, and in Christ the members of Christ make one with Christ, and the head of all these members is in heaven. But the body, even though it labors on earth, is not cut off from its head. For from above the head looks out and provides for the body. For if it did not provide, it would not say to that persecutor, still Saul, not yet Paul: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^1] And these things you know very well, and they are familiar to you. But let them not be recalled with weariness to those from whom they have not slipped, so that through their patience they may return to the heart even of those from whom they had slipped; for they are wholesome, and to be said more often. So whether one sings, or many sing—and many men are one man, because there is unity; and Christ, as we have said, is one, and all Christians are members of Christ.

**2.** What, then, do these sing? these members of Christ, what do they sing? For they love, and by loving they sing, by longing they sing. Sometimes they sing with tribulation, and sometimes they sing exulting, when they sing in hope. For our tribulation is in the present world, but our hope is of the world to come: and unless, in the tribulation of the present world, the hope of the world to come consoles us, we perish. So our joy, brothers, is not yet in reality, but already in hope. But our hope is so sure, as if the reality were already accomplished: for we do not fear, since Truth promises. For Truth can neither be deceived nor deceive: it is good for us to cleave to it; it sets us free, but only if we abide in its word. For now we believe, then we shall see: when we believe, hope is in this world; when we shall see, the reality will be in the world to come. But we shall see face to face[^2]: and we shall then see face to face, when we have hearts made clean. *For blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God*[^3]. But whence are hearts made clean, except through faith, as Peter says in the Acts of the Apostles: *cleansing their hearts by faith*[^4]. But our hearts are cleansed through faith, so that they may be fit to grasp the vision. For we now walk by faith, not yet by sight; as the apostle says: *As long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord.* And what does *we are pilgrims* mean? *For we walk by faith*, he says, *not by sight*[^5]. He, then, who is a pilgrim, and walks by faith, is not yet in the homeland, but is already on the way; but he who does not believe is neither in the homeland nor on the way. So then let us walk as though we are on the way; for the very King of the homeland was made the way. The King of our homeland is the Lord Jesus Christ; and there is truth, but here is the way. Where are we going? To the truth. By what way do we go? By faith. Where are we going? To Christ. By what way do we go? Through Christ. For he himself said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^6]. But he had once said to those who believed in him: *If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*[^7]. *And you will know*, he says, *the truth*, but *if you abide in my word.* In what word? As the apostle says: *This is the word of faith which we preach*[^8]. So first there is the word of faith; and if we abide in that word of faith, we shall know the truth, and the truth will set us free. The truth is immortal, the truth is unchangeable: the truth is that Word of which it is said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* And who sees this, except one with a clean heart? Whence are hearts made clean? *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^9]. So then, that the Word abides in itself is the truth to which we come, and which sets us free; but that the word of faith is preached, in which the Lord wishes us to remain, so that we may know the truth, this is: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.* You believe in Christ born in the flesh, and you will come to Christ born of God, God with God.

**3.** Those whom we read of sing exulting; these members of Christ sing this psalm exulting. And who exults here, except in hope, as I said? Let that very hope be sure for us too, and let us sing exulting. For those who sing are not strangers to us, nor is our voice absent from this psalm. So hear it as though you were hearing yourselves; so hear it as though in the mirror of the Scriptures you were looking at yourselves. For when you look at the Scriptures as a mirror, your face is made glad: when you find yourself like, in the exultation of hope, to certain members of Christ which sang these things, you too will be among those very members, and you will sing these things. Why, then, do these sing these things exulting? Because they have escaped. So it is in hope that they sing. For while we are here and are pilgrims, we have not yet escaped. Certain members have indeed gone ahead from that body of which we too are members, who can sing in truth. And this the holy martyrs have sung: for they have already escaped, and are in exultation with Christ, about to receive bodies now incorrupt, those very same bodies which were at first corruptible, in which they suffered punishments; from these will come for them the adornments of justice. So then, whether they are already in reality, or we in hope joining our affection to their crowns, and longing for such a life as we do not have here, but cannot have unless we have longed for it here, let us all sing together, and say: *Unless the Lord had been among us*[^10]. For they looked back on certain tribulations which they suffered, and considered, now set in that place and in blessedness and in security, through what they had passed, where they had come; and because it was difficult to be freed from there unless the hand of the deliverer were present, in joy they said: *Unless the Lord had been among us.* So they began to sing: they had not yet said from what they had escaped; so great is their exultation: *Unless the Lord had been among us.*

**4.** *Let Israel now say: Unless the Lord had been among us*[^11]. Let him say it now, because he has now escaped. For this psalm sets before us, to be gazed at, those who escape—that is, those who have already escaped. Let us too set them in our heart as already triumphing; and as though we too were there, as was said in the earlier psalm: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*[^12]. They were not yet there, but were on the way: but so great was the gladness of those hastening, and so great the hope of arriving, that, though still set on the way and laboring, they already seemed to themselves set there. So let us too now set ourselves in that triumph which will be in the world to come, when we shall exult over death now ended, now consumed; when we shall say: *Where, O death, is your striving? where, O death, is your sting?*[^13] now joined to the angels, and exulting with our King, who willed to be the first to rise again, although he did not refuse to be the first to die. For many died before him, but no one rose again before him unto eternity. But exulting with him, set there too in hope and in heart, because we have escaped, let us think what we have escaped—what scandals, what tribulations of the world, what persecutions of all the pagans, what deceits of all the heretics, what suggestions of the devil, what conflicts of the desires. Who would escape all these, *unless the Lord had been among us*? *Let Israel now say*; for Israel says securely: *Unless the Lord had been among us.* When? *When men rose up against us*[^14]. Do not, then, wonder: they were conquered; for they were men: but the Lord was among us, it was not a man that was among us; but men rose up against us. And yet men would oppress other men, were it not that in these men who could not be oppressed there was not a man, but the Lord.

**5.** [v. 3.] So then: *Unless the Lord had been among us, when men rose up against us.* For what would men do to you, exulting and singing, and securely holding everlasting blessedness? what would men rising up against you do to you, unless the Lord were among you? what would they do? *Perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive*[^15]. *They would have swallowed us up alive*; they would not first have killed us and so swallowed us up. O monstrous! O cruel! The Church does not swallow up like this: to Peter it was said: *Kill and eat*[^16]; not: Swallow up alive. How, then, does Peter—that is, the Church—kill, and so eat? and how would these who rose up against us *perhaps have swallowed us up alive, unless the Lord had been among us*? Because no one enters into the body of the Church unless first killed. What was, dies, so that what was not may be. Otherwise, he who is not killed and eaten by the Church can be in the number of the people that is seen by human eyes; but in the number of the people that is known to God, of which the apostle says: *The Lord knows who are his*[^17], he cannot be unless eaten, nor can he be eaten unless first killed. A pagan comes, idolatry still lives in him; he is to be grafted into the members of Christ: that he may be grafted in, he must be eaten; but he cannot be eaten by the Church unless first killed. Let him renounce the world, then he is killed; let him believe in God, then he is eaten. How, then, would they *have swallowed us up alive, unless the Lord had been among us*? For many persecutors arose at one time, and now they are not lacking. They rise up one by one, and sometimes they swallow men up alive, but those in whom the Lord is not. Therefore, before all else, those men said: *Unless the Lord had been among us*; because many are swallowed up in whom the Lord is not. These are they who are swallowed up alive, who know that it is evil, and consent with the tongue. For certain persecutors arose, and said to men: Offer incense; if you do not, we kill you. They loved this life, and the sweetness of this life held them. They did not love more the things God promised than the things they saw on earth. For they were ordered to believe the things they did not yet see; the things they loved, they saw. Retaining more the things they saw, they shut the Lord out of their hearts; and because the Lord was not in them, they were swallowed up alive. What does it mean, they were swallowed up alive? By offering incense to idols, knowing that an idol is nothing. For if they thought an idol were something, they would be swallowed up dead: but when they think an idol is nothing, and know that all those things of the pagans are empty, they live; and yet, when they do what the persecutors want, they are swallowed up alive. But they are swallowed up alive for this reason, because the Lord is not in them. But those in whom the Lord is are killed and do not die. But those who consent and live are swallowed up alive, and once swallowed up, they die. But those who suffered, and did not yield to the tribulations, exult and say: *Let Israel now say*; let him say it exulting, let him say it securely: *Unless the Lord had been among us, when men rose up against us, perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive.*

**6.** [v. 4.] *When their fury was kindled against us*[^18]. You know, brothers, that in one of the earlier psalms, at the very beginning of the songs of ascents, a certain man who had begun to ascend asked for help against the cunning tongue, and said: *Lord, deliver my soul from unjust lips, and from a deceitful tongue*[^19]. For first, when a man has begun to ascend and make progress, at the very outset of his ascent he suffers cunning tongues, flattering unto destruction, flattering unto evil persuasion: What are you doing? why are you doing this? Can one not live otherwise? can God not be served otherwise? You are the only one who wishes to be what others are not! And if you have found others who are with you, what does that flattering and cunning tongue say? Behold, those men could; perhaps you will not be able. You undertake it; you will fail: and it was better not to begin than to have begun and failed. The cunning tongue still flatters. If you persevere, the cunning and flattering tongue is conquered, it begins to be openly raging; and what flattered in order to seduce, threatens in order to terrify. But if the Lord is in you, and you have not abandoned Christ from your heart; just as by sharp arrows and desolating coals[^20] you conquered the cunning tongues—that is, by the words of God with which your heart was pierced through, and by the examples of the just who from the dead were brought to life, who from sinners were made just, as coals come back to life from death—just as, then, you conquered with arrows and desolating coals those who cunningly flattered and by flattering sought to seduce, so will you conquer these who now angrily threaten, because by flattering they could not seduce. They were conquered when they flattered; let them be conquered also when they threaten. But how are they conquered, *unless because the Lord was among us*? It is plain that you did not conquer, but he conquered who is in you. You bear such an Emperor, and are you conquered? Is he not the one you bear, who said: *I have conquered the world*[^21]? did he not, dying first, conquer the devil, since he was always above all creation, because the Word is God with God? Why did he conquer, except to teach you to fight with the devil? And yet, even when you have been taught, unless he who first conquered for you is in you, you are conquered. *Unless the Lord had been among us, when men rose up against us, perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive. When their fury was kindled against us*: now they are angry, now they openly rage: *perhaps the water would have drowned us*[^22]. By 'water' he means sinful peoples; and we shall see in what follows what kind of water. But whoever had consented to them, the water would have covered him. For he would die by the death of the Egyptians, he would not cross over by the example of the Israelites. For you know, brothers, that the people of the Israelites crossed through the water, and that very water covered the people of the Egyptians[^23]. *The water*, he says, *would have drowned us.*

**7.** [v. 5.] But what kind of water is this? It is a torrent; it flows with force, but it will pass away. For those rivers are called torrents which swell with sudden rains: they have great force; whoever runs into one is swept away—but one in whom the Lord is not; for one in whom the Lord is, his soul crosses the torrent. The torrent still flows, but the soul of the martyrs has already crossed. Still, as long as this world runs on by being born and dying, it is a torrent: hence persecutions, from this torrent. From this torrent that first one drank, our head, of whom it was said in the psalm: *He drank from the torrent on the way*[^24]. From the torrent, that water which signifies the persecuting people, from there he drank who said to the disciples: *Can you drink the cup which I am to drink?*[^25] *He drank from the torrent on the way.* What does it mean, *he drank on the way*? He drank in passing, he did not stick fast. He drank on the way; for of him it was perhaps said: *And he did not stand in the way of sinners*[^26]. He drank in passing. And what was said of this? *Therefore he will lift up his head*[^27]. *From the torrent*, he says, *he drank on the way; therefore he will lift up his head.* For our head has now been lifted up, because from the torrent he drank on the way; for our Lord suffered. If, then, our head has now been lifted up, why does the body fear the torrent? Without doubt, because the head has been lifted up, the body too will say afterward: *Our soul has crossed the torrent: perhaps our soul has crossed water without substance*[^28]. See what kind of water he was speaking of: *perhaps the water would have drowned us.* But what kind of water is without substance? What does it mean, *without substance*?

**8.** First, what does it mean: *perhaps our soul has crossed*? The Latin translators expressed as best they could what the Greeks say. For the Greek copies have *ara*; because it is a word of one who doubts, it was indeed expressed by the word of doubt, which is *perhaps*; but it is not quite that. We can render it by a word indeed less correctly joined in Latin, but suited to your understandings. What the Punic-speakers say, *iar*—not 'wood,' but when they doubt—this the Greeks render by *ara*, and this the Latin-speakers can or are accustomed to say: 'Do you suppose,' when they speak thus: 'Do you suppose I escaped this?' If, then, one says: 'Perhaps I escaped,' you see that it does not have that sense: but what I said, 'Do you suppose,' is said in common speech; in correct Latin it is not so said. And I was able to say it while expounding to you; for I often say even words that are not correct Latin, so that you may understand. But in Scripture this could not be put, since it would not be Latin; and, Latin failing, there was put in its place a word that would not have that sense. Yet understand it to be said thus: Do you suppose *our soul has crossed water without substance*? And why do they say: Do you suppose? Because the greatness of the danger makes it scarcely credible that one has escaped. They endured great slaughter, they were in great perils; they were so utterly pressed that they almost consented while alive, almost were swallowed up alive: so now, escaping, now secure, but recalling the very greatness of the danger: Do you suppose, they say, *our soul has crossed water without substance*?

**9.** What is water without substance, except the water of sins, without substance? For sins have no substance: they have want, not substance; they have neediness, not substance. In this water without substance that younger son lost all his substance. For you know that the younger son went into a far country, and said to his father: *Give me the portion of the substance that falls to me*[^29]. What do you want? It is better kept with your father; it is yours: you wish to squander it; you wish to set out farther off. *Give me*; but *give me.* He gave it to him. Having set out into a far country, living prodigally with harlots, he lost all his substance: he remained in want, he fed swine; in his want he remembers his father's riches. Unless want had struck him, he would not long for that fullness. So let all consider their sins, let them see whether their sins themselves have substance. For what did the sinner provoke God[^30]? If you do not see your sin before you do it, at least when you have done it, consider it. The sweetness of this world makes the throat sweet for a time; afterward it will be turned into great bitterness. Behold, you have sinned, and made a profit: what is it, that you have made a profit? In order to make a profit, you offended God; in order to increase your money, your faith was diminished, and your gold increased. What have you lost, and what have you gained? What you gained is called gold; what you lost is called faith: compare faith with gold; if faith were for sale in the market, would it have a price? You think of your gains, you do not think of your losses? Do you rejoice over your chest, and not lament over your heart? Something or other abounds in your chest; but see what has been diminished in your heart. When you open your chest, you will find coins that were not there: well, that you rejoice that what was not there is there. Attend to the chest of your heart; faith was there, and it is not. If you rejoice over the one, why do you not lament over the other? You have lost more than you have gained. Do you wish to see what you have lost? It could not be taken from you even by shipwreck. For sometimes men lose everything at sea, and come out naked. Many were shipwrecked with Paul[^31]: lovers of this world suffered shipwreck, and all came out naked; they lost both what they had outside, and found the house of their heart empty: but Paul bore in his heart the patrimony of his faith; by no waves, by no storms could it be taken from him; he came out naked, and came out rich. Such riches we ought to seek. But I do not see them, you say to me. Foolish soul, you do not see them with the eyes of your flesh; have the eye of the heart, and you see them. But you do not see faith. Why, then, do you see it in another? Why do you cry out when it is broken for you, if you do not see it? Let someone break faith with you; you cry out. So you wish it to be shown to you, and then you see it; when you are asked to show it, then you do not see it? What you cry out that another does not have toward you, lament that you do not have toward another. And see that the sin you commit is without substance. For what is gained from sin seems to be substance. But even this is not gained. For he has gold who knows how to use gold; but he who does not know how to use gold is had, he does not have; he is possessed, he does not possess. Be lords of gold, and not slaves of gold: for both God made gold, and he made you above gold; gold he made for your help, and you for his image. See what is above you, and you trample on what is below you. What, then, have you gained? Do you wish to see how the water is without substance? Take with you to the underworld what you have gained: what will you do? You have gained gold, you have lost faith: after a few days you go out of this life, the gold which you gained with your faith lost, you cannot carry with you; your heart, empty of faith, goes out to punishments, which, full of faith, would have gone out to a crown. Behold, what you have done is nothing, and for nothing you offended God. The water that drowned you is without substance. For what did the sinner provoke God[^32]? *Let those who do iniquity be confounded in vain*[^33]. For no one does not do iniquity in vain; but no one considers it.

**10.** Men go about, they hear that common proverb; and the proverbs of God lie asleep in them. What proverb? 'I prefer what I hold to what I hope for.' O unhappy man, what do you hold? Behold, you say: I prefer what I hold; hold it so as not to lose it, and say: I prefer what I hold. But if you do not hold it, why do you not hold that which you cannot lose? What, then, do you hold? Gold. Hold it, then; if you hold it, let it not be taken from you against your will. But if you too are dragged by your gold where you do not wish, and therefore a greater plunderer seeks you out, because he has found a lesser plunderer; therefore a greater eagle seeks you out, because you first caught the hare: the lesser was prey to you, you will be prey to the greater. Men do not see these things in human affairs; they are blinded by such great greed. It is a strange thing, brothers; those who consider it shudder. The more powerful seeks the weaker, and seeks to oppress him, for no other reason than that he has something to take from him: he sees that man suffer tribulation under him, for no other reason than because of what he has; and this very thing, for the sake of which the other suffers tribulation, he heaps up for himself. He did not consider, when he was persecuting the other; the other was fleeing, the other was being tormented, the other was afraid, the other was looking for where to hide himself: whence would he suffer these evils, except because he had something? At least in him learn what to flee: for the thing that tormented him, with you persecuting lest it be taken from you, will likewise torment you, with another persecuting. You notice that he is fat: if you seek him out because he is fat, fear to grow fat, lest another seek you out. And all things are done in vain: seek the end; darkness meets you: seek the reason; nothing meets you.

**11.** [v. 6.] So let those exult and rejoice in the Lord who say: *Our soul has crossed water without substance*, and let them receive their substance. They lost it living prodigally; but was the Father made poor? Let them return, and they will find there the riches which in a far-off pilgrimage they consumed with harlots; let them escape the water without substance, and say: *Blessed be the Lord, who did not give us as a prey to their teeth*[^34]. For the persecutors were hunters, and they set bait in the trap. What bait? The sweetness of this life, that each one, for the sweetness of this life, might put his head into wickedness, and be caught in the trap. Not those in whom the Lord was, those who say: *Unless the Lord had been among us*; they were not caught in the trap. Let the Lord be in you, and you too will not be caught in the trap; cry out: *Blessed be the Lord, who did not give us as a prey to their teeth.*

**12.** [v. 7.] *Our soul, like a sparrow, has been snatched from the trap of the hunters*[^35]. Because the Lord was in that very soul, therefore the soul, like a sparrow, has been snatched from the trap of the hunters. Why like a sparrow? Because, like a sparrow, it had fallen incautiously; and it could afterward say: God will pardon me. O unstable sparrow, fix your feet rather on the rock, do not go to the trap. You are caught, you are consumed, you are crushed. Let the Lord be in you, and from greater threats he will snatch you, from the trap of the hunters. Just as, if you see a bird as though already falling into the trap, you make a greater noise, that it may fly from the trap: so too the martyrs—perhaps some of them were already stretching their necks toward the sweetness of this life; the Lord who was in them made the noise of the pains of hell, and the sparrow was snatched from the trap of the hunters: *Our soul, like a sparrow, has been snatched from the trap of the hunters.* And what then? Will that trap last forever? The trap was the sweetness of this life: they did not stick fast in the trap, and they were killed; once they were killed, the trap was broken; the sweetness of this life no longer remained, by which they might again be caught, but it was shattered: was the sparrow too shattered? Far from it; because it was not in the trap: *The trap has been shattered, and we have been snatched away.*

**13.** [v. 8.] So let them cry out, because they have been snatched away; let them fly to God, let them triumph in God, because they have been snatched away: since the Lord was in them, that they might not be caught by the trap. Why was the trap shattered, and we snatched away? Do you wish to know why? *Our help is in the name of the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^36]. For if this help were not there, the trap would indeed not remain forever; but the sparrow, once caught, would be crushed. For this life will pass away; and those who have been caught by its sweetness, and through this sweetness have offended God, will pass away with this life. For the trap will be shattered; be sure of it: all the sweetness of this present life will cease to be, when the lot of its place is fulfilled; but it is needful not to stick fast, so that when the trap is shattered, you may then exult and say: *The trap has been shattered, and we have been snatched away.* But lest you think you can do this by your own strength, attend to whose work it is that you be snatched away (for if you grow proud, you fall into the trap), and say: *Our help is in the name of the Lord, who made heaven and earth.*

**14.** The psalm has been treated, and, as far as the Lord has deigned to help, expounded, as I think. You know very well that a sermon is owed to your charity tomorrow also; be present, and help us by your prayers. For you ought to remember our promise; nor would I say what I was about to treat, were I not wishing to be helped by the zeal of your faith and your prayers. For you remember that I promised you to treat of what was said in the Gospel: *The Law was given through Moses; Grace and truth came through Jesus Christ*[^37]. For heretics, especially the Manichees, are accustomed to find fault with the Law, and to say that God did not give it. This passage must be expounded, so that it may be recognized both that God gave the Law, and that the Law was given through Moses; so that it would not save, for a definite reason. The Law did not save, so that the giver of the Law himself, as Emperor, might be longed for, to grant pardon to sinners; and the Law was indeed given through Moses, but Grace and truth came through Jesus Christ. To this I wished to make you attentive. The mercy of the Lord will be present, not from our merits, but perhaps for the merit of your longing; nor from our resources, but from the abundance of his gifts: so that a matter quite necessary for men set in the New Testament may be so treated that the enemy may find no hiding places at all where he may conceal himself to deceive the faithful.
