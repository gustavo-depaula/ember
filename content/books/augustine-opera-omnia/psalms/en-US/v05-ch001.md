# Exposition of Psalm 99

**1.** You have heard the psalm, brothers, while it was being sung; it is brief, and not obscure, so that I may seem to have given you security, that you need not fear hard work. Yet let us look more attentively, and the more freely we may do so, the more diligently, at what those very things mean that ring out so openly, so that—as far as the Lord deigns to grant—they may be understood spiritually. The voice of God sounding through any instrument is still the voice of God; for nothing delights his ears but his own voice. For when we too speak, we delight him only when he himself speaks about us.

**2.** [v. 1.] *A psalm for confession*: so it is inscribed; this is its title: *A psalm for confession.* The verses are few, but heavy with great matters: let the seeds give birth in your hearts, so that a barn may be made ready for the Lord's harvest. This psalm of confession commands us this, and urges us to this: that we should shout for joy to God. And it does not urge some single corner of the earth, or some one dwelling or gathering of human beings; but because he knows that he has everywhere sown his blessing, from every quarter he demands a shout of joy.

**3.** [v. 2.] *Shout for joy* therefore *to the Lord, all the earth*[^1]. Does the whole earth now hear my voice? And yet the whole earth has heard this voice. The whole earth already shouts for joy to the Lord; and what does not yet shout will shout. For the blessing, reaching out as the Church begins from Jerusalem through all the nations, everywhere lays impiety low and everywhere builds up piety: and the good are mingled with the bad, and the bad are throughout the whole earth, and the good are throughout the whole earth. In the bad the whole earth murmurs; in the good the whole earth shouts for joy. What then is it to shout for joy? For the very title of the present psalm makes us very attentive to this word, since it is inscribed *For confession.* What is it to shout for joy in confession? There is a saying of another psalm: *Blessed is the people that knows the shout of joy*[^2]: surely it is some great thing whose understanding makes one blessed. May the Lord our God, then, the one who makes human beings blessed, grant me to understand what I say, and grant you to understand what you hear: *Blessed is the people that knows the shout of joy.* Let us therefore run toward this blessedness, let us understand the shout of joy, and not pour it out without understanding. What is the use of shouting for joy and obeying this psalm that says *Shout for joy to God, all the earth*[^3], and not understanding the shout of joy, so that our voice alone shouts and the heart does not shout? For the sound of the heart is understanding.

**4.** I am going to say what you know. One who shouts for joy does not utter words, but there is a certain sound of gladness without words: for it is the voice of a mind poured out in gladness, expressing its feeling as far as it can, though not grasping its meaning. A man rejoicing in his exultation, out of certain words that cannot be spoken and understood, bursts into a certain cry of exultation without words, so that he plainly rejoices by that very cry, but, as though filled with too much joy, cannot unfold in words what he rejoices over. Notice this in those who sing even disreputable songs. For our shout of joy will not be like theirs; we ought to shout for joy in righteousness, whereas they shout for joy in wickedness: so we in confession, they in confusion. Yet, that you may understand what I say—or rather call to mind a thing you already know—those who labor in the fields shout for joy most of all; gladdened by the abundance of the crops, whether reapers or grape-gatherers or those harvesting any fruits, rejoicing in the very fruitfulness and fertility of the earth, they sing as they exult; and among the songs they utter in words, they insert certain cries without words, in the uplifting of an exulting mind, and this is called the shout of joy. If anyone perhaps does not recognize this because he has never noticed it, let him take note from now on. And would that he might not find any to take note of, lest God find any to overthrow. But since the thorns do not cease to spring up, let us note in those who exult wrongly the shout of joy that is to be condemned, and let us offer to God the shout of joy that is to be crowned.

**5.** When, then, do we shout for joy? When we praise what cannot be spoken. For we consider the whole of creation, the earth and the sea and the sky and all that is in them; we consider that each thing has its own origins and causes, the power of seeds, the order of birth, the manner of remaining, the passing away of decay, that the cycles of the ages run on without any disturbance, that the stars are turned in a certain way from east to west, completing the courses of the years; we see the measurings of months, the spans of hours; and in all these there is some invisible something, which is called spirit or soul, present in all living things, a kind of trace of unity directing them to seek pleasure and flee distress, to preserve their own well-being; and present in man too is something he has in common with the angels of God—not, as with the beasts, such things as to live, to hear, to see, and the rest, but that he understands God, that this belongs properly to the mind, that just as the eye distinguishes white from black, so the mind distinguishes fairness from unfairness. In this whole consideration of creation, which we have been able somehow to name and run through, let the soul ask itself: Who made all these things? who created these? who created you yourself among them? What are these things you are considering? what are you who are considering them? Who is he who made the things to be considered and the one who considers them? who is he? Speak him; in order to speak him, think of him. For you can think something and perhaps cannot speak it; but in no way will you be able to speak what you could not think. Therefore think of him before you speak him; in order to think of him, draw near to him. For when you wish to see something well, so that you may have something to say, you draw near to look at it closely, lest perhaps by looking from afar you be deceived. But as these bodies are beheld by the eyes, so he is beheld by the mind, attended to and seen by the heart. And where is the heart by which he is seen? *Blessed*, he says, *are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^4]. I hear, I believe, I understand as I can, that God is seen by the heart, and cannot be beheld except by a pure heart; but I hear another Scripture: *Who will boast that he has a chaste heart? or who will boast that he is clean from sin?*[^5] I have considered the whole of creation, then, as far as I could; I have observed the bodily in heaven and on earth, and the spiritual in my very self who speak, who give life to my limbs, who direct my voice, who move my tongue, who pronounce words and distinguish perceptions. And when do I grasp myself within myself? How then can I grasp what is above me? Yet to the human heart the vision of God is promised, and a certain work of cleansing the heart is enjoined; this is what Scripture says: Make ready, before you see it, the means by which to see what you love. For when God and his name are heard, to whom is what he hears not sweet, except to one who is impious and far removed, made very far off? *For behold*, it says, *those who make themselves far from you shall perish*; and there follows, *you have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*[^6]. But what of us? Because they are far off, and therefore in darkness, and so with eyes so wounded in the darkness that they not only do not desire the light but even shudder at it; what is said to us who are found in the distance? *Draw near to him and be enlightened*[^7]. But that you may draw near and be enlightened, let your own darkness displease you; condemn what you are, that you may deserve to be what you are not. You are unrighteous, you ought to be righteous; you will never receive righteousness if unrighteousness still pleases you. Crush it in your heart, and cleanse it; cast it out of your heart, where the one you wish to see wishes to dwell. So the human soul draws near in whatever measure, the inner man recreated to the image of God, since he was created to the image of God; he had been made far off in proportion as he had gone into unlikeness. For one does not draw near to God, or withdraw from God, by intervals of space: made unlike, you have withdrawn far off; made like, you draw very near. See how the Lord wishes us to draw near, first making us like, that we may draw near. *Be*, he says, *like your Father who is in heaven, who makes his sun rise on the good and the bad, and rains on the just and the unjust*[^8]. Learn to love your enemy, if you would guard against an enemy. And to the degree that love grows in you, making you and calling you back to the likeness of God, it reaches even to enemies; that you may be like him who makes his sun rise not only on the good but on the good and the bad, and rains not only on the just but on the just and the unjust. The more you draw near to that likeness, the more you advance in love, and the more you begin to perceive God. And whom do you perceive? the one who comes to you, or the one to whom you return? For he never departed from you: God withdraws from you when you withdraw from God. All things are present to the blind as to the seeing: standing in one place, a blind man and a seeing man are both surrounded by the same forms of things; the one is present to them, the other absent: of two standing in one place, one is present, the other absent—not because the things themselves draw near to the one and withdraw from the other, but because of the unlikeness of their eyes. The one who is called blind, because that which is wont to be attuned to the light that clothes all things has been extinguished in him, is present in vain to the things he does not see; or rather he is more rightly called absent than present: for where his perception is not, he is rightly called absent; for this is to be absent, to be away in perception. So too God is everywhere present, everywhere whole. His wisdom reaches mightily from end to end, and orders all things sweetly. And what God the Father is, that too is his Word and his Wisdom, light from light, God from God. What then do you long to see? What you wish to see is not far from you. The Apostle says indeed that he is not placed far from any one of us: *for in him we live and move and have our being*[^9]. How great a misery it is, then, to be far from him who is everywhere!

**6.** Be like him, then, in piety, and loving in your thinking; for his invisible things are perceived, understood through the things that have been made. Gaze upon the things that have been made, marvel, seek the maker. If you are unlike, you are driven back; if you are like, you will exult. And when you have begun to draw near, made like, and to perceive God, in proportion as love grows in you—since love too is God—you will perceive something that you used to speak but did not speak. For before you perceived, you supposed you were speaking God; you begin to perceive, and there you perceive that what you perceive cannot be spoken. But when there you have learned that what you perceive cannot be spoken, will you be silent, and not praise? Will you then be mute in the praises of God, and not render thanks to him who willed to make himself known to you? Did you praise when you were seeking, and will you fall silent when you have found? By no means; you will not be ungrateful. Honor is owed, reverence is owed, great praise is owed. Consider who you are, earth and ashes; see who has deserved, and what, to see; see who, what—a man, God. I acknowledge not the merit of man, but the mercy of God. Praise, then, the one who has mercy. How, you say, shall I praise? That very little which I can perceive in part, in a riddle, through a mirror, I already cannot unfold. Hear the psalm, then: *Shout for joy to the Lord, all the earth.* You have understood the shout of joy of all the earth, if you shout for joy to the Lord. Shout to the Lord; do not divide your shout of joy among one thing and another. In the end, other things can somehow be spoken; he alone is ineffable, who spoke, and all things were made. He spoke, and we were made: but we cannot speak him. His Word, by which we were spoken, is his Son: that he might somehow be spoken by us who are weak, he became weak. We can say a shout of joy in place of a word, but we cannot say a word for the Word. *Shout for joy*, then, *to the Lord, all the earth.*

**7.** *Serve the Lord with gladness.* All slavery is full of bitterness: all who are bound by the condition of slavery both serve and murmur. Do not fear the slavery of that Lord: there will be no groaning there, no murmuring, no indignation; no one asks to be put up for sale from it, because it is sweet that we have all been redeemed. It is great happiness, brothers, to be a slave in that great house, even if in chains. Do not fear, slave in chains, but make confession to the Lord: ascribe your chains to your own deserts; make confession in your chains, if you wish them to be turned into ornaments. It was not in vain, nor without being heard, that it was said, *Let the groaning of those in chains enter into your sight*[^10]. *Serve the Lord with gladness.* Free is the slavery in the Lord's house; free is the slavery where it is not necessity but love that serves. *You*, he says, *were called to liberty, brothers: only do not give your liberty as an occasion for the flesh; but through the love of the Spirit serve one another*[^11]. Let love make you a slave, because truth has made you free. *If you remain*, he says, *in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you shall know the truth, and the truth shall set you free*[^12]. You are at once both slave and free: a slave, because you were made; free, because you are loved by God by whom you were made; indeed, free for this very reason, because you love him by whom you were made. Do not serve with murmuring; for your murmurings do not bring it about that you do not serve, but that you serve as a bad slave. You are a slave of the Lord, you are a freedman of the Lord; do not seek to be set free in such a way as to depart from the house of the one who freed you.

**8.** *Serve the Lord with gladness.* That gladness will be full and perfect when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality: then there will be perfect gladness, then that perfect shout of joy, then praise without failing, then love without scandal, then fruit without fear, then life without death. What of here? Is there no joy at all? If there is no joy, there is no shout of joy: how then, *Shout for joy to the Lord, all the earth?* There is plainly joy here too; from the hope of the future life there is tasted here the source by which we shall be satisfied there. But it is necessary that much of the wheat endure among the tares: there are grains among the chaff, there is the lily among the thorns. For what does the Church hear? *As the lily among thorns, so is my beloved among the daughters*[^13]. It does not say, among strangers, but *among the daughters.* O Lord, how you console, how you strengthen, how you frighten! What is it you say? *As the lily among*—what *thorns? So is my beloved among*—what *daughters?* Those you call thorns, those very ones daughters? He answers: They are thorns because of their ways; daughters because of my sacraments. Would, then, that there were groaning among the groans of strangers; there would be less to groan over. That is the greater groan: *For if an enemy had reproached me, I would surely have borne it; and if one who hated me had spoken great things against me, I would surely have hidden myself from him.* These are the words of the psalm: whoever knows our Scriptures, follows; whoever does not know them, let him learn, that he may follow. *If one who hated me had spoken great things against me, I would surely have hidden myself from him: but it was you, of one mind with me, my guide and my acquaintance, who together with me used to take sweet food*[^14]. What sweet food do those take with us who are not going to be with us forever? what sweet food, except *Taste and see how sweet the Lord is*[^15]? Among such it is necessary that we groan.

**9.** But where will the Christian go off to, so as not to groan among false brothers? Where will he go? what will he do? Will he seek the wilderness? Scandals will follow. Will the one who makes good progress separate himself, so as to put up with no human being at all? What if, before he made progress, no one had been willing to put up with him? If, then, because he makes progress, he is unwilling to put up with any human being, by the very fact that he is unwilling to put up with any human being he is convicted of not having made progress. Let your Charity attend: *Bearing with one another*, says the Apostle, *in love, being eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace*[^16]. *Bearing with one another*: have you nothing in you for another to bear with? I would be surprised if there is not; but suppose there is not; you are the stronger for bearing with the rest, in that you now have nothing in you for others to bear with. You are not borne with: bear with the rest. I cannot, you say. Then you do have something in you for others to bear with too. *Bearing with one another in love.* You desert human affairs, and set yourself apart, so that no one may see you; whom will you profit? Would you have reached this point if no one had profited you? Or, because you think you have had swift feet for crossing over, are you going to cut off the bridge? I exhort all; the voice of God exhorts all: *Bearing with one another in love.*

**10.** I will separate myself, someone says, with a few good men; with them it will be well for me. For to profit no one is impious and cruel. My Lord did not teach me this. For he did not condemn the servant who embezzled what he had received, but the one who did not put it out to use. Let the punishment of the embezzler be understood from the punishment of the lazy one. *Wicked and lazy servant*, says the Lord in condemning him: he does not say, You embezzled my money; he does not say, I gave to you, and you did not return to me intact what I gave: Because it did not grow, because you did not put it out to use, for that, he says, I will punish you. God is greedy for our salvation. So I will separate myself, he says, with a few good men: what have I to do with reckoning with crowds? Well, those few good men—out of what crowds were they strained? If, granted, those few are themselves all good; still it is a good human thought, and a praiseworthy one, to be with such as have chosen the quiet life; removed from the noise of the people, from restless crowds, from the great waves of the world, they are as if in harbor. Is that joy, then, already there? is that shout of joy that is promised already there? Not yet; but as yet groaning, as yet the anxiety of temptations. For even a harbor has an entrance from somewhere: if the harbor had an entrance on no side, no ship would enter it; it must, then, lie open on some side; but sometimes through that side where it lies open the wind rushes in; and where there are no rocks, the ships, dashed against one another, are wrecked. Where, then, is security, if not even in the harbor? And yet it must be confessed, granted, it is true, that they are somehow happier in harbor than on the open sea. Let them love one another; let the ships in harbor be well moored to one another, not dashed against one another: let there be preserved there an evenness of fairness, a constancy of love; and when perhaps the wind rushes in from that side where it lies open, let there be careful steering there.

**11.** For what will someone say to me who perhaps presides over such places—or rather serves the brothers in what are called monasteries? what will he say? I will be careful, I will admit no evil. How will you admit no evil? I will admit no evil man, no evil brother entering; with a few good men it will be well for me. Where do you come to know the one you perhaps wish to shut out? For an evil man to be known, he must be tested within: how, then, do you shut out one who is about to enter, who must afterward be tested, and cannot be tested unless he has entered? Will you repel all the evil? For you claim that you also know how to look into men. Do all come to you with bared hearts? Those who are about to enter do not know themselves; how much less do you? For many have promised themselves that they would carry out that holy life that holds all things in common, where no one calls anything his own, who are of one soul and one heart toward God: they were cast into the furnace, and they cracked. How, then, do you come to know one who is still unknown to himself? Will you shut out evil brothers from the assembly of the good? From your own heart, whoever you are who say this, shut out, if you can, all evil thoughts: let not even an evil suggestion enter into your heart. I do not consent, you say. Yet it entered, in order to make its suggestion. For we all wish to have hearts fortified, so that nothing may enter that is suggested for evil. But who knows from where it may enter? And we fight daily within our one heart; one man wrestles in his heart with a crowd. Greed suggests, lust suggests, gluttony suggests, that popular gladness suggests; all things make suggestions: he holds himself back from all, answers all, and turns away from all; it is hard for him not to be struck by some one of them. Where, then, is security? Here, nowhere; in this life, nowhere, except in the hope of God's promises alone. But there, when we have arrived there, there is perfect security, when the gates are shut and the bars of the gates of Jerusalem are made firm: there is the truly full shout of joy and great joy. But now do not, in your security, praise any sort of life; before death do not praise any man.

**12.** Now from this men are deceived, so that they either do not take up the better life or rashly attempt it; because both those who wish to praise it praise it so as not to mention the evils that are mixed in there, and those who wish to find fault find fault with so envious and perverse a mind that they shut their eyes against the goods, and exaggerate only the evils that are, or are thought to be, there. Hence it comes about that, whenever any profession is badly praised—that is, not carefully praised—when it has invited men by its praise, those who come there find some such as they did not believe were there; and, offended by the bad, they recoil from the good. Brothers, apply this lesson to your life, and so hear it that you may live. The Church of God is praised, to speak generally: great men are the Christians, the only Christians, great is the Catholic Church; they all love one another, each spends on the others what he can, time is given throughout the whole world to prayers, fastings, hymns, with one consent of peace God is praised. One who does not know that there is silence about the evils mixed in perhaps hears this, comes invited by the praise, finds evils mixed in that were not foretold to him before he came; he is offended by false Christians, he flees from true Christians. Again, the hateful, the slanderous, rush into faultfinding: What sort of Christians? what Christians? Misers, usurers. Are they not the very ones who fill theaters and amphitheaters at the games and at other spectacles, who fill the churches on feast days? Drunkards, gluttons, envious, persecutors of one another. There are such, but not only such. And that faultfinder, with a blind mind, says nothing of the good; and that praiser, with an incautious mind, says nothing of the bad. But if the Church of God is praised in this way in our time, how do the Scriptures of God praise it? See how I said just now: *As the lily among thorns, so is my beloved among the daughters*[^17]: a man hears, considers, the lily pleases him, he enters, he clings to the lily, he endures the thorns; he will deserve to be among the praises and the kisses of the bridegroom, who says, *As the lily among thorns, so is my beloved among the daughters.* So too with the clergy. Praisers of the clergy point there to good ministers, faithful stewards, bearers with all, spending their own hearts on those they wish to profit, not seeking what is their own but what is Jesus Christ's. They praise these, they forget that they are mixed with the bad. Again those who reprove the greed of the clergy, the wickednesses of the clergy, the quarrels of the clergy, coveting others' goods, drunkards, gluttons, fling these about. And you find fault enviously, and you praise incautiously: you who praise, mention the bad mixed in; you who find fault, see the good there too. So too in that common life of the brothers, which is in a monastery: Great men, holy, daily in hymns, in prayers, in praises of God, they live from this, their occupation is with reading; they labor with their hands, from this they support themselves; they ask nothing greedily, whatever is brought to them by pious brothers they use with sufficiency and with love; no one usurps for himself anything that another does not have; all love one another, all bear with one another. You have praised, you have praised: one who does not know what goes on within, who does not know how, when that wind enters, even ships in harbor dash against one another, enters as if hoping for security, expecting to have no one to put up with; he finds there evil brothers, who could not be found to be evil unless they were admitted (and they must first be put up with, in case they should perhaps be corrected; nor can they easily be shut out unless they have first been put up with): and he himself becomes a man of unbearable impatience. Who was looking for me here? I thought that there was love here. And, provoked by the trouble of a few men, since he has not persevered in fulfilling what he vowed, he becomes a deserter of so holy a purpose, and guilty of a vow not paid. Then, when he has gone out from there, he too becomes a faultfinder and a slanderer: and he says only those things which he asserts he could not endure; and sometimes they are true. But the true faults of the bad must be endured for the sake of fellowship with the good. Scripture says to him: *Woe to those who have lost endurance*[^18]. And, what is worse, he belches out the foul odor of his indignation, by which he may frighten off those about to enter; because he himself, when he had entered, could not hold out. What sort of men were they? Envious, quarrelsome, bearing with no one, greedy; this one did that there, and that one did that there. O wicked man, why are you silent about the good? Those you could not endure, you fling about: those who endured you when you were evil, you pass over in silence.

**13.** Rightly, then, dearest brothers, that magnificent saying in the Lord's Gospel, from the Lord's own mouth: *Two in the field; one will be taken, and one left: two at the mill; one will be taken, and one left: two in the bed; one will be taken, and one left*[^19]. Who are the *two in the field?* What the Apostle says: *I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the growth. You are God's field*[^20]. In the field we labor. The *two in the field* are the clergy: *one will be taken, and one left;* the good will be taken, the bad will be left. The *two at the mill* he referred to the laity. Why *at the mill?* Because, bound to the world, they are held back by the round of temporal affairs as by a millstone. And from there *one will be taken, and one left.* Which one will be taken from there? The one who does good works, who attends to the need of the servants of God, the need of the poor, faithful in confession, sure in the gladness of hope, watchful toward God, wishing evil on no one, loving as far as she can not only friends but even enemies, the woman knowing no man but her own husband, the man knowing no woman but his own wife; such will be taken even from the mill: but she who has been otherwise will be left. Others, however, say: We want quiet, we want to put up with no one, we remove ourselves from the crowds; it will be well for us in a certain security. If you seek quiet, you seek as it were a bed, that you may rest without any anxiety. And from there *one will be taken, and one left.* Let no one deceive you, brothers: if you do not wish to be deceived, and wish to love the brothers, know that every profession in the Church has its pretenders. I did not say that every man is a pretender, but that every profession has pretended persons: there are bad Christians, but there are good ones too. You seem to see more bad ones, because they are chaff, and do not let you reach the grains: there are grains there too; draw near, try, shake out, apply the judgment of the mouth. You find undisciplined nuns; is holy chastity therefore to be reproved? Many do not stay in their own houses, but go about others' houses, busybodies, speaking what they ought not, proud, sharp-tongued, drunken: even if they are virgins, what does intact flesh profit, with a corrupt mind? A humble marriage is better than a proud virginity. For if she married, she would not have a name by which to exalt herself, and would have a bridle by which to be governed. But are we, on account of bad virgins, going to condemn women holy in both body and spirit? or, on account of these praiseworthy ones, shall we be compelled to praise even those who are to be condemned? Everywhere one will be taken, and one left.

**14.** So, brothers, let us finish the psalm, since it is plain. *Serve the Lord with gladness*: it addresses you, whoever you are who endure all things in love, and rejoice in hope. *Serve the Lord*, not in the bitterness of murmuring, but *in the gladness* of love. *Enter into his sight, in exultation.* It is easy to exult outwardly; exult in the sight of God. Let not the tongue much exult; let the conscience exult. *Enter into his sight, in exultation.*

**15.** [v. 3.] *Know that the Lord himself is God.* Who does not know that the Lord himself is God? But he speaks of the Lord whom men did not suppose to be God: *Know that the Lord himself is God*—let that Lord not become cheap in your eyes: you crucified him, scourged him, smeared him with spittle, crowned him with thorns, clothed him in a robe of disgrace, hung him on the wood, fastened him with nails, struck him with a lance, set guards at the tomb; he himself is God. *Know that the Lord himself is God. He made us, and not we ourselves.* *He made us*: all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. What is it that you exult over? what is it that you are proud of? Another made you; and the one who made you suffers from you. But you so boast and glory and exalt yourselves, as though you had been made by your very selves. It is good for you that the one who made you should perfect you. *He made us, and not we ourselves.* We ought not to be proud: all the good that we have, we have from our maker; what we ourselves have made in ourselves, for that we are condemned; what he has made in us, for that we are crowned. *He made us, and not we ourselves. But we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.* The sheep and the one sheep: those very sheep are one sheep. And how most loving a shepherd of us we have! He left the ninety-nine, came down to seek the one, carries her back on his shoulders, redeemed by his own blood. Untroubled, the shepherd died for the sheep, who rising again possesses the sheep. *But we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.*

**16.** [vv. 4, 5.] *Enter into his gates in confession.* In the gates is the beginning; begin from confession. Hence *A psalm for confession*, there shout for joy. Confess that you were not made by yourselves, praise him by whom you were made. Let your good be from him, by withdrawing from whom you made your evil. *Enter into his gates in confession.* Let the flock enter the gates, let it not remain outside among the wolves. And how is it to enter? *In confession.* Let the gate, that is, the beginning, be confession for you. Hence in another psalm it is said, *Begin to the Lord in confession*[^21]. What there he calls *Begin*, here he has called *gates: Enter into his gates in confession.* And what then? When we have already entered, shall we not confess? Always confess, you always have something to confess. It is hard in this life for a man to be so changed that nothing is found in him to be reproved: you must reprove yourself, lest he reprove who is going to condemn. So even when you have entered the courts, confess. When will there be no confession of sins? In that rest, in that equality with the angels. But see what I said: There will be no confession of sins. I did not say: There will be no confession: for there will be a confession of praise. You will always confess—him as God, yourself as creation; him as protector, yourself as protected. In him you will somehow be hidden, as it is said, *You will hide them in the hiding of your face*[^22]. *In his courts confess to him in hymns.* In the gates confess; and in the courts, when you have entered, confess in hymns. Hymns are praises. When you enter, reprove yourself; when you have entered, praise him. *Open to me the gates of righteousness*, he says in another psalm; *entering into them I will confess to the Lord*[^23]. Did he say, when I have entered, I will then no longer confess? Even when he has entered he will confess. For what sins did our Lord Jesus Christ confess, when he said, *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^24]? He confessed by praising him, not by accusing himself.

**17.** *Praise his name, for the Lord is sweet.* Do not suppose that you will fail in praising. Your praise will be like eating: the more you praise, the more strength you acquire, and the sweeter becomes the one you praise. *Praise his name, for the Lord is sweet. His mercy is forever.* For when he has freed you, he will not cease to be merciful: that he should protect you always unto eternal life is the work of his mercy. *Forever*, then, *is his mercy, and his truth even to generation and generation.* Either take what is said, *to generation and generation*, as meaning every generation; or, in two generations, one earthly, the other heavenly. There is one generation that gives birth to mortals; another that gives birth to the eternal. His truth is both here and there. Do not suppose that his truth is not here: if his truth were not here, he would not say in another psalm, *Truth has arisen from the earth*[^25]; nor would Truth itself say, *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age*[^26].
