# On the Same Psalm 118 (20)

**1.** [v. 89.] The man who speaks in this psalm, as though wearied by the mutability of men, from which this life is full of temptations, amid his tribulations—on account of which he had said above, *The unjust have persecuted me,* and, *They have almost made an end of me on the earth*—inflamed with desire for the heavenly Jerusalem, looked up to the things on high and said: *For ever, O Lord, your word abides in heaven;* that is, in your angels who keep an eternal warfare without desertion.

**2.** [v. 90.] The following verse, after heaven, pertains consequently to earth. For it is one of those eight verses which pertain to this letter. For under each Hebrew letter eight are set, until the length of this psalm is ended. *From generation to generation is your truth; you founded the earth, and it abides.* After heaven, then, beholding the earth with the gaze of a faithful mind, he finds in it generations which are not in heaven, and says, *From generation to generation is your truth:* either signifying by this repetition all generations, from which the truth of God was never lacking in his saints, now fewer, now more, as the variety of times has held or will hold; or wishing two certain generations to be understood, one, namely, pertaining to the Law and the Prophets, the other to the Gospel. But why the truth never fails even these generations, as though opening the cause, *You founded,* he says, *the earth, and it abides;* calling those who are on the earth the earth. But no one can lay another foundation besides that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus[^1]. For not even of that generation pertaining to the Law and the Prophets was Christ not the foundation, having testimony from the Law and the Prophets. Or are Moses and the Prophets to be reckoned among the sons of the bondwoman who begets unto slavery, not of the free woman, who is our mother[^2]; of whom *a man shall say, Mother Sion; and a man was made in her, and the Most High himself founded her*[^3]? For he is both the Most High with the Father, and for our sake made most lowly in this mother; since he who was God over her *was made man in her.* By this foundation, then, O Lord, *you founded the earth, and it abides;* because, established on such a foundation, it will not be moved for ever and ever; abiding, surely, in those to whom you are to give eternal life. But those whom the bondwoman bore, pertaining to the old testament—in whose figures, nevertheless, the new lay hidden—relishing nothing but earthly promises, do not abide. For a servant does not remain in the house for ever, but a son remains for ever[^4].

**3.** [v. 91.] *By your ordinance the day abides.* For all these things are day: and this is the day which the Lord has made; let us exult and rejoice in it[^5]; and let us walk honestly as in the day[^6]. *For all things serve you. All things,* namely, of which he was speaking; *all things* that pertain to this day *serve you.* For the ungodly, of whom it is said, *I have likened your mother to the night*[^7], do not serve you.

**4.** [v. 92.] Then he looked to that whereby this earth is set free, that, founded, it may abide; and he added: *Unless your law were my meditation, then perhaps I would have perished in my humiliation.* This is the law of faith; not of empty faith, but of faith that works through love. Through this grace is obtained, which makes men strong in temporal tribulation, that they may not perish in mortal humiliation.

**5.** [v. 93.] *For ever,* he says, *I will not forget your statutes, for in them you have given me life.* Behold whence it came about that he did not perish in his humiliation. For with God not giving life, what is man, who could kill himself, but cannot give himself life?

**6.** [v. 94.] Then he adds and says: *I am yours, save me, for I have sought out your statutes.* It is not to be understood in passing, what was said, *I am yours.* For what is not his? Or perhaps, because God is said to be in the heavens, is it therefore to be supposed that something is not his on earth, when another psalm cries, *The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof, the world and all who dwell in it*[^8]? What is it, then, that this man thought he should so, in a way more intimately, commend himself to God as to say, *I am yours, save me,* except that he wished it to be understood that to his own harm he had wished to be his own—which is the first and greatest evil of disobedience? And as if saying, "I wished to be my own, and made myself lost," *I am yours,* he says, *save me, for I have sought out your statutes;* not my own wills, by which I was my own, but *your statutes,* that I might now be yours.

**7.** [v. 95.] *Sinners,* he says, *have waited for me, to destroy me; but I have understood your testimonies.* What is, *they have waited, to destroy me?* Have they, as it were, beset the road with ambush, waiting that, when he passed, they might kill him? Did he then fear to perish by the death of the body? Far from it. And what is, *they have waited for me,* except that I should consent with them to evil? For then they would destroy me. But whence he did not perish he said: *I have understood your testimonies.* But here the Greek word sounds more intimately to the ears of the Church: *I have understood your martyria.* Because, namely, even if they should kill me, not consenting to them, confessing your martyria I would not perish: but those who waited, that they might destroy me, for when I should consent, tortured me even while I confessed. Yet he did not abandon what he had understood, beholding and seeing, surely, the end without end, if he should persevere to the end.

**8.** [v. 96.] Finally he went on and added: *Of all consummation I have seen the end; your commandment is exceedingly broad.* For he had entered into the sanctuary of God, and had understood as to the last things[^9]. But all consummation seems to me in this place to be understood as: to strive even to death for the truth, and for the true and highest good to bear all evils; of which consummation the end is to excel in the kingdom of Christ, which has no end; and to have there, without death, without pain, and with great honor, the life acquired by the death of this life and by pains and reproaches. But that he added, *Your commandment is exceedingly broad;* I understand nothing but love. For what would it have profited, with any death whatever threatening, and amid however great torments, to confess those martyria, if love were not in the one confessing? Let us hear the Apostle: *Though I deliver,* he says, *my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^10]. But the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^11]. In this outpouring is the breadth in which the narrow way too is walked without straits, by the gift of him to whom it was said, *You have enlarged my steps under me, and my footsteps have not weakened*[^12]. Broad, then, is the commandment of love, that twofold commandment by which God and neighbor are commanded to be loved. But what is broader than that on which the whole Law hangs, and all the Prophets[^13]?
