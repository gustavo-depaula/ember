# On the Same Psalm 30 (3)

**1.** What remains of the psalm, on which we have already had two sermons, is somewhat more than a third part, and we see that the debt must be fulfilled by us today. Whence I ask your Charity that you would willingly bear with us in not lingering over its plainer words, so that those things may hold us which there is need to expound. For many things present themselves of their own accord to the minds of the faithful, many that need a brief reminder: but certain things, and these rarer, over which one must sweat that they may be understood. So that the time may suffice both for our strength and for yours, see how plain these things are; and recognize them rather with us, and in them praise God with us: and if the psalm prays, pray; and if it groans, groan; and if it gives thanks, rejoice; and if it hopes, hope; and if it fears, fear. For all the things written here are our mirror.

**2.** [v. 16.] *Rescue me from the hands of my enemies, and from those who persecute me*: let us say this, and let each one say this of his enemies; for it is good, and we ought to pray that God rescue us from the hands of our enemies. But we must understand who are the enemies for whom we should pray, and against whom we should pray. Human enemies, whatever they may be, are not to be held in hatred; lest, when an evil man hates one whom he suffers as evil, there be two evil men. Let the good man love even the one whom he suffers as evil, that there be but one evil man. Those are the enemies against whom we must pray: the devil and his angels: they envy us the kingdom of heaven, they do not wish that we should ascend whence they were cast down: from these let us pray that our soul be rescued. For when men too are stirred up against us, they become their vessels. Accordingly the apostle Paul, warning us how cautious we ought to be against our enemies, says to the servants of God who were suffering tribulations—surely from the seditions, wickednesses, enmities of men: *Your struggle is not against flesh and blood*, that is, not against men, *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of the world*. Of what world? of heaven and earth? God forbid. The ruler of this world is none but the Creator. But what world does he mean? The lovers of the world. In short, he adds and explains: What I call "world," *of this darkness*[^1]. What darkness, surely, except of the unbelieving and ungodly? For when out of the ungodly and unbelieving they had been made godly and faithful, the same apostle addresses them thus: *For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^2]. Against the spiritual hosts, he says, of wickedness in the heavenly places, against the devil and his angels you do battle: your foes you do not see, and you conquer. *Rescue me from the hands of my enemies, and from those who persecute me*.

**3.** [v. 17.] *Make your face shine upon your servant, save me in your mercy*. We were saying above, if your Charity remembers yesterday's discourse, whoever of you were present, that those especially persecute the Church who, being Christians, are unwilling to live well. For through these the Church has reproach, and from these it endures enmities: when they are rebuked, when they are not allowed to live badly, when one deals with them even by a word, they themselves brood evil things in their hearts, and seek occasion to break out. Among these is this man, groaning, and, if we wish, we are he; since they are the more numerous, and amid their multitude the good scarcely appear, like grains on the threshing floor, with which, when cleansed, the granaries of the Lord are to be filled. Therefore, groaning among these, this man says: *Make your face shine upon your servant*. For there is thought to be a certain confusion, when all are called Christians, both those who live well and those who live badly, all are signed with one mark, all approach one altar, all are washed in the same baptism, all utter the same Lord's Prayer, all are present at the celebration of the same mysteries: when are those distinguished who groan, and for whom one groans, unless he makes his face shine upon his servant? What then is, *Make your face shine upon your servant*? Let it appear that I belong to you, and let not the ungodly Christian likewise say that he belongs to you, lest in vain I have said to you in another psalm: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^3]. What he said there, *Distinguish my cause*, this he says here, *Make your face shine upon your servant*. And yet, lest he too be proud, and seem as it were to justify himself, he adds, and says: *Save me in your mercy*: that is, not in my righteousness, not in my merits, but *in your mercy*; not because I am worthy, but because you are merciful. Do not hear me according to judicial severity, but according to your most merciful goodness. *Save me in your mercy*.

**4.** [v. 18.] *O Lord, let me not be confounded, for I have called upon you*: he stated a great cause: *Let me not be confounded, for I have called upon you*. Do you wish that one who has called upon you be confounded? Do you wish it to be said: Where is the one in whom he presumed? But who even of the ungodly themselves does not call upon God? Unless, then, he said *I have called upon you* in a certain proper way, which could not be common to many, he would in no way dare to demand so great a reward for this calling. For God would answer him in a manner, in thought, and would say: What do you ask of me, that you be not confounded? Why? Because you have called upon me? Do not men daily, perhaps to accomplish the adulteries they covet, call upon me? do not men daily, that those from whom they expect an inheritance may die, call upon me? do not men daily who plot a fraud, that they may bring it to a prosperous end, call upon me? What, then, is it that you demand for a great reward, so as to say, *Let me not be confounded, for I have called upon you*? They indeed call, but they do not call upon you. You call upon God, when you call God into yourself. For this is to call upon him, to call him into yourself, in a manner to invite him into the house of your heart. But you would not dare to invite so great a householder, unless you knew how to prepare a dwelling for him. For if God says to you: Behold, you have called upon me, I come to you, where shall I enter? Shall I bear the great filth of your conscience? If you were to invite my servant into your house, would you not first take care to clean it? You call upon me into your heart, and it is full of robberies. Where God is called upon it is full of blasphemies, full of adulteries, full of frauds, full of evil desires, and you call upon me! Of such, in short, what does the psalm say in another place? *They did not call upon the Lord*[^4]. And surely they did call, and yet they did not call. Briefly I say, since a question has arisen—that a man should demand so great a reward, alleging one merit, by saying, *because I have called upon you*; when we see God called upon by so many evil men, a question has arisen, which must not be passed over. I say therefore briefly to the avaricious man: Do you call upon God? Why do you call upon God? That he may give me gain. You call upon gain, then, not God. Because this gain which you covet you cannot have through your servant, you cannot have through your tenant, through your client, through your friend, through your attendant; you call upon God, you make God the minister of your gain: God has become cheap to you. Do you wish to call upon God? Call upon him freely. O avaricious man, is it too little for you, if God himself fills you? If God should come to you without gold and silver, do you not want him? Why, then, does anything God made suffice you, to whom God himself does not suffice? Rightly, then, this man asks: *Let me not be confounded, for I have called upon you*. Call upon the Lord, brothers, if you do not wish to be confounded. For this man fears a certain confusion, of which he spoke in the earlier parts of the psalm: *In you, O Lord, I have hoped; let me not be confounded forever*. For that you may know that he fears this confusion, what did he add, when he had said, *Let me not be confounded forever, for I have called upon you?* *Let the ungodly be ashamed, and be brought down to hell*: with that confusion, surely, which is forever.

**5.** [v. 19.] *Let the deceitful lips be made dumb, which speak iniquity against the righteous one, in pride and contempt*. This righteous one is Christ: many lips speak iniquity against him in pride and contempt. Why in pride and contempt? Because he appeared contemptible to the proud, who came so humble. Do you not wish him to be despised by those who love honors, who received such great insults? Do you not wish him to be despised by those who hold this life a great thing, who died? Do you not wish him to be despised by those who think the death of the cross a base thing as of condemnation, who was crucified? Do you not wish him to be despised by the rich, who led a poor life in the world, though he was the creator of the world? All these things that men love, because Christ was unwilling to have them—that by not having them he might show that they are to be despised, not because he had no power to possess them—all who love these despise him. And whoever of his servants wishes to follow his footsteps, so as to walk himself in that humility in which he learned his Lord to have walked, is despised in Christ as a member of Christ: and when head and members are despised, the whole Christ himself is despised, because the whole righteous one is head and body. And it is necessary that the whole Christ himself be despised by the proud and the ungodly, that there may be fulfilled in them what is said: *Let the deceitful lips be made dumb, which speak iniquity against the righteous one, in pride and contempt*. When will those lips be made dumb? In this world? Never. Daily they cry out against the Christians, especially the humble; daily they blaspheme, daily they bark: they increase by their tongues the punishments by which they will thirst among the dead, and long for a drop of water in vain. Not now, then, are the lips of these made dumb. But when? When their iniquities will convict them to their face, as is said in the book of Wisdom: *Then the righteous will stand with great constancy against those who afflicted them. Then they will say: These are they whom we once held in derision and as a byword of reproach. How are they counted among the sons of God, and their lot is among the saints? We, senseless, esteemed their life madness*[^5]. Then will be made dumb the lips of those who speak iniquity against the righteous one, in pride and contempt. For now they say to us: Where is your God? What do you worship? What do you see? You believe, and you toil: it is certain that you toil, uncertain what you hope for. When what we hope for has come as certain, the deceitful lips will be made dumb.

**6.** [v. 20.] Accordingly see what follows—that the deceitful lips will be made dumb, which speak iniquity against the righteous one, in pride and contempt. This man who so groans attended, he saw the good things of God within in the spirit, he saw these good things which are seen in secret, but are not seen by the ungodly. He saw that they speak iniquity against the righteous one in pride and contempt for this reason: because they know how to see the good things of this world, but the good things of the world to come they do not even know how to think of. But to commend those very good things of the world to come to men—whom he bids to endure, not to love, present things—he cried out and added: *How great is the multitude of your sweetness, O Lord!* If this ungodly man says, Where is this multitude of sweetness? I will answer: How shall I show you the multitude of this sweetness, you who have lost your palate from the fever of iniquity? If you did not know honey, you would not cry out how well it tastes, unless you had tasted. You have no palate of the heart to taste these good things; what shall I do for you? How shall I show you? There is no one to whom to say: *Taste, and see that the Lord is sweet*[^6]. *How great is the multitude of your sweetness, O Lord, which you have hidden for those who fear you!* What is, you have hidden it for them? You have kept it for them, you have not denied it, so that they alone may come to it (for it is a good thing which cannot be common to the righteous and the ungodly); that by fearing they may come to it. For as long as they still fear, they too have not yet come: but they believe they will come, and they begin from fear. For nothing is sweeter than the immortality of wisdom, but the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord. *Which you have hidden for those who fear you*.

**7.** *But you have perfected it for those who hope in you, in the sight of the sons of men*. Not: You have perfected it in the sight of the sons of men; but, *for those who hope in you in the sight of the sons of men*: that is, you have perfected your sweetness for those who hope in you in the sight of the sons of men. As the Lord says: *He who denies me before men, I too will deny him before my Father*[^7]. Therefore, if you hope in the Lord, hope before men: lest perhaps you hide your hope in your heart, and fear to confess, when it is cast at you as a crime that you are a Christian. But to whom now is it cast as a reproach that he is a Christian? So few non-Christians have remained, that it is rather cast at them that they are not Christians, than that they themselves dare to cast at any that they are Christians. Yet I say to you, my brothers, begin, whoever hears me, to live as a Christian, and see whether it is not cast at you even by Christians—but in name, not in life, not in ways. No one feels it except one who has experienced it. Therefore mark, behold what you hear. Do you wish to live as a Christian? do you wish to follow the footsteps of your Lord? Let it be cast at you, you blush, and by blushing you let it go. You have lost the way. You seemed to yourself to have believed with the heart unto righteousness, but you have lost it; with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. If, then, you wish to walk the way of the Lord, even in the sight of men hope in God, that is, do not blush at your hope. As it lives in your heart, so let it dwell in your mouth: for not without cause did Christ will his sign to be fixed on our forehead, as on the seat of shame, lest a Christian blush at the reproaches of Christ. If, then, you do this in the sight of men, if therein you do not blush before men, if in the sight of the sons of men you deny Christ neither by mouth nor by deeds; hope that the sweetness of God will be perfected for you.

**8.** [v. 21.] What follows? *You will hide them in the hiddenness of your countenance*. What kind of place is this? He did not say: You will hide them in your heaven: he did not say: You will hide them in paradise: he did not say: You will hide them in Abraham's bosom. For to many of the faithful the future places of the saints are set down in the holy Scriptures. Let everything that is besides God become cheap. He who guards us in the place of this life, let him be himself our place after this life: because this psalm too says this to him above: *Be to me a protecting God, and a house of refuge*. We shall be, then, hidden in the countenance of God. What bosom there is in the face of God, you wait to hear from me? Cleanse the heart, that he himself may give light, and that the one you call upon may enter. Be his house, and he will be your house: let him dwell in you, and you will dwell in him. If in this world you receive him in your heart, he after this world will receive you in his countenance. *You will hide them*, he says. Where? *In the hiddenness of your countenance. From the troubling of men*: for there they are not troubled, when they are hidden; in the hiddenness of your countenance they are not troubled. Do you think there is anyone so happy in this world that, when he begins to hear the reproaches of men, because he serves Christ, he flees to God in his heart, and begins to have hope in his sweetness, and from the troubling of men, from whom he hears reproaches, enters into the countenance of God with his conscience? He enters, plainly, but only if he has that with which to enter, that is, if his conscience is not burdensome, if it does not make for him a great pack, at the narrow door. *You will hide them*, then, *in the hiddenness of your countenance, from the troubling of men. You will protect them in your tabernacle, from the contradiction of tongues*. One day you will hide them in the hiddenness of your countenance, from the troubling of men; so that henceforth no human troubling can be in them at all: but meanwhile, while they are on pilgrimage in this world, because those who serve you suffer many contradicting tongues, what do you do for them? *You will protect them in your tabernacle*. What is the tabernacle? The Church of this time; it is called a tabernacle for this reason, because it is still on pilgrimage on this earth. For a tabernacle is the dwelling of soldiers set on campaign. These are called tabernacles. A house is not a tabernacle. Fight on campaign as a pilgrim; so that, made safe in the tabernacle, you may be received gloriously into the house. For there will be in heaven your eternal house, if only you live well in this tabernacle. Therefore in this tabernacle you will protect them from the contradiction of tongues. Many tongues contradict; diverse heresies, diverse schisms resound; many tongues contradict the true doctrine: you, run to the tabernacle of God, hold the Catholic Church, do not depart from the rule of truth, and you will be protected in the tabernacle from the contradiction of tongues.

**9.** [v. 22.] *Blessed be the Lord, for he has wondrously shown his mercy in the city round about*. What is the fortified city? In the one land of Judea the people of God was set, as it were in the middle of the world, where the praises of God were spoken, and sacrifices offered to him, where prophecy did not cease singing of things to come, which we now see fulfilled: this people was as it were in the midst of the nations. This prophet attended, and saw that the Church of God was to come in all the nations: and because all the nations were round about on every side, which set in their midst the one nation of the Jews; he called these nations standing round on every side a "city round about" (*civitas circumstantiae*). You have indeed, O Lord, wondrously shown your mercy in the city of Jerusalem; there Christ suffered, there he rose again, there he ascended into heaven, there he did many wonders: but greater is your praise, because you have wondrously shown your mercy in the city round about, that is, you have poured out your mercy upon all the nations. Nor did you keep your ointment in that Jerusalem, as in a vessel; but as though the vessel were broken, the ointment was poured out through the world, that there might be fulfilled what is said in the holy Scriptures: *Your name is ointment poured out*[^8]. And thus you have wondrously shown your mercy in the city round about. For he ascended into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father, after ten days he sent the Holy Spirit: the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, they began to preach the great works of Christ; they were stoned, killed, put to flight. And when from there, as from one place, they were put to flight, like brands burning with divine fire, they filled the whole forest of the world, kindled with the fervor of the Spirit and the light of the truth: and the Lord wondrously showed his mercy in the city round about.

**10.** [v. 23.] *I said in my ecstasy*: recall the title of the psalm; behold, that is the ecstasy. See what he says: *I said*, he says, *in my ecstasy: I am cast away from the sight of your eyes*. In my dread I said, that is, *I said in my ecstasy*. He saw himself afraid within, by some great tribulation, such as are not lacking: he attended to his heart, fearful and trembling, and said: *I am cast away from the sight of your eyes*. If I were in your face, I would not so fear; if you attended to me, I would not so tremble. But since he says in another psalm: *If I said, My foot is moved, your mercy, O Lord, was helping me*[^9]; at once here too he says: *Therefore you have heard the voice of my prayer*. Because I confessed, because I said, *I am cast away from the sight of your eyes*; because I did not stand proud, but accused my heart, and in my tribulation, wavering, cried out to you: you heard my prayer. There is fulfilled, then, what I commended from that psalm. For what is, *I said in my ecstasy: I am cast away from the sight of your eyes*, this is in that psalm, *If I said: My foot is moved*. And what is in that one, *Your mercy, O Lord, was helping me*, this is in this one, *Therefore you have heard, O Lord, the voice of my prayer*. Attend to that in Peter: he sees the Lord walking on the waters, he thinks it a ghost. The Lord cries out: It is I, do not fear. Peter trusts, and says: If it is you, bid me come to you upon the waters: by this I prove whether it is you, if in your word I can do what you can. He says: Come. And the word of the one commanding became the power of the one hearing. Come, he says. And he went down: he began to walk, he was walking unafraid, as though hoping in him: but seeing a strong wind, he feared. *I said in my ecstasy: I am cast away from the sight of your eyes*. And when he began to sink, he cried out, Lord, I perish. And Jesus, stretching out his hand to him, lifted him up, saying: O you of little faith, why did you doubt?[^10] I said, then, in my dread: *I am cast away from the sight of your eyes*: and, as of one already beginning to perish in the sea, *you heard, O Lord, the voice of my prayer. And you heard, when I cried to you*. A cry to God is not with the voice, but with the heart. Many, silent with their lips, have cried with the heart: many, noisy with the mouth, with the heart turned away, could obtain nothing. If, then, you cry, cry within, where God hears. *When I cried*, he says, *to you, you heard the voice of my prayer*.

**11.** [v. 24.] Now, then, experienced, to what does he exhort us? *Love the Lord, all you his holy ones*. As though he said: Believe me, I am experienced; I had tribulations; I called upon him, and was not deceived; I hoped in God, and was not confounded: he enlightened my thoughts, he strengthened my trembling. *Love the Lord, all you his holy ones*: that is, you love the Lord, who do not love the world, that is, *all you his holy ones*. For to whom do I say, Love the Lord, who still loves the amphitheater? To whom do I say, Love the Lord, who still loves the mime, who still loves the pantomime, who still loves wine-bibbing, who still loves the pomps of the world, and all vanities, and lying follies? To whom do I say: Learn not to love, that you may learn to love; turn away, that you may be turned; pour out, that you may be filled. *Love the Lord, all you his holy ones*.

**12.** *For the Lord will require truth*. You know that now many evildoers seem to flourish; you know that now they are lifted up in their vanities: the Lord will require truth. *And he will repay those who abundantly work pride*. Bear, until you are carried up; endure, until you are free: for it is necessary that the Lord, requiring truth, repay those who abundantly work pride. Now you are going to say: When will he repay? When he wills. That he will repay, be sure; of the repayment do not doubt, of the time do not dare to give God counsel. Plainly he will require truth, and will repay those who abundantly work pride. To some he will repay even here, and we have both seen and learned that he repays. For indeed, when those who fear God are humbled—if perhaps they had shone in some dignity of this world—being humbled they did not fall, because they did not exclude God from their heart: their height is God. Job seemed humbled, his substance lost, his children lost, the things he kept lost, those for whom he kept them lost; he was left without inheritance, and, what is sadder, without an heir; he was left with only a wife, not his own consoler, but rather the devil's helper: he seemed humbled; see whether he was made wretched, see whether he was not in the hiddenness of the countenance of God. *Naked*, he says, *I came forth from my mother's womb, naked I shall return into the earth: the Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^11]. These jewels of the praise of God, whence are they? See without a poor man, within a rich man. Would these jewels of the praise of God come forth from his mouth, unless he had a treasure in his heart? You who wish to be rich, covet such riches as you cannot lose even in shipwreck. Therefore, when such men are humbled, do not think them wretched. You err, you do not know what they have within. You conjecture from yourselves, you who love the world, because, when you lose such things, you are left wretched. By no means think this; they have within wherein to rejoice. Their ruler is within, their shepherd and consoler is within. Those are the ones who fall badly, who put their hope in this world. What shone without is taken away, nothing remains within but the smoke of an evil conscience. They have no source of consolation, they have nowhere to go out, nowhere to return within, deserted by worldly pomp, empty of spiritual grace, truly they are humbled. And God does these things to many in this time, but not to all. For if he did them to no one, the divine providence would seem not to keep watch: if he did them to all, the divine patience would not be preserved. You, however, O Christian, have learned to endure, not to repay vengeance. Do you wish to be avenged, O Christian? Christ is not yet avenged. Or have you suffered a wicked man, and he has not suffered one? Did he not suffer first for you, who had nothing for which to suffer? For in you tribulation is the furnace of the goldsmith (if indeed you are gold, and not chaff), that you may be rid of your dross, not turned into ashes.

**13.** [v. 25.] *Love the Lord, all you his holy ones: for the Lord will require truth, and he will repay those who abundantly work pride*. But when will he repay? O, if only he would repay now! now I wished to see them humbled and laid low. Hear what follows: *Be strong*. Do not let your hands hang slack in tribulations, let not your knees waver. *Be strong, and let your heart take courage*: to suffer through and endure all the evils of this world, let your heart take courage. But who are those to whom the Prophet says this, *Be strong, and let your heart take courage*? Is it to those who love the world? No. But to whom he says it, hear: *All you who hope in the Lord*.
