# Exposition of Psalm 131

**1**. In this psalm there is commended to us the humility of the servant of God, the faithful one, in whose voice it is sung—which is the whole body of Christ. For we have often admonished your charity that it should not be taken as the voice of one man singing, but of all who are in the body of Christ. And because all are in his body, he speaks as one man: and he is one who is also many. For they are many in themselves, they are one in him who is one. But this is also the temple of God, of which the apostle says: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^1]; all who believe in Christ, and so believe as to love. For this is to believe in Christ, to love Christ: not as the demons believed[^2], but did not love; and therefore, although they believed, they said: *What have we to do with you, Son of God?*[^3] But let us so believe as to believe in him, loving him, and let us not say: *What have we to do with you?* but rather let us say: We belong to you; you have redeemed us. So all who so believe are like living stones, of which the temple of God is built[^4]; and like the imperishable wood of which that ark was framed, which could not be submerged in the flood[^5]. But this is the temple, that is, the men themselves, where God is asked and hears. For whoever prays to God outside the temple of God is not heard unto that peace of the heavenly Jerusalem, even if he is heard unto certain temporal things, which God has granted even to the pagans. For the demons too were heard, that they might go into the swine[^6]. To be heard unto eternal life is another thing, and is granted only to him who prays in the temple of God. But he prays in the temple of God who prays in the peace of the Church, in the unity of the body of Christ; which body of Christ consists of many believers throughout the whole world: and therefore he is heard who prays in the temple. For he prays in spirit and in truth who prays in the peace of the Church; not in that temple where there was a figure[^7].

**2.** For according to the figure the Lord cast out of the temple those men who were seeking their own things, that is, who went into the temple for selling and buying[^8]. But if that temple was a figure, it is plain that the body of Christ too, which is the true temple of which that was the image, has mingled in it buyers and sellers, that is, those seeking their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ[^9]. But they are cast out from there by the scourge of cords. For the cord signifies sins, as it is said through the prophet: *Woe to those who drag sins as a long rope*[^10]. But those drag sins like a long rope who add sins to sins; who, when they have committed one sin, in order to cover it, commit another. For just as, that a rope may be made, fiber is added to fiber, and it does not go straight, but is twisted: so all crooked deeds, which are sins, when they are added to one another, and sin upon sin, and sin is knotted to sin, a long rope is made. Of these the paths are crooked, and their steps winding[^11]. But what is that rope good for, except that by it the hands and feet of each one may be bound, and he be cast into the outer darkness? For you remember that it was said of a certain sinner in the Gospel: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^12]. There would be nothing with which his hands and feet might be bound, unless he himself had made the rope for himself. Whence most plainly it is written in another place: *Each one is bound by the cords of his own sins*[^13]. So because men are flogged by their own sins, therefore the Lord made a scourge of cords, and with it drove out of the temple all who were seeking their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ[^14].

**3.** So it is the voice of this temple in the Psalm. In this temple, as I said, God is asked, and hears in spirit and in truth; not in that bodily temple. For there was a shadow there, in which might be shown what was to come: therefore that has now fallen. Has, then, the house of our prayer fallen? Far from it. For that temple which fell could not be called this house of prayer, of which it was said: *My house will be called a house of prayer for all nations*[^15]. For you heard what the Lord Jesus Christ said: It is written: *My house will be called a house of prayer for all nations; but you have made it a den of robbers*[^16]. Did those who wished to make the house of God a den of robbers bring it about that the temple should collapse? So too those who live badly in the Catholic Church, as far as in them lies, wish to make the house of God a den of robbers; nor do they thereby overthrow the temple. For the time will come when, by the rope of their own sins, they are cast outside. But this temple of God, this body of Christ, this congregation of the faithful has one voice, and as one man sings in the Psalm. We have already heard his voice in many psalms; let us hear it in this one too. If we wish, it is our voice; if we wish, with the ear we hear him singing, and we sing with the heart. But if we do not wish, we will be in that temple like buyers and sellers, that is, seeking our own things: we enter the Church, not for the things that please the eyes of God. Let each one in your number see how he hears, whether he hears and mocks, whether he hears and casts it behind him, whether he hears and is in harmony, that is, perceives here his own voice, and joins the voice of his heart to the voice of this psalm. Yet the voice of this psalm is not silent; let those be instructed who can, rather who wish; let those who do not wish not hinder. Let humility be commended to us; from there it began.

**4**. [v. 1.] *O Lord, my heart is not exalted*[^17]. He offered a sacrifice. Whence do we prove that he offered a sacrifice? Because humility of heart is a sacrifice. It is said in another psalm: *For if you had wished a sacrifice, I would surely have given it*[^18]. He wished to make satisfaction enough to God for his sins, he wished to propitiate him, that he might receive pardon for his sins: and, as though seeking how to propitiate him: *If you had wished*, he says, *a sacrifice, I would surely have given it*; *in holocausts you will not delight.* So in vain he was seeking either rams, or bulls, or some such victim, with which God might be appeased. What then? because God does not delight in holocausts, does he not receive a sacrifice, and is appeased without sacrifice? If there is no sacrifice, there is no priest. But if we have a priest in heaven, who intercedes for us with the Father (for he entered into the holy of holies, within the veil, where the priest in the figure did not enter except once a year, as the Lord too in all time was offered once. He offered himself, he the priest, he the victim, and entered into the holy of holies once, and now he dies no more, nor will death have dominion over him any longer), we are secure, because we have a Priest; there let us offer a victim[^19]. Let us see what sacrifice we ought to offer; because our God does not delight in holocausts, as you heard in the psalm. But there it follows and shows what he offers: *A sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit: a contrite and humbled heart God does not spurn*[^20]. If, then, a humbled heart is a sacrifice to God, he offered a sacrifice who said: *O Lord, my heart is not exalted.* See in another place one offering thus: he says to God: *See my humility and my labor, and forgive all my sins*[^21].

**5.** *O Lord, my heart is not exalted, nor are my eyes lifted up on high; nor have I walked in great things, nor in wonders above me*[^22]. Let this be said more plainly, and be heard. I was not proud, I did not wish to become known to men as it were in wonders; nor did I seek anything above my strength, whereby I might boast among the unskilled. Let your charity attend; a great thing is commended. As that Simon the magician wished to walk in wonders above himself, therefore the power of the apostles delighted him more than the justice of Christians. But when he saw that through the laying on of the apostles' hands and through their prayers God gave the Holy Spirit to the faithful; and because then by a miracle the coming of the Holy Spirit was shown, so that all upon whom the Holy Spirit came spoke with tongues which they had not learned (nor is the Holy Spirit not now given because those who believe do not speak with tongues. For then it was necessary that they should speak with tongues, to signify that all tongues would believe in Christ. When what was signified was fulfilled, the miracle was taken away): when, then, Simon saw this, he wished to do such things, not to be such; and you know that he even thought the Holy Spirit could be bought with money. So he was of those who enter the temple to buy and sell; he wished to buy what he was arranging to sell: and truly, my brothers, because he was such, he had so entered to them. The Lord cast out of the temple those who were selling doves; but the dove signifies the Holy Spirit: so Simon wished to buy the dove, and to sell the dove; the Lord Jesus Christ came, who dwelt in Peter, and with the scourge of cords cast out the evil merchant[^23].

**6.** So there are men whom it delights to work miracles, and they demand a miracle from those who have made progress in the Church; and they themselves who seem to themselves to have made progress wish to do such things, and think they do not belong to God if they do not do them. But our Lord God, who knows what to grant to whom, and that the framework of the body may be kept in peace, addresses the Church through the apostle: *The eye cannot say to the hand: I have no need of you; or again the head to the feet: I have no need of you. If the whole body were an eye, where would be the hearing? if the whole body were hearing, where would be the smelling?*[^24] So in our members, brothers, you see how the individual members have their own office. The eye sees, and does not hear; the ear hears, and does not see; the hand works, and neither hears nor sees; the foot walks, and neither hears nor sees, nor does what the hand does. But in one body, if there is health, and the members do not quarrel among themselves, the ear sees in the eye, the eye hears in the ear; nor can it be objected to the ear that it does not see, so that it be said to it: You are nothing, you are lesser; can you see and distinguish colors, which the eye does? For the ear answers from the peace of the body, and says: I am where the eye is, I am in that body; in myself I do not see, in him with whom I am I see. So when the ear says: The eye sees for me; the eye says: The ear hears for me; the eyes and ears say: The hands work for us; the hands say: The eyes and ears see and hear for us: the eyes and ears and hands say: The feet walk for us: when all in one body work, if there is health there and the members are in harmony, they rejoice and rejoice together with one another. And if there is some trouble in any member, they do not desert one another, but suffer together with one another[^25]. Is it the case that, because in the body the foot seems as it were far from the eyes (for they are placed on high, while it is placed below), when perhaps the foot has trodden on a thorn, the eyes desert it; and is it not the case, as we see, that the whole body is contracted, and the man sits down, the spine of the back is curved, that the thorn which has stuck in the sole may be sought? All the members do what they can, that from the lowest and tiny place the thorn which had stuck may be drawn out. So then, brothers, whoever in the body of Christ cannot raise a dead man, let him not seek that, but let him seek not to be in discord in the body; just as, if the ear should seek to see, it can be in discord. For what it has not received, it cannot do. But if it should be objected and said to him: If you were just, you would raise a dead man, as Peter raised one. For in Christ the apostles seem to have done greater things than the Lord himself[^26]. But whence can it be that the branches are worth more than the root? But how do those seem to have done as it were greater things than he? At the voice of the Lord the dead rose, at the shadow of the passing Peter a dead man rose[^27]. This seems greater than that. But Christ could do it without Peter, Peter could not except in Christ: *For without me*, he says, *you can do nothing*[^28]. So when a man who makes progress hears this, as it were a slander objected by ignorant pagans, by men not knowing what they say; let him answer in the framework of the body of Christ and say: You who say: You are not just, because you do not work miracles; you could also say to the ear: You are not in the body, because you do not see. You too, he says, would do as Peter also did. But Peter did it also for me, because I am in that body in which Peter did it: in him I can do what he can do, from whom I am not divided; what I can do less, he suffers with me, and what he can do more, I rejoice together with him. The Lord himself cried out from above for his body: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^29] and no one was touching him, but for his body laboring on earth the head cried out from heaven.

**7.** If, then, brothers, each one acts justly in what he can, and does not envy in that which another can do more, but rejoices with him as one set with him in one body, that voice of the Psalm belongs to him: *O Lord, my heart is not exalted, nor are my eyes lifted up on high; nor have I walked in great things, nor in wonders above me.* For what exceeded my strength, he says, I did not seek, I did not stretch myself out there, I did not wish to be magnified there. For how to be feared is this exaltation from an abundance of graces, lest anyone grow proud over the gift of God, but rather keep humility, and do what is written: *The greater you are, the more humble yourself in all things, and before God you will find grace*[^30]: how to be feared, then, is pride over the gift of God, must again and again be commended to your charity, especially because the very brief psalm permits us to speak. The apostle Paul, although from a persecutor he became a preacher, obtained a more abundant grace in all apostolic labor than the other apostles, that God might the more show that what he gives is his, not man's. As physicians are accustomed to show the power of their art in desperate cases: so the Lord Jesus Christ, our physician and savior, in a desperate case, in one who had been a persecutor of the Church, showed the greatness of his art, so that he not only made him a Christian, but also an apostle; nor only an apostle, but, as he himself says, made him labor more than all those others[^31]. So he was of most excellent grace. And you see, brothers, that now in the Church the Letters of the apostle Paul flourish more than those of his fellow apostles. For the others did not write, but only spoke in the Church: for the things that are brought forward by those who err under their name, because they are not theirs, are disapproved, nor are they accepted by the Church. But the others who did write, neither wrote so much, nor with such great grace. Since, then, he was of great grace, and had deserved great gifts from God, what does he say in a certain place? *On account of the greatness of the revelations, lest I be exalted*. Attend, I tell you a fearful thing: *On account of the greatness*, he says, *of the revelations, lest I be exalted, there was given to me a thorn of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me*[^32]. What is this, brothers? Lest he be exalted as a young man, he was buffeted as a child. And by whom? By an angel of Satan. What is this? He is handed over, vehemently driven by a certain bodily pain: but bodily pains are very often sent by the angels of Satan; but they cannot do this unless permitted. For holy Job too was thus tested. Satan was permitted to test him, and struck him with a wound, so that he rotted with worms[^33]. For the unclean one was permitted, but the holy one was being tested. The devil does not know how many good things come about from him, even when he rages. Raging, he entered the heart of Judas; raging, he handed over Christ; raging, he crucified him; and, Christ being crucified, the whole world was redeemed. Behold, the savagery of the devil harmed the devil, but profited us. For by raging, he lost those whom he held, redeemed by the blood of the Lord, which, while he raged, he poured out. If he knew that he would suffer such great loss, he would not pour out on the earth the price by which the human race was redeemed. So then that angel of Satan was as it were willingly permitted to buffet the apostle; but yet the apostle was being cured. And because that which the physician had applied was troublesome to the sick man, he asked the physician to take it away. As when the physician applies to the inward parts some strong, troublesome, and burning poultice, by which yet he is to be cured whose inward parts were swollen; when the man has begun to burn and be tormented by the medicine, he asks the physician to take it away: but the physician consoles him, admonishes him to patience, because he knows how useful is what he has applied. So too the apostle goes on and says, when he had said: *There was given to me a thorn of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me.* And he said beforehand why: *Lest I be exalted by the greatness of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me. On account of which I asked the Lord three times*, he says, *that he would take it from me*[^34]. This is to say: I asked the physician to take from me the troublesome poultice which he had applied to me. But hear the voice of the physician: *And he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for strength is made perfect in weakness*[^35]. I know what I have applied, I know whence you are sick, I know whence you are healed.

**8.** If, then, dearly beloved, the apostle Paul could be exalted by the greatness of the revelations, unless he received an angel of Satan to buffet him; who can be secure about himself? He seems to walk more safely who has received less, provided he does not perversely seek what he has rightly not received: let him seek that without which he cannot be in the body of Christ, or without which it goes badly with him there. For a healthy finger is safer in the body than a bleary eye. The finger is a tiny thing; the eye is a magnificent thing, it can do much: and yet it is better to be a finger and be healthy, than to be an eye and be disturbed, bleary, blinded. So let no one seek in the body of Christ anything but health. According to health let him have faith; from faith his heart is cleansed, from the cleansing of the heart he will see that face of which it was said: *Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God*[^36]. And both he who has worked miracles, and he who has not worked miracles in the body of Christ, ought to rejoice in nothing but the face of God. The apostles returned, and said to the Lord, when they had been sent by the Lord: *Behold, Lord, in your name even the demons are subject to us*[^37]. The Lord saw that pride from the power of miracles might tempt them; and he who had come as a physician to heal our swellings and to bear our weaknesses, at once said: *Do not rejoice in this, that the demons are subject to you; but rejoice that your names are written in heaven*[^38]. Not all good Christians cast out demons; yet the names of all are written in heaven. He did not wish them to rejoice in that which they had as their own, but in that in which they held salvation along with the rest: he wished the apostles to rejoice in that in which you too rejoice. Let your charity attend. No one of the faithful has hope, if his name is not written in heaven. The names of all the faithful who love Christ, who walk his way humbly, which he himself, humble, taught, are written in heaven. Of anyone, however contemptible in the Church, who believes in Christ, and loves Christ, and loves the peace of Christ, the name is written in heaven; of anyone whom you despise. And what is there like between him and the apostles, who worked such great miracles? And yet the apostles are restrained from rejoicing in their own good, and are bidden to rejoice in that in which that contemptible man too rejoices.

**9.** [v. 2.] Not undeservedly, my brothers, with this humility he says: *O Lord, my heart is not exalted, nor are my eyes lifted up on high; nor have I walked in great things, nor in wonders above me. If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul; as one weaned from milk upon his mother, so be the recompense in my soul*[^39]. For he seems to have bound himself, as it were, with a curse. As in another place in a psalm he says: *O Lord my God, if I have done this; if there is iniquity in my hands, if I have repaid with evil those who repaid me, let me deservedly fall empty before my enemies*[^40], and the rest: so he seems to have said here too: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul.* Go on; as though he were to say: Let that befall me. As there too: *If I have repaid with evil those who repaid me*, let that befall me; what? *Let me deservedly fall empty before my enemies*: so here too: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul; as one weaned from milk upon his mother, so be the recompense in my soul.* Attend. You know that to certain weak ones the apostle says: *I gave you milk to drink, not food: for you were not yet able; but neither are you able even now*[^41]. There are weak ones who are not fit for strong food; they wish to stretch themselves to that which they cannot grasp: and if they have begun somehow to grasp something, or have seemed to themselves to grasp what they did not grasp, they are exalted by it, and grow proud by it; they seem to themselves to be, as it were, wise. But this befalls all heretics; who, being natural and carnal men, by defending their crooked opinions, which they could not see to be false, were shut out from the Catholic Church. I will tell your charity what I can. Our Lord Jesus Christ, you know, is the Word of God, according to that saying of John: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^42]. So he is bread; from him the angels live. Behold, bread is prepared for you; but grow from milk, that you may come to bread. And how, you say, do I grow from milk? What Christ became for your weakness, this first believe, and hold firmly. As, then, a mother, when she sees her son less fit to take food, gives him those very foods, but passed through her own flesh: for it is the same bread by which the infant is fed, by which his mother too is fed; but for the table the infant is less fit, for the breast he is fit. So the bread from the table is passed through the mother's breast, that thus the same nourishment may reach the little infant. So our Lord Jesus Christ, when he was the Word with the Father, through whom all things were made; who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God[^43]; such as the angels might grasp after their own measure, and on whom in heaven the Powers and Virtues, the intellectual spirits, might feed; but man, weak and wrapped in flesh, lay on the earth, nor could the heavenly bread reach him: that man might eat the bread of angels[^44], and the manna might come down to a truer people of Israel: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^45].

**10.** Wherefore to these very weak ones, whom he calls natural and carnal[^46], the apostle Paul says this: *Did I say that I knew anything among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified?*[^47] For Christ existed even not crucified: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^48]. And because that very *Word was made flesh*[^49], that very Word was crucified: but it was not changed into man; man was changed in him. Man was changed in him, that he might become better than he was, not that he might be turned into the very substance of the Word. So through that which was man, God died; and through that which was God, man was raised up, and rose, and ascended into heaven. Whatever man suffered cannot be said not to have been suffered by God, because God was God by assuming man; but he was not changed into man: just as you cannot say that you did not suffer injury, if your garment is torn. And when you complain either to friends or in court, you say this to the judge: He has torn me: you do not say: He has torn my cloak; but: He has torn me. If your garment could and deserved to be called 'you,' which is not you, but your garment; how much more did the flesh of Christ, the temple of the Word united with the Word, deserve to hear that whatever it suffered in the flesh, God himself suffered: although the Word could neither die, nor be corrupted, nor be changed, nor be killed; but whatever of these was suffered, was suffered in the flesh? And do not wonder that the Word suffered nothing: not even the soul of a man can suffer anything when the flesh is killed, the Lord himself saying: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^50]. If the soul cannot be killed, could the Word of God be killed? And yet what does he say? He scourged me, he buffeted me, he struck me, he tore me to pieces: none of this happens in the soul; and yet he says only, me, on account of the unity of that fellowship.

**11.** So our Lord Jesus Christ, the bread, made himself milk for us, becoming incarnate and appearing mortal; that in him death might be ended, and that we might not stray from the Word, believing in the flesh which the Word became. From this let us grow, let us be nourished by that very milk; before we are strong enough to grasp the Word, let us not depart from the faith of our milk. But those heretics, wishing to dispute about that which they could not grasp, said that the Son is less than the Father, and said that the Holy Spirit is less than the Son; and they made degrees, and brought into the Church three gods. For they cannot deny that the Father is God, nor can they deny that the Son is God, nor can they deny that the Holy Spirit is God. But if God the Father and God the Son and God the Holy Spirit are unequal, and are not of the same substance; there is not one God, but three gods. So, disputing about what they could not grasp, they were lifted up into pride; and there came to pass in them what is said in this psalm: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul; as one weaned from milk upon his mother, so be the recompense in my soul.* For the mother is the Church of God, from which they were separated: there they ought to have been suckled and nourished, that they might grow to grasp the Word, God with God, in the form of God equal to the Father.

## Humility excludes pride, not wisdom.

**12.** There was seen here, indeed, by those who treated these things before us, another opinion too, and another understanding in these words, which I will not pass over in silence to your charity. For they said this: Every proud man displeases God, and the human soul ought to humble itself, lest it displease God, and with its whole heart to look upon what was said: *The greater you are, the more humble yourself in all things, and before God you will find grace*[^51]. But again there are certain men who, when they have heard that they ought to be humble, let themselves go, wish to learn nothing, thinking that if they learn anything they will be proud; and they remain in milk alone. These Scripture reproves, saying: *And you have become such as need milk, not solid food*[^52]. For God wishes us to be so nourished by milk that we do not remain there; but, growing through milk, that we may come to solid food. So a man ought not to lift up his heart into pride, but ought to raise it into the doctrine of the word of God. For if the soul were not to be raised, it would not be said in another voice of a psalm: *To you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul*[^53]. And unless the soul pours itself out above itself, it does not come to the vision of God, and to the knowledge of his unchangeable substance. For now, while it is still in the flesh, it is said to it: *Where is your God?*[^54] But its God is within, and is spiritually within, and is spiritually on high; not as it were by intervals of place, as by intervals certain places are higher. For if such a height is to be sought, the birds surpass us toward God. So God is high within, and spiritually high; nor does the soul come to touch him, unless it passes beyond itself. For whatever you think of God according to the body, you greatly err. You are much an infant, if you think of God, even according to the human soul, that God either forgets, or is so wise as to become foolish, or does something and repents of it: for all these things are set down in the Scriptures, that God might be commended to us who are sucklings; not that we should hear these things of him in the proper sense, and understand as though God repents, and now learns something he did not know, and understands what he did not understand, and remembers what he had forgotten. For such things belong to the soul, not to God. Unless, then, it passes beyond even the measure of its own soul, it will not see that God is what is; who said: *I am who am*[^55]. And so that man to whom it was said: *Where is your God*, what did he say? *My tears have been my bread day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God?* But that he might find his God, what did he do? *These things I meditated*, he says, *and I poured out my soul above myself*[^56]. That he might find God, he poured out his soul above himself. So it is not said to you thus: Be humble, so that you may not be wise. Be humble, on account of pride; be high, on account of wisdom. Hear a clear sentence on this matter: *Do not be made children in your minds; but in malice be infants, that in your minds you may be perfect*[^57]. Surely it is explained, my brothers, where God wished us to be humble, where high: humble, for the avoiding of pride; high, for the grasping of wisdom. Take milk, that you may be nourished; be so nourished, that you may grow; grow so, that you may eat bread. For when you have begun to eat bread, you will be weaned, that is, you will no longer need milk, but solid food. And he seems to have said this: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul*; that is, if I was not in mind, but in malice, a little child. Signifying this, he said the foregoing words: *O Lord, my heart is not exalted, nor are my eyes lifted up on high; nor have I walked in great things, nor in wonders above me.* Behold, that in malice I was a little child. But because in understanding I was not a little child: *If I did not think humbly, but exalted my soul;* let there be recompensed to me what is recompensed to the little child who is weaned from his mother, that I may now be fit to eat bread.

**13.** And this opinion too, then, brothers, does not displease, because it is not against the faith. Yet it moves me, that it was said not only: *As one weaned from milk, so be the recompense in my soul*; but it was added: *As one weaned from milk upon his mother, so be the recompense in my soul.* Here something or other of this kind occurs to me, so that I see that it is a curse. For he is weaned not as an infant, but as one already somewhat grown: but he who is weak in earliest infancy, which is true infancy, is upon his mother; if perhaps he is weaned from the milk, he is destroyed. So not in vain was it added, *upon his mother.* For all can be weaned by growing. He who grows, and so is weaned, it is good for him; but he who is still upon his mother, it is destructive. So, brothers, we must take care and fear lest anyone be weaned before the time. For every grown child is separated from milk. But let no one be separated from milk then, when he is still upon his mother. But when he is carried in his mother's hands, who was carried in the womb (for he was carried in the womb, that he might be born; he is carried in the hands, that he might grow), he needs milk; he is still upon his mother. So let him not wish then to exalt his soul, when perhaps he is less fit to grasp food, but let him fulfill the precepts of humility. He has where to exercise himself: let him believe in Christ, that he may be able to understand Christ. He cannot see the Word, he cannot grasp the equality of the Word with the Father, the equality of the Holy Spirit with the Father and the Word he cannot yet see; let him believe this, and suck. He is secure, because when he has grown, he will eat what he could not before he grew by sucking: and he has where to stretch himself. *Do not seek things higher than you, and do not search out things stronger than you*[^58]; that is, things which you are less fit to grasp. And what shall I do, you say? shall I remain thus? *But the things that the Lord has commanded you, think on those always*[^59]. What has the Lord commanded you? Do mercy, do not abandon the peace of the Church, do not put hope in man, do not tempt God by desiring miracles. If there is already fruit in you, you recognize that with the good you endure the weeds until the harvest[^60]; that with the evil you can be for a time, not forever. The chaff is here mingled with you for this time on the threshing floor; in the barn it will not be with you. These things *which the Lord has commanded you, think on those always.* You will not be separated from milk as long as you are upon your mother; lest you die of hunger before you are fit to eat bread. Grow; your strength will be strong, and you will see what you could not, and grasp what you did not grasp.

**14.** What then? when I have seen what I could not see, and grasped what I could not grasp, will I now be secure? will I be perfect? No, as long as you live here. This is our perfection, humility. You heard with what the apostolic reading just now concluded, if it stuck in your memory; how that man who received the buffet, lest he be exalted in the revelations on account of the very greatness of the revelations, because he could be exalted unless he received an angel of Satan: and yet he to whom such great things were revealed, what does he say? *Brothers, I do not consider that I myself have laid hold*[^61]. Paul says: *Brothers, I do not consider that I myself have laid hold*; he who for this received the angel of Satan buffeting him, lest he be exalted in the greatness of his revelations[^62]. Who dares to say that he has comprehended? Behold, Paul has not laid hold, and says: *I do not consider that I have laid hold.* And what do you say, O Paul? I still run, he says, *that I may lay hold*. Is Paul still on the way, and do you think yourself in the homeland? *But one thing*, he says, *forgetting the things that are behind*. Do this too, and forget your past evil life. If vanity once delighted you, let it not delight you. *Forgetting the things that are behind*, he says, *stretched out toward the things that are before, according to my intention I follow toward the palm of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^63]. I hear the voice of God from above, and I run that I may lay hold. For he did not let me remain on the way, because he does not cease to address me. So, brothers, God does not cease to address us. For if he ceases, what is it that we are doing? what is it that the divine readings and the divine songs are doing? So forget the things that are behind, and stretch yourselves out toward the things that are before. Suck the milk in such a way that you grow toward food. When, then, you have come to the homeland, you will rejoice. And still attend to the apostle following toward the palm of the heavenly calling. For he says: *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind*. I do not speak, he says, to the imperfect, to whom I cannot yet speak wisdom, who are still given milk to drink, not fed with solid food; but I speak to those who already eat solid food. They now seem to be perfect, because they understand the equality of the Word with the Father: yet they do not yet see as it is to be seen, face to face; still in part, in a riddle[^64]. So let them run, because when the way is finished, then we return to the homeland: let them run, let them stretch themselves out. *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind; and if you are of any other mind in anything, this too God will reveal to you*[^65]. If perhaps you err in something, why do you not return to the milk of your mother? Because if you are not exalted, if you do not exalt your heart, if you do not walk in wonders above you, but keep humility, God will reveal to you that in which you are of another mind. But if this very thing in which you are of another mind you wish to defend, and obstinately to maintain, and against the peace of the Church; there comes upon you this curse which he spoke: when you are upon your mother and are separated from the milk outside, you will die of hunger outside the womb of your mother. But if you persevere in Catholic peace, if perhaps you are of another mind than you ought to be, God will reveal it to you who are humble. Why? Because God resists the proud, but to the humble he gives grace[^66].

**15**. [v. 3.] Therefore for this the psalm concluded: *Let Israel hope in the Lord, from this time now and forevermore*[^67]. For what is written in Greek, *heos tou aionos*, is rendered, *from this time now and forevermore.* But the word 'age' (saeculum) does not always signify this present age, but sometimes eternity; because 'eternal' is understood in two ways; 'unto eternity,' that is, either always without end, or until we come to eternity. So how is it to be understood here? Until we come to eternity, let us hope in the Lord God; because when we have come to eternity, there will no longer be hope, but the reality itself will be.
