# On the Same Psalm 113

**1.** [vv. 9-12.] Although perhaps for those who attend diligently there is one weaving of all the Psalms, so that none follows which cannot be joined to the preceding; yet let us consider this one as if both were one, this namely and the preceding. For since in that one it was said, "Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory: over your mercy and your truth: lest at some time the nations say: Where is their God?" because we worship an invisible God, who is known to the bodily eyes of none, but to the most pure hearts of a few: as if for this reason the nations could say: *Where is their God?* because they themselves can show their gods to the eyes; first he admonished that the presence of our God is perceived by works, because, although he is *in heaven above, in heaven and on earth all things whatsoever he willed he made.* And as if he said: Let the nations show their gods; *The images,* he says, *of the nations are silver and gold, the works of the hands of men*: that is, although we cannot show to your carnal eyes our God, whom you ought to have understood through works; yet do not be seduced by your vanities, because you can show with the finger the things you worship. For it would be much more honorable for you not to have what you can show, than that, in that which is shown by you to these eyes, the blindness of your heart be shown: for what do you show, except gold and silver? They have indeed images of bronze too, and of wood, and of clay, and of this and that other material; but the Holy Spirit preferred to commemorate the precious among them, because, when each one has blushed at that which is dearer to him, he is much more easily turned away from the veneration of the cheaper. For it was said in another place of the Scriptures, of the worshipers of images: *Saying to the wood: You are my father; and to the stone: You begot me*[^1]. But lest he seem wiser to himself who said this not to wood and stone, but to gold and silver; let him look here, let him direct here the ear of the heart: *The images of the nations are silver and gold.* Nothing abject and contemptible was named: and indeed to that mind which is not earth, both gold and silver are earth; but more beautiful and gleaming, more solid and firm. Do not, then, add the hands of men, that from that metal which the true God made, you wish to make a false god; or rather a false man, whom you may venerate for the true God; whom whoever should receive into friendship for a true man, would be mad. For the likeness of the form, and the imitated structure of the limbs, leads, and by a certain base affection snatches away the weak hearts of mortals: but as you show each thing fabricated, so show the offices of each, whose likeness, O human vanity, draws you.

**2.** [vv. 13-15.] *For* they *have a mouth, and will not speak; they have eyes, and will not see: they have ears, and will not hear; they have nostrils, and will not smell: they have hands, and will not handle; they have feet, and will not walk: they will not cry out in their throats.* Now, then, the maker is better than they, because he was able to fabricate these things with the movement and office of the limbs: which maker, however, you would certainly be ashamed to adore. You too are better, although you did not make them; since you do the things which they cannot do. The beast too is better: for to this end it was added: *They will not cry out in their throats.* For, since he had said above, *They have a mouth, and will not speak;* what need was there, after the limbs from head to feet have been run through, to repeat about the crying of the throat; unless, I believe, because the things which he commemorated of the other limbs we perceive to be common to men and beasts? For they both see, and hear, and smell, and walk, and some, like apes, handle with hands. But that which he had said of the mouth is proper to man; since beasts do not speak. But lest anyone refer the things said to the works of human limbs alone, and set only men before the gods of the nations; after all things he subjoined, saying: *They will not cry out in their throats*: which again is common to men and cattle. But if he had said this first, when he began to run through the limbs from the mouth: They have a mouth, and will not cry out; all these things too would be referred to the nature of man, nor would the perception of the hearer easily notice there anything of fellowship with beasts. But since he said that of the mouth, which is proper to man, and after the enumeration of the parts of the body which he seemed to have ended with the mention of the feet, he added: *They will not cry out in their throats;* he made the reader or hearer attentive, so that, while he asks why it was added, he may find himself admonished that to the images of the nations he ought to set before not only men, but even beasts: so that, if it is shameful to adore a beast, which God made seeing, hearing, smelling, handling, walking, crying out in its throat, they might see how shameful it was to adore a mute image, lacking life and perception; in which there is a likeness of limbs to this end, that the soul, given over to carnal perceptions, might as it were apply affection to a living and animate form, when it saw in it the things which it perceived in its own body to be living and animate. How much better, then, do mice and serpents, and the rest of that kind of living things, judge in a way, if so it must be said, of the images of the nations, in which, because they do not perceive human life, they do not care for the human figure? And so for the most part they nest in them, and unless they are frightened off by human movements, they seek for themselves no dwellings more fortified. A man, then, moves himself, that he may frighten off a living beast from his god; and worships as if powerful the one not moving himself, from whom he frightened off the better. For he frightened off a seeing thing from a blind one, a hearing thing from a deaf one, a crying thing from a mute one, a walking thing from an immovable one, a perceiving thing from an insensate one, a living thing from a dead one, or rather worse than a dead one. For a dead thing, as it is manifest that it does not live, so it is manifest that it once lived. Wherefore a god who neither lives nor lived, surely even a dead thing surpasses.

**3.** What is more manifest than this, my most beloved brothers? what more evident? What boy, asked, would not answer that it is certain that *the images of the nations have a mouth, and will not speak; have eyes, and will not see,* and the other things which the divine discourse wove together? Why, then, does the Holy Spirit care so greatly in very many places of the Scriptures to insinuate and inculcate these things as to the unknowing, as if they were not most open and most known to all; except because the likeness of limbs, which we are wont naturally to see living in living things, and to perceive in ourselves, although, as they assert, it is fabricated for some sign and set on an eminent platform, when it begins to be adored and honored by the multitude, begets in each one a most base affection of error, so that, since he does not find vital movement in that figment, he believes a hidden deity; yet, seduced by the form and moved by the authority as it were of wise institutions and obeying crowds, he does not suppose a likeness to a living body to be without some living inhabitant? Hence too such an affection of men invites the evil demons to possess the images of the nations, by whose presiding, by various deceit, deadly errors are sown and multiplied. And so in other places too the divine letters are watchful against these things, lest anyone say, when the images have been mocked: I do not worship this visible thing, but the deity which invisibly dwells there. Those very deities, then, the same Scripture in another psalm thus condemns: *For the gods of the nations,* it says, *are demons; but the Lord made the heavens*[^2]. The Apostle says too: *Not that an idol is anything, but that what the nations sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, and not to God. I do not wish you to be sharers with demons*[^3].

**4.** But they seem to themselves to be of a purer religion who say: I worship neither an image, nor a demon; but I gaze upon the bodily form as a sign of that thing which I ought to worship. And so they interpret the images, so that in one they say the earth is signified, whence they are wont to call a temple the temple of Tellus; in another the sea, as by the image of Neptune; in another the air, as of Juno; in another fire, as of Vulcan; in another the daystar, as of Venus; in another the sun, in another the moon, on whose images they impose the same names as of Tellus; in another this, in another that star, or this or that creation: for we cannot suffice to enumerate them all. Of whom again, when they begin to be harassed that they worship bodies, and chiefly earth, and sea, and air, and fire, the use of which is ready at hand for us (for of the heavenly things, since we cannot handle and touch them with our body, except by the rays of the eyes, they do not so blush); they dare to answer that they do not worship the bodies themselves, but the deities which preside over governing them. And so one sentence of the Apostle attests the punishment and condemnation of these men: *Who changed,* he says, *the truth of God into a lie, and worshiped and served the creature, rather than the Creator, who is blessed for ages*[^4]. For in the former part of this sentence, he condemned the images; but in the latter, the interpretations of the images: for by calling the forms made by a craftsman by the names of those things which God fabricated, they change the truth of God into a lie; but by holding and venerating the things themselves for gods, they serve the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed for ages.

**5.** But who adores or prays, gazing on an image, who is not so affected that he supposes himself to be heard by it, hopes that what he desires is bestowed by it? And so men bound by such superstitions for the most part set their back to the sun itself, and pour out prayers to the statue which they call the sun; and, when they are struck from behind by the sound of the sea, they strike the statue of Neptune which they worship for the sea itself, as if it perceived their groans. For this is what that figure of limbs does, and in a way extorts, that the soul living in the perceptions of the body supposes rather that the body perceives, which it sees most like to its own body, than the round sun and the diffused waves, and whatever it beholds not formed with the same lineaments with which those things are formed which it is wont to see living. Against this affection, by which human and carnal weakness can easily be captured, the Scripture of God sings things very well known, by which it may commemorate and as it were rouse the minds of men sleeping in the custom of bodies. *The images,* it says, *of the nations are silver and gold.* But God made silver and gold. *The works,* it says, *of the hands of men*: for they venerate this which they themselves made from gold and silver.

**6.** But indeed we too have very many instruments and vessels of this kind of material or metal for use in celebrating the sacraments, which, consecrated by the very ministry, are called holy, in honor of him to whom service is rendered from them for our salvation: and surely these instruments or vessels too, what else are they than the works of the hands of men? Yet do they have a mouth, and will not speak? do they have eyes, and will not see? do we make supplication to them, because through them we make supplication to God? That is most of all the cause of the insane impiety, that there avails more in the affections of the wretched a form like to a living thing, which makes supplication to be made to it, than that it is manifest not to be living, so that it ought to be scorned by the living. For images avail more to bend the unhappy soul, in that they have a mouth, have eyes, have ears, have nostrils, have hands, have feet, than to correct it, in that they will not speak, will not see, will not hear, will not smell, will not handle, will not walk.

**7.** [v. 16.] And so it follows that that too happens which follows in this psalm too; namely, that: *Let those who make them become like them, and all who trust in them.* Let these, then, see with open and perceiving eyes, and adore with closed and dead minds the images that neither see nor live.

**8.** [vv. 17-19.] *But the house* of *Israel hoped in the Lord. For hope* that *is seen is not hope: for what one sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, with patience we wait for it*[^5]. But that that very patience may last to the end: *He is their helper and their protector.* Or perhaps the spiritual (by whom the carnal are instructed in the spirit of meekness, because they, as it were superiors, make supplication for the inferiors) already see, and have now the thing which is still hope for the inferiors? It is not so: for *the house of Aaron too hoped in the Lord.* Therefore, that they too may perseveringly be extended to the things that are before, and perseveringly run, until they lay hold of that in which they were laid hold of, and know as they too were known: *He is their helper and their protector.* For both *fear the Lord, and hoped in the Lord: he is their helper and their protector.*

**9.** [vv. 20-21.] For we do not anticipate the mercy of the Lord by our merits: but: *The Lord was mindful of us, and blessed us. He blessed the house of Israel, he blessed the house of Aaron.* But blessing both: *He blessed all who fear the Lord.* Do you ask, which both? It is answered: *The little ones with the great.* That is, the house of Israel with the house of Aaron, those certainly who of that nation believed in the Savior Jesus; because "God was not well pleased with all of them*. But if some of them did not believe, will their unbelief make void the faith of God? Far from it*[^6]! *For not all who are of Israel, these are Israel; nor are all who are the seed of Abraham, sons*[^7]: but, as it is written: *The remnants were made safe*[^8]. For from the person of those who believed from there it is said: *Unless the Lord of Sabaoth had left us a seed, we would have become like Sodom, and been like Gomorrah*[^9]. But it is a seed for this reason, because, scattered through the lands, it was multiplied.

**10.** [v. 22.] For the great of the house of Aaron said: *May the Lord add upon you, upon you and upon your sons.* And so it was done. For the sons of Abraham raised up even from the stones drew near: there drew near the sheep which were not of this fold, that there might be one flock and one shepherd: there drew near the faith of all the nations, and the number grew not only of wise overseers, but even of obedient peoples; the Lord adding not only upon the fathers, who, going before in Christ, would lead the rest to imitate them, but also upon their sons, who would follow the pious footsteps of the fathers. For so he says to those whom through the Gospel he had begotten in Christ: *Be imitators of me, as I too am of Christ*[^10]. And so the Lord added, not only upon the mountains leaping like rams, but also upon the hills leaping like lambs of the flock.

**11.** [vv. 23-24.] Accordingly to both these, the great and the little, the mountains and the hills, the rams and the lambs, the Prophet says what follows: *Blessed are you by the Lord, who made heaven and earth.* As if he said: Blessed are you by the Lord, who made you heaven in the great, earth in the little: but heaven not this visible one, full of luminaries pertaining to these eyes. For *the heaven of heaven is the Lord's*; who raised and lifted up the minds of certain saints so far that they became teachable by no man, but by their own God: in comparison with which heaven, whatever is discerned by the eyes of the flesh is to be called earth; which *he gave to the sons of men,* that by its consideration—whether from that part which gives light above, as is that which is called sky, or from that which is given light below, to which properly the name of earth belongs, since the whole, as we have commemorated, is earth in comparison with that which is called the heaven of heaven; *this whole earth*, then, *he gave to the sons of men,* that by its consideration, as far as they can, they may conjecture the Creator, whom with hearts still weak, without this aid of conjecture, they cannot see.

**12.** There is another understanding too, from which I ought not to conceal, of these words, by which it was said: *The heaven of heaven is the Lord's, but the earth he gave to the sons of men*: so that the intention does not withdraw from what we said. For we had said that the great and the little are signified too by that which was added: *Blessed are you by the Lord, who made heaven and earth.* If, then, we take the great by the name of heaven, the little by the name of earth: since the little, by growing, will be heaven, and in that very hope are nourished with milk; so those great are heaven to the earth, when they nourish the little ones, so that they understand themselves to be even the heaven of heaven, while they think in what hope the little ones are nourished. But yet because they now draw the sincerity and richness of wisdom not from a man, nor through a man, but through God himself; they have received the little ones who will indeed be heaven, that they may know themselves to be the heaven of heaven; yet still an earth to which they may say: *I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth*[^11]. For to the very sons of men whom he made heaven, he gave an earth in which they may work, who knows how to provide for the earth through heaven. Let heaven and earth, then, abide in their God who made them, and live from him, confessing to him and praising him: for if they wish to live from themselves, they will die, as it is written: *From a dead man, as one who is not, confession perishes*[^12]. But: *The dead will not praise you, O Lord, nor all who go down into hell.* For your Scripture cries out in another place: *The sinner, when he comes into the depth of evils, scorns*[^13]. *But we who live, bless the Lord, from now and unto the age.*
