# Exposition of Psalm 111

**1.** [v. 1.] I believe that you attended, brothers, to the title of this psalm, and committed it to memory. *The conversion,* it says, *of Haggai and Zechariah.* These prophets were not yet, when these things were sung. For between the time of David, and the transmigration of the people of Israel into Babylon, fourteen generations are counted, as divine Scripture, and especially Matthew the evangelist, attests: but the renewal of the overthrown temple, according to the prophecy of holy Jeremiah, was hoped for from that transmigration after seventy years: which, when they were being fulfilled, under Darius king of Babylon these two prophets, Haggai and Zechariah, were filled with the Holy Spirit; and both, one after the other, within one year began to prophesy, which seems to pertain to the renewal of the temple, as was foretold so long before. But whoever fixes the eye of the heart on bodily deeds, and is not from there extended into the grace of spiritual understanding, dwells in thought upon the stones of the temple, by which the visible fabric, constructed by the hands of men, rises; nor is he himself made a living stone, fitted and apt to that temple, which the Lord first figured in his own body, when he said: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^1]. For the body of the Lord is more fully the holy Church itself, whose head ascended into heaven, who is most of all the living stone, the cornerstone, of whom blessed Peter says: *To whom coming, a living stone, rejected by men, but chosen and honored by God; you too, then, as living stones, are built up into a spiritual house, into a holy priesthood, offering spiritual victims acceptable to God through Jesus Christ: because Scripture contains, Behold, I set in Zion a cornerstone, chosen, precious, and he who believes in him will not be confounded*[^2]. Therefore, that each one may become a living stone fit for such a fabric, let him spiritually understand the renewal of the temple from the old ruin which was made in Adam, the repairing of the new people according to the new and heavenly man: that as we have borne the image of the earthly, we may bear too the image of him who is from heaven, by which we may, after all the ages of this world, as it were after seventy years, which are signified beforehand by a mystical number of perfection, and as it were after the captivity of a far-off pilgrimage, be constructed not into a mass that will fall, but be made solid in eternal immortality. For the spiritual Jerusalem, reckon to be no more the Jews' than yours. For as the Apostle says: *You are now no longer strangers and aliens; but you are fellow citizens of the saints and members of the household of God, built up upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone, in whom every building, joined together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord, in whom you too are built together into a dwelling of the Lord in the Spirit*[^3]. This is the temple of God, to which pertains the sacrament of the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah: to which again the same apostle says: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^4]. Whoever, then, converts himself to the work of this building-together, and to the hope of the holy and firm structure, as a living stone, from the ruinous downfall of this world; understands the title of the Psalm, understands the conversion of Haggai and Zechariah. Let him, then, sing what follows, not so much with the voice of the tongue, as of the life. For the perfection of the building will be that ineffable peace of wisdom, whose beginning is the fear of the Lord: thence, then, let him begin whom this conversion builds together.

**2.** [v. 1.] *For blessed is the man who fears the Lord, in his commandments he will will exceedingly.* Let God see, who alone both truly and mercifully judges, how much that man advances in his commandments: since *human life on earth is a temptation*[^5], as holy Job says. And again it is written: *For the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind thinking of many things*[^6]. But he who judges us is the Lord: nor ought we to judge before the time, until the Lord comes, and lights up the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart; and then there will be praise for each one from God. Let him, then, see how much each one advances in his commandments: yet he will will exceedingly who has loved the peace of that building-together; nor ought he now to despair, since *in his commandments he will will exceedingly,* and peace on earth to men of good will.

**3.** [vv. 2, 3.] Hence: *His seed will be powerful on the earth.* That the seed of the future harvest is the works of mercy, the Apostle is witness, who says: *But let us not fail in doing good; for in our own time we will reap*[^7]: and again: *But this,* he says, *he who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly*[^8]. But what, brothers, is more powerful than that the kingdom of heaven not only Zacchaeus buys with half of his goods, but even the widow with two mites, and each one possesses just as much there? what is more powerful than that the same kingdom avails both to the rich man with his treasures, and to the poor man with a cup of cold water? But there are those who do these things, while they seek earthly things, either hoping here for a reward from the Lord, or desiring to please men: but: *The generation of the upright will be blessed,* that is, their works, whose God is the good God of Israel, who are upright in heart; but the upright heart is, not to resist the Father correcting, and to believe him promising: not of those whose feet are moved, and their steps are poured out and slip, as is sung in another psalm, while they are zealous over sinners, seeing the peace of sinners, and suppose their works to perish, because a reward that will perish is not rendered to them. But that man who fears the Lord, and is fitted into the holy temple of God by the conversion of an upright heart, neither seeks the glory of men, nor covets earthly riches; and yet: *Glory and riches are in his house.* For his house is his heart; where, with God praising, he dwells more opulently with the hope of eternal life, than, with men flattering, in marbled and paneled roofs with the fear of eternal death. For his *righteousness abides for the age of the age*: this is his glory, these his riches. But the other man's purple and fine linen and splendid feasts, both when they are at hand, pass away; and when they have come to the end, his burning tongue will cry out, desiring a drop of water from the dripping finger.

**4.** [vv. 4-9.] *A light has arisen in the darkness for the upright in heart.* Rightly they direct their heart to their God, rightly they walk upright with their God, setting his will before themselves; and not proudly presuming anything of their own. For they remember that they were once darkness, but now light in the Lord. *Merciful and compassionate and just is the Lord God.* It delights that he is *merciful and compassionate,* but it frightens perhaps that he is *a just Lord God.* By no despair be afraid, blessed man who fear the Lord and will will exceedingly in his commandments: be sweet, have mercy and lend. For the Lord God is just in such a way that he makes judgment without mercy to him who did not do mercy: but *sweet is the man who has mercy and lends;* God does not remove him from his mouth, as if unsweet. *Forgive,* he says, *and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given to you*[^9]. In that you forgive, that it may be forgiven you, you have mercy; in that you give, that it may be given to you, you lend. For although by a general name all mercy is called by which the wretched are relieved, yet there is a difference where you expend neither the cost of money, nor the industry of bodily labor, but by forgiving what each one has sinned against you, freely obtain too pardon of your own sins. These two offices of kindness, of forgiving sins and of expending benefits, as in the Gospel which we commemorated, *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given to you;* so in this verse I think are distinguished: *Sweet is the man who has mercy and lends.* Let us not be lazy toward these things, brothers. He seeks glory who desires to be avenged; but attend to what is written: *Better is he who conquers anger, than he who takes a city*[^10]. He seeks riches who does not wish to give to the poor; attend to what is written: *You will have treasure in heaven*[^11]. You will not, then, be inglorious by forgiving; because over conquered anger one triumphs more praiseworthily: nor needy by giving; because the heavenly treasure is more surely possessed. This verse the earlier one was giving birth to: *Glory and riches are in his house.*

**5.** Whoever, then, does these things: *He will arrange his words in judgment.* The very deeds are the words by which he will be defended in judgment; which will not be for him without mercy, because he too did mercy. *Since for the age he will not be moved*: who, separated to the right, will hear: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the origin of the world*[^12]. For there none of their things are commemorated except the works of mercy. He will hear, then: *Come, blessed of my Father;* because *the generation of the upright will be blessed.* So: *In eternal memory the just will be: and from an evil hearing he will not fear;* which will be said to those who will be on the left: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^13].

**6.** And so he who here does not seek his own, but the things that are Jesus Christ's; endures labors most patiently, awaits the promises trustingly: *His heart is ready to hope in the Lord.* Nor is he broken by any temptations: *His heart is confirmed, he will not be moved, until he look upon his enemies.* His enemies here wished to see good things, and when invisible things were promised to them, they said: *Who will show us good things?*[^14] Let our heart, then, be confirmed, and let us not be moved, until we look upon our enemies. For they wish to see the good things of men dying on the earth; we believe we will see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.

**7.** But it is a great thing to have a confirmed heart and not be moved, when those rejoice who love what they see, and insult the one who hopes for what he does not see: and *he will not be moved until he look,* he too, not down to where his enemies are, but up *upon his enemies,* what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love him. How greatly is this worth which is not seen, and is bought for as much as each one has been able to have! For this that man too *scattered, gave to the poor*[^15]: he did not see, and bought; but he was keeping treasure in heaven, who deigned to hunger and thirst in the poor on earth. It is no wonder, then, if *his righteousness abides for the age of the age*: with that one as guardian who founded the ages. *His horn will be exalted in glory*: whose lowliness was scorned by the proud.

**8.** [v. 10.] *The sinner will see, and be angry*: namely that late and fruitless repentance. For with whom will he *be angry* more than with himself, when he will say: *What did pride profit us? and what did the boasting of riches confer on us?*[^16] seeing the horn of that man exalted in glory, who scattered and gave to the poor. *He will gnash with his teeth, and waste away*: because there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth. For he will not put forth leaves, and grow green, as would happen if he repented at the opportune time; but he will then repent, when *the desire of sinners will perish*: with no succeeding consolation. The desire of sinners will perish, when all things will pass away like a shadow, when, the hay withering, the flower will fall. But the Word of the Lord, which abides forever, as it was mocked by the vanity of the false blessed, so will it laugh over the perdition of those same true wretched ones.
