# Exposition of Psalm 147 (Part 2: vv. 12-20)

**1.** Your charity remembers that concerning the psalm which we have just now sung, we deferred the sermon to this day. For it was the very psalm, and it was read on the Lord's day, and was itself undertaken to be treated. But because, moved then by the Gospel reading, on account of the greatness of our fear and your benefit, we delayed in that which the Lord commended to us, the last day, and willed us, cautious and watchful, to await his coming[^1]; frightening us too by example, lest he condemn us in judgment; saying that the coming of the Son of man will be as in the days of Noah: they ate and drank, bought, sold, married, took wives, until Noah entered the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all[^2]. Anxious, then, and shaken by great fear (for who, if he believes, does not fear?), we delayed in that, as much as we could, that the sermon about your morals and your life and the life of us all might be prolonged, whereby we might be able not only to await that day securely, but even to desire it. For if we love Christ, surely we ought to desire his coming. For it is perverse, and I know not whether it is true, to fear lest the one you love come; to pray: *May your kingdom come*[^3]; and to fear lest you be heard. But whence the fear? because the judge is to come? Is he unjust? is he malevolent? is he envious? is he, finally, awaiting from another to know your case; lest perhaps the one you have instructed either deceive you by collusion, or, failing in too little eloquence and ability, be unable to show in words the good of your innocence? None of these. Who, then, is to come? why do you not rejoice? Who is to come to judge you, except he who came to be judged for your sake? Do not fear the accuser, of whom he himself said: *The prince of this world has been cast out*[^4]. Do not fear a bad advocate; for he is now your advocate, who then will be your judge. There will be he, and you, and your case; the discourse of your case, the testimony of your conscience. Whoever, then, fears the future judge, now correct your present conscience. Is it too little for you that he will not require the past? Then he will judge without any further span of time: but now with how great a span of time he commands? Then it will no longer be allowed to be corrected: now who hinders? When we commended this vehemently on the Lord's day, because truly, because this nearly alone is to be said; no little time flowed away, and we were compelled to defer this psalm, undertaken to be treated, to this day. Now it is itself at hand; let us attend to it, rather to God who has deigned through his mercy in his Spirit to dispense these sermons, in such a way as he himself knows to be expedient for our weaknesses. For what sick man dares to give counsel to the physician?

**2.** When it was read, I believe either all or many of you noticed that it has certain verses which must be knocked upon to be opened, especially where it says: *Who gives snow like wool, scatters mist like ashes: who sends his crystal like morsels of bread; before the face of his cold who will stand*[^5]? For having heard these things, whoever takes them according to the letter, thought indeed of divine works. For who gives snow, except God? and who scatters mist, except God? who hardens crystal, except he himself? And these three have fitting likenesses set against them. For wool is not unlike snow, or ashes mist, or morsels of white bread the brightness and whiteness of crystal. For crystal is a certain appearance after the manner of glass, but it is white. But this kind is handed down by those who know it; whence we ought not to doubt, since the most faithful Scripture bears witness: it is handed down, then, that crystal, snow hardened through many years and not melted, so congeals, that its melting is not easy. The snows of the past winter the coming summer easily dissolves; for there has not come to them, for the strengthening of their hardness, length of years. But where many snows through many years have been sent over one another, and by their abundance have overcome the violence of the summer—not of one summer, but of many, especially in those parts of the earth, that is, in the northern region, where not even in summer is a most fervent sun found; that long and aged hardness produces this appearance which is called crystal. Let your charity attend. What, then, is crystal? It is snow hardened by ice through many years, so that it cannot easily be dissolved by sun or fire. This we have expounded somewhat at length, because many are ignorant of it: nor ought those who perhaps know this, find the sermon about a thing they know burdensome, applied not for their sake, but for the sake of those who do not know it. These things, then, when you heard them as the Reader sounded them, I do not doubt you thought diverse things; that some said, and said truly: Great are the works of the Lord, of which a certain little portion has been mentioned, and that earthly, and known to nearly all years, how God snows, how he scatters mist, how also he solidifies crystal: that others said to themselves: Do you suppose these things were set in the Scriptures without cause, or do they truly have all that they sound? does not this snow and wool, mist and ashes, crystal and bread suggest something to us? But why did Scripture wish to speak thus, through certain as it were mists of likenesses? how much better would it speak more openly? Why do I either seek or hesitate, what those words demand? why in hearing do I either labor? why very often, the psalm being heard, do I depart unlearned? This is what I said a little before: Allow yourself to be cured; so you are to be healed. Very proud and headlong is the sick man who dares even to admonish the physician, even a man. Will a sick man dare to give counsel to a physician? Where a man is sick, and God cures, it is a great beginning of devotion and of health, before you know why anything was said, to believe that it ought to be said as it was said. For this devotion makes you capable of seeking what was said, and when you have sought it of finding it, and when you have found it of rejoicing. May this affection of your prayers be present to us with the Lord our God; and if not for our sake, certainly for your sake may he deign to grant what here lies hidden. Now, then, think of us, since we have promised a day of a certain spectacle and divine production, having pronounced these verses and not yet expounded them, as having brought forth certain wrapped-up bundles of our Producer. Surely for this they are brought forth wrapped up, that, unwrapped, they may be awaited: but you are prepared not only to gaze, but also to be clothed.

**3.** But we had said on the Lord's day, if your charity who were present remembers, that the very Gospel reading, which held us longer, so that the exposition of the Psalm was deferred, was read as fitting that same psalm. This we said then, but were not able to show, because we deferred the Psalm. Today, then, that very fittingness too must be shown. In that Gospel reading we were frightened about the last day[^6]. That terror brings forth security; for, frightened, we take precaution, and taking precaution we shall be secure. For as preposterous security drives into terrors, so ordered solicitude brings forth security. But we were frightened, lest we love the present life, failing, fleeting, transitory, as if there were no other: for if there is no other, let us love this one. If there is no other life, those who kept watch today for the amphitheater are happier than we. For what does the apostle say? *If in this life only we have hope in Christ, we are more wretched than all men*[^7]. So there is another life. Let each one question Christ in his faith: but the faith sleeps. Deservedly you waver, because Christ sleeps in the ship. For Jesus was sleeping in the ship, and the ship was tossing in the waters and in many tempests[^8]. So the heart tosses, when Christ sleeps. Christ always keeps watch: what is it, then, Christ sleeps? Your faith sleeps. Why are you still tossed by the tempest of doubt? Rouse Christ, rouse your faith: behold with the eyes of faith the future life, for the sake of which you believed, for the sake of which you are signed with his sign, who for this led this life, that he might show you how contemptible was what you loved, and how to be hoped for was what you did not believe. If, then, you have roused faith, and sent its eyes upon the last things, and the world to come in which we shall rejoice after the second coming of the Lord, after the judgment performed, after the kingdom of heaven handed over to the saints, if you have thought of that life, and the leisurely business of that life, of which we have often spoken, dearly beloved; our business will not toss, a leisurely business full of sweetness alone, interrupted by no trouble, wounded by no weariness, troubled by no cloud. What will our business be? To praise God; to love and to praise; to praise in love, to love in praises. *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^9]. Why, except because forever and ever they will love you? Why, except because forever and ever they will see you? This, then, my brothers, what a spectacle it will be in the vision of God? Men see a hunter, and are delighted: woe to the wretched, if they do not correct themselves! For those who see a hunter, and are delighted, will see the Savior, and will be saddened. What is more wretched than they, to whom the Savior will not be for salvation? It is no wonder, then, that to those to whom a man fighting is a pleasure, God freeing will not be for salvation. But we, brothers, if we remember ourselves in his members, if we covet, if we persevere, will see and will rejoice. That city will be, with all its citizens purified, with no seditious or turbulent one mingled in: that enemy who now envies, lest we come to that homeland, there can lie in wait for no one; because he is not permitted even to be there. For if now he is shut out from the heart of believers, how will he be shut out from the city of the living? What will it be, brothers, what will it be, I ask you, to be in that city, of which it is so great a joy even to speak? For this future life we ought to prepare our hearts: whoever prepares his heart for it, despises all this present life; this present life despised makes one secure to await the day about whose awaiting the Lord frightened us.

**4.** Since, then, this psalm tells us and sings of that future life, but the Gospel frightened us about the present; the Psalm makes love of the future, the Gospel makes fear of the present. Nor is the future delight passed over in silence in the letters of the New Testament, and much more in those places where the things that are to be understood are not brought forth as it were with certain wrappings; but there they seem open, that here they may be understood when obscure. When, then, the Gospel said to us: Observe the last day to come, the day of the coming of the Son of man[^10]; because he will find badly those who are now secure, for this reason because they are perversely secure; for they are secure in the pleasures of the world, when they ought to be secure with the desires of the world tamed: already surely the apostle has prepared us for that life, in the words which I then also mentioned: *For the rest, brothers, the time is short: it remains that those who have wives be as not having them; and those who buy, as not buying; and those who rejoice, as not rejoicing; and those who weep, as not weeping: and those who use this world, as not using it: for the figure of this world passes away. I wish you to be without anxiety*[^11]. He who sets all his joy and all his happiness in eating, drinking, taking a wife, buying, selling, using this world, is without anxiety, and such, but outside the ark: woe to him from the flood! But whoever, whether he eats, or drinks, or does anything, does all to the glory of God[^12]: and if there is some sadness about worldly things, so weeps that within in hope he rejoices; and if there is some gladness in worldly things, so rejoices that within spiritually he fears, neither gives himself to be corrupted by happiness, nor gives himself to be broken by adversity (for this is to weep as not weeping, and to rejoice as not rejoicing): whoever, even if he has a wife, having compassion on her weakness, renders, does not exact the debt; or if on account of his own weakness he takes a wife, lamenting rather that he could not be without a wife, than rejoicing that he took one: whoever sells what he knows that, even if it remained, would not make him blessed; whoever knows that what he buys will pass away; and of all these things, even if abounding, even if overflowing, does not presume, and from what he has does mercy with him who has not, that he too may receive what he has not, from him who has all things: whoever is such, securely awaits the last day, because he is not outside the ark; he is now counted among the imperishable wood, of which the ark is built[^13]. Let him not, then, fear the coming Lord; but hope, and desire: for he will not come to him to inflict punishments, but to end troubles. But this happens by the desire of that city. What the Gospel admonished, then, is fulfilled by the desire of that city, which the Psalm sings: so the Gospel fits this song.

**5.** [v. 12.] And what city this psalm sings, let us hear. Let us hear, and let us sing; our joy, when we hear this, is the song of our God. For we do not sing only when with voice and lips we sound a song; there is also a song within, because there are also ears of someone within. With the voice we sing, that we may rouse ourselves; with the heart we sing, that we may please him. It is called the Psalm *of Haggai and Zechariah.* Haggai and Zechariah were prophets. But these prophets were already in the captivity of that Jerusalem, which on earth bore the shadow of a certain heavenly one. In the captivity of that city, then, when they were in Babylon, these prophets prophesied the restoration of Jerusalem, prophesied a new city from the restoration of the old, the people being freed from captivity[^14]. We recognize this captivity too, if we truly recognize our pilgrimage. For in this world, in these tribulations of the age, in this manifold crowd of scandals, we groan as it were in captivity; but we shall be raised up: it is foretold that our city, new and equal, will be. For after the prophesying of these prophets, it even happened visibly, that all that pertained to the fulfilling of the image was unfolded. Jerusalem was restored after seventy years of captivity. Thus the prophet Jeremiah by the seventy years, by that sevenfold number, signifies the whole revolving of this time: for in the sevenfold number these days revolve, as you know; the same go away, the same return. After seventy years, then, when Jeremiah prophesied that the city of Jerusalem would be restored[^15], it came about, so that there too the image of things to come might be signified: it was signified to us that, after all this revolving of time, which revolves in the sevenfold number, that city of ours would be, now in eternity, in one day. For in that dwelling time does not revolve, because the dweller there does not slip away. This when the Prophets saw in spirit; they saw that one, they spoke of this one. But they spoke these things of this one, which might lead into that one; and all those things which were done according to time, according to bodily motions, according to the acts of men, were signs and foretellings of things to come.

**6.** Let us now hear that city sung, and let us be raised up to it. For the Spirit of God commends it much to us, pouring into us love of it, that we may sigh for it, and groan in our pilgrimage, and desire to come to it. Let us love it, and the very loving is to walk. Behold, let us love it from a holy mouth, from a prophetic mouth and the Spirit of God speaking: *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem*[^16]. Still set in captivity, they see those flocks, rather the one flock of all the citizens, gathered from every side to that city; they see the joy of the mass, after the threshings, after the winnowings, now sent into the barn, fearing nothing, suffering nothing of labor and trouble; and still set here, and dwelling in that very contrition, they send ahead the joy of hope, and pant toward that city, as it were joining their hearts to the angels of God, and to that future people about to abide with them in joy. *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem.* For what will you do, O Jerusalem? Surely both labor and groaning will pass. What will you do? Will you plow, sow, plant new shoots, sail, do business? What will you do? Or in those works, although good and coming from mercy, must you still be exercised? Consider your number, consider your fellowship on every side: see whether anyone hungers, to whom you may hold out bread; see whether anyone thirsts, to whom you may give a cup of cold water; see whether anyone is now a pilgrim among you, whom you may receive into your lodging; see whether anyone is sick, whom you may visit; see whether anyone quarrels, whom you may reconcile; see whether anyone dies, whom you may bury[^17]. What, then, will you do? *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem;* behold this is your business. As it is usually written in inscriptions: Use it happily: *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem.*

**7.** Be Jerusalem: remember those of whom it was said: *O Lord, in your city you will reduce their image to nothing*[^18]. These are those who now rejoice in such shows: among them are those who for this reason did not come today, because there is a public game. For whom is it a game? for whom a loss? Or whence the game? or whence the loss? For not only those who produce such things are struck with loss; but with a greater loss are those struck who gladly gaze at such things. Their chest is emptied of gold; but the breast of these is despoiled of the riches of justice. Many producers lament, selling their estates: how ought sinners to lament, losing their souls! Was it for this that the Lord cried out on the Lord's day: *Watch*, that today there should be such watching? I beg you, O citizens of Jerusalem, I adjure you by the peace of Jerusalem, by the redeemer, by the builder, by the ruler of Jerusalem, that you turn your prayers for them to God. Let them see, let them feel that they trifle; and, very intent on those spectacles which please them, let them at some time also look upon themselves, and displease themselves. For in many we now rejoice that it is done; and we too at some time sat there, and were mad: and how many do we think now sit there, who will be not only Christians, but even bishops? From past things we conjecture future ones; from those things which have already been done, we foretell what God is to do. Let your prayers keep watch; not in vain do you groan, brothers. Surely those who have escaped, praying for those who are in peril, because they too were in the number of those in peril, are heard; and God will draw his people from the captivity of Babylon, will redeem them altogether and rescue them, and the number of the saints bearing the image of God will be completed. There will not be there those whose image God will spurn in his city, and reduce to nothing; because they themselves in their own city, that is, Babylon, reduced his image to nothing. There will be that people praising the Lord, which the Spirit of prophecy now foresees; and it says that we should exult in hope, and desire the very reality. *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem; praise your God, O Zion. Praise* (collauda), because you consist of many; *praise* (lauda), because you have been made one. *Many*, says the apostle, *we are one in Christ Jesus*[^19]. Since, then, we are many, we praise together; since we are one, we praise. The same many who are one; because he in whom we are one is always one.

**8.** Why, says this Jerusalem, do I praise the Lord, and praise my God, O Zion? Which Zion, the same Jerusalem. Two names for different reasons. Jerusalem is called 'Vision of peace': Zion is called 'Watching' (Speculatio). See whether these names sound anything but spectacles; lest the nations think they have spectacles, and we do not. Sometimes, the theater or amphitheater being dismissed, when from that hollow there has begun to be vomited forth the crowd of the lost, sometimes holding in their soul the phantasms of their vanity, and feeding their memory with things not only useless, but even pernicious, rejoicing in them as in sweet things, but pestilential; they very often see, as happens, the servants of God passing by, they recognize them by the very habit, whether of dress or of head, or have them marked on the forehead, and they say among themselves and with themselves: O these wretches, what they lose! Brothers, let us ask the Lord for their goodwill, because they think that good. They wish us well: but he who loves iniquity hates his own soul[^20]. If he hates his own soul, how will he love my soul? Yet by that very perverse and empty and vain goodwill, if it is to be called goodwill, they grieve that we lose what they love; let us pray that they may not lose what we love. See what kind of Jerusalem he exhorts to praise, or rather foresees will praise. For the praises of that city, then, when we shall see and love and praise, are not to be exhorted and roused by a prophetic voice: but for this reason now the Prophets say this, that they may drink in, as much as they can, set in this flesh, the future joys of the blessed, and, pouring them forth into our ears, may rouse love of that city. Let us glow with desire, let us not be slothful in spirit.

**9.** [v. 13.] But see what kind of Jerusalem he says will praise God, and whence she will praise: from a certain perfection of blessedness. *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem; praise your God, O Zion.* And as though he said: Whence shall I praise securely? *Because he has strengthened*, he says, *the bars of your gates*[^21]. Attend, my brothers. *He has strengthened*, he says, *the bars of your gates.* The strengthening of bars is not of open gates, but of closed ones: whence many manuscripts have: *He has strengthened the bolts of your gates.* Let your charity attend. He says that Jerusalem, shut up, praises the Lord. *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem; praise your God, O Zion.* We praise together now, we praise now, but amid scandals. Many whom we do not wish enter; many, even though we do not wish, go out; therefore scandals grow frequent: *And because iniquity has abounded*, says the Truth, *the love of many grows cold*[^22]: on account of those entering, whom we cannot judge; and those going out, whom we cannot hold. Why this? Because there is not yet perfection, not yet that blessedness. Why this? Because it is still the threshing floor, not yet the barn. Then, therefore, what, except that you may not fear any such thing to come? For *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem, praise your God, O Zion; because he has strengthened the bars of your gates. He has strengthened.* He did not say: He merely set up: *He has strengthened the bars of your gates.* Let no one go out, let no one enter. Let no one go out, we rejoice: Let no one enter, we fear. Do not fear even this; when you have entered, it will be said; only be in the number of the virgins who carried oil with them[^23].

**10.** For those virgins signify souls. For there were not truly five, but in those five there are thousands. In that number five thousands are understood, not of women only, but also of men: because each sex is called woman, because the Church; and each sex, that is, the Church, is called a virgin. *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ*[^24]. Virginity in the flesh belongs to few, in the heart it ought to belong to all. Virginity of the flesh is a body untouched; virginity of the heart is faith uncorrupted. So the whole Church is called a virgin, and in the masculine gender the people of God is named: each sex is the people of God, and one people, and an only people; and one Church, and an only dove: and in this virginity there are thousands of saints. So the five virgins signify all the souls about to enter into the kingdom of God; not without cause by the number five, since the five senses of the body are well known to all. For through five gates something enters through the body to the soul: either through the eyes enters what you may evilly covet, or through the ears, or through the smell, or through the taste, or through the touch. Whoever does not admit corruption through these five gates is counted among the five virgins. But corruptions are admitted through unlawful desires. But what is lawful and unlawful, the books of the Scriptures are full of. So it is necessary that you be among those five virgins. You will not fear what was said: Let no one enter. For this is said, and this will be done; but when you have entered: no one will shut against you; but when you have entered, the gates of Jerusalem will be shut, and the bars of its gates strengthened. For if you wish to be either not a virgin in heart, or, even if a virgin, among the foolish virgins; you will remain outside, and in vain you will knock.

**11.** Who are the foolish virgins? They too are five: who, except the souls which have this continence of the flesh, so as to avoid the corruptions coming from all the senses, which senses I have already enumerated? They avoid as it were the corruptions coming from every side, and do not bear their good in their conscience before the eyes of God: but they wish thence to please men, and follow another's judgment. They hunt for the favors of the crowd; they are cheap to themselves, when they wish to be dear to spectators; their conscience does not suffice them. Rightly they do not carry oil with them. But oil is the very glorying, on account of its brightness and splendor. But what does the apostle say? See the wise virgins carrying oil with them: *But let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself, and not in another*[^25]. These, then, are the wise virgins. But the foolish do indeed light their lamps; their works do indeed seem to shine: but they will fail, and be extinguished; because they are not fed from the inner oil. And all sleep when the bridegroom delays: because from each kind of men they fall asleep into death; both from those foolish, and from these wise, when the coming of the Lord delays, they go into this bodily and visible death, which it is known to all Christians that the Scriptures set in place of sleep. When the apostle spoke of the sick: *For this reason*, he says, *many among you are weak and sick, and sleep enough*[^26]: They sleep, he says, they die. But behold the bridegroom is to come, and all will rise, but not all will enter. The works of the foolish virgins will fail, who do not have the oil of conscience; they will not find from whom to buy what the flatterers used to sell them. For it is said by those who mock, not by those who envy: *Go, buy for yourselves.* For the foolish had asked of the wise, and had said to them: *Give us oil, because our torches are going out.* What did the wise say? *Lest perhaps it not suffice for us and for you, go rather to those who sell, and buy for yourselves*[^27]. This was to admonish: What now do those profit you from whom you are accustomed to buy adulation? *And when they go*, he says, *those others entered, and the door was shut*[^28]. When they go in heart, when they think such things, while they alienate themselves from that intention, and recall their past things behind them, they go as it were to those who sell: and they do not then find those who favor, do not then find those who praise, by whom they were accustomed to be praised, and as it were roused to good works, not by the strength of a good conscience, but by the goading of another's tongue.

**12.** And that which was said: *Lest perhaps it not suffice for us*, was said with great consideration of humility. For the oil which we carry in our conscience is our judgment about ourselves, of what kind we are; and it is difficult for anyone to judge of himself perfectly. My brothers, however much a man makes progress, however much he stretches himself to the things before, and forgets the past[^29]; if he now says to himself: It is well: there comes forth a rule from the treasuries of God, it examines to the very liquid; and who will boast that he has a chaste heart? and who will boast that he is clean from sin[^30]? But what did Scripture say? *Judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy*[^31]. However much you make progress, you will hope in mercy. For if justice without mercy is brought forth, in anyone it will find something to condemn. What Scripture consoles us? That which exhorts to do mercy, that we may altogether grow frequent in giving out what we have more than enough of. But we have many superfluous things, if we keep only the necessary: for if we seek empty things, nothing suffices. Brothers, seek what suffices for the work of God, not what suffices for your cupidity. Your cupidity is not the work of God. Your shape, your body, your soul, all this is the work of God. Seek what suffices, and you will see how few they are. For the widow, two coins sufficed to do mercy, two coins sufficed to buy the kingdom of God[^32]. For clothing the hunters so many times, what suffices the producer? See that not only are the things few which suffice you; but God himself does not seek many things from you. Seek how much he has given you, and from it I take what suffices: the rest which lie superfluous are the necessities of others. The superfluous things of the rich are the necessities of the poor. Other men's goods are possessed, when superfluous things are possessed.

**13.** So, doing mercies of this kind, and especially that one which costs nothing: *Forgive, as we forgive*[^33]; by which you give out nothing but love, which grows as you give it out: doing, then, and being fervent in those very good works of mercy—which good works, as we have already said, will then not be necessary, because there will be no one wretched on whom mercy is to be done; you will await judgment securely, not so much secure of your justice, as secure of the mercy of God, because you too gave mercy beforehand. *For judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy: but mercy*, he says, *is exalted above judgment*[^34]. Do not think, brothers, that then he is not just, when he has mercy on us, or departs from the rule of his justice. Both when he condemns, he is just; and when he has mercy, he is just. For what is so just, as to render mercy to one who gives it beforehand? What so just, as that with the measure with which you have measured, it be measured back to you[^35]? Give to a needy brother. To which brother? To Christ. If, then, because to a brother, to Christ; and because to Christ, to God, who is over all blessed forever[^36]: God wished to be in need from you; and do you draw back your hands? Surely you stretch out your hand, and ask of God: hear Scripture: *Let not your hand be stretched out to receive, and drawn back to give*[^37]. God wishes it to be given out to him from that which he gave. For what do you give that he did not give? for what do you have that you did not receive[^38]? Or do you indeed—I do not say, to God, but to anyone—give something of your own? You give of his, who commands that you give. Be a dispenser, not an invader. Doing these things, then, and if with good humility you say of that oil: *Lest perhaps it not suffice for us*, you enter and it is shut. Hear the apostle saying this: *But to me it is a very little thing that I be judged by you*[^39]. For when can you judge my conscience? when examine with what mind I do whatever I do? how much can men judge of another? More a man surely of himself; but God more of a man, than a man of himself. So if you are such, you will enter; you will be among those five virgins: the rest, the foolish, will be shut out. For this you have in the Gospel: The door will be shut, and they will stand and cry out: *Open to us;* and it will not be opened: *Because he has strengthened the bars of your gates. He has strengthened*, he says, *the bars of your gates*: stand secure, praise secure, praise without end. Your gates are strongly shut; no friend goes out, no enemy enters: *He has strengthened the bars of your gates.*

**14.** *He has blessed your sons within you*[^40]. They do not wander outside, they are not pilgrims; within they rejoice, within they praise, within they are blessed; within they do not travail, because they now bring forth no one. They are sons, they are saints: these holy sons now praising and rejoicing, were brought forth and born by their mother love, and shut in by gathering love. Hear love bringing them forth: endowed with which the apostle Paul, bearing not only a fatherly, but also a motherly heart toward his sons: *My sons*, he says, *whom I am again in travail with*[^41]. When Paul was in travail with them, love was in travail; when love was in travail with them, the Spirit of God was in travail; *for the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^42]. So let it gather those whom it brought forth in travail and bore. Now already within they are sons, they are secure: they have flown from the nest of fear, flown into the heavenly things, flown into the eternal things; they now fear nothing temporal.

**15.** [v. 14.] *He has blessed your sons within you.* Who? *Who has set your borders peace*[^43]. How did you all exult? Love this, my brothers. We are much delighted, when from your hearts the love of peace cries out. How did it delight you? I had said nothing, I had expounded nothing; I pronounced the verse, and you cried out. What cried out of you? The love of peace. What did I show to your eyes? Whence do you cry out, if you do not love it? whence do you love it, if you do not see it? Peace is invisible. What is the eye by which it was seen, that it might be loved? For it would not be acclaimed, unless it were loved. These are the spectacles which God shows, of invisible things. With how great a beauty has the understanding of peace struck your hearts? What shall I now say of peace, or of the praise of peace? Your affection has anticipated all my words: I do not fulfill it; I cannot, I am weak. Let us defer all the praises of peace to that homeland of peace. There we will praise it more fully, where we will have it more fully. If we so love it begun in us, how shall we praise it perfected? Behold, this I say, O beloved sons, O sons of the kingdom, O citizens of Jerusalem, since in Jerusalem is the vision of peace; and all who love peace are blessed in it, and they themselves enter when the gates are shut, and the bars strengthened. This which, named, you so love and cherish, that very thing follow after, that very thing desire; that very thing in the home, that very thing in business, that very thing in wives, that very thing in sons, that very thing in slaves, that very thing in friends, that very thing in enemies love.

**16.** This is the peace which the heretics do not have. What does peace do in the still uncertain things of this region, in this pilgrimage of our mortality; when as yet no one is visible to another, no one sees the heart of another? what does peace do? It does not judge of uncertain things, it does not affirm unknown things; it is more inclined to believe well of a man, than to suspect evil. It does not grieve much to err, when it believes well even of an evil man; but perniciously, when it perhaps thinks evil of a good man. I do not know of what kind he is: what do I lose, if I believe that he is good? If it is uncertain, it is allowed that you beware, lest perhaps it be true; yet do not condemn, as though it were true. This peace commands. *Seek peace*, he says, *and follow it*[^44]. What does heresy admonish? It condemns the unknown, it condemns the whole world: The whole world has perished, there is no Christian, Africa alone has remained. You have judged well. From what tribunal do you pronounce sentence on the world? in what forum did the world stand before you? I do not wish it to be believed of me, but neither of you: let Christ be believed, let the Spirit of God through the Prophets be believed, let the Law of Moses be believed. What did Moses say of these future times? To Abraham it was said: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^45]. Or do you doubt what the seed of Abraham is? I think that when the apostle has said it, you will not doubt: or if you doubt even the apostle, why *Peace, peace; and there is no peace*[^46]? What does the apostle say? *To Abraham the promises were made, and to his seed. He does not say: And to seeds, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^47]. Behold, before thousands of years it was said to Abraham: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*: what before thousands of years was said, and believed by one, now we already see fulfilled. This we read, this we behold, and you resist, coming from athwart? What will you say? Do not believe. Whom? The Spirit of God? God speaking to Abraham? And whom do I believe? You? I do not say this, you will say. You do not say this? do you not say: Believe me rather than the Spirit of God and God speaking to Abraham? What, then, do you say to me? That one handed it down, and that one handed it down. Do you recite this from the Gospel, from the Apostle, from the Prophets? Examine all the Scriptures, read me this from those whom I believe; for you I do not believe. Whence are you to read it? This my father told me, he says, this my grandfather, this my brother, this my bishop. But this God told Abraham: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed.* One man hears and believes, and it comes about in many after many times. When it is said, it is believed: when it is fulfilled, is it doubted? This, then, Moses said, let the Prophets too say it. See the transaction of our purchase. Christ hangs on the wood; see at how much he buys, and so you will see what he buys. He is about to buy something; that something you do not yet know. See, see at how much, and you will see what. He shed blood, he bought with his blood, he bought with the blood of the spotless Lamb, he bought with the blood of the only Son of God. What was bought with the blood of the only Son of God? Still see at how much. The prophet said long before it came to pass: *They have dug my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones*[^48]. I see a great price, O Christ; let me see what you bought: *They will be reminded, and will be turned to the Lord, all the ends of the earth*[^49]. In one and the same psalm I see the buyer, and the price, and the possession. The buyer is Christ, the price his blood, the possession the world. Let us hear the very prophetic words, contradicting the heretic litigators. Behold the possession of my Lord. I read the right in the psalm: *They will be reminded, and will be turned to the Lord, all the ends of the earth, and all the families of the nations will adore in his sight.* And see one disputing, see one defending his right: *For his is the kingdom, and he will have dominion over the nations*[^50]. He who bought, Christ, not he who apostatized, Donatus. *They will adore*: rightly, *all the families of the nations will adore in his sight.* Why rightly? *Because the kingdom is the Lord's, and he will have dominion over the nations.* That Moses said, this the Prophets, and many other thousands. Who numbers the testimonies about the Church spread through the whole world? who numbers them? There are not so many heresies against the Church, as there are testimonies of the Law for the Church. What page does not sound this? what verse does not speak this? All cry out for the Lord's unity, because he set the borders of Jerusalem peace. You bark against these things, heretic! Whence rightly is it said in that city, what is written in the Apocalypse: *Dogs outside*[^51]. You bark against these things. Whence have you judged of the world, as I had begun to say? from what tribunal? From the presumption, namely, of your heart. A high tribunal, but ruinous. Moses said this, the Prophets said this; and they still do not believe, who wish to seem Christians!

**17.** A certain rich man was tormented among the dead, he desired a drop of water from the finger of the poor man whom he had despised before his door, since he was burning in the flames[^52]. And when it was not given him, because *judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy*[^53]; when, then, it was not given him, he said to Abraham: *Father Abraham, send Lazarus. I have there five brothers; let him announce to them the things I suffer here, lest they too come into this place of torments*[^54]. And what did Abraham say to him? *They have there Moses and the Prophets.* And he: *No, father Abraham; but if someone from the dead rises again, they will believe*[^55]. And Abraham: *If they do not hear Moses*, he says, *and the Prophets, neither, if someone rises from the dead, will they believe*[^56]. Of whom did he say: *They have there Moses and the Prophets*? Of those surely who were still living, for whom the time of correction was still ample, who had not yet come into those places of torments. *They have there*, he says, *whom they may hear, Moses and the Prophets.* They do not believe these, *but if someone rises from the dead, they will believe. If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither, if someone rises from the dead, will they believe.* It is the sentence of Abraham. The sentence of Abraham where, and whence? From a certain lofty place, and full of rest and happiness, which he who was tormented in the flame saw, lifting up his eyes; when in his bosom, that is, in his secret place, he saw the poor man happily exulting: thence was brought forth that sentence. See from what tribunal. For there God dwells, because in the saints God dwells. Whence the apostle desires and says: *To be dissolved and to be with Christ, is by far the best*[^57]: and to that robber it is said: *Today you will be with me in paradise*[^58]. So the Lord, abiding with Abraham and in Abraham, said that sentence: *They have there Moses and the Prophets; if they do not hear them, neither, if someone rises from the dead, will they believe him.* O heretics, you have here Moses and the Prophets: you still live, you can still hear, it is still allowed to be corrected, to bridle animosity; it is still granted to hold the truth: dispute with yourselves whether Moses and the Prophets are to be heard, who have shown such great testimonies of their faith; since we see human affairs so run, as they were foretold by them. Why do you still hesitate to believe Moses and the Prophets? why hesitate to hear them? Or do you perhaps seek one rising from the dead, whether he too may tell you about his Church? This the rich man among the dead sought, that someone be sent from the dead to his brothers: he was rebuked because he sought this; Moses and the Prophets ought to have sufficed his brothers. For this he sought in vain, that you, now admonished by the example, lest, seeking in vain and too late, you be tormented as he was, might hear Moses and the Prophets. What did Moses say? *In your seed all the nations will be blessed.* What did the Prophets say? *They will be reminded, and will be turned to the Lord, all the ends of the earth.* Will you still say to me: Let someone rise from the dead; I do not believe, unless someone comes thence, and tells me? O Lord, thanks to your mercy: you willed to die, that someone might rise from the dead; and that someone, not just anyone, but the Truth rose from the dead. He who would tell the truth from the dead, even if he did not come to the dead; yet, on account of the voices of the unskilled wicked, behold he himself died, behold he rose from the dead. What do you say, heretic? what do you say? Now I will hear you, all your excuses are ended; even if you said the words of the rich man set among the dead, Christ has risen from the dead: do you deign to hear even him? Behold, what perhaps, like the dead rich man, you, living, desired, he has risen from the dead: not your father, not your grandfather, not those who defamed I know not whom by the name of tradition rose. But behold, they did not defame, they said the truth; do you wish to know how it is nothing to me? Let us hear together what he who rose from the dead said. Why should I delay over more things? Let us hear, now let the Gospel be opened, let what was done be read as though it is being done: let the past deeds be set before our eyes, for the sake of avoiding future things. Behold Christ rising from the dead showed himself to his disciples. It is his marriage; he is the bridegroom, the Church the bride. Behold the bridegroom who was said to be dead, consumed, finished; behold he rose whole, behold he is shown to the eyes of the disciples, behold he is offered to the hands that touch, behold they touched the scars, the wounds which were despaired of. He offered himself to be seen by their eyes, to be touched by their hands: they think it is a spirit; for they had despaired of their salvation. He exhorts them, confirms them in faith: *Handle, and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^59]. They touch, they rejoice, they tremble. *And while they still trembled for joy*; so you have it written. Even if things are certain, things that are very glad are scarcely believed. A certain doubt as of one believing more slowly, seasons the pleasure of one holding. It is necessary that a man rejoice the more, when what he had despaired of comes. For to season and increase that very joy, he was unwilling to be recognized at once. He held the sight of his disciples, of those two whom he found on the way conversing now in despair, and saying: *But we hoped that he was the one to redeem Israel*[^60]. They had thought this, now they did not think it. Now hope was not with them, and Christ was with them: but he who gave himself back, brought hope back too. Afterward, then, he said—after they recognized him in the breaking of bread, and he offered himself to the other disciples, when they thought it was a spirit: *Handle and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have. And while they still trembled for joy: Have you here*, he says, *anything to eat*[^61]? He took, he blessed, he ate, and gave to them. The truth of the body was expressed, all suspicion of falsity removed. What then? *Did you not know that all things written in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms about me had to be fulfilled*[^62]? And because they believed Moses and the Prophets: for true was what Abraham said: *If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither, if someone rises from the dead, will they believe*: because, then, they believed Moses and the Prophets, and were not of those whom Abraham rebuked, they heard what the Lord said: *Did you not know that all things written in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms about me had to be fulfilled?* Behold those who believed Moses and the Prophets, see how from their testimony they believe him who rose from the dead: *Then he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures; and he said to them: Because so it is written, and so it behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day*[^63].

**18.** You have now the bridegroom of the Church. And Moses was not silent about this, the Prophets were not silent about this, that Christ would rise from the dead on the third day; would suffer, would rise again. The bridegroom has been described to us, that we may not err. But there arise I know not who, and because we do not err about the bridegroom, and they too seem to believe what we believe about the bridegroom, in order to call us away from the members of the bridegroom, they say to us: He is indeed the bridegroom whom you believe, whom we too believe: but the bride is not that Church which you hold. And which is it? The party of Donatus. And is this what you say: do you say this, or the bridegroom? Do you say it, or God through Moses? Behold, through Moses I hold the Church: it was said by Moses: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed.* Do you say it, or the Spirit of God through the Prophets? Behold, through the Prophets I hold the Church: it was said through the Prophet: *They will be reminded, and will be turned to the Lord, all the ends of the earth.* Behold I now hold the testimony of the Law and the testimony of the Prophets; let us hear too him who rose from the dead. He showed himself the bridegroom, we hold him. He confirmed it by showing, by giving testimonies. For this Moses, this the Prophets, *that Christ had to suffer, and to rise on the third day.* Now, then, since we both hold the bridegroom from his very words, and I think that you now begin to believe with me both these words of Moses, and the Prophets; let us believe too him who rose from the dead. Let him go on, and say. O Lord, I now see the bridegroom Christ; it is done: let no one call me away from the members of your bride, and let you not be my head, if I am not among his members; tell me something about the Church too; because I now do not doubt about its bridegroom. Hear too about the Church. He goes on and says: *That repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name*[^64]. Nothing truer, *that repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name.* But where this? For some say: *Behold here;* others: *Behold there.* And what does he himself say? *Do not believe: there will arise false christs and false prophets, and they will say: Behold here, and behold there*[^65]. For they do not say of the head himself: *Behold here, and behold there;* for it is known that Christ is in the heavens: but of the Church where Christ is, who says: *Behold, I am with you, even to the consummation of the age*[^66]. But the Lord says: *Do not believe*: he who says: *Behold here, and behold there*, shows parts; I bought the whole. Let this Gospel tell me this: tell this yourself from the Gospel, since you have now risen from the dead, that those who believe Moses and the Prophets may believe you; tell me this yourself. I hear: *Christ had to suffer and to rise on the third day, and that repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^67]. What is it, heretic? Surely when I recited Moses, when I recited the Prophets, you deferred to him who was to rise from the dead: behold he has risen, behold he has said it; the Church of Christ and the wife of Christ has as little doubt, as little doubt has the body of Christ, shown to the eyes, touched by the hands of the disciples. Behold, he who rose from the dead showed both: he showed the head, he showed the members; he showed the bridegroom, he showed the bride. Either believe both with me, or you believe that to your damnation. For what, that he rose from the dead, that he rose in the same body? Well, that he showed the scars; that as he was crucified, as buried, so given back, so shown: you believe most excellently; hear him speaking in whom you believe. *That repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name.* Where? Through the broad lands. If I wished to say it myself, now wrestling against the heretics, now fighting, now having with them a conflict of so great a question; I would not say it so against present heretics, as he said it against future ones. What more do you want? Remission of sins is preached in the name of Christ. Where? *Through all the nations.* Whence? Beginning *from Jerusalem.* Communicate with this Church. Why do we quarrel? For this Church began from that earthly Jerusalem, that it might thence rejoice in God in that heavenly Jerusalem. For from this it begins, at that it ends. In that the whole Church will be, from this it took the beginning of faith.

**19.** Read the Acts of the Apostles, if perhaps I lie, how the disciples were gathered there, when the Holy Spirit came; that it may be shown you what the Lord said: *Beginning from Jerusalem;* how they spoke with the tongues of all upon whom the Holy Spirit came[^68]. Why do you not wish to speak with the tongues of all? Behold there all tongues sounded. Why now does not he to whom the Holy Spirit is given speak with the tongues of all? For this was then the sign of the Holy Spirit coming upon men, that they spoke with the tongues of all. Now what will you say, heretic? that the Holy Spirit is not given? I do not say: Where? Is it given, or not given? If it is not given; what is it you do, by speaking, baptizing, blessing? what is it you do? You celebrate empty things. So it is given. If it is given; why do not those to whom it is given speak with the tongues of all? Has the gift of God failed, or is the fruit less? The weeds have grown, but the wheat too: *Let both grow until the harvest*[^69]. He did not say: Let the weeds grow, let the wheat diminish; both grew. Why does the Holy Spirit not now appear in all tongues? On the contrary it appears in all tongues: for then the Church was not yet spread through the whole world, that the members of Christ might speak in all the nations. Then there was fulfilled in one, what was foretold in all. Now the whole body of Christ speaks with the tongues of all: and where it does not yet speak, it will speak. For the Church will grow, until it occupies all tongues. What you deserted, how far has it grown! Hold with us as far as it has reached, that with us you may come to those it has not yet reached. I speak with the tongues of all, I dare to say to you. I am in the body of Christ, I am in the Church of Christ: if the body of Christ now speaks with the tongues of all, I too am in all tongues; mine is Greek, mine is Syriac, mine is Hebrew, mine is that of all the nations, because I am in the unity of all the nations.

**20.** So, brothers, the Church began from Jerusalem; it went through all the nations: what more evident than this testimony of the Law, the Prophets, the Lord himself? Everywhere the voices of the apostles sound, rendering testimony to our hope in the unity of the body of Christ. Rejoice in the grain, bear with the weeds, groan in the threshing, sigh for the barn. That time will come in which we shall rejoice, the bars of the gates of Jerusalem strengthened. Let him enter who is to enter. He who will enter there manifest, does not enter here feigned. But he who enters here feigned, is outside. He is outside, and does not know it: the winnowing-fan will prove it, the bars will prove it. He who now is truly within and in truth within, there is firmly within: but he who is here within by enduring, there is within by rejoicing. For the borders of Jerusalem are peace; because: *He has set*, he says, *your borders peace.* Now we desire the peace, which here we have in hope. For as yet in ourselves what kind of peace is there? The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh[^70]. Where is full peace in one man? When it will be full in one man, then it will be full in all the citizens of Jerusalem. When will there be full peace? When this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality[^71]: then full peace, then firm peace; nothing quarrels against the soul in man, not it against itself, wounded in some part; not the frailty of the flesh, not the need of the body, not hunger, not thirst, not cold, not heat, not weariness, not any want, not the provocation of a quarrel, not even that very anxious caution both of avoiding the enemy and of loving him. All these things, my brothers, quarrel against us; there is not yet full and perfect peace. What you cried out a little while ago, peace being named, you cried out from desire; your cry was from thirst, not from satisfaction: because there will be perfect justice, where there is perfect peace. Now we hunger and thirst for justice. *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they will be satisfied*[^72]. How will they be satisfied? When we come to peace. Therefore when he had said: *Who has set your borders peace;* because there is satisfaction and no want, he at once added: *And with the fat of wheat satisfying you.*

**21.** Brothers, since that peace of which we speak is not yet in the whole of us, that is, in each one of us whole, it perhaps still delights your spirit to hear: but if nothing resists and rebels from the body, we will finish the Psalm. I never feel you wearied; yet I fear, God knows, lest I be a burden to you or to certain brothers: and I see the zeal of many demanding of me this labor and this sweat, which I believe in the Lord will not be fruitless. I rejoice that there is so great a pleasure in the truth of the word of God, that your good zeal in good and about good overcomes the zeal of the mad who are in the amphitheater. Would they, if they stood so long, still be gazing? So, brothers, let us hear the rest, because you wish it. May the Lord be present, may he be present both to our strength and to our minds. *Who has set your borders peace*, he says to that Jerusalem: *and with the fat of wheat satisfying you.* The hunger and thirst for justice pass, satisfaction succeeds. What will be there the fat of wheat, except that bread which came down from heaven to us[^73]? In the homeland itself how will he satisfy, who in the pilgrimage thus fed?

**22.** [v. 15.] Now he is about to speak of our very pilgrimage, by which one comes to that Jerusalem where we will praise the Lord together, will praise the Lord our God, we Jerusalem, we Zion, when the bars of our gates will be strengthened. For he who will then satisfy with the fat of wheat, what does he do in this pilgrimage? This which follows: *Who sends his word to the earth*[^74]. Behold, on earth we labor, weary, languid, sluggish, cold: when would we be lifted to the fat of wheat that satisfies, unless he sent his word to the earth, by which we were weighed down; to the earth, by which we were hindered from returning? He sent his word, he did not desert us even in the desert, he rained manna from heaven. *Who sends his word to the earth;* and his word came to the lands. How? or what is his word? *Even unto swiftness his word runs.* He did not say: His word is swift: *his word runs even unto swiftness.* Let us understand, brothers; he could not choose a better word. The hot grows hot by heat, the cold grows cold by cold, the swift becomes swift by swiftness. What is hotter than heat itself, by which whatever becomes hot grows hot? What is colder than cold itself, by which whatever becomes cold grows cold? What, then, is swifter than swiftness itself, by which whatever runs swiftly becomes swift? Many things can be called swift, and some more and some less; and each thing is swift in proportion as it shares in swiftness. That thing shares more in swiftness; therefore it is more swift: or it shares less in swiftness; therefore it is less swift. So than swiftness itself what is swifter? So how far does it run? *Even unto swiftness.* Increase as much as you wish that the word is swift, and say: It is swifter than this or that, than birds, than winds, than angels. Is any of these so much, as swiftness itself, even unto swiftness itself? What is swiftness itself, brothers? It is everywhere, it is not in a part. This belongs to the Word of God, not to be in a part, to be everywhere through itself the Word, from which is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God[^75], not yet having assumed flesh. If we think of God in the form of God, the Word equal to the Father; it is the Wisdom of God, of which it was said: *It reaches from end to end mightily*[^76]. How great swiftness! *It reaches from end to end mightily.* But perhaps it reaches by immobility. If by immobility, as some mass of rock may fill some place; it is said that it reaches from the end of the same place to the end, but not by motion. What, then, do we say? does that Word have no motion, and is that Wisdom dull? And where is what is said of the Spirit of Wisdom? When many things were said: *Keen*, he says, *mobile, sure, undefiled*[^77]. So surely the Wisdom of God is mobile. If, then, mobile; when it touches this, does it not touch that? or does it touch this, and desert that? And where is the swiftness? This swiftness brings about, that it both is always everywhere, and is nowhere held shut in. But we cannot think these things; we are sluggish. Who can think them? And truly, brothers, since I have said as I could (if indeed I could, if I understood), and as you could you have understood. But what does the apostle say? *But to him who is able to do above what we ask, or understand*[^78]. What does he here show? That as often as we have understood, we have not understood as it is. Why this? *For the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul*[^79]. So on earth we are cold. For swiftness is fervent; and all fervent things are swifter, all cold things slower. We are slow, therefore cold. But that Wisdom runs even unto swiftness. So it is most fervent; nor is there one who can hide himself from its heat[^80].

**23.** [v. 16.] We, then, who are weighed down by the sluggishness of a cold body, by the chain of this corruptible earthly life, do we have no hope of grasping the Word, which runs even unto swiftness? Or has he indeed deserted us, cast down as we are from corporeal things into the lowest depths? Did he not predestine us before we were born in this mortal and sluggish body? He, then, who predestined us, gave snow to the earth, that is, gave us. For now let us come to those somewhat obscure verses of the Psalm; let those wrappings begin to be unwound, since the word of God, the more it is spoken through us, finds you the more eager. Behold, we sluggish ones have, as it were, frozen here on this earth. And as it happens with snow—for it freezes above and falls to the depths—so, when love grows cold, human nature falls down into these lands, and, wrapped in a sluggish body, has become as it were snow. But among this snow are the predestined sons of God. For he gives *snow like wool*[^81]. What is, *like wool*? That is, of the snow which he gave, of these who are still sluggish in spirit and cold whom he predestined, he is going to make something. For wool is the matter of a garment: wool, when it is seen, is a kind of preparation for a garment. Therefore, because he predestined these who for a time crawl cold upon the earth, and do not yet burn with the spirit of love (for he is still speaking of predestination), God gave these as it were wool; he is going to make a garment from them. Rightly on the mountain did the garment of Christ shine like snow. The garment of Christ shone like snow, as though from that wool a tunic had now been made; of which wool, that is, of the snow which he gave like wool, the predestined were still sluggish: but wait, see what follows; because he gave them like wool, a tunic is made from them. For just as the Church is called the body of Christ, so the Church itself is called the garment of Christ: hence that which the Apostle says, *that he might present to himself a glorious Church, having neither spot nor wrinkle*[^82]. Therefore let him present to himself a glorious Church, having neither spot nor wrinkle; let him make for himself a garment from that wool which he predestined in the snow. While they are still among unbelieving men, and cold, and sluggish, let him make a garment from this wool. That it may be washed from its spots, let it be cleansed by faith; that it may have no wrinkle, let it be stretched on the cross. *Who gives snow like wool.*

**24.** Now if they are predestined, they are to be called. For *whom he predestined, them he also called*[^83]. Now how are they called out of the sickness of that body, that they may be made whole? How are they called? Hear the Gospel: *I did not come to call the just, but sinners to repentance*[^84]. He begins, then, now by the predestination of that snow to recognize his own torpor, to accuse his own sin; he begins by his calling to come to repentance. Rightly, then, *he who gives snow like wool*, for the making of the future tunic; and also, for the calling to repentance, *scatters mist like ashes. Mist*, he says, *he scatters like ashes*: who? *He who gives snow like wool.* For whom he predestined, he calls to repentance; because *whom he predestined, them he also called.* But ashes pertain to repentance. Hear him calling to repentance, when he reproached certain cities, saying: *Woe to you, Chorazin, woe to you, Bethsaida; for if in Tyre and Sidon the mighty works had been done which have been done in you, they would long ago*, he says, *have repented in sackcloth and ashes*[^85]. Therefore *he scatters mist like ashes.* What is, *he scatters mist like ashes*? When anyone is called to understand God, and it is said to him: Take hold of the truth, he begins to wish to take hold of the truth, he is not equal to it, he sees himself suffering a kind of darkness which before he did not see. To this end, then, is that mist: that you may know that you do not know, and may know what you ought to know, and may see yourself too weak to know what ought to be known. For if in this mist you have already presumed to know, you will hear from the Apostle: *He who thinks he knows something does not yet know as he ought to know*[^86]. Therefore you have not yet comprehended, you still suffer the mist. But he has not deserted you who kindled for you the lamp of his flesh. You do not stray in the mist; follow by faith. But because you try to see and cannot, let you repent of your sins; for the mist is scattered like ashes. Now let you repent that you were defiant against God; now let you repent that you followed your own evil ways. You have come into this difficulty of that blessed vision; and the mist which God scatters like ashes will be wholesome for you. You yourself are still mist, but as it were ashes. For penitents still roll in ashes, my brothers, as it were testifying that they are like them, saying to their God: *I am ashes.* For a certain Scripture has said: *I despised myself and wasted away, and I esteemed myself earth and ashes*[^87]. This is the humility of the penitent. When Abraham speaks to his God, and when he wishes the burning of Sodom to be revealed to him: *I*, he says, *am earth and ashes*[^88]. How was this humility always present in great and holy men? Therefore *he scatters mist like ashes*: why? Because *whom he predestined, them he also called*, he who did not come *to call the just, but sinners to repentance.*

**25.** [v. 17.] *Who sends his crystal like morsels of bread.* There is no need to labor again to say what crystal is. For we have spoken of it before, and I believe it has not slipped from your love. What, then, is, *he sends his crystal like morsels of bread*? As that snow is his, because it is of the predestined; as that mist is his, because they themselves are called to repentance, who are predestined to salvation: so in a certain way the crystal is his. What is crystal? It has hardened greatly, it has frozen greatly; it can no longer be easily melted like snow. Snow hardened over the space of many years and through a succession of ages is called crystal: and this he sends like morsels of bread. What does this mean? They were exceedingly hard, to be compared no longer to snow but to crystal: and they too are predestined, and called; and some of them in such a way that they might feed others, and be useful to others as well. And what need is there for us to count up the many we perhaps know, this one or that one? It occurs to anyone who thinks, from among those he knows, how hard perhaps and stubborn and resistant against the truth they were, and now they preach the truth: they have become morsels of bread. Who is that one bread? *We who are many*, says the Apostle, *are one body in Christ*[^89]: *we who are many are one bread, one body*[^90], he himself says. Therefore if the one bread is the whole body of Christ, the members of Christ are morsels of bread. Out of certain hard ones he makes his members, and useful for feeding others. Why do we go through the many? Let us look at the most well-known one, the apostle Paul. Nothing is more known to us than this man, nothing more delightful, nothing more familiar in the Scriptures. And if there have been any who out of such hardness as he himself was in were made bread, with him set before us as an example, let them all come to mind, that this sense may be unfolded: *He sends his crystal like morsels of bread.* Behold, the apostle Paul was crystal, hard, resisting the truth, crying out against the Gospel, as one hardening against the sun. How hard he was, growing up in the Law, instructed at the feet of Gamaliel the teacher of the Law! Did he not hear Moses and the Prophets preaching Christ? Great hardness! Surely the gentiles had not heard the Prophets, had not heard Moses: they were cold, but they were not crystal. He who believed in the words preaching Christ, and yet did not believe in Christ when he came, had hardened exceedingly. Because, then, he was crystal, he seemed shining and white; but hard and exceedingly icy. How shining and white? *A Hebrew of the Hebrews, according to the Law a Pharisee*[^91]. See the brightness of the crystal. Hear the hardness of the crystal: *according to zeal persecuting the Church of Christ*[^92]. Among those who stoned the holy martyr Stephen, there was this hard one, and perhaps harder than the rest: for he kept the garments of all who stoned him, that he might stone him by the hands of all[^93].

**26.** [v. 18.] Therefore we see snow, mist, crystal: it is good that he breathe upon them, and melt them. For if he does not breathe, if he himself does not dissolve the hardness of this ice: *Before the face of his cold who will stand? Before the face of his cold*, of whom? Of God. Whence is his cold? Behold, he deserts the sinner, behold, he does not call, behold, he does not open the understanding, behold, he does not pour in grace: let a man be melted, if he can, from the ice of folly. He cannot. Why can he not? *Before the face of his cold who will stand?* See, then, that man freezing, and saying: *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive under the law of sin which is in my members. Wretched man that I am, who will deliver me from the body of this death?* Behold, I grow cold, behold, I freeze; by what heat shall I be melted, that I may run? *Who will deliver me from the body of this death?*[^94] *Before the face of his cold who will stand?* And who will deliver himself, if he deserts him? And who delivers? *The grace of God, through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^95]. Hear here too the grace of God: *Who sends his crystal like morsels of bread: before the face of his cold who will stand?* Is there, then, despair? Far from it. For there follows: *He will send forth his word, and will melt them.* Therefore let not the snow despair, let not the mist despair, let not the crystal despair. For from snow, as from wool, a tunic is made. That mist found salvation in repentance; because *whom he predestined, them he also called.* But although there be among the predestined the very hardest, who have frozen, as it were, over a long time, and have become crystal; they will not be hard for the mercy of God. *He will send forth his word, and will melt them.* What is, *will melt them*? Lest perhaps you understand *will melt them* in a bad sense; he will liquefy, he will dissolve them. For they are hard through pride. Rightly is pride also called stupor; for whatever is stupid is cold. Men who suffer a chill say daily: I was struck stupid. Therefore pride is stupor. *He will send forth his word, and will melt them.* And indeed heaps of snow, when they are warmed, dissolve into lowliness. As, then, stupor raises up as it were a mountain of snow, so pride raises up the foolish. *He will send forth his word, and will melt them.* Behold, that crystal Saul came, after the slaying and stoning of Stephen, stupefied with a certain hardness against Christ, and asked for letters from the priests, that he might everywhere bring forth Christians, breathing slaughter[^96]. This man is hard and icy against the fire of God. But although he was hard, although he was icy, behold, he who *will send forth his word, and will melt them*, cried out from heaven, fervent: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^97] By that one voice such great hardness of the crystal was dissolved. Therefore *he will send forth his word, and will melt them.* Let there be no despairing of the crystal; how much less of the snow, or of the mist? Of the crystal itself let there be no despair. Hear a certain voice of the crystal: *I who was formerly a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent*[^98]. But why did God melt the crystal? That the snow itself might not despair of itself. For he says: *Therefore I obtained mercy, that in me Christ Jesus might show forth all patience, for the instruction of those who would believe in him unto eternal life*[^99]. Therefore God cries out to the gentiles: I have melted the crystal, come, you snows. *He will send forth his word, and will melt them; his spirit will breathe, and the waters will flow.* Behold, the crystal and the snows are dissolved, they go into waters; let those who thirst come and drink. Saul, hard as crystal, persecuted Stephen to death; Paul, now in the living water, calls the gentiles to the fountain. *His spirit will breathe, and the waters will flow.* A fervent spirit: whence it is said in another psalm: *Turn, O Lord, our captivity, like a torrent in the south*[^100]. For captive Jerusalem had, as it were, frozen in Babylon: the south wind blows, the stiffness of captivity is dissolved, and the fervor of love runs to God. *His spirit will breathe, and the waters will flow.* There will be made in them a fountain of water springing up unto eternal life[^101].

**27.** [v. 19.] *Declaring his word to Jacob, his justices and his judgments to Israel.* What justices? what judgments? Because whatever the human race has suffered here before, when it was snow, and mist, and crystal, it suffered rightly for its pride and its lifting up against God. Let us go back to the origin of our fall, and see how most truly it is sung in the psalm: *Before I was humbled, I sinned.* But he who says: *Before I was humbled, I sinned*, himself says: *It is good for me that you have humbled me, that I may learn your justifications*[^102]. These justifications, then, Jacob learned from God, who made Jacob himself wrestle with the Angel; in the person of which Angel the Lord himself had wrestled. He held him, he used force to hold him, he prevailed to hold him: by mercy he made himself be held, not by weakness. Therefore Jacob, wrestling, prevailed, held him; and he asks to be blessed by him whom he seemed to have conquered[^103]. How did he understand with whom he had wrestled, whom he held? Why did he wrestle by force, and hold him? Because *the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force*[^104]. Why, then, did he wrestle? Because it was with labor. Why do we scarcely hold what we easily lost? Lest, by easily receiving back what we lost, we learn to lose what we hold. Let a man labor to hold; he will hold firmly what he holds after labor. These judgments, therefore, God made manifest to Jacob and to Israel. I will say it more plainly: that is, even the just who are here suffer labors, dangers, troubles, sufferings according to their desert, by the just judgment of God. For he alone can say that he suffered here without cause; though even so not without cause, because it was for our sake: he who alone can say: *What I did not take away, then I paid*[^105]; he who alone could say: *Behold the prince of this world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^106]. And as if it were said to him: Why, then, do you suffer? there follows and he says: *But that all may know that I do the will of my Father, rise, let us go hence*[^107]. Let all the rest, who suffer by their own desert, by the judgment of God, and for justice, not claim for themselves, as it were, the suffering of innocence such as was in Christ. Hear the apostle Peter: *It is time*, he says, *that judgment begin from the house of the Lord.* Exhorting the martyrs and witnesses of God to bear most patiently all the threats of the raging world, he says to them: It is time *for the beginning of judgment from the house of God. But if the beginning is from us, what will be the end of those who do not believe the Gospel of God? and if the just man is scarcely saved, where will the sinner and the impious appear?*[^108] *Declaring his word to Jacob, his justices and his judgments to Israel.*

**28.** [v. 20.] *He has not done so to every nation.* Let no one deceive you: this judgment of God has not been declared to any nation—how both the just and the unjust suffer; how all suffer according to their desert; how the just themselves are delivered in the grace of God, not in their own merits. This has not been declared to every nation; but to Jacob alone, to Israel alone. What, then, do we do, if he has not declared it to every nation, but only to Jacob, only to Israel? Where shall we be? In Jacob and in Israel. *His judgments he has not made manifest to them.* To whom? To all nations. And how were the snows called, when the crystal was melted? how were the gentiles called, when Paul was justified? How, except that they might be in Jacob? The wild olive was cut off, that it might be grafted into the olive[^109]. They now pertain to the olive; they ought now no longer to be called gentiles, but one nation in Christ, the nation of Jacob, the nation of Israel. Why the nation of Jacob and the nation of Israel? Because Jacob is from Isaac, Isaac from Abraham. But what was said to Abraham? *In your seed all the nations will be blessed.* This same thing was said to Isaac, this also to Jacob[^110]. We belong, therefore, to Jacob; because we belong to Isaac, we belong to Abraham. For the seed of Abraham, not as I or any man interpret it, but as the holy Apostle interprets it, is Christ: and he himself says: *He does not say: And to seeds, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^111]. If there is one seed, one Jacob, one Israel, and all the nations are one in Christ. Therefore what he revealed to Jacob himself and to Israel himself pertains to all nations; and only those are to be reckoned among the other nations who, unwilling to believe in Christ, do not wish to depart from the wild olive and be grafted into the olive. The sterile and bitter branches will remain in the woods. Let Jacob rejoice. What is Jacob? Supplanter; because Jacob supplanted his brother[^112]. For *blindness in part has happened to Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in*[^113]. Of Jacob was made Israel. What is Israel? Now let us all hear, all Israel, whether you who are here among the members of Christ, or you who are outside and not outside, and through all nations everywhere outside, everywhere within; let Israel himself hear, who from Jacob was made Israel. What is Israel? Seeing God. Where will he see God? In peace. In what peace? In the peace of Jerusalem; because: *He has set*, he says, *your borders peace.* There we will praise, all being one in the One unto the One; because thereafter we will not be many and dispersed.
