# Exposition of Psalm 60

**1.** [v. 1] We have undertaken to consider this psalm with Your Charity. It is brief; the Lord will be present, that we may speak from it sufficiently, and briefly. As much as he himself helps who bids us speak, so will I be obliging to the willing, that I be not troublesome to the slow, nor too much for the few, nor burdensome to the busy. Its title does not detain us. For it is, *Unto the end, in hymns, for David himself. In hymns*, certainly in praises. *Unto the end*, certainly unto Christ. *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^1]. And *for David himself*, we ought to take none other than him who came from the seed of David, that he might be a man among men, and make men equal to the angels. But the voice in this psalm, if we are in his members and in his body, as, with him exhorting, we dare to presume, we ought to recognize as ours, not as that of some stranger. But I did not say ours as though only of those of us who are now present; but ours who are throughout the whole world, who are from the east even to the west. And that you may know that it is thus our voice, he speaks here as if one man: but it is not one man, but as one a unity speaks. But in Christ we all are one man; because the head of this one man is in heaven, and the members still labor on earth: and because they labor, see what he says.

**2.** [vv. 2-3] *Hear, O God, my entreaty, attend to my prayer.*[^2] Who says it? As if one. See whether it is one: *From the ends of the earth I cried to you, while my heart was in anguish.* Now, then, not one; but therefore one, because Christ is one, of whom we are all members. For what one man cries from the ends of the earth? None cries from the ends of the earth, except that inheritance of which it was said to the Son himself: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth*[^3]. This possession of Christ, then, this inheritance of Christ, this body of Christ, this one Church of Christ, this unity which we are, cries from the ends of the earth. But what does it cry? What I said above, *Hear, O God, my entreaty, attend to my prayer: from the ends of the earth I cried to you.* That is, this I cried to you: *from the ends of the earth*: that is, from everywhere.

**3.** But why did I cry this? *While my heart was in anguish.*[^4] He shows himself to be throughout all the nations in the whole world in great glory, but in great temptation. For our life in this pilgrimage cannot be without temptation: because our advancement comes about through our temptation, nor does anyone become known to himself except when tempted, nor can he be crowned unless he has conquered, nor can he conquer unless he has fought, nor can he fight unless he has had an enemy and temptations. This one, then, is in anguish, crying from the ends of the earth, but yet is not forsaken. Since he willed to prefigure us ourselves, who are his body, even in that body of his, in which he both died, and rose again, and ascended into heaven; so that, where the head went before, there the members may trust that they will follow. Therefore he transfigured us into himself, when he willed to be tempted by Satan. Just now it was read in the Gospel that the Lord Jesus Christ in the desert was tempted by the devil. Plainly Christ was tempted by the devil. For in Christ you were tempted, because Christ had from you flesh for himself, from himself salvation for you; from you death for himself, from himself life for you; from you reproaches for himself, from himself honors for you; therefore from you temptation for himself, from himself victory for you. If in him we were tempted, in him we overcome the devil. Do you attend to the fact that Christ was tempted, and not attend to the fact that he conquered? Recognize yourself tempted in him, and recognize yourself conquering in him. He could have kept the devil from himself: but if he were not tempted, he would not furnish you, by tempting, the lesson of conquering. And so it is no wonder, if, set among temptations, this one cries from the ends of the earth. But why is he not conquered? *On a rock you have exalted me.* Now then here we recognize who cries from the ends of the earth. Let us recall the Gospel: *Upon this rock I will build my Church*[^5]. Therefore she cries from the ends of the earth, whom he willed to be built upon a rock. But that the Church might be built upon a rock, who was made a rock? Hear Paul saying: *But the rock was Christ*[^6]. In him, then, we have been built up. Therefore that rock on which we have been built was first struck by the winds, the blast, the rain, when Christ was tempted by the devil. Behold, in what firmness he willed to establish you. Deservedly our voice is not empty, but is heard: for we have been set in great hope; *On a rock you have exalted me.*

**4.** [vv. 3-4] *You have led me, because you have become my hope.*[^7] Unless he had become our hope, he would not lead us. He leads as a leader, and leads in himself as the way, and brings through to himself as the homeland. He leads us, then. Whence? Because he has become our hope. Whence has he become our hope? Behold, thus, as you heard, because he was tempted, because he suffered, because he rose again, he has become our hope. For what do we say to ourselves when we read these things? For God will not destroy us, for whose sake he sent his Son to be tempted, to be crucified, to die, to rise again: for God does not truly despise us, for whose sake he did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all. So, then, he has become our hope. In him you see both your labor and your reward; labor in the passion, reward in the resurrection. So, then, he has become our hope. For we have two lives; but one in which we are, the other which we hope for. That in which we are is known to us; that which we hope for is unknown to us. Bear with that in which you are, and you will have that which you do not yet have. How do you bear with it? So as not to be conquered by the tempter. By his labors, his temptations, his sufferings, his death, Christ demonstrated to you the life in which you are; by his resurrection he demonstrated to you the life in which you will be. For we did not know except that man is born and dies; that man rises again and lives forever we did not know: he took on what you knew, and demonstrated what you did not know. Therefore, then, he has become our hope in tribulations, in temptations. See the Apostle saying: *And not only this, but we also glory*, he says, *in tribulations: knowing that tribulation works patience, patience proving, proving hope; but hope does not confound: because the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^8]. Therefore he himself has become our hope, who gave us the Holy Spirit: and we now walk toward hope; for we would not walk, unless we hoped. What does the Apostle himself say? *For what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience.* And likewise: *For by hope we have been saved*[^9].

**5.** *You have led me, because you have become my hope: a tower of strength from the face of the enemy.*[^10] My heart is in anguish, says this unity from the ends of the earth, and I labor amid temptations and scandals: the pagans envy, because they are conquered; the heretics lie in wait, veiled with the cloak of the Christian name; within, in the Church itself, the wheat suffers violence from the chaff: amid all these things, when my heart is in anguish, I will cry from the ends of the earth. But he himself does not forsake me who exalted me upon the rock, that he may lead me even to himself: because even if I labor, the devil lying in wait against me through so many places and times and occasions, here is for me a tower of strength; whither, when I have fled, I shall not only avoid the darts of the enemy, but shall also myself, secure, hurl at him whatever I wish. For Christ himself is the tower; he himself has become for us a tower from the face of the enemy, who is also the rock upon which the Church is built. Do you beware lest you be struck by the devil? Flee to the tower: never will the diabolical darts follow you to that tower; there you will stand fortified and fixed. But how will you flee to the tower? Lest perhaps anyone set in temptation seek this tower bodily; and, when he does not find it, be wearied, or fail in temptation. The tower is before you: remember Christ, and enter the tower. How do you remember Christ, so as to enter the tower? Whatever you suffer, think that he first suffered, and think with what end he suffered, that he might die and rise again. Hope for yourself too such an end as went before in him, and you have entered the tower by not consenting to the enemy. For if you consent to the enemy, then the dart of the assailant reaches you. Do you rather hurl at him the darts by which he may be struck, by which he may be conquered. What are these darts? The words of God, your faith, your very hope, good works. I do not say: Be so in that tower that you be idle there, and it suffice you that the enemy's darts do not reach you. Do something there; let not your hands cease: your good works are swords slaying the enemy.

**6.** [v. 5] *I will be a lodger in your tent unto the ages.*[^11] You see that it is he of whom we spoke who cries. Which of us is a lodger unto the ages? We live here a few days, and pass on: for here we are lodgers, we shall be dwellers in heaven. There you are a lodger, whence you are to hear the voice of the Lord your God: Move on. For from that eternal home in the heavens no one will bid you move on. Here, then, a lodger. Whence it is said in another psalm too: *I am a lodger with you, and a sojourner, like all my fathers*[^12]. Here, then, we are lodgers; there the Lord will give us eternal dwellings: *Many*, he says, *are the dwellings with my Father*[^13]. Those dwellings he will give not as to lodgers, but as to citizens about to remain forever. Yet here, brethren, because the Church was to be on this earth not for a small time, but here the Church will be unto the end of the age: therefore here he said: *I will be a lodger in your tent unto the ages.* Let the enemy rage as much as he will, let him assail me, let him prepare snares for me, let him grow frequent in scandals, and make my heart be in anguish: *I will be a lodger in your tent unto the ages.* The Church will not be conquered, will not be uprooted, nor will it yield to any temptations whatever, until the end of this age comes, and that eternal dwelling receives us from this temporal one, to which he will lead us who has become our hope. *I will be a lodger in your tent unto the ages.* If you are to be a lodger for long, as if we said this to him, you are then going to labor on earth amid so great temptations: for if the Church were here for a few days, the snares of the tempter would quickly be ended. Well; you would wish the temptations to be of a few days: but how would it gather all the born, unless it were here for long, unless it were extended unto the end? Do not envy the rest who will come after; do not, because you have already passed over, wish to cut off the bridge of mercy: let it be here unto the ages. And what of the temptations, which must abound the more the scandals come? For he himself said: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*[^14]. But that Church which cries from the ends of the earth is among those of whom there follows, saying: *But he who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved*[^15]. But whence will you persevere? What are your powers amid so great scandals, amid so great temptations, amid so great battles? by what powers do you conquer the enemy whom you do not see? surely by your own? Therefore, because this lodger will be here unto the ages, what hope has he that he may last? *I will be covered under the covering of your wings.* Behold why we are secure amid so great temptations, until the end of the age comes, and the eternal ages receive us: because we are covered under the covering of his wings. There is the heat of the age, but great is the shade under the wings of God: *I will be covered under the covering of your wings.*

**7.** [v. 6] *Because you, O God, have heard my prayer.*[^16] Which? That with which it began: *Hear, O God, my entreaty, attend to my prayer: from the ends of the earth I cried to you*[^17]. This I cried to you from the ends of the earth. Therefore *I will be covered under the covering of your wings, because you have heard my entreaty.* We are admonished, then, brethren, not to cease from praying, as long as it is the time of temptations. *You have given an inheritance to those who fear your name.* Let us, then, persevere in the fear of God's name: the eternal Father does not deceive us. Do sons labor that they may receive the inheritance of their parents, to whom, when dead, they are to succeed? do we not labor to receive the inheritance from that Father, to whom not dying we may succeed, but with whom in that very inheritance we may live forever! *You have given an inheritance to those who fear your name.*

**8.** [v. 7] *You will add days upon the days of the king, his years.*[^18] This, then, is the king whose members we are. The king is Christ, our head, our king. You have given him days upon days; not only these days in this time with an end, but days upon these days without end. *I will dwell*, he says, *in the house of the Lord, unto length of days*[^19]. Why, *unto length of days*, except because now there is shortness of days? For every thing that has an end is short: but the days of this king are days upon days, so that Christ reigns in his Church not only in these passing days, but the saints reign with him in those days that have no end. There is one day, and there are many days. Because there are many days, I have already said: *Unto length of days*: because there is one day, it is understood thus: *You are my Son, today I have begotten you*[^20]. He called one day *today*: but that day is not set in the middle between yesterday and tomorrow, nor is its beginning the end of yesterday, nor its end the beginning of tomorrow. For the years of God too are spoken of: *But you are the selfsame, and your years will not fail*[^21]. As years, so days, so one day: whatever you wish you say of eternity. Therefore you say whatever you wish, because whatever you say, you say too little. But for this reason it is necessary that you say something, that there may be that from which you may think of what cannot be said. *You will add days upon the days of the king, his years, even to the day of generation and generation.* Of this generation and of the future generation: of this generation which is compared to the moon, because the moon is born, grows, is perfected, grows old, and sets; so are these mortal generations: and of the generation by which we are regenerated by rising again, and we shall remain forever with God, when no longer as the moon, but what the Lord says: *Then the just will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^22]. For the moon is set figuratively in the Scriptures for the mutability of this mortality. Therefore he was going down to Jericho from Jerusalem who fell among robbers: because Jericho is a Hebrew word, and is interpreted in Latin "Moon." He was going down, then, as from immortality to mortality: and deservedly on the way was that Adam wounded by robbers and left half-dead, from whom is the whole human race. Therefore *you will add days upon the days of the king, his years, even to the day of generation*, I take it as the mortal generation. Of what other generation did you make mention? Of what? hear:

**9.** [v. 8] *He will remain forever in the sight of God.*[^23] In what respect, or for what? *His mercy and truth, who will seek out for him?* He says elsewhere too: *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth, to those who seek out his covenant and his testimonies*[^24]. A great matter, concerning truth and mercy, but we promised brevity. Receive briefly what mercy and truth are; because it is no small thing that was said: *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth.* It is called mercy, because God attends not to our merits, but to his own goodness, that he might forgive us all our sins, and promise eternal life: but truth, because he does not deceive in rendering what he promised. Let us recognize it here, and let us do it: so that, as God showed us his mercy and his truth—mercy by forgiving our sins, truth by showing his promises—so we too may here do mercy and truth; mercy toward the weak, toward the needy, toward even our enemies; truth by not sinning, nor adding sin upon sin. Since he who promises himself much from the mercy of God, it creeps into his mind to make God unjust, and to think that if he remains a sinner, and is unwilling to withdraw from his iniquities, that one will come, and is going to set him there where he sets his servants who obey him. And will this be just, that he set you there, persevering in sins, where he is going to set those who have withdrawn from sins? Do you wish to be so unjust that you make God too unjust? Why, then, do you wish to turn God to your will? Do you turn yourself to the will of God. Who, then, is it that does this, except one of those few, of whom it is said: *He who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved*[^25]. Deservedly here too: *His mercy and truth, who will seek out for him?* What is, *for him*? It would suffice, *who will seek out*: why did he add, *for him*, except because many seek to learn his mercy and truth in his books; and when they have learned, they live for themselves, not for him; they seek their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's; they preach mercy and truth, and do not do mercy and truth? But by preaching it, they know it: for they would not preach it, unless they knew it. But he who loves God and Christ, in preaching his mercy and truth, will himself seek it for him, not for himself: that is, not that he himself may have temporal advantages from this preaching, but that he may profit his members, that is, his faithful, by ministering with truth what he knows; so that he who lives may live no longer for himself, but for him who died for all. *His mercy and truth, who will seek out for him?*

**10.** [v. 9] *So I will play to your name, O God, unto the age of the age, that I may pay my vows from day to day.*[^26] If you play to the name of God, do not play for a time. Do you wish to play unto the age of the age? do you wish to play forever? Pay him your vows from day to day. What is, pay him your vows from day to day? From this day to that day. Persevere in paying your vows in this day, until you come to that day: this is: *He who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved.*
