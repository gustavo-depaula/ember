# Exposition of Psalm 137

**1.** [v. 1.] I do not think you have forgotten that we commended to you—rather, reminded you—what everyone instructed in the holy Church ought to know: whence we are citizens, and where we are pilgrims, and that the cause of our pilgrimage is sin, but the gift of our return is the remission of sins and the justification of the grace of God. You have heard and you know that two cities, mingled together for the time in body, but separated in heart, run through these volumes of the ages until the end; one whose end is eternal peace, and is called Jerusalem; the other whose joy is temporal peace, and is called Babylon. You hold too the interpretations of the names, if I am not mistaken: that Jerusalem is interpreted 'Vision of peace'; Babylon, 'Confusion.' Jerusalem was held captive in Babylon, not the whole of her; for her citizens are also the angels. But as to what pertains to the men predestined to the glory of God, who are to be by adoption coheirs of Christ, whom from that very captivity he redeemed by his blood: that this portion of the city of Jerusalem is held captive in Babylon on account of sin, but begins to go out from there first in heart by the confession of iniquity and the love of justice, then afterward at the end of the age to be separated also in body, we commended in that psalm which we first here treated with your charity, which begins thus: *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion; and to you a vow will be rendered in Jerusalem*[^1]. But today we have sung: *By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat and wept, when we remembered Zion*[^2]. See that in that one it was said: *A hymn befits you, O God, in Zion;* but here: *By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat and wept, when we remembered Zion*: that Zion, where a hymn befits God.

**2.** What, then, are the rivers of Babylon, and what is our sitting and weeping in the remembrance of Zion? For if we are citizens from there, we do not only sing this, but also do it. If we are citizens of Jerusalem, that is, of Zion, and in this life, in this confusion of this world, in this Babylon, we dwell not as citizens but are held as captives; we must not only sing these things, but also do them, with a devout affection of heart, with a religious longing for the eternal city. This city too which is called Babylon has its lovers, who care for temporal peace, and hope for nothing beyond, and fix their whole joy there, end it there, and we see them labor very much for the earthly commonwealth: but even in it whoever conduct themselves faithfully, if they do not there seek pride and perishing elation and hateful boasting; but show true faith, which they can, as long as they can, to whom they can, as far as they see earthly things, and as far as they understand the form of the city; God does not let them perish in Babylon: for he predestined them as citizens of Jerusalem. God understands their captivity, and shows them another city, for which they ought truly to sigh, for which they ought to strive in all things, to the obtaining of which they ought, as far as they can, to exhort their fellow citizens who are pilgrims with them. Therefore the Lord Jesus Christ says: *He who is faithful in a little, is faithful also in much*: and again he says: *If you have not been faithful in what is another's, who will give you what is your own*[^3]?

**3.** Yet, dearly beloved, attend to the rivers of Babylon. The rivers of Babylon are all the things that are loved here and pass away. Someone, for example, has loved to practice farming itself, to grow rich from it, to occupy his mind there, to take pleasure from it; let him attend to the outcome, and see that what he loved is not the foundation of Jerusalem, but a river of Babylon. Another has said: It is a great thing to be a soldier; all farmers dread those who soldier, defer to them, tremble at them: if I am a farmer, I will fear the soldier; if I am a soldier, I will be feared by the farmer. O madman, you have cast yourself into another river of Babylon, and one more turbulent and more rapacious. You wish to be feared by a lesser man; fear a greater: he who fears you can suddenly become greater than you; but he whom you must fear will never be lesser. To be an advocate, he says, is a great thing, most powerful eloquence; to have in all matters dependents hanging on the tongue of their eloquent patron, and from his mouth hoping either for losses or gains, either death or life, either ruin or safety. You do not know where you have cast yourself: this too is another river of Babylon; and what sounds much, the noise of the water beats against the rocks. Attend that it flows, attend that it slips away; and if you attend that it flows and slips away, beware that it sweeps you off. To sail and to trade is a great thing, says another; to know many provinces, to take gains from every side, not to be beholden to any powerful man in the city, always to travel, and to feed the mind with the variety of business and nations, and to return rich with the increase of gains. This too is a river of Babylon. When will your gains stand still? when will you be free of presumption, when will you be secure from the things you acquire? The richer you are, the more fearful. By one shipwreck you will come out naked, and rightly you will lament yourself in the river of Babylon, because you were unwilling to sit and weep by the rivers of Babylon.

**4.** So the other citizens of holy Jerusalem, understanding their captivity, attend to the human wishes and the diverse desires of men, sweeping people this way and that, dragging, driving them into the sea: they see these things, and do not cast themselves into the rivers of Babylon; but they sit by the rivers of Babylon, and weep by the rivers of Babylon, either over those who are swept away, or over themselves who have deserved to be in Babylon: sitting, however, that is, humbled. *By the rivers*, then, *of Babylon, there we sat and wept, when we remembered Zion.* O holy Zion, where all stands and nothing flows! who has cast us headlong into these things? why did we abandon your Founder and your fellowship? Behold, set amid things flowing and slipping away, scarcely can anyone swept away by the river escape, if he can hold onto a piece of wood. Humbled, then, in our captivity, let us sit by the rivers of Babylon, let us not dare to cast ourselves into those rivers; nor let us dare, in the evil and sadness of our captivity, to raise ourselves up proudly, but let us sit, and so weep. Let us sit by the rivers of Babylon, not under the rivers of Babylon: let our humility be such as not to submerge us. Sit by the river, not in the river, not under the river: but yet sit humble, do not speak as in Jerusalem. For there you will stand; because of that very hope another psalm speaks, and sings, saying: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*[^4]. There you are raised up, if here, by repenting and confessing, you have humbled yourself. *In the courts*, then, *of Jerusalem our feet were standing; but by the rivers of Babylon we sat and wept, when we remembered Zion.* Thence you ought to weep, by remembering Zion.

**5.** For many weep with a Babylonian weeping, because they also rejoice with a Babylonian joy. Those who rejoice over gains and weep over losses, both are of Babylon. You ought to weep, but by remembering Zion. If you weep by remembering Zion, then even when it goes well with you according to Babylon you ought to weep. Therefore it is said in a certain psalm: *I found tribulation and sorrow, and I called upon the name of the Lord*[^5]. What does he mean by saying: *I found*? Some tribulation, as it were, to be sought, he found as though sought. And when he had found it, with what gain did he find it? He called upon the name of the Lord. It makes a great difference whether you find tribulation, or are found by tribulation. For he says in another place: *The sorrows of hell found me*[^6]. What is: *The sorrows of hell found me*? What is: *I found tribulation and sorrow*? When suddenly sadness seizes you, your worldly affairs by which you were delighted being disturbed: when that sudden sadness finds you, from a quarter where you did not think you could be made sad, it happens, and you are made sad; the sorrow of hell has found you. For you thought yourself above; yet you were below: and when the sorrow of hell found you, there you found yourself below, you who thought yourself above. For you found yourself grievously affected with sorrow, with the sadness of some evil, from which perhaps you had presumed you would not be made sad; the sorrow of hell found you. But when it goes well with you, when all worldly things smile, none of yours has died, nothing in your vineyard has perhaps either withered, or been struck by hail, or appeared barren, your cask has not turned sour, your beast has not suffered a miscarriage, you have not been dishonored in any dignity which you hold according to the world, on every side your friends both live and keep their friendship for you, dependents are not lacking, your sons obey, your slaves tremble, your wife is in accord, the house is called happy; there find tribulation, if you can, so that, having found tribulation, you may call upon the name of the Lord. The divine word seems to teach something perverse, to weep in gladness, and to be glad in grief. Hear one glad in grief: *We glory*, he says, *in tribulations*[^7]. But one weeping in gladness, see whether he has found tribulation. Let each one attend to his very happiness, in which his soul has exulted, and in a certain way swelled with joy, and lifted itself up, and said: I am happy: let him attend whether that happiness does not flow away, whether he can be sure of it that it abides forever. But if he is not sure, and sees that what he rejoices over flows away; it is a river of Babylon: let him sit above it, and weep. But he will sit and weep, if he has remembered Zion. O that peace which we will see with God! O that holy equality of the angels! O that vision and beautiful spectacle! Behold, in Babylon the things that hold us are beautiful: let them not hold us, let them not deceive us. One thing is the solace of captives, another the joy of the free. *By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat and wept, when we remembered Zion.*

**6.** [v. 2.] *On the willows in the midst of it we hung up our instruments*[^8]. The citizens of Jerusalem have their instruments, the Scriptures of God, the precepts of God, the promises of God, a certain meditation on the world to come; but when they dwell in the midst of Babylon, they hang up their instruments on her willows. Willows are unfruitful trees; and they are set here in such a way that nothing good can be understood of the willows; though elsewhere perhaps something can. Now understand barren trees, growing by the rivers of Babylon. These trees are watered by the rivers of Babylon, and bear no fruit. As there are men greedy, avaricious, barren of good work; so the citizens of Babylon, being even the trees of that region, are fed by these pleasures of passing things, as though watered by the rivers of Babylon: you seek fruit, and nowhere find it. When we endure such men, we are conversant with those who are in the midst of Babylon. For there is much difference between the midst of Babylon and the outer parts of Babylon. There are those who are not in the midst of it, that is, are not overwhelmed by so great a desire of the world and by its delights. But those who, to say it plainly and briefly, are very much evil, are in the midst of Babylon, and barren trees, like the willows of Babylon. When we see them, and find them so barren that it is difficult for us to see in them anything by which they could be led to right faith, or to good works, or to the hope of the world to come, or to the desire of liberation from the captivity of mortality; we know the Scriptures which we might tell them: but because we find no fruit in them from which we might begin, we turn our face away from them, and say: These men do not yet have understanding, do not grasp; whatever we tell them, they will take amiss and adversely. So by deferring the Scriptures concerning them, we hang up our instruments on the willows: for we do not consider them worthy to carry our instruments. So we do not, by inserting them, fasten our instruments on them, but by deferring, we hang them up. For the willows of Babylon are unfruitful trees, fed by temporal pleasures, as though by the rivers of Babylon.

**7.** [v. 3.] And see whether the Psalm does not follow with this: *On the willows*, he says, *in the midst of it we hung up our instruments. For there those who led us captive asked of us the words of songs; and those who carried us off, a hymn*[^9]: you supply, *asked of us.* The words of songs and a hymn asked of us those who led us captive. Who led us captive, brothers? whom have we felt at some time to be our captors? That Jerusalem indeed felt the Babylonians, the Persians, the Chaldeans, and the men of those nations and regions to be her captors; and this afterward, not when these psalms were being sung. But we have already told your charity that all the things which according to the letter happened in that city were our figures: and it can easily be shown that we are captives. For we do not yet breathe in the airs of that freedom; for we do not yet enjoy the purity of truth, and that wisdom which, abiding in itself, renews all things[^10]. We are tempted by the delights of temporal things, and daily we wrestle with the suggestions of unlawful pleasures: scarcely do we breathe even in prayer; we understand that we are captives. But who led us captive? what men? what nation? what king? If we are redeemed, we were captives. Who redeemed us? Christ. From whom did he redeem us? From the devil. So the devil and his angels led us captive; nor would they lead us, unless we consented. We were led captive. I have said who our captors are. They are also the robbers wounding that traveler who was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho; whom, wounded and half-dead, they left[^11]. Him our guardian, that is, the Samaritan (for 'Samaritan' is interpreted 'Guardian'), to whom it was objected by the Jews and said: *Do we not say truly that you are a Samaritan, and have a demon?*[^12] and of the two objections he rejected one, and held one: *I*, he said, *do not have a demon*[^13]; but he did not say: I am not a Samaritan: for if that Samaritan does not guard, surely we perish: the Samaritan, then, passing by, saw the wounded and injured man left by the robbers, and, as you know, gathered him up. As sometimes by the name of robbers are set down those who inflicted on us the wounds of sins, now by the consent of our captivity; so they are also called our captors.

**8.** These, then, *who led us captive*, the devil and his angels, when did they say to us, and when did they *ask of us the words of songs*? What, then, do we understand? That when such men question us, in whom the devil works, he himself is to be understood to question us, who works in them. The apostle says: *And you, when you were dead in your offenses and sins, when you lived in them according to the world of this age, according to the prince of the power of this air, the spirit who now works in the sons of unbelief, among whom we too*, he says, *were once conversant*[^14]. He shows that, being redeemed, he had now begun to go out from Babylon. But yet what does he still say? That we contend with our enemies. And lest we be angry at the men who bring persecutions upon us, the apostle took our attention away from the hatred of men, and directed it to a struggle with certain spirits, whom we do not see, and with whom we contend. For he says: *Your wrestling is not against flesh and blood*; that is, against the men whom you see, by whom you seem to suffer evils, who bring persecutions upon you: for we are commanded to pray for them. *Your wrestling is not*, he says, *against flesh and blood*, that is, against men; *but against the principalities, and powers, and rulers of this world of darkness*[^15]. What did he say, *of this world*? Of the lovers of the world. These he called also darkness, that is, the wicked, the criminal, the unbelieving, the sinners; to whom, now believing, he gives congratulation in this way, so as to say: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^16]. So he established that we have a wrestling with those principalities; they led us captive.

**9.** But just as the devil entered into the heart of Judas, that he might betray the Lord[^17], but would not enter unless he gave him place; so many evil men of the midst of Babylon, by carnal and unlawful desires giving place to the devil and his angels in their hearts, that he may work in them and through them, sometimes question us, and say to us: Expound to us your reasoning. Many pagans say these things to us: Expound the reasoning, why Christ came, and what Christ profited the human race? Are not things in human affairs worse since Christ came than they were before, and were human affairs more prosperous then than now? Let the Christians tell us what good Christ brought? whence do they think human affairs more prosperous, because Christ came? For you see: if the theaters and amphitheaters and circuses stood unharmed, if nothing fell of Babylon, if there were an abundance of pleasures flowing around for men about to sing and dance to base songs, if the lust of the fornicators and harlots had quiet and security, if he did not fear hunger in his house who shouts that the mime-actors be clothed; if all these things flowed on without ruin, without any disturbance, and there were great security of trifles, then the times would be happy, and Christ would have brought great happiness to human affairs. But because iniquities are cut down, that, cupidity being uprooted, the love of Jerusalem may be planted; because bitternesses are mixed into the temporal life, that the eternal may be desired; because men are instructed by scourges, receiving a fatherly discipline, lest they find a judiciary sentence; Christ brought no good, and Christ brought labors. And you begin to tell a man how great goods Christ did, and he does not grasp it. For you set before him those who do what you have just now heard in the Gospel, sell all their things and give to the poor, that they may have treasure in heaven and follow the Lord[^18]: you say to him: Behold what Christ brought. How many do these things, distributing their goods to the needy, and becoming poor, not by necessity, but by will, following God, hoping for the kingdom of heaven! These men mock as fools: And such goods, they say, did Christ bring, that a man should lose his goods, and, when he gives to the needy, remain needy himself? What, then, will you do? You do not grasp the goods of Christ: for another has filled you, who is the adversary of Christ, to whom you have given place in your heart. You look back to former times, and as it were the old times seem to you to have been happier, which were as olives hanging on the tree, the winds blowing, as it were enjoying a certain freedom of the breeze in a certain wandering desire of their own. The time came for the olive to be put into the press: for it was not always to hang on the tree: now the year is at its end. Not without cause are certain psalms inscribed: *For the presses*[^19]: on the tree freedom, in the press pressure. For when human affairs are crushed and pressed, you notice that avarice grows; notice that continence too grows. Why are you so blind that you see the dregs flowing through the streets, but do not see the oil in the vats? And this not without cause. For those who behave badly are publicly known; but those who turn to God, and are purified from the filth of evil desires, are hidden: because even in the press, or from the press, the dregs flow openly, the oil is strained out secretly.

**10.** You acclaim these things, and rejoice at these things; because you can now sit by the rivers of Babylon, and weep. But those who led us captive, when they enter into the hearts of men, and question us through the tongues of those whom they possess, and say to us: *Sing to us the words of songs;* render to us the reasoning of the coming of Christ, and what is the other life. I wish to believe; teach me the reasoning why you command me to believe. I answer him and say: O man, how do you not wish me to command you to believe? You are full of evil desires: if I tell those goods of Jerusalem, you do not grasp them; you must be emptied of that with which you are full, that you may be able to be filled with that of which you are empty. So do not readily tell this man anything; he is a willow tree, he is a barren tree: do not strike the instrument that it may sound, but rather hang it up. But he will say: Tell me, sing to me, render me the reasoning. Do you not wish, he says, that I learn? You do not hear with a good mind, you do not knock in such a way that it is fitting it be opened to you. He has filled you who led me captive, he himself questions me about you. He is cunning, he seeks deceitfully: he does not seek what he may learn, but what he may reprove. So I will not tell, I will hang up my instrument.

**11.** [v. 4.] But what will he still say? *Sing to us the words of songs, sing to us a hymn, sing to us of the songs of Zion.* What do we answer? Babylon carries you, Babylon contains you, Babylon nourishes you, Babylon speaks through you; you do not know how to grasp anything but what shines for a time, you do not know how to meditate on things eternal, you do not grasp what you ask. *How shall we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land*[^20]? Truly, brothers, so it is. Begin to wish to preach the truth, however little you know, and see how necessary it is that you suffer such mockers, exactors of the truth, full of falsehood. Answer those who exact of you what they cannot grasp, and say from the confidence of your holy song: *How shall we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land?*

**12.** [v. 5.] But see how you conduct yourself among them, O people of God, O body of Christ, O noble pilgrimage (you are not from here, you are from elsewhere); lest, when those who say to you: *Sing to us the words of songs, tell us a hymn, sing to us of the songs of Zion*, are as it were loved by you, and you seek their friendships, and fear to displease such men; Babylon begin to delight you, and you forget Jerusalem. Fearing this, then, see what he subjoins, see what follows. For this man who was singing suffered; and this man is we, if we will: he suffered men everywhere questioning such things and flattering with adulation, biting in their reproof, falsely praising, exacting what they do not grasp, unwilling to pour out that with which they are full; and amid crowds of such men, as though in peril, he raised his mind to the remembrance of Zion, and such a soul bound itself by a certain oath, and said: *If I forget you, O Jerusalem;* amid the words of the captors, amid the words of the deceitful, amid the words of those who question evilly, who seek and are unwilling to learn.

**13.** Behold, from there was that rich man who questioned the Lord: *Good Master, what shall I do to obtain eternal life*[^21]? Did he not, asking about eternal life, as it were ask for a song of Zion? *Keep the commandments*, the Lord said to him. And he, out of haughtiness, when he had heard: *I have already fulfilled all these*, he said, *from my youth*[^22]. And the Lord told him something of the songs of Zion, and he knew that he would not grasp it; but he gave us an example, how many ask as it were for counsel toward eternal life, and praise us so long as we answer what they ask. He gave a lesson from him, as though we were to say sometime to such men: *How shall we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land?* Behold he says: *Do you wish to be perfect? Go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^23]. That he may learn many songs of the songs of Zion, let him first cast off his hindrances, walk unencumbered, lest he be weighed down by any burden; and he will learn something of the songs of Zion. But that man went away sad. Let us say after him: *How shall we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land?* And he indeed went away; yet the Lord gave hope to the rich. For the disciples, made sad, said: *Who then can be saved?* And he answered them: *What is impossible for men, is easy for God*[^24]. For the rich too have a certain way of their own, and they have received a song of Zion, a song of which the apostle says: *Command the rich of this world not to be proud-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, who provides us all things abundantly to enjoy*. Adding what they ought to do, he now touches the instrument, he does not hang it up: *Let them be rich in good works, let them give readily, share, lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^25]. This song of the songs of Zion the rich have received: first, *not to be proud-minded.* For riches exalt; and those whom they exalt, those rivers carry off. What, then, is commanded these men? Before all things, *not to be proud-minded.* What riches do, this let them beware in riches; let them beware pride in riches. For that itself is the evil which riches especially bring to incautious men. For gold is not evil, which God created; but the avaricious man is evil, abandoning the Creator, turned to the creature. So let him beware this there, lest he be proud, and sit by the river of Babylon. For this was said to him: *Not to be proud-minded*: so let him sit. *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*: so let him sit by the rivers of Babylon. For if he hoped in the uncertainty of riches, he is dragged off by the river of Babylon. But if he has humbled himself, and not been proud, and not hoped in the uncertainty of riches, he sits by the river, sighs for the eternal Jerusalem, remembering Zion; and that he may come to Zion, he gives out. Behold you have the song which the rich have received of the songs of Zion. Let them work, let them touch the instrument, let them not be idle, when they have found a man saying to them: What is this you do? you lose your goods by giving out so much; lay up treasure for your sons. When they have seen that he does not grasp it, and understood that he is a willow tree, let them not readily tell why they act, and what they do; let them hang up their instruments on the willows of Babylon. But apart from the willows let them sing, let them not cease, let them work. For they do not lose what they give out. They entrust it to a servant, and it is safe; do they entrust it to Christ, and it perishes?

**14.** You have heard the song of the rich of the songs of Zion; hear too the song of the poor. The same Paul speaks: *We brought nothing into this world, but neither can we take anything away: having food and covering, with these let us be content. For those who wish to become rich fall into temptation and many desires, foolish and harmful, which plunge a man into ruin and destruction.* These are the rivers of Babylon. *For the root of all evils is avarice; which some coveting have made shipwreck of the faith, and have entangled themselves in many sorrows*[^26]. Are these songs, then, contrary? They are not contrary. See what was said to the rich: *Not to be proud-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*, to do good works, to give, to lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future. But to the poor what was said? *Those who wish to become rich fall into temptation.* He did not say, those who are rich; but: *Those who wish to become rich.* For if they were already rich, they would hear the other song: the rich man hears that he may give out, the poor man hears that he may not desire.

**15.** [v. 5.] But when you are conversant among such men, who do not grasp the song of Zion, hang up, as I said, your instruments on the willows in the midst of it; defer what you are about to say. They begin to be fruitful trees, the trees are changed, and they will have good fruit: then it is now permitted to us to sing in the ears of those who hear. But when you are conversant among these clamorers, questioning evilly, resisting the truth, bind yourselves not to wish to please them, lest you forget Jerusalem; and let your one soul say, made one out of many by the peace of Christ, let that very captive Jerusalem say, acting here on earth: *If I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand be forgotten*[^27]. He bound himself vehemently, my brothers: *Let my right hand be forgotten;* he obligated himself dreadfully. Our right hand is eternal life, our left hand is temporal life. Whatever you do for the sake of eternal life, the right hand works. If into the love of eternal life in your works you mix the desire of temporal life, or of human praise, or of some worldly advantage; your left hand has known what your right hand does. And you know it is in the precept of the Gospel: *Let not your left hand know what your right hand does*[^28]. *If*, then, he says, *I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand be forgotten.* And truly so it happens: he foretold, he did not wish it: To such men who forget Jerusalem, this happens which he said; their right hand forgets them: for eternal life abides in itself; they remain in temporal delight, and make for themselves a right hand of what is a left hand.

**16.** Attend to these things, brothers; on account of the right hand let me instill this in you, as far as the Lord grants, for the salvation of all. You remember perhaps that I once treated here of certain men who make what is a left hand into a right hand; that is, who have more temporal goods, and place blessedness in them, not knowing what true happiness is, the true right hand. These Scripture calls foreign sons, as it were citizens not of Jerusalem, but of Babylon: for the psalm says in a certain place: *O Lord, free me from the hand of foreign sons, whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* For it follows and says: *Whose sons are like new plants established; their daughters adorned like the likeness of a temple; their storerooms full, overflowing from this into that; their sheep fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth; their oxen fat: there is no breach of the fence, nor outcry in their streets*[^29]. Is it, then, sin to have this happiness? No; but to make that a right hand, when it is a left hand. Therefore, going on, what does he say? *They have called blessed the people that has these things.* Behold whence their mouth has spoken vanity, because they have called blessed the people that has these things. You indeed are a citizen of Jerusalem, who do not forget Jerusalem, lest your right hand forget you; behold those who have spoken vanity have called blessed the people that has these things: tell me a song of Zion. *Blessed*, he says, *is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^30]. Question your hearts, brothers, whether you desire the goods of God, whether you desire that city Jerusalem, whether you covet eternal life. Let all this earthly happiness be a left hand to you; let that be your right hand which you will have forever: and if you have a left hand, do not presume upon the left hand. Do you not rebuke one who wishes to eat with the left hand? If you think an injury is done to your table when a guest eats with the left hand; how is an injury not done to the table of God, if you make what is a right hand into a left hand, and what is a left hand into a right hand? What then? *If I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand be forgotten.*

**17.** [v. 6.] *Let my tongue cleave to my jaws, unless I remember you*[^31]. That is, let me be struck dumb, he says, unless I remember you. For why does he speak, why does he make a sound, who does not sound the songs of Zion? That is our tongue, the song of Jerusalem. The song of the love of this world is a foreign tongue, a barbarous tongue, which we learned in captivity. So he will be dumb to God, who has forgotten Jerusalem. And it is too little to remember; for the enemies too remembered her, wishing to overthrow her. What is this city, they say? who are the Christians? and what kind are the Christians? O that there were no Christians! The captive crowd has already conquered its captors, and yet they murmur, and rage, and wish to slay among themselves the holy city on pilgrimage: just as Pharaoh wished to slay that very people, when he killed the males that were born, and left the females; he choked the virtues, he nourished the desires. So it is too little to remember: see how you remember. For some things we remember with hatred, some with love. So when he had said: *If I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand be forgotten. Let my tongue cleave to my jaws, unless I remember you;* he at once added: *If I do not set Jerusalem before me at the beginning of my joy.* For there is the highest joy, where we enjoy God to the full, where we are secure of a cohering brotherhood and a citizen fellowship. No tempter there will violate us, no one will be able to strike us toward any enticement, nothing there will delight us but the good. There all need will die, there the highest happiness will arise. *If I do not set Jerusalem before me at the beginning of my joy.*

**18.** [v. 7.] And he turns himself to the Lord against the enemies of that city: *Remember, O Lord, the sons of Edom*[^32]. Edom is the same who is also Esau: for you heard just now, when the apostle was read: *Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*[^33]. There were two sons in one womb, both twins, in the womb of Rebecca, sons of Isaac, grandsons of Abraham: both were born; one for the inheritance, the other for disinheritance. But this Esau was the enemy of his brother, because the younger son got ahead of his blessing; and it was fulfilled: *The greater will serve the lesser*[^34]. Who, then, is the greater, and who is the lesser, and who is the greater who will serve the lesser, we now understand. The people of the Jews seemed the greater, the people of the Christians the lesser in time. And see how the greater serves the lesser. They carry our books; we live by their books. But that you may understand in general, brothers, the greater and the lesser; the carnal man is called the greater, and the spiritual man is called the lesser; because first is the carnal, and afterward the spiritual. You have the apostle plainly saying: *The first man, of the earth, earthly; the second man, of heaven, heavenly. As the earthly, such also the earthly; and as the heavenly, such also the heavenly. As we have borne the image of the earthly, let us bear also the image of him who is of heaven*[^35]. But there above he had said: *But not first what is spiritual, but what is animal; afterward, the spiritual*[^36]. And he calls animal the same one whom he shows to be carnal. The man who is born begins to be animal, begins to be carnal. If he turns himself from the captivity of Babylon to the return to Jerusalem, he is renewed, and there comes about a certain renewal according to the new and inner man; and he becomes lesser in time, greater in power. So Esau is all the carnal, but Jacob all the spiritual: the lesser are the elect, the greater those reprobate. Does Esau too wish to be chosen? Let him become lesser. But he was called Edom from a certain red food of lentils, that is, a certain rosy food. The lentil was well cooked, mashed; this Esau coveted from his brother Jacob, and yielded to him his primacy, conquered by the desire of eating that lentil[^37]. But he yielded the food of pleasure, and took the honor of dignity. So by a certain agreement between them it came about that the one is greater, the other lesser, so that the greater might serve the lesser; and he was called Edom. But it is interpreted, as far as those say who knew that tongue, Edom, 'Blood': for in Punic too Edom is said to mean 'blood.' Do not wonder: to the blood belong all the carnal. *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*[^38]. There Edom does not belong; there belongs Jacob, who lacked the carnal food, and took the spiritual honor. But that one became an enemy. All the carnal are enemies of the spiritual: all such, coveting present things, persecute those whom they see meditating on things eternal. Against these, this man, looking toward Jerusalem, and asking God that he be rescued from captivity, what does he say? *Remember, O Lord, the sons of Edom.* Free us from the carnal, from those who imitate that Edom, who are the elder brothers, but are enemies. They were born first; but those born later have conquered for the primacy: because the desire of the flesh cast them down; the despising of desire raised these up. They live, and envy, and persecute.

**19.** *Remember, O Lord, the sons of Edom, in the day of Jerusalem*[^39]. The day of Jerusalem in which she labored, in which she was led captive; or that day of the happiness of Jerusalem, in which she is freed, in which she arrives, in which she is joined to eternity? *Remember*, he says, *O Lord*, do not forget, *the sons of Edom.* Of whom? *Of those saying: Empty it out, empty it out, even to the foundation in her.* Remember, then, that day, when they wished to overthrow Jerusalem. For how many persecutions the Church has suffered! How the sons of Edom, that is, carnal men, subject to the devil and his angels, worshipping stones and wood, following the desires of the flesh, were saying: Extinguish the Christians, take away the Christians, let not one remain; overthrow them even to the foundation! Were not these things said? And when these things were said, the persecutors were reproved, the martyrs were crowned. *Of those saying: Empty it out, empty it out, even to the foundation in her.* The sons of Edom say: *Empty it out, empty it out;* and God says: Serve. Whose words can prevail, except God's, who said: *Because the greater will serve the lesser*? *Empty it out, empty it out, even to the foundation in her.*

**20.** [v. 8.] And he turns himself to her: *Unhappy daughter of Babylon*[^40]. Unhappy in your very exultation, in your very presumption, and in your enmities: *unhappy daughter of Babylon.* The city itself is called Babylon and daughter of Babylon: just as there is Jerusalem and daughter of Jerusalem, and just as there is Zion and daughter of Zion, by this manner the Church is called both Church and daughter of the Church. On account of succession she is called daughter, on account of precedence she is called mother. There was a first Babylon; did the same people remain in it? By the succession of Babylon there was made a daughter of Babylon. O *unhappy daughter of Babylon*, you! *Blessed is he who will repay you your recompense.* Unhappy you, blessed he.

**21.** [v. 9.] For what have you done, and what will be repaid to you? Hear: *Blessed is he who will repay you your recompense which you repaid to us.* What recompense does he mean? For this the Psalm closes with: *Blessed is he who will hold and dash your little ones against the rock*[^41]. He calls her unhappy, but blessed the one who will repay her the recompense which she repaid to us. We ask, what recompense: *Blessed*, he says, *is he who will hold and dash your little ones against the rock.* That is the recompense. For what did this Babylon do to us? We have already sung in that psalm: *The words of the wicked have prevailed against us*[^42]. For when we were born, this confusion of this world found us little ones; and it choked us, while still infants, with the vain opinions of various errors. An infant is born who will be a citizen of Jerusalem, and in the predestination of God is already a citizen, but for the time held captive; when he learns to love, what is it but what his parents whisper to him? They instruct and teach him avarice, plunder, daily lies, the various cults of idols and demons, the unlawful remedies of charms and amulets. What will the infant yet do, a tender soul, attending to what the grown-ups do, except to follow what he has seen them do? So Babylon persecuted us, the little ones; but God gave us the knowledge of himself when we were now grown up, that we might not follow the errors of our parents. Which I mentioned then too as foretold by the prophet: *To you the nations will come from the end of the earth, and will say: Truly our fathers worshipped a lie, a vanity which did not profit them*[^43]. Now, as young men, they say what, as little ones, they had been slain by following those very vanities: but by casting away the vanities, and coming to life again toward God, let them make progress, and repay Babylon. What recompense will they repay her? The one she repaid us. Let her little ones in turn be choked; rather, let her little ones in turn be dashed, and die. Who are the little ones of Babylon? The evil desires as they are born. For there are those who struggle with an old desire. When a desire is born, before evil habit gives it strength against you, while the desire is little, by no means let it receive the strength of evil habit; while it is little, dash it. But you fear lest, though dashed, it does not die; dash it *against the rock*: *but the rock was Christ*[^44].

**22.** Brothers, let the instruments not cease in working; sing to one another the songs of Zion. As gladly as you have heard, so the more gladly do what you have heard; if you do not wish to be the willows of Babylon, fed from her rivers, and bearing no fruit. But sigh for the eternal Jerusalem; where your hope goes before, let your life follow: there we will be with Christ. Christ is now our head; he governs us now from above: he will embrace us with himself in that city; we will be equal to the angels of God. We would not dare to suspect this of ourselves, unless Truth promised it. This, then, covet, brothers, this think on day and night. Whatever of the world smiles happily, do not presume upon it: do not gladly converse with your desires. The enemy is greater, let him be slain against the rock: the enemy is small, let him be dashed against the rock. Both slay the greater against the rock, and dash the lesser against the rock. Let the rock conquer. On the rock be built, if you do not wish to be carried off either by the river, or by the winds, or by the rain. If you wish to be armed against temptations in the world, let the desire of the eternal Jerusalem grow and be strengthened in your hearts. The captivity will pass, happiness will come, the last enemy will be condemned, and with the King, without death, we will triumph.
