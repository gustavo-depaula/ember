# Exposition of Psalm 48 (2)

**1.** [v. 14] Yesterday, the psalm being begun, an end was owed, as your Charity remembers. But we had come even to that verse where the Spirit of God designates men attending to nothing but present worldly and earthly things, and thinking of nothing for the future after this life, nor judging any felicity to be except the riches and honors of this world, and a transitory power; but after their death attending to nothing except how pompous funerals may be procured for them, and they may be buried in monuments built with marvelous work, and their names may be called upon in their lands by their houses; but not procuring for themselves where the spirit may be after this life, fools not trembling at the voice of Christ, saying: *Fool, this night your soul shall be taken from you, and the things you have prepared, whose shall they be?*[^1] nor attending, after the daily splendid feasts, and the purple and fine linen, to the rich man condemned to torments in hell; and after labors and sores and hunger, to the poor man resting in the bosom of Abraham: not caring for these things, but attending to what is present, and after this death not providing, except how their name, which is reproved in heaven, may be called upon on earth. Describing such men, then, the Holy Spirit says: *This way of theirs is a scandal to them, and afterward they will bless in their own mouth.* As the Lord Jesus Christ said of certain ones, who first come to faith cleansed by the word of God and by exorcisms in the name of Christ, that they may receive the grace of God, that they may be baptized, and afterward returning to worse evils than they had committed before: *The last things shall become for them worse than the first*[^2]: this Peter the apostle says: but the Lord: *And the last things,* he says, *of that man shall be worse than the first were*[^3]. Why? Because first he was either an open pagan, afterward he is cloaked with the Christian name, hidden under the veil of religion, a bad man. And he will be worse, because he is hidden, as he says: *And afterward they will bless in their own mouth*: that is, you hear the name of God and the name of Christ in the lips, in the heart you do not find it. Of whom it was said: *This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me*[^4]. Thus far, then, the psalm had been treated.

**2.** [v. 15] Then the verses to be examined and treated today begin thus: *Like sheep set in hell, death is shepherd to them*[^5]. To whom? To those whose way is a scandal to them. To whom? To those who attend to present things alone, while they do not think of future things; to those who do not think there is a life except this one, which is to be called death. Not undeservedly, then, like sheep in hell, they have death for shepherd. What is, they have death for shepherd? Is death some thing, or some power? Death surely is either the separation of soul from body: and that indeed which men fear, is the separation of soul from body: but the true death which men do not fear, is the separation of soul from God. And for the most part, when men fear that one which separates soul from body, they fall into that one where the soul is separated from God. This, then, is death. But how is *death shepherd to them*? If Christ is life, the devil is death. But we have in many places of Scripture that Christ is life. But the devil is death; not because he himself is death, but because through him death. For whether that one in which Adam fell, was administered to man by his persuasion, or this one in which the soul is separated from the body, they have for author him who, first falling through pride, envied the one standing, and by invisible death cast down the one standing, so that he should pay even the visible death. Those pertaining to him, have death for shepherd: but we who think of the future immortality, and not without cause carry on our forehead the sign of the cross of Christ, have no shepherd but life. The shepherd of the unfaithful is death, the shepherd of the faithful is life. If, then, the sheep whose shepherd is death are in hell, in heaven are the sheep whose shepherd is life. What then? Are we now in heaven? In heaven according to faith. For if not in heaven, where: The heart on high? If not in heaven, whence does the apostle Paul say: *For our conversation is in the heavens*[^6]? In body we walk on earth, in heart we dwell in heaven. We dwell there, if we send there something which holds us there. For no one dwells with the heart, except whence he thinks: but he thinks thence, where he treasures up. He treasured up on earth, his heart does not withdraw from earth; he treasured up in heaven, his heart does not come down from heaven: the Lord openly saying: *Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also*[^7].

**3.** These, then, whose shepherd is death, seem to flourish for a time, and the just to labor: but why? Because it is still night. What is, it is night? The merits of the just do not appear, and the felicity of the ungodly is as it were named. So long does the grass seem more cheerful than the tree, as long as it is winter. For the grass through winter is vigorous, the tree through winter is as it were dry: but when the sun has come forth more fervent in summer time, the tree which through winter seemed dry, is filled with leaves, brings forth fruits; but the grass withers: you will see the honor of the tree, the grass is dry. So now too the just labor, before the summer comes. There is life in the root, it does not yet appear in the branches. But our root is love. And what does the Apostle say? That we ought to have our root on high, that life may be our shepherd, because our dwelling ought not to withdraw from heaven, because in this earth we ought to walk as dead: so that, living above, below we may be dead; not that, dead above, below we may live. Because, then, our life and our heart ought not to withdraw from above, what does the Apostle say? *For you are dead*: and lest you should fear: *Your life is hidden,* he says, *with Christ in God.* Behold where our root is. But when our honor will appear as in leaves and fruits, it follows and says: *When Christ shall appear, your life, then you also shall appear with him in glory*[^8]. And it will be morning. For now it is not morning. Let the proud and the rich of this world now swell, let the ungodly insult the good, the unfaithful the faithful, and say: What does it profit you that you have believed? what more do you have, because you have Christ? Let the faithful answer, if they are truly faithful: It is night, what we hold is not yet seen. Let not the hands rest in good works. Whence elsewhere it is said: *In the day of my tribulation I sought out God with my hands by night before him, and I was not deceived*[^9]. Our labor will appear in the morning, and there will be fruit in the morning: so that those who now labor, may afterward rule; and those who now boast and grow proud, may afterward be subjected. For what follows? *Like sheep set in hell, death is shepherd to them. And the upright shall rule over them in the morning.*

**4.** I think this verse is now plain, because we spoke beforehand: *The upright shall rule over them in the morning*[^10]. Endure the night, desire the morning. Do not think that the night has life, and the morning does not have life. Does he, then, who sleeps live, and he who rises not live? Is not he who sleeps more like death? And who are they who sleep? Those whom the apostle Paul rouses, if at least they wish to wake. For to certain ones he says: *Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you*[^11]. Those, then, who are enlightened by Christ, now wake, but the fruit of the watching does not yet appear: it will appear in the morning, that is, when the uncertain things of this world have passed. For this is the night: do they not seem to you as it were darkness? He does evil; he lives, flourishes, terrifies, is honored: he does good; he is rebuked, blasphemed, accused, labors, is terrified: it is as it were darkness. But in the root there is vigor, fruit, abundance: life is not yet in the branches, but the root has not dried up: it is like one withering; but the time comes, it is clothed with its honor, made fruitful with its fruits. Then those of whom it was said that we should not envy them: for what does the psalm say of them? *For like grass they shall quickly wither, and like the herbs of the meadow they shall quickly fall*[^12]: they shall fall, when they shall see the saints at the right, who were insulted while laboring, and they shall say within themselves, doing penance, but late and fruitless penance. Those who were unwilling now to do fruitful penance, will then do fruitless penance. What, then, will they say now in fruitless penance? *These are they whom once we had in derision, and as a likeness of reproach.* I speak the words of the book of Wisdom: those recognize them who are wont to hear them. For these are the future words of the bad, when they shall see the judge, and now all the faithful at his right hand, and all his saints judging with him: this they have to say, their words Scripture speaks: *These are they whom once we had in derision, and as a likeness of reproach; we senseless ones esteemed their life madness*[^13]. For when each one begins to live to God, to despise the world, to be unwilling to avenge his own injuries, to be unwilling to have riches here, not to seek earthly felicity here, to despise all things, to think only of the Lord, not to forsake the way of Christ, not only by pagans is it said: He is mad; but, what is more to be grieved, because even within many sleep and are unwilling to wake, by their own, by Christians, he hears: What do you suffer? My brothers, to a man living according to the way of Christ, who says: What do you suffer? what do we think he says? We shudder at the Jews, because they said to the Lord Jesus Christ: *You have a demon*: and when we hear the Gospel recited, we beat our breasts. The Jews said a wicked thing to Christ. *You have a demon*: act, you, Christian, when you see the devil expelled from a man's heart, and Christ dwelling there, and you say: What do you suffer? does he seem to you to have a demon? It was said of the Lord himself too that he was mad, when he spoke words which they could not grasp; it was said: *He is mad, he has a demon*: and yet some woke from sleep, and said: *These are not the words of one having a demon*[^14]. So now too, brothers, as long as both the nations, and those who inhabit the world, and the earth-born, and the sons of men, and the rich and the poor, that is, both those who pertain to Adam, and those who pertain to Christ, hear these words, some say: *He has a demon;* others say: *These are not the words of one having a demon.* For some hold the way of the world, and hear these things for a time; others do not hear without cause, but do what was said: *Give ear, you who inhabit the world.* And when they do these things, the fruit is uncertain. But those who do badly, and choose the way of the world, *death is shepherd to them*: but those who choose the way of God, life is shepherd to them. That very life will come about to judge, and to damn with their shepherd those to whom it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels.* But those who were insulted, and who were derided because they believed, will hear from that very life which they have as shepherd: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^15]. *The upright, then, shall rule over them,* not now, but *in the morning.* Let no one say: Why am I a Christian? I command no one, I would command the iniquitous. Do not hasten: you will rule, but in the morning. *And their help shall grow old in hell from their glory.* Now they have glory; in hell they shall grow old. What is *their help*? The help from money, the help from friends, the help from their own power. But when a man has died, in that day all his thoughts shall perish[^16]. However much glory he seemed to have among men, while he lived, so much oldness and corruption of punishments shall he have, when he has died, among the dead.

**5.** [v. 16] *But yet God will redeem my soul*[^17]. See the voice of one hoping about the future: *But yet God will redeem my soul.* Perhaps it is still the voice of one wishing to be freed from oppression. Someone is in prison, he says, God will redeem my soul: someone in chains, God will redeem my soul: someone suffers peril in the sea, is tossed by waves and raging tempests, what does he say? God will redeem my soul. They wish to be freed for this life. This is not the voice of this man. Hear what follows: *God will redeem my soul from the hand of hell, when he shall have received me.* He means that redemption, which Christ has now shown in himself. For he descended to hell, and ascended into heaven. What we saw in the head, we find in the body. For what we believe in the head, those who saw it announced to us; and through them we saw, because we are all one body. But are those better who saw, and we worse to whom it was announced? Life itself does not say this, our shepherd himself. For he insults a certain disciple of his doubting, and desiring to touch the scars: and when he had touched the scars, and had cried out, saying: *My Lord and my God;* seeing the doubting disciple, and beholding the whole world about to believe: *Because you have seen,* he says, *you have believed: blessed are those who do not see, and believe*[^18]. *But yet God will redeem my soul from the hand of hell, when he shall have received me.* What here, then? Labor, oppression, tribulation, temptation: hope nothing else. Where is joy? in hope of the future. For the Apostle says: *Always rejoicing.* In such great tribulations, always rejoicing, always sad: always rejoicing, because he himself said: *As sad, but always rejoicing.* Our sadness has its *as;* our joy does not have *as,* because it is in sure hope. Why does our sadness have its *as*? Because it will pass like a dream, *and the upright shall rule in the morning.* For your Charity knows, that he who tells a dream, adds *as if.* As if I was sitting, as if I was speaking, as if I was dining, as if I was riding, as if I was disputing. All *as if;* because when one wakes, he does not find what he saw. As if I had found a treasure, says the beggar. If it were not *as if,* he would not be a beggar: but because it was *as if,* he is a beggar. And so now those who to worldly joys open their eyes, and shut their heart, their *as if* passes, and comes their true. The *as if* of these is the felicity of the world, their true is punishment. But our *as if* is sadness; our joy is not *as if.* For the Apostle does not say: As if rejoicing, but always sad; or: As if sad, and as if rejoicing: but he says: *As sad, but always rejoicing. As needy*: and there too *as,* for *as if,* he set *But enriching many.* And when he said this, the Apostle had nothing: he had dismissed all his own, possessed no riches. And what does he say, following? *As having nothing*: and that very having nothing was the apostle's *as if.* *And possessing all things*[^19]: there he did not say *as if.* As if he was needy; not as if, but truly he enriched many. As if he had nothing: not as if, but truly he possessed all things. Whence did he truly possess all things? Because he clung to the Creator of all things. *But yet,* he says, *God will redeem my soul from the hand of hell, when he shall have received me.*

**6.** [v. 17] What, then, of those who wish to flourish here? You will see a man, bad, flourishing, and perhaps your feet will totter, and you will say in your soul, God, I know the deeds of this man, what crimes this man has done, and behold he flourishes, terrifies, dominates, is exalted, his head does not ache, nothing in his house has been diminished: and you will fear, because you have believed, and perhaps your heart says: Wretched me! I think I have believed without cause, God does not regard human affairs. God, then, rouses us: and what does he say? *Do not fear, when a man has been made rich*[^20]. For why did you fear, because a man has been made rich? You feared lest you believed without cause, lest perhaps you had lost the labor of your faith, and the hope of your conversion: because perhaps gain with fraud occurred to you, and you could, if you committed that gain with fraud, be rich, and not labor; and attending to what God threatened, you abstained from fraud, and despised the gain: you see another to have made gain from fraud, and to suffer nothing evil; and you are afraid to be good. *Do not fear,* the Spirit of God says to you, *when a man has been made rich.* Do you not wish to have eyes except for present things? He who rose again promised future things, he did not promise peace in this earth and rest in this life. Every man seeks rest; he seeks a good thing, but does not seek it in his own region. There is no peace in this life: in heaven is promised us what we seek on earth; in the future world is promised what we seek in this world.

**7.** [v. 18] *Do not fear when a man has been made rich; and when the glory of his house has been multiplied*[^21]. Why, *do not fear*? *Because not when he dies, will he take all.* You see him living, think of him dying. You attend to what he has here, attend to what he carries with him. What does he carry with him? He has much gold, has much silver, much of estates, of slaves: he dies; those remain, he does not know for whom. For even if he leaves them to whom he wills, he does not keep them for whom he wills. For many both acquired things not left to them, and many lost things left to them. All those things remain, then, and he carries with him, what? Perhaps someone says: He carries with him that with which he is wrapped, and what is spent on him for a precious and marbled tomb, for furnishing a memorial, this he carries with him. I say: Not even this; for these things are displayed to one not perceiving. If you adorn a sleeping man, not waking, he has them with him on the couch: perhaps ornaments are upon the body of the one lying, and perhaps he sees himself in his dreams in rags. What he perceives is more to him than what he does not perceive. Although that too, when he wakes, will not be: yet to the sleeper that was more which he saw in his dreams, than that which he did not perceive. What then, brothers (let men say to themselves: Let it be spent at my death; why do I leave my heirs rich? They will have much of mine, let me too have something of mine, on my body), what will the dead body have? what will the rotting flesh have? what will the flesh not perceiving have? If that rich man, whose tongue was dry, had something, then a man has something of his own. Brothers, do we so read in the Gospel, that that rich man with silk and fine-linen coverings appeared in the fire? Was he such among the dead as he was at the feasts at table? When he thirsted, and desired a drop, all those things were not there. Not, then, does a man take all things with him, nor does what the burial carries off, the dead man carry with him. For where the sense is, there the man: where there is no sense, there is not the man. There lies the vessel which contained the man, the house which had the man. Let us call the body a house, let us call the spirit the dweller of the house. The spirit is tormented among the dead: what does it profit it, that the body lies in cinnamon and spices, wrapped in precious linens? As if the master of the house should be sent into exile, and you adorn his walls. He in exile is in need, and fails with hunger, scarcely finds himself one cell where he may take sleep, and you say: He is happy, for his house is adorned. Who would not think you either to be joking, or to be mad? You adorn the body, the spirit is tormented. Give something to the spirit, and you have given something to the dead. But what will you give it, when it desired one drop, and does not receive it? For here he despised to send before himself something. Why did he despise it? Because this way of theirs is a scandal to them. He did not think there was life except the present, he did not think except how, wrapped in precious garments, he might be buried. His soul was taken from him, as the Lord says: *Fool, this night your soul shall be taken from you, and the things you have prepared, whose shall they be?*[^22] And there was fulfilled in him what this psalm says: *Do not fear, when a man has been made rich, and when the glory of his house has been multiplied: because not when he dies will he take all, nor shall his glory descend together with him.*

**8.** [v. 19] *Because his soul shall be blessed in his lifetime*[^23]. Let your Charity attend: *Because his soul shall be blessed in his lifetime.* As long as he lived, he did well for himself. This all say, but they say it falsely. The blessing is from the mind of the one blessing, not from the truth itself. For what do you say? Because he ate and drank, because he did what he wished, because he feasted splendidly, did he therefore do well for himself? I say: He did badly for himself. Not I say it, but Christ: He did badly for himself. For that rich man, when he daily feasted splendidly, was thought to do well for himself: but when he began to burn among the dead, then was found bad what was thought good. For what he had eaten among the living, this he digested among the dead. I mean the iniquity, brothers, which he feasted on. He ate precious feasts with the mouth of the flesh, with the mouth of the heart he ate iniquity. What among the living he ate with the mouth of the heart, this among the dead in those punishments he digested. And indeed he had eaten it temporally, forever he badly digested it. Is iniquity, then, eaten? perhaps someone says: What is it that he speaks? Is iniquity eaten? Not I say it, hear the Scripture: *As an unripe grape is a vexation to the teeth, and smoke to the eyes, so is iniquity to those who use it*[^24]. For he who has eaten iniquity, that is, who has gladly had iniquity, will not be able to eat justice. For bread is justice. Who is the bread? *I am the living bread who came down from heaven*[^25]. He is the bread of our heart. As he who eats with the mouth of the body unripe grapes, his teeth grow stiff and dull, and he becomes less fit to eat bread, and there remains to him to praise what he sees, and not to be able to eat: so he who has used iniquity, and been fed on sins in the heart, begins to be unable to eat bread, he praises the word of God, and does not do it. Whence does he not do it? Because when he begins to do it, he labors; just as we feel the teeth labor after unripe grapes, when we begin to eat bread. But what do those do whose teeth have grown dull? They restrain themselves a little from unripe grapes, and the teeth return to their stability, and lean upon the bread. So too we praise justice: but if we wish to eat justice, let us restrain ourselves from iniquities; and there is born in the heart not only the delight of praising justice, but also the ease of eating it. For if a Christian says: God knows that it delights me, but I cannot do it: he has shattered teeth, he has long eaten iniquity. Therefore is justice too eaten? If it were not eaten, the Lord would not say: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice*[^26]. Therefore *because his soul shall be blessed in his lifetime*: in life it shall be blessed, in death it shall be tormented.

**9.** *He will confess to you, when you do well to him*[^27]. Attend and be fed, let it cling in your hearts; eat, see such men, and do not be such: beware such words. *He will confess to you, when you do well to him.* How many are there Christians, brothers, who give thanks to God then, when gain befalls them! This is: *He will confess to you, when you do well to him*: he will praise you, and say: Truly you are my God: he has freed me from prison, I will confess to him. Gain comes to him, he confesses; an inheritance comes, he confesses: he suffers a loss, he blasphemes. What kind of son are you, who, when your father amends you, then he displeases you? Would he amend you, unless you displeased him? or if you so displeased that he hated you, would he wish to amend you? Give thanks, then, to the amender, that you may receive the inheritance from God who amends you. For you are instructed, when you are amended. But he amends much, because what you are to receive is great. For if you weigh what you are amended by with what you are to receive, you will find that it is nothing that you are amended by. The apostle Paul says this: *For that which is at present light and momentary of our tribulation, works for us, beyond an incredible measure, an eternal weight of glory.* But when? *Not regarding,* he says, *the things that are seen, but the things that are not seen; not the temporal things, but the eternal. For the things that are seen are temporal; but the things that are not seen are eternal*[^28]. And again: *The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the future glory which shall be revealed in us*[^29]. What, then, is it that you suffer? But you always suffer. I grant it. From when you were born, through all your ages even to your old age, until you die, suppose that you suffer this which Job suffered; what he suffered for some days, suppose someone suffers from very infancy: what you suffer passes, is finished; what you are to receive will have no end. I do not now want you to compare the punishment with the reward: compare the times with eternity, if you can.

**10.** *He will confess to you, when you do well to him*[^30]. Do not be such, brothers: see that for this reason we say these things, for this we sing, for this it is treated, for this we sweat: do not do these things. Your businesses prove you: sometimes in your business you hear the truth, and you blaspheme, you blaspheme the Church. Why? Because you are a Christian. If it is so, I take myself off to the party of Donatus: I wish to be a pagan. Why? Because you bit the bread, and your teeth ache. When you saw that bread, you praised it; you began to eat, and your teeth ache: that is, when you heard the word of God, you praised it together, when it is said to you: Do this, you blaspheme. Do not wish it so: say this: The bread is good, but I cannot eat it. But now if you see with the eyes, you praise; when you have begun to press the teeth, you say: This bread is bad, and what kind is he who made it? So it comes about that you confess God, when God does well to you; and you lie, when you sing, *I will bless the Lord at all times, his praise always in my mouth*[^31]. It is required of your heart the singing of your lips: you sang in the Church, *I will bless the Lord at all times*: how *at all times*? If at all times there is gain, at all times he is blessed: if sometimes there is loss, he is not blessed, but blasphemed. Surely you bless at all times, surely his praise is always in your mouth? You will be such as he now describes; *He will confess to you, when you do well to him.*

**11.** [vv. 20-21] *He shall enter even to the generations of his fathers*[^32]: that is, he will imitate his fathers. Because the iniquitous who now are, have brothers, have fathers. The ancient iniquitous are the fathers of those present; and those who now are iniquitous, are the fathers of the later iniquitous: just as the fathers of the just, the ancient just, are the fathers of the just who now are; and those who now are, are the fathers of those who will be. The Holy Spirit wished to show that there is no bad equity, when there is murmuring against it: but these from the origin even to the generation of the fathers have their father. Adam begot two, and in the one iniquity, in the other justice: iniquity in Cain, justice in Abel. Iniquity seemed to prevail over justice, because the unjust Cain killed the just Abel in the night. Was it morning? But *the upright shall rule over them in the morning.* The morning will come, and it will be seen where Abel is, and where Cain. So all who are according to Cain, and so all who are according to Abel even to the end of the world. *He shall enter even to the generations of his fathers: even forever he shall not see the light.* Because even when he was here, he was in darkness, rejoicing in false goods, and not loving the true goods; and therefore from here he will go to Tartarus: from the darkness of dreams the darkness of torments will receive him. Therefore *even forever he shall not see the light.* But why this? What he set in the middle of the psalm, this too at the end: *Man, when he was in honor, did not understand; he has been compared to the senseless beasts, and has been made like them.* But you, brothers, consider yourselves made men to the image and likeness of God. The image of God is within, it is not in the body: it is not in these ears which you see, and eyes, and nostrils, and palate, and hands, and feet: but it is made, yet: where there is understanding, where there is mind, where there is reason for investigating truth, where there is faith, where there is your hope, where your love, there God has his image: there at least you understand and see that these things pass, because so he said in another psalm: *Although a man walks in an image, yet he is troubled in vain: he treasures up, and does not know for whom he gathers these things*[^33]. Do not be troubled, because, whatever these things are, they are transitory, if you are men set in honor, and you understand. For if you, men set in honor, do not understand, you are compared to the senseless beasts, and are made like them.
