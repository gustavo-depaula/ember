# Exposition of Psalm 33 (2)

**1.** You who were present yesterday, I do not doubt that you remember our promise. For it is now time, in the name of the Lord, to pay what is owed. He himself inspired us to promise; he himself will grant that we pay, always being debtors of love. For love is the very thing that is always repaid and always owed, as the Apostle says, *Owe no one anything, except to love one another*[^1]. We expounded the title of this psalm yesterday; and since its exposition detained us rather long, we deferred expounding the text of the same psalm. Let us hear, then, what the Holy Spirit says, through the mouth of his holy prophet, in this discourse of the psalm, agreeing with the title we treated yesterday. Those who were not present here too seek this debt; but lest perhaps, through so many other delays, we defraud those to whom we must now repay what we owe, let those who are present today and were not present yesterday recognize, as much as they can, what is briefly recalled. And if anything moves them that they ought to inquire about more diligently, they will find our ears open in the name of Christ at other times, so that these matters not be crowded out.

**2.** We said that it is written in the book of Kings that David, when he was fleeing Saul, wished to hide with a certain king of Gath, named Achish. But since his glory had been recalled there, lest through spite that king to whom he had fled should contrive something against him, he feigned madness, and as though seized by frenzy, *he changed his countenance, and,* as we read, *he behaved frantically, and beat upon the gates of the city like a drum, and was carried in his own hands, and fell down at the doors of the gate. And king Achish said: Why have you brought this man here to me? Do I have need of a madman?*[^2] And so he sent him away, that what is here written might be fulfilled: *He changed his countenance, and he sent him away, and he departed.* Now he sent away king Achish; but here it is said that *he changed his countenance before Abimelech, and he sent him away, and he departed.* We said, however, that the names were changed so that the sacrament might be shown; lest, if the same name were repeated in the title of the psalm, it should seem that nothing was prophesied to us in a sacrament, but as it were that deeds done were narrated. Each name, then, contains a great sacrament. For Achish is interpreted "how is he"; Abimelech is interpreted "my father's kingdom." In that saying "how is he," ignorance is signified, so that you may understand the word of one wondering and not recognizing; but in that which is said "Abimelech," the kingdom of the Jews is signified. For from the person of Christ it can be said "my father's kingdom," because his father according to the flesh is David, and the kingdom of David was among the nation of the Jews. Before the kingdom of his father, then, *he changed his countenance, and he sent him away, and he departed;* because there was the sacrifice according to the order of Aaron, and afterward he himself instituted, from his body and blood, the sacrifice according to the order of Melchizedek. He changed his countenance, then, in the priesthood, and sent away the nation of the Jews, and came to the Gentiles. What, then, is *he behaved frantically*? He was full of affection. For what is so full of affection as the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ, who, seeing our infirmity, in order to free us from eternal death, took up temporal death with so great an injury and reproach? *And he beat like a drum;* because a drum is not made unless skin is stretched out on wood; and David beat like a drum, signifying that Christ was to be crucified. *He beat like a drum at the gates of the city;* what are *the gates of the city,* but our hearts which we had shut against Christ, who from the drum of the cross opened the hearts of mortals? *And he was carried in his own hands;* how was he *carried in his own hands*? Because when he commended his very body and his blood, he took into his own hands what the faithful know; and in a certain way he was carrying himself, when he said, *This is my body*[^3]. *And he fell down at the doors of the gate;* that is, he humbled himself. For this is to fall down even to the beginning of our faith. For the door of the gate is the beginning of faith, where the Church begins, and reaches even to the form: so that when it believes those things which it does not see, it may deserve to enjoy them fully, when it begins to see face to face. Such is the title of the psalm; we have heard it briefly: let us now hear the very words of him behaving frantically and beating like a drum at the gate of the city.

**3.** [v. 2] *I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall always be in my mouth*[^4]. Christ says it; let the Christian say it too; because the Christian is in the body of Christ; and for this reason Christ became man, that man might become a Christian, so that the angel said, *I will bless the Lord.* When shall I bless the Lord? When he does you good? When worldly goods abound? When there is great abundance of grain, oil, wine, gold, silver, slaves, cattle, and this mortal health persists unwounded and uncorrupted, and all things born grow, nothing is carried off by untimely death, all happiness abounds in the house, all things flow round about—then will you bless the Lord? No: but *at all times.* Therefore both then, and when these things, according to the times and according to the scourges of the Lord our God, are disturbed, taken away, born in lesser number, and what has been born slips away. For these things happen, and from them follow want, need, toil, grief, and temptation. But you who have sung, *I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall always be in my mouth,* both when he gives these things, bless him, and when he takes these things away, bless him. Because he gives, he takes away; but he does not take himself away from the one who blesses him.

**4.** But who is it that blesses the Lord at all times, except the one humble in heart? For our Lord taught that very humility in his body and blood; because when he commends his body and blood, he commends his humility, in that which is written in the history itself, in that as it were frenzy of David, which we passed over: *And the spittle ran down upon his beard.* When the Apostle was being read, you heard the very spittle, but running down upon the beard. Someone says: What spittle did we hear? Was not the Apostle just now read, when he said, *The Jews ask for signs, and the Greeks seek wisdom*? Just now was read, *But we preach,* he says, *Christ crucified* (for then he was beating like a drum), *to the Jews indeed a scandal, but to the Gentiles folly; but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the Power of God and the Wisdom of God; because what is foolish of God is wiser than men, and what is weak of God is stronger than men*[^5]. For the spittle signifies folly, the spittle signifies weakness. But if what is foolish of God is wiser than men, and what is weak of God is stronger than men, let not the spittle, as it were, offend you, but attend, because it runs down upon the beard. For as weakness is shown by the spittle, so power by the beard. He covered his power, then, with the body of his weakness; and what was weak outwardly appeared as it were in the spittle; but within, the divine power was veiled as it were by the beard. Therefore humility is commended to us. Be humble, if you wish to bless the Lord at all times, and let his praise always be in your mouth. For Job did not bless the Lord only when all things abounded for him, by which we read that he was rich and happy, both in cattle and in servants and in his house, happy in children and in all things. All were taken away at one time, and he fulfilled what is written in this psalm, saying, *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it has pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^6]. Behold, you have an example of one blessing the Lord at all times.

**5.** [v. 3] But why does a human being bless the Lord at all times? Because he is humble. What is it to be humble? To be unwilling to be praised in oneself. He who wishes to be praised in himself is proud. He who is not proud is humble. Do you not wish, then, to be proud? That you may be able to be humble, say what has been said: *In the Lord shall my soul be praised; let the meek hear, and be glad*[^7]. Therefore those who do not wish to be praised in the Lord are not meek, but fierce, harsh, lofty, proud. The Lord wishes to have meek beasts of burden: be a beast of burden of the Lord, that is, be meek. He sits upon you, he himself guides you: do not fear that you may stumble and go headlong. Your infirmity indeed is yours, but attend to who presides over you. You are the colt of a donkey, but you carry Christ. For he too came on the colt of a donkey to the city, and that beast was meek. Was that beast praised? Was it said to the beast, *Hosanna, son of David; blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^8]? The little donkey was carrying; but the one who was carried was praised by those going before and those following. And perhaps the beast was saying, *In the Lord shall my soul be praised; let the meek hear, and be glad.* That donkey never said it, brothers; but let the people say it who imitate that beast, if they wish to carry their Lord. Perhaps the people are angry, because they are compared to the little donkey on which the Lord sat; and some proud and lofty ones will say to me, "Behold, he has made us donkeys." Let whoever says this be a donkey of the Lord; let him not be a horse and mule, in which there is no understanding. For you know the psalm where it is said, *Do not be like the horse and mule, in which there is no understanding*[^9]. For the horse and mule sometimes raise their neck and by their fierceness throw off their rider. They are tamed by bridles, by the muzzle, by blows, until they learn to be subject and to carry their master. But you, before your mouth is beaten with the bridle, be meek, and carry your Lord: do not wish to be praised in yourself, but let him be praised who sits upon you, and you will say, *In the Lord shall my soul be praised; let the meek hear, and be glad.* For when the unmeek hear, they are not glad, but angry; and they are the ones who say that we have made them donkeys. But those who are meek, let them deign to hear and to be what they hear.

**6.** [v. 4] There follows, *Magnify the Lord with me*[^10]. Who is this who exhorts us to magnify the Lord with him? Whoever he is, brothers, who is in the body of Christ, ought to give his effort to this, that the Lord be magnified with him. For whoever he is, he loves the Lord. And how does he love him? So as not to envy his fellow-lover. For whoever loves carnally must love with a deadly jealousy: if perhaps he could, as a great thing, see naked the one whom he has desired with deadly love, does he wish that another also should see her? He must be wounded by jealousy and spite if another too should see her. And so chastity is preserved if either he sees who is permitted, and another does not see, or not even he himself. The Wisdom of God is not so: we shall see her face to face, and we shall all see her, and no one there will be jealous. She shows herself to all, and is whole, and is chaste to all. They are changed into her, and she is not changed into them. She is the truth, she is God. Have you ever heard, brothers, that our God could be changed? The truth, surpassing all things, is the Word of God, is the Wisdom of God, through whom all things were made; she has her lovers. But what does her lover say? *Magnify the Lord with me.* I do not wish to magnify the Lord alone, I do not wish to love alone, I do not wish to embrace alone. For if I embrace him, another does not lack a place to put his hands. There is such breadth in Wisdom herself that all souls may embrace her and enjoy her fully. And what shall I say, brothers? Let those be ashamed who so love God that they envy others. Lost men love a charioteer, and whoever loves a charioteer or a hunter wishes that the whole people might love him along with him; and he exhorts and says: Love that pantomime with me, love with me that one and that shameful thing. He cries out among the people, that the shameful thing be loved along with him; and a Christian does not cry out in the Church, that the truth of God be loved along with him! Rouse love in yourselves, then, brothers, and cry out to each one of you, and say, *Magnify the Lord with me.* Let this fervor be in you. Why are these things recited to you and expounded? If you love God, snatch all who are joined to you to the love of God, and all who are in your house; if the body of Christ, that is, the unity of the Church, is loved by you, snatch them to enjoy it, and say, *Magnify the Lord with me.*

**7.** [v. 4] *And let us exalt his name together.* What is *let us exalt his name together*? That is, into one. For many manuscripts have it thus: *Magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name into one.* Whether it be said *together* or *into one,* the same thing is said. Therefore snatch whom you can, by exhorting, carrying, asking, disputing, giving an account, with meekness, with gentleness; snatch them to love; so that if they magnify the Lord, they may magnify him into one. The party of Donatus too seems to itself to magnify the Lord: what has the whole world done to offend them? Let us say to them, brothers, *Magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name into one.* Why do you wish to magnify the Lord in division? He is one; why do you wish to make two peoples for God? Why do you wish to scatter the body of Christ? For surely he himself was hanging on the cross when he was beating like a drum; and as he hung on the cross, he gave up his spirit; and those who had hung him up came and found that he had given up his spirit, and they did not break his legs; but with the robbers still living on the cross they broke theirs, so that by that very pain, by a shortening of death, they might be freed from that torment, as was wont to be done to the crucified. So the persecutor came, and found that the Lord had quietly laid down his spirit; for he himself said, *I have power to lay down my soul*[^11]. For whom did he lay down his soul? For his whole people, for his whole body. The persecutor came, then, and did not break the legs of Christ; Donatus came and tore apart the Church of Christ. The whole body of Christ on the cross is intact among the hands of persecutors, and among the hands of Christians the body of the Church is not intact. Let us cry out, then, brothers, with groaning, as much as we can, saying, *Magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name into one.* For the Church cries out to them: it is the voice of the Church, crying out to those who have cut themselves off. From what were they torn apart? Through pride. But Christ teaches humility, when he commends his body and blood; which we have said to your Holiness is done and celebrated in this text of the psalm, where the body and blood of Christ are commended, where the humility of Christ is commended, which he deigned to take up for us.

**8.** [v. 5] *I sought the Lord, and he heard me*[^12]. Where did the Lord hear? Within. Where does he give? Within. There you pray, there you are heard, there you are made blessed. You prayed, you were heard, you were made blessed; and the one who stands beside you does not know it: the whole thing was done in secret, as the Lord says in the Gospel, *Enter into your chamber, shut your door, and pray in secret, and your Father who sees in secret will reward you*[^13]. When, therefore, you enter your chamber, you enter your heart. Blessed are they who rejoice when they enter their heart, and find nothing evil there. Let your Holiness attend: how those who have wicked wives are unwilling to enter their houses, how they go out to the marketplace and rejoice; the hour has come for them to enter their house, they are saddened: for they are about to enter to weariness, to grumbling, to bitterness, to overthrows; because the house is not well ordered where there is no peace between husband and wife; and it is better for such a one to wander about outside. If, then, those are wretched who, when they return to their own walls, fear lest they be overthrown by some disturbances of their household, how much more wretched are those who are unwilling to return to their own conscience, lest there they be overthrown by the quarrels of their sins! Therefore, that you may be able gladly to return to your heart, cleanse it; for *blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God*[^14]. Take away from it the filth of desires, take away the stain of greed, take away the rot of superstitions, take away sacrileges and evil thoughts; hatreds, I do not say against a friend, but even against an enemy: take away all these things; enter into your heart, and you will rejoice there. When you have begun to rejoice there, the very cleanness of your heart will delight you, and will make you pray; just as, if you come to some place where there is silence, where perhaps there is quiet, where the place is clean, you say, "Let us pray here"; and the orderliness of the place delights you, and you believe that God will hear you there. If, then, the cleanness of a visible place delights you, why does the uncleanness of your heart not offend you? Enter, cleanse all things, lift your eyes to God, and at once he will hear you. Cry out, and say, *I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and from all my tribulations he delivered me*[^15]. Why? Because when you have been enlightened, when you have begun to have a good conscience here, tribulations remain, because something weak remains, until death is swallowed up in victory, and this mortal puts on immortality: it is necessary that in this world you be scourged; it is necessary that you suffer some temptations and suggestions. God will cleanse all things, will deliver you from all tribulation; seek him.

**9.** *I sought the Lord, and he heard me.* Those, then, who are not heard do not seek the Lord. Let your Holiness attend: he did not say, "I sought gold from the Lord, and he heard me"; "I sought old age from the Lord, and he heard me"; "I sought this or that from the Lord, and he heard me." It is one thing to seek something from the Lord, another to seek the Lord himself. *I sought,* he says, *the Lord, and he heard me.* But you, when you pray and say, "Kill that enemy of mine," do not seek the Lord, but as it were make yourself a judge over your enemy, and make your God a torturer. How do you know whether the one whose death you seek may not be better than you? Perhaps by this very thing, that he does not seek yours. Therefore do not seek anything outside from the Lord, but seek the Lord himself, and he will hear you, and even while you are still speaking he will say, *Behold, I am here*[^16]. What is *Behold, I am here*? Behold, I am present; what do you wish, what do you seek from me? Whatever I give you is cheaper than I am: have myself, enjoy me, embrace me; you cannot yet wholly; touch me by faith, and you will cleave to me (this God says to you), and the rest of your burdens I will lay down from you, so that you may cleave wholly to me, when I shall have changed this mortal thing of yours into immortality; that you may be equal to my angels, and always see my face, and rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you; because you sought the Lord, and he heard you, and from all your tribulations he delivered you.

**10.** [v. 6] We said who that exhorter was, that lover who does not wish to embrace alone what he loves, and says, *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*[^17]. For he says what he himself has proved. For some spiritual one in the body of Christ, or even our Lord Jesus Christ himself according to the flesh, the head exhorting the other members, what does he say? *Draw near to him, and be enlightened.* Or rather some spiritual Christian invites us to draw near to our Lord Jesus Christ himself. But let us draw near to him, and be enlightened; not as the Jews drew near to him, to be darkened. For they drew near to him to crucify him: let us draw near to him to receive his body and blood. They by the crucified were darkened; we, by eating and drinking the crucified, are enlightened. *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*—behold, it is said to the Gentiles. Christ crucified was among the Jews raging and seeing; the Gentiles were absent: behold, those drew near who were in darkness, and those who did not see were enlightened. From where do the Gentiles draw near? By following with faith, by gaping with the heart, by running with love. Your feet are your love. Have two feet, do not be lame. What are the two feet? The two precepts of love, of God and of neighbor. With these feet run to God, draw near to him: because both that you might run he himself exhorted you, and he himself sprinkled his light in such a way that you may follow him magnificently and divinely. *And your faces shall not blush. Draw near,* he says, *to him, and be enlightened; and your faces shall not blush.* No face blushes but the proud one. Why? Because he wishes to be lofty, and when he has suffered reproach, or ignominy, or a fall according to the world, or some affliction, he blushes. But do not fear, draw near to him, and you will not blush. Whatever the enemy may have done to you, he seems to be superior to you among men: but you are superior to him before God. "I captured, I bound, I killed." How superior to themselves they seem who say these things! How superior to themselves the Jews seemed when they were buffeting the Lord, when they spat in his face and struck his head with a reed, when they crowned him with thorns, when they wrapped him in an ignominious garment! How superior they were! And he seemed inferior, because he fell down at the doors of the gate: but he did not blush. For he was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world[^18]. As, then, light cannot be confounded, so neither does it allow the enlightened to be confounded. *Draw near,* then, *to him, and be enlightened; and your faces shall not blush.*

**11.** [vv. 7-8] But someone says: How do I draw near to him? I am laden with so many evils, so many sins; so many crimes cry out from my conscience; how do I dare to draw near to God? How? If you humble yourself by repentance. But I am ashamed, you say, to do penance. Draw near to him, then, and you will be enlightened, and your face will not blush. For if the fear of being ashamed calls you back from penance, and penance makes you draw near to God: do you not see that you bear the punishment in your face, that your face has blushed for this reason, that it did not draw near to God; and that it did not draw near because it is unwilling to do penance? As the prophet attests: *This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him*[^19]. He teaches you how you may be heard. You are not heard for this reason, that you are rich. Lest perhaps you cried out and were not heard, hear why: *This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him.* Cry out poor, and the Lord hears. And how shall I cry out poor? So that even if you have something, you do not therefore presume on your own strength; so that you understand yourself to be needy, so that you understand yourself to be poor as long as you do not have him who makes you rich. And how did the Lord hear him? *And from all,* he says, *his tribulations he saved him.* And how does he save from all tribulations? *The angel of the Lord will encamp round about those who fear him, and will deliver them*[^20]. So it is written, brothers; not as some faulty manuscripts have it, "The Lord will send an angel round about those who fear him, and will deliver them"; but thus: *The angel of the Lord will encamp round about those who fear him, and will deliver them.* Whom did he here call the angel of the Lord, who will encamp round about those who fear him, and will deliver them? Our Lord Jesus Christ himself was called in prophecy the Angel of great counsel, the messenger of great counsel: so the prophets called him[^21]. He himself, then, the Angel of great counsel, that is, the messenger, will encamp upon those who fear the Lord, and will deliver them. Do not fear, then, that you may lie hidden: wherever you fear the Lord, that Angel knows you, who will encamp round about, and will deliver you.

**12.** [v. 9] Now he wishes openly to speak of that very sacrament, by which he was carried in his own hands. *Taste, and see that the Lord is sweet*[^22]. Does not the psalm open itself and show you that as it were madness and steady frenzy, the sane madness and sober drunkenness of that David, who in a figure showed I know not what, when from the person of king Achish they said, "How is he?"—when the Lord was saying, *Unless one eats my flesh and drinks my blood, he will not have life in himself*? And those in whom Achish reigned, that is, error and ignorance, what did they say? *How can this man give us his flesh to eat?* If you are ignorant, taste, and see that the Lord is sweet; but if you do not understand, you are king Achish. David will change his countenance, and will withdraw from you, and will send you away, and will depart.

**13.** *Blessed is the man who hopes in him*[^23]. What need is there to expound this at length? Whoever does not hope in the Lord is wretched. Who is it that does not hope in the Lord? He who hopes in himself. Sometimes—and this is worse, my brothers, attend—sometimes human beings are unwilling to hope in themselves, but in other human beings. "So long as so-and-so is safe, you can do nothing to me." And perhaps he is speaking of a man already dead. In this city he says, "So long as that man is safe," and that man is perhaps dead in some other place. And how quickly do men say this; and they do not say, "I trust in God, that he does not permit you to harm me." They do not say, "I trust in my God, that even if he permits you something against me, against my soul he will not permit you." But when they say, "So long as that man is safe," neither do they themselves come to have safety, and they burden those through whom they think to have safety.

**14.** [vv. 10-11] *Fear the Lord, all you his saints, for there is no want to those who fear him*[^24]. For many are unwilling to fear the Lord, lest they suffer hunger. It is said to them: Do not commit fraud. And they say: From where do I feed myself? A craft cannot be without imposture, business cannot be without fraud. But God punishes fraud. Fear God. But if I fear God, I will not have a means to live. *Fear the Lord, all you his saints, for there is no want to those who fear him.* He promises abundance to the trembling and the doubting, lest perhaps, if he fears the Lord, superfluous things abandon him. Did the Lord feed you when you despised him, and will he abandon you when you fear him? Attend, and do not say: He is rich, and I am poor; I fear the Lord, and he, by not fearing him, has acquired so much, while I, by fearing him, am naked! See what follows: *The rich have wanted and gone hungry, but those who seek the Lord shall not be diminished in any good*[^25]. If you take it according to the letter, it seems to deceive you. For you observe many unjust rich people dying amid their riches, not having been made poor while they live; you see them grow old, brought to the end of life amid great stores of riches, the pomp of a funeral celebrated for them with great lavishness, a rich man brought even to the tomb who breathed his last on ivory couches, with his household weeping around; and you say in your mind, if perhaps you know any of his sins and crimes: "I knew how much that man did; behold, he grew old, died in his bed, his household carries him out, so great a pomp of funeral is celebrated: I knew what he did; Scripture has deceived and failed me, where I hear and sing, *The rich have wanted and gone hungry.* When was that man poor? When did he go hungry? *But those who seek the Lord shall not be diminished in any good.* Every day I rise to the basilica, every day I genuflect, every day I seek the Lord, and I have no good; that man did not seek the Lord, and amid such great goods he has died!" The snare of scandal chokes the one who thinks thus. For he seeks mortal food on earth, and does not seek the true reward in heaven, and puts his head into the snare of the devil; his throat is constricted, and the devil holds him to do evil, so that thus he imitates that rich man, whom he sees dying amid such great abundance.

**15.** Do not, then, understand it so. And how shall I understand it? In spiritual goods. But where are they? They are not seen with the eyes, but with the heart. I do not see those goods. He who loves them sees them. I do not see justice. For it is not gold, it is not silver. If it were gold, you would see it; because it is faith, you do not see it. And if you do not see faith, why do you love a faithful servant? Ask yourself what kind of servant you love. Perhaps you have a servant who is handsome, tall, well-built, but a thief, worthless, deceitful; and you have another perhaps small in stature, ugly in face, of foul color, but faithful, thrifty, sober: attend, I beg you, which of these two do you love? If you ask the eyes of the flesh, the handsome unjust one wins with you; if the eyes of the heart, the ugly faithful one wins. You see, then, that what you wish another to display to you, that is, faith, you too should display to him. Why do you rejoice over the one who displays faith to you, and praise him for these goods which are not seen except by the eye of the heart? When you have been filled with spiritual riches, will you be poor? And that rich man was rich for this reason, that he had an ivory couch; and are you poor, whose heart's chamber is full of so many gems of virtues, of justice, truth, love, faith, patience, and endurance! Unfold your riches, if you have them, and compare them to the riches of the rich. Yet that man, in the market, found precious mules and bought them. If you found faith for sale, how much would you give for it, which God wished you to have for free, and you are ungrateful? Therefore those rich ones are in want, they are in want; and, what is more serious, they are in want of bread. Do not perhaps suppose that they are in want of gold and silver, although they are in want of this too. How much did a certain man have, and what satisfied him? He died in want, because he wished to acquire more than he held. They are in want of bread too. Why are they in want of bread too? If you do not understand bread, he said, *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^26]; and, *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^27]. *But those who seek the Lord shall not be diminished in any good.* But with what good, we have already said.

**16.** [v. 12] *Come, children, hear me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord*[^28]. You think, brothers, that I say it; suppose that David says it, suppose that the Apostle says it: rather, suppose that our Lord Jesus Christ himself says it: *Come, children, hear me.* Let us hear him together, hear him through us: for he wishes to teach, that humble one, that one beating like a drum, that one behaving frantically, he wishes to teach us. And what does he say? *Come, children, hear me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord.* Let him teach, then; let us offer our ear, let us offer our heart. Let us not open the ears of the flesh and shut those of the heart; but as he himself said in the Gospel, *Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear*[^29]. Who would be unwilling to hear Christ teaching through the prophet?

**17.** [v. 13] *Who is the man who wishes for life, and loves to see good days?*[^30] He asks. Does not everyone among you answer, "I"? Or is there anyone among you who does not love life, that is, who does not wish for life, and does not love to see good days? Do you not every day grumble this, and say this: "How long do we suffer these things? Every day worse and worse: in our parents' time the days were more joyful, the days were better." Oh, if you asked your parents themselves, they would likewise grumble to you about their own days. "Our fathers were blessed, we are wretched, we have evil days; that man ruled, we thought that when he died some relief might be given; worse things have come. O God, show us good days!" *Who is the man who wishes for life, and loves to see good days?* Let him not seek good days here. He seeks a good thing, but he does not seek it in its own region. As if you were seeking some just man in that country where he did not dwell, it would be said to you: You seek a good man, you seek a great man; seek him, but not here; in vain you seek him here, you will never find him. You seek good days; let us seek them together, but not here. But our fathers had them. You are mistaken: all here have toiled. Read the Scriptures: for this reason God willed them to be written, that we might be consoled. In the times of Elijah there was a famine; our fathers suffered it. The heads of dead beasts of burden were sold for gold; they killed their own and ate them; and two women agreed between themselves to kill their sons and eat them; one killed her son, and both ate him; the other was unwilling to kill her son, and the one who had first killed her son demanded him; and such a quarrel came before the king, they brought themselves before the king, contending about the killing of their sons. From such foods may God turn away those we read about. Always there are evil days in the world, but always good days in God. Abraham had good days, but within, in his heart; he had evil days, when from famine he changed his region, and sought food. So all sought. Did Paul have good days, who says, *In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness*[^31]? But let not servants be angry: the Lord himself did not have good days in this world; reproaches, injuries, the cross, and so many evils he suffered.

**18.** [v. 14] Let not the Christian, then, grumble; let him look at whose footsteps he follows. But if he loves good days, let him hear the one teaching and saying, *Come, children, hear me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord.* What do you wish? Life and good days. Hear, and do: *Keep your tongue from evil*[^32]. Do that. "I do not wish to," says the wretched man: "I do not wish to keep my tongue from evil, and I wish for life, and good days." If your laborer were to say to you, "I both lay waste this vineyard and demand wages from you; you brought me to the vineyard that I might prune and trim it, I cut down all the useful wood, I cut off the very trunks of the vines, so that you have nothing here to gather; and when I have done these things, you are to repay me my labor"—would you not call him mad? Would you not drive him from your house before he put his hand to the pruning-hook? Such are the men who both wish to do evil things, and to swear falsely, and to blaspheme against God, and to grumble, and to commit fraud, and to get drunk, and to quarrel, and to commit adultery, and to apply charms, and to go to soothsayers, and to see good days. It is said to him: You cannot, while doing evil, seek a good reward. If you are unjust, will God too be unjust? "What, then, shall I do?" What do you wish? "I wish for life, I wish for good days." *Keep your tongue from evil, and your lips that they speak no guile*: that is, fraud to no one, a lie to no one.

**19.** [v. 15] But what is *Turn away from evil*[^33]? It is too little that you harm no one, kill no one, do not steal, do not commit adultery, do not commit fraud, do not bear false witness. *Turn away from evil*: when you have turned away, you say, "I am secure, I have accomplished everything, I shall have life, I shall see good days." Not only *turn away from evil,* but *and do good.* It is too little that you do not despoil: clothe the naked. If you have not despoiled, you have turned away from evil; but you will not do good, except when you have taken in a stranger into your house. So, then, turn away from evil, that you may do good. *Seek peace, and pursue it.* He did not tell you, "You will have peace here": seek it, and pursue it. Where do I pursue it? Where it has gone before. For the Lord is our peace; he rose again and ascended into heaven. *Seek peace, and pursue it*: because you too, when you have risen, this mortal will be changed, and you will embrace peace, where no one will be troublesome to you. For there is perfect peace, where you will not hunger. For here bread makes peace for you: take away bread, and see what a war there will be within your bowels. The just themselves, how they groan here, brothers! that you may know that we seek peace here, but shall attain it in the end. But let us have it here in part, that we may deserve to have it there in whole. What is "in part"? Let us be of one mind here, let us love our neighbor as ourselves. So love your brother as yourself, have peace with him. But there cannot but arise some quarrels, such as arose even among brothers and among saints, between Barnabas and Paul; but not such as would kill concord, not such as would destroy love. For you yourself sometimes resist yourself, and yet do not hate yourself. For everyone who repents of anything quarrels with himself. He sinned, he returns, he is angry with himself for having done this, for having committed that. He has a quarrel with himself, then, but that quarrel tends toward concord. See how a certain just man quarrels with himself and says, *Why are you sad, my soul, and why do you trouble me? Hope in the Lord, for I shall still confess to him*[^34]. When he says to his soul, *Why do you trouble me,* surely it was troubling him. Perhaps he himself wished to suffer for Christ, and his soul was saddened. And he who knew and said, *Why are you sad, my soul, and why do you trouble me,* did not yet have peace with himself; but in his mind he clung to Christ, that his soul might follow him, and not trouble him. Seek peace, then, brothers. The Lord said, *These things I speak to you, that in me you may have peace. Peace on earth I do not promise you*[^35]. In this life there is no true peace, nor tranquillity. The joy of immortality, the fellowship of the angels, is promised. But whoever does not seek it while he is here will not have it when he comes there.

**20.** [v. 16] *The eyes of the Lord are upon the just*[^36]. Do not fear, then; toil: the eyes of the Lord are upon you. *And his ears to their prayers.* What more do you wish? If the head of the household did not hear a grumbling servant in a great house, the servant would complain and say, "How much we suffer here, and no one hears us!" Do you say that of God: "How much I suffer, and no one hears me"? "If he heard me," you perhaps say, "he would take away my tribulation: I cry out, and am in tribulation." Only hold to his ways, and when you are in tribulation he hears you. But he is a physician; you still have something rotten, I know not what. You cry out: but he is still cutting; and he does not withdraw his hand until he has cut as much as seems good. For that physician is cruel who hears a man, and spares the wound and the rot. How do mothers rub their children in the baths for their health? Do not the little ones cry out amid their hands? Are those mothers, then, cruel, that they do not spare, do not heed the tears? Are they not full of tenderness? And yet the children cry out, and are not spared. So too our God is full of love: but for this reason he seems not to hear, that he may heal and spare forever.

**21.** [v. 17] *The eyes of the Lord are upon the just, and his ears to their prayers.* Perhaps the wicked say: Then I do evil things securely, because the eyes of the Lord are not upon me: God now attends to the just, he does not see me, and whatever I do, I do securely. At once the Holy Spirit, seeing the thoughts of men, added and said, *The eyes of the Lord are upon the just, and his ears to their prayers; but the countenance of the Lord is against those who do evil things, to cut off their memory from the earth*[^37].

**22.** [v. 18] *The just cried, and the Lord heard them, and from all their tribulations he delivered them*[^38]. The three young men were just: from the furnace they cried to the Lord, and amid their praises the fires grew cold. The flame could not draw near and harm the innocent and just young men praising God, and he delivered them from the flame. Someone says: Behold, truly the just are those who were heard, as it is written, *The just cried, and the Lord heard them, and from all their tribulations he delivered them*: but I cried, and he did not deliver me; either I am not just, or I do not do what he commands me, or perhaps he does not see me. Do not fear; only do what he commands; and if he does not deliver you bodily, he will deliver you spiritually. For he who took the three young men from the flame, did he take the Maccabees from the flame? Were they not singing hymns in the fires, were they not breathing their last in the fires? The God of the three young men, is he not also the God of the Maccabees? Those he delivered, and those he did not deliver; rather, he delivered both; but the three young men he so delivered that the carnal too might be confounded; the Maccabees, however, he did not so deliver, in order that those who were persecuting them might go to greater punishments, when they thought they had crushed the martyrs of God. He delivered Peter, when the angel came to him while he was in chains, and said to him, *Rise, and go out*; and suddenly the chains were loosed, and he followed the angel, and he delivered him[^39]. Had Peter lost his justice, when he did not deliver him from the cross? Did he not deliver him then? And he did deliver him then. Or did he live long that he might become unjust? Perhaps he heard him more afterward than before, when he truly delivered him from all afflictions. For when he first delivered him, how much did he afterward endure! For he sent him afterward where he could suffer no evil.

**23.** [vv. 19-20] *The Lord is near to those who are contrite of heart, and he will save the humble of spirit*[^40]. God is high; let the Christian be humble. If he wishes the high God to draw near to him, let him be humble. Great mysteries, brothers. God is above all things: you raise yourself, and do not touch him; you humble yourself, and he himself descends to you. *Many are the tribulations of the just*[^41]. Does he say: Let Christians be just for this reason, let them hear my word for this reason, that they may suffer no tribulation? He does not promise this; but he says, *Many are the tribulations of the just.* The more, if they are unjust, the fewer tribulations they have; if they are just, they have many. But after few tribulations or none, the unjust will come to eternal tribulation, from which they will never be delivered; but the just, after many tribulations, will come to eternal peace, where they will never suffer anything evil. *Many are the tribulations of the just; and from all these the Lord will deliver them.*

**24.** [v. 21] *The Lord guards all their bones; not one of these shall be broken*[^42]. And this too, brothers, let us not take carnally. The bones are the supports of the faithful. For as in our flesh the bones make the support, so in the Christian heart faith makes the support. The patience, then, which is in faith, these are the bones within. They are the ones that cannot be broken. *The Lord guards all their bones; not one of these shall be broken.* If he had said this of our Lord God Jesus Christ—"The Lord guards all the bones of his Son; not one of these shall be broken"—as also in another place it is prefigured of him, when the lamb was said to be slain, and it was said of it, *You shall not break a bone of it*[^43]: it was fulfilled in the Lord; because when he hung on the cross, he breathed his last before they came to the cross, and they found the body already lifeless, and were unwilling to break his legs, that what is written might be fulfilled. But he promised this also to the rest of the Christians: *The Lord guards all their bones; not one of these shall be broken.* Therefore, brothers, if we see some saint suffering tribulations, and perhaps either cut by a physician or so beaten by some persecutor that his bones are broken, let us not say, "That man was not just"; for the Lord promised this to his just ones, of whom he says, *The Lord guards all their bones; not one of these shall be broken.* Do you wish to see that he meant other bones, which we said are the supports of faith, that is, patience and endurance in all tribulations? For these are the bones that are not broken. Hear, and consider in the very passion of the Lord what I say. The Lord was in the middle, crucified; beside him were two robbers: one insulted, the other believed; one was damned, the other justified; one had his punishment both here and in the time to come, but to the other the Lord said, *Amen I say to you, today you shall be with me in paradise*[^44]: and yet those who came did not break the Lord's bones, but broke the robbers'; so the bones of the robber who blasphemed were broken just as the bones of the robber who believed. Where, then, is that which was said, *The Lord guards all their bones; not one of these shall be broken*? Behold, the one to whom he said, *today you shall be with me in paradise,* could he not guard all his bones? The Lord answers you: Rather, I did guard them; for the support of his faith could not be broken by those blows by which his legs were broken.

**25.** [vv. 22-23] *The death of sinners is most evil*[^45]. Attend, brothers, because of what we were saying. Truly the Lord is great, and his mercy; truly he who gave us his body to eat, in which he suffered so much, and his blood to drink. How does he regard those who think evil and say: That man died an evil death, he was consumed by beasts: that man was not just, therefore he perished evilly; for otherwise he would not have perished? Is that man, then, just who dies in his house and on his bed? "This, then," you say, "is what I marvel at: that I knew his sins and crimes, and he died well, in his house, within his own threshold, with no injury of pilgrimage, none even at an early age." Hear: *The death of sinners is most evil.* What seems to you a good death is most evil, if you see within. You see one lying without on the bed; do you see within him snatched away to hell? Hear, brothers, and consider from the Gospel what the most evil death of sinners is. Were there not two in this world, a rich man who was clothed in purple and fine linen and feasted splendidly every day; and another, a poor man who lay at his gate full of sores, and the dogs came and licked his sores, and he desired to be filled with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table? Now it happened that the poor man died (that poor man was just), and was carried by the angels into the bosom of Abraham[^46]. One who had seen his body lying at the threshold of the rich man, with no one to bury it, what might he perhaps have said? "So may that enemy of mine die, and that one who persecutes me; so may I see him." He curses the body with spittle, the wounds stink; and that man rests in the bosom of Abraham. If we are Christians, let us believe; if we do not believe, brothers, let no one pretend to be a Christian. Faith leads us through. As the Lord said those things, so they are. Or does the astrologer tell you something, and it is true; and Christ tells you, and it is false? But by what death did that rich man die? What kind of death could there be amid purple and fine linen, how sumptuous, how pompous? What funeral rites were there? With how many spices was that corpse buried? And yet, when he was in torments among the dead, he desired that a drop of water be dripped from the finger of that despised poor man onto his burning tongue, and did not obtain it. Learn, then, what it is: *The death of sinners is most evil,* and do not inquire of those laid out on couches with precious garments, and flesh wrapped in many riches, displaying the pomp of lamentation, the household mourning, the throng of attendants going before and following as the body is borne out, the marbled and gilded monuments. For if you inquire of these, they answer you falsely, that the death of many—not lightly sinners, but utterly wicked—is the best, who deserved so to be mourned, so embalmed, so covered, so borne out, so buried. But inquire of the Gospel, and it will show your faith the soul of the rich man burning in torments, whom all the honors and attendances availed nothing, which the vanity of the living rendered to his dead body.

**26.** But because there are many kinds of sin, and it is difficult not to be a sinner, or perhaps in this life impossible, he at once added of what kind of sinners the death is most evil. *And those who hate the just one,* he says, *shall offend*[^47]. What just one, but the one who justifies the ungodly? What just one, but the Lord Jesus Christ, who is also the propitiation for our sins? Those, then, who hate him have the most evil death; because they die in their sins, who are not reconciled to our God through him: *for the Lord will redeem the souls of his servants*[^48]. For death is to be understood as either most evil or best according to the soul, not according to the reproaches or honors of bodies which men see. *And none of those who hope in him shall offend.* This is the measure of human justice: that the mortal life, however much it advances, since it cannot be without fault, in this does not offend, while it hopes in him in whom is the remission of faults. Amen.
