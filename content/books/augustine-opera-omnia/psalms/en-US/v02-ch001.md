# Exposition of Psalm 33

**1.** This psalm seems to have nothing obscure in its text, nothing that needs an expositor; but its title makes us attentive and calls for those who knock. For just as it is here written that *blessed is the man who hopes in him*[^1], so let us all hope that he will open to those who knock. For he would not urge us to knock if he were unwilling to open to those who knock. If it sometimes happens that one who had resolved always to keep the door shut, worn out, rises against his own resolve at the hand of the one knocking and opens, so as not to endure the knocking any longer, how much more ought we to hope that he will open more quickly who says, *Knock, and it shall be opened to you*[^2]? I knock now, with the intention of my heart, at the Lord God, that he may deign to reveal this mystery to us; let your Charity knock with me, by your intention to hear and your humility in praying for us. For it is, as we must admit, a hidden and great mystery.

**2.** The title of the psalm runs thus: *A psalm of David, when he changed his countenance before Abimelech, and he sent him away, and he departed*[^3]. We look in the Scriptures, in the deeds recorded for us about David, for when this happened, just as we find the title of another psalm: *When David fled from the face of his son Absalom*[^4]. For we read in the books of Kings and find when David fled from the face of his son Absalom; and it is most true, because it happened, and what happened was written down; and although the title of the psalm is written thus in a mystery, still it is drawn from a deed that was done. So too I believe that what is here written, *When he changed his countenance before Abimelech, and he sent him away, and he departed,* is written in the books of Kings, where everything pertaining to the deeds of David is written for us; but we do not find it, and yet we find something from which it appears that this is drawn. For it is written that when David was fleeing his persecutor Saul, he betook himself to Achish king of Gath, that is, to the king of a certain nation neighboring the kingdom of the Jews; there he lay hidden, to escape the persecution of Saul. His glory was still recent, the glory for which his good deed earned him envy, when he killed Goliath and in a single battle won glory and security of the kingdom for both king and people. But Saul, who had been in anguish when Goliath challenged them, once Goliath was laid low, began to be his enemy by whose hand he had slain the enemy, and he envied the glory of David; especially because the people, set in exultation and with a chorus now formed, the women sang the glory of David, that Saul had struck down his thousands and David his ten thousands. Stirred by this, because the boy had begun to have greater glory through a single battle and was now set above the king in everyone's praises, Saul, as is the way of the plague of spite and the pride of the world, began to envy and to persecute him. Then David, as I said, had betaken himself to the king of Gath, who was called Achish. But it was suggested to that king that he was harboring one who had begun to have great glory among the people of the Jews, and it was said to him, *Is this not David, for whom the Israelite women made a chorus, saying: Saul struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands?*[^5] Now if Saul had begun to envy him on account of this glory, was David not to fear that this king too, to whom he had fled, might wish to crush one whom he could have as an enemy near at hand if he kept him safe? *He feared him also, and,* as it is written, *he changed his countenance before them, and he behaved frantically, and beat upon the gates of the city like a drum, and was carried in his own hands, and fell down at the doors of the gate, and the spittle ran down upon his beard*[^6]. That king with whom he was hiding saw him and said to his men, *Why have you brought this madman to me? Is he going to come into my house?*[^7] And so he sent him away, casting him out; and David withdrew from there unharmed by this feigning of madness. On account of this feigning of madness, then, what is here written seems to belong to that history: *A psalm of David, when he changed his countenance before Abimelech, and he sent him away, and he departed.* But that man was Achish, not Abimelech. Only the name does not seem to fit, for the deed is designated in almost the very words alike in the Psalms and in the book of Kings where it is written. For this very reason the change of name ought all the more to move us to seek the sacrament. For neither did that take place without cause, although it did take place, but because it prefigured something; nor was this written down without cause, with the name changed.

**3.** Surely you see, brothers, the depth of the sacraments. If there is no mystery in Goliath's being killed by a boy, there is no mystery in his changing his countenance and behaving frantically and beating upon the gates like a drum and falling at the gates of the city and the doors of the gate, and the spittle running down upon his beard. How can it be that this did not signify something, when indeed the Apostle plainly says, *Now these things happened to them as a figure; but they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^8]? If the manna signifies nothing, of which the Apostle says, *And they ate the same spiritual food*[^9]; if it signifies nothing that the sea was divided and the people led through the midst, that they might escape the persecution of Pharaoh, though the Apostle says, *For I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea*[^10]; if it signifies nothing that water flowed from the struck rock, though the Apostle says, *And the rock was Christ*[^11]; if, then, those things signify nothing, although they were done; if, finally, the two sons of Abraham, born according to the human order of birth, signify nothing, and yet the Apostle calls those two sons the two Testaments, the Old and the New, and says, *These are the two Testaments, which are an allegory*[^12]; if, then, those things signify nothing which you see, on apostolic authority, to have been done in a mystery of things to come, we ought to judge that this also signifies nothing which I just now related to you from the book of Kings about David. Therefore it does not signify nothing, both that the name was changed and that it was said *before Abimelech.*

**4.** Attend with me. For everything I have just said pertains, as it were, to the hand of the one knocking; it is not yet opened. We knocked when we said these things; you too knocked when you heard them; let us still knock by praying, that the Lord may open to us. We have the interpretation of the Hebrew names; learned men were not lacking who rendered the names for us from Hebrew into Greek, and from there into Latin. Consulting these names, then, we find that Abimelech is interpreted as "my father's kingdom," and Achish as "how is he." Let us attend to these names; from there it begins to be opened to us who knock. If you ask what Achish is, the answer is "how is he." "How is he" is the saying of one who wonders and does not understand. Abimelech, "my father's kingdom"; David, "strong in hand." David is a figure of Christ, just as Goliath is a figure of the devil; and David's laying Goliath low is Christ, who killed the devil. But what is Christ, who killed the devil? Humility killed pride. When, therefore, I name Christ, my brothers, humility above all is commended to us. For he made a way for us through humility; because through pride we had withdrawn from God, we could not return to him except through humility, and we had no one to set before ourselves to imitate. For the whole mortality of human beings had swollen with pride. And if there existed any man humble in spirit, as were the prophets and patriarchs, the human race disdained to imitate humble men. Therefore, lest a human being disdain to imitate a humble human being, God became humble, so that at least in this way the pride of the human race would not disdain to follow the footsteps of God.

**5.** Now there was, as you know, a sacrifice of the Jews, formerly according to the order of Aaron in the victims of cattle, and this was in a mystery; there was not yet the sacrifice of the body and blood of the Lord, which the faithful know, and those who have read the Gospel, the sacrifice that is now spread throughout the whole world. Set before your eyes, then, the two sacrifices, both the one according to the order of Aaron and this one according to the order of Melchizedek. For it is written, *The Lord has sworn, and he will not repent: you are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek*[^13]. Of whom is it said, *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek*? Of our Lord Jesus Christ. Who was Melchizedek? The king of Salem. Salem was a city before, the one that afterward, as the learned have handed down, was called Jerusalem. So before the Jews reigned there, that priest Melchizedek was there, who is described in Genesis as priest of God Most High. He met Abraham when he freed Lot from the hand of his pursuers and laid low those by whom Lot was held, and freed his kinsman; after the freeing of his kinsman, Melchizedek met him. And so great was Melchizedek that by him Abraham was blessed. He brought forth bread and wine, and blessed Abraham, and Abraham gave him tithes. See what he brought forth, and whom he blessed. And it was said afterward, *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.* David said this in the Spirit, long after Abraham; but Melchizedek lived in the times of Abraham. Of what other does he say, *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek,* except of him whose sacrifice you know?

**6.** The sacrifice of Aaron, then, was taken away, and there began to be a sacrifice according to the order of Melchizedek. Therefore *some unknown one changed his countenance.* Who is this unknown one? Let him not be unknown, for our Lord Jesus Christ is known. He willed our salvation to be in his body and blood. And from what did he commend his body and blood? From his humility. For unless he were humble, he would neither be eaten nor drunk. Look at his loftiness: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^14]. Behold the eternal food; but the angels eat it, the heavenly Powers eat it, the celestial Spirits eat it, and in eating they are filled, and what satisfies and gladdens them remains whole. But what human being could reach that food? Whence a heart fit for that food? It was necessary, then, that that table should turn to milk and reach the little ones. And how does food become milk? How is food turned into milk, unless it passes through the flesh? For this is what the mother does. What the mother eats, that the infant eats; but because the infant is less fit to feed on bread, the mother makes the bread flesh, and through the humility of the breast and the juice of milk, she feeds the infant from that same bread. How, then, did the Wisdom of God feed us from that same bread? *Because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^15]. See, then, the humility: for a human being ate the bread of angels, as it is written, *He gave them the bread of heaven; man ate the bread of angels*[^16]; that is, that eternal Word by which the angels are fed, which is equal to the Father, a human being ate; because *though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal with God.* The angels are filled with him; *but he emptied himself,* so that a human being might eat the bread of angels, *taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man; he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross*[^17]; so that now from the cross the flesh and blood of the Lord, a new sacrifice, might be commended to us. Because *he changed his countenance before Abimelech,* that is, before the father's kingdom. For the father's kingdom was the kingdom of the Jews. How the father's kingdom? The kingdom of David, the kingdom of Abraham. For the kingdom of God the Father is the Church rather than the people of the Jews; but according to the flesh, the father's kingdom is the people of Israel. For it was said, *And God will give him the throne of David his father*[^18]. It is shown, then, that according to the flesh David is the father of the Lord; but according to divinity Christ is not the son but the Lord of David. The Jews indeed know Christ according to the flesh; according to divinity they do not know him. Therefore he put a question to them and said, *Whose son do you say that the Christ is?* And they answered, *The son of David. And he: How then does David in the spirit call him Lord, when he says: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool under your feet? If, then, David in the spirit calls him Lord, how is he his son? And they could not answer him*[^19]; because they knew nothing in the Lord Christ except what was seen with the eyes, not what was understood with the heart. But if they had eyes within, as they had without, from what they saw without they would understand the son of David; from what they understood within they would understand the Lord of David.

**7.** *He changed his countenance, then, before Abimelech.* What is *before Abimelech*? Before the father's kingdom. What is "before the father's kingdom"? Before the Jews. *And he sent him away, and he departed.* Whom did he send away? He sent away the very people of the Jews, and he departed. You look now for Christ among the Jews, and you do not find him. Why did he send away and depart? Because *he changed his countenance.* For clinging to that sacrifice according to the order of Aaron, they did not hold the sacrifice according to the order of Melchizedek; and they lost Christ, and the Gentiles began to have him, to whom he had not earlier sent heralds. For to the Jews he had sent heralds; David himself, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and the rest of the prophets he had sent, and few from among them came to know him, and those few in comparison with the perishing; for there were many. We read that there were thousands. For it is written, *A remnant shall be saved*[^20]. But you look now for circumcised Christians, and you do not find them. Yet in the recent times of the faith there were many thousands of Christians from among the circumcision. You look now, and you do not find them. Rightly you do not find them. For *he changed his countenance before Abimelech, and he sent him away, and he departed.* And before Achish he changed his countenance, and he sent him away, and he departed. For the names were changed so that the change of names might rouse us to the meaning of the mystery; lest we suppose that nothing is narrated or recalled in the Scriptures of the Psalms except what is found done in the books of Kings, and so not seek there the figures of things to come, but take them as mere deeds done. When, therefore, the names are changed, what is said to you? Something here is shut; knock, do not cling to the letter; for the letter kills, but desire the spirit, for the spirit gives life[^21]; the spiritual understanding saves the believer.

**8.** How, then, did he send away king Achish? Attend, brothers. I said Achish is interpreted "how is he." Recall the Gospel: when our Lord Jesus Christ was speaking of his body, he said, *Unless one eats my flesh and drinks my blood, he will not have life in himself; for my flesh is food indeed, and my blood is drink indeed*[^22]. And his disciples who were following him were terrified and shuddered at the saying, and not understanding they supposed that our Lord Jesus Christ was saying something hard, namely, that they were to eat his flesh which they saw, and to drink his blood; and they could not endure it, as though saying, "How is he?" For error and ignorance and folly are in the person of king Achish. For where it is said "how is he," he is not understood; where he is not understood, there is the darkness of ignorance. So in them was the kingdom of ignorance, like king Achish; that is, the kingdom of error ruled over them. But he was saying, *Unless one eats my flesh and drinks my blood.* Because he had changed his countenance, it seemed like frenzy and madness to give his flesh to be eaten by men, and his blood to be drunk. Therefore David was thought to be, as it were, mad, when Achish himself said, "You have brought this madman to me." Does it not seem madness: "Eat my flesh, and drink my blood"? And in saying, *Whoever does not eat my flesh and drink my blood will not have life in himself,* he seems to be, as it were, mad. But to king Achish he seems mad, that is, to the foolish and the ignorant. Therefore he sent them away and departed; understanding fled from their heart, lest they be able to grasp him. And what did they say? As it were, "How is he?"—which is what Achish means. For they said, *How can this man give us his flesh to eat?*[^23] They thought the Lord was mad, and did not know what he was saying, and was raving. But he who knew what he was saying, in that change of his countenance and as it were frenzy and madness, was proclaiming the sacraments, and was behaving frantically, and beating upon the gates of the city like a drum.

**9.** We must ask what it is that he "behaved frantically" and "beat upon the gates like a drum." Not without cause was it said, *He fell down at the doors of the gate*; not without cause was it said, *And the spittle ran down upon his beard*; these things were not said in vain. With the reward of understanding, a lengthy sermon ought not to be burdensome. You know, brothers, that the Jews themselves, before whom he changed his countenance, and whom he sent away, and departed, are idle today. If those who lost Christ, whom he sent away and departed, have an empty idleness, we have a fruitful leisure, that we may understand Christ, who sent them away and came to us. Nothing was done in vain, even in that frenzy of David, where it is said that *he behaved frantically, and beat upon the gates of the city like a drum, and was carried in his own hands, and fell down at the doors of the gate, and the spittle ran down upon his beard. He behaved frantically;* what is *he behaved frantically*? He had affection. What is it to have affection? Because he had compassion on our infirmities; and therefore he willed to take up that very flesh, in which he might kill death. Having compassion on us, then, he is said to have had affection. Therefore the Apostle reproaches those who are hard and without affection. For reproaching certain people, he says, *Without affection, without mercy*[^24]. Where there is affection, there is mercy. Where is there mercy? He had mercy on us from above. For if he had been unwilling to empty himself, remaining in that form in which he was eternally equal to the Father, we should always have remained in death; but to free us from eternal death, to which the sin of pride had led us, he humbled himself, made subject unto death, even death on a cross. Therefore he behaved frantically, because he came even unto death on a cross. And since one who is crucified is stretched out on wood, and to make a drum the skin, that is, the hide, is stretched out on wood, it was said *and he beat like a drum,* that is, he was crucified, he was stretched out on wood. *He behaved frantically,* that is, he had affection toward us, so as to lay down his soul for his sheep. *He beat like a drum.* How? *At the gates of the city.* The gate is that which is opened to us, so that we may believe in God. We had shut our gates against Christ, and had opened them to the devil; against eternal life we had a closed heart; but the Lord our God, because we human beings had a heart closed against eternal life, and could not see the Word which the angels see, opened the hearts of mortals by the cross, that is, he beat upon the gates of the city like a drum.

**10.** *And he was carried in his own hands.* But this, brothers, how could it be done in a human being? Who understands it? For who is carried in his own hands? A man can be carried in the hands of others; in his own hands no one is carried. How it may be understood in David himself, according to the letter, we do not find; but in Christ we do find it. For Christ was carried in his own hands when, commending his very body, he said, *This is my body*[^25]. For he was carrying that body in his own hands. This is the humility of our Lord Jesus Christ; it is much commended to human beings. To it he urges us, brothers, that we may live, that is, that we may imitate his humility; that we may strike down Goliath, and holding fast to Christ may conquer pride. *For he fell down at the doors of the gate.* What is *he fell down*? He cast himself down to humility. What is *at the doors of the gate*? At the beginning of faith, by which we are made safe. For no one begins except from the beginning of faith, as it is said in the Song of Songs, *You will come, and you will pass over from the beginning of faith*[^26]. We are to come face to face, as it is written, *Beloved, we are children of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be; we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^27]. *We shall see;* when? When these things have passed away. Hear also the apostle Paul: *We see now through a mirror in an enigma, but then face to face*[^28]. Before, then, we see face to face the Word which the angels see, we still have need of the doors of the gate, at which the Lord fell down, humbling himself even unto death.

**11.** What is it that *the spittle ran down upon his beard*? For in this *he changed his countenance before Abimelech,* or Achish, *and he sent him away, and he departed.* He sent away those who did not understand. To whom did he depart? To the Gentiles. Let us, then, understand what they could not. The spittle ran down upon the beard of David; what is *spittle*? As it were, the words of an infant; for spittle runs from infants. Were they not as it were infantile words: "Eat my flesh, and drink my blood"? But those infantile words veiled his power. For power is understood in the beard. The spittle, then, running down upon his beard, what is it but weak words veiling his power? Your Holiness has understood, I think, the title of this psalm. If we should now wish to expound the psalm, it is to be feared that what you have heard may slip from your hearts. We have expounded the title of this psalm in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ; since tomorrow is the Lord's Day, and we owe you a sermon, let us put it off until tomorrow, so that you may also gladly hear the text of the psalm.
