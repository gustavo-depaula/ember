# On the Same Psalm 118 (3)

**1.** [vv. 4-6.] Who is it, dearest ones, who says to the Lord: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly. Would that my ways were directed to keep your justifications. Then I will not be confounded, while I look into all your commandments?* Who is it who says this, except each member of Christ, or rather the whole body of Christ? And what is it: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly?* whether, *you commanded exceedingly;* or, *to be kept exceedingly?* Whichever of these we understand, this seems said against that memorable and noble sentence which the Greeks praise among their wise men, and the Latins by praising consent to: *Nothing too much.* For if this is true, that nothing be done too much; how is it true what is here said: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly?* when would God command anything either too much, or wish it to be kept too much, if everything excessive were worthy of reproof? We would say, then, that we are held by no authority of the Greek wise men, looking at what is written: *Has not God made foolish the wisdom of this world?*[^1] and we would rather believe that sentence to be false, by which it was said, *Nothing too much,* than the divine eloquence, where we read and sing: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly;* unless not Greek pride, but true reason recalled us. For "too much" is said of whatever is more than is fitting. For "too little" and "too much" are two things contrary to one another. "Too little" is what is less than is fitting; and "too much," more than is fitting. In the middle of these is the measure, which is said: It is enough. Since, then, it is useful in life and morals that we do absolutely nothing more than is fitting; surely we ought rather to confess than deny that the sentence is true: *Nothing too much.* But sometimes the Latin language so misuses this word, that we find "too much" (nimis) set for what is "greatly" (valde), both in the sacred letters, and we set it in our discourses. For here too: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly,* we understand only Greatly, if we understand rightly: and: I love you too much, if we say to someone most dear, we wish to be understood as loving not more than is fitting, but greatly. Finally that Greek sentence does not have this word which is read here: for there it is meden agan, which is "nothing too much"; but here it is sphodra, which is Greatly. But sometimes, as we said: "Too much" is found set for what is "Greatly," and we say it: whence some Latin manuscripts too do not have: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly*: but, *greatly.* Greatly, then, God commanded this, and greatly the commandments of God ought to be kept.

**2.** But humble piety or pious humility, and faith not unmindful of grace, attend to what it adds: *Would that,* he says, *my ways were directed to keep your justifications.* You have indeed commanded, but would that what you commanded be done in me. Where you hear *Would that,* recognize the voice of one wishing; but, the voice of one wishing being recognized, lay aside the pride of one presuming. For who would say that he wishes for what he so has in the power of his choice, that, needing no help, he can accomplish it? Therefore if a man wishes for what God commands; God must be asked, that he himself give what he commands. For from whom is it to be wished, except from him, from whom, the *Father of lights, every best gift and every perfect gift,* as holy Scripture witnesses, *descends*[^2]? But because of those who suppose that we are divinely helped to do righteousness only by this, that the commandments of God are brought to our knowledge, that, once known, they may be fulfilled now without any grace of God, by the powers of our will alone; he does not wish his ways to be directed to keep the justifications of God, except by his commandments now received, with him commanding. For to this pertains what he set before: *You have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly.* As if he said: I have now received the law, I now know it; for you have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly: and your commandments are holy, and just, and good; but sin works death for me through the good, unless your grace help. *Would that,* then, *my ways were directed to keep your justifications.*

**3.** *Then I will not be confounded, while I look into all your commandments.* The commandments of God, whether when they are read, or when they are recalled by memory, must be gazed at as a mirror, according to the apostle James saying: *If anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he will be compared to a man considering the face of his birth in a mirror; for he considered himself, and went away, and immediately forgot what kind he was: but he who looks into the perfect law of liberty, and remains, not made a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, he will be blessed in his deed*[^3]. Such this man wishes to be, that he may look as in a mirror at the commandments of God, and not be confounded; because he wishes to be not only a *hearer* of them, but a *doer.* Therefore he wishes his ways to be directed to keep the justifications of God. Whence directed, except by the grace of God? Otherwise he will have the law of God, not where he may rejoice, but where he may be confounded, if he wishes to look into the commandments which he does not do.

**4.** [v. 7.] *I will confess,* he says, *to you, O Lord, in the directing of my heart, in that I have learned the judgments of your righteousness.* This is not the confession of sins, but of praise: as he too says, in whom there was no sin: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^4]; and as it is written in the book of Ecclesiasticus: *These things you will say in confession: All the works of the Lord are very good*[^5]. *I will confess,* he says, *to you in the directing of my heart.* Certainly if my ways are directed, I will confess to you, because you did it, and this is your praise, not mine. For then *I will confess in that I have learned the judgments of your righteousness,* if I have a directed heart, namely with my ways directed to keep your justifications. For what will it profit me that I have learned them, if with a perverse heart I go off on crooked ways? for I will not rejoice in them, but be accused by them.

**5.** [v. 8.] Then he adds: *I will keep your justifications.* All of which are certainly connected from that which he said: *Would that my ways were directed to keep your justifications: then I will not be confounded, while I look into all your commandments*; and *I will confess to you in the directing of my heart,* and *I will keep your justifications.* But what is it that follows? *Do not forsake me even unto greatly;* or, as some manuscripts have, *even unto too much,* for what is *greatly*: for this word is Greek here too, that is, sphodra: as if he wishes to be forsaken by the Lord, but not *even unto greatly.* Far from it. But because God had forsaken the world by the desert of sins; he would have forsaken it *even unto greatly,* if not even so great a medicine had profited it, that is, the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord: but now, according to this prayer of the body of Christ, he did not forsake it *even unto greatly;* because *God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself*[^6]. This can also be understood thus, that this is the voice of him who, when he had said in his abundance: *I shall not be moved forever*[^7], as if confident in his own virtue; that God might show him that not by his desert, but in his own will he had furnished strength to his beauty, turned his face away from him, and he became disturbed. Finding himself, then, and now not presuming on himself, he cries: *Do not forsake me even unto greatly.* For if you have forsaken me, that without your help I may appear weak, do not *even unto greatly,* lest I perish. *You, then, have commanded your commandments to be kept exceedingly*: now I cannot excuse myself from ignorance; but since I am weak, *would that my ways be directed to keep your justifications. Then I will not be confounded, while I look into all your commandments*; then *I will confess to you in the directing of my heart, in that I have learned the judgments of your righteousness*; then *I will keep your justifications*: and if you have forsaken me, lest I glory in myself, do not even unto greatly, and, justified by you, I will glory in you.

[^2]: Jas 1:17
[^3]: Jas 1:23-25
[^4]: Matt 11:25
[^5]: Sir 39:16
[^6]: 2 Cor 5:19
