# Exposition of Psalm 74

**1.** [v. 1.] This psalm brings the medicine of humility to the swelling of pride, and consoles the humble in hope: doing this, that no one may presume proudly upon himself, nor any humble person despair of the Lord. For God's promise is ratified, certain, fixed and unshaken, faithful and free of all doubt, which consoles the afflicted. For the whole *life of man on earth,* as it is written, *is a trial*[^1]. Nor is prosperity to be chosen, as it were, and adversity alone to be shunned: but both are to be guarded against; the one lest it corrupt, the other lest it break; so that for every man, in whatever state of affairs he conducts himself in this life, there be no refuge but God, and no joy but in his promises. For this life, overflowing with whatever happiness, deceives many; God deceives no one. Since, then, for every man turned to God the delight is changed, the pleasures are changed (for they are not taken away, but changed): yet all our pleasures in this life are not yet present in reality; but the hope itself is so certain that it is to be preferred to all the pleasures of this world, as it is written: *Delight in the Lord.* And lest you should think you already have what he promises, he added at once: *And he will give you the petitions of your heart*[^2]. If, then, you do not yet have the petitions of your heart, from what do you delight in the Lord, except that you hold a sure promiser, who by promising made himself a debtor? So that the hope of this entreaty may remain with us, and we may be brought into what God promised, the title of this psalm speaks: *Unto the end, do not corrupt.* What is *do not corrupt*? What you promised, display. But when? *Unto the end.* There, then, let the mind's eye be directed; *unto the end*: let all things that occur on the way be passed by, that one may arrive at the end. Let the proud exult in present happiness; let them swell with honors, glitter with gold, overflow with households, be crowded about by the attendance of clients: these things pass away; they pass like a shadow. When that end comes, where all who now hope in the Lord rejoice, then to those others sadness without end will come. When the humble receive what the proud mock, then the swelling of the proud will be turned to grief. Then will be that voice which we know in the book of Wisdom: for then, seeing the glory of the saints, those who, while these were here humbled, bore with it; those who, while these were here exalted, did not consent—then they will say: *These are they whom we once held in derision.* Where they also say: *What did pride profit us? And what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All those things have passed away like a shadow*[^3]. Because they presumed upon corruptible things, their hope will be corrupted; but our hope will then be reality. So that the promise of God may remain whole and firm and certain toward us, we have said from the heart of faith: *Unto the end, do not corrupt.* Do not fear, then, that any of the powerful may corrupt the promises of God. He does not corrupt them, because he is truthful; he has no one more powerful by whom his promise might be corrupted: let us, then, be certain of the promises of God; and let us now sing, where the psalm begins.

**2.** [v. 2.] *We will confess to you, O God, we will confess to you, and we will call upon your name.* Do not call upon him before you confess; confess, and call upon him. For the one you call upon, you call into yourself. For what is it to call upon, except to call into oneself? If he is called upon by you—that is, if he is called into you—to whom does he come? He does not come to the proud. He is certainly on high; the exalted one does not reach him. To reach all lofty things, we raise ourselves up; and if we cannot reach them, we look for some contrivances or ladders, that, lifted up, we may reach the lofty: with God it is the opposite; he is both on high, and is reached by the humble. It is written: *The Lord is near to those who have bruised their heart*[^4]: the bruising of the heart is piety, humility. He who bruises himself is angry with himself: let him have himself for an angry judge, that he may have God favorable; let him have himself for a judge, that he may have God for a defender. So God, being called upon, comes. To whom does he come? He does not come to the proud. Hear another testimony: *The Lord is high, and looks upon the lowly things; but the lofty he knows from afar*[^5]. *The Lord is high, and looks upon the lowly things,* not from afar; but the lofty he knows from afar. And lest, when it was said that he looks upon the lowly, the proud should rejoice at impunity, as though he who dwells on high does not know them when they are proud; they are terrified, and it is said to them: He sees you, and knows you, but from afar. These he makes blessed, to whom he draws near: but you, he says, O proud, O lofty, will not be unpunished, because he knows you; you will not be blessed, because he knows you from afar. See, then, what you do; for if he knows, he does not pardon. Better, then, that he pardon than that he take note. For what is it to pardon (ignoscere), except not to take note (non noscere)? What is it not to take note? Not to advert to; for "advertence" (animadversio) is also commonly said of one who takes vengeance. Hear the man praying that he may pardon: *Turn away your face from my sins*[^6]. What, then, will you do, if he turns his face away from you? It is grievous, and to be feared, lest he desert you. Again, if he does not turn his face away, he takes note. God knows how to do this, God is able to do this—both to turn his face away from the sinner, and not to turn it away from one who confesses. And so in one place it is said to him: *Turn away your face from my sins;* and in another place it is said to him: *Do not turn your face away from me*[^7]. There: Turn away from my sins; here: Do not turn away from me. Confess, then, and call upon him: for by confessing you cleanse the temple to which he may come when called. Confess, and call upon him. Let him turn his face away from your sins; let him not turn it from you: let him turn his face away from what you made; let him not turn it from what he made. For he made you a man; you made your sins. Confess, then, and call upon him; say: *We will confess to you, O God: we will confess to you.*

**3.** The very repetition is a confirmation, that you may not regret having confessed. For you have not confessed to anyone cruel; not to an avenger, not to a mocker: confess in security. Hear another voice of the psalm exhorting: *Confess to the Lord, for he is good*[^8]. What is *for he is good*? Why are you afraid to confess? He is good; he pardons the one who confesses. Fear to confess to a human judge, lest perhaps he punish the one who has confessed; do not fear to confess to God: by confessing make favorable the one whom by denying you do not make ignorant. *We will confess to you, O God; we will confess,* and now in security *we will call upon your name.* We have emptied our hearts by confession; you have terrified, you have cleansed. Confession makes us humble, and you draw near to the humble, you who withdraw from the lofty. That there is firmness of a statement in repetition, we are taught in many places of the Scriptures. Hence it is that the Lord says: *Amen, amen*[^9]. Hence in some psalms: *So be it, so be it*[^10]: for signifying the thing, one *so be it* would suffice; for signifying firmness, the second *so be it* was added. Pharaoh, the king of Egypt—you know that, when Joseph was already there shut up in prison for the love of chastity, that same Pharaoh saw a dream known to us all: that seven lean cows had devoured seven fat ones; and again, that seven thin ears had devoured seven plentiful ones. And how did Joseph interpret it? If you remember, these are not two dreams, but one vision. *There is one interpretation of them,* he says; *but that you saw it again,* he says, *avails for confirmation*[^11]. I have said this so that you may not think repetition in the words of the holy tongue to be, as it were, an appetite for verbosity. Often there the repetition has the force of confirmation. *My heart is ready, O God,* he says; *my heart is ready*[^12]. In another place he says: *Wait for the Lord, act manfully; and let your heart be strengthened, and wait for the Lord*[^13]. Innumerable such things are throughout all the Scriptures. For these it is enough to have commended to you this manner of speaking, which you should observe in all similar cases: now attend to the matter. *We will confess to you,* he says, *and we will call upon you.* I have said why confession precedes calling upon him: because the one you call upon, you invite. But the one called upon does not wish to come, if you have been lifted up: if you have been lifted up, you cannot confess; and you do not deny to God the things he does not know. And so your confession does not teach him, but cleanses you.

**4.** And now he has confessed, he has called upon him: rather, they have confessed, they have called upon him; and it is said from the person of one: *I will recount all your wondrous works.* By confessing he emptied himself of evils, by calling upon him he filled himself with goods, by recounting he poured out that with which he had filled himself. And see, brothers; when they were confessing, they were many: *We will confess to you, O God; we will confess to you, and we will call upon your name.* For the hearts of those confessing are many, but those of believers are one. Why are the hearts of those confessing many, but those of believers one? Because men confess diverse sins; they believe one faith. Now, then, when Christ has begun to dwell in the inner man through faith[^14], and, being called upon, has begun to possess the one who confessed; the whole Christ comes to be—head, and body, and out of many, one. Hear now the words of Christ. For they seemed, as it were, not to be his words: *We will confess to you, O God; we will confess to you, and we will call upon your name.* Now the speech begins from the person of the head. But whether the head speaks, or the members speak, Christ speaks: he speaks from the person of the head, he speaks from the person of the body. But what was said? *They shall be two in one flesh. This is a great sacrament; but I,* he says, *speak in Christ and in the Church*[^15]. And he himself in the Gospel: *Therefore now they are not two, but one flesh*[^16]. For that you may know these to be in a certain way two persons, and again one by the joining of marriage, in Isaiah, as it were one person speaks, and says: *As on a bridegroom he has set a miter on me, and as a bride he has clothed me with an ornament*[^17]. He called himself bridegroom from the head, bride from the body. One, then, speaks; let us hear him, and in him let us too speak: let us be his members, that this voice may be ours too. *I will recount,* he says, *all your wondrous works.* Christ proclaims himself, he proclaims himself also in his members already existing, that he may draw others too, and that those who were not may come and be joined to his members, through which members his Gospel has been preached; and that one body may be made under one head, in one spirit, in one life.

**5.** [v. 3.] And what does he say? *When I receive,* he says, *the time, I will judge righteousnesses.* When will he judge righteousnesses? When he has received the time. The time itself is not yet. Thanks to his mercy: first he preaches righteousnesses, and so judges righteousnesses. For if he wished to judge before preaching, who would be found to be freed? Who would present himself to be acquitted? Now, then, is the time of preaching: *I will recount,* he says, *all your wondrous works.* Hear him recounting, hear him preaching; for if you despise him: *When I receive the time,* he says, *I will judge righteousnesses.* I forgive now, he says, the sins of one who confesses; I will not spare afterward one who despises. *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^18], is said in a psalm. *Mercy and judgment*: now mercy, afterward judgment; in which mercy sins are forgiven, in which judgment sins are punished. Do you wish not to fear the punisher of sins? Love the forgiver; do not refuse, do not exalt yourself, do not say: I have nothing for him to forgive me; for hear what follows: *When I receive the time, I will judge righteousnesses.* Did Christ receive the time? Did the Son of God receive the time? The Son of God did not receive the time; but the Son of man received the time. Yet he is the same, both the Son of God through whom we were made, and the Son of man through whom we were remade. Man having been assumed, he himself was not assumed; man having been changed for the better, he himself in no way changed for the worse, did not cease to be what he was, but received what he was not. What was he? *Though he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal to God*: these are the Apostle's words. And what did he receive? *But he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^19]. As he received the form of a servant, so too he received the time. Was he then changed? Was he diminished? Made meaner? Fallen into defect? God forbid. What, then, is this: *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*? He is said to have emptied himself by receiving an inferior nature, not by degenerating from his equal nature. Therefore, brothers, what is this: *When I receive the time, I will judge righteousnesses*? He received the time as the Son of man; he governs the times as the Son of God. Hear that as the Son of man he received the time of judging. He says in the Gospel: *He gave him power to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*[^20]. According to that he is the Son of God, he never received the power of judging; because he never lacked the power of judging: according to that he is the Son of man, he received the time, as of being born and suffering, as of dying and rising and ascending, so of coming and judging. His body too says these things in him; for he will not judge without them: indeed he says in the Gospel: *You shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^21]. Therefore the whole Christ says—that is, head and body in the saints: *When I receive the time, I will judge righteousnesses.*

**6.** [v. 4.] But now what? *The earth has flowed away.* If the earth has flowed away, from what did it flow away but from sins? For this reason they are also called offenses (delicta). To offend (delinquere) is, as it were, to flow down (defluere) from some liquid, away from the stability of the firmament of virtue and righteousness. For everyone sins by desire of lower things: just as he is strengthened by love of higher things, so he fails, and as it were melts away, by desire of lower things. Attending to this flowing of things in the sins of men, the merciful giver—the forgiver of sins, not yet the exactor of punishments—attends, and says: *The earth has flowed away, and all that dwell in it.* The earth itself, surely, flowed away in those who dwell in it. It is an explanation that follows, not an addition. As though you were to say: How has the earth flowed away? Were its foundations withdrawn, and was something in it sunk by some chasm? What I call earth means *all who dwell in it.* I found, he says, the earth a sinner; and what did I do? *I established its pillars.* What pillars did he establish? By "pillars" he means the apostles: so the apostle Paul speaks of his fellow-apostles: *Those who seemed,* he says, *to be pillars*[^22]. And what would those pillars be, unless they were strengthened by him? For by a certain earthquake even the pillars themselves wavered; in the Lord's passion all the apostles despaired. Therefore those pillars which wavered at the Lord's passion were strengthened at his resurrection. The beginning of the building cried out through his pillars, and in all those pillars the architect himself cried out. For the apostle Paul was one pillar of them, when he said: *Do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ?*[^23] Therefore: *I,* he says, *established its pillars*: I rose again; I showed that death is not to be feared, I showed to those who feared that not even the body itself perishes in those who die. The wounds terrified them; the scars strengthened them. The Lord Jesus Christ could have risen without any scar: for what was so great for that power, to restore the frame of the body to such wholeness that no trace at all of a past wound would appear? He had the power by which he could heal it even without a scar; but he willed to have something by which to strengthen the wavering pillars.

**7.** [v. 5.] We have now heard, brothers, what is not kept silent daily; let us hear what he cried out through these pillars. It is the time of hearing, on account of that terror: *I will judge righteousnesses, when I receive the time.* He is to receive the time of judging righteousnesses; you have, even now, the time of doing righteousnesses. If he kept silent, you would have nothing to do; but with the pillars strengthened, he cries out. What does he cry out? *I said to the wicked: Do not act wickedly.* He cries out, my brothers; surely you cry out, it delights you—hear him crying out. By him I beseech you, let that voice terrify you: those voices of yours ought not so much to delight us, as those voices to terrify you. *I said to the wicked: Do not act wickedly.* But they have already done so, and they are guilty; already *the earth has flowed away, and all that dwell in it.* Those who crucified Christ were pricked in their heart; they acknowledged their sin, they learned something from the apostles, that they might not despair of the indulgence of the preacher[^24]. For the physician had come, and therefore had not come to the healthy: *The healthy,* he says, *have no need of a physician, but those who are ill; I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance*[^25]. Therefore: *I said to the wicked: Do not act wickedly.* They did not listen. For long ago it was said to us; we did not listen, we fell, we were made mortal, we were born mortal: *the earth has flowed away.* Let them at least now hear, that they may rise, the physician who came to the sick: him whom the healthy refused to hear, that they might not fall; let those lying down hear, that they may rise. *I said to the wicked: Do not act wickedly.* What can we do: We have already done it. *And to those who offend: Do not exalt your horn.* What is this? If you have committed iniquity through desire, do not defend it through arrogance: confess, if you have done it. For he who does not confess, and is wicked, exalts his horn. *I said to the wicked: Do not act wickedly; and to those who offend: Do not exalt your horn.* The horn of Christ will be exalted in you, if your horn is not exalted. Your horn is from iniquity; the horn of Christ is from majesty.

**8.** [vv. 6-8.] *Do not, then, be lifted up: do not speak iniquity against God.* Hear now the voices of many; let each one hear, and be pricked. What are men accustomed to say? Does God truly judge concerning human affairs? And is there that judgment of God? Or does he indeed care what is done on earth? So many wicked men overflow with happiness; the innocent are pressed down with troubles! But to that man some evil befell, God chastising and admonishing, and he knows his own conscience; he knows that he can suffer something according to the merit of his sins: from what does he argue against God? Because he cannot say, I am righteous; what do we suppose he says? There are worse wicked men, and yet they do not suffer these things. This is the iniquity that men speak against God. But see how iniquitous it is: while he wishes himself to seem righteous, he makes God unrighteous. For he who says: I unjustly suffer what I suffer; makes him unjust by whose judgment he suffers, and himself just, who unjustly suffers. I ask you, my brothers, is this fair, that God be believed unjust, and you just? But when you say these things, you speak iniquity against God.

**9.** What does he say in another psalm? *These things you have done*: certain sins having been enumerated. *These things,* he says, *you have done, and I kept silent.* What is *I kept silent*? He is never silent in his precept, but for the time he is silent in punishment; he rests from vengeance, he does not pronounce sentence upon the condemned. But that man says this: I did this and that, and God did not take vengeance; behold, I am sound, no evil has befallen me. *These things you have done, and I kept silent: you suspected the iniquity, that I would be like you.* What is *that I would be like you*? Because you are wicked, you supposed me wicked too; as though I were an approver of your crimes, not an opponent, not an avenger. And what does he say to you afterward? *I will reprove you, and set you before your own face*[^26]. What is this? Because now, by sinning, you place yourself behind your own back, you do not see yourself, you do not look at yourself; I will set you before yourself, and inflict punishment upon you from yourself. So too here. *Do not speak iniquity against God.* Attend. Now many speak this iniquity; but they do not dare to do so openly, lest they be shunned as blasphemers by pious men: in their heart they gnaw at these things, within they feed on such wicked food; it delights them to speak against God, and even if they do not break forth with the tongue, they are not silent in the heart. Hence in another psalm it is said: *The fool said in his heart: There is no God*[^27]. *The fool said;* but he feared men: he did not wish to say it where men would hear; and he said it there where he himself would hear, the one of whom he said it. Therefore here too in this psalm, dearly beloved, attend, since, having said this: *Do not speak iniquity against God,* he saw many doing this in their heart, and he added: *For neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the desert mountains; for God is judge.* God is the judge of your iniquities. If he is God, he is present everywhere. Where will you take yourself away from the eyes of God, so as to speak in some quarter what he does not hear? If God judges from the east, withdraw into the west, and say whatever you wish against God; if from the west, go into the east, and speak there; if he judges from the desert mountains, go into the midst of the peoples, where you may mutter to yourself. He judges from no place, who is everywhere hidden, everywhere public; whom no one is permitted to know as he is, and whom no one is allowed not to know. See what you do. You speak iniquity against God. *The Spirit of the Lord has filled the whole world* (another Scripture says this); *and that which contains all things has knowledge of the voice: therefore he who speaks unjust things cannot lie hidden*[^28]. Do not, then, conceive of God in places; he is with you, such as you will have been. What is "such as you will have been"? Good, if you have been good; and he will seem evil to you, if you have been evil: but a helper if you have been good, an avenger if you have been evil. There you have a judge in your secret place. Wishing to do some evil, you withdraw from the public into your house, where none of your enemies may see; from the open parts of your house, set before the face, you remove yourself into your chamber; you fear a witness in your chamber too from elsewhere, you withdraw into your heart, there you ponder: he is more inward than your heart. Wherever you flee, then, there he is. Whither will you flee from yourself? Will you not, wherever you flee, follow yourself? But since he is more inward even than you yourself, there is nowhere to flee from God when angry, except to God when appeased: there is, in short, nowhere to flee. Do you wish to flee from him? Flee to him. Therefore do not speak iniquity against God, not even there where you speak. *Iniquity,* he says, *he has pondered on his bed*[^29]. What has he pondered on his bed? His heart is his bed, saying: *Sacrifice the sacrifice of righteousness, and hope in the Lord.* But above he said: *Speak in your hearts, and be pricked with compunction in your chambers*[^30]. As many prickings of crimes as you have there, have so many compunctions of confessions there. Where, then, you speak iniquity against God, there he judges you: he does not defer the judgment, but the penalty. He already judges, he already knows, he already sees: the penalty remains; when it has been presented, you will also have the penalty, when the face has appeared of that man who here was mocked, who was judged, who was crucified, who stood before a judge; when he has appeared judging in his presence, then you will have the penalty, if you have not corrected yourself. What, then, shall we do now? Let us anticipate his face. Anticipate him in confession: he will come gentle whom you had made angry. *Nor from the desert mountains; for God is judge*: not from the east, not from the west, not from the desert mountains. Why? *For God is judge.* If he were in some place, he would not be God: but because God is judge, not a man, do not await him from places. His place you will be, if you are good, if, having confessed, you call upon him.

**10.** *This one he humbles, and this one he exalts.* Whom does that judge humble, whom does he exalt? Attend to those two in the temple, and you see whom he humbles, and whom he exalts. *They went up into the temple to pray,* he says; *one a Pharisee, and the other a publican.* The Pharisee said: *I thank you, that I am not as the rest of men, unjust, robbers, adulterers, as is also this publican: I fast twice in the week; I give tithes of all that I possess.* He had gone up to the physician, and was showing his sound members; he was covering his wounds. What, then, of that other, who knew better whence he might be healed? *But the publican stood afar off, and beat his breast.* You see him standing afar off: yet he drew near to the one he was calling upon. *And he beat his breast, saying: God, be merciful to me a sinner. Amen I say to you; that publican went down justified rather than that Pharisee: for everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled; and he who humbles himself shall be exalted*[^31]. The verse of this psalm has been expounded. What does God the judge do? *This one he humbles, and this one he exalts;* he humbles the proud, he exalts the humble.

**11.** [v. 9.] *For in the hand of the Lord there is a cup of pure wine full of mixture.* Rightly. *And he has poured out from this into that; yet its dregs are not emptied out: all the sinners of the earth shall drink.* Be renewed a little; there is something obscure here: but, as we heard in the most recent reading of the Gospel: *Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you*[^32]. But you say: Where shall I knock, that it may be opened to me? *Neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the desert mountains; for God is judge.* If he is present here and there, and absent nowhere, where you stand, there knock; there only stand, because by standing one knocks. What, then, is this? This first question arises: *full of pure wine, with mixture*: how *pure,* if *with mixture*? Besides: when he says *a cup in the hand of the Lord,* I am speaking to those instructed in the Church of Christ; surely you ought not to paint God in your heart as circumscribed in human form, lest, the temples being closed, you fashion idols in your hearts. This cup, then, signifies something. We shall find it. But *in the hand of the Lord* means in the power of the Lord: for the hand of God is used to signify the power of God. For of men too it is often said: He has this in hand; that is, he has it in his power, he does it when he wills. *It is full of pure wine, with mixture.* He himself consequently expounded it: *He has poured out,* he says, *from this into that; yet its dregs are not emptied out.* Behold whence it was full of mixed wine. Let it not, then, frighten you, that it is both pure and mixed: pure on account of its sincerity, mixed on account of the dregs. What, then, is the wine there, and what the dregs? And what is: *He has poured out from this into that,* in such a way that its dregs were not emptied out?

**12.** Recall whence he came to this: *This one he humbles, and this one he exalts.* What was figured for us in the Gospel by two men, the Pharisee and the publican, taking this more broadly, let us understand two peoples, that of the Jews and that of the Gentiles: the people of the Jews, that Pharisee; the people of the Gentiles, that publican. The people of the Jews boasted of their own merits; that of the Gentiles confessed its own sins. Whoever knows in the holy Letters both the apostolic Epistles and the Acts of the Apostles, sees there what I say: not to make it long, how the apostles exhorted the Gentiles not to despair because they had lain in great sins; and how they restrained the Jews from exalting themselves as though by the justifications of the Law, and from supposing themselves therefore righteous, but the Gentiles to be sinners, because the Jews had both the Law, and the temple, and the priesthood[^33]. But those others were all worshipers of idols, venerators of demons, set, indeed, afar off; as that publican stood afar off. As those others withdrew by being proud, so these drew near by confessing. *A cup,* then, *full of pure wine in the hand of the Lord,* as far as the Lord grants me to understand (for another may understand better, for such is the obscurity of the Scriptures: it is difficult that they bring forth a single meaning. Yet whatever meaning emerges, it must agree with the rule of faith: we neither envy our betters, nor as little ones do we despair. What seems to us I tell to Your Charity, not so as to stop your ears against others who may perhaps say something better). *A cup full of pure wine, with mixture* seems to me to be the Law that was given to the Jews, and all that Scripture of what is called the Old Testament; there are the weights of all its meanings. For there the New Testament lies hidden, as it were in the dregs of bodily sacraments. The circumcision of the flesh is the matter of a great sacrament, and from it is understood the circumcision of the heart. That temple of Jerusalem is the matter of a great sacrament, and from it is understood the body of the Lord. The land of promise is understood as the kingdom of heaven. The sacrifice of victims and cattle has a great sacrament; but in all those kinds of sacrifices is understood that one sacrifice and the one victim on the cross, the Lord: in place of all which sacrifices we have one, because those prefigured these—that is, by those, these were prefigured. That people received the Law; it received commands just and good. What is so just as: *You shall not kill, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not steal, You shall not bear false witness, Honor your father and mother, You shall not covet your neighbor's property, You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, You shall adore one God, and him alone shall you serve*[^34]? All these belong to the wine. But those carnal things settled, as it were, so that they remained with them, and from them all the spiritual understanding was poured out. But *a cup in the hand of the Lord,* that is, in the power of the Lord; *of pure wine,* that is, of the sincere Law; *it is full of mixture,* that is, with the dregs of bodily sacraments. And because *this one he humbles,* the proud Jew, *and this one he exalts,* the confessing Gentile, *he has poured out from this into that,* that is, from the Jewish people into the Gentile people. What did he pour out? The Law: the spiritual understanding was strained out from it; *yet its dregs are not emptied out,* because all the carnal sacraments remained with the Jews. *All the sinners of the earth shall drink.* Who shall drink? *All the sinners of the earth.* Who are the sinners of the earth? The Jews were indeed sinners, but proud; the Gentiles, however, were sinners, but humble: *All sinners shall drink;* but see who the dregs, who the wine. For those, by drinking the dregs, vanished away; these, by drinking the wine, were justified: I would dare to say even made drunk, and I will not fear; and would that you all might be so made drunk! Recall: *How glorious is your inebriating cup!*[^35] For what? Do you suppose, my brothers, that all those who by confessing Christ were even willing to die were sober? They were drunk to such a degree that they did not recognize their own. All their kinsmen, who strove to turn them away from the hope of heavenly rewards by earthly enticements, were not recognized, were not heard by the drunken. Were they not drunk, whose heart had been changed? Were they not drunk, whose mind had been estranged from this world? *All the sinners of the earth,* he says, *shall drink.* But who shall drink the wine? Sinners shall drink, but not that they remain sinners; that they may be justified, not that they may be punished.

**13.** [v. 10.] *But I*: for all drink; but apart, *I,* that is, Christ with his body; *will rejoice forever; I will sing to the God of Jacob*: in that promise to come at the end, of which it is said: *Do not corrupt*[^36]. *But I will rejoice forever.*

**14.** [v. 11.] *And I will break all the horns of sinners; and the horns of the righteous shall be exalted.* This is: *This one he humbles, and this one he exalts*[^37]. Sinners do not wish their horns to be broken for them, which without doubt will be broken at the end. Do you not wish him to break it then? Break it yourself today. For you heard above; do not despise it: *I said to the wicked: Do not act wickedly; and to those who offend: Do not exalt your horn*[^38]. When you heard: *Do not exalt your horn,* you despised it, and you exalted your horn; you will come to the end, where it will come to pass: *I will break all the horns of sinners, and the horns of the righteous shall be exalted.* The horns of sinners, the dignities of the proud; the horns of the righteous, the gifts of Christ: for by horns are understood high places. Hate earthly loftiness on earth, that you may be able to have the heavenly. You love the earthly; it does not admit you to the heavenly: and it will pertain to your confusion that your horn is broken; just as it will pertain to your glory, if your horn is exalted. Now, then, is the time of choosing; then it will not be. You will not be able to say: Let me be dismissed and I will choose: for the words have gone before: *I said to the wicked.* If I did not say it, prepare your excuse, prepare your defense: but if I did say it, anticipate it with confession, lest you come to condemnation; because then confession will be too late, and there will be no defense.
