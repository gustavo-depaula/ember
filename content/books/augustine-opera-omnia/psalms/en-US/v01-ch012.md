# Exposition of Psalm 12

**1.** [v 1.] *For Christ is the end of the Law unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^1]. *How long, Lord, will you forget me unto the end?* that is, do you put me off from understanding Christ spiritually, who is the Wisdom of God, and the right end of every intention of the soul. *How long do you turn away your face from me?* Just as God does not forget, so neither does he turn away his face; but the Scripture speaks after our manner: now God is said to turn away his face, while he does not give to the soul the knowledge of himself, the soul which does not yet have a pure eye of mind.

**2.** [v 2.] *How long shall I set counsel in my soul?* There is no need of counsel, except in adverse things. Therefore, *How long shall I set counsel in my soul*, was said thus, How long shall I be in adverse things? Or surely it is an answer, so that this is the sense, So long, Lord, will you forget me unto the end, and so long will you turn away your face from me, as I set counsel in my soul: so that, unless anyone sets counsel in his soul that he may perfectly work mercy, God does not direct him unto the end, nor offer him the full knowledge of himself, which is face to face. *Sorrow in my heart through the day?* is understood, how long shall I set? But *through the day* signifies continuation, so that day is understood as time; from which everyone desiring to be stripped sets sorrow in his heart, praying to ascend to eternal things, and not to suffer the human day.

**3.** [v 3.] *How long will my enemy be exalted over me?* either the devil, or carnal habit.

**4.** [v 4.] *Look upon me and hear me, Lord my God. Look*, is referred to what was said, *How long do you turn away your face from me? Hear*, is referred to what was said, *How long will you forget me unto the end? Enlighten my eyes, lest I ever sleep in death.* The eyes of the heart must be understood, lest they be closed by a delightful failing into sin.

**5.** [v 5.] *Lest at any time my enemy say: I have prevailed against him.* The insulting of the devil is to be feared. *Those who trouble me will exult, if I am moved.* The devil and his angels: who did not exult over the righteous man Job, when they troubled him; because he was not moved, that is, did not withdraw from the stability of faith.

**6.** [v 6.] *But I have hoped in your mercy.* Because the very fact that a man is not moved and remains fixed in the Lord, he ought not to attribute to himself, lest, while he boasts that he was not moved, he be moved by that very pride. *My heart will exult in your salvation*: in Christ, in the Wisdom of God. *I will sing to the Lord who has granted good things to me*: spiritual good things, not pertaining to the human day. *And I will sing to the name of the Lord most high*: that is, with joy I give thanks, and in a most ordered way I use the body, which is the singing of the spiritual soul. But if any difference is here to be considered: *I will sing* with the heart, *I will make melody* with works, to the Lord, which he alone sees: but *to the name* of the Lord, which becomes known among men: which is useful not to him, but to us.

[^1]: Rom 10:4
