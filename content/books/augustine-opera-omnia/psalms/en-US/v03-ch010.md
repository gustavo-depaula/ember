# Exposition of Psalm 59

**1.** [vv. 1-2] The title of this psalm is somewhat lengthy; but let it not terrify us, because the Psalm is brief. So let us attend as though we had heard a somewhat lengthy Psalm: since in the name of Christ we speak in the Church of God to those nourished and to be nourished, and to those not strangers to the savor of these Scriptures from which the world is a stranger, these things ought not always to be as if new to you. For if you have ruminated with delight in the mouth of thought the things you have very often heard, and have not buried them, as it were in the belly, by forgetfulness; that very recollection and memory of yours will be able to help us much, that we may not speak much in unfolding, as to the untaught, the things we know that you already know. Surely we remember that you have often heard what we say: it is scarcely possible to find in the Psalms voices except those of Christ and the Church, or of Christ only, or of the Church only, which certainly we too are in part. And through this, when we recognize our own voices, we cannot recognize them without affection: and we are the more delighted, when we feel ourselves to be from there. David the king was one man, but he did not figure one man: namely, when he figured the Church consisting of many, stretched out unto the ends of the earth; but when he figured one man, he figured him who is the mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus. In this psalm, then, or rather in the title of this psalm, certain victorious deeds of David are spoken of, that he acted bravely in subduing certain enemies, and making them tributary; when, after the death of Saul his persecutor, he received his kingdom manifestly in Israel. For even before he suffered persecution, he was king, but was known to God alone. Afterward, then, his kingdom now being manifest, and evidently and eminently received, he subdued those who are mentioned in this title; and the title of the Psalm was marked thus: *Unto the end, for those who will be changed, for the inscription of a title, for David himself, for teaching, when he set fire to Mesopotamia of Syria, and Syria of Sobal, and Joab turned back and struck Edom, in the Valley of Salt twelve thousand.*[^1] We read these things in the books of Kings, that all those whom he named were subdued by David, that is, Mesopotamia of Syria, and Syria of Sobal, Joab, Edom. These things were done, and as they were done so they are there recorded, so they are read: let him read who wishes. But yet, as the prophetic spirit is wont in the titles of the Psalms to withdraw somewhat from the exact account of the deeds done, and to say something that is not found in the history, and from this rather to admonish, that such titles are not inscribed for the sake of knowing the deeds done, but for the sake of prefiguring things to come; as it was said that before Abimelech he changed his countenance, and let him go, and departed, while the Scripture of Kings indicates that he did this not before Abimelech, but before king Achish: so in this title too we find something by which we are admonished to something. For in that narration of the wars and brave deeds of king David, where all those whom we mentioned were subdued, we do not read that he set fire to anything. But here especially this is set down, which is not written there, that he set fire to Mesopotamia of Syria, and Syria of Sobal. Now, then, let us begin to discuss these things according to the significations of things to come, and to call back the dimness of the shadows into the light of the word.

**2.** *Unto the end*, what it is you know: *For the end of the Law is Christ*[^2]. Those who are changed you know. For who, except those who pass from the old life into the new? For far be it that here a blameworthy change be understood. Not as Adam was changed from righteousness to iniquity, and from delights to labor; but as those are changed to whom it was said: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^3]. But these are changed *for the inscription of a title.* You know the inscription of the title. The title was fixed above the cross of the Lord, written: *This is the King of the Jews*[^4]: they are changed for the inscription of this title who pass into the kingdom of Christ from the kingdom of the devil. Well are they changed for the inscription of this title. But they are changed, as follows, *for teaching.* For when he had said: *For those who will be changed for the inscription of a title;* he added, *for David himself, for teaching*: that is, they are changed not for themselves, but for David himself, and they are changed for teaching. For Christ is not so king as though about to reign over the age; since he plainly said: *My kingdom is not of this world*[^5]. Therefore let us pass into his teaching, if we wish to be changed for the inscription of the title, not for ourselves, but for David himself; so that those who live may now live no longer for themselves, but for him who died and rose again for them. When, then, would Christ change us, unless he had done what he said: *I came to send fire upon the world*[^6]? If, then, Christ came to send fire upon the world, certainly wholesomely and usefully; not as he is going to send the world into the fire. But how *fire upon the world*? Since, then, he came to send fire upon the world, let us now seek what is the Mesopotamia that was set on fire, what is the Syria of Sobal. Let us, then, ask the interpretations of the names according to the Hebrew speech, in which this Scripture first spoke. Mesopotamia they say is interpreted "Raised-up calling." Now the whole world has been raised up by the calling. Syria is interpreted "Lofty." But that which was lofty was set on fire and humbled: and as that was humbled which had been exalted, so let that be exalted which had been humbled. Sobal is interpreted "Vain old age." Thanks be to Christ who set it on fire. When the old shrubs are set on fire, green growth comes up; and more briskly and more abundantly and more greenly the new are born, when the fire has gone before in the burning of the old. Let not, then, the fire of Christ be feared; it consumes hay. For all flesh is hay, and all the splendor of man is like the flower of hay. He set fire, then, to those with that fire. *And Joab turned back.* Joab is interpreted "Enemy." The enemy was turned: understand it as you wish. If turned to flight, it is the devil; if turned to faith, it is the Christian. How to flight? From the heart of the Christian: *The prince of this world*, he says, *has now been cast out*[^7]. But the Christian turned to the Lord, why an enemy turned? Because made faithful, who had been an enemy. *He struck Edom.* Edom is interpreted "Earthly." That earthly one ought to be struck. For why should the earthly one live, who ought to live as heavenly? The earthly life, then, was killed; let the heavenly one live. For as we have borne the image of the earthly, let us bear also the image of him who is from heaven. See him being killed: *Mortify your members that are upon the earth*[^8]. But when he had struck Edom, he struck *twelve thousand in the Valley of Salt.* Twelve thousand is the perfect number, to which perfect number the number twelve of the Apostles too is assigned: for not in vain, except because through the whole world the word was to be sent. But the Word of God which is Christ is in the clouds, that is, in the preachers of truth. But the world consists of four parts. Its four parts are most known to all, and often mentioned in the Scriptures: which were also called the four winds, east, west, north, and south. To all these four parts the word was sent, that in the Trinity all might be called. The number twelve is made by three times four. Deservedly, then, twelve thousand earthly ones were struck; the whole world was struck: for out of the whole world the Church was chosen, killed from the earthly life. Why, *in the Valley of Salt*? The valley is humility: salt signifies savor. For many are humbled, but vainly and foolishly: in vain old age they are humbled. One suffers tribulation for money, suffers tribulation for temporal honor, suffers tribulation for the conveniences of this life; he is going to suffer tribulation, and be humbled: why not for the sake of God? why not for the sake of Christ? why not for the sake of the savor of salt? Or do you not know it was said to you: *You are the salt of the earth;* and: *If the salt loses its savor, it is good for nothing else, except to be cast out*[^9]? It is good, then, to be wisely humbled. Behold now, are not the heretics humbled? Were not laws given against them even by men, against whom the divine laws reign, which had condemned them even before? Behold they are humbled, behold they are put to flight, behold they suffer persecution, but without savor; for foolishness, for vanity. For now the salt has lost its savor: therefore it has been cast out, that it may be trodden underfoot by men. We have heard the title of the Psalm; let us hear also the words of the Psalm.

**3.** [v. 3] *O God, you have rejected us, and destroyed us.*[^10] Surely it is not that David speaking, who struck, who set on fire, who subdued; and not those to whom he did these things, that those who were evil might be struck and rejected, and again be made alive and return that they might be good? This slaughter indeed David made, that one mighty in hand, our Christ, whose figure that man bore; he did these things, he gave this slaughter by his sword and his fire: for both he brought into this world. Both: *I came to send fire upon the world*[^11], you have in the Gospel; and: *I came to send a sword upon the earth*[^12], you have in the Gospel. He brought fire whence Mesopotamia of Syria and Syria of Sobal might be set on fire; he brought a sword whence Edom might be struck. Now, then, this slaughter was made for the sake of those who are changed for the inscription of the title for David himself. Let us hear, then, their voice: wholesomely they were struck, raised up let them speak. Let them say, then, those changed for the better, changed for the inscription of the title, changed for the teaching for David himself; let them say: *O God, you have rejected us, and destroyed us: you were angry, and had mercy on us.* You destroyed us, that you might build us up; you destroyed us badly built up, you destroyed the vain old age, that there might be a building into the new man, a building to remain forever. Deservedly *you were angry, and had mercy on us.* You would not have had mercy, unless you had been angry. You destroyed us in your anger; but your anger was against our old age, that the old age might be destroyed. But you had mercy on us for the sake of newness, for the sake of those who are changed for the inscription of the title: because even if our outer man is corrupted, yet the inner is renewed day by day.

**4.** [v. 4] *You have shaken the earth, and troubled it.*[^13] How was the earth troubled? By the conscience of sins. Where do we go? where do we flee, when that sword is brandished: *Repent; for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^14]? *You have shaken the earth, and troubled it. Heal its contritions, for it has been moved.* It is not worthy to be healed, if it has not been moved: but you speak, you preach, you threaten concerning God, you are not silent about the coming judgment, you admonish God's precept, you do not rest from these things; and he who hears, if he does not fear, if he is not moved, is not worthy to be healed. Another hears; he is moved, he is goaded, he beats his breast, he pours out tears: *Heal its contritions, for it has been moved.*

**5.** [v. 5] After these things, the earthly one struck, the old age set on fire, the man changed for the better, light made for those who were darkness, there follows what is written elsewhere: *Son, drawing near to the service of God, stand in righteousness and fear, and prepare your soul for temptation*[^15]. The first labor, that you displease yourself, that you fight down sins, that you be changed for the better: the second labor, in regard to the fact that you have been changed, to bear the tribulations and temptations of this age, and among them to persevere unto the end. Speaking, then, of these, signifying these, what does he add? *You have shown your people hard things*: now your people, made tributary after the victory of David. *You have shown your people hard things.* In what? In the persecutions which the Church of Christ endured, when so much blood of the martyrs was poured out. *You have shown your people hard things: you have given us to drink the wine of pricking.* What is, *of pricking*? Not of destruction. For it was not a destruction destroying, but a medicine burning. *You have given us to drink the wine of pricking.*

**6.** [v. 6] Why this? *You have given to those who fear you a sign, that they may flee from the face of the bow.*[^16] Through temporal tribulations, he says, you have signified to your own to flee from the wrath of everlasting fire. For the apostle Peter says: *It is time that judgment begin from the house of God.* And exhorting the martyrs to endurance, when the world raged, when slaughters were made by persecutors, when far and wide the blood of the faithful was poured out, when in chains, in prisons, in torments, Christians suffered many hard things; lest, then, in these hard things they fail, Peter addresses them: *It is time that judgment begin from the house of God; and if the beginning is from us, what will the end be for those who do not believe the Gospel of God? and if the just will scarcely be saved, where will the sinner and the ungodly appear*[^17]? What, then, will be in the judgment? The bow is stretched; it is still in threatening, not yet in presentation. And see what is in the bow. Is not the arrow to be sent forward? Yet the string is stretched backward in the contrary direction to that in which it is to be sent; and the more its stretching has gone backward, the greater the force with which it runs forward. What is it I have said? The more the judgment is deferred, the greater the force with which it will come. Therefore let us give thanks to God even for temporal tribulations, because he has given his people a sign, *that they may flee from the face of the bow*: so that his faithful, exercised in temporal tribulations, may be worthy to escape the damnation of eternal fire, which is to find all who do not believe these things. *You have given to those who fear you a sign, that they may flee from the face of the bow.*

**7.** [v. 7] *That your beloved may be rescued. Save me with your right hand, and hear me.*[^18] With your right hand, O Lord, save me: so save me that I may stand at the right hand. *Save me with your right hand*: I do not ask temporal safety; concerning this let your will be done. As to what may profit us for a time, we are utterly ignorant; for we do not know what we should pray for as we ought: but *save me with your right hand*, so that even if in this time I suffer some tribulations, the night of all tribulations being over, I may be found at the right hand among the sheep, not at the left among the goats. *Save me with your right hand, and hear me.* Because now I ask that which you wish to give; I do not cry with the words of my offenses by day, that you not hear, and by night, that you not hear, and not unto folly for me; but certainly unto admonition, adding savor from the Valley of Salt, that in tribulation I may know what I ask: but I ask eternal life; therefore hear me, because I ask your right hand. Let Your Charity understand, then, that every faithful one having the word of God in his heart, fearing with fear the future judgment, living approvably, lest through him the holy name of his Lord be blasphemed, beseeches many things according to the age, and is not heard; but unto eternal life is always heard. For who does not ask health, when he is sick? And yet perhaps it is still useful for him to be sick. It can happen that in this you are not heard: yet you are not heard according to your will, that you may be heard according to your benefit. But when you ask that which God gives you—that God give you eternal life, that God give you the kingdom of heaven, that God give you to stand at the right hand of his Son, when he comes to judge the earth—be secure; you will receive, if you do not now receive: for the time has not yet come that you should receive. You are heard, and do not know it: what you ask is done, even if you do not know in what it is done. The matter is in the root, not yet in the fruit. *Save me with your right hand, and hear me.*

**8.** [v. 8] *God has spoken in his holy one.*[^19] Why do you fear lest what God has spoken not come about? If you had some weighty and wise friend, how would you speak? He has spoken these things, it is necessary that what he has spoken come about: he is a weighty man, he does not use levity, he is not easily moved from his opinion, what he promised is fixed. But yet he is a man, who sometimes wishes to do what he promised, and cannot. About God there is nothing for you to fear: because he is truthful, it is established; because he is almighty, it is established; he cannot deceive you, he has the means to do it. Why, then, do you fear lest you be deceived? It is needful that you do not deceive yourself, and persevere unto the end, when he is going to give what he promised. *God has spoken in his holy one.* In what *holy one of his*? *God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself*[^20]. In that holy one, of whom you heard elsewhere: *O God, your way is in the holy one*[^21]. *God has spoken in his holy one. I will rejoice, and divide Shechem.* Because God has spoken this, it will come about: it is the voice of the Church, *God has spoken in his holy one.* It does not state the words which God spoke; but because *God has spoken in his holy one*, and nothing can come about except as God has spoken, consequently these things happen: *I will rejoice, and divide Shechem, and measure out the valley of tents.* Shechem is interpreted "Shoulders." But according to the history, Jacob, returning from Laban his father-in-law with all his own, hid in Shechem the idols which he had from Syria, where, having long sojourned, he was at last coming from there. And he made tents there for his sheep and herds, and called that place *Tents*[^22]. And these I will divide, says the Church. What is this, *I will divide Shechem*? If it be referred to the history where the idols were hidden, it signifies the Gentiles. I divide the Gentiles. What is, I divide? For faith is not of all. What is, I divide? Some will believe, others will not believe: but yet let those who believe not fear, among those who do not believe. For they are now divided by faith; afterward they will be divided in the judgment, the sheep at the right, the goats at the left. Behold, we find how the Church divides Shechem. How does it divide shoulders, according to the interpretation of the name? The shoulders are divided, so that some are weighed down by their own sins, others take up the burden of Christ. For he was seeking godly shoulders, when he said: *For my yoke is gentle, and my burden is light*[^23]. Another burden presses and weighs you down; but the burden of Christ lifts you up: another burden has weight; the burden of Christ has wings. For if you pull the feathers from a bird, you, as it were, take away a weight; and the more you take away the weight, the more it will remain on the earth. The one you wished to unburden lies there: it does not fly, because you took away the weight; let the weight return, and it flies. Such is the burden of Christ. Let men carry it; let them not be lazy: let those not be attended to who are unwilling to bear it; let those carry it who wish, and they will find how light it is, how sweet, how delightful, how it snatches up to heaven and rescues from earth. *I will divide Shechem, and measure out the valley of tents.* Perhaps because of Jacob's sheep, the valley of tents is understood as the nation of the Jews, and it too is divided: for those who believed passed over from there; the rest remained outside.

**9.** [v. 9] *Gilead is mine.*[^24] These are names read in the Scriptures of God. Gilead has the voice of its own interpretation and of a great mystery: for it is interpreted "Heap of testimony." How great a heap of testimony in the martyrs? *Gilead is mine*: mine is the heap of testimony, mine are the true martyrs. Let others die for their old vanity without salt; do they pertain to the heap of testimony? Because even if I deliver my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing. But when in a certain place the Lord admonished about keeping peace, he set salt before it: *Have*, he says, *salt in yourselves, and have peace among yourselves*[^25]. Therefore, *Gilead is mine*: but Gilead, that is, the heap of testimony, was evidently made in great tribulation. Then the Church was ignominious among men, then reproach was cast at that widow, because she was Christ's, because she bore the sign of the cross on her forehead: it was not yet honor; it was then a crime. When, then, it was not honor, but a crime, then the heap of testimony was made; and through the heap of testimony the love of Christ was enlarged; and through the enlargement of the love of Christ the nations were occupied. There follows: *And Manasseh is mine*: which is interpreted "Forgotten." For to this one it had been said: *You will forget the confusion forever, and you will not be mindful of the ignominy of your widowhood*[^26]. There was, then, sometime a confusion of the Church, which it has now forgotten: for of the confusion and ignominy of its widowhood it is no longer mindful. For when there was a certain confusion among men, the heap of testimony had been made. Now no one even remembers that confusion, when it was ignominy to be a Christian; now no one remembers it, now all have forgotten it, now *Manasseh is mine. And Ephraim is the strength of my head.* Ephraim is interpreted "Fruitfulness." Mine, he says, is the fruitfulness, and this fruitfulness is the strength of my head. For my head is Christ. And whence is fruitfulness his strength? Because unless the grain fell into the earth, it would not be multiplied, it would remain alone. Christ fell into the earth, then, in the passion, and fruitfulness followed in the resurrection. *And Ephraim is the strength of my head.* He hung and was despised: the grain was within, it had the power to draw all things after itself. As in a grain the numbers of seeds lie hidden; something abject appears to the eyes, but the power of turning matter into itself and bringing forth fruit is hidden: so in the cross of Christ the power was hidden; the weakness appeared. O great grain! Surely he is weak hanging, surely before him that people wagged their heads, surely they said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^27]. Hear his strength: *What is weak of God is stronger than men*[^28]. Deservedly so great a fruitfulness followed: this is mine, says the Church.

**10.** [v. 10] *Judah is my king: Moab is the pot of my hope.*[^29] *Judah is my king*: what Judah? He who is of the tribe of Judah. What Judah, except him to whom Jacob himself said: *Judah, your brothers will praise you*[^30]? *Judah is my king.* What, then, should I fear, when Judah my king says: *Do not fear those who kill the body*[^31]? *Judah is my king: Moab is the pot of my hope.* Why, *pot*? Because tribulation. Why, *of my hope*? Because Judah my king went before. For where he went before, why do you fear to follow? Where did he go before? Through tribulations, through straits, through reproaches. The way was hedged in, but before he passed through: after he passed through, follow; the way now lies open by his passing. *I am alone*, he says, *until I pass over*[^32]: a single grain, until it passes over; when it has passed over, fruitfulness will follow. *Judah is my king.* Therefore, because *Judah is my king: Moab is the pot of my hope.* Moab is understood among the Gentiles. For that nation was born from sin, that nation was born from the daughters of Lot who lay with their intoxicated father, misusing their father. It were better that they remained barren, than that they so became mothers. But that was a certain figure of those who misuse the law. For do not attend to the fact that "law" in the Latin tongue is of the feminine gender; in the Greek it is of the masculine: but whether it be of the feminine gender in speaking, or of the masculine, the manner of speaking does not prescribe to the truth. For the law rather has masculine force, because it rules, is not ruled. But further, what does the apostle Paul say? *The law is good, if one uses it lawfully*[^33]. But those daughters of Lot did not use their father lawfully. But as good works are born, when one uses the law well: so evil works are born, when one uses the law badly. Accordingly those women, misusing their father, that is, misusing the law, begot the Moabites; through whom are signified evil works. Hence the tribulation of the Church, hence the boiling pot. Of this pot it is said in a certain place of prophecy: *A pot set on fire from the north*[^34]. Whence, except from the parts of the devil who said: *I will set my seat to the north*[^35]? The greatest tribulations, then, do not arise against the Church, except from those who use the law badly. What then? Is the Church going to fail from this, and, on account of the pot, that is, the abundance of scandals, not going to persevere unto the end? Did not Judah its king foretell this to it? Did he not say to it: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*[^36]? The pot boiling, love grows cold. Why do you not rather, O love, also grow hot against the pot? Or do you not know it was said to you, when your king was speaking of that abundance of scandals: *He who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved*[^37]? Persevere, then, unto the end against the pot of scandals. The pot of iniquity burns, but greater is the flame of love. Do not be conquered; persevere unto the end. Why do you fear the Moabites, the evil works of those who use the law badly? For what, Judah your king who went before, did he not endure such men? Do you not know that the Jews, by using the law badly, killed Christ? Therefore hope, and where your king went before, follow. Say, *Judah is my king.* And from the fact that *Judah is my king, Moab* has become what? *The pot of my hope*, not of my consuming. See in tribulations the pot of hope; hear the Apostle: *But we also glory*, he says, *in tribulations.* It is now a pot; but see whether this man unfolds the pot of hope. *Knowing that tribulation works patience, and patience proving, and proving hope*[^38]. If tribulation works patience, patience proving, proving hope, and tribulation is the pot which works hope; deservedly *Moab is the pot of my hope. But hope does not confound.* And what? Do you grow hot against the pot? Yes, plainly, *because the love of God is poured out in our hearts, through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^39].

**11.** *Into Idumea I will extend my sandal.*[^40] The Church speaks: I will reach *even into Idumea.* Let tribulations rage, let the world boil with scandals, *I will extend my sandal even into Idumea;* even to those very ones who lead an earthly life (since Idumea is interpreted "Earthly"); even to those, *into Idumea I will extend my sandal.* The sandal of what thing, except of the Gospel? *How beautiful are the feet of those who proclaim peace, who proclaim good things!* and: *Feet shod in the preparation of the Gospel of peace*[^41]. Plainly, since *tribulation works patience, and patience proving, and proving hope,* the pot will not consume me: *For the love of God is poured out in our hearts, through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.* Let us not fail to preach the Gospel, let us not fail to announce the Lord. *Even into Idumea I will extend my sandal.* Do not even the earthly themselves serve? Even if they are bound by earthly desires, yet they adore Christ. Today we see, brethren, how many earthly ones commit, for gain, frauds, for frauds, perjuries; because of fears, they consult diviners, astrologers: all these are Idumeans, earthly; and yet all these adore Christ, are under his sandal; now he has extended his sandal even into Idumea. *The foreigners are made subject to me. The foreigners*, who are they? Aliens, not pertaining to my race. *They are made subject*, because many adore Christ, and are not going to reign with Christ. *The foreigners are made subject to me.*

**12.** [v. 11] *Who will lead me into the city of encirclement?*[^42] What is the city of encirclement? If you remember, I have already mentioned it in another psalm, where it was said: *And they will go round the city*[^43]. For the city of encirclement is the surrounding of the Gentiles; which surrounding of the Gentiles had in its midst one nation of the Jews, worshiping one God: the rest of the surrounding of the Gentiles entreated idols, served demons. And it was mystically called the city of encirclement; because on every side the Gentiles had poured themselves around, and stood around her who worshiped one God. *Who will lead me into the city of encirclement?* Who, except God? This he wishes to say, how he will lead through those clouds, of which it was said: *The voice of your thunder in the wheel*[^44]. The wheel itself is the city of encirclement, which is called a wheel, that is, the circle of the lands. *Who will lead me into the city of encirclement? Who will lead me even into Idumea?* that is, that I may reign even over the earthly, that even those may venerate me who are not of me, who are unwilling to make progress from me.

**13.** [v. 12] *Who will lead me even into Idumea? Will it not be you, O God, who have rejected us? and you will not go forth, O God, in our powers.*[^45] Will you not lead us, who rejected us? But why did you reject us? Because *you destroyed us.* Why did you destroy us? Because *you were angry, and had mercy on us.* You, then, will lead, who rejected, who will not go forth, O God, in our powers; you will lead. What is, *you will not go forth in our powers*? The world is going to rage, the world is going to trample us down, there is going to be a heap of testimony by the poured-out blood of the martyrs, and the raging pagans are going to say: *Where is their God*[^46]? Then *you will not go forth, O God, in our powers*: you will not appear against them; you will not show your power, such as you showed in David, in Moses, in Joshua son of Nun, when the Gentiles yielded to their strength, and, slaughter being made, and great devastation rendered, you led your people into the land you promised. This you will not then do; *you will not go forth, O God, in our powers,* but you will work within. What is, *you will not go forth*? You will not appear. For surely, when the chained martyrs were led, when they were shut in prison, when they were produced to be held in mockery, when they were exposed to beasts, when they were struck with iron, when they were burned with fire, were they not despised as forsaken, as without a helper? How did God work within? how did he console within? how did he make sweet for them the hope of eternal life? how did he not forsake their hearts, where man dwelt in silence, well if good, ill if evil? Surely, then, because he did not go forth in their powers, did he therefore forsake them? Did he not rather, by not going forth in their powers, lead the Church even to Idumea, lead the Church even into the city of encirclement? For if the Church wished to make war and use the sword, it would seem to fight for the present life: but because it despised the present life, therefore it was made a heap of testimony concerning the future life.

**14.** [v. 13] You, then, O God, who will not go forth in our powers: *Give us help from tribulation; and vain is the salvation of man.*[^47] Let those go now who do not have salt, and let them wish temporal salvation for their own, which is vain old age. *Give us help*: give from there whence you were thought to forsake, from there come to aid. *Give us help from tribulation; and vain is the salvation of man.*

**15.** [v. 14] *In God we will do power, and he himself will bring our enemies to nothing.*[^48] We will not do power in the sword, not in horses, not in coats of mail, not in shields, not in the might of an army; not outwardly. But where? Within, where we lie hidden. Where within? *In God we will do power*: and as it were cast off, and as it were trampled down, as men of no moment we shall be, but *he himself will bring our enemies to nothing.* Finally, this was done concerning our enemies. The martyrs were trampled down: by suffering, by enduring, by persevering unto the end, in God they did power. He himself too did what follows: he brought their enemies to nothing. Where are now the enemies of the martyrs, except perhaps that now drunkards persecute with cups those whom then the furious persecuted with stones?
