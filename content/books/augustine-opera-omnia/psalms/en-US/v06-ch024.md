# Exposition of Psalm 145

**1.** We have desired to praise the Lord with you: and since he has deigned to grant this; that the praise which we say to him may have its order, lest perhaps by some excess it offend the one whom it praises, we seek a better path of praise in the Scripture of God, lest from that very way we turn aside, neither to the right nor to the left. For I dare to say to your charity: that God might be well praised by man, God praised himself; and because he deigned to praise himself, therefore man found how he might praise him. For it cannot be said to God what was said to man: *Let not your own mouth praise you*[^1]. For that a man praise himself is arrogance: that God praise himself is mercy. It profits to love him whom we praise; by loving the good, we are made better. And so, since he knows this profits us, that we love him, by praising himself he makes himself lovable; and in this he provides for us, that he makes himself lovable. So he exhorts our heart to his praise; he has filled his servants with his Spirit, that they might praise him. And since his Spirit in his servants praises him, what else but that he himself praises himself? So this psalm begins thus:

**2.** [v. 1.] *I will exalt you, my God, my king; and I will bless your name forever, and forever and ever*[^2]. You see that the praise of God is begun, and that very praise is carried on to the end of the Psalm. Finally, the title of the Psalm is: *Praise itself of David.* And since he is called David, who came to us from the seed of David[^3], but he himself is our king, ruling us, and leading us into his kingdom: *Praise to David himself* is understood as Praise to Christ himself. But Christ according to the flesh is David, because the son of David: but according to divinity he is the Creator of David, and the Lord of David. Finally, the apostle too, when he honored the first people of God, whence the apostles themselves believed, and the many first churches were made, doing in many thousands of men what just now in the Gospel one rich man heard, and went away sad[^4]; that is, selling all that they had, and distributing to the poor, and seeking perfection in the Lord[^5]: when, then, he praised that first people, he said thus: *Whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all, God blessed forever*[^6]. Since, then, from them is Christ according to the flesh, therefore David: but since he himself is over all, God blessed forever, therefore: *I will exalt you, my God, my king; and I will bless*, he says, *your name forever, and forever and ever.* Perhaps *forever*, Here; but *forever and ever*, Unto eternity. So now begin to praise, if you are to praise unto eternity. He who does not wish to praise in the passing of this world, will be struck dumb when the world without end comes. Accordingly in the following verses he said nearly this.

**3.** [v. 2.] Lest anyone understand otherwise what he said: *I will praise your name forever;* and seek another world, when he might praise: *On every*, he says, *day I will bless you*[^7]. Praise, then, and Bless the Lord your God on every day; that, when the individual days have ended, and there has come without end the one day, you may go from praises to praise, as from virtues to virtue[^8]. *On every*, he says, *day I will bless you;* no day will pass on which I do not bless you. Nor is it a wonder if on your glad day you bless your God. What, if perhaps some sad day dawns, as human affairs go, as there is an abundance of scandals, as there is a multiplication of temptations? What, then, if something sad happens to a man? will you cease to praise God? will you cease to bless your Creator? If you cease, you have lied in saying: *On every day I will bless you, O Lord.* But if you do not cease, even though it seems to you to go ill on the sad day, it will go well with you in your God. For there is, even when it goes ill with you, that wherein it may go well with you. For if it goes ill with you in some evil, without doubt it will go well with you in some good. And what is so good as your God; of whom it was said: *No one is good, except God alone*[^9]? For how secure this praise is, and how secure this 'going well' is, understand from the good itself. For if you rejoice over a good that happens to you through a day, perhaps on another day this good passes away over which you rejoice. It went well with me, I had a good day: because perhaps gains came, or you were invited, or you were a long time at a feast. You rejoice, because you were a long time at a feast; another grieves over you, because you were not ashamed. Yet over whatever such good you rejoice, surely it is transitory. But if you rejoice in the Lord your God, you will hear Scripture saying: *Delight in the Lord*[^10]. You will rejoice the more firmly, the more sure he is in whom you will rejoice. For if you rejoice over a coin, you fear a thief; but if you rejoice over God, what do you fear? Lest someone take God from you? No one will take God from you, if you do not let him go. For God is not as this light shining from heaven. We do not approach it when we wish, because it does not shine in every place. Through our weakness perhaps it happens, that in winter it delights us to be in that very light: but now in summer you see that we rather sought that place, that we might not stand in that very light. But in your God, when you stand, and delight in the light of his truth, you do not seek a place by which to approach him; but the conscience approaches, the conscience withdraws. What was said: *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*[^11]: was said to the mind, not to a vehicle; was said to the affections, not to the feet. And when you stand in him, you will not suffer heat. For the Spirit will breathe on you, and under his wings you will hope[^12].

**4.** You see, then, that you have how you may delight on every day. For your God will not desert you, even if something sad happens. For how sad was what happened to the holy man Job! How suddenly, how many evils! how all the things over which he was thought to rejoice—not over which he did rejoice—were withdrawn by the devil tempting! how even his sons died! What was kept perished, those for whom it was kept perished; nor yet did he perish who gave both it and them. And those very sons perished to the present world, to be recognized and received in the future. Yet that man, having something else in which to rejoice, in whom was true what we just now mentioned: *On every day I will bless you;* was it because that day on which all had perished dawned sad, that therefore the inner light in his heart failed? For he stood in that light, and said: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^13]. So he praised on every day, who praised even on so sad a day. It is a brief lesson, that you always praise God, and with a true heart, not a false one, say: *I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall ever be in my mouth*[^14]. It is a brief lesson, namely, that you know him to give mercifully when he gives, to take away mercifully when he takes; and do not believe yourself deserted by his mercy, who either coaxes you by giving, lest you fail; or rebukes you exulting, lest you perish. So whether in his gifts, or in his scourges, praise. The praise of the one scourging is the medicine of the wound. *On every*, he says, *day I will bless you.* Plainly on every day, brothers, bless; whatever happens, altogether bless God. For that nothing happen which you cannot bear, he himself sees to it. Therefore with fear you ought to be, when it goes well with you; nor prepare yourself for this, as though never to be tempted. For if you are never tempted, you are never tested. Is it not better to be tempted and tested, than, untempted, to be reproved? *And I will praise your name forever, and forever and ever.*

**5.** [v. 3.] *Great is the Lord, and exceedingly to be praised*[^15]. How much was he about to say? what words was he about to seek? How great a conception did he shut up in one *exceedingly*? Think as much as you wish. But when can he be thought, who cannot be grasped? He is *exceedingly to be praised; and of his greatness there is no end.* Therefore he said, *exceedingly;* because *of his greatness there is no end*: lest perhaps you begin to wish to praise, and think you can by praising finish him whose greatness can have no end. Do not, then, think that you can sufficiently praise him of whose greatness there is no end. Is it not, then, better that, as he himself is not finished, neither be his praise finished? His greatness is without end, and let your praise be without end. Of his greatness what was said? *Of his greatness there is no end.* Of your praise what? *I will praise your name forever, and forever and ever.* So as of his greatness there is no end, so of your praise there will be no end. For not when you are dead in this flesh will you cease to praise the Lord. It was indeed said: *Not the dead will praise you, O Lord*[^16]: but those of whom it is said: *From a dead man, as from one who is not, confession perishes*[^17]; not those of whom he says: *He who believes in me, though he die, will live*[^18]: because *the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob is not the God of the dead, but of the living*[^19]. For if you will never be other than his, you will never be silent from his praise. Will you be able to fear lest, while you live here, you be his; and when you are dead, you be not his? Hear the apostle promising you security: *Whether we live, we live to the Lord; whether we die, we die to the Lord: whether, then, we live, or die, we are the Lord's*[^20]. And whence has it come about that you are his even when dead? Because by the price of his blood he redeemed you even when dead. How does he lose a dead servant, whose death is your price? Therefore, when he had said: *Whether we live, or die, we are the Lord's;* to show that very price: *For to this end*, he says, *Christ died and rose again, that he might be Lord both of the living and of the dead*[^21].

**6.** [v. 4.] Yet, because *of his greatness there is no end*, and we ought to praise him whom we do not grasp (for if we grasp him, of his greatness there is an end: but if of his greatness there is no end, we can grasp something of him; yet we cannot grasp the whole God); as it were failing in his greatness, that we may be refreshed by his goodness, let us look to his works, and from his works praise the worker, from the things made the Maker, from the creature the Creator. Let us see what he has done here, the things that are known to us, the things that are manifest to us. For his immense goodness and boundless greatness—how many other things has it made which we do not know? Since indeed we extend the keenness of our eyes as far as heaven, and from the sun and the moon and the stars draw it back again to the earth; and all this is the space in which our gaze wanders: beyond the heavens who may extend even the keenness of the mind, I do not say of the flesh? So as far as his works are known to us, let us praise him through his works. For the invisible things of him, from the foundation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made[^22]. *Generation and generation will praise your works*[^23]. Every generation will praise your works. For perhaps every generation is meant by, *generation and generation.* For he was not going to say, *generation and generation*, so long as he might finish the number of all the generations; but the repetition of the speaker sent the mind of the thinker into the infinite. Behold, this generation which is now in the flesh, about to pass hence as it came, praises the works of God; and that which succeeds it as it gives way, will surely praise the works of God; and after that there will be another; and to the end of the age, how many generations! Signifying this, he said: *Generation and generation will praise your works.* Or did he perhaps wish to intimate two certain generations by this repetition? For we are in this generation sons of God; we shall be in another generation sons of the resurrection. Scripture called them sons of the resurrection; the resurrection itself it called regeneration. *In the regeneration*, he says, *when the Son of man will sit in his majesty*[^24]. Likewise in another place: *For they will neither marry, nor take wives, since they are sons of the resurrection*[^25]. So: *Generation and generation will praise your works.* We praise now the works of the Lord, while we live in this mortality; and if we praise fettered, how shall we praise crowned! So let us now attend, in this generation, to the works of the Lord, in whose praise it is said: *Generation and generation will praise your works;* since of your greatness there is no end. We are permitted to gaze upon your works, that you may be praised who work such things.

**7.** *And they will announce your power*[^26]. For they will not praise your works, except that they may announce your power. Praises are set forth for boys in school, and all such things are set forth to be praised, which God has worked: there is set forth to a man the praise of the sun, the praise of heaven, the praise of earth; and, to come even to lesser things, the praise of the rose, the praise of the laurel: all these are works of God, they are set forth, taken up, praised; the works are celebrated, the worker is passed over in silence. I wish the Creator to be praised in his works; I do not love an ungrateful praiser. Do you praise what he made, and are silent about him who made it? As though, unless he were so great, you would find anything to praise. In that which you see, what is praised there? The beauty, the usefulness, some power, some potency of these things. If beauty delights you, what is more beautiful than the one who made it? If usefulness is praised, what is more useful than he who made all things? If power is praised, what is more powerful than he by whom all things were made; by whom too the things made are not left, but all things ruled and governed? Not, then, as certain eloquent dumb men, praising the creature, forgetting the Creator; not so does generation and generation praise you in your servants, when it praises your works. But how does it praise? *And they will announce your power.* In praising your works, they will announce your power. These praisers, holy and good faithful, true praisers, not ungrateful to grace, when they praise these and those works of God, the highest and the lowest, heavenly and earthly, among the very works of God which they praise, find themselves too, because they too are among the works of God. For he who made all things, he himself made us among all things. Accordingly if you praise the works of God, you will praise yourself too; because you too are a work of God. Where, then, is: *Let not your own mouth praise you*[^27]? Behold, it has been found how you may both praise yourself, and not be arrogant. Praise God in yourself, not yourself: not because you are such, but because he made you; not because you can do anything, but because he can in you and through you. And by this they will praise you, *and announce your power;* not their own, but *yours.* Learn, then, to praise. Gazing on the works, marvel at the craftsman; by giving thanks, not by arrogating. Praise because he himself made it, because he so established it, because he granted such things.

**8.** [vv. 5-6.] Finally, see what follows: *Your power*, he says, *they will announce. And the magnificence of the glory of your holiness they will speak, and your wonders they will narrate. And the power of your terrible deeds they will tell, and your greatness they will narrate. The memory of the abundance of your sweetness they will pour forth*[^28]: only yours. This considerer of the works, see whether he is bent away from the worker to the work; see whether he has fallen from him who made, to the things he made. From the things that were made, he has made for himself a step toward him, not a fall from him to these things. For if you love these things more than him, you will not have him. And what does it profit you to abound in works, if the worker deserts you? Surely love these things too; but love him more, and love these things on account of him. Announce his power, speak the magnificence of the glory of his holiness, narrate his wonders, tell the power of his terrible deeds. For he is both lovable and terrible. For he neither only coaxes nor only threatens. If he did not coax, there would be no exhortation; if he did not threaten, there would be no rebuke. So your praisers will tell *even the power of your terrible deeds;* they will tell the power of your creature punishing and giving discipline, they will not be silent. For they will not preach your eternal kingdom, and be silent about the eternal fire. For the praise of God, setting you on the way, ought to show you both what you should love, and what you should fear, and what you should seek, and what you should flee; what you should choose, and what you should reject. The time of choosing is now, of receiving it will be afterward. So let the power of the terrible deeds be told. *And your greatness*, he says, *they will narrate.* So infinite, so as of your greatness there is no end; they will not be silent about it. That, I say, *your greatness*, of which I had said above: *And of your greatness there is no end, they will narrate it.* How will they narrate it, if there is no end? They will narrate it, when they praise it; and because there is no end of it, so too of its praise there will be no end. Let us prove that there will be no end of its praise. *Blessed*, he says, *are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^29]. *And your greatness* that, that infinite one, *they will narrate it.*

**9.** [v. 7.] *The memory of the abundance of your sweetness they will pour forth*[^30]. O happy feasts! What will they eat who will so pour forth? *The memory of the abundance of your sweetness.* What is, *the memory of the abundance of your sweetness*? Because you have not forgotten us, when we had forgotten you. For all flesh had forgotten God; yet he did not forget his works. This memory of his over us, because he did not forget us, is to be preached, is to be narrated; and because it is very sweet, is to be eaten and poured forth. So eat, that you may pour forth; so receive, that you may give. You eat, when you learn; you pour forth, when you teach; you eat, when you hear; you pour forth, when you preach: yet you pour forth this which you have eaten. Finally, that most eager feaster, the apostle John, for whom the very table of the Lord did not suffice, unless he reclined on the breast of the Lord[^31], and from his secret place drank divine secrets, what did he pour forth? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*[^32]. *The memory*, then, *of the abundance of your sweetness they will pour forth.* How is it not enough, the memory of you, nor the memory of your abundance, or the memory of your sweetness; but *the memory of the abundance of your sweetness*? For what does it profit if it abounds and is unsweet? Likewise it is troublesome, if it is sweet and too little.

**10.** So: *The memory of the abundance of your sweetness they will pour forth*: because you have not forgotten us; and, not having forgotten, you have reminded us, that you may bring us too back into memory. *They will be reminded*, for, *and will be turned to the Lord, all the ends of the earth*[^33]. Because, then, *the memory of the abundance of your sweetness they will pour forth*, understanding that in themselves there is nothing good which is not from you; and that they could not have been turned to you, unless they were reminded by you; nor could they have been brought back into the memory of you, if you had forgotten them: considering these things in your grace: *And in your justice they will exult*[^34]. These things, I say, considering in your grace, *they will exult in your justice*, not their own. Brothers, if you wish to pour forth grace, drink grace. What is, drink grace? Learn grace, understand grace. We, before we existed, were absolutely nothing; and we were made men, when before we were nothing: then, now men ourselves, from the stock of that sinner, we were both malicious, and sons of wrath by nature, as also the rest[^35]. Let us, then, attend to the grace of God, not only by which he made us, but also by which he remade us. To whom, then, we owe that we are, to him we owe that we have also been justified. Let no one as it were attribute to God that he is, and attribute to himself that he is just. For it is better what you wish to attribute to yourself, than what to him: for you are something better in that you are just, than in that you are a man. You give something lower to God; and something higher to yourself. Give the whole to him, in the whole praise him: do not fall from the hand of the craftsman. That you might be, who made you? Is it not written that God took clay from the earth, and formed man[^36]? Before you were a man, you were clay; before you were clay, you were nothing. But do not give thanks to your craftsman for this workmanship alone; hear another workmanship in which he made you. *Not of works*, he says, *lest perhaps anyone be lifted up*[^37]. But he who said: *Not of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up;* what did he mention above? *By grace you have been saved through faith; and this not of yourselves*[^38]. They are the apostle's words, not mine: *By grace you have been saved through faith; and this not of yourselves.* Although this very thing which he had said: *By grace;* surely, *not of yourselves*: but lest anyone understand otherwise, he deigned to open this more plainly. Give an understander, and he said the whole: *By grace you have been saved.* Where you hear, *by grace*, understand Freely. If, then, freely, you brought nothing, you deserved nothing: for if something is rendered to merits, it is wages, not grace. *By grace*, he says, *you have been saved through faith.* Expound that more openly on account of the arrogant, on account of those who please themselves, on account of those ignorant of the justice of God and wishing to establish their own. Hear this same thing more openly: *And this*, he says, that *by grace you have been saved, is not of yourselves, but is the gift of God*[^39]. But perhaps we too did something, that we might deserve the gifts of God. *Not of works*, he says, *lest perhaps anyone be lifted up.* What then? do we not work well? On the contrary we work. But how? With him working in us; because through faith we give place in our heart to him who in us and through us works good things. For hear whence you work good things: *For we are his handiwork, created in Christ Jesus in good works, that we may walk in them*[^40]. This is the abundant sweetness of his memory toward us. Pouring this forth, his preachers will exult in his justice, not their own. What, then, that we may be, that we may praise, that we may exult in your justice, that we may pour forth the memory of the abundance of your sweetness, what have you done toward us, O Lord, whom we praise? Let us say, and while we say it, let us praise.

**11.** [v. 8.] *Merciful and compassionate is the Lord, long-suffering and very merciful. The Lord is sweet to all, and his mercies are over all his works*[^41]. Unless he were such, there would be no recovery for us. Attend to yourself; what did you, a sinner, deserve? a despiser of God, what did you deserve? See whether anything occurs to you but punishment, whether anything occurs to you but torment. You see, then, what was owed to you, and what he gave who gave freely. Pardon was given to the sinner, the spirit of justification was given, love and charity was given, in which you may do all good things; and over these he will give also eternal life, and the fellowship of the angels: all of mercy. Boast your merits nowhere, because even your merits themselves are his gifts: *And in your justice they will exult*. *Merciful and compassionate is the Lord*: who made all things freely. *Long-suffering*: for how many sinners does he bear with? *Merciful and compassionate is the Lord*, in those to whom he has given pardon: in those to whom he has not yet given it, *long-suffering*, not condemning, but waiting, by that very waiting crying out: *Turn to me, and I will turn to you*[^42]; and with excessive long-suffering: *I do not wish*, he says, *the death of the impious, only that he turn back and live*[^43]. He indeed is long-suffering; but you, according to the hardness of your heart and impenitent heart, store up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each one according to his works[^44]. For he is not now so long-suffering in bearing with you, as never to be just in taking vengeance. He has distributed the times: he calls you now, he exhorts you now; he waits until you come to your senses, and you delay! His mercy is great even in this, that he has made the day of life uncertain for you, that you may not know when you will depart hence; and that, while you daily hope to depart, at some time you may be converted: and in this his mercy is great. For if he had set a day for all, he would make sins abound from security. So he gave hope of pardon, lest by despairing you sin more. And both hope and despair are to be feared in sins. See the voice of one despairing, to increase sins, and see the voice of one hoping, to increase sins, and how to each voice the providence and mercy of God runs to meet it. Hear the voice of one despairing: Now, he says, I am to be damned; why do I not do whatever I wish? Hear also the voice of one hoping: The mercy of the Lord is great; when I am converted, he will forgive me all things: why do I not do whatever I wish? He despairs, that he may sin; he hopes, that he may sin. Both are to be feared, both are perilous; woe from despair! woe from perverse hope! To both these perils and both these evils, how does the mercy of God run to meet them? What do you say, you who by despairing wished to sin? Now I am to be damned; why do I not do whatever I wish. Hear Scripture: *I do not wish the death of the impious, only that he turn back and live*[^45]. By this voice of God he is brought back into hope: but another snare is to be feared, lest by that very hope he sin more. What, then, did you too say, you who by hope sin more? When I am converted, God will forgive me all things; I will do whatever I wish. Hear, you too, Scripture: *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day; for suddenly his wrath will come, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^46]. Do not, then, say: Tomorrow I will be converted, tomorrow I will please God; and all today's and yesterday's things are forgiven me. You say true, that God has promised pardon to your conversion; but to your delay he has not promised tomorrow.

**12.** [v. 9.] *The Lord is sweet to all, and his mercies are over all his works.* Why, then, does he condemn? why does he scourge? Or are those whom he condemns, are those whom he scourges, not his works? Plainly his works. And do you wish to know that *over all his works are his mercies*? Hence is that long-suffering by which he makes his sun rise over the good and the evil: are not *his mercies over all his works*, who rains over the just and the unjust[^47]? are not *his mercies over all his works*? Long-suffering, he waits for the sinner, saying: *Turn to me, and I will turn to you*: are not *his mercies over all his works*? And when he says: *Go into the eternal fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels*[^48]; here is not his mercy, but his severity. His mercy was rendered to his works; his severity is not against his own works, but against your works. Finally, if you take away your evil works, and there remain in you nothing but his work, his mercy will not desert you: but if you do not let go your works, his severity will be against your works, not against his works.

**13.** [v. 10.] *Let all your works confess to you, O Lord, and let your saints bless you. Let all your works confess to you*[^49]. For what? is the earth not his work? are the woods not his works? are cattle, beasts, fishes, birds not his works? Plainly these too are his works. And how will these too confess to him? I see indeed his works confess to him in the angels; for the angels are his works: and men are his works; and when men confess to him, his works confess to him: do woods and stones have a voice of confession? Plainly let all his works confess to him. What do you say? Both earth and woods? All his works. If all praise, why do not all confess? For confession is said not only of sins, but also of praise: lest perhaps wherever you hear confession, you think there can now be only that of sin. For it is so much thought, that when it sounds in the divine words, at once there is a custom of beating the breast. Hear that there is also confession of praise. Did the Lord Jesus Christ have sin? And yet he says: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^50]. Confession, then, is in praise. Accordingly what is to be taken: *Let all your works confess to you, O Lord*? Let all your works praise you. But such a question returns in praise, as there was in confession. For if therefore the earth, woods, and all senseless things cannot confess, because they do not have a voice of confessing; therefore neither will they praise, because they do not have a voice of preaching. Yet do not those three young men enumerate all things, walking among the harmless flames; to whom there was space, not only not to burn, but even to praise God? To all they say, from the heavenly things even to the earthly: *Bless, say a hymn, and exalt him above all forever*[^51]. Behold how they say a hymn. Let no one think this, that a dumb stone or a dumb animal has the reason of understanding God: those who have thought this have strayed far from the truth. God ordered all things, and made all things: to some he gave sense, and understanding, and immortality, as to the angels; to some he gave sense and understanding with mortality, as to men; to some he gave bodily sense, and gave neither understanding nor immortality, as to cattle; but to some neither sense, nor understanding, nor immortality, as to herbs, woods, stones: yet these too cannot be lacking in their own kind, and by certain degrees he ordered creation, from earth even to heaven, from visible to invisible, from mortal to immortal. This connection of creation, this most orderly beauty, ascending from the lowest to the highest, descending from the highest to the lowest, nowhere interrupted, but tempered with unlike things, the whole praises God. Why, then, does the whole praise God? Because when you consider it and see it beautiful, you in it praise God. There is a certain voice of the dumb earth, the appearance of the earth. You attend and see its appearance, you see its fruitfulness, you see its powers, how it conceives the seed, how very often it brings forth what was not sown: you see, and by your consideration you as it were question it; and the very inquiry is a question. But when you have inquired admiring, and have searched it out, and have found a great power, a great beauty, and an excellent virtue, since it could not have this virtue from itself and of itself; at once it comes into your mind, that it could not be from itself, except from that Creator. And this which you have found in it is the voice of its confession, that you may praise the Creator. Considering the whole beauty of this world, does not its very appearance answer you as with one voice: I did not make myself, but God?

**14.** [v. 11.] So: *Let all your works confess to you, O Lord, and let your saints bless you.* So that in the confession of your works your saints may bless you, let your saints themselves look upon the creature confessing. And hear their voice blessing. For when your saints bless you, what do they say? *The glory of your kingdom they will tell, and your power they will speak*[^52]. How powerful is God, who made the earth! how powerful is God, who filled the earth with good things! how powerful is God, who gave their own lives to the animals! how powerful is God, who gave diverse seeds to the inward parts of the earth, that they might sprout so great a variety of shrubs, so great a beauty of trees! how powerful is God, how great is God! You question, the creature answers; and from the answer, as from the confession of the creature, you, holy one of God, bless God, and speak his power.

**15.** [v. 12.] *That they may make known to the sons of men your power, and the glory of the greatness of the beauty of your kingdom*[^53]. Your saints, then, commend the glory of the greatness of the beauty of your kingdom; the glory of the greatness of the beauty. There is a certain greatness of the beauty of your kingdom; that is, your kingdom has beauty, and great beauty. Since whatever has beauty, has beauty from you; how great a beauty has your kingdom itself! Let not the kingdom terrify us, it has also a beauty in which we may delight. For of what kind is that beauty in which the saints will fully enjoy, to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^54]! Whence will they go? Whither will they go? See, brothers; and if you can, as much as you can, think of the beauty of that kingdom which is to come; whence in our prayer it is: *May your kingdom come*[^55]. For that very kingdom we wish to come; this coming kingdom the saints preach. Attend to this world; it has beauty. What beauty have the earth, the sea, the air, the heaven, the stars! Do not all these terrify every considerer? Does not the beauty itself so stand out, that as it were nothing more beautiful can be found? And here in this beauty, in this beauty now almost ineffable, here live with you both little worms and mice, and all the things that creep on the earth; these live with you in this beauty. Of what kind is the beauty of that kingdom, where there live with you none but angels? Therefore it was too little for him to say the glory of the beauty. For the glory of the beauty even of any appearance set in this world could be spoken, whether of the earth growing green, or of the heaven shining: but *of the greatness of the beauty of your kingdom*, commends something which we do not yet see, which, unseen, we believe, which, believed, we desire, for the desire of which we endure all things. There is, then, a certain greatness of beauty: let it be loved before it is seen, that when it has been seen it may be held.

**16.** [v. 13.] *Your kingdom.* What kingdom do I mean? *The kingdom of all the ages*[^56]. For the kingdom of this world too has its beauty; but there is not there that greatness of beauty, such as is in the kingdom of all the ages. *And your dominion is in every generation and generation.* This is that repetition, either signifying every generation, or that generation which will be after this generation.

**17.** *The Lord is faithful in his words, and holy in all his works. The Lord is faithful in his words*[^57]: for what did he promise, and not give? *The Lord is faithful in his words.* He has promised certain things and not yet given them; but let him be believed from the things he has given. *The Lord is faithful in his words.* We could believe him merely speaking: he was unwilling to be believed merely speaking, but willed his Scripture to be held; just as, if you were to say to some man, when you promised something: You do not believe me, behold I write it for you. For because generation goes, and generation comes, and so these ages run past with mortals giving way and succeeding, the Scripture of God ought to abide, and a certain bond of God, which all passing by might read, and hold to the way of his promise. And how great are the things which he has rendered from this bond? Do men hesitate to believe him about the resurrection of the dead and about the world to come, which alone now remains to be rendered; when, if he makes a reckoning with the unbelievers, the unbelievers blush? If God should say to you: You hold my bond; I promised judgment, the separation of the good and the evil, an everlasting kingdom to the faithful, and you will not believe? There in my bond read all the things I promised, draw up a reckoning with me: surely at least by reckoning the things I have rendered, you can believe that I will render what I owe. In that bond you have promised the only Son, whom I did not spare, but handed over for you all[^58]: now reckon it among the things rendered. Read the bond; I promised there that I would give the pledge of the Holy Spirit through my Son[^59]: reckon it rendered. I promised there the blood and crowns of the most glorious martyrs: reckon it rendered; let the mass of my debt rendered admonish you. But that this glory of the martyrs too might be rendered, it was promised you in the bond, where it is written: *For your sake we are put to death all the day*[^60]; so that this might be rendered: *The nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things; the kings of the earth stood up, and the princes came together as one, against the Lord, and against his Christ*[^61]. The princes came together as one, conspiring against the Christians. What? the faith of those very kings, did I not both promise it in the bond, and render it in fact? Attend to where I promised: *All the kings of the earth will adore him; all nations will serve him*[^62]. Ungrateful man, you read the debt, you discern it rendered, do you not believe the promise? Read another thing in my bond: That *the nations raged*: That *my enemies spoke evils of me*, that is, of Christ: *When will he die and his name perish*[^63]? Since they did and said all these things, read what I promised, to which I bound myself by paying: *The Lord will prevail against them, and will destroy all the gods of the nations of the earth; and they will adore him, each one from his own place*[^64]? Surely he has now prevailed; he has destroyed all the gods of the nations of the earth. Does he not do this too, and render this? Before the eyes of all he sets the payment of his debts: certain things he rendered under our forefathers, which we did not see; certain things he rendered in our times, which they did not see: through all the generations he rendered the things which are written. And what remains? Is he not believed, all these things being rendered? What remains? Behold, you have made the reckoning; he has rendered so much: has he become unfaithful on account of the few things remaining? Far from it. Why? Because: *The Lord is faithful in his words, and holy in all his works.*

**18.** [v. 14.] *The Lord confirms all who are falling*[^65]. But which *all who are falling*? All absolutely who are falling, but falling in a certain way. For many fall from him; but many fall from their own thoughts[^66]. If they had evil thoughts, they fall from them, and *the Lord confirms all who are falling.* Those who lose something in this world and are saints, are as it were dishonored in this world; from rich they become poor, from honored they become abject, and yet they are saints of God; they are as it were falling. But *the Lord confirms all who are falling.* For the just man falls seven times and rises again; but the impious will be weakened in evils[^67]. When evils befall the impious, they are weakened in them: when evils befall the just, *the Lord confirms all who are falling.* Job had fallen from the glory of the former light of temporal things, by which he shone for a time, he had fallen from the glory of his house. Do you wish to know how far he had fallen? He sat on the dunghill: and the Lord confirmed the falling one. How much did he confirm him? That even in that grave wound with which he had been struck over his whole body, he answered his tempting wife, whom alone the devil had left him as a helper: *You have spoken like one of the foolish women: if we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure evil things*[^68]? How he had confirmed the falling one! *The Lord confirms all who are falling. When he falls*, he says, *the just man will not be troubled, because the Lord confirms his hand*[^69]. *And he raises up all who are cast down*: all who belong to him. For God resists the proud[^70].

**19.** [v. 15.] *The eyes of all hope in you, and you give them food in due season*[^71]. Exactly as one refreshing a sick man in due season, when he ought to receive, then you give; and what he ought to receive, this you give. And so sometimes it is desired of him, and he does not give: he who cares for him knows the hour of giving. Why do I say these things, brothers? Lest anyone, when perhaps he has not been heard asking something just from God: for when he asks something unjust, he is heard to his own punishment; but asking something not unjust, if perhaps he has not been heard, let him not be diminished in mind, let him not fail; let his eyes wait for the food, which God gives in due season. When he does not give, he therefore does not give, lest what he gives should harm. For the apostle was not asking anything unjust, when he asked that the thorn of the flesh be taken from him, the angel of Satan by whom he was buffeted: and yet he asked, and did not receive; because it was still the time of exercising weakness, not of the due season of food. *My grace*, he says, *is sufficient for you: for strength is made perfect in weakness*[^72]. The devil asked Job to be tempted, and received it[^73]. Attend, my brothers, a great mystery, to be learned, repeated, held in mind, never to be forgotten, on account of the abundance of temptations in this world. What shall I say? Is the apostle truly to be compared to the devil? The apostle asks, and does not receive; the devil asks, and receives. But the apostle did not receive on account of his perfection; the devil received to his own damnation. Finally, Job himself received health in due season. Yet he was deferred, that he might be tested, and long sat in his wound, and said many things; and asked God that these things be taken from him, and God did not take them away. He heard the devil sooner, to tempt him, than Job himself, to heal him. Learn, then, not to murmur against God, and when you are not heard, let not fail in you what was written above: *On every day I will bless you.* The Son himself, the Only-begotten himself, had surely come to suffer, to pay what he did not owe, to die in the hands of sinners, to blot out by his blood the bond of our death; for this he had come: and yet, that he might show you an example of patience, he transfigured the body of our humility, conformed to the body of his glory[^74]. *Father*, he says, *if it can be done, let this cup pass from me*[^75]. And that he might fulfill, although he did not receive what he seemed to ask: *On every day I will bless you: But not what I will*, he said, *but what you will, Father.* *The eyes of all hope in you, and you give them food in due season.*

**20.** [v. 16.] *You open your hand, and fill every living thing with blessing*[^76]. Even if you do not give sometimes, yet you give in due season: you defer, you do not withhold, and this in due season.

**21.** [v. 17.] *The Lord is just in all his ways*[^77]. Both when he strikes, and when he heals, he is just, and with him there is no iniquity. Finally, all the saints set in tribulations first praised his justice, and so asked for benefits. First they said: It is just what you do. So Daniel asked, so the other saints: Just are your judgments, we have suffered rightly, we have suffered justly[^78]. They did not attribute injustice to God, did not attribute to him iniquity and foolishness. They first praised the one scourging, and so felt the one feeding: *The Lord is just in all his ways.* Let no one think him unjust, when he perhaps suffers something evil; but let him praise his justice, and accuse his own injustice: *The Lord is just in all his ways, and holy in all his works.*

**22.** [v. 18.] *The Lord is near to all who call upon him*[^79]. And where is that: *It will be that, when they call upon me, I will not hear them*[^80]? So see what follows: *To all who call upon him in truth.* For many call upon him not in truth. They seek something else from him, and do not seek him. Why do you love God? Because he gave me health. This is plain, he himself gave it: for there is no health from anyone, except from him. Because he gave me, he says, a rich wife, I who had nothing, and one serving me. And this too he himself gave; you say true. He gave, he says, sons, both many and good, he gave a household, he gave all good things. Do you love for that? for that you seek nothing more? Be hungry, still knock at the door of the head of the household; he still has something to give. You are in beggary with all these things which you have received, and you do not know it. You still bear the ragged flesh of mortality; have you received that robe of the glory of immortality, and as it were now satisfied do you not ask? *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be satisfied*[^81]. So if God is good, because he gave you these things; how much more blessed will you be, when he has given you himself? You desired so many things from him; I ask you, desire him too. For these things are not truly sweeter than he, nor in any part to be compared to him. So he who sets God himself, from whom he received the things over which he rejoices, before all those things which he received, calls upon God in truth. For that you may know, if it is set before such men and said: What, if all these things over which you rejoice, God should wish to take from you? then he will no longer be loved; there will be no one to say: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^82]. But perhaps the one from whom he took it, what does he say? God, what have I done to you? Why have you taken from me, and given to them? You give to the wicked, and take from your own. You accuse God as unjust, and praise yourself as just. Be converted; accuse yourself, praise him. Then you will be upright, when in all the good things he does, God pleases you; in all the evils you suffer, God does not displease you. This is to call upon God in truth. Those who so call upon the Lord, he hears them: *he is near*, that is, he has not yet given what you wish, yet he is there. As a physician, if perhaps he applies something either to the eye, or to the inward parts, which heals by burning; if the sick man asks that it be taken from him, the physician waits for the time, does not do what the sick man asks; yet he does not withdraw. And he is near, and does not do it; and therefore the more he does not do it, because he is near. For by healing he applied what he applied, and by healing he does not do what he is asked. He does not hear you to your present will, hearing you to your future health; and this surely to your will: for surely even he who does not wish to be burned wishes to be healthy. *The Lord is near*, then, *to all who call upon him.* But to which *all*? *To all who call upon him in truth.* All such, falling, he confirms, who *call upon him in truth.*

**23.** [v. 19.] *He will do the will of those who fear him*[^83]. He will do it, he will do it: even if he does not do it at the moment, yet he will do it. Surely, if you fear God for this, that you may do his will, behold he too in a certain way ministers to you, does your will. *And he will hear their prayers, and will save them.* You see that the physician hears for this, that he may make whole. When this? Hear the apostle saying: *For by hope we have been saved. But hope which is seen is not hope: but if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^84]: salvation, namely, which Peter says is *prepared to be revealed in the last time*[^85].

**24.** [v. 20.] *The Lord guards all who love him, and all sinners he will destroy*[^86]. You see that there is severity in him, in whom there is such great sweetness. He will save all who hope in him, all the faithful, all who fear him, all who call upon him in truth; *and all sinners he will destroy.* Which *all sinners*, except those persevering in sins; daring to find fault with God, not with themselves; disputing daily against God; despairing of the pardon of their offenses, and from that very despair heaping up offenses; or perversely promising themselves pardon, and from that very promise not withdrawing from sins and impiety? The time will come that all these will be separated, and those two parts will be made, one at the right hand, the other at the left; the just will receive the eternal kingdom, those will go into the eternal fire[^87]: *And all sinners he will destroy.*

**25.** [v. 21.] Since these things are so, and we have heard the blessing of the Lord, the works of the Lord, the wonders of the Lord, the mercies of the Lord, the severity of the Lord, the providence in all his works, the confession of all his works; attend to what he concludes in his praise: *The praise of the Lord my mouth will speak, and let all flesh bless his holy name forever, and forever and ever*[^88].
