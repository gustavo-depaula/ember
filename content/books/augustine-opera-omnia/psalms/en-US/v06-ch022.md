# Exposition of Psalm 142

**1.** To the solemnity of the martyrs, as the devotion of your gathering, so the sermon of our service is owed. But your charity ought to remember how many things were said yesterday. For not because, through the eagerness of your inner mouth, you were present through the whole sermon as though fresh, ought we therefore to forget the common weakness: first, because the excellent words ought to be honored, as it is written: But the excellent words of wisdom are the words of the Lord God. Through us, as through little vessels, they are ministered to you: and if the vessels are of clay, yet the bread is heavenly. The apostle says: *We have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the eminence of the power may be God's*[^1]. But what is the treasure, the same is also the bread. For if the treasure and the bread were not the same, it would not be written elsewhere of that very treasure: *A desirable treasure rests in the mouth of the wise; but the foolish man swallows it down*[^2]. Whence we admonish your charity that the things which by hearing you store, as in the belly of memory, you should as it were ruminate again by turning them over and thinking. For this is: *A desirable treasure rests in the mouth of the wise; but the foolish man swallows it down*: briefly he said: The wise man ruminates, the fool does not ruminate. But what is this, plainly and in Latin? The wise man thinks over the things he has heard; but the foolish man hands over to forgetfulness the things he has heard. For not for any other reason were those animals which ruminate called clean in the Law, those which do not ruminate unclean: for every creature of God is clean[^3]. To the Maker God the pig is as clean as the lamb. For he created all things very good[^4]: and: *Every creature of God is good*[^5], the apostle says; and: *All things are clean to the clean*[^6]. So although in nature both are clean; yet in signification the lamb signifies something clean, the pig signifies something unclean: the lamb signifies the innocence of the wisdom that ruminates; the pig signifies the uncleanness of the folly that forgets. A short Psalm was said for the solemnity; let us see whether it can be treated briefly too.

**2.** [v. 1.] *With my voice I cried to the Lord*[^7]. It would suffice: *With my voice I cried to the Lord;* perhaps not in vain was added, *my.* For many cry to the Lord, not with their own voice, but with the voice of their body. So let the inner man, in whom Christ has begun to dwell through faith[^8], cry to the Lord with his own voice, not in the noise of the lips, but in the affection of the heart. Not where man hears does God hear: unless you cry with the voice of lungs and sides and tongue, man does not hear you; your thought is a cry to the Lord. *With my voice I cried to the Lord; with my voice I entreated the Lord.* What he said, *I cried;* he expounded, when he said, *I entreated.* For even those who blaspheme cry to the Lord. In the earlier verse he set down the cry, in the following one he expounded the cry. As though it were demanded: With what cry did you cry to the Lord? *To the Lord*, he says, *I entreated*: my cry, my entreaty; not reviling, not murmuring, not blasphemy.

**3.** [v. 2.] *I will pour out my prayer before him*[^9]. What is, *before him*? In his sight. What is, in his sight? Where he sees. But where does he not see? For we say thus: Where he sees, as though somewhere he does not see. But in this assembly of bodily things both men see, and animals see: but he sees even where man does not see. For your thought no man sees, but God sees. There, then, pour out your prayer, where he alone sees who rewards. For in secret the Lord Jesus Christ commanded us to pray: but if you recognize your chamber, and cleanse it, there you ask God. *But when you pray*, he says, *do not be as the hypocrites, who stand in the streets and lanes praying, that they may be seen by men. But you, when you pray, enter into your chamber, and shut the door, and entreat your God in secret; and he who sees in secret will repay you*[^10]. If men are to repay, pour out your prayer before men: if God is to repay, pour out your prayer before him, and shut the door, lest the tempter enter. The tempter does not cease to knock, that he may break in; if he finds it shut, he passes by. Therefore the apostle, because it is in our power to shut the door—the door of the heart, not of the walls; for there too is the chamber—because, then, it is in our power to shut this door: *Do not give place*, he says, *to the devil*[^11]. For if he has entered and possessed it, notice that you shut it negligently or neglected to shut it.

**4.** But what is it to shut the door? This door has as it were two leaves; of desire, and of fear. Either you desire something earthly, and through this it enters; or you fear something earthly, and through this it enters. So shut the door of fear and of desire against the devil, open it to Christ. How do you open those leaves to Christ? By desiring the kingdom of heaven, by fearing the fire of hell. Through the desire of the world the devil enters, through the desire of eternal life Christ enters: through the fear of temporal punishments the devil enters, through the fear of eternal fire Christ enters. Behold, the martyrs shut the door against the devil, opened it to Christ. This world promised them many things: they laughed, they shut the leaf of desire against the devil. This world threatened beasts, fires, crosses: they did not fear, they shut the leaf of fear against the devil. Let us see whether they opened them to Christ: *He who has confessed me*, he says, *before men, I too will confess him before my Father, who is in heaven*[^12]. So they loved the kingdom of heaven, where Christ will confess them. How will he confess them? *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^13]. He will confess them set at the right hand. Let us see whether they also opened to Christ the leaf of fear, which they shut to the devil. In one and the same place the Lord admonishes this, that it be both shut to the devil, and opened to himself. *Do not*, he says, *fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^14]: he admonished that the leaf of fear be dashed in the devil's face. So is nothing to be feared? Is not, then, an entrance of fear opened to Christ, which was shut to the devil? He at once added, as though saying: You have shut him out, open to me: *But fear him*, he says, *who has power to kill both soul and body in the fire of hell*[^15]. Now, then, if you have believed, and opened to Christ, shut against the devil. Christ is within, there he dwells: pour out your prayer before him, do not seek that he hear you from afar. For the Wisdom of God is not far away, which *reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly*[^16]. So within, with you, before him pour out your prayer: there are his ears. *Because neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the deserted mountains; for God is the judge*[^17]: but if he is the judge, see what cause you bear in your heart.

**5.** [v. 3.] *I will pour out my prayer before him; my tribulation I will announce in his sight*[^18]. It is a repetition, both in the two earlier verses, and in these following ones; there are two sentences, but each said twice. The one is that: *With my voice I cried to the Lord, with my voice I entreated the Lord*: the other is: *I will pour out my prayer before him, my tribulation I will announce in his sight.* For what is, *before him;* is, *in his sight*: and what is, *I will pour out my prayer;* is, *I will announce my tribulation.* When do you do this? One set in persecution speaks: *While my spirit failed from me.* Why did your spirit fail, O martyr set in tribulation? That I might not arrogate to myself my own strength, that I might know that another works in me that very strength. *But when they hand you over to the judges* (so the Lord admonished those whom he wished to make martyrs), *do not think what you will speak; for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^19]. So let your spirit fail, let the Spirit of God speak. Rightly he wished to make them poor in spirit: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^20]. Blessed, then, are the poor in their own spirit, rich in the Spirit of God. For every man who follows his own spirit is proud. Let him subject his own spirit, that he may take in the Spirit of God. He was going up to the summit, let him settle in the valley. If he goes up to the summit, the water flows off him: if he settles in the valley, he will be filled with it, and he becomes that belly of which it was said: *Rivers of living water will flow from his belly*[^21]. So *while my spirit failed from me, in your sight I announced my tribulation*, made humble, confessing to you while my spirit failed, when I am full of your Spirit.

**6.** And perhaps men heard that my spirit failed in me, and despaired of me, and said: We have caught him, we have crushed him: *And you knew my paths.* They thought me cast down, you saw me standing. Those who persecuted and had seized me thought my feet entangled: but their feet were bound, and they fell; but we rose up, and were set upright[^22]. For my eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he himself will pluck my feet from the snare[^23]. I persevered by walking: *For he who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^24]. They thought me crushed, but I was walking. Where was I walking? In the paths which they did not see, who thought me captured; in the paths of your justice, in the paths of your precepts. *For you knew my paths;* the persecutor did not know them: for he would not envy me in them, but would walk with me in them. What are these paths, except those ways of which it is said in another place: *The Lord knows the way of the just, and the way of the impious will perish*[^25]? He did not say: The way of the impious he does not know: *He knows that of the just, but that of the impious will perish.* For what he does not know, perishes. In many places of the Scripture we find that for God to acknowledge is to preserve; for God to acknowledge is to keep; not to acknowledge is to condemn. For whence is he who knows all things going to say at the end: *I do not know you*[^26]? Let them not rejoice from this and say: We will not be punished, because the judge does not know us. They are already punished, if the judge does not know them. So the ways which were said the Lord knows, these here are called paths, when he says: *You knew my paths.* For every path is a way; not every way is a path. Why, then, are those ways called paths, except because they are narrow? The way of the impious is broad, the way of the just is narrow.

**7.** What is the way, the same is also the ways: just as what is the Church, the same is the churches; and what is heaven, the same is the heavens. They are said in the plural, and said in the singular. On account of the unity of the Church, one Church: *One is my dove, one is she of her mother*[^27]. On account of the fraternal congregations through the various places, there are many churches: *The churches*, he says, *of Judea which are in Christ rejoiced that he who once persecuted us now preaches the faith which once he laid waste; and in me they glorified God*[^28]. So he said churches, and so he says one Church: *Be without offense to the Jews, and to the Greeks, and to the Church of God*[^29]. So then both ways, and way; and paths, and path. Why paths, and why path? As we rendered an account why churches and church, we ought to render an account of this too. The paths of God were so called, because the precepts are many: and because those same many precepts are brought back to one, since the fullness of the law is love[^30]; therefore these ways, in the many precepts, are gathered into one, and it is called one, because our way is love. Let us see whether love is a way. Let us hear the apostle: *And still a more eminent way I show you*[^31]. What do you call, O apostle, *the more eminent way*? What I call, hear: *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal: and if I have prophecy, and know all mysteries, and all knowledge; and if I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but have not love, I am nothing: and if I distribute all my goods to the poor, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^32]. So he called love *the more eminent way.* This great way, brothers, has a great wonder. This way surely, because it is the more eminent, is also the more excellent; for that is eminent which is high. Nothing is higher than the way of love, and none walk in it but the humble. So these paths he calls the precepts of love. *You*, he says, *knew my paths*: you know that what I suffer for you, I suffer out of love; you know that love in me endures all things; you know that even if I hand over my body to be burned, I have that without which it profits a man nothing.

**8.** But who knows these ways of a man, my brothers, except truly he to whom it was said: *You knew my paths*? For all human deeds are before the eyes of men; it is uncertain with what heart they are done. And how many impious men, measuring themselves, from themselves say of us that we seek these things in the Church—honors, praises, temporal advantages? How many say that I therefore speak to you, that you may acclaim and praise me, and that I have this end and this intention when I speak? And how do I show them that I do not speak with that intention? It remains for me to say: *You knew my paths.* How do they know what not even you know? How do they know what I scarcely know? For neither do I judge myself: for he who judges me is the Lord[^33]. Peter presumed something of himself, not knowing it, and the physician saw something else in his strength. So let it be cried to the Lord devoutly and purely, because it is truly cried: *You knew my paths.* But do you wish him to lead you through those paths? Be gentle, be meek; do not be fierce, do not be proud, do not be of stiff and raised neck, *like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*[^34]. For when you are gentle, when you are meek, the Lord mounts you, and will lead you through his ways: for he will direct the meek in judgment, he will teach the meek his ways[^35]. *You*, then, *knew my paths.*

**9.** *In this way in which I was walking, they have hidden a trap for me*[^36]. This way in which he was walking is Christ: there they hid a trap, who persecute in Christ, on account of the name of Christ. There, then, *they have hidden a trap for me.* What do they envy in me? what do they persecute in me? That I am a Christian. If, then, they persecute this, that I am a Christian: *In the way in which I was walking, they have hidden a trap for me.* As far as in them lies, in the way in which I was walking they themselves hid a trap for me: as far as pertains to their desire, as far as pertains to their effort, as far as pertains to their wish, in that very way they wished me to have a trap by which I might be caught. But *the Lord knows the way of the just*[^37]; *and you knew my paths.* This indeed they wished; but you do not permit them to set a scandal in you, because you are my way. For the heretics too wish to hide a scandal for us in the name of Christ, and they themselves are deceived. What they think to set in the way, they set outside the way, because they themselves too are outside the way: they cannot set a trap where they themselves are not. But this was said according to their desire, according to their wish and opinion: for expressly in another place it was said: *Beside the paths they have set scandals for me*[^38]. This which was said, *in the way;* was said according to their desire and wish: that which was said, beside the way, that is, *beside the paths;* was said according to the truth. For in reality they do not set it in the path, do not set it in the way itself, because Christ is the way[^39]: but indeed beside the paths they set it. Christ does not allow them to set it in the way, lest there be no way by which we may go: yet he allows them to set it beside the way, that we may not turn aside from the way. The pagan thinks he sets a scandal in the way, when he says to me: You worship a crucified God. He reproves the cross of Christ, which he does not understand. He thinks he sets in Christ what he sets beside the way. I will not go out from Christ, and I will not fall from the way into the trap. Let him exult over the crucified Christ; let me see on the foreheads of kings the cross of Christ. What he mocks at, there I am saved. Nothing is prouder than a sick man who derides his medicine. If he did not deride it, he too would receive it, and be healed. That cross is a sign of humility; but he, in excessive pride, does not recognize whence the swelling of his soul may be healed. But if I recognize it, I walk in the way. So far am I from blushing at the cross, that I do not have the cross of Christ in a hidden place, but bear it on my forehead. We receive many Sacraments in one way and another: some, as you know, we receive in the mouth, some we receive over the whole body. But because on the forehead one blushes, he who said: *He who is ashamed of me before men, I will be ashamed of him before my Father, who is in heaven*[^40]; that very ignominy, which the pagans deride, he set in a way in the place of our shame. You hear a man insult the shameless, and say: He has no forehead. What is: He has no forehead? He is shameless. Let me not have a bare forehead; let the cross of my Lord cover it. So *in the way in which I was walking, they have hidden a trap for me*, as far as in them lies; for they set it beside the way: but I will be safe, if I do not withdraw from the way. *Do you not know that you walk in the midst of snares*[^41]? says Scripture. What is, *in the midst of snares*? In the way of Christ, both on this side snares, and on that side snares; snares on the right, snares on the left: snares on the right, the prosperity of the world; snares on the left, the adversity of the world: snares on the right, promises; snares on the left, terrors. You, walk in the midst of snares, do not withdraw from the way: let neither the promise catch you, nor the terror dash you down. *In this way in which I was walking, they have hidden a trap for me.*

**10.** [v. 4.] *I considered toward the right hand, and saw*[^42]. He considered toward the right hand, and saw: he who considers toward the left hand is blinded. What is it to consider toward the right hand? Where those will be to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom*[^43]. For there will be some on the left, to whom it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels*[^44]. So, with the whole world raging and threatening in the persecutions, the insults and terrors of men growing more frequent on every side, he, despising present things, looked toward future things, attended toward the right hand where he would be; there he meditated on being, thither he attended, and saw, and therefore bore all things: but those who persecuted did not see. Therefore, when he said: *I considered toward the right hand, and saw;* he went on and said: *And there was no one who knew me.* For when you endure all things, who knows what you attend to; whether you direct your eye toward the right hand, or toward the left? In what you endure, if you seek the praise of men, you have attended toward the left: in what you endure, if you seek the promises of God, you have attended toward the right. You have attended toward the right, you will see: you have attended toward the left, you will be blinded. But even when you see toward the right, there will be no one to acknowledge you. For who consoles you, except the Lord to whom you say: *And you knew my paths*? *Nor was there one who knew me.*

**11.** *Flight has perished from me*[^45]. He says, as it were, that he is hemmed in: *Flight has perished from me.* Let the persecutors exult, he is overwhelmed, he is captured, he is hemmed in, he is conquered; flight has perished from him. From him flight has perished, who does not flee. But he who does not flee suffers whatever he can for Christ; that is, he does not flee in mind. For it is allowed to flee in body, it is granted, it is permitted, the Lord saying: *If they persecute you in one city, flee into another*[^46]. So he who does not flee in mind, from him flight has perished. But it matters why he does not flee; because he is hemmed in, because he is captured, or because he is strong: for from the captive too flight has perished, and from the strong flight has perished. What flight, then, is to be guarded against? what flight is to perish from us? That of which the Lord says in the Gospel, that *the good shepherd lays down his life for his sheep: but the hireling, and one who is not the shepherd, when he sees the wolf coming, flees*[^47]. When he sees the robber, why does he flee? *Because he has no care for the sheep*[^48]. Such a flight had perished from this man; whether we take this voice from the person of the head himself, of that Christ the Lord, who died for all; or from his members, our martyrs, because they too suffered for the brothers. Hear John saying: *For as he laid down his life for us, so we too ought to lay down our lives for the brothers*[^49]. But when they lay it down, Christ lays it down; because when they suffer persecution, he himself cries out: *Why do you persecute me*[^50]? *Flight has perished from me; and there is no one who seeks out my soul.* So is there no one who seeks his soul? He sees men wishing to rage to his death, wishing to pour out his blood; how is there no one who seeks his soul? And this is taken in two ways. As 'flight perishes' is taken in two ways, because both from the captive and from the strong flight perishes; so in two ways the soul of a man is sought, either by persecutors, or by lovers. So, then: *There is no one who seeks my soul*: of those he said: Surely they persecute my soul, and they do not seek my soul. But if they seek my soul, they will find it cleaving to you: and if they know how to seek it, they know also how to imitate it. For that you may know that the soul of a man is sought also by persecutors, hence it is said elsewhere: *Let those be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul*[^51].

**12.** [v. 5.] *I cried to you, O Lord; I said: You are my hope*[^52]. When I was enduring, when I was in tribulation, *I said: You are my hope.* My hope here; therefore I endure. But *my portion*, not here, but *in the land of the living.* God gives a portion in the land of the living; but not anything of himself, outside himself. What will he give to one who loves him, except himself?

**13.** [v. 6.] *Attend to my prayer; for I have been brought very low*[^53]. Brought low by the persecutors, brought low in confession. He humbles himself invisibly, brought low by enemies visibly. So he is raised up by him visibly and invisibly. Invisibly the martyrs are now raised up: visibly they will be raised up, when this corruptible has put on incorruption in the resurrection of the dead[^54], when even this very thing, against which alone the persecutors could rage, will be restored. *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^55]. And what perishes? what do they kill? Or is even this permitted them, that what they have killed should perish? It will not perish. Hear the Lord's own promise: *Amen I say to you, a hair of your head will not perish*[^56]. Why, then, are you anxious about the rest of the members, where there will not be the loss of a hair?

**14.** *Rescue me from those who persecute me*[^57]. From whom do you think he prays to be rescued? From the men who were persecuting? Are then those men themselves our enemies? We have other invisible enemies, who persecute otherwise. Man persecutes, that he may kill the body; another persecutes, that he may ensnare the soul. And therefore through his vessels—because of him it was said: *He works in the sons of unbelief*[^58]—through his vessels, that is, men in whom he works, he carries out the persecution of the body, that within there may come about the ruin of the heart. For if, the body falling, the soul stands, the trap has been broken, and we have been freed[^59]. So there are also other enemies of ours, from whom we ought to entreat God to rescue us, lest they seduce us, whether broken by worldly troubles, or led on by enticements. Who are these enemies? Let us see whether they are plainly described by some servant of the Lord, by some perfect soldier who has joined battle with them. Hear the apostle saying: *You have not a wrestling against flesh and blood*[^60]. Do not, as it were, turn your hatreds upon men, and reckon them your enemies, and think yourselves ground down by their enmities: these men whom you fear are flesh and blood; *You have not a wrestling against flesh and blood.* So he wished to say, despising the mortality of men. And against whom? *Against the principalities*, he says, *and powers, and rulers of this world, of this darkness*[^61]. You had been terrified, when you heard, *rulers of the world.* If the rulers of this world, are you going to go beyond the world, where you do not suffer them? are you going to go beyond the world, where you may be rescued from them? So take *the rulers of the world* and *of this darkness*, not as rulers of heaven and earth; for that is the building of God. Heaven and earth are called the world, and evil men are called the world. Why are these too the world? Because they love the world: and therefore darkness, because impious. And so now, many of that very number believing, what does the apostle say? *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^62]. Before, then, you were light, when you were darkness, attend to what ruler you had. What ruler do the wicked have, except the devil, as the devout and faithful have Christ as ruler? So he called the devil and his angels *the rulers of the world*, that is, the rulers of the lovers of the world, the rulers of sinners, which is, *of this darkness*: these we have as enemies, from whom we ought to entreat the Lord that we may be rescued.

**15.** Hear 'world' and 'world' plainly in one place in holy Scripture, in the Gospel: the world which God made; the world which the devil rules, that is, the lovers of the world. For the men themselves God made; the lovers of the world he did not make. For to love the world is sin; but sin God did not make. Hear, then, as I had begun to say, 'world' and 'world.' *He was in this world*, it was said. Of whom was it said: *He was in this world*, except of the Wisdom of God, which is Christ Jesus; of whom was said what I mentioned a little before: *It reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly*[^63]? *For it reaches everywhere on account of its cleanness, and nothing defiled runs into it*[^64]. So: *He was in this world, and the world was made through him, and the world did not know him.* You have heard of two worlds: *The world was made through him, and the world did not know him*[^65]. The world which was made through Jesus is not ruled by those principalities and powers of darkness; but the world which did not know Jesus, that is, the lovers of the world, the sinners, the wicked, the proud, and the unbelievers. Whence are sinners the world? Because they love the world, and by loving inhabit the world; just as a house is called both the building and the inhabitants. A good house, very often a good building; and a good house, because good men dwell in it. And: Beware that house, because it is bad, is said in two ways. This house is bad, beware; perhaps on account of ruin, lest something fall and crush you: it is said also in another way: Beware that bad house, lest you run into the snare of the hunters, lest you, poor, be oppressed by the rich, lest you suffer some fraud. As, then, house and house, so world and world. But why also are the just, since they too are in the world, not called the world? Because the apostle says: *Walking in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh*[^66]; *for our conversation is in the heavens*[^67]. The just man dwells here in the flesh, but in heart is with God. And he too is called the world, if without cause he hears: Lift up your heart: but if there is a cause why he hears it, let him dwell on high. *For you are dead*, he says, *and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^68]. But those whose life is here, that is, whose desire and love clings here, is ground down here, is entangled here, are rightly called inhabitants of the world: and they are rightly called the world, those who inhabit the world; as those are rightly called a house, who inhabit a house. So world and world: *The world was made through him, and the world did not know him*[^69]. Behold the world made through the Lord, behold the world which did not know the Lord. Praise the building, and love the builder; and do not love to dwell in the building, but dwell in the builder.

**16.** *Rescue me from those who persecute me; for they have grown strong over me*[^70]. Who said, *they have grown strong over me*? The body of Christ cries out, it is the voice of the Church, the members of Christ cry out: The number of sinners has greatly grown. *Because iniquity has abounded, the love of many will grow cold*[^71]. *Rescue me from those who persecute me; for they have grown strong over me.*

**17.** [v. 7.] *Lead my soul out of prison, that it may confess to your name*[^72]. This prison has been understood variously by those before us. And perhaps it is itself the prison which is written in the title, 'cave.' For the title of this psalm runs thus: *An understanding of David himself, when he was in the cave, a prayer.* What is the cave, the same is also the prison. We have set forth two things to be understood; but when we have understood one, both will be there. Merits make a prison. For in one and the same dwelling, to one it is a home, to another it is a prison. Those who guard shut-up men; even if they guard in their own home, those who are guarded more closely are in prison: are those too to be said to be in prison? There is one dwelling both for the one and for the other; for the one freedom makes it a home, for the other servitude makes it a prison. So it seemed to some that the cave and the prison is this world; and the Church prays this, that it be led out of prison, that is, of this world, of under the sun, where all is vanity. For it is said: *All is vanity, and presumption of spirit in every work of man, in which he himself labors under the sun*[^73]. So beyond this world, in I know not what rest, God promises us what we are to be; perhaps therefore from this place we cry out: *Lead my soul out of prison.* Our soul is in Christ through faith and hope, as I said a little before: *Your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^74]. But our body is in this prison, in this world. If he said: Lead my body out of prison; we would securely take the world as the prison. Yet perhaps, on account of certain earthly desires that hold us, against which we struggle and fight, because: *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind*[^75]; rightly we say: Lead my soul out of this world, that is, out of the labors and pressures of this age. For not the flesh which you made, but the corruption of the flesh, and the pressures, and the temptations, are a prison for me.

**18.** But some have said that this prison and cave is this body, so that this is: *Lead my soul out of prison.* But this understanding too wavers a little. For what great thing is: *Lead my soul out of prison*, Lead my soul out of the body? Do not the souls of robbers and criminals go out of the body, and go into worse punishments than they suffered here? What great thing, then, is asked: *Lead my soul out of prison;* when, whether late or soon, it must necessarily go out? Perhaps the just man says: Let me now die; lead my soul out of this prison of the body. If he hastens too much, he does not have love. He ought indeed to desire this and covet it, as the apostle says: *Having a desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ; for it is by far the best*[^76]. But where is love? So it follows: *To remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^77]. So let God lead out of the body, when he wills. Our body too could be called a prison, not because what God made is a prison, but because it is penal and mortal. For two things are to be considered in our body; the workmanship of God, and the punishment of desert. This whole form, posture, gait, the ordered members, the dispositions of the senses, to see, to hear, to smell, to taste, to touch; all this framework and the distinction of the structure, could not have been made except by God, who has worked all things, heavenly, earthly, highest and lowest, visible and invisible. What, then, in it is penal of ours? That the flesh is corruptible, that it is frail, that it is mortal, that it is in need: this will not be in the reward. For it is not that there will be no body, since the body rises. But what will not be there? Corruption: for this corruptible will put on incorruption[^78]. So if the flesh is a prison for you, the body is not your prison, but the corruption of your body. For your body God made good, because he is good; corruption the just one brought in, because he is the judge: that you have from his benefit, that you have from his punishment. This, then, perhaps he said: *Lead my soul out of prison*, Lead my soul out of corruption. If we understand it thus, it is no blasphemy; the understanding holds.

**19.** Finally, brothers, as I think, he said this: *Lead my soul out of prison*, Lead it out of straits. For to a man rejoicing even a prison is wide, and to a sad man a meadow is narrow. So he prays to be led out of straits. For although in hope he has breadth, yet in the present reality he is straitened. Attend to the straits of the apostle: *I had no rest in my spirit*, he said; *because I did not find my brother Titus*[^79]. In another place: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I do not burn*[^80]? He, then, who was both weak and burned, was he not in punishments and prison? But these punishments out of love bring forth a crown. Whence again he says: *There remains for me a crown of justice, which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge*[^81]. To this avails: *Lead my soul out of prison, that it may confess to your name.* Now led out of corruption, what has it to confess? Sins are not there, but praises are: but confession is understood in two ways, both in our sins, and in the praise of God. In our sins confession is well known, and so well known to all the people, that when the name of confession has been heard in a reading, whether it is said in praise, or said of sins, fists run to the breast. The name of confession of sin, then, is well known: let us seek confession in praise. Where do we find it? You have in the Scriptures: *And these things you shall say in confession: All the works of the Lord, that they are very good*[^82]. So here confession is of praise. In another place the Lord himself says: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^83]. What did he confess? sins? So for Christ to confess was to praise. And hear the praise of the Father: *Because you have hidden these things*, he says, *from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones*[^84]. Because, then, after these straits of corruption we will dwell in the house of God, our whole life will be nothing but the praise of God. It has already often been expounded to you that, when need withdraws, all the businesses of need pass away; for there will be nothing for us to do there. I do not say day and night, where there is no night; but the whole day, when there is one day, there will be nothing for us to do there, except to praise him whom we love, because then we shall also see. Now, unseen, we desire him, and praise; then, when we see what we love, how shall we praise? The praise will be without end, because the love is without end. Because, then, this we shall do there, therefore *lead my soul out of prison, that it may confess to your name.* *For blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^85]. Now the prison hinders, because *the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul*[^86]. Not the body weighs down the soul; but *the body which is corrupted.* So it is not the body, but corruption, that makes the prison. *Lead my soul out of prison, that it may confess to your name.* Now the voice which follows sounds from the head, our Lord Jesus Christ. And it is itself such as yesterday's last verse. For yesterday's last, if you remember, was this: *I am alone, until I pass over*[^87]. And here what is the last? *The just will wait for me, until you repay me.*
