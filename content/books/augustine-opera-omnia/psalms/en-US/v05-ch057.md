# Exposition of Psalm 119

**1.** [v. 1.] It is a short psalm, and a very useful one, which we have just heard sung to us, and to which, by singing, we have responded. You will not labor long in hearing, nor will you labor fruitlessly in doing. For it is, as its title is noted beforehand, *A Song of Ascents.* In Greek it is written so. Steps are either of those descending or of those ascending: but the steps, as they are set in these psalms, signify those ascending. Let us understand, then, as those about to ascend: and let us not seek ascents with bodily feet, but, as is written in another psalm, *He has arranged ascents in his heart, in the valley of weeping, in the place which he has appointed*[^1]. He said ascents. Where? That is, *in the heart.* From where? From the *valley of weeping.* And as to where it ascends, human speech as it were fails, and it cannot be explained, perhaps not even thought. You have just heard, when the Apostle was read: *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man*[^2]. *Into the heart of man it has not ascended;* let the heart of man ascend there. Therefore, because *eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man;* how could it be said where one is to ascend? Therefore, since it could not be said, he says, *In the place which he has appointed.* What more shall I tell you, says the man through whom the Holy Spirit was speaking? Into such a place, or such a place? Whatever I say, you think something earthly, you creep on the ground, you bear flesh: the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that thinks on many things[^3]. To whom shall I speak? Who will hear? Who will grasp where we will be after this life, if we have ascended in the heart? Because, then, no one, hope for some ineffable place of blessedness, which he has appointed for you who has appointed ascents even in your heart. But where? *In the valley of weeping.* The valley signifies humility; the mountain signifies loftiness. There is a mountain to which we ascend, a certain spiritual loftiness. And what is this mountain to which we ascend, except the Lord Jesus Christ? He himself made for you, by suffering, the valley of weeping, who made, by abiding, the mountain of ascent. What is the valley of weeping? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^4]. What is the valley of weeping? *He offered his cheek to the one striking him; he was filled with reproaches*[^5]. What is the valley of weeping? He was buffeted, smeared with spittle, crowned with thorns, crucified. This is the valley of weeping from which you must ascend. But in whom must you ascend? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^6]. For that very *Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.* So he descended to you, that he might abide in himself: he descended to you, that he might become for you the valley of weeping; he abode in himself, that he might be for you the mountain of ascent. *It will be,* says Isaiah, *in the last days, the mountain of the Lord made manifest, prepared on the top of the mountains*[^7]. Behold to where you must ascend. But do not think anything earthly, nor, because you heard "mountain," think of some high places of earth: nor, when you hear "rock" or "stone," let hardness be understood by you; nor, when you hear "lion," think of ferocity; nor, when you hear "lamb," think of a beast. None of these is he in himself, and all of them he became for you. From here, then, one must ascend; to there one must ascend; from his example, to his divinity. For he made an example for you by humbling himself. For those who were unwilling to ascend from the valley of weeping were pressed down by him. For they wished, too hastily, to have ascent; they thought of high honors, they did not think of the way of humility. Let your Charity understand what I say: that two disciples wished to sit at the sides of the Lord, one at the right, the other at the left: the Lord saw that they thought too hastily and in reverse order about honors, when they ought first to learn to be humbled, that they might be exalted; and the Lord said to them, *Can you drink the cup which I am going to drink*[^8]? For he, in the valley of weeping, was going to drink the cup of the passion: but they, not attending to the humility of Christ, wished to grasp the loftiness of Christ. He called them back to the way, as those going astray; not to deny them what they wished, but to show by what way they should come.

**2.** And so, my brethren, let us sing this psalm of ascent, about to ascend in the heart; because, that we might ascend, there was a descent to us. For Jacob saw a ladder, and on that ladder were shown to him those ascending and descending: he saw both[^9]. We can suppose that those seen ascending are the advancing; those descending, the failing: because in truth we find this too in the people of God; some advance, some fail. Those ladders could signify them, but perhaps better are all the good understood on those ladders, both ascending and descending. For not in vain was it not said "falling," but "descending." But there is much difference between to descend and to fall. For because Adam fell, therefore Christ descended: the one fell, the other descended; the one fell by pride, the other descended by mercy. But he himself did not descend alone: and indeed from heaven he alone descended; but many saints, imitating him, descend to us, and have descended to us. For in a certain loftiness of heart the Apostle dwelt, when he said, *For whether we are beside ourselves, it is to God*[^10]. For in that he had gone beyond in mind, he had gone beyond to God. For, going beyond in mind every human frailty, every temporality of the world, all things whatever that, by being born and setting, vanish, these passing things; he dwelt in heart in a certain ineffable contemplation, as far as he could, of which he says that he heard ineffable words which it is not lawful for a man to speak[^11]. But he could not speak those things to you: yet he himself could somehow see those things which he could not speak to you. And so if he wished always to remain in that which he saw, and could not speak, he would not raise you up to where you too could see. But what did he do? He descended. For there he says, *For whether we are beside ourselves, it is to God, or whether we are sober, it is for you*[^12]. What is, *we are sober?* We speak so that you may be able to grasp. Because Christ too made himself such, by being born and suffering, that men could speak of him: for of a man a man speaks easily. Of God, when does a man speak so as God is? But of a man a man speaks easily. That the great, then, might descend to the little ones, and yet not speak to them anything except something great; he himself who was great was made little, that of him the great might speak to the little. You have just heard what I say, when the Apostle was read. But if you noticed, he said this: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal*[^13]. Therefore to the spiritual he speaks in the heights; but that he may speak to the carnal, he descends. For, that you may know that, when he descended, he speaks of him who descended; behold, John speaks of him abiding in himself: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made*[^14]. Take it, if you can; seize it, it is food. But you will say to me: That indeed is food, but I am an infant, I must be suckled, that I may be able to become fit to eat food. Therefore, because you must be nourished with milk, but he is food, that very food has been passed through flesh to your gullet. As, then, a mother eats food, that she may pass it, made milk, through her flesh to her infant; so the food of the Angels, the Lord, *the Word was made flesh*[^15], and was made milk: and the Apostle says, *I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not yet able, but not even now indeed are you able*[^16]. Therefore, by giving milk, he descended to the little ones; and because he descended, he gave the one descending. For he says, *Did I say that I knew anything among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified*[^17]? For if he said only, *Jesus Christ,* Jesus Christ is so also according to his divinity, according to that which the Word was with God, the Son of God Jesus Christ: but this—that is, said in this way—the little ones do not grasp. How, then, do those who take milk grasp it? *Jesus Christ,* he says, *and him crucified.* Suck what he was made for you, and grow up to that which he is. There are, then, those ascending and descending. On those ladders are both ascending and descending. Who are the ascending? Those who advance to the understanding of spiritual things. Who are the descending? Those who, although, as far as men can, they enjoy the understanding of spiritual things, yet descend to the little ones, that they may say to them such things as they can grasp, and, nourished with milk, may be able to become fit and strong to grasp spiritual food. Isaiah, brethren, was himself too one of the descending to us: for even the steps of his descending appear. For when he said of the Holy Spirit, *There will rest,* he says, *upon him the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and godliness, and the Spirit of the fear of the Lord*[^18]: he began from wisdom, and descended down to fear. As he who was teaching descended from wisdom down to fear; you who learn, if you advance, ascend from fear to wisdom: for it is written, *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^19]. Now, then, hear the psalm. Behold, let us set before our eyes a man about to ascend: where about to ascend? *In the heart.* From where about to ascend? From humility, that is, from the *valley of weeping.* To where about to ascend? To that ineffable thing which, since it could not be said, was said, *In the place which he has appointed.*

**3.** When, therefore, a man has thus begun to arrange his ascent—this I say more openly, when a Christian man has begun to think of advancing—he begins to suffer the tongues of those who oppose. Whoever has not yet suffered them has not yet advanced: whoever does not suffer them does not even try to advance. Do you wish to know what we say? Rather, let him experience what we hear together. Let him begin to advance, let him begin to wish to ascend, to wish to despise earthly, frail, temporal things, to hold the happiness of the world as nothing, to think of God alone, not to rejoice in gains, not to waste away at losses, to wish to sell even all his goods and give to the poor, and to follow Christ; let us see how he suffers the tongues of those who disparage and contradict much, and—what is more grievous—of those who, as if by counseling, turn him away from salvation. For he who counsels someone counsels unto salvation, counsels unto what profits; but that one, as if counseling, draws him back from salvation. Because, then, it seems to have the cloak of one counseling, and has the venom of one destroying, it is called a deceitful tongue. About to ascend, then, against these very tongues he first beseeches God: for he says, *To you, O Lord, when I was in tribulation, I cried, and you heard me.* Whence did he hear him? That he might now set him at the steps of ascending.

**4.** [v. 2.] And because, being now about to ascend, he was heard, what does he pray? *O Lord, rescue my soul from unjust lips and from a deceitful tongue.* What is a deceitful tongue? The crafty one, having the image of counseling, and the ruin of harming. They are the ones who say: And are you going to do this, which no one has done? And will you alone be a Christian? And if he shows others doing this, and reads the Gospel where the Lord commanded it to be done, and reads the Acts of the Apostles; what do they say with their crafty tongue and unjust lips? You will perhaps not be able to fulfill it; it is much that you undertake. Some by forbidding deter, others by praising press the more. For because such is the life that has now occupied the world, so great is the authority of Christ, that not even a pagan now dares to reproach Christ. He is read who cannot be reproached, because he said: *Go, sell all that you have, and distribute to the poor, and follow me*[^20]: Christ cannot be contradicted, the Gospel cannot be contradicted, Christ cannot be reproached; the deceitful tongue turns itself to a praise that forbids. If you praise, exhort. Why do you press by praising? Better that you blamed, than that you deceitfully praised. For what would you say in blaming? Far be it: that life is foul, that life is evil. But because you know that, when you have said these things, you can be pressed by evangelical authority; you turn yourself to another kind of dissuading, that, by falsely praising, you may forbid me from true praise; nay, by praising Christ, you may forbid me from Christ, saying: What is this? Behold, those have done it, perhaps you will not be able: you begin to ascend, you fall. He seems to admonish; he is a serpent, he is a deceitful tongue, he has venom. Pray against it, if you wish to ascend, and say to your God, *O Lord, rescue my soul from unjust lips and from a deceitful tongue.*

**5.** [vv. 3-4.] And your Lord says to you: *What will be given to you, or what will be added to you against the deceitful tongue?* That is, what you may have against the deceitful tongue, what you may set against the deceitful tongue, whereby you may fortify yourself against the deceitful tongue, *what will be given to you, or what will be added to you?* He asked, exercising us; for he himself is going to say what he asks. For he answered, adding to himself as he asks: *The sharp arrows of the mighty, with desolating coals;* or, *with devastating* ones. Whether you say *desolating* or say *devastating* (for in different manuscripts it is written differently), it signifies the same. See: the coals are called devastating, because by devastating and desolating they easily lead to desolation. Who are these coals? Let your Charity first understand what the arrows are. *The sharp arrows of the mighty* are the words of God. Behold, they are thrown, and they pierce hearts: but when hearts have been pierced by the arrows of the word of God, love is roused, not destruction procured. The Lord knows how to shoot arrows unto love; and no one shoots arrows unto love more beautifully than he who shoots with the word; nay, he shoots the heart of the one loving, that he may help the one loving; he shoots, that he may make him love. But they are arrows, when we act with words. But who are the devastating coals? It is too little to act with words against the crafty tongue and unjust lips, it is too little to act with words; and one must act with examples too. Examples are the devastating coals. And why they are called devastating, let your Charity briefly grasp. First, see how one must act with examples. The crafty tongue knows how to say nothing—the craftier it is—except: See that you cannot fulfill it; it is much for you to undertake this. You have received the evangelical precept, you have the arrow; but you do not yet have the coals. It is to be feared lest the arrow alone be not strong enough against the deceitful tongue: there are the coals too. As, for instance, God begins to say to you: You cannot, why can that one? Why was another able? Are you more delicate than that senator? Are you weaker than this one, or that one in health? Are you weaker than women? Women were able, and men are not able? Delicate rich men were able, and the poor are not able? But I, he says, have sinned much, and am a great sinner. There are reckoned too those who sinned much; and they loved the more, the more was forgiven them: as it was said in the Gospel, *To whom little is forgiven, he loves little*[^21]. When, therefore, these have been enumerated, and men have been named by name who were able, that one, having received the arrow in his heart, the desolating coals being also added, has his earthly thought desolated in him. For what is "is desolated"? Is brought to desolation. But there were in him many things which had badly put forth leaves, many carnal thoughts, many worldly loves: these are burned by the desolating coals, that the place may be made pure and desolate, in the purity of which place God may make his building; because there had been there the ruin of the devil, and Christ is built there: for as long as the devil remains there, Christ cannot be built. The desolating coals come, and cast down what had been badly built, and to the desolated place comes the structure of perpetual happiness. See, then, why they are called coals. Because those who turn to the Lord revive from the dead. But coals, when they are kindled, before they are kindled, were extinguished. For extinguished coals are called dead; burning ones are called living. The examples, then, of many unjust men who turned to the Lord are called coals. You hear men marvel and say: I knew that one, how drunken he was, how wicked, what a lover of the circus or the amphitheater, what a cheat: now how he serves God, how innocent he has become! Do not marvel; he is a coal. You rejoice over one alive whom you bewailed extinguished. But when you praise one alive, if you know how to praise, apply him to the dead one that he may be kindled; that is, whoever is still slow to follow God, bring to him the coal that was extinguished, and have the arrow of the word of God, and the devastating coal, that you may meet unjust lips and the crafty tongue.

**6.** [v. 5.] What follows? This man received the burning arrows, let him receive the devastating coals. Now he repels the crafty tongue and unjust lips, now he ascends a step, he begins to advance: but he still lives among the evil, among the unjust; the threshing floor is not yet winnowed: suppose that wheat has been made, is it now in the barn? Still it must be pressed by much chaff; and the more it advances, the more it sees greater stumbling blocks among the people. For if it does not advance, it does not see iniquities; if one is not a true Christian, he does not see the feigned ones. For indeed, brethren, the Lord teaches us this too from that parable, of the wheat and the tares. *But when the blade had grown and made fruit, then the tares too appeared*[^22]: that is, because the evil do not appear to any man, unless he himself has been made good; because *when the blade had grown and made fruit, then the tares appeared.* Now, therefore, this man has begun to advance, and has begun to see the evil, and many evils which before he did not know, and he cries to the Lord: *Alas for me, that my sojourning has become far off,* he says! I have withdrawn far from you, my pilgrimage has become far off. I have not yet come into that homeland, where I shall live with no evil; I have not yet come to that fellowship of the Angels, where I shall not fear stumbling blocks. But why am I not yet there? Because *my sojourning has become far off.* Sojourning is pilgrimage: he is called a sojourner who dwells in a foreign land, not in his own city. *Far off,* he says, *has my sojourning become.* And where far off? Sometimes, my brethren, when a man is on pilgrimage, he lives among better men than he would perhaps live among in his own homeland: but it is not so, when we are on pilgrimage from that heavenly Jerusalem. For a man changes his homeland, and sometimes in pilgrimage it goes well with him: he finds faithful friends by being on pilgrimage, whom he could not find in his homeland. He had enemies, so that he was driven from his homeland; and when he was on pilgrimage, he found what he did not have in his homeland. Not so is that homeland Jerusalem, where all are good: whoever is on pilgrimage from there is among the evil; nor can he withdraw from the evil, except when he has returned to the fellowship of the Angels, that he may be there whence he is on pilgrimage. There all are righteous and holy, who enjoy the Word of God without reading, without letters: for what to us is written through pages, they discern through the face of God. What a homeland! A great homeland, and wretched are the pilgrims from that homeland.

**7.** But that he says, *My pilgrimage has become far off;* it is especially their voice, that is, of the Church itself, which labors on this earth. It is the voice of her who cries from the ends of the earth in another psalm, saying: *From the ends of the earth I have cried to you*[^23]. Which of us cries from the ends of the earth? Neither I, nor you, nor that one: but from the ends of the earth the whole Church itself, the whole inheritance of Christ cries; because the Church is his inheritance, and of the Church it was said: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth*[^24]. If, then, the possession of Christ is even to the ends of the earth, and the possession of Christ is all the saints, and all the saints are one man in Christ, because the holy unity is in Christ; that one man says: *From the ends of the earth I have cried to you, while my heart was in anguish*[^25]. This man's pilgrimage, then, has become far off among the evil. And as if it were said to him: With whom, then, do you dwell, that you groan? *My pilgrimage,* he says, *has become far off.* But what if he were with the good? If he were with the good, he would not say, *Alas for me!* Alas is a voice of misery, a voice of calamity and unhappiness; but yet in hope, because he has now at least learned to groan. For many are both wretched, and do not groan, and are on pilgrimage, and are unwilling to return. This man, now wishing to return, recognizes the unhappiness of his pilgrimage: because he has recognized it, he returns; and begins to ascend, because he has begun to sing the Song of Ascents. Where, then, does he groan, and among whom does he dwell? *I have dwelt with the tents of Kedar.* Because this word is Hebrew, without doubt you did not understand it. What is, *I have dwelt with the tents of Kedar?* Kedar, as far as we remember from the interpretation of Hebrew names, signifies "Darkness." Kedar, interpreted in Latin, is called "Darkness." But you know that Abraham had two sons, whom indeed the Apostle mentions, and says they are unto the image of two testaments: one was of the bondwoman, the other of the free woman. Of the bondwoman was Ishmael; of the free woman Sarah, whom he received out of despair by faith, was Isaac. Both of the seed of Abraham, but not both heir of Abraham. One born of Abraham, but yet does not inherit: the other also heir; not only son, but also heir. In Ishmael are all who worship God carnally. For to them the old testament too pertains: because so the Apostle said, *Wishing to be under the Law, have you not heard the Law? For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one of the bondwoman, and one of the free woman; which things are in allegory. For these are the two testaments*[^26]. What are the two testaments? One old, and the other new. The old testament from God, and the new testament from God; as of Abraham both Ishmael and Isaac. But Ishmael unto the earthly, Isaac unto the heavenly kingdom. Therefore the old testament has earthly promises, an earthly Jerusalem, an earthly Palestine, an earthly kingdom, earthly safety, the subjugation of enemies, abundance of children, fruitfulness of crops. All these are earthly promises. In figure they are understood spiritually, as the earthly Jerusalem was a shadow of the heavenly kingdom, and the earthly kingdom was a shadow of the kingdom of heaven. Ishmael in shadow, Isaac in light. If, then, Ishmael in shadow, no wonder that there is darkness there. For thicker shadows are darkness. Therefore Ishmael in darkness, Isaac in light. Whoever here too, even in the Church, seek earthly happiness from God, still pertain to Ishmael. They are the ones who contradict the spiritual who are advancing, and disparage them, and have unjust lips and crafty tongues. Against whom this man, ascending, prayed, and to him were applied the desolating coals, and the sharp arrows of the mighty. For among them he still lives, until the whole threshing floor is winnowed: therefore he said, *I have dwelt with the tents of Kedar.* For the tents of Ishmael too were called Kedar. So the book of Genesis has it; so it has it, that Kedar pertains to Ishmael. Therefore Isaac is with Ishmael; that is, those who pertain to Isaac live among those who pertain to Ishmael. These wish to ascend upward, those wish to press downward: these wish to fly to God, those try to pluck out their wings. For in the Apostle it is said thus: *But as then, he who was born according to the flesh persecuted him who was according to the spirit; so it is now too*[^27]. The spiritual, then, suffer persecution from the carnal. But what does the Scripture say? *Cast out the bondwoman and her son; for the son of the bondwoman will not be heir with my son Isaac*[^28]. But this that it says, *Cast out,* when will it be? When the threshing floor has begun to be winnowed. But now, before it is cast out: *Alas for me, that my sojourning has become far off! I have dwelt with the tents of Kedar.* And he expounds to us who these are who pertain to the tents of Kedar.

**8.** [v. 6.] *My soul has been much on pilgrimage.* Lest you should understand a bodily pilgrimage, he said the soul is on pilgrimage. The body is on pilgrimage in places, the soul is on pilgrimage in affections. If you love the earth, you are on pilgrimage from God: if you love God, you ascend to God. Let us exercise ourselves in the love of God and neighbor, that we may return to love. If we fall to the earth, we wither and rot. But this man, who had fallen, there was a descent to him, that he might ascend. Attending to the time of his pilgrimage, he said that he was on pilgrimage in the tents of Kedar. Why? Because *my soul has been much on pilgrimage.* There it is on pilgrimage, where it ascends. Not in body is it on pilgrimage, not in body does it ascend. But where does it ascend? *Ascents,* he says, *in the heart.* If, then, it ascends in the heart, nothing ascends through the ascents of the heart except the soul which is on pilgrimage. But until it arrives, *my soul has been much on pilgrimage.* Where? In *the tents of Kedar.*

**9.** [v. 7.] *With those who hated peace, I was peaceful.* But that you may hear, dearest brethren, you will not be able to prove how truly you sing, unless you have begun to do what you sing. However much I say it, in whatever way I expound it, with whatever words I turn it, it does not enter into the heart of him in whom is not its work. Begin to act, and see what we say. Then at each single word the tears flow forth, then the psalm is sung, and the heart does what is sung in the psalm. For how many sound with the voice, and are mute in heart? And how many are silent with the lips, and cry out with their affection? Because the ears of God are toward the heart of man: as the ears of the body are toward the mouth of man, so the heart of man is toward the ears of God. Many with closed mouth are heard, and many in great cries are not heard. We ought to pray with our affections, and to say: *My soul has been much on pilgrimage: with those who hated peace, I was peaceful.* For what else do we say to these heretics, except: Know peace, love peace? You say you are righteous. But if you were righteous, you would groan among the chaff as the grain. For because there are grains in the Catholic Church, and they are true grains; therefore they bear the chaff, until the threshing floor is winnowed; because among the chaff they cry, *Alas for me, that my sojourning has become far off! I have dwelt with the tents of Kedar.* I have dwelt, he says, with the chaff. But as from chaff much smoke goes out, so from Kedar darkness. *I have dwelt with the tents of Kedar: my soul has been much on pilgrimage.* It is the voice of the grain, groaning among the chaff. These things we say to those who hate peace; and we say, *With those who hated peace, I was peaceful.* Who are they who hate peace? Those who tear apart unity. For if they had not hated peace, they would have remained in unity. But of course they separated themselves for this reason, that they might be righteous, that they might not have the unjust mixed in. Either this is our voice, or theirs; choose whose it is. The Catholic Church says: Unity is not to be abandoned, the Church of God is not to be cut off. God will judge afterward concerning the evil and the good. If now the evil cannot be separated from the good, they are to be borne for a time: the evil can be with us on the threshing floor, in the barn they cannot. And perhaps those who appear evil today will be good tomorrow; and those who today are proud of their goodness will tomorrow be found evil. Whoever, therefore, humbly bears the evil for a time, himself will arrive at everlasting rest. This is the Catholic voice. But of what sort is their voice, who understand neither what they say, nor the things about which they make assertions[^29]? *Touch not the unclean thing*[^30]; and: *He who touches the unclean thing will be defiled*[^31]: let us separate ourselves, lest we be mingled with the evil. And we to them: Love peace, love unity. You do not know from how many good you are separating yourselves, while you slander them as if evil. They rage, they are fierce, when we say these things: for they seek even to put us to death. Their assaults have often appeared, their ambushes have appeared. When, therefore, we live among their ambushes, and to those to whom we say, Love peace, they oppose us; is not this our voice: *With those who hated peace, I was peaceful? When I spoke to them, they made war on me without cause.* But what is it, brethren, *they made war on me?* And it would be too little, if he did not add, *without cause.* To those to whom we say, Love peace, love Christ: do we say, Love and honor us? But: Honor Christ. We do not wish to be honored, but him. For what are we to the apostle Paul? Who yet said to those little ones, whom evil men and evil persuaders wished to cut off from unity into schisms; what did he say to them? *Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul*[^32]? This we too say: Love peace, love Christ. For if they love peace, they love Christ. When, therefore, we say, Love peace; this we say, Love Christ. Why? Because of Christ the Apostle says: *For he himself is our peace, who made both one*[^33]. If, then, Christ is peace for this reason, because *he made both one,* why have you made of one, two? How, then, are you peaceful, that, while Christ makes one of two, you make of one, two? But because we say these things, with those who hate peace we are peaceful; and yet those who hate peace, when we spoke to them, made war on us without cause.
