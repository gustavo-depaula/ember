# Exposition of Psalm 84

**1.** We have besought the Lord our God, that he show us his mercy, and give us his salvation. This indeed was said in prophecy, when these Psalms were first being spoken and written: but as for this present time, the Lord has already shown the Gentiles his mercy, and given them his salvation. He indeed has shown it; but many refuse to be healed, so as to see what he shows. But because he himself heals the eyes of the heart to see him; therefore, when he had said: *Show us your mercy,* as if many blind men would say: How shall we see, when you begin to show? he added: *And give us your salvation.* For by giving his salvation, he heals in us that by which we can see what he shows: not as a man who is a physician himself cures, so as to show this light to those whom he has cured; and that light which he is to show is one thing, but the physician himself who cures the eyes to which he may show the light, which light he is not, is another. Not so, then, is the Lord our God: for he himself is the physician who cures that by which we can see, and he himself is the light which we can see. Yet let us, attentive, run briefly through the whole Psalm, as we can, as the Lord grants, on account of the narrowness of time.

**2.** [v. 1.] Its title is: *Unto the end, for the sons of Korah, a Psalm.* Let us understand "the end" as none but him whom the Apostle speaks of: *For Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^1]. Therefore when first in the title of the Psalm it set: *Unto the end;* it directed our heart unto Christ. If we attend to him, we shall not err; because he himself is the truth toward which we hasten, and he himself is the way by which we run[^2]. What is, *for the sons of Korah?* Korah is interpreted from the Hebrew word into Latin as "bald." Therefore *for the sons of Korah,* for the sons of the bald one. Who is this bald one? Not that we should mock him, but that we should weep before him[^3]. For certain ones mocked, and were laid waste by demons: as in the book of Kingdoms the boys mocked the bald Elisha, and said after him: *Bald-head, bald-head;* bears came forth from the wood, and devoured the boys mocking ill[^4], and to be mourned by their parents. This deed signified by a certain prophecy our Lord Jesus Christ to come. For he was mocked by the Jews mocking as it were a bald one, because he was crucified in the place of Calvary[^5]. But we, if we have believed in him, are his sons. To us, then, this psalm is sung, where it is inscribed, *for the sons of Korah*: for we are the sons of the bridegroom[^6]. For that bridegroom, giving the pledge to his bride, his blood and the Holy Spirit, by which he has enriched us meanwhile in this pilgrimage; yet still keeps for us his hidden riches. For whence he gave such a pledge, what is it that he keeps?

**3.** [v. 2.] And so the Prophet sings to him for the future, and uses words as if of past time; he speaks as done what was to come; because with God even what is to come is already done. There, then, the Prophet saw things future to us, but already done in his most certain providence and predestination, as he says even in that psalm where all recognize Christ; for it is recited as if the Gospel were read: *They pierced my hands and feet, they numbered all my bones; but they themselves considered and gazed upon me, they divided my garments among themselves, and over my garment they cast lots*[^7]. Who, when the reader reads the Psalm, does not recognize the Gospel? And yet when it was said in the Psalm, it was not said: They will pierce my hands and feet; but, *they pierced my hands and feet*: nor was it said: They will number my bones; but, *they numbered my bones*: nor was it said: They will divide my garments; but, *they divided my garments among themselves.* All these things which the Prophet saw as future, he indicated as past: so here too: *You have blessed, O Lord, your land;* as though he had already done it.

**4.** *You have turned away the captivity of Jacob.* His ancient people is Jacob, the people of Israel, born of the seed of Abraham, in the promise to be one day the heir of God. There was indeed that people, then, to whom the Old Testament was given; but in the Old Testament the New Testament was figured: that was a figure, this the expression of the truth. But in that figure, according to a certain foretelling of things to come, a certain land of promise was given to that people, in a certain region where the people of the Jews dwelt; where there is also the city of Jerusalem, whose name we have all heard. This land, then, when that people had received it, it suffered many troubles from its enemies, the neighbors warring against it on every side: and when it sinned against its God, it was given into captivity; not unto destruction, but unto discipline; not with the Father condemning, but scourging. And when it was possessed, it was freed, and several times that nation was taken captive and freed: and now it is in captivity, and this for a great sin, that it crucified its Lord. What, then, according to those men, do we take, that he says: *You have turned away the captivity of Jacob?* Or do we perhaps understand here another captivity, from which we all wish to be freed? For we all belong to Jacob, if we belong to the seed of Abraham. For this the Apostle says: *In Isaac shall your seed be called; that is, not those who are the children of the flesh, these are the children of God, but the children of the promise are reckoned as seed*[^8]. If the children of the promise are reckoned as seed, the Jews by offending God degenerated; we, by deserving God, were made of the race of Abraham, not belonging to the flesh, but belonging to faith. For by imitating faith, we were made sons: but they, by degenerating from faith, deserved to be disinherited. For, that you may know that they lost that which they had been born of Abraham, when they arrogantly boasted in the hearing of the Lord Jesus Christ, glorying of blood and not of life, and said to the Lord: *We have Abraham as our father;* the Lord said to them as to degenerates: *If you are the sons of Abraham, do the works of Abraham*[^9]. If, then, those were therefore no longer sons, because they did not do the works of Abraham; we are therefore sons, because we do the works of Abraham. What are the works of Abraham which we do? Now Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness[^10]. Therefore we all belong to Jacob, imitating the faith of Abraham, who believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness. What, then, is that captivity from which we wish to be freed? for I suppose none of us is now among the Barbarians, nor has any armed nation rushed in, and led us captive. But now I show a certain captivity, in which we groan, and from which we desire to be freed. Let the apostle Paul come forward, let him himself tell it; let him be our mirror, let him speak, and let us see ourselves there: for there is no one who does not recognize himself here. That blessed apostle, then, says: *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man;* within, the law of God delights me: *but I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind.* Now you have heard the law, you have heard the fight; the captivity you had not yet heard, hear what follows: *fighting,* he says, *against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members.* We have recognized the captivity: who of us is there who does not wish to be freed from this captivity? And from where will he be freed? Since this same Psalm sang it as future: *You have turned away the captivity of Jacob.* To whom did he say it? To Christ, on account of *Unto the end,* on account of the sons of Korah: for he has turned away the captivity of Jacob. Hear Paul himself too confessing. When he said that he was led captive by the law in his members fighting against the law of his mind, he cried out under that captivity, and said: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* He asked who it might be, and straightway it occurred to him: *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^11]. Of this grace of God the Prophet says to this our Lord Jesus Christ: *You have turned away the captivity of Jacob.* Attend to the captivity of Jacob, attend that this is it: You have turned away our captivity: not by freeing us from the Barbarians, on whom we have not run; but by freeing us from evil works, from our sins, through which Satan ruled over us. For if anyone is freed from his sins, the prince of sins has nothing whereby to rule over him.

**5.** [vv. 3-4.] For how did he turn away the captivity of Jacob? See that this liberation is spiritual, see that it is enacted within: *You have remitted,* he says, *the iniquity of your people; you have covered all their sins.* Behold whence he turned away the captivity, because he remitted the iniquity: iniquity held captive, the iniquity remitted you are freed. Confess, then, that you are in captivity, that you may be worthy to be freed: for he who has not understood his enemy, how does he call upon a liberator? *You have covered all their sins.* What is, *you have covered?* That you might not see them. What is, that you might not see them? That you might not take vengeance on them. You willed not to see our sins; and therefore you did not see them, because you willed not to see them: *You have covered all their sins. You have allayed all your anger; you have turned away from the anger of your indignation.*

**6.** [v. 5.] And since these things are said of the future, although the words sound past; it follows, and says: *Convert us, O God of our salvations.* What he was just now narrating as if it had been done, how does he pray that it be done; except because he wished to show that he had spoken the past words in prophecy? But that what he said was already done was not yet done, he shows from this, because he prays that it be done: *Convert us, O Lord of our salvations; and turn away your anger from us.* Were you not just now saying: *You have turned away the captivity of Jacob; you have covered all their sins: you have allayed all your anger; you have turned away from the anger of your indignation?* How is it here: *And turn away your anger from us?* The Prophet answers you: I say those things as if done, because I see things future; but because they are not yet done, I pray that they may come, which I have already seen. *Turn away your anger from us.*

**7.** [v. 6.] *Do not be angry with us forever.* For by the anger of God we are mortal, and by the anger of God on this earth in want and in the labor of our face we eat bread. For this Adam heard, when he sinned[^12]; and that Adam was all of us, because in Adam all die: what he heard, followed upon us too. For we were not yet we, but we were in Adam: therefore whatever befell Adam himself, followed upon us too, that we should die; for all of us were in him. For those sins of parents do not pertain to children, which parents commit when the children are already born: for once born, the children pertain to themselves, and the parents pertain to themselves. And so those who are born, if they hold the bad ways of their parents, must needs bear also their deserts: but if they change themselves, and have not imitated their bad parents, they begin to have their own desert, not the desert of their parents. But so far is your father's sin not to harm you, if you change yourself, that it does not even harm your father himself, if he changes himself. But what our root received unto this mortality, it drew from Adam. What did it draw? This frailty of the flesh, this torment of pains, this house of poverty, this bond of death, and the snares of temptations: we bear all these in this flesh; and this is the anger of God, because it is the vengeance of God. But because it was to be that we should be regenerated, and by believing be made new, and in the resurrection all that mortality should be consumed, and the newness of the whole man be restored; for as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all shall be made alive[^13]: seeing this, the Prophet says: *Do not be angry with us forever, nor extend your anger from generation to generation.* There was a first generation, mortal, from your anger; there will be a second generation, immortal, from your mercy.

**8.** [v. 7.] What then? did you bestow this on yourself, O man, that, because you were converted to God, you deserved his mercy; but those who were not converted did not obtain mercy, but found anger? But how could you, that you might be converted, except you were called? Did not he who called you, turned away, himself bestow it that you might be converted? Do not, then, arrogate to yourself even the conversion itself; because unless he called you fleeing, you could not be converted. Therefore the Prophet too, attributing the benefit of conversion itself to God, prays this, and says: *O God, you, converting, will give us life.* And not as though we ourselves of our own accord without your mercy are converted to you, and you will give us life: but, *you, converting, will give us life;* so that not only our quickening is from you, but also the very conversion that we may be quickened. *O God, you, converting, will give us life; and your people will rejoice in you.* By his own evil he will rejoice in himself; by his own good he will rejoice in you. For when he wished to have joys from himself, he found lamentation in himself: but now, because our whole joy is God, let him who wishes to rejoice securely rejoice in him who cannot perish. For what, my brothers, do you wish to rejoice in silver? Either the silver perishes, or you; and no one knows which first: yet this is established, that both are to perish; which first, is uncertain. For neither can a man here remain always, nor can silver here remain always; so gold, so a garment, so a house, so money, so broad estates, so finally this light. Do not, then, wish to rejoice in these things: but rejoice in that light which has no setting; rejoice in that light which yesterday does not precede, nor tomorrow follow. What is this light? *I am,* he says, *the light of the world*[^14]. He who says to you: *I am the light of the world,* calls you to himself. When he calls you, he converts you; when he converts you, he heals you; when he has healed you, you will see your converter, to whom it is said: *And your people will rejoice in you.*

**9.** [v. 8.] *Show us, O Lord, your mercy.* This is what we have sung, and have already spoken of here. *Show us, O Lord, your mercy; and give us your salvation; your salvation,* your Christ. Happy is he to whom God shows his mercy. He is the one who cannot be proud, to whom God shows his mercy. For by showing him his mercy, he persuades him that whatever good the man himself has, he has only from him who is all our good. And when a man sees that whatever good he has he has not from himself, but from his God; he sees that all that is praised in him is of the mercy of God, not of his own merits: and by seeing these things he is not proud, by not being proud he is not exalted, by not exalting himself he does not fall, by not falling he stands, by standing he clings, by clinging he abides, by abiding he enjoys fully, and rejoices in the Lord his God. He himself who made him will be his delights; and those delights no one corrupts, no one interrupts, no one takes away. What powerful man will threaten to take him away? what evil neighbor, what robber, what plotter takes God away from you? And he can take from you all that you possess in body, he does not take from you him whom you possess in heart. He himself is the mercy, which would that God show us: *Show us, O Lord, your mercy; and give us your salvation*: give us your Christ: for in him is your mercy. Let us too say to him: Give us your Christ. He has indeed already given us his Christ: yet still let us say to him: Give us your Christ; because we say to him: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^15]. And what is our bread, except he himself who said: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^16]? Let us say to him: Give us your Christ. For he gave us Christ, but as man: him whom he gave us as man, that very one he will give us as God. For to men he gave a man; because he gave him such to men as could be grasped by men: for God Christ no man could grasp. He was made to men a man, he kept himself God for gods. Or have I perhaps said it arrogantly? Truly arrogantly, unless he himself had said: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High*[^17]. To that very adoption we are renewed, that we may be made sons of God. We are so already, but by faith: we are so in hope, we are not yet so in reality. *For in hope we have been saved,* as the Apostle says. *But hope that is seen is not hope: for what one sees, why does he hope for? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it through patience*[^18]. For what do we *wait for through patience,* except to see what we believe? For now we believe what we do not see: by remaining in that which, believing, we do not see, we shall deserve to see what we believe. Therefore what does John say in his Epistle? *Dearly beloved, we are the sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be.* Who would not exult, if suddenly it were said to I know not what pilgrim, ignorant of his birth, suffering some want, and set in trouble and labor: You are the son of a senator; your father rejoices in an ample patrimony in your land; I call you back to your father? with what joy would he exult, if this were said by a promiser who was not deceitful? Therefore there came an apostle of Christ who was not deceitful, and says: What is it that you despair of yourselves? what is it that you afflict yourselves, and are worn down with grief? what is it that, by following your concupiscences, you wish to be worn down in the want of these pleasures? You have a father, you have a homeland, you have a patrimony. Who is this father? *Dearly beloved, we are the sons of God.* Why, then, do we not yet see our father? Because *it has not yet appeared what we shall be.* We are so already, but in hope: for *what we shall be has not yet appeared.* And what shall we be? *We know,* he says, *that when he shall appear, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^19]. But this he said of the Father, but of the Son the Lord Jesus Christ he did not say it: and perhaps by seeing the Father, not the Son, we shall be blessed? Hear Christ himself: *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^20]. For when the one God is seen, the Trinity is seen, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Hear more expressly that the vision of the Son himself will bestow on us blessedness, and there is no difference between the vision of him and the vision of the Father. He himself says in the Gospel: *He who loves me keeps my commandments, and I will love him, and will show myself to him.* He was speaking to them, and saying, *I will show myself to him.* Why? Was it not he himself who was speaking? But the flesh was seen by the flesh; the divinity the heart did not see. But for this the flesh saw the flesh, that through faith the heart might be cleansed, whence God might be seen. For it was said of the Lord: *Cleansing their hearts by faith*[^21]: and the Lord said: *Blessed are the pure of heart, for they shall see God*[^22]. Therefore he promised us to show himself to us. Of what kind is his beauty, brothers, think. All these beautiful things which you see, which you love, he made. If these are beautiful, what is he? if these are great, how great is he? Therefore from these things which we love here, let us rather desire him; and despising these, let us love him: that by that love itself, through faith, we may cleanse the heart, and our cleansed heart may find the sight of him. The light which will be shown to us ought to find us sound: this faith now does. This, then, we have said here. *And give us your salvation*: give us your Christ, may we know your Christ, may we see your Christ; not as the Jews saw him, and crucified him, but as the angels see him, and rejoice.

**10.** [v. 9.] *I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me.* The Prophet said it. God was speaking within him, and the world was making a din for him outside. Restraining himself somewhat, then, from the din of the world, and turning himself to himself, and from himself to him whose voice he heard within; as it were stopping his ear against the tumultuous unrest of this life, and against the soul weighed down by the body that is corrupted, and the sense pressing down by the earthly habitation, thinking many things[^23], he says: *I will hear what the Lord God speaks in me*: and he heard, what? *For he will speak peace to his people.* The voice of Christ, then, the voice of God is peace, it calls to peace. Come now, he says, whoever are not yet in peace, love peace: for what better can you find of me than peace? What is peace? Where there is no war. What is, where there is no war? Where there is no contradiction, where nothing resists, nothing is adverse. See whether we are already there; see whether there is not yet a conflict with the devil, see whether all the saints and faithful do not wrestle with the prince of demons. And how do they wrestle with him whom they do not see? They wrestle with their own concupiscences, to which he suggests sins: and by not consenting to what he suggests, even if they are not conquered, yet they fight. There is, then, not yet peace, where there is a fight. Or give me a man who suffers nothing of temptation in his flesh, that he may be able to say to me that there is now peace. He perhaps suffers nothing of temptation in unlawful pleasures, at least he suffers the suggestions themselves: either there is suggested to him what he rejects, or what he is delighted by, from which he restrains himself. But behold now nothing unlawful delights him; at least he fights every day against hunger and thirst: for what righteous man is free from this? Hunger and thirst fight, then, weariness of the flesh fights against us, the delight of sleep fights, oppression fights. We wish to keep vigil; we doze: we wish to fast; we hunger and thirst: we wish to stand; we are tired out: we seek to sit; if we do this too for long, we fail. Whatever we provide ourselves for refreshment, there again we find failing. Are you hungry, someone says to you? you answer: I am hungry. He sets food before you; he had set it for refreshment: persevere in that which he set; surely you wished to be refreshed, do this always; by doing this, in that which you had applied for refreshment, there you find weariness. By much sitting you were tired; you rise, by walking you are refreshed: persevere in that by which you are refreshed, by much walking you are wearied; you seek to sit again. Find me something by which you may refresh yourself, where, if you persevere, you do not again fail. Of what kind, then, is this peace which men have here, with so many troubles, lusts, wants, weariness resisting? This is not the true, this is not the perfect peace. What will the perfect peace be? *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality: then will come about the word that is written: Death has been swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your sting? where, O death, is your strife*[^24]? For where there is still mortality, how is there full peace? For from death comes this weariness which we find in all our refreshments. It is from death, because we bear a mortal body; which indeed the Apostle calls dead even before the loosing of the soul: *The body indeed,* he says, *is dead because of sin*[^25]. For if you persevere in that by which you refresh yourself, you will even die. Persevere in much eating; that very thing will kill you: persevere in much fasting; from that you will die: sit always, so as not to wish to rise; you will die from that: walk always, so as not to wish to rest; you will die from that: keep vigil always, so as not to wish to sleep; you will die from that: sleep always, so as not to wish to keep vigil; you will die from that. When, then, death has been swallowed up in victory, these things will not be; and there will be full and eternal peace. We shall be in a certain city: brothers, when I speak of it, I am unwilling to finish, and especially when scandals increase. Who would not desire that city, from which a friend does not go out, into which an enemy does not enter, where there is no tempter, no seditious one, no one dividing the people of God, no one wearying the Church in the service of the devil; when their very prince is sent into the eternal fire, and these with him, whoever consent to him, and refuse to withdraw from him? There will be, then, a purged peace in the sons of God, all loving one another, seeing themselves full of God, when God will be all in all[^26]. We shall have God as a common spectacle; we shall have God as a common possession; we shall have God as a common peace. For whatever it is that he now gives us, he himself will be to us in place of all that he gives: he himself will be the perfect and full peace. This he speaks to his people; this he wished to hear who said: *I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me; for he will speak peace to his people, and over his saints, and to those who convert their heart to him.* Come now, brothers, do you wish that this peace which God speaks pertain to you? Convert your heart to him; not to me, or to that man, or to any man. For whoever man wishes to convert the hearts of men to himself, falls with them. Which is better, that you fall with him to whom you are converted, or that you stand with him with whom you are converted? Our joy, our peace, our rest, the end of all troubles, is none but God: blessed are *those who convert their heart to him.*

**11.** [v. 10.] *Yet his salvation is near to those who fear him.* There were certain ones who already feared him in the nation of the Jews. Throughout all lands everywhere idols were worshiped; demons were feared, not God: in that nation God was feared. But on account of what was he feared? In the Old Testament he was feared, lest he give them into captivity, lest he take away their land, lest he batter their vines with hail, lest he make their wives barren, lest he take away their children from them. For these carnal promises of God still held small souls, and on account of these God was feared: but he was near to those who feared him even on account of these. The pagan sought land from the devil; the Jew sought land from God: it was one thing that they sought; but not one was he from whom they sought. By seeking what the pagan sought, this man was yet distinguished from the pagan, because he sought these things from him who had made all things. And God was near to those to whom he was far from the Gentiles; yet he looked upon both those who were far and those who were near, as the Apostle says: *And coming, he preached peace to you who were far off, and peace to those who were near*[^27]. Whom did he call those who were near? The Jews, because they worshiped one God. Whom did he call those who were far off? The Gentiles, because they had let go of him by whom they had been made, and worshiped those things which they themselves had made. For not by regions is anyone far from God, but by affections. You love God, you are near; you hate God, you are far off. Standing in one place, you are both near and far. Therefore, brothers, the Prophet looked at these things: although he saw the general mercy of God upon all; yet he saw something special and proper in the Jewish nation, and said: *Yet. I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me; for he will speak peace to his people*: and his people will not be Judea alone, but will be gathered from all nations; *for over his saints he will speak peace, and to those who convert their heart to him,* and all who shall convert their heart to him from the whole world. *Yet his salvation is near to those who fear him, that glory may dwell in our land*: that is, in that land in which the Prophet had been born, may a greater glory dwell; because thence Christ began to be preached. Thence the Apostles, and first sent there: thence the Prophets, there first the temple, there sacrifice was made to God, there the Patriarchs, there he himself even came of the seed of Abraham, there Christ was manifested, there Christ appeared: for thence the Virgin Mary, who bore Christ. There he walked on his own feet, there he did marvelous things. Finally, he conferred so great an honor on that nation, that when a certain Canaanite woman entreated him, seeking the health of her daughter, he said to her: *I am not sent except to the sheep that are lost, of the house of Israel*[^28]. Therefore, seeing this, the Prophet says: *Yet his salvation is near to those who fear him, that glory may dwell in our land.*

**12.** [v. 11.] *Mercy and truth have met each other.* Truth in our land in the person of the Jews, mercy in the land of the Gentiles. For truth where? Where were the utterances of God. Mercy where? In those who had let go of their God, and turned themselves to demons. Did he then despise them too? As if he said: Call even those fugitives far off, who have withdrawn much from me; call them, let them find me seeking them, because they themselves were unwilling to seek me. Therefore: *Mercy and truth have met each other; righteousness and peace have kissed each other.* Do righteousness, and you will have peace; that righteousness and peace may kiss each other. For if you do not love righteousness, you will not have peace: for these two love each other, righteousness and peace, and kiss each other; so that he who does righteousness finds peace kissing righteousness. They are two friends; you perhaps want one, and do not do the other: for there is no one who does not want peace; but not all want to work righteousness. Ask all men: Do you want peace? With one mouth the whole human race answers you: I wish it, I desire it, I want it, I love it. Love righteousness too; because they are two friends, righteousness and peace; they kiss each other: if you do not love the friend of peace, peace itself will not love you, nor will it come to you. For what great thing is it to desire peace? Any wicked man desires peace. For peace is a good thing. But do righteousness; because righteousness and peace kiss each other, they do not quarrel. Why do you quarrel with righteousness? Behold, righteousness says to you: Do not steal; and you do not hear: Do not commit adultery; you do not wish to hear: Do not do to another what you do not wish to suffer, do not say to another what you do not wish said to you also. You are an enemy of my friend, peace says to you; why do you seek me? I am the friend of righteousness, whomever I find an enemy of my friend, I do not approach him. Do you wish, then, to come to peace? Do righteousness. Therefore another psalm says to you: *Turn away from evil, and do good* (this is to love righteousness); and when you have already turned away from evil, and done good, *seek peace, and pursue it*[^29]. For you will not seek it long, because it too will meet you, that it may kiss righteousness.

**13.** [v. 12.] *Truth has arisen from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven. Truth has arisen from the earth*: Christ was born of a woman. *Truth has arisen from the earth*: the Son of God has come forth from the flesh. What is truth? The Son of God. What is earth? The flesh. Ask whence Christ was born, and you see that *truth has arisen from the earth.* But this truth which has arisen from the earth, was before the earth, and through it heaven and earth were made; but that righteousness might look down from heaven—that is, that men might be justified by divine grace—truth was born of the Virgin Mary; that he might be able to offer for them, to be justified, a sacrifice, the sacrifice of the passion, the sacrifice of the cross. And whence would he offer a sacrifice for our sins, unless he died? but whence would he die, unless he received from us that whereby he might die? That is, unless from us he took mortal flesh, Christ could not die; because the Word does not die, the divinity does not die, the Power and Wisdom of God does not die. How would he offer a sacrifice, a saving victim, if he did not die? but how would he die, unless he put on flesh? how would he put on flesh, unless truth arose from the earth? *Truth has arisen from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven.*

**14.** We can say from here another sense. *Truth has arisen from the earth*: confession from man. For you were a sinful man. O earth, you who, when you sinned, heard: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^30], let truth arise from you, that righteousness may look down from heaven. How does truth arise from you, since you are a sinner, since you are wicked? Confess your sins, and truth will arise from you. For if, when you are wicked, you say that you are righteous, how will truth arise from you? But if, when you are wicked, you say that you are wicked; *truth has arisen from the earth.* Attend to that Publican praying far from the Pharisee in the temple, who did not even dare to lift his eyes to heaven, but struck his breast, saying: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*: behold, *truth has arisen from the earth,* because confession of sins was made by the man. What, then, follows? *Amen I say to you, that that Publican went down justified rather than that Pharisee: for everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled; and he who humbles himself shall be exalted*[^31]. *Truth has arisen from the earth,* in the confession of sins; *and righteousness has looked down from heaven,* that that Publican might go down justified rather than that Pharisee. For that you may know that truth pertains to the confession of sins, the evangelist John says: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.* How, then, truth arises from the earth, that righteousness may look down from heaven, hear him following and saying: *If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity*[^32]. *Truth,* then, *has arisen from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven.* What *righteousness has looked down from heaven?* As of God saying: Let us spare this man, because he himself has not spared himself; let us pardon, because he himself acknowledges. He has turned to punishing his sin; let me too turn to freeing him. *Truth has arisen from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven.*

**15.** [v. 13.] *For the Lord will give sweetness, and our land will give its fruit.* One verse remains, I ask that what I am about to say be without weariness. Attend to a necessary matter, my brothers; attend, take it in, carry it away with you, and let not the seed of God be empty in your hearts. *Truth,* he says, *has arisen from the earth,* confession of sins from man; *and righteousness has looked down from heaven,* that is, justification was given by the Lord God to the one confessing, so that the impious himself may acknowledge that he cannot be made pious, unless he do it to whom he confesses, by believing in him who justifies the impious[^33]. Your sins, then, you can have; you will not have good fruit, unless he give it to whom you confess. Therefore, when he had said: *Truth has arisen from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven;* as though it were said to him: What is it that you said: *Righteousness has looked down from heaven?* *For,* he says: *The Lord will give sweetness, and our land will give its fruit.* Let us, then, look at ourselves; and if we find nothing in ourselves but sins, let us hate sins, and desire righteousness. For when we have begun to hate sins, that very hatred of sins begins to make us like God; because we hate this which God too hates. When, then, you have begun to hate sins and to confess to God, when unlawful delights sweep you away and lead you to things that do not profit you, groan to God: and confessing to him your sins, you will deserve from him delight, and he will give you the sweetness of doing righteousness, that righteousness may begin to delight you, whom formerly iniquity delighted; so that you who first rejoiced in drunkenness, may rejoice in sobriety; and you who first rejoiced in thefts, to take from a man what you did not have, may seek to give to one who does not have what you have; and you whom it delighted to seize, may be delighted to give; you whom it delighted to look on at the games, may be delighted to pray; you whom trifling and adulterous songs delighted, may be delighted to say a hymn to God; to run to church, you who first ran to the theater. Whence was this sweetness born, except because *the Lord will give sweetness, and our land will give its fruit?* For behold, see what I say: behold, we have spoken to you the word of God, we have scattered the seed in your devout hearts, finding your breasts as it were furrowed by the plow of confession; with devotion and attention you received the seed, think upon the word which you heard, as it were breaking the clods, lest the birds snatch the seed, that what is sown may be able to germinate there; and unless God rains, what does it profit that it is sown? This is: *The Lord will give sweetness, and our land will give its fruit.* For let him by his visitations, in leisure, in business, in the house, in bed, at the banquet, in conversation, in walking, visit your hearts, where we are not. Let the rain of God come, and let what is sown there bear fruit; and where we are not, and we rest secure, or do something else, let God give the increase to the seeds we have scattered, that, attending afterward to your good conduct, we too may rejoice in the fruit: *Since the Lord will give sweetness, and our land will give its fruit.*

**16.** [v. 14.] *Righteousness will go before him, and will set its steps in the way.* That righteousness is the one which is in the confession of sins: for it is truth itself. For you ought to be righteous in yourself, so as to punish yourself: this is the first righteousness of man, that you punish yourself evil, and God make you good. Since, then, this is the first righteousness of man, it becomes the way for God, that God may come to you: there make a way for him, in the confession of sins. Therefore John too, when he baptized in the water of penitence, and wished those repenting of their former deeds to come to him, said this: *Prepare the way for the Lord, make straight his paths*[^34]. You were pleasing to yourself in your sins, O man; let what you were displease you, that you may be able to be what you were not. *Prepare the way for the Lord*: let this righteousness go before, that you confess your sins: he will come and visit you, because *he will set his steps in the way;* for there is now where he may set his steps, there is where he may come to you. But before you confessed your sins, you had blocked off to yourself the way of God; there was no way by which he might come to you. Confess your life, and you open the way; and Christ will come, *and set his steps in the way,* to form you by his footsteps.
