# Exposition of Psalm 106

**1.** This psalm commends to us the compassions of God, proved in us, and therefore sweeter to those who have experienced them. And it would be a wonder if it could be sweet to anyone, except to him who has learned in himself that which he hears in this psalm. Yet it was written not for some one or two, but for the people of God, and set forth for it to recognize itself as it were in a mirror. Whose title is not now to be treated: for it is *Alleluia,* and twice *Alleluia.* Which it is our custom to sing solemnly at a certain time, according to the ancient tradition of the Church: for not even this do we sing on certain days without a sacrament. We sing Alleluia indeed on certain days, but we think it every day. For if by this word the praise of God is signified, even if not in the mouth of the flesh, certainly in the mouth of the heart: *His praise is always in my mouth*[^1]. But that this title has Alleluia not once, but twice, is not proper to this psalm, but another earlier one too has it so. And as far as appears from its text, that one was sung of the people of Israel; but this is sung of the whole Church of God spread throughout the world. Perhaps not without merit it has Alleluia twice: because of which we cry both *Abba, Father.* Although *Abba* is nothing other than *Father*: yet the Apostle did not in vain say, *In whom we cry, Abba, Father*[^2]: except that one wall, coming to the cornerstone, cries, *Abba;* the other from the other side cries, *Father;* in that cornerstone certainly, which is our peace, who made both one. Let us see, then, here what we are admonished of, and whence we may rejoice, and whence we may groan, and whence we may ask for help; whence we are deserted, whence we are helped; what we are through ourselves, what through the mercy of God; how our pride is crushed, that his grace may be glorified. Let what I am about to say, if it can be, occur to each man in himself. But I speak to men who walk the way of God, and are set in some spiritual advancement: whence if any perhaps for this reason understand me less, let them find where they are, and by making progress hasten to understanding. But I do not think God will desert our effort, that what we say may reach all, whether the experienced or the inexperienced, so that the experienced may approve, the inexperienced may desire, and my discourse may be sweet to all: which will first be sweet to the Lord, if it is truthful; but it will be truthful, if it is not from myself to me, but from him. So the Psalm began.

**2.** [v. 1.] *Confess to the Lord, for he is sweet, for his mercy is unto the age.* Confess this, that he is sweet: if you have tasted, confess. But he cannot confess who was unwilling to taste: for whence will he say that to be sweet which he does not know? But you, if you have tasted how sweet the Lord is: *Confess to the Lord, for he is sweet*: if you have tasted with eagerness, belch it out by confession. *For his mercy is unto the age*, that is, forever. For here *unto the age* is set thus, because in some places of Scripture too, *unto the age*, that is, what is said in Greek eis ton aiona, is understood as forever. For his mercy is not for a time, and not forever; since this mercy of his is over men for this reason, that they may live with the angels forever.

**3.** [vv. 2, 3.] *Let those say it who were redeemed by the Lord.* Redeemed indeed the people of Israel too seems to have been from the land of Egypt, from the hand of slavery, from unfruitful labors, from works of mud: yet let us see whether it is they themselves who say these things, who were freed from Egypt by the Lord. It is not so. But who are these? *Whom he redeemed from the hand of the enemies.* Still anyone can take those too as redeemed from the hand of the enemies, that is, of the Egyptians. Let it be properly expressed who they are for whose sake this psalm wishes these things to be sung. *From the regions he gathered them.* They can still be the regions of Egypt, for there are many regions even of one province. Let it say openly: *From the east and the west, from the north and the sea.* These, then, we now understand as redeemed in the whole world. This people of God, freed from great and broad Egypt, is led as it were through the Red Sea, that in Baptism it may finish off the enemies. For by the sacrament as of the Red Sea, namely Baptism consecrated by the blood of Christ, the pursuing Egyptians, the sins, are blotted out; and as you escape, no enemy who pressed you remains. These, then, let them say these things; and let us hear now, brothers (since this people of God is being led) what is done here in the gathering of all the nations redeemed through Christ: not as if these things which are sung happen at once in all, but in each one of the believers; but in that people otherwise. For the whole people, the whole nation from the seed of Abraham according to the flesh, the whole multitude of the house of Israel was once led out of Egypt, once led through the Red Sea, once led through to the land of promise; for all those in whom these things happened were together: *But all these things happened to them in figure; and were written for our correction, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^3]. But we, not all at once, but little by little and one by one believing, are gathered into a certain one city, and into one people of God; but in each one of us, even singly, these things happen which are written, they happen in the people. For the people is from individuals, not individuals from the people: for is one man from peoples? But the people is constituted of individual men. Whatever, then, as I speak, you recognize in yourself, whoever you are who have experienced it, do not by thinking as it were remain in yourself, and suppose that it happens in you alone; but believe that these things happen either in all, or in nearly all, who come to this people, and are redeemed from the hand of the enemies by the precious blood.

**4.** For he is going to repeat constantly what we sang just now: *Let them confess to the Lord his compassions, and his wonders to the sons of men.* These verses, as far as I could notice, which you can too, he repeats four times; in which number, as far as we were able, with the Lord helping, to scrutinize, he signifies to us certain four temptations, from which he frees us to whom his compassions are confessed. For suppose a man at first seeking nothing, living according to the old life with seductive security, supposing that there is nothing else after this life that is at some time to be ended; a certain negligent and slothful man, having a heart overwhelmed by the allurements of the world, and lulled to sleep by deadly delights: that this man may be roused to seek the grace of God, that he may become anxious, and as it were wake from sleep, does not the hand of God rouse him? But yet by whom he was roused, he does not know. But he begins now to be God's, when he has come to know the faith of the truth. But before he knows it, he grieves at his error. For he finds himself in error, he wishes to know the truth, he knocks where he can, he tries what he can, he wanders where he can, he suffers even hunger for the truth itself. The first temptation, then, is of error and hunger. When, wearied in this temptation, he cries out to God, he is led through to the way of faith, whence he may begin to journey to the city of rest. He is led, then, to Christ, who said: *I am the way*[^4].

**5.** When, then, he has been there, now knowing what he ought to observe, sometimes by attributing much to himself, and as it were presuming on his own strength, he begins to wish to fight against sins, and because of pride to be overcome. He finds himself, then, bound by the difficulties of desires, and unable to walk the way because of the fetters: he feels himself shut in by the difficulty of vices; and, as if a wall of impossibility being raised, and the gates closed, he finds no way to escape so as to live rightly. He now knows how he ought to live: for before he was in error, and suffered hunger for the truth; but now he has received the food of truth, and is set in the way: he hears: Live well, according to what you know; for before you did not know how you should live, now you have received and know. He tries, he cannot; he feels himself bound, he cries out to the Lord. The second temptation, then, is of difficulty in working well, as that first was of error and hunger. He cries out in this too to the Lord: the Lord frees him from his necessities, breaks the bonds of difficulty, sets him in the working of equity. It now begins to be easy for him which had been difficult, to abstain from evils, not to commit adultery, not to steal, not to kill, not to commit sacrilege, not to covet another's: there has come to be an ease which had before been a difficulty. The Lord could bestow this without difficulty; but if we had this without difficulty, we would not recognize the giver of this good. For if at first, when he willed, he could, and did not feel the desires struggling against him, nor would the soul, weighed down by its bonds, be dashed about; he would attribute to his own strength what he felt himself able to do, and would not confess to the Lord his compassions.

**6.** After these two temptations, the first of error and want of the truth, the second of difficulty in working well, a third temptation takes up the man: I speak to one who has now passed these two. For these two, I confess, are known to many. For who does not know that he came from ignorance to the truth, from error to the way, from hunger of wisdom to the word of faith? Then many wrestle with the difficulties of their vices, and, still bound by habit, groan as in an enclosure and fetters. They recognize this temptation too, although they now say, if perhaps they say: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?*[^5] For see the tightest bonds: *The flesh*, he says, *desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; that you may not do the things that you wish*[^6]. Accordingly, one who has now been helped by the Spirit, that, as he did not wish to be an adulterer, so he is not; as he did not wish to be a thief, so he is not; and all those other things which men wish to conquer, and, often bent, are overcome, so that they cry out to God, that he rescue them from their necessities, and, freed from there, confess to the Lord his compassions: whoever, then, is such, and has conquered those difficulties, and now lives commendably among men, without any complaint of bad morals, is taken up by a third temptation of a certain weariness in the delay of this life, so that sometimes neither to read nor to pray delights him. The third temptation is contrary to the former: for before he was in danger from hunger, afterward from distaste. Whence is this too, except from a certain languor of the soul? Now adultery does not allure you, nor yet does the word of God delight you. Now, after the danger of inexperience and of cupidity, from which two you rejoice to have escaped, see lest weariness and distaste slay you. Nor is this a light temptation: recognize yourself in it, and cry out to the Lord, that he free you here too from your necessities; and when you have been freed from this temptation, let them confess to him his compassions.

**7.** But freed from error, freed from the difficulty of working well, freed from the weariness and distaste of the word of God, perhaps you will be worthy to have a people committed to you; you may be set at the helm of the ship, to govern the Church. There is the fourth temptation. The storms of the sea shaking the Church disturb the pilot. Finally, those three temptations every pious believer in the people of God can experience; this fourth is ours. For the more we are honored, the more we are in danger. It is to be feared lest the danger of error turn anyone of you from the truth; it is to be feared lest each one's cupidity conquer him, and he choose to follow it, rather than from its difficulties to cry out to the Lord; it is to be feared lest to each one of you the word of God grow less savory, and he die of distaste: but the temptation of governing, the temptation of dangers in ruling the Church, touches us especially. But how will you too be strangers to it, if the whole ship is in danger? Which I said for this reason, lest in this fourth temptation, as if our own (where it is needful that you not cease from prayers, for you are the first to be shipwrecked), you be less anxious, and pray less for us. For what, brothers, because you do not sit at the same helm, do you not sail in the same ship?

**8.** After these four temptations, four cries, four deliverances, four confessions of the Lord's compassions, generally in this psalm the Church herself is consequently commended; that you may most evidently know of which the Psalm was speaking from the beginning. But she is commended in such a way that the grace of God is preached to us in all things, who *resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^7]: because for this reason he came, *that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind*[^8]; because *every valley will be filled, and every mountain and hill brought low*[^9]. This being commended, something is said which may also be understood of the heretics, by whom, as it were by civil wars, the Church is shaken; and the Psalm is concluded, which I have now expounded, more briefly perhaps than you thought. For so far do I think I have expounded this whole somewhat lengthy psalm, that you now expect from me not the office of an expositor, but almost of a reader, if you hold what I said. For I think they are set before your eyes; but, that they may be better commended, let them be briefly repeated. The first temptation of error and hunger for the word; the second of difficulty in conquering desires; the third of weariness and distaste; the fourth of storm and dangers in governing the Churches: and in all these the cries, and the deliverances, and the confessions of the compassions of God. At the end is made the commending of the Church herself, who has both been made safe through the grace of our God, not through her own merit: and the affliction of the enemies is commemorated because of pride; with whom extinguished, the Church was raised up: and because of certain treacheries of diminution from the heretics, and as it were of domestic losses, and from these the divine benefits around the Church; and the conclusion of the Psalm. Let us now read rather than discuss.

**9.** [vv. 2-9.] *Let those say it who were redeemed by the Lord, whom he redeemed from the hand of the enemies; from the regions he gathered them, from the east and the west, and the north and the sea.* Let the Christians themselves, then, say it, called together from the whole world. *They wandered in the wilderness, in the dryness, they did not find the way of the city of habitation.* We have heard of a wretched wandering: what of the want? *Hungering and thirsting, their soul failed in them.* But why did it fail? for what good? For God is not cruel; but he commends himself, which is expedient for us, that, when we fail, he may be asked, and, by helping, be loved. And therefore after this error and hunger and thirst: *And they cried to the Lord, when they were troubled; and he rescued them from their necessities.* And what did he bestow on them, because they were wandering? *And he led them into the right way.* They did not find the way of the city of habitation, they burned with hunger and thirst, and failed: *And he led them into the right way, that they might go to the city of habitation.* How he relieved their hunger and thirst, he does not yet say, but await this too. *Let them confess to the Lord his compassions, and his wonders to the sons of men.* Say it, you experienced ones, to the inexperienced, now set in the way, now directed to find the city, now at last freed from hunger and thirst: *For he satisfied the empty soul, and filled the hungering soul with good things.*

**10.** [vv. 10-17.] Live well, then; you are now set in the way, you have now heard what you ought to do, what to hope. What else takes you up, since you try and are overcome? *Sitting in darkness and in the shadow of death, fettered in beggary and iron.* Whence this, except that you were attributing to yourself, because you did not recognize the grace of God, because you reproved the counsel of the Lord concerning you? For see what he adds: *Because they embittered the eloquences of the Lord;* through pride, not knowing the righteousness of the Lord, and wishing to establish their own. *And they exacerbated the counsel of the Most High. And their heart was humbled in labors.* And now fight against desire. With God ceasing from his help, you can labor, you cannot conquer. And when you have been pressed by your own bad habit, your heart will be humbled in labors; that now, your heart humbled, you may learn to cry, *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* *Their heart was humbled*, then, *in labors; they were made weak, and there was no one to help them.* What, then, remains, except why it was done? *For if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would be wholly from the Law: but Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe. But the Law entered in, that the offense might abound*[^10]. You received the word, you received the precept, and you do not cease to do what you were doing badly; and, the precept received, you increase sins through transgression. O proud one, if you did not know yourself, at least learn yourself humbled: you will cry out, you will be freed from necessity; freed, you will confess the compassions of the Lord. *And they cried to the Lord, when they were troubled, and from their necessities he made them safe.* They were freed from the second temptation, there remains that of weariness and distaste. But first see what he bestowed on them, freed. "And he led them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and broke their bonds. Let them confess to the Lord his compassions, and his wonders to the sons of men." Why? what difficulties did he conquer? "Because he crushed the gates of bronze, and broke the bars of iron. He took them up from the way of their iniquity: for because of their injustices they were humbled." Because they attributed it to themselves, not to God: because they established their own righteousness, not knowing the righteousness of God, they were humbled. They found that they could not without the help of him, on whose strength alone they were presuming.

**11.** [vv. 18-22.] But what other kind remains? *Their soul abominated every food.* Now they suffer distaste, languish with distaste, are in danger from distaste: unless perhaps you suppose that they could be killed by hunger, and cannot be by distaste. See what follows, when he had said, *Their soul abominated every food;* lest you should suppose them as it were secure from fullness, and not rather see them dying of distaste: *And they drew near*, he says, *even to the gates of death.* What, then, remains? That you should not attribute to yourself the fact that the word of God delights you, nor be puffed up by any arrogance from this, and, greedy for food, proudly leap upon those who are in danger from distaste. Understand that this too was bestowed on you, that it is not from yourself to yourself. *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^11] Understanding this, then, and in danger from this vice and languor, do what follows: *And they cried out to the Lord, when they were troubled, and from their necessities he freed them.* And because it was a languor not to be delighted: *He sent his word, and healed them.* See what evil distaste has; see from what he frees, to whom the one in distaste cries. *He sent his word, and healed them: and rescued them.* From what? Not from error, not from hunger, not from the difficulty of conquering sins, but *from their corruption.* It is a certain corruption of the mind, to feel distaste at what is sweet. Therefore for this benefit too, as for the others above: *Let them confess to the Lord his compassions, and his wonders to the sons of men. And let them sacrifice the sacrifice of praise.* For now, that he may be praised, the Lord is sweet. *And let them tell his works in exultation*: not with weariness, not with grief, not with anxiety, not with distaste, but *in exultation.*

**12.** [vv. 23-31.] That fourth remains, in which we are all in danger. For we are all in the ship: some work, some are carried; yet together all are both in danger in the tempest, and saved in the harbor. For after all these things there follows: *Those who go down to the sea in ships, doing work in many waters*: that is, among many peoples. For that waters are often set for peoples, the Apocalypse of John is witness, where John asking what those waters were, it was answered him: *They are peoples*[^12]. Those, then, who do work in many waters, *they saw the works of the Lord, and his wonders in the deep.* For what is deeper than human hearts? Thence for the most part winds burst out, the storms of seditions and dissensions disturb the ship. And what is done in these? God, wishing that both those who govern and those who are carried should cry out to him: *He spoke, and the spirit of the storm stood.* What is it, *it stood?* It remained, it endured: still it disturbs, long it tosses, it rages, and does not pass. *For he spoke, and the spirit of the storm stood.* And what did this spirit of the storm do? *And its waves were lifted up. They ascend even to the heavens,* by hearing: *they descend even to the deeps,* by fearing. *They ascend even to the heavens, they descend even to the deeps*: outside battles, inside fears. *Their soul wasted away in evils. They were troubled, and were moved, like a drunk man.* Those who sit at the helm, and who faithfully love the ship, perceive what I say: *They were troubled, and were moved like a drunk man.* Certainly when they speak, when they read, when they discourse, they appear wise: woe from the tempest! *And all*, he says, *their wisdom was swallowed up.* Sometimes all human counsels fail: wherever each one turns himself, the waves roar, the tempest rages, the arms fail; where the prow may be driven, to what wave the side may be bared, whither the ship, driven, may be let go, by what rocks it may be restrained lest it perish, is utterly not seen by the governors. And what remains, except what follows? *And they cried out to the Lord, when they were troubled, and from their necessities he led them out. And he commanded the storm, and it stood into a breeze.* It did not stand into a tempest, but *into a breeze. And its waves were silent.* Hear about this matter the voice of a certain governor endangered, humbled, freed: *I do not wish*, he says, *you to be ignorant, brothers, of our affliction, which happened in Asia, that we were burdened beyond strength, and beyond measure,* (I see all his wisdom swallowed up) *so that we were weary*, he says, *even of living.* And what? would he thus desert those failing? or did they not fail for this reason, that he might find glory among them? Finally, what follows? *But we ourselves had in ourselves the answer of death, that we should not be trusting in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead*[^13]. *And he commanded the storm, and it stood into a breeze.* Now those had in themselves of themselves the answer of death, whose whole wisdom had been swallowed up. *And its waves were silent. And they rejoiced, because they were silent: and he led them into the harbor of their will. Let them confess to the Lord his compassions.* Everywhere absolutely, everywhere *let them confess to the Lord,* not our merits, not our strength, not our wisdom, but *his compassions.* Let him be loved in all our deliverance, who was called upon in all our tribulation. *Let them confess to the Lord his compassions, and his wonders to the sons of men.*

**13.** [vv. 32-38.] And see whence he says it, whence he prefaced all these things, whence he enumerated all these, where these things are done. *And let the peoples exalt him in the Church, and in the seat of the elders let them praise him. And let them exalt him,* this is, *let them praise him*: *and let them praise him,* this is, *let them exalt him.* Let the peoples and the elders, the merchants and the governors, exalt, praise. For what did he do in this Church? what did he establish? whence did he rescue her? what did he bestow on her? How he resisted the proud, gave grace to the humble: the proud, namely, first the people of the Jews, arrogant and exalting themselves over the lineage of Abraham, and that to that nation were entrusted the eloquences of God. These things did not avail them to health, but to exaltation of heart, to swelling rather than to greatness. What, then, did God do, resisting the proud, giving grace to the humble; cutting off the natural branches because of pride, grafting in the wild olive because of humility? what did God do? Hear these two things: God, first, how he resists the proud, then, how he gives grace to the humble. *He set the rivers into a desert.* Waters ran there, prophecies ran: seek now among the Jews a prophet, you find none. *For he set the rivers into a desert, and the issues of waters into thirst. He set the rivers into a desert.* Let them say: *Now there is no prophet, and he will know us no more*[^14]. *He set the rivers into a desert, and the issues of waters into thirst, a fruitful land into salt-flats.* You seek there the faith of Christ, you find none; you seek a prophet, you find none; you seek a priest, you find none; you seek a sacrifice, you find none; you seek a temple, you find none. Why this? Because: *He set the rivers into a desert, and the issues of waters into thirst; a fruitful land into salt-flats.* Whence, by what desert? *From the malice of those who dwell in it.* Behold how he resists the proud: hear how he gives grace to the humble. *He set the desert into pools of waters, and the land without water into issues of waters. And he made the hungering to dwell there.* Since to him it was said: *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek*[^15]. For you seek a sacrifice among the Jews; you have none according to the order of Aaron, because he set the rivers into a desert: you seek one according to the order of Melchizedek; among them you do not find it, but throughout the whole world it is celebrated in the Church. *From the rising of the sun to its setting the name of the Lord is praised*[^16]. And God says to those whose rivers he set into a desert: *I have no will in you, says the Lord, nor will I accept a sacrifice from your hands, for from the rising of the sun to its setting, a clean sacrifice is offered to my name*[^17]. Where all the sacrifices were unclean, when they were a desert, when they were squalid, when all the nations were salt-flats; there now are springs, there now rivers, there now pools of waters, and issues of waters. Therefore he resisted the proud, but gave grace to the humble. *And he made the hungering to dwell there*: because: *The poor will eat, and be satisfied*[^18]. *And they established a city of habitation*: meanwhile of habitation in hope; since: *He who hears me*, he says, *will dwell in hope*[^19]. *And they established a city of habitation. And they sowed fields, and planted vineyards, and made the fruit of grain*: in which that laborer rejoices who says, *Not because I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit*[^20]. *And he blessed them, and they were multiplied exceedingly, and their cattle were not diminished.* This stands. *For the firm foundation of God stands, because the Lord knows who are his*[^21]. Cattle and beasts are called those in the Church who walk simply, but are useful; not much learned, but full of faith. Therefore both the spiritual and the carnal *he blessed, and they were multiplied exceedingly, and their cattle were not diminished.*

**14.** [vv. 39-42.] *And they were made few, and were vexed.* Whence this, from outside? Rather from within. For that they might be made few, *they went out from us, but they were not of us*[^22]. But he speaks as it were of these, of whom they were speaking before, that they may be distinguished with understanding; because he speaks as it were of the same, on account of the common Sacraments. For they pertain to the people of God, even if not through virtue, certainly through the appearance of piety; for of them we have heard the Apostle: *In the last times grievous times will be at hand; for men will be lovers of themselves*[^23]. The first evil, *lovers of themselves;* certainly pleasing themselves. Would that they displeased themselves, and pleased God; would that they cried out in difficulties, and were freed from necessities. But presuming much on themselves, *they were made few.* It is manifest, brothers; all who divide themselves from unity are made few. For there are many, but in unity, while they are not separated from unity: for when the multitude of unity begins not to pertain to them, in heresy and in schism they are few. *And they were made few, and were vexed by the tribulation of evils and grief. Contempt was poured out upon the princes.* For they were reprobated by the Church of God: and rather, because they wished to be princes, therefore they were scorned, and were made salt that has lost its savor cast out; therefore it is trodden underfoot by men. *Contempt was poured out upon the princes. And he seduced them in the trackless place, and not in the way.* Those above in the way, those directed to the city, finally led, not seduced: but these seduced in the trackless place. What is it, *he seduced them? God handed them over to the desires of their heart*[^24]. For this is, *he seduced,* he gave them over to themselves. For if you ask properly, they seduce themselves. *For he who supposes himself to be something, when he is nothing, seduces himself*[^25]. What is it, then, *he seduced them?* He let them go. *In the trackless place, and not in the way*: for how are men in the way who hold a part, and leave the whole? how in the way? What, then, is the way, or where is the way recognized? *May God*, he says, *have mercy on us, and bless us; may he enlighten his countenance upon us, that we may know on earth your way*[^26]. On what earth? *Among all the nations your salvation.* Certainly such men, that they may be diminished, that they may be made few, go out from here; from the multitude of unity all went out, as I commemorated a little before was said of them: *They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us.* But if perhaps they are ours in the hidden foreknowledge of God, it is necessary that they return. How many not ours as it were within, and how many ours as it were outside? *The Lord knows who are his.* And those not ours who are within, when they have found occasions, go out; and those ours who are outside, when they have found occasions, return. Take, then, that which God knows, according to this *he seduced them in the trackless place, and not in the way.* And what did he do with them? What I had begun to say, which you should attend to. He could have put up with them within always, but we would not profit from them: but when they are separated, and disquiet us by malicious questions, there is set before us from them both a zeal for inquiry, and an example of fear. Each one trembles, when he sees another to have gone out, as if from his going-out it were said to him: *Wherefore let him who supposes himself to stand see that he does not fall*[^27]. They are useful, then, because they go out: for if they were within, and were so bad, nothing would be profited from them. What was said of them in a certain psalm? *The congregation of bulls,* that is, of the stiff-necked and the proud; *the congregation of bulls among the cows of the peoples. Cows* he calls the seducible souls, which easily consent to the seducing bulls. But why this? *That those who are proved by silver may be shut out*[^28]. What is it, *that they may be shut out?* That they may appear, that they may stand out, those who are proved in the eloquence of God. For when the heretics are answered out of necessity, the Catholics are built up out of usefulness. This sentence Paul plainly expressed: *For there must be*, he says, *heresies, that those who are proved may be made manifest among you*[^29]. There must be seducing bulls too, *that those who are proved by silver* may be manifest, that is, *shut out.* What is it, *proved by silver? The eloquences of the Lord, chaste eloquences; silver tried by fire for the earth, purged sevenfold*[^30]. Whoever are proved in this silver, that is, in the eloquence of the Lord, cannot fully bring out this silver, except disquieted by the questions of the heretics. And here notice that it was not omitted: behold *contempt was poured out upon the princes,* upon those bulls. Why were they scorned? Announcing something else. What were they scorned as? Anathematized. *For whoever announces to you anything other than what you have received, let him be anathema.* What is so scorned as salt that has lost its savor, which, cast out, is trodden underfoot? And see whether they are not princes; hear Paul himself: *Even if we, or an angel from heaven, evangelize to you anything other than what you have received, let him be anathema*[^31]. They are princes, they are learned, they are great, they are precious stones. What still will you say; are they perhaps angels? And yet, *even if an angel from heaven announce to you anything other than what you have received, let him be anathema*: because the devil himself too is an angel fallen from heaven. *Contempt was poured out*, then, *upon the princes. And he helped the poor man from beggary.* What is this, brothers: The princes were scorned, and the poor man was helped? The proud were cast away, and the humble was instructed. This he did, and by doing this *he helped the poor man from beggary.* That man is a beggar, attributing nothing to himself, awaiting all from the mercy of God; before the Lord's door he cries daily, knocking that it may be opened to him, naked and trembling that he may be clothed, casting his eyes to the ground, and beating his breast. This beggar, this poor man, this humble man God helped greatly, even from that very separation of the heretics; because they were made few, and vexed, and seduced in the trackless place and not in the way. Finally, what afterward, from those diminished, seduced, made few, vexed, in the helped poor man what comes to be? *And he set the families like sheep.* As if you understood one poor man and one beggar, of whom he said, *And he helped the poor man from beggary*: this poor man is many families, this poor man is many peoples; many Churches one Church, one people, one family, one sheep. *And he set the families like sheep.* Great are these mysteries, great these sacraments, how deep, full of mysteries; how sweetly found, because long hidden. Therefore: *The upright will see, and rejoice; and all iniquity will stop its mouth.* That iniquity chattering against unity, and forcing the truth to be made manifest, convicted *will stop its mouth.*

**15.** [v. 43.] *Who is wise? and he will keep these things, and will understand the compassions of the Lord.* See with what end he closed: *Who is wise? and he will keep these things.* And what will the wise man keep? That is, if he is poor, he keeps it; if he is not rich, that is, if he is not proud, not puffed up, he keeps these things. For why does he keep these things? Because *he will understand the compassions of the Lord*: not his own merits, not his own strength, not his own power; but *the compassions of the Lord,* who led the erring and the needy into the way and fed him; who loosed and freed the one fighting against the difficulty of sins, and bound by the bonds of habit; who restored the one in distaste at the word of God, and almost dying of a certain weariness, by sending the medicine of his word; who led the one in danger amid shipwrecks and stormy perils, the sea calmed, to the harbor: who finally set him in that people, where he gives grace to the humble, not in that where he resists the proud; and made him his own, that, remaining within, he might be multiplied, not that, going out, he might be diminished. This the upright see, and rejoice. *All iniquity*, then, *will stop its mouth,* and he who is *wise will keep these things.* Whence will he keep them? Through humility, by understanding the compassions of the Lord; because everywhere it was said: *Let them confess to the Lord his compassions, and his wonders to the sons of men.*
