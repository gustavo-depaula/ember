# Exposition of Psalm 34 (2)

**1.** Let us turn our minds to the rest of the psalm, and beseech our Lord and God both for soundness of understanding and for the fruit of acting well. How far the discussion went yesterday, I believe your Charity remembers: from that very place let us take our beginning today. For we understand here the voice of Christ; that is, the voice of the head and of the body of Christ. When you hear "Christ," do not separate the bridegroom from the bride, and understand that great sacrament: *They shall be two in one flesh*[^1]. If two in one flesh, why not also in one voice? For the head did not endure temptations here while the body does not endure them; or rather, was there any cause for the head to suffer, except to furnish an example to the body? For the Lord suffered by will, we by necessity; he by compassion, we by condition. Accordingly, his voluntary passion is our necessary consolation; so that when we perhaps endure such things, we may look upon our head, that, admonished by his example, we may say to ourselves: If he, what of us? And as he, so we too. For however much the enemy may have raged, he could come only as far as the death of the body: which body even itself he could not extinguish in the Lord, because on the third day he rose again. What was done in him on the third day, this is done in our body at the end of the world. The hope of our resurrection is deferred; is it taken away? Let us recognize here, then, the voices of Christ, dearly beloved, and let us separate them from the voices of the ungodly. For they are the voices of the body suffering persecution and straits and temptations in this world. But since many here suffer both for their sins and for their crimes, the cause, not the punishment, must be discerned with great vigilance. For a criminal can have a punishment like a martyr's, yet an unlike cause. There were three on the cross: one the Savior, another to be saved, another to be damned: the punishment of all equal, but the cause unequal.

**2.** [vv. 11-12] Let our head, then, say, *Unjust witnesses rising up asked me of things I did not know*[^2]. But let us say to our head: Lord, what did you not know? Was there indeed anything you did not know? Did you not know the hearts of those who questioned you? Had you not foreseen their treacheries? Had you not knowingly given yourself into their hands? Had you not come to suffer from them? What, then, did you not know? He did not know sin: and he did not know this sin, not as though by not judging it, but by not committing it. There are such ways of speaking even in daily use, when you say of someone, "He does not know how to stand," that is, because he does not stand; and, "He does not know how to do good," because he does not do good. "He does not know how to do evil," because he does not do evil. What is foreign to the deed is foreign to the conscience; what is foreign to the conscience seems foreign even to knowledge. So God is said not to know, just as art does not know faults; and yet through art the things known are judged. This, then, our head answers us who question, from the truth of his own Gospel, when we have said, Lord, what did you not know? What could you have been asked which you did not know? He answers: I did not know iniquities, I was asked about iniquities. You have it in the Gospel, if you do not believe that I am ignorant of iniquities, that I am ignorant even of the iniquitous themselves, to whom I shall say at the end, *I do not know you; depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^3]. Did he not know those whom he was condemning? Or can he justly condemn, unless he is a good knower? And yet the good knower did not lie in saying, *I do not know you*: that is, you are not fitted to my body, you do not cleave to my rules; you are faults; but I am the art itself, which has no fault, and in which one learns only not to commit a fault. *Unjust witnesses rising up asked me of things I did not know.* What did Christ so not know as to blaspheme? On this he was questioned by his persecutors, and because he spoke the truth, he was judged to have blasphemed. But by whom? Of whom there follows: *They repaid me evil for good, and barrenness to my soul*[^4]. I brought fruitfulness, they repaid barrenness; I life, they death; I honor, they reproaches; I medicine, they wounds; and in all these things which they repaid, there was indeed barrenness. This barrenness he cursed in the tree, where, seeking fruit, he found none. There were leaves, and there was no fruit: there were words, and there were no deeds. See in words abundance, and in deeds barrenness: you who preach against stealing, steal; you who say not to commit adultery, commit adultery[^5]. Such were those who asked Christ of things he did not know.

**3.** [v. 13] *But as for me, when they were troublesome to me, I put on sackcloth, and humbled my soul in fasting; and my prayer shall be turned into my own bosom*[^6]. We are indeed taught, brothers, that we belong to the body of Christ, that we are members of Christ: and we are admonished, in all our tribulation, not to think how we may answer our enemies, but how by praying we may render God favorable, and above all that we not be conquered by temptation; and then, that even those who persecute us may be converted to the soundness of justice. There is no greater, no better business in tribulation than to withdraw from that noise which is without, and to go into the inner sanctuaries of the mind; there to call upon God, where no one sees the one groaning and being aided; to shut the door of that chamber against every trouble brought from without, to humble oneself in confession of sin, to magnify and praise God, both as he corrects and as he consoles: this in every way is to be held to. Yet we have said this in the body, that is, in us: but in our Lord Jesus Christ what such do we recognize? With the Gospel examined and most diligently searched, we do not find that the Lord, in any passion and tribulation of his, put on sackcloth. We read indeed that he fasted after he was baptized: but we have heard of no sackcloth there, we have read of none; and he fasted not yet when the Jews were persecuting, but when the devil was tempting. I do not say that the Lord fasted at the time when they were asking him of things he did not know, and when they were repaying evil for good, by harassing, persecuting, seizing, scourging, wounding, killing: but yet in all these things, brothers, if with pious curiosity we lift the veil a little, and search the inner parts of this Scripture with the intent eye of the heart, we find that the Lord did this too. He calls his flesh, perhaps, the sackcloth of its mortality. Why sackcloth? Because of the likeness of the flesh of sin. For the Apostle says, *God sent his Son in the likeness of the flesh of sin, that from sin he might condemn sin in the flesh*[^7]: that is, he clothed his Son in sackcloth, that from the sackcloth he might condemn the goats. Not because there was sin—I do not say in the Word of God, but not even, I say, in that holy soul and mind of the man whom the Word of God and Wisdom had fitted to himself for the unity of person; nor was there in that very body any sin—but there was the likeness of the flesh of sin in the Lord; because death is only from sin, and that body indeed was mortal. For unless it were mortal, it would not die; if it did not die, it would not rise again; if it did not rise again, it would not show us an example of eternal life. So, then, that death is called sin, which was made for sin, just as we say "the Greek tongue," "the Latin tongue," not the very member of flesh, but what is done through the member of flesh. For the tongue among our members is one of the rest, like the eyes, nose, ears, and the others: but the Greek tongue is Greek words; not because the words are the tongue, but because the words are through the tongue. You say of someone, "I recognized his face," speaking of a member of the body: and you also say, "I recognized the hand of the absent one," not the hand on the body, but the writing made through the hand which was on the body. So, then, the sin of the Lord, which was made from sin, because from it he took flesh, from that very mass which had earned death from sin. For—to say it more quickly—Mary from Adam was mortal because of sin, Adam died because of sin, and the flesh of the Lord from Mary died for the blotting out of sins. With this sackcloth the Lord clothed himself: and therefore he was not recognized, because under the sackcloth he lay hidden. *When they,* he says, *were troublesome to me, I put on sackcloth*: that is, they raged, I lay hidden. For if he were unwilling to lie hidden, he could not even die, since at one point of time he put forth a certain drop of his power—if even a drop it is to be called—when they wished to seize him, and at his one question, *Whom do you seek?* they all went backward and fell[^8]. He would not have humbled so great a power in his passion, unless he lay hidden under the sackcloth.

**4.** So, then, *I put on sackcloth, and humbled my soul in fasting.* Again, if we have understood the sackcloth, how do we understand the fasting? Did Christ wish to eat, when he sought fruit on the tree, and would have eaten if he had found it? Did Christ wish to drink, when he said to the Samaritan woman, *Give me to drink*[^9], when he said on the cross, *I thirst*[^10]? What did Christ hunger for, what did he thirst for, but our good works? For in those who were crucifying and persecuting, because he had found no good works, he was fasting: for they repaid barrenness to his soul. For what a fast his was, who scarcely found one robber to taste on the cross! For the apostles had fled, and had hidden themselves in the crowd. And that Peter who had promised that he would persevere even to the death of the Lord had now three times denied, had now wept, and was still hiding in the crowd, was still afraid lest he be recognized. Finally, when they saw him dead, they all despaired even of salvation itself: whom, despairing, he found after the resurrection, and speaking with them found them grieving and mourning, hoping nothing now. For some of them spoke with him thus, when he said, *What are you talking about among yourselves?* For they were speaking about him: *You alone,* they said, *are a stranger in Jerusalem, and have not known what our priests and rulers did concerning Jesus of Nazareth, who was mighty in deeds and words, how they crucified and killed him? But we were hoping that he was the one to redeem Israel*[^11]. The Lord would have remained in a great fast, had he not refreshed those whom he devoured. For he refreshed them, consoled them, confirmed them, and converted them into his body. So in this way too our Lord was in a fast.

**5.** *And my prayer,* he says, *shall be turned into my own bosom.* In this verse plainly there is a great bosom, and may the Lord grant that it become penetrable to us. For in the bosom a secret is recognized. And indeed, brothers, we too are well admonished to pray thus in our own bosom, where God sees, where God hears, where no human eye penetrates, where none sees but he who comes to aid; where Susanna prayed, and when her voice was not heard by men, yet it was heard by God. And we are well admonished of this: but in our Lord we ought to understand something more, because he too prayed. And indeed we do not recognize his sackcloth in the Gospel according to the letter. Nor his fast at the time of the passion according to the letter: and so we have expounded those things as said in allegory and likeness, as we could. But his prayer we have heard even from the cross: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?*[^12] But there too we were present. For when did the Father forsake him, from whom he never departed? We read also that Jesus prayed alone on the mountain, we read that he spent the night in prayer; even at the time of his very passion. *Into my own bosom,* then, *shall my prayer be turned.* I know not what better thing to understand about the Lord: meanwhile now what occurs—perhaps something better will occur afterward, either to me or to someone better—*into my own bosom shall my prayer be turned,* I understand this to be said because he had the Father in his bosom. For God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself[^13]. He had within himself the one to whom he prayed: he was not far from him, because he himself had said, *I am in the Father, and the Father is in me*[^14]. But because prayer belongs rather to the man himself—for according to what Christ is as Word, he does not pray, but hears, and does not seek to be aided, but with the Father aids all—what is *My prayer shall be turned into my own bosom,* but, in myself the humanity, in myself, intercedes the divinity?

**6.** [v. 14] *As a neighbor, as our brother, so I pleased; as one mourning and saddened, so was I humbled*[^15]. He looks to his body: now let us here see ourselves. When we rejoice in prayer, when our mind is made serene, not by the prosperity of the world, but by the light of truth: whoever feels this light knows what I say, and sees here, and recognizes what was said, *As a neighbor, as our brother, so I pleased.* For so does the soul then please God, not placed far off; *In him,* it says, *we are moved and have our being*[^16]: as a brother, as one near, as a friend. But if it is not such that it can so rejoice, so shine, so draw near, so cleave, and sees itself far from there, let it do what follows: *As one mourning and saddened, so was I humbled. As our brother, so I pleased,* he said, drawing near: *As one mourning and saddened, so was I humbled,* he said, removed and placed far off. For what does one mourn, but what he desires and does not have? And sometimes both happen in one man, that at one time he draws near, and at another is made far: he draws near by the light of truth, he is made far by the cloud of the flesh. For we do not, brothers, draw near to God, who is everywhere and is contained by no place, through places, nor are we removed from him through places. To draw near to him is to become like him; to withdraw from him is to become unlike him. When you see two things nearly alike, do you not say, "This is near to that"? And when unlike things are shown to you, although held in one place and often in one hand, do you not say, "This appearance is far from that"? You hold both, you join both, and you say, "This thing is far from that": not indeed in place, but in unlikeness. If, then, you wish to draw near, be like; if you do not wish to be like, you will go far off. But when you are like, rejoice; when unlike, groan: that groaning may rouse desire, or rather desire may rouse groaning, and that through groaning you may draw near, you who had begun to go far off. Did not Peter draw near, when he said, *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^17]? And again the same man was made far by saying, *Lord, far be it from you, this shall not be*[^18]. Finally, as to a neighbor, what did he say to the one drawing near? *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona*[^19]. As to one going far off and unlike, what did he say? *Get behind me, Satan*[^20]. To the one drawing near: *Flesh and blood did not reveal it to you,* he said, *but my Father who is in heaven*[^21]: his light flooded you, by his light you shine. But when, made far off, he contradicted the future passion of the Lord for our salvation, *You do not relish,* he said, *the things that are of God, but the things that are of men*[^22]. Rightly, setting both things together, a certain man in a psalm says, *I said in my ecstasy: I am cast away from before your eyes*[^23]. He would not say it in ecstasy unless he drew near: for ecstasy is an excess of the mind. He poured out his soul above himself, and drew near to God: and through a certain cloud, and the weight of the flesh, again cast down to earth, recalling where he had been, and seeing where he was, he said, *I am cast away from before your eyes.* Therefore, *As a neighbor, as our brother, so I pleased,* may he grant that it come to pass in us. But when it does not come to pass, let at least this come to pass: *As one mourning and saddened, so was I humbled.*

**7.** [v. 15] *And against me they rejoiced and gathered together,* into one. They glad, I sad. But just now we heard in the Gospel, *Blessed are those who mourn*[^24]. If blessed are those who mourn, wretched are those who laugh. *Against me they rejoiced and gathered together: scourges were gathered upon me, and they did not know it*[^25]. Since they asked me of things I did not know, they themselves too did not know whom they were questioning.

**8.** [v. 16] *They tempted me, and mocked me with mockery*[^26]. That is, they derided me, they insulted me: this to the head, this to the body. Attend, brothers, to the glory of the Church which now is; look back at its past reproaches, look back at how Christians were once put to flight on every side, and wherever found, mocked, beaten, killed, thrown to beasts, burned, and men rejoiced against them. What to the head, this also to the body. For as to the Lord on the cross, so to his body in all that persecution which has now taken place: nor do persecutions of them even now cease. Wherever they find a Christian, they are wont to insult, harass, deride, to call him dull, witless, of no heart, of no skill. Let them do whatever they wish, Christ is in heaven: let them do whatever they wish, he has honored his punishment, he has now fixed his cross on the foreheads of all: the ungodly is permitted to insult, but not permitted to rage; yet from what the tongue brings forth, it is understood what he bears in his heart. *They gnashed at me with their teeth*[^27].

**9.** [v. 17] *Lord, when will you look on? Restore my soul from their malice, my only one from the lions*[^28]. For to us it is slow, and from our person this is said: *When will you look on?* that is, when shall we see vengeance on those who insult us? When will the judge, worn out by weariness, hear that widow? Yet our judge defers our salvation not by weariness, but by love; by reason, not by want; not because he cannot even now come to aid, but that the number of us all may be fulfilled even to the end. And yet what do we say out of desire? *Lord, when will you look on? Restore my soul from their malice, my only one from the lions*: that is, my Church from the raging powers.

**10.** [v. 18] Finally, do you wish to know what that only one is? Read what follows: *I will confess to you, O Lord, in a great Church; among a weighty people I will praise you*[^29]. Plainly *in a great Church I will confess to you, among a weighty people I will praise you.* For confession is made in every multitude, but not in all is God praised: the whole multitude hears our confession, but not in all the multitude is there praise of God. For in this whole multitude, that is, in the Church which is spread throughout the whole world, there is chaff and grain: the chaff flies away, the grain remains; therefore *among a weighty people I will praise you.* Among the weighty people, whom the wind of temptation does not carry off, among these God is praised. For in the chaff he is always blasphemed. When our chaff is regarded, what is said? "Behold how Christians live, behold what Christians do"; and there comes to pass what is written, *For my name is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you*[^30]. Wickedly, enviously, you inspect the threshing-floor, you who are wholly in the chaff; the grains do not easily meet you: seek, and you will find a weighty people, among whom you may praise the Lord. Do you wish to find it? Be such yourself. For if you are not such, it is hard that not all should seem to you such as you are: *And comparing,* says the Apostle, *themselves with themselves*[^31], they do not understand. *Among a weighty people I will praise you.*

**11.** [vv. 19-21] *Let them not insult me who are my adversaries unjustly*[^32]: for they insult me over my chaff. *Who hate me without cause*: that is, those whom I in no way harmed. *And wink with their eyes*: that is, the hypocrites, the dissemblers. *For to me indeed they spoke peaceful things*[^33]. What is *winking with their eyes*? Declaring with the face what they do not bear in the heart. And who are they who *wink with their eyes*? *For to me indeed they spoke peaceful things; and in their anger they thought deceitfully. And they opened wide their mouth against me*[^34]. First winking with their eyes, those lions seeking to seize and devour, first fawning, they spoke peaceful things, and in their anger they thought deceitfully. What peaceful things did they speak? *Master, we know that you accept no one's person, and teach the way of God in truth: Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or is it not lawful?*[^35] To me indeed they spoke peaceful things. What, then? Did you not recognize them, and did they deceive you by winking with their eyes? Rather, he recognized them; therefore he said, *Why do you tempt me, hypocrites?*[^36] Afterward they opened wide their mouth against me, crying, *Crucify, crucify!*[^37] *They said: Aha, aha, our eyes have seen*[^38]. This now by way of insult: *Aha, aha, prophesy to us, Christ*[^39]. As their peace was feigned, when they tempted him about the coin, so now their praise is insulting. *They said: Aha, aha, our eyes have seen*: that is, your deeds, your wonders. This is the Christ. *If he is the Christ, let him come down from the cross, and we believe him. He saved others, he cannot save himself*[^40]. *Our eyes have seen.* This is the whole of what he boasted, that he called himself the Son of God. But the Lord, suffering, clung to the cross: he had not lost his power, but was showing his wisdom. For what was great for him to come down from the cross, who afterward could rise from the tomb? But he would have seemed to yield to those insulting him: and this was fitting, that, rising, he should show himself to his own, and not to them, in a great sacrament; because his resurrection signified new life, and new life is known to friends, not to enemies.

**12.** [v. 22] *You have seen, O Lord; do not be silent*[^41]. What is *do not be silent*? Judge. For of judgment it is said in a certain place, *I have kept silence; shall I always keep silence?*[^42] And of the deferral of judgment it is said to the sinner, *These things you did, and I kept silence; you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you*[^43]. How is he silent who speaks through the prophets, who speaks with his own mouth in the Gospel, who speaks through the evangelists, who speaks through us when we speak the truth? What, then? He is silent from judgment, not from precept, not from teaching. But this judgment of his the prophet in a certain way invokes, and foretells: *You have seen, O Lord; do not keep silence*: that is, because you will not be silent, because it is necessary that you judge. *Lord, do not depart from me.* Until judgment comes, do not depart from me, as you promised: *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the world*[^44].

**13.** [v. 23] *Rise up, O Lord, and attend to my judgment*[^45]. To what judgment? Because you have been afflicted, because you have been tortured by toils and griefs? Do not many wicked also suffer these things? To what judgment? Are you just because you suffer these things? No: but what? *To my judgment.* How does it follow? *Attend to my judgment, my God and my Lord, to my cause.* Not to my punishment, but to my cause: not to that which the robber has in common with me, but to that of which *blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of justice*[^46]. For this cause is distinguished. For the punishment is alike for the good and the wicked. And so it is not the punishment that makes martyrs, but the cause. For if the punishment made martyrs, all the mines would be full of martyrs, all chains would drag martyrs, all who are struck with the sword would be crowned. Therefore let the cause be discerned. Let no one say: Because I suffer, I am just. Because he who first suffered, suffered for justice: therefore he added a great exception: *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of justice.* For many who have a good cause inflict persecution; and those who have a bad cause suffer persecution. For if persecution could not be inflicted well, it would not be said in the psalm, *The one who secretly slandered his neighbor, him I persecuted*[^47]. Then, brothers, does not a good and just father persecute a dissolute son? He persecutes his vices, not him; not what he begot, but what the son added. The physician, indeed, who is brought for healing, is he not often armed with iron? But against the wound, not against the man. He cuts, that he may heal: and yet when he cuts the sick man, the man grieves, cries out, resists, and if perhaps he has lost his mind through fever, he even strikes the physician; nor yet does the physician desist from the patient's health; he does what he knows, he disregards the one cursing and reviling. Are not all the lethargic roused, lest they be pressed by heavy sleep into death? And this they suffer from their sons, whom they begot most dear; and a son is not dear unless he is troublesome to a sleeping father. The lethargic are roused, the frenzied are bound: but yet both are loved. Let no one, then, say: I suffer persecution: let him not winnow the punishment, but prove the cause; lest, if he do not prove the cause, he be numbered with the wicked. Therefore how vigilantly, how excellently he here commended it: *Lord, attend to my judgment,* not to my punishments: *my God and my Lord, to my cause.*

**14.** [vv. 24-26] *Judge me, O Lord, according to my justice*[^48]. That is, according to my cause. Not according to my punishment, but *according to my justice, O Lord my God;* that is, according to this judge me.

**15.** *And let them not insult me*: my enemies. *Let them not say in their heart: Aha, aha, to our soul*[^49]; that is: We have done what we could, we killed, we took away. *Let them not say*: Show that they did nothing. *Let them not say: We have swallowed him up.* Whence those martyrs say, *If the Lord had not been with us, perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive*[^50]. What is *they would have swallowed us up*? They would have transferred us into their body. For you swallow this, what you transfer into your body. The world wishes to swallow you: you, swallow the world, transfer it into your body, slay and eat. Which was said to Peter, *Slay and eat*[^51]: kill in them what they are, make them what you are. But they, on the contrary, if they have persuaded you to ungodliness, you are swallowed up by them. Not when they persecute you are you swallowed up by them; but when they persuade you of what they are. *Nor let them say: We have swallowed him up.* You, swallow up the body of the pagans. Why the body of the pagans? It wishes to swallow you; do to it what it wishes to do to you. Therefore perhaps that calf was ground up and put into water, and given to drink, that the body of the ungodly might be swallowed up by Israel. *Let them be ashamed and put to shame together who rejoice at my evils; let them be clothed with confusion and shame*[^52]: so that we may swallow them up, ashamed and confounded. *Who speak malice against me*: let them be ashamed, let them be confounded.

**16.** [vv. 27-28] What of you now, head with the members? *Let them exult and be glad who wish my justice*[^53]: those who have clung to my body. *And let them always say: Let the Lord be magnified, who wish the peace of his servant. And my tongue shall meditate on your justice, all the day your praise*[^54]. And whose tongue endures to meditate all day on the praise of God? Behold, just now the sermon has become a little longer, and you grow weary. Who endures to praise God all day? I suggest a remedy, by which you may praise God all day, if you wish. Whatever you do, do it well, and you have praised God. When you sing a hymn, you praise God: what does your tongue do, except that your conscience too praise? You have ceased from singing the hymn, you withdraw to be refreshed: do not get drunk, and you have praised God. You withdraw to sleep: do not rise to do evil, and you have praised God. You do business: do not commit fraud, and you have praised God. You till a field: do not stir up a lawsuit, and you have praised God. In the innocence of your works prepare yourself to praise God all day.
