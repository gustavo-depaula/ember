# Exposition of Psalm 97

**1.** [v. 1.] *Sing to the Lord a new song.* The new man knows it, the old man does not know it. The old man is the old life, and the new man the new life: the old life is drawn from Adam, the new life is formed in Christ. But it is said in this psalm to the whole world that it should sing a new song. For more openly elsewhere it is said thus: *Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^1]; that those who cut themselves off from the communion of the whole world may understand that they cannot sing the new song, because the new song is sung in the whole, not in a part. And here attend and see this said. And when it is said to the whole world that it should sing a new song, this is understood, that peace sings the new song. *Sing to the Lord a new song; for the Lord has done wonderful things.* What wonderful things? Behold just now the Gospel was read, and we heard the wonderful things of the Lord. The only son of his mother, who was a widow, was being carried out dead: the Lord, having mercy, made them stand; they set him down; and he said: *Young man, I say to you, arise*[^2]. And that dead man sat up, and began to speak, and he gave him back to his mother. Behold the Lord did wonderful things; but much greater wonderful things are these, that he raised the whole world from everlasting death, than that he raised again the only son of a widow mother. *Sing,* then, *to the Lord a new song; for the Lord has done wonderful things.* What wonderful things? Hear: *His right hand has saved for him, and his holy arm.* What is the holy arm of the Lord? Our Lord Jesus Christ. Hear Isaiah: *Who has believed our report? and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed*[^3]? The holy arm, then, and his right hand, is the same one. Therefore our Lord Jesus Christ is the arm of God, and the right hand of God: therefore: *It has saved for him.* It was not only said: His right hand has saved the world; but: *It has saved for him.* For many are saved for themselves, not for him. Behold how many desire that bodily health, and receive it from him; by him they are healed, and are not healed for him? How are they healed by him, and not healed for him? Health received, they grow wanton: those who when sick were chaste, healed become adulterers; those who, when they were sick, harmed no one, their strength regained, invade and oppress the innocent: they have been healed, but not for him. Who is he who is healed for him? He who is healed within. Who is he who is healed within? He who believes in him, that, when he has been healed within, reformed into a new man, afterward he may receive at the end even this which languishes for a time, this mortal flesh, its own most perfect health. Let us be healed, then, for him. But that we may be healed for him, let us believe in his right hand; because *his right hand has saved for him, and his holy arm.*

**2.** [v. 2.] *The Lord has made known his salvation.* That very right hand, that very arm, that very salvation, is our Lord Jesus Christ, of whom it was said: *And all flesh shall see the salvation of God*[^4]: of whom also that Simeon who received the infant in his hands said: *Now you dismiss, Lord, your servant in peace; for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^5]. *The Lord has made known his salvation.* To whom did he make it known? to a part, or to the whole? Not to some part. Let no one deceive, let no one beguile, let no one say: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there*[^6]: he who says: *Behold, here is, behold, there,* shows parts. To whom *has the Lord made known his salvation?* Hear what follows: *Before the sight of the nations he has revealed his righteousness.* The right hand of God, the arm of God, the salvation of God, and the righteousness of God, is our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

**3.** [v. 3.] *He was mindful of his mercy to Jacob, and of his truth to the house of Israel.* What is: *He was mindful of mercy and of truth?* That he might promise, he had mercy; because he promised and exhibited mercy, truth followed: mercy sent the promise before, the promise rendered truth. *He was mindful of his mercy to Jacob, and of his truth to the house of Israel.* And what? to Jacob only, and to the house of Israel only? The house of the Jews and that progeny of Abraham according to the flesh is wont to be called the house of Israel, and Israel is Jacob. For Jacob is the son of Isaac, and Isaac himself is the son of Abraham. Therefore Jacob was the grandson of Abraham; and from Jacob the twelve sons, and from the twelve sons the whole progeny of the Jews. Was Christ promised to them only? If you examine what Israel is, Christ was promised to Israel. Israel is "seeing God." We shall see in vision, if now we see by faith. Let our faith have eyes, and the truth of faith will be exhibited: let us believe in him whom we do not see, and rejoicing we shall see him; let us desire the unseen, and we shall enjoy the seen. Therefore now too Israel by faith; but then Israel by vision, face to face[^7]. Not now through a glass, not in a riddle; but, as it was said by John: *Dearly beloved, we are the sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is*[^8]. For this vision prepare your hearts, for this joy prepare your souls: as, if God wished to show the sun, he would admonish you to prepare the eyes of the flesh; but because he deigns to show you the appearance of his wisdom, prepare the eyes of the heart. Blessed are the pure of heart, for they shall see God[^9]. *He was mindful of his mercy to Jacob, and of his truth to the house of Israel.* Who is this Israel? Lest perhaps you think of the one nation of the Jews, hear what follows: *All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.* It was not said, all the earth; but, *all the ends of the earth*: as it is said, from end to end. Let no one cut off, let no one scatter; strong is the unity of Christ. He bought the whole, who gave so great a price: *All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.*

**4.** [v. 4.] Since, then, they have seen: *Make a joyful noise to God, all the earth.* Now you know what it is to make a joyful noise. Rejoice and speak. If you cannot speak what you rejoice in, make a joyful noise; let the joyful noise express your joy, if speech cannot: yet let not the joy be mute; let not the heart be silent of its God, let it not be silent of his gifts. If you speak to yourself, you have been healed for yourself; if his right hand has healed you for him, speak to him for whom you have been healed. *All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God. Make a joyful noise to God, all the earth; sing, and exult, and make music.*

**5.** [v. 5.] *Sing psalms to the Lord our God on the lyre, on the lyre and with the voice of a psalm*[^10]. Sing psalms, not with the voice alone; take up works as well, so that you do not merely sing but also act. The one who sings and acts plays the psalm on the lyre and on the psaltery.

**6.** [v. 6.] And see what sort of instruments are added in the comparisons: *with trumpets of beaten metal and the sound of the horn trumpet*[^11]. What do the trumpets of beaten metal and the horn trumpets mean? Trumpets of beaten metal are of bronze, produced by hammering. If by hammering, then by being struck. You will be trumpets of beaten metal, brought forth for the praise of God, if you make progress while you are afflicted: tribulation is the hammering, progress is the bringing forth. Job was a trumpet of beaten metal when, suddenly struck by such great losses and by the loss of his children, made a trumpet of beaten metal by that hammering of so great a tribulation, he rang out: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done; blessed be the name of the Lord*[^12]. How he rang out! What a sweet sound he gave! This trumpet of beaten metal is still being hammered: he was given over into the power of the enemy, so that even his flesh was struck; it was struck, it began to rot and to swarm with worms. Eve was put forward for his seduction, his wife kept for the service of the devil, not for the comfort of her husband; she suggests blasphemy, but he does not comply. Adam complied with Eve in paradise; Job drives Eve away upon the dunghill. For Job was sitting on the dunghill while he oozed and rotted with worms. Job rotting on the dunghill is better than Adam whole in paradise. But Eve was still there, while Adam was no longer there. He answered Eve, who had been made ready for that supplanting and temptation, and said to her—(See, we have heard how this trumpet is hammered. The devil struck him from head to foot with a grievous wound, and, rotting with worms, he sat on the dunghill. We have heard how he was hammered; let us hear how he rings out: let us hear, if you please, the sweet voice of this trumpet of beaten metal.)—*You have spoken*, he says, *like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure evils?*[^13] O strong sound! O sweet sound! Whom would that sound not rouse, even one asleep? Whom would it not rouse to confidence in God, so that he might advance secure into battle against the devil, about to win not by his own strength but by the strength of him who tests him? For he too does the hammering; the hammer would not act of itself. For the prophet, recalling the future punishment of that devil, says: *The hammer of the whole earth has been broken*[^14]. By the hammer of the whole earth he meant the devil to be understood. By that very hammer, placed in the hand of God—that is, in the power of God—the trumpets of beaten metal are hammered, so that they may sound forth the praises of God. See how (I dare to say it, my brothers) even the Apostle was being hammered by that hammer: *Lest I be exalted*, he says, *by the greatness of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me*[^15]. See, he is being hammered; let us see how he rings out: *For this reason*, he says, *I three times asked the Lord that he would take it from me; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you, for strength is made perfect in weakness*[^16]. I wish to perfect a trumpet, says that maker; I shall not perfect it unless I hammer it: *strength is made perfect in weakness*. And hear now that very trumpet of beaten metal ringing out well: *When I am weak, then am I strong*[^17]. And the Apostle himself, as an apostle clinging to Christ, clinging to that right hand by which the hammer is held to bring forth the trumpet, placed in that right hand himself acts by means of that hammer; he says of certain men: *Whom I have handed over to Satan, that they may learn not to blaspheme*[^18]. He handed them over to the hammer to be hammered. They sounded badly before they were brought forth; perhaps, brought forth and made trumpets of beaten metal, having lost their blasphemy, they sounded the praises of the Lord. These are the trumpets of beaten metal.

**7.** What is the sound of the horn trumpet? The horn rises above the flesh: it must, by overcoming the flesh, be firm to endure and capable of carrying the voice. But how does it come to this? Because it has overcome the flesh. Whoever wishes to be a horn trumpet, let him overcome the flesh. What is it to overcome the flesh? To transcend carnal affections, to conquer carnal lusts. Hear the horn trumpets: *If, then, you have risen with Christ*, the Apostle says, *seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things upon the earth*[^19]. What is it, *seek the things that are above*? That is, rise above the flesh, do not think carnal thoughts. They were not yet horn trumpets, to whom he still spoke thus: *Brothers, I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal men. As to little ones in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not solid food; for you were not able to take it; nor indeed are you yet able, for you are still carnal*[^20]. Therefore they were not horn trumpets, because they had not risen above the flesh. The horn both clings to the flesh and rises above the flesh; and although it springs from the flesh, it overcomes the flesh. If, then, you are spiritual out of being carnal, you still tread the earth in the flesh, yet you burst forth into heaven in the spirit: *For walking in the flesh*, he says, *we do not war according to the flesh*[^21]. As for what the Apostle said to those people, brothers, let us not pass it over. What did he say to them, by which he proved that those carnal men savored carnal things and were not yet made horn trumpets? *For when each of you says, I am of Paul; another, I am of Apollos; another, I am of Cephas: are you not carnal, and do you not walk according to man? What then is Apollos? what is Paul? Ministers of God, through whom you believed. I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase*[^22]. He wishes them, then, to be lifted up from the hope they had placed in man, and to touch the spiritual things of Christ, so that they might be horn trumpets, if they overcame the flesh. Brothers, do not insult the brothers whom the mercy of God has not yet converted; know that as long as you do this, you savor the flesh. That is not the trumpet which delights the ears of God: the trumpet of insult makes fruitless war. Let the horn trumpet lift you up against the devil, not the trumpet of the flesh against your brother. *With trumpets of beaten metal and the sound of the horn trumpet, shout for joy in the sight of the Lord the king*[^23].

**8.** [vv. 7-9.] And while you shout for joy and exult with trumpets of beaten metal and the sound of the horn trumpet, what follows? *Let the sea be stirred up, and its fullness*[^24]. Brothers, when the Apostles preached the truth, like trumpets of beaten metal and horn trumpets, the sea was stirred up, the waves rose, the storms grew, persecutions of the Church arose. From what was the sea stirred up? When men shouted for joy, when they sang psalms to God; God's ears were delighted, the waves of the sea were aroused. *Let the sea be stirred up, and its fullness, the world of the earth and all who dwell in it*[^25]. Let the sea be stirred up in persecutions. *The rivers shall clap their hands together*[^26]. Let the sea be moved, and the rivers shall clap their hands together: persecutions arise, and the saints rejoice in God. From what do the rivers clap their hands? what is it to clap the hands? To rejoice in works. To clap is to rejoice; the hands are works. What rivers? Those whom God has made rivers, by giving them that water, the Holy Spirit. *If anyone thirsts*, he says, *let him come and drink. He who believes in me, rivers of living water shall flow from his belly*[^27]. These rivers were clapping their hands, these rivers were rejoicing in works, and were blessing God.

**9.** *The mountains shall exult before the face of the Lord, for he comes, for he comes to judge the earth*[^28]. Great are the *mountains*. God comes to judge the earth, and they rejoice. For there are mountains which will tremble at the Lord's coming to judge the earth. Therefore there are good mountains and there are evil mountains: the good mountains are spiritual greatness; the evil mountains are the swelling of pride. *The mountains shall exult before the face of the Lord, for he comes, for he comes to judge the earth*[^29]. Why will he come, and how will he come? *For he comes to judge the earth. He shall judge the world in justice, and the peoples in equity*[^30]. Let the mountains rejoice, then; for he will not judge unjustly. When some human judge is about to come, one to whom conscience cannot lie open, even innocent men tremble, if they await from him the reward of praise or fear the punishment of condemnation: but when he comes who cannot be deceived, let the mountains rejoice, let them rejoice in security; they will be enlightened by him, not condemned; let them rejoice, because the Lord will come to judge the world of the earth in equity; and so, if the just mountains rejoice, let the unjust tremble. But see, he has not yet come; what need is there for them to tremble? Let them correct themselves, and rejoice. It is in your power how you await the coming Christ. He delays his coming for this reason: that when he comes he may not condemn you. See, he has not yet come; he is in heaven, you on earth: he delays his coming, but do not you delay your resolve. His coming is harsh to the harsh, gentle to the godly. See, then, what you yourself are: if you are harsh, you are permitted to grow gentle; if you are gentle, rejoice already that he will come. For you are a Christian. So it is, you say. I believe that you pray and say: *Your kingdom come*[^31]. You wish him to come, whom you fear may come. Correct yourself, so that you may not pray against yourself.
