# On the Same Psalm 118 (31)

**1.** [v. 169.] Let us now hear the voice of one praying: since we know who prays, and we recognize ourselves too, if we are not reprobate, among the members of this one who prays. *Let my prayer draw near in your sight, O Lord;* that is, let my prayer, which is made in your sight, draw near to you. For the Lord is near to those who have bruised their heart[^1]. *According to your utterance give me understanding.* He asks for the promise. For he says this, *According to your utterance;* as if he said, according to your promise. For the Lord promised this, where he says, *I will give you understanding*[^2].

**2.** [v. 170.] *Let my petition enter in your sight, O Lord; according to your utterance rescue me.* He in a way repeats what he asked. For what he had said before, *Let my prayer draw near in your sight, O Lord,* is like what he says afterward, *Let my petition enter in your sight, O Lord;* and what before, *According to your utterance give me understanding,* agrees with what after, *According to your utterance rescue me.* For by receiving understanding he is rescued who, by not understanding through himself, is deceived.

**3.** [v. 171.] *My lips,* he says, *will pour forth a hymn, when you have taught me your statutes.* We know how God teaches those who are teachable by God. For everyone who has heard from the Father and has learned comes to him who justifies the ungodly[^3]: so that not only by holding in memory, but also by doing, he keeps the statutes of God. For thus he who glories glories not in himself, but in the Lord, and a hymn is poured forth.

**4.** [v. 172.] But now, because he has learned and praised God his teacher, he then wishes to teach. *My tongue,* he says, *will utter your utterances, for all your commandments are righteousness.* When he says he will utter these things, surely he becomes a minister of the word. For although God teaches within, yet faith is from hearing; and how do they hear without a preacher[^4]? For because God gives the increase, there is not therefore no planting and watering[^5].

**5.** [vv. 173-174.] But he knows what perils will follow from those who contradict and persecute, when he has become an utterer of the utterances of God: on account of which he added: *Let your hand be, that it may save me; for I have chosen your commandments.* That I might not fear, nor only my heart hold, but my tongue also utter your utterances; *I have chosen your commandments,* and pressed down fear by love. Let your hand be, then, that you may save me from a hand not your own. But God so made the martyrs safe, when he did not allow them to be killed in the soul: for according to the flesh the safety of man is vain. It can also be taken thus, *Let your hand be,* that the hand of God be understood as Christ; according to that of Isaiah, *And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed*[^6]? For the Only-begotten was not made, since through him all things were made; but he was made of the seed of David, that he might be Jesus, that is, Savior, who already was Creator. But since it is familiar to Scripture: *Let your hand be;* and, *The hand of the Lord was made;* I do not know whether this sense can be maintained in all those places. Surely, where we hear what follows, *I have longed for your salvation, O Lord;* even with all enemies unwilling, may Christ, the salvation of God, meet us: that they longed for him the righteous of old most truly confess; for him the Church longed, to come from the womb of his mother; for him the Church longs, to come from the right hand of the Father. But to this sentence is joined: *And your law is my meditation;* because the law bears witness to Christ.

**6.** [v. 175.] But in this faith, when with the heart it is believed unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation[^7]; let the nations rage, let the peoples meditate vain things[^8], let the flesh certainly be killed while it preaches you: *My soul will live, and will praise you, and your judgments will help me.* For those are the judgments which it was time should begin at the house of the Lord. But *they will help me,* he says. And who does not see how much the blood of the Church has helped the Church? How great a harvest has risen up in the whole world from that sowing?

**7.** [v. 176.] At the very last he plainly opens himself, and shows what person was speaking through the whole psalm. *I have gone astray,* he says, *like a lost sheep; seek your servant, for I have not forgotten your commandments.* Some manuscripts do not have *seek,* but *give life.* For there is a difference of one syllable, by which in Greek they differ from each other; whence the Greek manuscripts themselves vary. But whichever of these it is, let the lost sheep be sought, let the lost sheep be given life, for which its shepherd left ninety-nine on the mountains, and, seeking it, was torn by Jewish thorns[^9]. But it is still sought, let it still be sought, having been found in part let it still be sought. For in that part in which this man says, *I have not forgotten your commandments,* it has been found; but through those who choose, gather, love the commandments of God, it is still sought, and through the blood of its shepherd, poured out and dispersed, it is found in all the nations.

**8.** As I could, as far as I was helped by the Lord, I have treated and expounded this great psalm. This, surely, the wiser and more learned have done, or will do, better; yet not for that reason ought our ministry to have been lacking to it, especially to the brethren who demanded it of me, to whom I am a debtor of this office. But that I said nothing about the Hebrew alphabet, where eight verses lie under each single letter, and so the whole psalm is woven together, let it not be wondered at, since I found nothing that pertained properly to this psalm: for it does not alone have these letters. Let those, indeed, who cannot find this in the Greek and Latin Scripture, since it was not preserved there, know that all eight verses, in the Hebrew manuscripts, begin from that letter which is set before them; as has been indicated to us by those who knew those letters. Which was done much more carefully than our people are accustomed to do, whether in Latin or in Punic, in the psalms they call abecedarian. For not all the verses, until the period is closed, but only the first, begin from that same letter which they set before them.
