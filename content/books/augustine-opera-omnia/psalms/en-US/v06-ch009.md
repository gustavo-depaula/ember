# Exposition of Psalm 130

**1.** [vv. 1-3.] Since we presume that you keep watch not only with the eyes of the body, but also with the heart, we must sing with understanding: *Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice*[^1]. For this is the voice of one ascending, belonging to the song of ascents. So each one of us ought to see in what depth he is, from which he cries to the Lord. Jonah cried from the depth, from the belly of the whale[^2]. He was not only under the waves, but even in the entrails of the beast; and yet that body and those waves did not shut off his prayer from reaching God, and the belly of the beast could not hold back the voice of the one praying. It penetrated all things, it burst through all things, it reached the ears of God: if indeed it should be said that, bursting through all things, it reached the ears of God, when the ears of God were in the heart of the one praying. For where does he not have God present, whose voice is faithful? Yet we too ought to understand out of what depth we cry to the Lord. For this mortal life is a depth for us. Whoever has understood himself to be in the depth cries, groans, sighs, until he is rescued from the depth, and comes to him who sits above all the deeps and above the Cherubim, above all that he created, not only bodily but also spiritual things; until the soul comes to him, until his image, which is man, is freed by him—the image which in this depth, tossed as it were by constant waves, has been worn away: and unless it is renewed and repaired by God, who impressed it when he formed man (Man could be capable of his own fall; he is not capable of his own resurrection), it is always in the depth; unless it is freed, as I said, it is always in the depth. But when it cries out of the depth, it rises from the depth, and the very cry does not allow it to be very far down. For they are deeply in the depth who do not even cry out of the depth. Scripture says: *The sinner, when he comes into the depth of evils, despises*[^3]. Now see, brothers, what kind of depth it is where God is despised. When someone has seen himself overwhelmed with daily sins, pressed down by certain heaps and masses of iniquities, if it is said to him that he should pray to God, he mocks. In what ways? First he says: If God were displeased by misdeeds, would I be living? If God cared about human affairs, in the face of such great crimes as I have committed, would I not only be living, but even have it well with me? For this usually happens to those who are deeply in the depth, and prosper in their iniquities: and the more they seem to be happy, the more they are sunk in the depth; for happiness is deceptive, and is itself the greater unhappiness. Then men are also accustomed to say this: Since I have already done many things and damnation threatens, I lose this, that I do not do whatever I can; and: Since I am lost by this, why do I not do whatever I can? As desperate robbers are accustomed to say: The judge will kill me for ten murders just as for five, just as for one; why do I not now do whatever comes my way? This is: *The sinner, when he comes into the depth of evils, despises.* But the Lord Jesus Christ, who did not despise even our depths, who deigned to come even to this life, promising the remission of all sins; roused man even from the depth, that he might cry out of the depth under the masses of sins, and that the voice of the sinner might reach God: whence does one cry, if not out of the depth of evils?

**2.** And see that the voice of the sinner cries out of the depth: *Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice. Let your ears be attentive to the voice of my supplication*[^4]. Whence does he cry? Out of the depth. Who, then, is it that cries? The sinner. And in what hope does he cry? Because he who came to loose sins gave hope even to the sinner set in the depth. Therefore what follows after these words? *If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?*[^5] Behold, he has revealed out of what depth he cries. For he cries under the masses and waves of his iniquities. He looked around at himself, he looked around at his life; he saw it covered on every side with disgraces and crimes: wherever he looked back, he found nothing good in himself, no serenity of justice could meet him. And when he saw such great and so many sins on every side, and the troops of his crimes, as though terrified, he cried out: *If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?* He did not say: I will not endure it; but, *who will endure it?* For he saw nearly the whole of human life barked at all around by its sins, all consciences accused by their thoughts, no chaste heart found presuming on its own justice. If, then, a chaste heart cannot be found that may presume on its own justice; let the heart of all presume on the mercy of God, and say: *If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?*

**3.** [vv. 4-6.] But why is there hope? *Because with you there is forgiveness*[^6]. And what is this forgiveness, except sacrifice? And what is the sacrifice, except what was offered for us? Innocent blood, poured out, blotted out all the sins of the guilty: so great a price given redeemed all captives from the hand of the captivating enemy. So with you there is forgiveness. For if with you there were no forgiveness, if you wished to be only a judge and were unwilling to be merciful, you would mark all our iniquities, and seek them out; who would endure it? who would stand before you, and say: I am innocent? who would stand in your judgment? So there is one hope: *Because with you there is forgiveness. For the sake of your law I have endured you, O Lord*[^7]. What law? The one that made men guilty? For a holy, just, good law was given to the Jews; but it could make them guilty[^8]. A law was not given that could give life, but one that might show his sins to the sinner[^9]. For the sinner had forgotten himself, nor did he see himself; a law was given to him, that he might see himself. The law made him guilty; the Giver of the law freed him: for the Giver of the law is the Emperor. A law was given to frighten and bind into guilt; and the law did not loose from sins, but showed the sins. And perhaps, set under that very law, this man noticed in the depth how much he had done against the law, and so he cried out, saying: *If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?* So there is a law of the mercy of God, a law of the forgiveness of God. That was a law of fear, there is another law of love. The law of love grants pardon to sins, blots out the past, admonishes about the future; on the way it does not desert its companion, it becomes the companion of him whom it leads on the way. But one must agree with the adversary, while one is with him on the way[^10]. For the word of God is your adversary, as long as you do not agree with it. But you agree, when it begins to delight you to do what the word of God says. Now what was an adversary becomes a friend: so, the way being finished, there will be no one to hand you over to the judge. So *for the sake of your law I have endured you, O Lord*; because you deigned to bring a law of mercy, to forgive me all my sins, to give me admonitions for the rest, that I might not offend you: in those very admonitions, if perhaps I stumble in anything, you have given me a remedy by which I may pray to you, saying: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^11]. This law you have established for me, that as I forgive, it may be forgiven me. *For the sake of* this *law I have endured you, O Lord.* I waited for when you would come and free me from all distress, because in that very distress you did not desert the law of mercy.

**4.** Hear what law he means, if you have not yet understood that he now means the law of love; hear the apostle: *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^12]. Who bear one another's burdens, except those who have love? Those who do not have love are a burden to themselves; but those who have love bear one another. Someone has injured you, he asks pardon: if you do not forgive, you do not bear your brother's burden; if you forgive, you bear the weak man. And you too, if perhaps you fall into some weakness, as a man, he ought to bear you as you bear him. Hear what came before: *Brothers*, he says, *if a man be overtaken in some fault, you who are spiritual instruct such a one in a spirit of gentleness.* And lest perhaps, because he had admonished the spiritual, they should seem secure to themselves, he at once added: *Looking to yourself, lest you too be tempted.* Then what I mentioned, he added: *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^13]: whence he says: *For the sake of your law I have endured you, O Lord.* Deer are said, when they cross straits to nearby islands for the sake of pasture, to place their heads upon one another; and one, who is in front, alone carries the head and does not place it upon another: but when he too has grown tired, he takes himself from the front part and returns to the rear, that he too may rest upon another: and so all carry their burdens, and reach what they desire; and they do not suffer shipwreck, because love is, as it were, a ship for them. So love carries burdens; but let it not fear being pressed down by such burdens: let each one take care that he not be pressed down by his own sins. For when you bear your brother's weakness, his sins do not burden you. Plainly, if you consent, then your own sins press you down, not another's. For whoever consents to a sinner is weighed down not by another's sins, but by his own. For consenting to another's sin becomes your own sin; and there is no reason why you should complain that another's sins press you down. For it is said to you: They press you down, but they are your own. You saw a thief, you ran with him[^14]. What is this? With your feet you walked to the theft; rather, in mind you joined yourself to the thief: what was his alone became yours; because it pleased you. But if it displeased you, and you prayed for him, and, being asked, gave pardon, that you might be able with a free brow to say in your prayers, which the heavenly Jurist dictated to you: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^15]; you have learned to bear your brother's burdens; that another too may bear what is yours, and there may be fulfilled in you what the apostle says: *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^16]. So securely you sing what was just now said: *For the sake of your law I have endured you, O Lord.*

**5.** But he who does not keep this law neither endures the Lord; nor, if he wished to endure, is there a reason why he should endure; in vain he endures. For the Lord is to come, and to find your sins; but that you have lived in perfect justice, he is not to find. Murders perhaps (for they are grave and far greater) he is not to find; adultery he is not to find, thefts he is not to find, plunder he is not to find, sorceries he is not to find, idolatry he is not to find; these he is not to find. Will he, then, find nothing? Hear the word of the Gospel: *Whoever says to his brother: You fool*. From these most minute sins of the tongue, who refrains? But perhaps you say: They are small. *He will be liable*, he says, *to the fire of hell*[^17]. If it seemed to you small or slight to say to your brother: *You fool*, at least let the fire of hell seem to you great: if you despised the lesser sin, at least be deterred by the greatness of the punishment. But you say: They are lesser, they are minute, without which this life cannot be. Heap up the minute things, and they make a huge pile. For grains too are minute, and yet they make a mass: and drops are minute, and yet they fill rivers, and sweep away masses. So that man too, considering how many minute daily sins a man commits, if nothing else, at least through thoughts and the tongue, attends to how many they are; and if he attends to how minute they are, he sees that through many minute things a great pile is made; and not thinking, as it were, of his former sins, but of human frailty itself, now ascending he cries: *Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice. Let your ears be attentive to the voice of my supplication. If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?* I can avoid murders, adulteries, plunder, perjuries, sorceries, idolatry; but can I avoid the sins of the tongue? can I avoid the sins of the heart? It is written: *Sin is iniquity*[^18]. *Who*, then, *will endure it*, *if you mark iniquities?* if you wish to deal with us as a severe judge, not a merciful father, who will stand before your eyes? But *with you there is forgiveness; for the sake of your law I have endured you, O Lord.* What is that law? *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^19]. Who bear one another's burdens? Those who faithfully say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^20].

**6.** *My soul has endured for your word*[^21]. No one endures, except one who has not yet received what was promised: for he who has already received, what does he endure? We have received the remission of sins; but the kingdom of heaven was promised to us: our debts have been blotted out; but our reward is still to come: we have received pardon; but eternal life we do not yet hold. But he who gave pardon, he himself also promised life eternal. If it were our word, we ought to fear: because it is the word of God, it does not deceive. So securely we hope in the word of him who cannot deceive. *My soul has hoped in the Lord, from the morning watch even until night*[^22]. What does he say? did he hope in the Lord for one day, and is his whole hope finished? *From the morning watch even until night, it has hoped in the Lord.* This morning watch is the end of the night; from this *even until night my soul has hoped in the Lord.* So it is to be understood, lest we think we are to hope in the Lord for one day: *From the morning watch even until night.* What, then, do you think, brothers? This is it: *From the morning watch even until night my soul has hoped in the Lord*: because the Lord through whom our sins were forgiven us rose from the dead in the morning watch, that we might hope that this will come to pass in us, which went before in the Lord. For now our sins have been forgiven, but we have not yet risen: if we have not yet risen, there has not yet come to pass in us what went before in our head. What went before in our head? That the flesh of that very head rose; the spirit of that head, did it die? But what died in him rose. And he rose on the third day; and in a certain way the Lord said this to us: What you have seen in me, hope for in yourselves; that is, because I have risen, you too will rise.

**7.** But there are those who say: Behold, the Lord has risen; is it therefore to be hoped that I too can rise? Surely for that reason: for the Lord rose in this, which he received from you. For he would not rise unless he had died, and he would not have died unless he bore flesh. What did the Lord receive from you? Flesh. What did he himself come as? The Word of God, which was before all things, through whom all things were made. But that he might receive something from you, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us[^23]. He received from you what he might offer for you; just as the priest receives from you what he may offer for you, when you wish to appease God for your sins. Now it has been done, so it has been done. Our priest received from us what he might offer for us: for he received flesh from us; in that very flesh he was made the victim, was made the holocaust, was made the sacrifice. In the passion he was made the sacrifice; in the resurrection he renewed that which had been slain, and gave it as your firstfruits to God, and said to you: All your things are now consecrated, since such firstfruits of you have been given to God; hope, then, that there will come to pass in you too what went before in your firstfruits.

**8.** So because he rose from the morning watch, our soul has from this begun to hope: and how long? *Until night*; until we die. For every death of ours in the flesh is, as it were, a sleep. You have begun to hope from the time the Lord rose; do not cease to hope until you go out of this life. For if you do not hope until night, all that you had hoped for is blotted out. For there are men who begin to hope, but do not persevere until night. They begin to suffer some tribulations, they begin to suffer temptations, they see evil and unjust men flourish in temporal happiness: and because they hoped such things from the Lord, that they might here be happy, they attend to those who have committed crimes having what they themselves desired to have; and their feet fail, and they cease to hope. Why? Because they did not begin to hope from the morning watch. What is this? They did not begin to hope from the Lord that which went before in the Lord from that morning watch: but they hoped from the Lord that, if they were Christians, they would have a house full of grain, wine, oil, silver, gold; that none of them would die untimely; that if anyone had no sons, he would receive them; if he had not taken a wife, he would take one; that not only no woman in his house would miscarry, but not even his cattle; that his casks would not turn sour, that his vineyard would not be struck by hail. He who so hoped in the Lord notices that those who do not worship the Lord abound in these things, and his feet fail[^24], and he does not hope until night; because he did not begin to hope from the morning watch.

**9.** Who, then, begins thus to hope from the morning watch? He who hopes from the Lord that which he began to show from the morning watch in which he rose. For before, no one had risen to live forever. Let your charity attend. The dead were raised before the coming of the Lord: for Elijah too raised a dead man[^25], and Elisha[^26]; but they rose again to die again. Those whom the Lord himself raised rose to die again; whether that young man, the widow's son[^27], or that girl of twelve years, the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue[^28], or Lazarus[^29]: they were raised otherwise, all to die again; once they were born, but twice they died. No one had risen never to die, except the Lord. But when did the Lord rise never to die? *From the morning watch.* Hope this too from the Lord, that you will rise, not as Lazarus rose, not as the widow's son and the ruler of the synagogue's daughter rose, not as those whom the ancient prophets raised; but hope that you will rise as the Lord did, so that after the resurrection by which you will have risen, you may no longer fear that you will die: and you have begun to hope from the morning watch.

**10.** But hope until night, until this life is finished, until there is the night of the whole human race at the setting of the world. Why until this point? Because after this night there will no longer be hope, but the reality itself. *For hope which is seen is not hope*, the apostle says: *for what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^30]. If, then, we ought patiently to await what we do not see, let us hope until night, that is, until the end of this our life or of the world. But when this night has passed, then will come what we hoped for; and we will no longer hope, nor yet will we be in despair. For there is a reproach of those who despair, and sometimes we detest a man, and say: He has no hope. It is not always evil to have no hope. While we are in this life, it is evil to have no hope; for he who now has no hope will afterward have no reality. So now we ought to have hope. But when the reality has come, will there be hope? *For what a man sees, why does he hope for it?* The Lord our God will come, first to show to the human race that very form in which he was crucified and rose again, that the devout and the impious may see it: let the devout see, and rejoice that they have found what they believed before they saw; and let the impious blush that they did not believe what they will see. Blushing, they will be condemned, and rejoicing, they will be crowned. To the confounded it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*; to those rejoicing it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^31]: which when they have received, there will no longer be hope, because the reality will be held. So, hope being done with, that night will pass; but until that comes to pass, from the morning watch let our soul hope in the Lord.

**11.** And he returns to this: *From the morning watch let Israel hope in the Lord. From the morning watch even until night, my soul has hoped in the Lord.* But what has he hoped? *From the morning watch let Israel hope in the Lord.* Not only *let Israel hope in the Lord*, but, *from the morning watch let Israel hope.* Do I, then, find fault with the hope of the world, when one hopes from God? No; but there is another hope proper to Israel. Let not Israel hope, as its highest good, for riches, not for health of body, not for an abundance of earthly things: rather, he is to have tribulation here, if perhaps it happens to him to suffer some troubles for the sake of the truth. For the martyrs did not fail to hope in God, and yet they suffered such things as robbers, as the wicked, suffer: thrown to beasts, burned with fires, struck with the sword, torn with claws, bound with chains, killed in prison—suffering all these evils, did they not hope in the Lord? or did they hope for this, that, lacking these evils, they might enjoy this life? Not at all; because they hoped from the morning watch. What is this? They considered that morning watch in which their Lord rose, and saw that before he rose he too had suffered such things as they were suffering, and they did not despair that they too, after such sufferings, would rise to eternal life. *Israel has hoped in the Lord, from the morning watch even until night.*

**12.** [vv. 7-8.] *Because with the Lord is mercy, and with him is plentiful redemption*[^32]. Magnificently! it could not be better said in its place, on account of what he said: *From the morning watch let Israel hope in the Lord.* Why? Because from the morning watch the Lord rose; and the body ought to hope this, which went before in the head. But lest this thought be suggested: It was allowed to the head to rise, because it was not pressed down by sins, there was no sin in it; what shall we do? Shall we hope for such a resurrection as went before in the Lord, when we are weighed down by our sins? But see what follows: *Because with the Lord is mercy, and with him is plentiful redemption. And he himself will redeem Israel from all his iniquities*[^33]. So if he was pressed down by his own sins, the mercy of God is at hand. For that one went before without sin, in order to blot out the sins of those who follow. Do not presume upon yourselves, but presume from the morning watch. See your head risen and ascended into heaven. In him there was no fault, but through him your faults will be blotted out: *He himself will redeem Israel from all his iniquities.* Because Israel could sell himself, and become sold under sin; he cannot redeem himself from his iniquities. He could redeem who could not sell himself: he who committed no sin, he himself is the redeemer from sin. *He himself will redeem Israel.* Whence will he redeem? From this iniquity, or from that? *From all his iniquities.* So let one about to come to God not fear some of his iniquities: let him only come with a full heart, and now cease to do what he formerly did, and not say: That iniquity is not forgiven me. For if he says this, on account of that very thing which he thinks is not forgiven him, he does not convert himself, and by doing the rest, even that which he did not fear is not forgiven him. Because I have committed, he says, a great crime, and it cannot be forgiven me; I will now do the rest too: for I lose this, that I do not do it. Do not fear: you are in the depth, do not despise to cry out of the depths to the Lord, and to say: *If you mark iniquities, O Lord; Lord, who will endure it?* Mark him, and wait for him, and endure for the sake of his law. What law did he give you? *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.* Hope that you will rise, and then be in every way without sin, since he rose who was first without sin. Hope from the morning watch. Do not say: I am not worthy on account of my sins. You are not worthy; but *with him is plentiful redemption, and he himself will redeem Israel from all his iniquities.*
