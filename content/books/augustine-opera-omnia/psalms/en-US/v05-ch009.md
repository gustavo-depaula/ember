# On the Same Psalm 103 (3)

**1.** Your Charity remembers, since there is one word of God spread out in all the Scriptures, and through the many mouths of the saints one Word sounds, which, since it is in the beginning God with God, there has no syllables, because it has no times; nor should we wonder that, on account of our weakness, it descended to the particles of our sounds, since it descended to take up the weakness of our body: yet this psalm has already made for us many sermons, and the very mysteries which are here held shut, that they might be opened to those who knock, have brought us no small delays of time over several days, when they are pronounced, when they are commended, when they are shown to be shut, when they are opened, when they are dug out, when they are shown; and through this your Charity remembers, as I said, that on the past day too we could not reach the end of this psalm, and deferred it to this day. The Lord willed both to grant us a time of repayment, and that I, a debtor, satisfy the debt, and that he make the exactors more secure: let him, then, himself give the good that we may render, who did not render whatever evil we had done.

**2.** [vv. 24, 25.] They cried out, as you know, as with piety and joy you recall, the inward parts of our heart cried out with the Psalm, and said: *How magnified are your works, O Lord! You made all things in wisdom: the earth is filled with your creation.* Whatever was made by God was made in wisdom, and was made through wisdom. Whatever knows wisdom, and whatever does not know wisdom, and yet is in the creation of God, was made in wisdom, and was made through wisdom. Those who know wisdom have wisdom as their light; those who do not know it have wisdom yet as their maker, while they themselves are held by unwisdom: and those who have that light, have the maker too; not all who have the maker have the light too. And indeed among men there are many who become partakers of it, and are called wise; many who are without it, and are named foolish. For this reason they are called fools by a name of fault, because, if they were to be eager for wisdom, if they asked, if they sought, if they knocked, they could come to a participation in it: for it is denied not to nature, but to negligence. But there are other creatures which cannot become partakers of wisdom; as all the beasts, all the cattle, all the trees, which have no perception at all. Because they cannot be partakers of wisdom, were they therefore not made in wisdom and through wisdom? God, then, does not demand understanding of the horse and the mule: but he says to men, *Do not be like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*[^1]. What for the horse is nature, for man is a fault. This, then, God says: I do not demand a participation in my wisdom from those things which I did not make to my image; but where I made it, from there I demand, and I require the use of that thing which I gave. Men, then, rendering to God the things that are God's, if they render to Caesar what is Caesar's; that is, rendering to Caesar his image, and rendering to God his image, raise their very mind, not to themselves, but to their maker, and to the light whence they are, and to a certain spiritual warmth from which they grow fervent, and removed from which they grow cold, and withdrawing from which they are darkened, and returning to which they are illumined: and because they piously said to him, *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, you will lighten my darkness*[^2]; the darkness of earthly folly being shaken off, opening their mouth and drawing in the spirit, they raise, as I said, the trusting eye of the heart; and they look around with the mind on the whole world, the earth, the sea and the sky, and seeing all things beautifully arranged, running in order, divided into kinds, supported by seeds, changed by successions, running in seasons, the maker pleases them in these things, that they too may please the maker in his workmanship; and they cry out for great joy, because truly to this gladness nothing can be compared: *How magnified are your works, O Lord! You made all things in wisdom.* Where is that very wisdom in which you made all things? by what perception is it reached? by what eye is it seen? by what zeal is it sought? by what merit is it possessed? By what, do you suppose, except by its grace? He who granted that we should be, grants that we should be good. He grants it to the converted, who, before they were converted, and when, turned away, they were going after their own ways, did he not seek them? did he not descend? did not the Word become flesh, and dwell among us? did he not light the lamp of his flesh, while he hung on the cross, and seek the lost coin? He sought and found it, the neighbors rejoicing together, that is, all the spiritual creation that touches God most nearly. With the neighbors rejoicing the coin was found; with the angels rejoicing the human soul was found. It was found, therefore let it rejoice, and say: *How magnified are your works, O Lord! You made all things in wisdom.*

**3.** *The earth is filled with your creation.* With what creation of yours is the earth filled? With all trees and shrubs, with all animals and cattle; and with the whole human race itself, the earth is filled with the creation of God. We see it, we know it, we read it, we recognize it, we praise it, and in these things we preach; and in these things we cannot praise as much as our heart overflows from the good beholding. But we ought rather to attend to that creation, of which the Apostle says: *If, then, anyone is a new creation in Christ, the old things have passed away; behold, all things have been made new*[^3]. What *old things have passed away?* Among the gentiles all idolatry, among the Jews themselves all that bondage of the Law, all those sacrifices foretelling the present sacrifice. The oldness of man then abounded; he came to renew his work, he came to recast his silver, who would form his coin, and we behold the earth full of Christians believing in God, turning away from their former uncleannesses and idolatry, from past hope to the hope of the new age: and behold, it is not yet in fact, and is already held in hope, and through that very hope we already sing and say: *The earth is filled with your creation.* For not yet do we sing this in the homeland, not yet in that rest which is promised, not yet with the bars of the gates of Jerusalem strengthened; but still in pilgrimage gazing on this whole world, and men everywhere running to the faith, fearing the Gehennas, scorning death, loving eternal life, spurning the present, and, filled with joy at such a spectacle, we say: *The earth is filled with your creation.*

**4.** [v. 25.] But this age is still shaken by the waves of temptations, still disturbed by the tempests and storms of tribulations and swellings: yet by this way one goes. Let the sea threaten, and swell with waves, and give birth to storms; by this way one goes, wood has been given us in which to sail: *The earth is filled with your creation.* But we are not yet in the land of the living, this is still the land of the dying; but we cry out, and say: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^4]. In the land of the dying my hope, in the land of the living my portion. Behold this earth which is filled with the creation of God. He who is still in this land of the dying, not yet in the land of the living, by what does he pass over? Hear what follows: *This sea great and spacious, there things creeping of which there is no number; animals small and great.* It signifies a terrible sea: *there* are *things creeping of which there is no number.* Treacheries creep in this age, and suddenly seize the incautious: but who counts the creeping temptations? They creep, but beware lest they steal upon you. Let there be watching in the wood; even in the waters, even in the waves we are safe: let not Christ sleep, let not faith sleep; and if it has slept, let it be roused; he will command the winds, he will calm the sea; the way will be finished, there will be rejoicing in the homeland. *There things creeping of which there is no number; animals small and great.* For I see still in this fearful sea those who do not yet believe: for they are tossed in the bitter and barren waters; but they are both small and great. We know this; many of the small of the world have not yet believed, many of the chief men of the world have not yet believed: *animals small and great* are in this sea. They hate the Church, they are pressed by the name of Christ; they do not rage, because they are not permitted: not bursting out into the hands, the savagery is shut up in the heart. For all, whether small or great, *animals small and great*, who now grieve that the temples are shut, the altars overthrown, the images broken, laws passed that to sacrifice to idols is a capital crime; all who grieve at these things are still in the sea. What, then, of us? by what shall we go to the homeland? Through the sea itself, but in the wood. Do not fear the danger; the wood carries you which contains the world. Therefore attend: *This sea great and spacious, there things creeping of which there is no number; animals small and great.* Do not fear, do not be terrified; desire the homeland, understand the pilgrimage.

**5.** [v. 26.] *There the ships will pass to and fro.* Behold, in that which terrified, the ships swim, and are not submerged. We understand the ships to be the Churches; they pass to and fro amid tempests, amid the storms of temptations, amid the waves of the world, amid the animals small and great. The pilot is Christ on the wood of his cross. *There the ships will pass to and fro.* Let not the ships fear, let them not greatly attend to where they swim, but by whom they are steered. *There the ships will pass to and fro.* What passage do they find sad, when they perceive Christ as pilot? They will pass securely, let them pass perseveringly, they will come to the due end, they will be led through to the land of rest.

**6.** There is in this sea also something that surpasses all the animals small and great. What is this? Let us hear the Psalm: *This dragon which you formed to make sport of him.* There things creeping of which there is no number, there animals small and great; there the ships will pass to and fro, and will not fear, not only the things creeping of which there is no number, and the animals small and great, but not even the dragon who is there: *Whom you formed*, he says to God, *to make sport of him.* A great secret, and yet I will say what you know. You know a certain dragon, the enemy of the Church: you have not seen him with the eyes of the flesh, but you see him with the eyes of faith. He is the one who is also called a lion: of him Scripture says, *You will trample on the lion and the dragon*[^5]. He is subject to your head, he will be subject to his body too; only let the members cling to their head, so as to be his members. It was said of the first woman, whom this dragon seduced; namely Eve, to whom he gave the counsel of death, and crept into the feminine heart by cunning persuasion in the manner of a serpent. There happened what we know, what we too did there, what we grieve over. For in those two men is the whole human race: thence the propagation of death, thence the debts, the offenses, even in little ones. *For who is clean*, says Scripture, *in your sight? not even the infant whose life is of one day on the earth*[^6]. A transmission of sin, a transmission of death from the first sin. For you know too what was said to the woman, or rather to the serpent, when God heard the sin of the first man: *She will watch your head, and you will watch her heel*[^7]. Said in a great mystery, said in figure of the future Church, made from the side of her husband, and this while he slept. For Adam was a figure of the one to come. This the Apostle says: *Who is a figure of the one to come*[^8]. It was prefigured what was to be, the Church was made from the side of the Lord sleeping on the cross. For from the pierced side of the crucified, the Sacraments of the Church flowed forth. What, then, was said to the Church? Now hear, understand, beware: *She will watch your head, and you her heel.* O Church, watch the head of the serpent. What is the head of the serpent? The first suggestion of sin. There comes into your mind something or other unlawful; do not hold your mind there, do not consent. This that comes into the mind is the head of the serpent; tread on the head, and you will escape the other motions. What is it, tread on the head? Scorn the suggestion itself. But it suggested gain: there is great gain there, there is much gold there; if you do this fraud, you will be rich. It is the head of the serpent, tread on it. What is it, tread on it? Scorn what it suggested. But it suggested much gold. And what does it profit a man, if he gains the whole world, but suffer the loss of his soul? Let the gain of the world perish, lest there be loss of the soul. Saying these things, you watched the head of the serpent, and trod on it. But the devil watches your heel. What is it, he watches your heel? When you slip from the way of God. You watch the first suggestion, he watches your fall. For if you have slipped, you will fall; if you fall, he will possess you. But that you may not fall, do not go out of the way. God paved a narrow path for you; whatever is outside it, is slippery. Therefore Christ is the light, and Christ is the way: *It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^9]; and: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^10]. Through me you go, to me you go. If, then, he himself is the light, and he himself is the way; if you withdraw from him, you will be neither in the light, nor in the way. And what follows you? What a certain psalm says of the impious: *Let their way become darkness and slipperiness*[^11].

**7.** This dragon, then, our ancient enemy, fervent with anger, cunning in treachery, is in the great sea. *This dragon which you formed to make sport of him.* Now do you make sport of the dragon: for it was for this that the dragon was made here. He, falling by his own sin from the lofty dwelling of the heavens, and from an angel made a devil, received a certain place of his own in this great and spacious sea. What you suppose to be his kingdom is his prison. For many say: Why has the devil received such great power, that he should have dominion in this age, and avail so much, be able to do so much? How much does he avail, or how much can he do? Unless permitted, he can do nothing. Act so that he be not permitted in you; or if he be permitted to tempt, let him depart conquered, and not possess. For he was permitted to tempt certain holy men, servants of God: they overcame him, because they did not withdraw from the way, they did not slip, whose heel he was watching. That holy Job sat on the dunghill, and ran in the way: see how he watched his head, and how the other watched his heel. The one repelled the suggester, the other hoped for the slipping one: he took even his little woman; he withdrew all that he had, he left only his helper, not a consoler of her husband, but rather a temptress; he took even her, not watching his head. For she was still Eve; but he was no longer Adam. All being taken away, Job remained with a wife, through whom he might be tempted; and with God, by whom he might be governed. What was made suddenly poorer than he, if you consider his house? What richer than he, if you think of his heart? See the poverty of the house: all things were taken away. See the riches of the heart: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it was done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^12]. *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away:* he knew his ruler, and his tempter; he knew the permitter of his tempter. Let the devil, he says, attribute nothing to himself; he has the will to harm, but the power, unless he received it, he would not have; as much power as he received, so much I suffer: I do not suffer, then, from him, but from the one who gave the power; let the pride of my tempter be scorned, let the scourges of my Father be endured. The tempter was repelled, his head was watched, he could not penetrate into the heart. He besieged the walled city from outside, but did not take it by storm. Another temptation came: he was permitted at his body, he struck him with a grievous wound from head to feet; he wasted with rottenness, teemed with worms, his house lost, he sat on the dunghill. There Eve, captured, set by the devil under her husband not for help, but for a fall, suggests that God be blasphemed. First she suggested in paradise that God be scorned, now that God be blasphemed. She prevailed then over the whole man, now she is conquered by the rotting one: in paradise she cast him down, on the dunghill she was overcome. But that dragon was watching whether Job would slip in his tongue. For every man has feet in his act, in what he does; in what he is moved, there he as it were walks. He said many things; for how many things Job said, those who read know: in all those many words the serpent was watching the heel of the slipping one. But he who watched the head of the serpent repelled every suggestion. He answered the woman too, as it was fitting to answer a woman: *You have spoken*, he said, *like one of the foolish women: if we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, will we not endure evils? And in all those things which he said, he nowhere slipped*[^13]. Which many do not understand in those words, and take certain things there as if Job said something hard against God.

**8.** For among many things he said this too, as if vexed against God, as it seemed to those not understanding: but he bore the great person of a great prophecy: *Would that*, he says, speaking to God, *would that there were for us an arbiter!* What is it, *Would that there were for us an arbiter?* As one who would judge between us, and by whose judging my cause might prevail. So it is taken at the first sound: but examine yourself, lest you slip; for that serpent always attends to your heel. What did Job seem to have said? *Would that there were for us an arbiter!* would that there were some intermediary who would judge between me and you! This man to God, this man on a dunghill, this even an angel in heaven to God: Would that there were for us an arbiter! But what did he foresee? what did he wish for? *Many*, says the Lord, *just men and prophets wished to see what you see, and did not see it*[^14]. He desired an arbiter. What is an arbiter? An intermediary to settle a cause. Were we not enemies of God, and did we not have a bad cause against God? Who would end this bad cause, except that intermediary arbiter, who, unless he came, the way of mercy had perished? Of whom the Apostle says: *For there is one God and mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^15]. If not man, not mediator; because as God he is equal to the Father. He says in another place: *But a mediator is not of one; but God is one*[^16]. A mediator is between two: therefore Christ is mediator between man and God. Not because God, but because man: for because God, equal to the Father; but equal to the Father, not mediator. But that he might be mediator, let him descend from the higher to the lower, from equality with the Father; let him do what the Apostle says: *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man*[^17]. Let him pour out his blood, blot out our bond, settle between us and God; correcting our will toward righteousness, bending his sentence toward mercy. As, then, we have expounded this one thing, as far as the Lord granted, which seems hard, said by Job; so the other things too which seem there as it were harsh and blasphemous, have their understandings. Which we would suppose to be otherwise, unless God bore witness, both before Job spoke, and after he had spoken all. God first bore witness, saying: *A man without complaint, a true worshiper of God*[^18]. God said this, said this before his temptation. But lest anyone, perhaps misunderstanding in those words, be scandalized, and suppose that Job was indeed a just man before the temptation, but in the grievous temptation failed, and slipped into sacrilegious blasphemy; when all the sermons were finished, both of Job himself, and of his friends by whom consolation was rendered to him, the Lord gives witness that they did not speak the truth, as his servant Job. *For you have not spoken*, he says, *before me anything true, like my servant Job*[^19]. Then he commands that Job offer sacrifices for them, by which their sins may be loosed.

**9.** Come now, my brothers, whoever wishes to watch the head of the serpent, and to pass over this sea securely; because it is necessary that this serpent dwell here, and, as I had begun to say, the devil fallen from heaven received this place; let him watch its head, against the fear of the world, and against the cupidity of the world. For from this he suggests something, either whence you fear, or whence you desire; either he tempts your love, or your fear. You, if you fear the Gehennas, love the kingdom of God, will watch his head. The head avoided, you will be secure; he will neither hold your slipping, nor rejoice at your ruin. But let no one say, as I said: He has great power. For men see as it were how much power he has received; what he lost, they do not see. But holy Job himself, in his mystical and deeply secret words, speaking of this power which the devil is said to have, and describing him in many ways in figures of likenesses, expounding what he is, or what he avails, said this too: *There is nothing made like him on the earth, to be made sport of by my angels.* God speaks there in the book of Job: *There is nothing made like him on the earth, to be made sport of by my angels. He sees everything high; and he himself is king of all that are in the waters*[^20]. To which testimony this in the Psalm agrees. For when he had spoken of the sea great and spacious, where the animals small and great, where the things creeping of which there is no number, where the ships sail safe in the wood, he said: *This dragon which you formed to make sport of him.* But if to make sport of, how does God make sport of him? Or has he handed him over to be made sport of, that is, to be mocked? We would suppose that God made sport of him, unless the Scripture of Job had solved the question; for there it was said: *To be made sport of by my angels.* Do you wish to make sport of the dragon? Be an angel of God. But you are not yet an angel of God. Until you are, if you hold that course so as to be one, there are angels who make sport of the dragon, lest he harm you. For the angels of the heavens are set over the airy powers, and thence proceeds the word that is done here. For they gaze on the fixed law, the eternal law, commanding without writing, without syllables, without noise, always fixed and standing; the angels gaze on it with a pure heart, and from it they do whatever is done here, and the powers from it are ordered from the highest to the lowest. And if the powers of the highest heavens are ruled by the word of God, how much more the lower and earthly? There remains, then, in the bad only the cupidity of harming. This cupidity of harming a man has in his power, the will to destruction. But if he has been able to harm anyone, let him not glory; he himself did not harm, power was given to him. It was said once, the sentence is firm: *There is no power except from God*[^21]. What, then, do you fear? Let the dragon be in the waters, let the dragon be in the sea; you are about to pass over there. He was so formed that he might be made sport of, he was ordered here to this place, he was assigned to these seats. You suppose these seats to be something great for him, because you do not know the seats of the angels whence he fell: what seems to you his glorying is his condemnation.

**10.** Receive a likeness briefly; for truly it is something great to know and understand this. Suppose this whole administration of creation to be some great house: this great house has a master, has servants, and among the servants themselves it has some near it in better outfits of clothing, treasuries, storehouses, great possessions; it has also servants in the lowest ministries, with powers so subject to it that it has some even for cleaning the sewers: from the highest stewards to these last and lowest ministries, how many degrees there are. If, then, some great steward offends, and by the punishment of his master, for example, becomes a doorkeeper in some last place; if, exercising the power given him, he disturbs those wishing to enter or go out, according to the measure of power he received from the master, but they do not know that he was once a great steward; they suppose his power to be great, because they do not know what he lost. And yet, my brothers, that doorkeeper of whom I spoke, by the likeness of this great earthly house, can do something without his master knowing, and disturb someone without his bidding: but this one is not even set at that door by which we enter to God. For Christ is that door, and through Christ we enter to eternal life. But there is a certain door by which one enters into this age, a certain door of mortality: about these losses and supplies of this weak flesh, at this door he is as it were a certain doorkeeper; here he has power in this sea, by which the ships pass to and fro, but not so much as to do anything without his Lord knowing or willing. Lest perhaps someone say: That one indeed lost the great power of the higher outfits; but I am in these lowest things, here he can have me in his power, I must serve him. Do not be deceived; your Lord knows you, and so knows you that he has your hairs numbered. What, then, do you fear? He is perhaps going to tempt your flesh: it is the scourge of your Lord, not the power of your tempter. He wishes to harm the salvation that is promised; but he is not permitted: but that he may not be permitted, have Christ as your head; repel the head of the dragon, do not consent to his suggestion, do not slip from your way. *This dragon which you formed to make sport of him.*

**11.** [vv. 27-29.] For do you wish to see how he does not harm you, unless permitted? *All things*, he says, *wait for you, O Lord, that you may give them food in due time.* And this dragon too wishes to eat, but does not eat whom he wishes. *All things wait for you, O Lord, that you may give them food in due time. All things*: both the things creeping of which there is no number, and the animals small and great, and the dragon himself, and all your creation, with which you have filled the earth; *All things wait for you that you may give them food in due time*, to each its own food. You have your food, the dragon too has his food. If you live well, you will have Christ as food; if you withdraw from Christ, you will be the food of the dragon. *All things wait for you that you may give them food in due time.* What was said to the dragon himself? *You will eat earth.* It was said to the dragon: *You will eat earth all the days of your life*[^22]. You have heard the food of the dragon. You do not wish God to give you to be eaten by the dragon; do not be the food of the dragon, that is, do not abandon the word of God. For where it was said to the dragon, *You will eat earth;* there it had already been said to the transgressor too: *You are earth, and into earth you will go*[^23]. You do not wish to be the food of the serpent? do not be earth. How, you say, shall I not be earth? If you do not taste earthly things. Hear the Apostle, that you may not be earth. For the body you carry is earth, but you do not be earth. What is this? *If you have risen*, he says, *with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God; taste the things that are above, not the things on earth*[^24]. If you do not taste earthly things, you are not earth; if you are not earth, you are not eaten by the serpent, to whom earth is given as food. God gives the serpent his food, when he wishes, whom he wishes: but he judges well, he cannot be deceived, he does not give him gold instead of earth. *All things wait for you, O Lord, that you may give them food in due time. When you have given to them, they will gather.* They are before them; but they will not gather, unless you have given. Job was before the devil; and indeed he did not devour Job, but did not dare even to tempt, except when he had given. *They wait for you: when you have given to them, they will gather;* if you do not give, they will not gather.

**12.** And what of us, brothers? what food do we have? There follows about our food too. *But when you open your hand, all things will be filled with goodness.* What is it, O Lord, that you open your hand? Your hand is Christ. *And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?*[^25] To whom it is revealed, to him it is opened: for revelation is an opening. *But when you open your hand, all things will be filled with goodness.* When you reveal your Christ, *all things will be filled with goodness.* But they do not have goodness from themselves; for sometimes it is proved to them: *But when you turn away your face, they will be troubled.* Many, filled with goodness, attributed to themselves what they had, and wished to glory as it were in their own justifications, and said to themselves: I am just, I am great; and they became pleasing to themselves. And the Apostle sounded to them: *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^26] But God, wishing to prove to a man that he has from him whatever he has, that with goodness he may have humility too, sometimes disturbs him; turns his face away from him, and he falls into temptation; and shows him that, in that he was just, and walked rightly, it was done by his governing. *But when you turn away your face, they will be troubled.* See what he says in another psalm too: *I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever*[^27]. He presumed on himself; he was filled with goodness, and supposed that all the goodness was to himself from himself, and said in his heart: *I shall not be moved forever.* But because he had now perceived that he had received the grace of God, for that which he had experienced, he rendered thanksgiving: *O Lord, in your will you furnished strength to my beauty; but you turned away your face from me, and I became troubled*[^28]. So here too: *When you open your hand;* you will open your hand, and *all things will be filled with goodness;* not by their own hand, but by your hand opened: *But when you turn away your face, they will be troubled.*

**13.** But why do you do this? why do you turn away your face, that they be troubled? *You will take away their spirit, and they will fail.* Their spirit was their pride: they glory, they attribute to themselves, they justify themselves. Turn away your face, then, that they be troubled: take away their spirit, and let them fail; let them cry out to you: *Quickly hear me, O Lord; my spirit has failed. Do not turn away your face from me*[^29]. *You will take away their spirit, and they will fail, and they will be turned into their dust.* The man, repenting of his sin, finds that he did not have strength from himself; and he confesses to God, saying that he is earth and ashes. O proud one, you have been turned into your dust, your spirit has been taken away; now you do not boast, you do not exalt yourself, you do not justify yourself; you see that you were made from dust, and, the Lord turning away his face, you fell back into your dust. Ask, then, confess your dust and your weakness.

**14.** [v. 30.] And see what follows: *You will send forth your spirit, and they will be created.* You will take away their spirit, you will send forth yours: *you will take away their spirit*, they will not have their own spirit. Are they then deserted? *Blessed are the poor in spirit*: but they are not deserted, *for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^30]. They were unwilling to have their own spirit, they will have the Spirit of God. For this he said to the future martyrs: *When they take you and lead you, do not think how or what you may speak: for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^31]. Do not attribute fortitude to yourselves. If it is yours, he says, and not mine, it is hardness, not fortitude. *You will take away their spirit, and they will fail, and they will be turned into their dust: you will send forth your spirit, and they will be created. For we are his formation*, the Apostle said, *created in good works*[^32]. From his Spirit we received grace, that we might live for righteousness: because he is the one who justifies the impious. *You will take away their spirit, and they will fail: you will send forth your spirit, and they will be created; and you will renew the face of the earth*: that is, with new men, confessing themselves to be justified, not just from themselves, that the grace of God may be in them. See what sort those are for whom the face of the earth has been renewed. Paul says: *I labored more than all of them.* What is it, Paul? Attend whether you, whether your spirit. *Not I*, he says, *but the grace of God with me*[^33].

**15.** [v. 31.] What then? Because, when he has taken away our spirit, we will be turned into our dust, usefully gazing on our weakness, that, having received his Spirit, we may be re-created. See what follows: *Let the glory of the Lord be forever.* Not yours, not mine, not that man's, or that man's; *let the glory of the Lord be*, not for a time, but *forever. The Lord will rejoice in his works.* Not in yours, as if yours: because even your works, if bad, are through your iniquity; if good, through the grace of God. *The Lord will rejoice in his works.*

**16.** [v. 32.] *Who looks on the earth, and makes it tremble; who touches the mountains, and they will smoke.* O earth, you used to exult over your goodness, you attributed to yourself the strength of your wealth; behold, the Lord looks back, and makes you tremble. Let him look on you, and make you tremble: for better is the trembling of humility than the confidence of pride. See how God looks on the earth, and makes it tremble. To the earth as it were presuming on itself and exulting the Apostle speaks: *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation. With fear and trembling: for it is God who works in you*[^34]. You say, O Paul: *Work out;* you say that we should work: why *with trembling? For it is God*, he says, *who works in you.* Therefore *with trembling*, because *God works.* Because he gave it, it is not from yourself that you have it; with fear and trembling you will work: for if you do not tremble at him, he will take away what he gave. Work, then, with trembling. See another psalm: *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult to him with trembling*[^35]. If one must exult with trembling, God looks on, an earthquake happens: with God looking on, let our hearts tremble; then will God rest there. Hear him in another place: *Upon whom will my spirit rest? Upon the humble, and the quiet, and the one who trembles at my words*[^36]. *Who looks on the earth, and makes it tremble; who touches the mountains, and they will smoke.* The mountains were proud, they boasted, God had not touched them: he touches them, and they will smoke. What is it for the mountains to smoke? To render prayer to the Lord. Behold great mountains, proud, huge mountains, did not ask God: they wished to be asked, and did not ask the higher one. For who is powerful, and swollen, and proud on earth, who would deign to ask God humbly? I speak of the impious, not of the cedars of Lebanon which the Lord planted. Any impious one, an unhappy soul, does not know how to ask God, and wishes to be asked by men. He is a mountain, it is needful that God touch him, and he smoke: when he has begun to smoke, he will render prayer to God, as it were a sacrifice of the heart. He smokes to God, then he beats his breast: he begins to weep too, because smoke too brings out tears. *Who touches the mountains, and they will smoke.*

**17.** [v. 33.] *I will sing to the Lord in my life.* What will he sing? Whatever there is, he will sing it. Let us sing to the Lord in our life. Our life now is hope; our life afterward will be eternity: the life of mortal life is the hope of immortal life. *I will sing to the Lord in my life; I will sing psalms to my God, as long as I am.* Since in him I am without end, as long as I am, I will sing psalms to my God. Lest perhaps, when we have begun to sing psalms to God in that city, we suppose we will do something else: our whole life will be to sing psalms to God. If what we praise comes into weariness, our praising too can come into weariness. If he is always loved, he is always praised by us: *I will sing psalms to my God, as long as I am.*

**18.** [v. 34.] *Let my discourse be sweet to him: but I will rejoice in the Lord. Let my discourse be sweet to him*: what is the discourse of man to God, except the confession of sins? Confess to God what you are, and you have disputed with him. Dispute with him, do good works, and dispute. *Wash yourselves, be clean*, says Isaiah, *take away the wickednesses from your souls, from the sight of my eyes; cease from your wickednesses, learn to do good, judge for the orphan, and justify the widow, and come, let us dispute, says the Lord*[^37]. What is it to dispute with God? Reveal yourself to him who knows, that he may reveal himself to you who do not know. *Let my discourse be sweet to him.* Behold, this is sweet to the Lord, your discourse; the sacrifice of your humility, the contrition of your heart, the holocaust of your life, this is sweet to God. But what is sweet to you? *But I will rejoice in the Lord.* This is the mutual discourse I spoke of: reveal yourself to him who knows, and he will reveal himself to you who do not know. Sweet to him is your confession, sweet to you is his grace. He told you of himself. Whence did he tell you of himself? Through the Word. What Word? Christ. And he told you, and he told of himself. Because he sent Christ, he told of himself. So plainly, let us hear the Word himself: *He who has seen me, has seen the Father too*[^38]. *But I will rejoice in the Lord.*

**19.** [v. 35.] *Let sinners fail from the earth.* He seems to rage. O holy soul, which here sings and groans! Would that our soul were with that soul! would that it were coupled to it, and associated, and joined to it! it would see also the mercy of the one raging. For who grasps this, except one who is filled with love? *Let sinners fail from the earth.* You shudder, because he curses. And who curses? A holy one. Without doubt he is heard. But it was said to the saints: *Bless, and do not curse*[^39]. What, then, is it that he says: *Let sinners fail from the earth?* Let them plainly fail; let their spirit be taken away, and let them fail, that he may send forth his spirit, and they be re-created. *Let sinners fail from the earth, and the unjust, so that they be not.* What should not be, except the unjust? Therefore let them be justified, that they be not unjust. He saw this, and was filled with joy, and recalls the first verse of the psalm: *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* Let our soul bless the Lord, brothers, because he deigned to give us both the means and the speech, and to you the attention and the zeal. Let each one as he can recall what he has heard; in conversation with one another belch out your fattening, ruminate what you have received, let it not go into the bowels of your forgetfulness. Let the desirable treasure rest in your mouth. With great labor it was sought and found, with great labor it was announced and discoursed; let our labor be fruitful for you, and let our soul bless the Lord.
