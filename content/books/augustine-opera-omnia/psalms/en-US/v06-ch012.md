# Exposition of Psalm 132

**1.** [vv. 1-2.] It would indeed have been just, dearly beloved, that we should rather hear my brother and colleague, present to us all. And now he did not refuse, but deferred it: and so I make this known to your charity, that you may hold him to his promise along with me. But it was not unreasonable to your charity that I should be the first to obey his bidding: for he forced me to be now his hearer, on this condition indeed, that I too be his, since in that very love we are all his hearers, who is the one Master in heaven for us[^1]. So apply your mind to the Psalm, which, as you know, we have to treat in order. It too is headed *A Song of ascents*; and somewhat longer than the others under the same title. So let us not delay, except where necessity compels; that we may be able, if the Lord permits, to expound the whole. For you too ought not to hear everything as though untaught; you ought to help us somewhat from past hearings, that it not be necessary for everything to be said by us as though new. And new indeed we ought to be, because oldness ought not to creep upon us; but we must also grow and make progress. For of this very progress the apostle says: *Even though our outer man is corrupted, yet the inner is renewed day by day*[^2]. Let us not so make progress that from new we become old, but let the newness itself grow.

**2.** *Remember, O Lord, David, and all his meekness. As he swore to the Lord, vowed a vow to the God of Jacob*[^3]. David, according to the truth of the things done, was one man, king of Israel, the son of Jesse. He was indeed meek, as the divine Scripture shows and commends him, and so meek that he did not repay even his persecutor Saul evil for evil. And he kept toward him such great humility that he confessed him to be the king, but himself a dog: and not insolently nor proudly, though he was more powerful in God, did he answer the king; but he sought rather to appease him by humility than to provoke him by pride. Saul too was given into his power, and this by the Lord God, that he might do to him whatever he wished: but because it had not been commanded him to kill, but he had only been given into his power (and it is allowed a man to use his own power); he turned what God had granted him rather to gentleness. For if he wished to kill, he would be rid of his enemy: but how would he say: Forgive me my debts, as I also forgive my debtors[^4]? Saul entered the cave where David was, not knowing that David was there: he entered for the needs of the belly. David rose gently and slowly behind him, cut off a little piece from his garment, that he might show it to him, that he might know that he had had him in his power; and that he had spared him not from necessity, but from will, and had been unwilling to kill him[^5]. Perhaps it is that very meekness he commended just now, saying: *Remember, O Lord, David, and all his meekness.* This according to the truth of the things done, which the divine Scripture, as we said, contains. But we are accustomed in the Psalms not to attend to the letter, as in all prophecy, but through the letter to search out mysteries. And your charity remembers that we are accustomed to hear in all the Psalms the voice of a certain man; who is one having a head and a body. But the head is in heaven, the body on earth: but where the head has gone before, the body too is to follow. And I no longer say who is the head, who are the body; because I speak to those who know.

**3.** So the humility of David is commended, the meekness of David is commended; and it is said to God: *Remember, O Lord, David, and all his meekness.* For what purpose *remember, O Lord, David*? *As he swore to the Lord, vowed a vow to the God of Jacob.* So remember for this, that he may fulfill what he promised. David himself vowed as though having it in his power, and asks God to fulfill what he vowed: there is the devotion of one vowing, but there is the humility of one praying. Let no one presume by his own strength to render what he has vowed: he who exhorts you to vow, he himself helps you to render. So let us see what he vowed, and from this we understand how David is to be taken in the figure. Now 'David' is interpreted 'Strong in hand.' For he was a great warrior. Presuming indeed upon the Lord his God, he finished all his wars, laid low all his enemies, with God helping him, as the dispensation of that kingdom required; yet prefiguring a certain one strong in hand for warring down the enemies, the devil and his angels. But these enemies the Church wars down. And how does she war them down? By meekness. For by meekness our King himself conquered the devil. He raged; the other endured: he was conquered who raged; he conquered who endured. In this meekness the body of Christ, which is the Church, conquers its enemies. Let it be strong in hand, let it conquer by working. But since the body of Christ is both a temple, and a house, and a city; and he who is the head of the body is both the inhabitant of the house, and the sanctifier of the temple, and the king of the city: as the Church is all those, so Christ is all these. What, then, have we vowed to God, except that we be the temple of God? We can offer him nothing more pleasing than to say to him what is said in Isaiah: Possess us[^6]. Indeed, in these earthly possessions it is the head of the household who is benefited, when the possession is given to him: not so is the possession which is the Church; the possession itself is benefited, that it be possessed by such a one.

**4.** [vv. 3-5] What, then, does he say: *As he swore to the Lord, vowed a vow to the God of Jacob*? Let us see what vow. To swear is to promise firmly. Attend to this vow, that is, what he had vowed, with what ardor he vowed it, with what love, with what longing; and yet, to fulfill it, he prayed to the Lord, saying: *Remember, O Lord, David, and all his meekness.* In this meekness he vowed a vow, that he might be the house of God: *If I shall enter into the tabernacle of my house, if I shall go up onto the couch of my bed. If I shall give sleep to my eyes.* It seemed too little to say, *sleep to my eyes: And slumber to my eyelids; and rest to my temples*[^7], if I shall give them, *until I find a place for the Lord, a tabernacle for the God of Jacob.* Where did he seek a place for the Lord? If he was meek, he sought it in himself. For what is the place of the Lord? Hear the prophet: *Upon whom will my Spirit rest? Upon the humble, and quiet, and trembling at my words*[^8]. Do you wish to be the place of the Lord? Be humble, and quiet, and trembling at the words of God, and you yourself will become what you seek. For if there is not done in you what you seek, what does it profit you in another? God indeed sometimes works through an evangelist only the salvation of another, if the evangelist says and does not do; and there comes to be, through his tongue, a place for the Lord in another, but he himself does not become a place for the Lord. But he who does well the things he teaches, and so teaches, becomes a place for the Lord together with the one whom he teaches: because all who believe make one place for the Lord. For the Lord has a place in the heart; because there is one heart of all who are joined in love.

**5.** How many thousands believed, my brothers, when they laid the prices of their goods at the feet of the apostles[^9]! But what does Scripture say of them? Surely they became the temple of God; not only the temple of God individually, but all of them together the temple of God. So they became a place for the Lord. And that you may know that one place was made for the Lord in them all, Scripture says: *They had one soul and one heart toward God*[^10]. But many, that they may not make a place for the Lord, seek their own things, love their own things, rejoice in their own power, covet their private property. But he who wishes to make a place for the Lord ought to rejoice not in the private, but in the common. This they did with their private goods; they made them common. What they had as their own, did they lose it? If they alone had it, and each one had his own; he would have only this, which he had as his own: but when he made common what was his own; the things too that were the others' became his own. Let your charity attend: because on account of those things which we possess individually, there arise lawsuits, enmities, discords, wars among men, tumults, dissensions against one another, scandals, sins, iniquities, murders. On account of what? On account of those very things which we possess individually. Do we quarrel on account of those things which we possess in common? This air we breathe in common, the sun we all see in common. Blessed, then, are those who so make a place for the Lord that they do not rejoice in their private property. So such a one he was describing, who said: *If I shall enter into the tabernacle of my house.* This was private. He knew that by the private he was hindered from making a place for the Lord, and he mentions the things that belonged to himself: I will not enter *into the tabernacle of my house, until I find*: what? when you have found a place for the Lord, will you enter into your tabernacle? Or will that very place be your tabernacle, where you have found a place for the Lord? Why? Because you yourself will be the place of the Lord, and with those who will be a place for the Lord, you will be one.

**6.** Let us, then, brothers, abstain from the possession of private property; or from the love of it, if we cannot from the possession; and we make a place for the Lord. It is much for me, someone says. But see who you are, about to make a place for the Lord. If some senator wished to have lodging at your house—I do not say a senator, but the procurator of some great man according to the world—and said: Something in your house offends me; even if you loved this thing, you would yet take it away, that you might not offend him, for whose friendship you were striving. And what does the friendship of a man profit you? Perhaps there you would find not only no help, but even danger. For many were not in danger before they were joined to the great; they longed for the friendships of the great, only to run into greater dangers. Securely long for the friendship of Christ: he wishes to lodge with you; make a place for him. What is, make a place for him? Do not love yourself, love him. If you love yourself, you shut yourself against him; if you love him, you open to him: but if you open and he enters, you will not perish by loving yourself, but you will be found with the one who loves you.

**7.** *If I shall enter into the tabernacle of my house, if I shall go up onto the couch of my bed.* For the private property itself, where a man rests, makes men proud; therefore he said: *If I shall go up.* In the thing which each one possesses as private, it must be that he is proud: for from it a man stretches himself against another man, when both are flesh. What is man, brothers? Flesh. And what is another man? Other flesh. And yet the rich flesh stretches itself against the poor flesh; as though that flesh brought anything when it was born, or took anything away when it dies. To this end it had more, that it might swell more. But this man who wishes to find a place for the Lord says: *If I shall go up onto the couch of my bed.*

**8.** *If I shall give sleep to my eyes.* For many, when they sleep, do not make a place for the Lord. Such the apostle rouses: *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead; and Christ will give you light*[^11]; and in another place: *But let us who are of the day be sober and watchful: for those who sleep, sleep by night; and those who are drunk, are drunk by night*[^12]. By night he means iniquity, in which those fall asleep by coveting these earthly things. And all those happinesses of the world that are seen are the dreams of those who sleep. And just as one who sees treasures in his sleep is rich while sleeping; but he will wake, and be poor: so all these vain things of this world, in which men rejoice, they rejoice in while asleep: they will wake when they do not wish, if they do not now wake while it is useful; and they will find that those were dreams, and have passed away, as Scripture says, *like the dream of one rising*[^13]: and in another place: *They have slept their sleep, and all the men of riches have found nothing in their hands*[^14]. *They have slept their sleep*, the sleep is finished, *and they have found nothing in their hands*; because in their sleep they were seeing transitory riches. So this man who wishes to find a place for the Lord said also: *If I shall give sleep to my eyes.* But there are some who do not sleep, but doze. They draw themselves back somewhat from the love of temporal things, and again roll back into it; like those dozing, they frequently nod the head. Wake up, shake off sleep; by dozing you are about to fall. The Psalm does not wish him who wishes to find a place for the Lord to give sleep to his eyes, nor slumber to his eyelids.

**9.** *And rest*, he says, *to my temples.* From the rest of the temples sleep comes to the eyes. The temples are about the eyes. As a coming sleep, there is a heaviness of the temples. For in men about to sleep the temples begin to grow heavy; and when they have felt their temples grow heavy, sleep is already imminent: which, if it is to be given to the eyes, they give rest to their temples, and sleep comes; if they do not give rest to their temples, sleep does not come. So when something temporal has begun to delight you toward sin, your temples are already heavy. Do you wish to wake, and not to sleep, nor to doze? Do not trust yourself to such delight; it will have greater pains than sweetnesses. By this thought, as it were rubbing your brow, you shake off sleep, and prepare a place for the Lord.

**10.** *Until I find a place for the Lord, a tabernacle for the God of Jacob.* Although sometimes the tabernacle of God is called the house of God, and the house of God the tabernacle of God: yet, more distinctly, dearest brothers, the tabernacle is taken to be the Church according to this present time; but the house is the Church, the heavenly Jerusalem, where we will go. For a tabernacle is as it were of those campaigning and fighting: the tabernacles of soldiers are in array, on expedition; whence soldiers are called tent-companions (contubernales), as it were having and dwelling in the same tents. So as long as we have an enemy with whom we may fight, we make a tabernacle for God. But when the time of fighting has passed, and there has come that peace which surpasses all understanding, as the apostle says: *And the peace of Christ which surpasses all understanding*[^15]; for however much you think of that peace, the mind, still set in this heaviness of the body, grasps it less: when that homeland has come, it will now be a house, no adversary will tempt, so that it should be called a tabernacle. We will not go forth to fight, but will remain to praise. For what is said of that house? *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^16]. In the tabernacle we still groan, in the house we will praise. Why? Because groaning belongs to those on pilgrimage, praise to those now abiding in the homeland. Here first let a tabernacle be sought for the God of Jacob.

**11**. [v. 6.] *Behold, we heard of it in Ephrathah*[^17]. Of what *it*? The seat of the Lord. *We heard of it in Ephrathah. We found it in the fields of the wood.* Did he hear there, where he found; or did he hear in one place, and find in another? Let us, then, ask what *Ephrathah* is, where he heard; likewise let us ask what is *in the fields of the wood*, where he found. 'Ephrathah,' a Hebrew word, is interpreted in Latin 'Mirror,' as those have handed down who translated the Hebrew words set in the Scriptures into another tongue, that the meaning might reach us. For from Hebrew they translated into Greek, and from Greek they were translated for us into Latin. For there were those who kept watch over the Scriptures. If, then, Ephrathah is 'mirror,' that house which was found in the fields of the wood was heard of in a mirror. A mirror has an image: all prophecy is an image of things to come. So the future house of God was foretold in the image of prophecy. For we heard of it in a mirror, that is: *We heard of it in Ephrathah. We found it in the fields of the wood.* What are the fields of the wood? The fields of the forests. For it is not as is commonly said: That estate (saltus), for example, having so many centuries of land. A 'saltus' properly is a place still uncultivated and wooded. For some manuscripts have, *in the fields of the forest.* What, then, were the fields of the wood, except the uncultivated nations? what were the fields of the wood, except where there were still the brambles of idolatry? Yet just as there were brambles of idolatry there, there we found a place for the Lord, a tabernacle for the God of Jacob. What *we heard of in Ephrathah, we found in the fields of the wood*; what was foretold in an image to the Jews was made manifest in the faith of the Gentiles.

**12.** [v. 7.] *We will enter into his tabernacles*[^18]: whose? The Lord God of Jacob's. Those who enter in order to dwell, they are the ones who enter in order to be dwelt in. Into your house you enter that you may dwell, into the house of God that you may be dwelt in. For better is the Lord, who, when he begins to dwell in you, will make you blessed. For if you are not dwelt in by him, you will be wretched. That son who said: *Give me the portion of the patrimony that falls to me*[^19], wished to be in his own power. It was well kept with the father, that it might not be squandered with harlots. He received it, it came into his power; setting out into a far country, he poured out all with harlots. At last he suffered hunger, remembered his father; he returned, that he might be filled with bread. So enter, that you may be dwelt in; and not as though you were your own, but his: *We will enter into his tabernacles.*

**13.** *We will adore in the place where his feet stood*[^20]. Whose feet? The Lord's, or those of the very house of the Lord? For it is the house of the Lord where he says it ought to be adored. *We will adore in the place where his feet stood.* Outside his house God does not hear unto eternal life. For he belongs to the house of God who is framed together in love with the living stones. But he who has not love makes a collapse; and when he collapses the house stands. For let no one threaten the house where he has begun to be as it were a stone, if he should wish to collapse, as though he did some harm to the house. For so too the first people of the Jews had grown proud, saying: For God will not defraud our father Abraham, to whom he promised such great things of his seed: and they did all evil things as though secure of the promise of God, that not for the merits of those who committed crimes, but for the merits of Abraham he would spare them, and gather the sons of Abraham, however evil, into his house unto eternal life. But what does John say? *Brood*, he says, *of vipers*. When the very sons of Abraham came to him, to be baptized in the water of repentance, he did not say to them: Brood of Abraham, but *of vipers.* For they were such as those whom they imitated; not sons of Abraham, but sons of the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Girgashite, the Jebusite, and of all who offended God: they were their sons, because they followed their deeds. *Brood of vipers, who has shown you to flee from the wrath to come? Bring forth, then, fruit worthy of repentance. And do not say: We have Abraham for our father. For God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*[^21]. I know not what stones he saw then in the fields of the wood, from which sons of Abraham were raised up. For they are sons rather who imitated his faith, than who were born of his flesh. So let no one threaten the house of God, as though to say: I withdraw myself, and the house will collapse. For it is good for him that he be built in, and have love. For if he himself collapses, the house will stand. And so, brothers, the house of God is in those whom he predestined and foreknew would persevere. Of them it was said: *Where his feet stood.* For there are those who do not persevere, nor do his feet stand in them. They are not, then, the Church; they do not belong to that tabernacle now, then house. But where did his feet stand? *Because iniquity has abounded, the love of many will grow cold*. In those in whom love grows cold, his feet do not stand. But what did he go on to say? *But he who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^22]. Behold those in whom his feet stood: in that place adore, that is, be of such as those in whom the feet of the Lord stood.

**14**. But if you wish to understand it of the house itself: *Where his feet stood*, of the house; let your feet stand in Christ. But your feet will then stand, if you persevere in Christ. For what is said of the devil? *He was a murderer from the beginning, and did not stand in the truth*[^23]. So the feet of the devil did not stand. Likewise of the proud, what does he say? *Let not the foot of pride come to me, and let not the hand of sinners move me. There they fell who work iniquity; they were driven out, and could not stand*[^24]. So the house of God is one whose feet stand. Whence that rejoicing John, what does he say? *He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him.* If he does not stand, he does not hear him. *And he rejoices with joy on account of the voice of the bridegroom*[^25]. Deservedly he stands, because he rejoices on account of the voice of the bridegroom: for if he rejoiced on account of his own voice, he would fall. So now you see why those have fallen who rejoice at their own voice. For that friend of the bridegroom said: *This is he who baptizes*[^26]. Certain men say: We baptize: rejoicing at their own voice, they could not stand; and they do not belong to that house of which it is said: *Where his feet stood.*

**15.** [v. 8.] *Arise, O Lord, into your rest*[^27]. To the sleeping Lord he says: *Arise.* You already know who slept, and who rose again. For he himself says in a certain place in a psalm: *I slept troubled*[^28]. Well is it said to him: *Arise, O Lord, into your rest.* You will no longer be troubled; because *Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^29]. It is his voice in another psalm: *I slept and took rest, and rose up, because the Lord will receive me*[^30]. He himself slept, to him it is said: *Arise, O Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your sanctification*: that is, arise, that the ark of your sanctification too may arise, which you have sanctified. He himself is our head; his ark, his Church: he rose first, the Church too will rise. But the body would not dare to promise itself resurrection, unless the head first rose. *Arise, O Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your sanctification.* And the body of Christ which was born of Mary has been understood by some as the ark of sanctification; so that he would be saying this: *Arise, O Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your sanctification.* Arise with your body, that those who did not believe may touch it. *Arise, O Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your sanctification.*

**16**. [v. 9.] *Let your priests be clothed with justice, and let your saints rejoice*[^31]. When you arise from the dead, and go to the Father, let that royal priesthood be clothed with faith[^32], because the just man lives by faith[^33]; and, having received the pledge of the Holy Spirit, let the members rejoice in the hope of the resurrection, which went before in the head: for the apostle says to them: *Rejoicing in hope*[^34].

**17.** [v. 10.] *For the sake of David your servant, do not turn away the face of your Christ*[^35]. This was said to God the Father: *For the sake of David your servant, do not turn away the face of your Christ.* The Lord was crucified in Judea; he was crucified by the Jews; troubled by them, he slept. Among whose raging hands he slept, he rose to judge them: and he says in a certain place: *And raise me up, and I will repay them*[^36]. And he repaid, and is to repay. For how great things the Jews afterward suffered, when the Lord was slain, they themselves know. From the very city where they slew him, all were driven out. What then? did all even of the stock of David perish, and of the tribe of Judah? No: for some of them believed, and many thousands of men of them believed, and this now after the resurrection of the Lord. They raged, to crucify him, and afterward they began to see miracles done in the name of the crucified one; and they trembled the more that the name of him could do so much, who as it were in their hands could do nothing; and, pricked in heart, now believing that a hidden divinity had been in him whom they had thought like other men, and asking counsel of the apostles[^37], they heard: *Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^38]. So because Christ rose to judge those by whom he was crucified, and turned away his face from the Jews, and turned it to the Gentiles; God is as it were entreated for the sake of the remnant of Israel, and it is said to him: *For the sake of David your servant, do not turn away the face of your Christ.* If the chaff is condemned, let the grains be gathered. *Let the remnant be saved*[^39], as Isaiah says. And plainly the remnant were saved: from there were the twelve apostles, from there more than five hundred brothers to whom the Lord showed himself after the resurrection[^40]; from there so many thousands baptized, who laid the prices of their goods before the feet of the apostles[^41]. So there was fulfilled what here God was entreated: *For the sake of David your servant, do not turn away the face of your Christ.*

**18.** [v. 11.] *The Lord swore the truth to David, and he will not repent of it*[^42]. What is, he swore? He confirmed by himself the thing promised. What is, *he will not repent of it*? He will not change it. For God does not suffer the pain of repentance, or is deceived in anything, so as to wish to correct that in which he erred. But just as a man, when he repents, wishes to change what he did; so where you hear that God repents, hope for that very change. God works it otherwise, although he calls it by the name of repentance, otherwise than you: for you do it because you had erred; but he does it because he avenges, or because he frees. He changed the kingdom of Saul, when he repented, as it was said: and in that very place where Scripture says: *He repented*; there a little after it is said: *For he is not as a man, that he should repent*[^43]. So when he changes his works by his unchangeable counsel; on account of that change, not of counsel, but of work, he is said to repent. But this he so promised, that he would not change it. Just as it is said also that: *The Lord swore, and he will not repent of it: You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek*[^44]: so this too, because it was so promised, that it would not be changed, because it was necessarily to come to pass and to remain; he said: *The Lord swore the truth to David, and he will not repent of it: Of the fruit of your womb I will set upon your throne.* He could have said: Of the fruit of your loins: why did he wish to say: *Of the fruit of the womb*? And that too, if he said it, he would say truly: but he wished to say more significantly: *Of the fruit of the womb*; because Christ was born of a woman, whom a man did not approach.

**19**. [v. 12.] What then? *The Lord swore the truth to David: Of the fruit of your womb I will set upon your throne. If your sons shall keep my covenant, and these my testimonies which I shall teach them; their sons too shall sit forever upon your throne*[^45]. If your sons shall keep, their sons too shall sit forever. The parents earn merit for the sons. What if his sons should keep, and their sons should not keep? Why is the blessedness of the sons promised to the merit of the fathers? For what does he say: *If your sons shall keep, their sons too shall sit forever* (he did not say: If your sons shall keep, they shall sit upon your throne; and if their sons shall keep, they too shall sit upon your throne: but he said: *If your sons shall keep, their sons too shall sit upon your throne*), except that he wished the sons here to be understood as their fruit? *If your sons*, he says, *shall keep my law, and your sons shall observe these my commandments which I shall teach them*; *their sons too shall sit upon your throne*: that is, this will be their fruit, that they sit upon your throne. For now, brothers, all we who labor in Christ, all we who tremble at his words, who in whatever way try to do his will, and groan asking him to help us, that we may fulfill what he commands; do we already sit on those seats of blessedness which are promised us? No: but holding to the commandments we hope this will come to pass. That very hope is spoken of as in sons; because the hope of a man living in this life is his sons, his fruit is his sons. For this reason men too, excusing their avarice, say that they keep for their sons what they keep; and unwilling to give to the needy, they excuse themselves under the name of devotion, because their sons are their hope. For all men who live according to this world say that their hope is to beget and leave sons. Therefore under the name of sons he set that very hope, and said: *If your sons shall keep my covenant, and these my testimonies which I shall teach them; their sons too shall sit forever upon your throne*: that is, they will have such fruits, that hope will not deceive them, that they may come there where they hope to come. So now they are as it were fathers, men of future hope: but when they have obtained what they hope for, they are sons; because that which they obtain, they have brought forth and begotten in good works. And this is kept for them in the time to come, because posterity itself is accustomed to be called by the name of sons.

**20.** Or if you take the sons themselves as men, understand it said of them too. *If your sons shall keep my covenant, and these my testimonies which I shall teach them*; so that this is the sense: *If your sons shall keep my covenant, and these my testimonies which I shall teach them, and their sons*, that is, if they shall keep them; so that here you make a pause, and then add, *they shall sit forever upon your throne*, that is, both your sons and their sons, but all, if they shall keep them. What then if they do not keep them? has the promise of God perished? No: but it was said and promised for this reason, because God foresaw; what, except that they would believe? But lest anyone should as it were threaten the promises of God, and wish to put it in his own power whether what God promised would be fulfilled; therefore he also said *he swore*: in which he showed that it will without doubt come to pass. How, then, did he set there: *If they shall keep*? Lest you now boast of the promises, and let yourself not keep them. Then you will be a son of David, if you keep them; but if not, you will not be a son of David. To the sons of David God promised. Do not say: I am a son of David, if you degenerate. If the Jews, who are born of that very stock, do not say it (rather, they do say it, but they rave. For the Lord plainly says: *If you are the sons of Abraham, do the deeds of Abraham*[^46]. Thence he denied that they were sons, because they did not do the deeds); how do we say that we are sons of David, who are not of his stock according to the flesh? It remains that we are not sons except by imitating his faith, except by worshipping God as he worshipped. If, then, what you do not hope for through stock, you are unwilling to attain through deeds; how will it be fulfilled in you that you will sit upon the throne of David? and if it is not fulfilled in you, do you think it will not be fulfilled? And how did he find it in the fields of the wood? and how did his feet stand? Whatever, then, you may be, that house will stand.

**21.** [v. 13.] *Because the Lord has chosen Zion; he has chosen it beforehand for a dwelling for himself*[^47]. Zion itself is the Church, it is also that Jerusalem to whose peace we run, which is a pilgrim not in the angels, but in us, which from its better part awaits the part that is to return; from which the Letters have come to us, which are daily recited. That very city is Zion, which the Lord has chosen beforehand.

**22**. [v. 14.] *This is my rest forever and ever*[^48]. Now they are the words of God. *My rest*; there I rest. How God loves us, brothers, that because we rest, he says that he rests! For he himself is never troubled, nor rests in that way; but he says that he rests there, because we will have rest in him. *Here I will dwell, because I have chosen it beforehand.*

**23.** [v. 15.] *Blessing I will bless her widow, and I will fill her poor with loaves*[^49]. Every soul that understands itself to be deserted by all help, except that of God alone, is a widow. For how does the apostle describe a widow? *But she who is truly a widow and desolate has hoped in the Lord*[^50]. But he was speaking of those widows whom we all so call in the Church. But he had said: *She who lives in pleasures is dead while living*; and he did not count her among the widows. But describing the holy widows, what does he say? *But she who is truly a widow and desolate has hoped in the Lord, and continues in prayers and supplications night and day.* There he added: *But she who lives in pleasures is dead while living*[^51]. Whence, then, is she a widow? Because she has no help from anywhere, except from God alone. Those who have husbands are as it were proud of the help of their husbands: widows seem deserted, and their help is stronger. So the whole Church is one widow, whether in men, or in women, whether in the married, or in married women, whether in the young, or in the old, or in virgins: the whole Church is one widow, deserted in this world, if she feels it, if she knows her widowhood; for then help is at hand for her. Do you not recognize this widow in the Gospel, my brothers, when the Lord said that one ought always to pray, and not to faint? *There was a certain judge in a city*, he said, *who neither feared God nor reverenced man; and a certain widow appealed to him daily, and said: Avenge me of my adversary.* And by appealing daily the widow wore him down. *For the judge himself said within himself, who neither feared God nor reverenced man: Even though I do not fear God, nor am abashed before man, yet on account of the weariness which this widow gives me, I will avenge her*[^52]. If a wicked judge heard the widow, that he might not suffer weariness; will God not hear the Church, whom he exhorts to entreat him?

**24.** Likewise: *I will fill her poor with loaves*: what is this, brothers? Let us be poor, and then we will be filled. Many, presuming upon the world and proud, are Christians; they adore Christ, but are not filled: for they are filled, and abound in their pride. Of such it is said: *A reproach to those who abound, and contempt for the proud*[^53]: these abound; and therefore they eat, but are not filled. And what was said of them in the psalm? *All the rich of the earth ate and adored*[^54]. They adore Christ, they venerate Christ, they supplicate Christ; but they are not filled with his wisdom and justice. Why? Because they are not poor. But the poor, that is, the humble of heart, the more they hunger, the more they eat; and the more they hunger, the more they are empty of the world. He who is full rejects whatever you are about to give him; because he is full. Give me a hungry man, give me those of whom it was said: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^55]; and these will be the poor of whom he now says: *And I will fill her poor with loaves.* Because in that very psalm where it was said: *All the rich of the earth ate and adored*; it was said there too of the poor, and exactly in the way it is said in this psalm: *The poor shall eat, and shall be filled, and shall praise the Lord, those who seek him*[^56]. Where it was said: *All the rich of the earth ate and adored*; there it was said: *The poor shall eat, and shall be filled.* Why is it that, when the rich were said to have adored, they were not said to be filled; but when the poor were spoken of, they were said to be filled? And whence are they filled? this fullness, brothers, of what kind is it? God himself is the bread. The bread, that it might become milk for us, came down to the earth; and he said to his own: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^57]. Therefore there in the psalm: *The poor shall eat, and shall be filled.* With what shall they be satisfied? Hear what follows: *And shall praise the Lord, those who seek him.*

**25.** So be poor, be among the members of that widow, let your help be in the one God alone. Money is nothing; you will not have help from it. Many have been cast headlong on account of money, many have perished on account of money: many on account of much money were sought out by robbers; they would be safe, if they had not the reason why they should be sought. Many presumed upon more powerful friends: those upon whom they presumed fell, and dragged down with them those who presumed upon them. Look at the examples of the human race. What great thing is it that is told you? We do not speak of these things only from these Scriptures; read them in the world. Take care not to presume upon money, upon a man as friend, upon honor and the boasting of the world. Take away all these: but if you have them, give thanks to God if you despise them. But if you are puffed up by them, do not wait until you are the prey of men; you are already the prey of the devil. But if you do not presume upon these things, you will be among the members of that widow, who is the Church, of whom it was said: *Blessing I will bless her widow*: you will also be poor, of such as those of whom it was said: *And I will fill her poor with loaves.*

**26.** But sometimes—which must not be passed over in the saying—you find a proud poor man, and a humble rich man: we suffer such daily. You hear a poor man as it were groaning under a rich man, and when the more powerful rich man oppresses him, then you see him humble: sometimes not even then, but proud even then; whence you see what he would be, if he had something. So the poor man of God is in the soul, not in the purse. Sometimes a man goes forth having a full house, fertile lands, many estates, much gold and silver, knows that one must not presume upon them, humbles himself to God, does good with them; so his heart is raised to God, that he knows that those riches not only profit him nothing, but even hinder his feet, unless God rules and God comes to his aid: and he is counted among the poor who are filled with loaves. You find another, a beggar, puffed up, or not puffed up only because he has nothing, yet seeking something to be puffed up by. God does not attend to the means, but to the desire: and he judges him according to the desire by which he gapes after temporal things, not according to the means, which it does not fall to him to attain. Whence of the rich the apostle says: *Command the rich of this world not to be proud-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, who provides us all things abundantly to enjoy.* What, then, should they do with their riches? It follows and says: *Let them be rich in good works; let them give readily, share*. And see that here they are poor: *Let them lay up*, he says, *for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^58]. When they have laid hold of it, then they will be rich; but while they do not yet have it, let them know themselves to be poor. So it comes about that all the humble of heart, set in the twofold love, whatever they have in this world, God counts among his poor, whom he fills with loaves.

**27.** [vv. 16-17.] *Her priests I will clothe with salvation, and her saints will exult with exultation*[^59]. We are now at the end of the Psalm; let your charity attend a little. *Her priests I will clothe with salvation, and her saints will exult with exultation.* Who is our salvation, except our Christ? What, then, is: *Her priests I will clothe with salvation*? *As many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ*[^60]. *And her saints will exult with exultation.* Whence *will they exult with exultation*? Because they are clothed with salvation: not in themselves. For they were made light, but in the Lord; for before they were darkness[^61]. And therefore he added: *There I will raise up a horn for David*[^62]: that one may presume upon Christ, that will be the height of David. For the horn signifies height: and what kind of height? Not fleshly. Therefore all the bones are wrapped in flesh: the horn goes beyond the flesh. Spiritual height is the horn. And what is spiritual height, except to presume upon Christ; not to say: I do it, I baptize; but: This is he who baptizes? There is the horn of David. And that you may know that there is the horn of David, attend to what follows: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ.* What is the lamp? You already know the words of the Lord about John: *He was a burning and shining lamp*[^63]. And what does John say? *This is he who baptizes*[^64]. In this, then, the saints will exult, in this the priests will exult; because all that is good in them is not theirs, but his who has the power of baptizing. So he comes securely to his temple, everyone who has received Baptism; because it is not man's, but his in whom the horn of David has been raised up.

**28.** [v. 18.] *But upon him my sanctification will flourish*[^65]. Upon whom? Upon my Christ. For when he says *my Christ*, it is the voice of the Father, who says: *Blessing I will bless her widow, and I will fill her poor with loaves. Her priests I will clothe with salvation, and her saints will exult with exultation.* He who says: *There I will raise up a horn for David*, is God. He himself says: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ*, because he is both our Christ, and the Father's Christ: he is our Christ, when he saves us and rules us, as he is also our Lord, but the Son of the Father; he is Christ both ours and the Father's. For if he were not the Father's Christ, it would not have been said above: *For the sake of David your servant, do not turn away the face of your Christ.* *But upon him my sanctification will flourish.* Upon Christ it flourishes. Let no man take it to himself, because he himself sanctifies; otherwise it will not be true: *But upon him my sanctification will flourish.* The glory of sanctification will flourish. So the sanctification of Christ is in Christ himself, the power of the sanctification of God is in Christ. For what he said, *it will flourish*, he wishes to pertain to glory: for when trees flourish, then they are beautiful. So sanctification is in Baptism; thence it flourishes and is bright. Whence has the world yielded to this beauty? Because it flourishes in Christ: for set it in the power of man; how does it flourish, when all flesh is hay, and all the glory of the flesh is as the flower of hay[^66]?
