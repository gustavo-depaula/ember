# Exposition of Psalm 122

**1.** Just as unclean love inflames the soul and summons it, doomed to perish, toward coveting earthly things and chasing after things that pass away, and casts it down into the depths and plunges it into the abyss, so holy love lifts it to the things above, sets it aflame for things eternal, and rouses the soul toward those things that neither pass away nor die, and lifts it from the depth of hell to heaven. Yet every love has its own force, nor can love lie idle in the soul of one who loves; it must lead somewhere. But do you wish to know what kind of love it is? Look where it leads. We are not, then, urging you to love nothing, but urging you not to love the world, so that you may freely love him who made the world. For a soul bound by earthly love has, as it were, birdlime on its wings; it cannot fly. But once it is cleansed of the foulest affections of this world, then, with its wings spread and its two pinions freed from every hindrance—that is, the two commandments of the love of God and the love of neighbor—it flies. Where does it fly, if not by rising up to God in its flight, since it rises by loving? Before it is able to do so, it groans on earth, if the desire to fly is already within it, and it says: *Who will give me wings like a dove, and I will fly away and be at rest*[^1]? But where will it fly, if not away from the midst of scandals, where even this man was groaning whose voice this is that I have called to mind? Away, then, from the midst of scandals, away from mingling with evil men, away from the chaff with which the grain is mingled, it wishes to fly, where it need not endure union and fellowship with any wicked man, but may live in the holy fellowship of the angels who are citizens of the eternal Jerusalem.

**2.** [v. 1.] This psalm, then, which today we have undertaken to expound to your holiness, longs for that very Jerusalem—that is, this man who in this psalm ascends; for it is a *Song of ascents*, which we have often told your charity is not a song of those going down but of those going up. This man, then, wishes to ascend. And where does he wish to ascend, if not to heaven? What does 'to heaven' mean? Does he wish to ascend in order to be with the sun and the moon and the stars? Far from it. Rather, in heaven there is the eternal Jerusalem, where our fellow citizens, the angels, dwell; from those very fellow citizens of ours we are pilgrims on earth. In our pilgrimage we sigh; in the city we shall rejoice. But in this pilgrimage we have also found companions who have already seen that city and who invite us to run toward it. Over these this man rejoices, who also says: *I rejoiced at those who said to me: We will go into the house of the Lord*[^2]. Brothers, let it come to your charity's mind, if perhaps some festival of the martyrs and some holy place is named, where on a fixed day the crowds flow together to celebrate the solemnity—how those crowds rouse one another, how they exhort one another and say: Let us go, let us go. And they ask: Where shall we go? And the answer is given: To that place, to the holy place. They speak to one another, and as if set on fire they make, one by one, a single flame; and that single flame, made out of the conversation of those who kindle one another, sweeps them away to the holy place, and the holy thought sanctifies them. If, then, holy love sweeps men away like this to a temporal place, what kind of love must that be which sweeps men away, all in one accord, to heaven, saying to one another: *We will go into the house of the Lord*? Let us run, then, let us run, for we will go into the house of the Lord. Let us run, and let us not grow weary, because we shall arrive there where we shall not be wearied. Let us run into the house of the Lord, and let our soul rejoice in those who say these things to us. For those who say these things to us saw the homeland before us, and from afar they cry out to those who come after: *We will go into the house of the Lord*; walk, run. The apostles saw it, and they said to us: Run, walk, follow; *We will go into the house of the Lord*. And what does each one of us say? *I rejoiced at those who said to me: We will go into the house of the Lord*. I rejoiced in the prophets, I rejoiced in the apostles. For all of these said to us: *We will go into the house of the Lord*.

**3.** [v. 2.] *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*[^3]. See, you have the house of the Lord, if you were asking what the house of the Lord is. In that house of the Lord, the one who built the house is praised: he himself is the delight of all who dwell in the house; he himself is the sole hope here and the reality there. So then, those who run, what ought they to think? That they are, as it were, already there and standing there. For it is a great thing to stand there among the angels and not to fail. For he who fell from there did not stand in the truth[^4]. Whoever have not fallen stand in the truth; and he stands who enjoys God, while he who wishes to enjoy himself falls. But who wishes to enjoy himself? He who is proud. Therefore that man who always wished to stand in the courts of Jerusalem said: *In your light we shall see light*[^5]—not 'in my light'; and: *With you is the fountain of life*[^6]—not 'with me.' And what did he add? *Let not the foot of pride come to me, and let not the hand of sinners move me. There all those who work iniquity have fallen; they have been driven out and could not stand*[^7]. If, then, those men could not stand because they were proud, ascend in humility, so that you may say: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*. Consider what you will be there; and although you are still on the way, set this before your eyes, as if you were standing there, as if you were already rejoicing without ceasing among the angels, and as if there were fulfilled in you what was said: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; they will praise you forever and ever*[^8]. *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem.* Which Jerusalem? For this present Jerusalem too is usually so called; but this Jerusalem is the shadow of that one. And what is great about standing in this Jerusalem, when this Jerusalem could not stand but was turned into ruin? Is it, then, as something great that the Holy Spirit utters this from the inflamed heart of this lover, when he says: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*? Is it not that Jerusalem to which the Lord said: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those sent to you*[^9]? What great thing, then, was this man coveting—to stand among those who were killing the prophets and stoning those sent to them? Far be it that one who loves like this, who burns like this, who so wishes to come to that Jerusalem our mother, of whom the apostle speaks as *eternal in the heavens*[^10], should think of this present Jerusalem in that way.

**4.** [v. 3.] Hear at last—do not take it on my word; hear what follows, and what Jerusalem it points out to our minds. For when he had said: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem*, as though it were said to him: Of which Jerusalem do you speak? Of which Jerusalem are you speaking? he at once added: *Jerusalem, which is built as a city*[^11]. Brothers, when David was saying these things, that city was complete; it was not being built. I do not know, then, what city he is speaking of, which is now being built, to which living stones run in faith, of whom Peter says: *And you, like living stones, are built up into a spiritual house*[^12]—that is, the holy temple of God. What does it mean, *like living stones be built up*? You live, if you believe; and if you believe, you become the temple of God, since the apostle Paul says: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^13]. This city, then, is now being built; stones are cut from the mountains by the hands of those who preach the truth, and they are squared so as to enter into the everlasting structure. Still many stones are in the craftsman's hands; may they not fall from the craftsman's hands, so that they may be built up complete into the structure of the temple. This, then, is the *Jerusalem which is built as a city*: its foundation is Christ. The apostle Paul says: *For no one can lay any other foundation than that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^14]. When a foundation is laid in the earth, walls are built up above it, and the weight of the walls presses downward, because the foundation is laid below. But if our foundation is in heaven, let us be built toward heaven. Bodies built this structure of this basilica, which you see has risen up so wide; and because bodies built it, they laid the foundation below; but since we are built up spiritually, our foundation has been laid on high. Let us run, then, to where we are built up; for of that very Jerusalem it was said: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem.* But of which Jerusalem? *Of Jerusalem, which is built as a city.* He said too little to show which Jerusalem he meant when he said *it is built as a city*; that could still be understood as the bodily one. For what if someone should stand up and say: Even at the time of David, when these things were being said and sung, that city was already complete; but he saw in the spirit that it would fall and be built again. For that city was conquered, and the captive people were carried over into Babylon, and this is called in the Scriptures the deportation to Babylon[^15]. And Jeremiah prophesied that after seventy years of captivity that city which had been destroyed by those who waged war against it could be rebuilt[^16]. Perhaps someone says: This is what David saw in the spirit—that the city of Jerusalem would fall to those who waged war against it and could again, after seventy years, be rebuilt; and that is why he said: *Jerusalem, which is built as a city*; so do not think that the city is meant which consists of the saints, as of living stones. What follows, to remove all doubt? For he says: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem.* But which Jerusalem do I mean? Is it, he says, this one which you see standing, raised up with bodily walls? No; rather, *Jerusalem, which is built as a city.* Why not 'a city,' but *as a city*—except that this structure of walls, which was in Jerusalem, was a visible city, just as it is properly called a city by everyone, while that other is built as a city, because those too who enter into it are *like living stones*[^17]; for they are not truly stones, are they? As those men are like stones, yet not stones, so that city is like a city, yet not a city; for he said *it is built*. Indeed, by the word 'building' he wished us to understand the structure and framework of bodies and walls. For a city is properly understood as consisting of the men who dwell in it. But he made clear to us that he called the city a town by saying *it is built.* And because a spiritual building has a certain likeness to a bodily building, it is therefore *built as a city.*

**5.** But let what follows speak and take away all doubt, since we must not take it in a fleshly way: *Jerusalem, which is built as a city. Its sharing is in the selfsame*[^18]. Now then, brothers, whoever lifts up the gaze of the mind, whoever lays aside the darkness of the flesh, whoever cleanses the eye of the heart, let him lift it up and see the *selfsame*. What is the *selfsame*? How shall I put it, except as the *selfsame*? Brothers, if you can, understand the *selfsame*. For whatever else I say, I do not say the *selfsame*. Yet let us try, by certain neighboring words and meanings, to lead the weakness of the mind to think about the *selfsame*. What is the *selfsame*? That which is always in the same way; which is not now one thing and now another. What, then, is the *selfsame*, except that which is? What is that which is? That which is eternal. For that which is always now in one way and now in another is not, because it does not abide; it is not altogether nonexistent, but it does not exist in the highest degree. And what is that which is, except he who, when he was sending Moses, said to him: *I am who am*[^19]? What is this, except he who, when his servant said: *Behold, you send me; if the people say to me: Who sent you? what shall I say to them?*[^20], would give no other name than: *I am who am*; and added, and said: *You shall say therefore to the children of Israel: He who is has sent me to you*[^21]. Behold the *selfsame: I am who am: He who is has sent me to you.* You cannot grasp it; it is much to understand, much to lay hold of. Hold on to what was made for your sake, the one whom you could not grasp. Hold on to the flesh of Christ, into which you, sick and left half-dead from the wounds of robbers, were lifted, so that you might be brought to the inn and there be healed[^22]. Let us run, then, to the house of the Lord, and come to the city where our feet may stand—the city *which is built as a city, whose sharing is in the selfsame.* For what ought you to hold on to? That Christ was made for your sake, since he is himself the Christ; and Christ himself is rightly understood as: *I am who am*, in the way that he is in the form of God. There where *he did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^23], there is the selfsame. But that you might be made a sharer in the selfsame, he himself first became a sharer of you; and the Word was made flesh, so that the flesh might share in the Word. And because the Word that was made flesh and dwelt among us[^24] came from the seed of Abraham, and it was promised to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob that in their seed all the nations would be blessed[^25]—and from this we see the Church spread throughout the whole world—God speaks to the weak. He sought firmness of heart when he said: *I am who am*; he sought firmness of heart and a raised keenness of contemplation when he said: *He who is has sent me to you.* But perhaps you do not yet have contemplation: do not fail, do not despair. He who is willed to be a man, as you are; and so he went on to say to Moses, as though Moses were afraid of the name. What name? Which is: *Is. And the Lord said to Moses: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob: this is my name forever*[^26]. Do not despair of yourself because I said: *I am who am*, and: *He who is has sent me to you*; for you now waver, and amid the changeableness of things and the variability of human mortality you cannot perceive what the selfsame is. I am coming down, because you cannot come up. *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.* In the seed of Abraham hope for something, so that you may be strengthened to see the one who came to you in the seed of Abraham.

**6.** This, then, is the selfsame, of which it was said: *You will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the same, and your years will not fail*[^27]. Behold the *selfsame*, whose *years do not fail.* Brothers, do not our years fail daily, and never stand still at all? For those that have come are already no more; and those that are to come are not yet; the former have already failed, and the latter, which are to come, will fail. In this very one day, then, brothers—behold, what we are now speaking is in a moment. The past hours have gone by, the future ones have not yet come; and when they have come, they too will pass and fail. What are the years that do not fail, except those that stand still? If, then, the years stand still there, and those very years that stand still are one year, and that one year that stands still is one day; because that one day has neither rising nor setting, is neither begun from yesterday nor shut off by tomorrow, but that day always stands still—and you call that day what you will: if you wish, it is years; if you wish, it is a day; whatever you think it, still it stands still. Of that very stability that city is a sharer, *whose sharing is in the selfsame*; rightly, then, since it becomes a sharer of that stability, does this man who runs there say: *Our feet were standing in the courts of Jerusalem.* For all things stand still there, where nothing passes away. Do you too wish to stand still there and not pass away? Then run there. No one has the selfsame of himself. Pay attention, brothers. What a body has is not the selfsame, because it does not stand still in itself. It is changed through the ages, changed through changes of place and time, changed through diseases and bodily failings: it does not, then, stand still in itself. The heavenly bodies do not stand still in themselves: they have certain changes of their own, even if hidden; certainly they are moved from place to place, they rise from the east to the west, and again come round to the east: they do not, then, stand still; they are not the selfsame. The human soul itself does not stand still either. By how many changes and thoughts it is varied! by how many pleasures it is altered! by how many desires it is buffeted and torn apart! The very mind of man, which is called rational, is changeable; it is not the selfsame. Now it wills, now it does not will; now it knows, now it does not know; now it remembers, now it forgets: therefore no one has the selfsame of himself. He who wished to have the selfsame of himself, so as to be, as it were, the selfsame to himself, fell: the angel fell, and was made the devil. He poured pride into man, and by envy cast down with himself the one who was standing[^28]. These wished to be the selfsame to themselves; they wished to rule over themselves, to be lords over themselves; they refused to have the true Lord, who truly is the selfsame, to whom it is said: *You will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the same.* Now then, after so much faintness, after so many diseases, difficulties, and labors, let the soul, humbled, return to the selfsame, and let it be in that city *whose sharing is in the selfsame.*

**7.** [v. 4.] *For there the tribes went up.* We were asking where one who has fallen goes up; for we said that it is the voice of a man ascending, of the Church ascending. Do we suppose where he goes up? where he goes? where he is lifted up? *There*, he says, *the tribes went up.* Where did the tribes go up? Into the city *whose sharing is in the selfsame.* There, then, one ascends, into Jerusalem. But the man who was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho fell among robbers[^29]. Had he not gone down, he would not have fallen among robbers. But since by going down he fell among robbers, let him by going up come to the angels. Let him ascend, then, for the tribes ascended. But what are tribes? Many know, many do not know. But let us too who know come down to those who do not know what tribes are, so that they may ascend with us where the tribes ascended. Tribes can by another name be called courts, but not properly. And so tribes cannot properly be called by another single name, but by a neighboring word they are called courts. For if we say 'courts' properly, only those courts are understood that are in individual cities, one to each city; whence 'curials' and 'decurions,' that is, because they are in a court (curia) or in a tenth-part (decuria); and you know that individual cities have individual courts of this kind. But there were, or at some time there used to be, in these cities too, courts of the peoples; and one city has many courts, just as Rome has thirty-five courts of the people. These are called tribes. The people of Israel had twelve of these, according to the sons of Jacob.

**8.** Twelve were the tribes of the people of Israel; but there were evil ones there, and there were good ones there. For how evil were the tribes that crucified the Lord! How good were the tribes that knew the Lord! Those tribes, then, which crucified the Lord, are the tribes of the devil. So when he said here: *For there the tribes went up*, lest you should understand all the tribes, he added: *the tribes of the Lord*[^30]. What does *the tribes of the Lord* mean? Those that knew the Lord. For out of those very twelve evil tribes, there were good ones there from the good tribes that knew the builder of the city; and these were the grains among those tribes, grains that were mingled among the chaff. But they ascended, not with the chaff, but as tribes purified, chosen, as the tribes of the Lord. *There the tribes went up, the tribes of the Lord.* What does *the tribes of the Lord* mean? *A testimony for Israel.* What this is, brothers, hear: *A testimony for Israel*, that is, those in whom it is known that he is truly Israel. For what is Israel? The interpretation of its name has already been given, and let it be given often; for perhaps, even if it was given recently, it has slipped your minds. By saying it, let us see to it that it does not slip even the minds of those who have not known how, or have not been willing, to read: let us be their book. 'Israel' is interpreted 'seeing God'; or rather, when the word is examined more carefully, 'Israel' is interpreted thus: 'There is, seeing God'—both: 'There is, seeing God.' For man in himself is not; for he is changed and turned, if he does not share in him who is the selfsame. Then he is, when he sees God. For then he is, when he sees him who is; and by seeing him who is, he himself too, after his own measure, comes to be. So then he is Israel; Israel is seeing God. The proud man, therefore, is not Israel, because his sharing is not in the selfsame, because he wishes to be the selfsame to himself. He who wishes to be his own beginning is not Israel. Every feigned man, therefore, is not Israel, because every proud man must necessarily be feigned. This I say, brothers: every proud man must necessarily wish to seem what he is not; otherwise, my brothers, it cannot be. And would that the proud man wished to seem what he is not in such a way as to wish to seem, for example, a flute player when he was not a flute player. For he would soon be put to the proof; it would be said to him: Play; let us see whether you are a flute player. He could not; he would be found to have wished falsely to seem what he was not. If he said he was eloquent, it would be said to him: Speak, and prove it. If he spoke, he is found not to be what he professed. But the proud man—what is worse—wishes to seem just when he is not; and because it is difficult to understand justice, it is difficult to recognize the proud. The proud, then, wish to seem what they are not: therefore their sharing is not in the selfsame; they do not belong to Israel, who is 'seeing God.' Who, then, belongs to Israel? He who is a sharer in the selfsame. Who is he who shares in the selfsame? He who confesses that he is not what God is, and that from him he has whatever good he can have; that of himself he is nothing but sin, and that from him he has justice. Such is the man in whom there is no guile. And the Lord, seeing Nathanael, what did he say? *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^31]. If, then, those are true Israelites in whom there is no guile, those tribes ascend to Jerusalem in whom there is no guile. And they are themselves *a testimony for Israel*; that is, through them it is recognized that there were grains among that chaff, when, the threshing floor being seen, the whole was thought to be chaff. There were, then, grains there; but when they shall have ascended to that height of splendor, when the threshing floor shall have been winnowed[^32], then there will be *a testimony for Israel*; then all the evil will say: Truly there were just men among the evil, when all seemed to us to be evil, and we thought all were such as we ourselves were. *A testimony for Israel.* Where do they ascend? why do they ascend? *To confess to your name, O Lord.* Nothing more magnificent can be said. As pride presumes, so humility confesses. As he is a presumptuous man who wishes to seem what he is not, so he is a confessor who does not wish to seem what he himself is, and loves what God is. To this, then, the Israelites ascend, in whom there is no guile, because they are true Israelites, because in them is the testimony for Israel; to this they ascend, *to confess to your name, O Lord.*

**9.** [v. 5.] *For there seats sat in judgment.* A strange riddle, a strange question, if it is not understood. He says 'seats,' which the Greeks call thrones. The Greeks call seats thrones, as it were seats of honor. So then, my brothers, it is no wonder if men sit on seats, on chairs; but how can we understand that the seats themselves sit? As if someone were to say: Let the chairs sit here, or let the seats sit here. One sits on a chair, one sits on seats, one sits on chairs; the seats themselves do not sit. What, then, is this: *For there seats sat in judgment*[^33]? Surely you often hear God saying: *Heaven is my throne; but the earth is my footstool*[^34]. In Latin the whole is put thus: *Heaven is my seat.* Who are these, if not the just? who are the heavens, if not the just? Whoever is heaven, they themselves are heavens; for as the one Church, so the churches; they are so many as to be one; so too the just: the just are heaven in such a way that they are heavens. And in them God sits, and out of them God judges. And not without cause was it said: *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^35]. For the apostles were made heaven. Whence were they made heaven? Because they were justified. As the sinner was made earth, to whom it was said: *You are earth, and to earth you shall go*[^36], so those who were justified were made heaven. They bore God, and out of them God flashed miracles, thundered terrors, rained down consolations. They were, then—they were—heaven, and they declared the glory of God. For that you may know that they themselves were called heaven, it says there in the same psalm: *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^37]. You ask whose; and you will find: the heavens'. If, then, heaven is the seat of God, but the apostles are heaven, they too were made the seat of God; they are the throne of God. It was said in another place: *The soul of the just man is the throne of wisdom*[^38]. A great thing, a great thing was said: *The throne of wisdom is the soul of the just man*; that is, in the soul of the just man wisdom sits as on its chair, as on its throne, and from there judges whatever it judges. So then they were thrones of wisdom, and therefore the Lord said to them: *You will sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^39]. So they too will sit upon twelve seats, and they themselves are the seats of God: of them indeed it was said: *For there seats sat. For there seats sat.* Who sat? *The seats.* And what are the seats? Those of whom it was said: *The soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom.* What are the seats? The heavens. What are the heavens? Heaven. What is heaven? Of which the Lord says: *Heaven is my seat.* And the just themselves are seats, and they have seats; and in that Jerusalem the seats will sit. For what purpose? *In judgment. You will sit*, he says, *upon twelve seats*—O you seats—*judging the twelve tribes of Israel.* Judging whom? Those below who are on earth. Who will judge? Those who were made heaven. But those who will be judged will be divided into two parts; one will be at the right hand, the other at the left. The saints will judge with Christ. For *he will come into judgment with the elders of the people*, says Isaiah[^40]. There are some, then, who will judge with him; others who will be judged by him and by those who will judge with him. These, then, will be divided into two parts: one will be set at the right hand, to whom will be counted up the alms they gave; the other will be set at the left, to whom will be counted up cruelty and the barrenness of mercy. And to those set at the right hand it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* Why? *I was hungry*, he says, *and you gave me to eat.* And they: *When did we see you hungry?* And he: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^41]. So then what is it, brothers? Those will judge of whom it was said that they should make friends of the mammon of iniquity, *so that they too*, he says, *may receive you into the eternal dwellings*[^42]. The saints will sit with the Lord to attend to those who showed mercy, and will take them, set apart at the right hand, into the kingdom of heaven; and this is the peace of Jerusalem. What is the peace of Jerusalem? That the bodily works of mercy be joined to the spiritual works of preaching, and that peace come about by giving and receiving. For the apostle, who said that these alms are a matter of giving and receiving, says: *If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great matter if we reap your fleshly things*[^43]? And of this very thing he says in another place: *He who gathered much had nothing over; and he who gathered little had no lack*[^44]. Why did *he who gathered much have nothing over*? Because what he had more than enough of, he gave to the needy. And what does it mean: *He who gathered little had no lack*? Because he received from the one who had more than enough, *so that there might be*, he says, *equality*[^45]. This is the peace of which it is said: *Let there be peace in your strength.*

**10.** [v. 6.] For when he had said: *For there seats sat in judgment, seats over the house of David*—that is, over the family of Christ, to whom in due time they gave food—he at once said, as though to those very seats: *Ask for the things that are for the peace of Jerusalem*[^46]. O you seats, who already sit to judge, and have been made the seats of the Lord who judges (since those who judge ask questions, and those who are judged are questioned): *Ask*, he says, *for the things that are for the peace of Jerusalem.* By asking, what will they find? That some have shown mercy, others have not. Those whom they find to have shown mercy, these they will call to Jerusalem; for these *are for the peace of Jerusalem.* Love is a strong thing, brothers; love is a strong thing. Do you wish to see how strong love is? Whoever, through some necessity, has not been able to fulfill what God commands, let him love the one who fulfills it, and in that one he fulfills it. Let your charity attend. For example, a man has a wife whom he cannot send away; he must obey the apostle who says: *Let the husband render to his wife her due*; and: *Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek to be loosed*[^47]. It comes to his mind that better is that life of which the same apostle says: *I would that all were as I myself am*[^48]. He looks to those who have done this; he loves them, and in them he fulfills what he cannot in himself. Love is a strong thing. It is itself our virtue; for if we are not in it, whatever else we have profits us nothing. *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels*, says the apostle, *but have not love, I have become as sounding brass or a tinkling cymbal.* He adds another great thing: *And if I distribute all my substance, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^49]. But if there be love alone, which finds nothing to distribute to the poor, let it love: let it give only a cup of cold water[^50]; as much will be reckoned to it as to Zacchaeus, who gave half of his estate to the poor[^51]. Why is this? The one gave so little, the other gave so much, and as much will be reckoned to him? Just as much, plainly. For the means were unequal, but the love was not unequal.

**11.** Those, then, ask questions; you, consider what you are. Now behold, it has been said to us: *We will go into the house of the Lord.* We have surely rejoiced in those who said to us: *We will go into the house of the Lord.* See, then, whether we are going. For we go not by our feet, but by our affections. See whether we are going; let each one of you ask himself what he is like toward the holy poor man, toward the needy brother, toward the needy beggar: let him see whether his heart is not narrow. For the seats that will sit in judgment must question you, and they must find the things that are for the peace of Jerusalem. And how do they ask? As the seats of God. God asks. If anything is hidden from God, it can also escape those seats when they ask anything. *Ask for the things that are for the peace of Jerusalem.* But what are the things that are for the peace of Jerusalem? *And abundance*, he says, *for those who love you*[^52]. He turns his voice to Jerusalem herself. There is abundance for those who love her. Abundance out of want: here in want, there abundant; here weak, there strong; here needy, there rich. Whence made rich? Because they gave here what they received from God for a time, and received there what God will afterward repay forever. Here, my brothers, even the rich are poor. It is good for a rich man to recognize that he is poor: for if he thinks himself full, that is a swelling, not a fullness. Let him recognize that he is empty, so that he may be able to be filled. What does he have? Gold. What does he not yet have? Eternal life. Let him attend to what he has, and see what he does not have. Brothers, out of what he has, let him give, so that he may receive what he does not have; let him buy out of what he has that which he does not have. *And abundance for those who love you.*

**12.** [v. 7.] *Let there be peace in your strength.* O Jerusalem! O city that are built as a city, whose sharing is in the selfsame, *let there be peace in your strength*, let there be peace in your love; for your strength is your love. Hear the Song of Songs: *Love is strong as death*[^53]. A great saying, brothers: *Love is strong as death.* The fortitude of love could not be expressed more magnificently than by saying: *Love is strong as death.* For who resists death, brothers? Let your charity attend. One resists fire, waves, the sword; one resists powers, one resists kings: death comes alone—who resists it? Nothing is stronger than it. Therefore love has been compared to its strength, and it was said: *Love is strong as death.* And because love itself kills what we were, so that we may be what we were not, love works in us a certain death. By that very death he was dead who said: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^54]; by that very death those were dead to whom he said: *For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^55]. *Love is strong as death.* If, then, it is strong, it is mighty and of great power, and it is itself the strength, and through it the weak are ruled by the strong, the earth by heaven, the peoples by the seats; therefore: *Let there be peace in your strength*, let there be peace in your love. And through this strength, through this love, through this peace, let there be *abundance in your towers*—that is, in your high places. For few will sit in judgment; but many set at the right hand will make up the people of that city. For many will belong to each of the high ones, by whom they are received into the eternal dwellings; and there will be abundance in her towers. But the fullness of delights and the sufficiency of riches is God himself, the selfsame himself, the very one whose sharing is the city's sharing in the selfsame: he himself will be our abundance too. But whence? Through love, that is, through strength. And in whom is love, brothers? In him who does not seek his own things in this life[^56]. Hear the apostle who has love; he says: *Please all men in all things, as I also please all men in all things*[^57]. Where, then, is what you said: *If I still pleased men, I would not be a servant of Christ*[^58]? And now you say that you please, now you exhort that they too should please? But he did not set up the end so that anyone should please for his own sake and not for love's sake. He who seeks his own glory does not seek the salvation of others. For he says: *As I also please all men in all things, not seeking what is profitable to me, but to the many, that they may be saved*[^59].

**13.** [v. 8.] So here too, when he was speaking of love, he says: *For the sake of my brothers and my kinsmen, I spoke peace concerning you*[^60]. O Jerusalem, city whose sharing is in the selfsame, I, in this life and on this earth, I, poor, a pilgrim and groaning, not yet fully enjoying your peace, and preaching your peace—I do not preach it for my own sake, as the heretics do, who, seeking their own glory, say: Peace be with you, and do not have the peace they preach to the peoples. For if they had peace, they would not be tearing unity apart. I, he says, *spoke peace concerning you.* But why? *For the sake of my brothers and my kinsmen*: not for the sake of my own honor, not for the sake of my money, not for the sake of my life; for: *To me to live is Christ, and to die is gain*[^61]. But *I spoke peace concerning you, for the sake of my brothers and my kinsmen.* For he himself desired *to be dissolved and to be with Christ*; yet he who would preach these things to his kinsmen and brothers said: *To remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^62]. *For the sake of my brothers and my kinsmen, I spoke peace concerning you.*

**14.** [v. 9.] *For the sake of the house of the Lord my God, I sought good things for you*[^63]. I did not seek good things for my own sake: for then I would be seeking them not for you, but for myself; and so I would not have them either, because I would not be seeking them for you; but: *For the sake of the house of the Lord my God*, for the sake of the Church, for the sake of the saints, for the sake of the pilgrims, for the sake of the needy, that they may ascend—since we say to them: *We will go into the house of the Lord*—for the sake of that very *house of the Lord my God, I sought good things for you.* These things, brothers, fuller and necessary, take up, eat, drink; grow strong, run, lay hold.
