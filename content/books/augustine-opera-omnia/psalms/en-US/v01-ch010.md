# Exposition of Psalm 10

**1.** [vv 1.2.] This title does not need a new treatment; for it has already been sufficiently treated what *to the end* is. Let us, then, see the very text of the psalm, which seems to me to be sung against the heretics, who, by recalling and exaggerating the sins of many in the Church, as though among themselves the righteous were either all or the more part, strive to turn us away and snatch us from the breasts of the one true mother Church, affirming that Christ is among them, and as though piously and zealously admonishing us, that by crossing over to them we may migrate to Christ, whom they lyingly claim to have. Now it is known that Christ, in prophecy, when he was suggested allegorically by many names, was also called a mountain. And so it is to be answered to these, and to be said: *In the Lord I trust, how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?* I hold one mountain in which I trust, how do you say that I should cross over to you, as though there were many Christs? Or if you say that you are mountains through pride, one must indeed be a sparrow feathered with the virtues and precepts of God; but these very things forbid flying to those mountains, and placing hope in proud men. I have a house where I may rest, because in the Lord I trust: for the sparrow too finds a house for itself, and the Lord has become a refuge for the poor. Let us say, then, with all confidence, lest, while we seek Christ among the heretics, we lose him: *In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?*

**2.** [v 3.] *For behold, sinners have bent the bow, they have prepared their arrows in the quiver, that they may shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart.* These are the terrors of those who threaten us with the sinners, that we may cross over to them as to the righteous. *Behold*, they say, *sinners have bent the bow*: the Scriptures, I believe, which they, by interpreting carnally, send out from there poisoned sentences. *They have prepared their arrows in the quiver*: namely those same words, which by the authority of the Scriptures they are about to hurl, they have prepared in the secret of the heart. *That they may shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart*: so that, when they have perceived that, the light of the Church being darkened on account of the multitude of the unlearned and carnal, they cannot be refuted, they may corrupt good ways by evil conversations. But against all these terrors it is to be said: *In the Lord I trust.*

**3.** But how fittingly the moon signifies the Church, I remember I promised to consider in this psalm. There are two probable opinions about the moon: but which of these is true, I think a man can either not at all, or only with the greatest difficulty, know. For when it is asked whence it has its light, some say it has its own, but that half of its globe shines and half is dark; and while it moves in its circle, the same part by which it shines is gradually turned toward the earth, so that it can be seen by us, and therefore it appears at first as it were horned. For if you make a ball white on half and dark on half; if you hold the part that is dark before your eyes, you see no whiteness, and when you begin to turn that white part toward your eyes, if you do it gradually, you will first see horns of whiteness, then it gradually grows, until the whole bright part is set before the eyes, and nothing of the other dark part is seen: but if you continue to turn it still gradually, the darkness begins to appear, and the brightness to lessen, until it returns again to horns, and at last is wholly turned away from the eyes, and again that dark part alone can be seen: which they say happens when the light of the moon seems to grow up to the fifteenth moon, and again to lessen up to the thirtieth, and return to horns, until in it utterly no light appears. According to this opinion the moon in allegory signifies the Church, because in part the Church shines spiritually, but in part it is dark carnally: and at times the spiritual part appears to men in good works; but at times it lies hidden in the conscience, and is known to God only, when it appears to men only in body; as happens when we pray in the heart, and seem as it were to do nothing, when we are bidden to have our hearts not toward the earth, but upward to the Lord. But others say that the moon does not have its own light, but is illumined by the sun; but when it is with the sun, it has toward us that part by which it is not illumined, and therefore no light is seen in it; but when it begins to withdraw from it, it is illumined even on that part which it has toward the earth, and necessarily begins from the horns, until on the fifteenth it comes opposite the sun; for then at the setting of the sun it rises, so that whoever has observed the setting sun, when he has begun not to see it, turning to the East, sees the moon rising; and thence, on the other side, when it begins to approach the sun, it turns toward us that part by which it is not illumined, until it returns to horns, and thence does not appear at all; because then that part which is illumined is upward toward heaven, but that which the sun cannot make shine, toward the earth. So according to this opinion too, the moon is understood as the Church, because it does not have its own light, but is illumined by the only-begotten Son of God, who in many places in the holy Scriptures is allegorically called the sun. Whom certain heretics, not knowing and not being able to discern him, try to turn away the senses of the simple to this corporeal and visible sun, which is the common light of the flesh of men and of flies; and they turn away some, who, as long as they cannot behold with the mind the inner light of truth, refuse to be content with the simple catholic faith, which is the one salvation of the little ones, and the one milk by which one comes with sure strength to the firmness of more solid food. Whichever, then, of these two opinions is true, the moon is fittingly taken allegorically as the Church. Or if, in these obscure matters, more laborious than fruitful, it is either not pleasing, or there is no leisure, to exercise the mind, or the mind itself is not able, it is enough to behold the moon with the eyes of the common people, and not to seek out the obscure causes, but with all to perceive both its increases and its fullness and its losses. Which, if it wanes for this reason, that it may be renewed, even to the unlearned multitude demonstrates the figure of the Church, in which is believed the resurrection of the dead.

**4.** Then it is to be asked what is taken in this psalm as *the dark moon*, in which sinners prepared to shoot at the upright of heart. For the moon can be called dark in more than one way: for both when it is ended in its monthly course, and when its brightness is interrupted by a cloud, and when, full, it is eclipsed, it can be called a dark moon. It can, then, be understood of the persecutors of the martyrs, that they wished to shoot at the upright of heart in the dark moon: either while the Church was still new, because it had not yet shone forth greater over the earth, and overcome the darkness of the gentile superstitions: or because, by the tongues of blasphemers and of those defaming the Christian name, as though by mists the earth were covered, the bright moon, that is, the Church, could not be seen clearly: or because by the very slaughters of the martyrs and so great a shedding of blood, as by that eclipse and darkening by which the moon seems to show a bloody face, the weak were deterred from the Christian name; in which terror the sinners hurled deceitful and sacrilegious words, that they might pervert even the upright of heart. It can also be understood of those sinners whom the Church contains, that then, an occasion of this dark moon being found, they committed many things which are now cast at us as reproaches by the heretics, when the authors of those things are said to have done them. But however it stands with what was done in the dark moon, now that the catholic name is spread and celebrated throughout the whole world, what is it to me to be disturbed by things unknown? For *In the Lord I trust*; nor do I listen to those saying to my soul: *Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow. For behold, sinners have bent the bow, that they may shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart.* Or if even to them now the moon seems dark, because they wish to make it uncertain which is the catholic Church, and try to charge it with the sins of carnal men, of whom it contains many; what is that to him who truly says: *In the Lord I trust?* By which voice each one both shows himself to be grain, and tolerably bears with the chaff until the time of winnowing.

**5.** *In the Lord*, then, *I trust.* Let those fear who trust in man, and cannot deny that they are of the party of a man, by whose gray hairs they swear: and when in conversation it is asked of them, of what communion they are, unless they say they are of that one's party, they cannot be recognized. Tell me what these do, when there are recalled to them so innumerable and daily sins and crimes of theirs, of which that society is full? Can they say, *In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?* For those do not trust in the Lord who say that the holy sacraments exist only if they are given through holy men. And so, when it is asked of them, who are the holy, they are ashamed to say, We are; nay even if they are not ashamed to say this, those who hear are ashamed for them. And so these compel those who receive the sacraments to place their hope in a man, whose heart they cannot see. And cursed is everyone who places his hope in man. For what is it to say, I am one who is holy, but, Place your hope in me? What if you are not holy? Or show your heart. But if you cannot, where shall I see that you are holy? Or perhaps you will say what is written, *By their works you will know them*[^1]. I plainly see wondrous works, the daily violences of the Circumcellions flying about everywhere under bishops and presbyters as leaders, and calling their terrible cudgels "Israels," which men now living daily see and feel. But the times of Macarius, about which they raise odium, very many have not seen, and no one now sees: and whatever catholic saw them could say, if he wished to be a servant of God, *In the Lord I trust*; which he says even now, when he sees many things in the Church that he does not wish, who perceives himself still swimming within those nets full of good and bad fish, until one comes to the end of the sea, where the bad are separated from the good. But what do these answer, if to one of them the one whom they baptize should say: How do you bid me presume? For if it is the merit both of the giver and of the receiver, let it be the merit of God the giver, and of my conscience as receiver: for these two things are not uncertain to me, his goodness, and my faith. Why do you interpose yourself, of whom I can know nothing certain? Let me say, *In the Lord I trust.* For if I trust in you, whence do I trust if you have done nothing evil this night? Finally, if you wish me to believe you, can I do more than believe concerning you? Whence, then, do I trust in those with whom you communicated yesterday, and communicate today, and will communicate tomorrow, whether even in these three days they have committed nothing evil? But if neither you nor me does that which we do not know defile, what cause is there that you should rebaptize those who did not know the times of the surrender and the odium of Macarius? What cause is there that you should dare to rebaptize Christians coming from Mesopotamia, who have heard neither the name of Caecilian nor of Donatus, and deny that they are Christians? But if the sins of others which they do not know defile them, then whatever is committed day by day in your party without your knowing it holds you guilty, you who vainly cast the constitutions of the emperors at the Catholics, when in your camps private cudgels and fires so rage. Behold to what they have fallen, who, when they saw sinners in the Catholic Church, could not say, *In the Lord I trust*, and placed their hope in man. Which they would altogether say, if they were not, either themselves, or these too, such as they thought those to be from whom, by sacrilegious pride, they pretended to wish to be separated.

**6.** [v 4.] Let the catholic soul, then, say: *In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow? For behold, sinners have bent the bow, they have prepared their arrows in the quiver, that they may shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart*: and let it turn its speech from them to the Lord, and say, *Because they have destroyed what you have perfected.* And let it say this, not against these alone, but against all heretics. For all, as far as in them lies, have destroyed the praise which God perfected out of the mouth of infants and nurslings, while by vain and scrupulous questions they harass the little ones, and do not allow them to be nourished with the milk of faith. As though it were said to this soul, Why do these say to you, *Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?* why do they terrify you with the sinners who have bent the bow to shoot in the dark moon at the upright of heart? It answers: For this reason indeed they terrify me, *because they have destroyed what you have perfected.* Where, but in their conventicles, where the little ones, ignorant of the inner light, they do not nourish with milk, but slay with poisons? *But the righteous, what has he done?* If Macarius offended you, if Caecilian offended you; what has Christ done to you, who said, *My peace I give you, my peace I leave you*[^2]: which you have violated by wicked dissension? What has Christ done to you, who bore with his betrayer with such patience, that he handed to him the first Eucharist, prepared by his hands and commended by his mouth, just as to the rest of the Apostles? What has Christ done to you, who sent that same betrayer of his, whom he named a devil, who before the betrayal of the Lord could not even show fidelity with the Lord's money-bags, together with the rest of the disciples to preach the kingdom of heaven; that he might show that the gifts of God reach those who receive with faith, even if he through whom they receive be such as Judas was?

**7.** [v 5.] *The Lord is in his holy temple.* So indeed, as the Apostle says, *For the temple of God is holy, which you are. But whoever violates the temple of God, him will God destroy*[^3]. He violates the temple of God who violates unity: for he does not hold the head, from which the whole body, joined and knit together through every touch of supplying, according to the working in the measure of each part, makes the increase of the body, unto the building up of itself in love. In this his holy temple the Lord is; which consists of his many members, each bearing its own office, built up by love into one building; which he violates, whoever, for the sake of his own principality, is sundered from the catholic society. *The Lord is in his holy temple, the Lord's seat is in heaven.* If you take heaven to be the righteous man, as you take earth to be the sinner, to whom it was said, *You are earth, and into earth you will go*[^4]; you will understand what was said, *The Lord is in his holy temple*, to be repeated when it was said, *The Lord's seat is in heaven.*

**8.** *His eyes look upon the poor.* Surely his to whom the poor man has been left, and who has become a refuge for the poor. And therefore all the seditions and tumults within these nets, until they are brought to the shore, about which the heretics insult us to their own destruction and to our correction, come about through those men who refuse to be the poor of Christ. But do they turn away the eyes of God from those who wish to be this? For *His eyes look upon the poor.* Is it to be feared lest in the crowd of the rich he cannot see the few poor, whom, guarded in the bosom of the catholic Church, he may nourish? *His eyelids question the sons of men.* Here *sons of men* I would gladly take by that rule, as those regenerated from the old men through faith. For these are exercised, in certain obscure places of the Scriptures, as it were by the closed eyes of God, that they may seek: and again in certain manifest places, as it were by the open eyes of God, they are enlightened that they may rejoice. And this frequent closing and opening in the holy books are as it were the eyelids of God, which question, that is, which prove the sons of men, who are neither wearied by the obscurity of things, but exercised, nor puffed up by knowledge, but confirmed.

**9.** [v 6.] *The Lord questions the just and the impious.* Why, then, do we fear lest the impious harm us in anything, if perhaps they communicate the sacraments with us with no sincere heart, when he questions the just and the impious? *But he who loves iniquity, hates his own soul*: that is, the lover of iniquity harms not him who believes God and does not place his hope in man, but only his own soul.

**10.** [vv 7.8.] *He will rain snares upon sinners.* If clouds are understood generally as Prophets, whether good or evil (who are also called false prophets); so the false prophets are ordered by the Lord God, that from them he may rain snares upon sinners: for no one falls into following them except a sinner; either for the preparation of the last punishment, if he prefers to persevere in sinning; or for the dissuading of pride, if at some time he should seek God with a sincerer care. But if clouds are understood only as good and true Prophets; even of these it is manifest that God rains snares upon sinners, although from these he also waters the pious to bear fruit: *To some*, says the Apostle, *we are the odor of life unto life, to some the odor of death unto death*[^5]. For not only Prophets, but all who water souls with the word of God, can be called clouds: who, when they are ill understood, God rains snares upon sinners; but when they are well understood, he makes fruitful the breasts of the pious and faithful. As, for example, that which is written, *And the two will be in one flesh*[^6], if anyone interpret it of lust, he rains a snare upon the sinner; but if you understand it, as he who says, *But I say in Christ and in the Church*[^7], he rains a shower upon the fertile earth: but from the same cloud, that is, the divine Scripture, both were done. Likewise the Lord says: *Not what enters into your mouth defiles you, but what comes out*[^8]. The sinner hears this, and prepares his gullet for voracity: the righteous hears this, and is fortified against the superstition of distinguishing foods. And here too, then, from the same cloud of the Scriptures, according to each one's deserts, both a rain of snares is poured upon the sinner, and a rain of abundance upon the righteous.

**11.** *Fire and brimstone and the spirit of the storm are the portion of their cup.* This is their punishment and end, those through whom the name of God is blasphemed, that first they be laid waste by the fire of their own desires, then be cast out by the stench of their evil works from the assembly of the blessed, finally, snatched away and submerged, pay ineffable penalties. For this is the portion of their cup; as the cup of the righteous, your cup that makes drunk, how excellent it is! For they will be made drunk with the abundance of your house. But I think it is called a cup so that we may not suppose that anything beyond measure and limit, even in the very punishments of sinners, is done by divine providence. And therefore, as though rendering the reason why this is done, he added: *Because the Lord is righteous, and has loved acts of righteousness.* Not in vain in the plural, except that he speaks of men, so that for the righteous, acts of righteousness are understood to be put; for in many righteous there seem to be as it were many righteousnesses, although there is one righteousness of God, of which all partake: as if one face should look into many mirrors, what is single in it is rendered in the plural from those many. Therefore he again refers it to singularity, by saying, *His face has seen equity*: perhaps it is put for, *His face has seen equity*, as if it were said, Equity was seen in his face, that is, in the knowledge of him; for the face of God is the power by which he becomes known to the worthy: or surely, *His face has seen equity*, because he does not offer himself to be known to the evil, but to the good; and this itself is equity.

**12.** But if anyone wishes to understand the moon as the synagogue, let him refer the psalm to the passion of the Lord, and say of the Jews, *Because they have destroyed what you have perfected*; and of the Lord himself, *But the righteous, what has he done?* whom they charged as a destroyer of the Law: whose precepts they themselves had destroyed by living perversely, and by despising them, and establishing their own; so that the Lord himself speaks according to the man, as he is wont, saying, *In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?* on account, namely, of the terrors of those who desired to seize and crucify him: when sinners wishing to shoot at the *upright of heart*, that is, those who had believed in Christ, *in the dark moon*, that is, the synagogue filled with sinners, is not absurdly understood. To which agrees also what is said, *The Lord is in his holy temple, the Lord's seat is in heaven?* that is, the Word in the man, or the Son of man himself who is in the heavens. *His eyes look upon the poor*: either whom he took up according to God, or for whose sake he suffered according to the man. *His eyelids question the sons of men.* The closing and opening of the eyes, which is probably put under the name of eyelids, we can take as his death and resurrection, where he proved the sons of men, his disciples, both terrified by his passion, and gladdened by his resurrection. *The Lord questions the just and the impious*: now governing the Church from heaven. *But he who loves iniquity, hates his own soul*: why this is so, the following teaches; for *He will rain snares upon sinners*, to be taken according to the earlier exposition, and all the rest down to the end of the psalm.
