# On the Same Psalm 26

**1.** The Lord our God, addressing and consoling us, those namely whom by his just judgment he looks upon as eating bread in the sweat of our face, deigns to speak from us to us, that he may show that he is not only our maker, but also our indweller. These voices of the psalm, which we have heard, and in part have sung, if we say that they are ours, it is to be feared how we say what is true: for they are voices rather of the Spirit of God than ours. Again, if we say that they are not ours, surely we lie. For there is no groaning but of those who labor: or can all this voice, which has here sounded, full of sorrow and tears, be his who can never be wretched? The Lord, then, is merciful, we are wretched: the merciful one, deigning to speak to the wretched, deigns even to use in them the voice of the wretched. So both are true, that the voice is ours, and that it is not ours; and that the voice is the Spirit of God's, and that it is not his. It is the voice of the Spirit of God, because we would not say these things unless he inspired it: but it is not his, because he is neither wretched nor labors. But these are voices of the wretched and of those who labor. Again they are ours, because they are voices indicating our wretchedness: likewise they are not ours, because it is by his gift that we deserve even to groan.

**2.** [v 1.] *A psalm of David before he was anointed.* This the title of the psalm has: *A psalm of David before he was anointed*, that is, before he was anointed. For he was anointed as king. And only the king was then anointed, and the priest: these two persons were anointed at that time. In two persons was prefigured the one future king and priest, in both offices one Christ, and therefore Christ from the chrism. But not only our head was anointed, but also his body, we ourselves. Now he is king, because he rules and leads us; priest, because he intercedes for us. And indeed he alone was such a priest, that he himself was also the sacrifice. He offered to God no other sacrifice than himself. For he would not find besides himself a most pure rational victim, as an immaculate lamb redeeming us by the shedding of his blood, incorporating us into himself, making us his members, that in him we too might be Christ. Therefore the anointing pertains to all Christians: but in the earlier times of the Old Testament it pertained to two persons only. From this it appears that we are the body of Christ, because all are anointed: and all in him are both Christ's and Christ, because in a manner the whole Christ is head and body. This anointing will perfect us spiritually in that life which is promised to us. But this is the voice of one desiring that life; it is the voice of one desiring the grace of God, which will be perfected in us at the end: therefore it was said: *Before he was anointed.* For we are now anointed in the sacrament, and by that very sacrament something is prefigured which we shall be. And that something to come, ineffable, we ought to desire, and in the sacrament to groan, that we may rejoice in that thing which is foreshown by the sacrament.

**3.** Behold what he says: *The Lord is my illumination and my salvation, whom shall I fear?* he illumines me, let the darkness withdraw; he saves me, let weakness withdraw; walking in the light, firm, whom shall I fear? For God does not give such a salvation as can be wrested away by anyone; or is it such a light as can be darkened by anyone. The Lord illumining, we the illumined; the Lord saving, we the saved: if, then, he is the one illumining, we the illumined; and he the one saving, we the saved; apart from him we are darkness and weakness. But having in him a hope certain and fixed and true, whom shall we fear? The Lord is your illumination, the Lord is your salvation. Find one more powerful, and fear. To the most powerful of all, to the almighty, I so belong, that he both illumines me, and saves me, and I do not fear anyone besides him. *The Lord is the protector of my life, of whom shall I be afraid?*

**4.** [v 2.] *While the harmful draw near over me, to eat my flesh: those who trouble me, my enemies, they themselves were weakened, and fell.* And so what shall I fear, or whom shall I fear? of whom shall I be afraid, or of what shall I be afraid? Those who persecute me, they themselves are weakened, they themselves fall. But why do they persecute? *To eat my flesh.* What is my flesh? My carnal affections. Let them rage by persecuting, nothing in me dies but what is mortal. There will be in me something to which the persecutor cannot reach, where my God dwells. Let them eat my flesh: the flesh being finished, I shall be spirit, and spiritual. And indeed my Lord promises me so great a salvation, that even now this mortal flesh, which seems to be given over to the hands of the persecutors, shall not perish forever; but what was shown in my head rising again, this let all the members hope. Whom shall my soul fear, whom God inhabits? Whom shall my flesh fear, when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption? Do you wish to know that those who persecute us eat the flesh, but that even this flesh of ours is not to be feared for? *The animal body is sown, the spiritual body will rise*[^1]. But how great should be the confidence in him who knows how to say: *The Lord is my illumination, and my salvation, whom shall I fear? The Lord is the protector of my life, of whom shall I be afraid?* The emperor is protected by shield-bearers, and does not fear; the mortal is protected by mortals, and is secure: shall the mortal, protected by the immortal, both fear and be afraid?

**5.** [v 3.] But how great should be the confidence in him who says these things, hear: *If camps stand against me, my heart will not fear.* Camps are fortified, but what is more fortified than God? *If war rise up against me.* What does war do to me? Can it take from me my hope? can it take from me what the Almighty gives? As he who gives is not conquered, so what he gives is not taken away. If what is given can be taken away, the giver is conquered. Therefore not even those things which we receive temporally can anyone take from us, my brothers, except him alone who gave them. The spiritual things which he bestows, he will not take away, unless you let them go: but the carnal and temporal things he himself takes away; because whoever else takes them away, takes them away by his granting the power. We know this, and we read in the book of Job, that not even he who seems for a time to have as it were the greatest power, the devil, can do anything unless permitted. He received power over the lowest, and lost the greatest and highest. And this is not the power of one angry, but the punishment of one condemned. Nor, then, can he himself have any power, unless permitted. And in that book which I have recalled you have it, and in the Gospel the Lord says: *This night Satan has asked to sift you like wheat, and I have prayed for you, Peter, that your faith may not fail*[^2]. But it is permitted either for our punishment, or for our proving. Therefore because no one can take from us what God gives, let us fear nothing but God: whatever else rages, whatever else is proud against us, let our heart not fear it.

**6.** [v 4.] *If war rise up against me, in this I will hope.* In what? *One thing*, he says, *I have asked of the Lord.* In the feminine gender he named a certain benefit, as though he said: One petition. And as we are accustomed to say, speaking, for example: You have two; we do not say "Two," this manner of speaking the Scripture has used: *One thing*, he says, *I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek.* Let us see what he asks who fears nothing. Great security of heart. Do you wish to fear nothing? Ask this one thing, which the one who fears nothing asks, or which he asks that he may fear nothing? *One thing*, he says, *I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek.* This is what is done here by those who walk well. What is this? what is that one thing? *That I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life.* This is the one thing: for that is called a house where we shall always remain. In this pilgrimage it is called a house, but properly it is called a tabernacle: a tabernacle is for those on pilgrimage, and in a manner for those in military service, and fighting against an enemy. When, then, there is a tabernacle in this life, it is manifest that there is also an enemy: for to have tabernacles together is to be tent-companions; and you know that this is a name of those in military service. Therefore here a tabernacle, there a house. But this tabernacle too is sometimes, by an abuse of nearness, called a house, and a house sometimes in the same way is called a tabernacle: yet properly that is a house, this a tabernacle.

**7.** But what we shall do in that house, you have another psalm expressing evidently: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house, forever and ever they will praise you*[^3]. With this desire, if it must be said, burning, and seething with this love, he desires to dwell all the days of his life in the house of the Lord: in the house of the Lord all the days of his life, not as though to be ended, but eternal. For days are spoken of as years are, of which it was said, *and your years will not fail*[^4]. For the day of eternal life is one day without setting. This, then, he said to the Lord: I have desired this, One thing I have asked, This I will seek. And as though we said to him: And what will you do there? what is your delight there? what is the recreation of the heart? what are those delights, whence joys will abound? For you will not endure there, unless you are happy. But whence will that happiness come? For here we have the diverse happinesses of the human race, and each one is called wretched when what he loves is taken from him. Men, then, love diverse things; and when each one seems to have what he loves, he is called happy. But he is truly happy, not if he has what he loves; but if he loves what is to be loved. For many are more wretched by having what they love than by lacking it: for, loving harmful things, the wretched are more wretched by having them. And God, when we love badly, mercifully denies us what we love: but, angry, he gives to the one loving what he loves badly. You have the Apostle saying evidently: *God handed them over to the desire of their heart*[^5]. He gave, then, what they loved, but by condemning. You have again him denying what was asked: *For which I asked the Lord three times*, he says, *that he would take it from me* (namely the sting of the flesh), *and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for power is perfected in weakness*[^6]. Behold, those he gave over to the desire of their heart; to the apostle Paul he denied what he prayed: to them he gave unto damnation, to him he denied unto health. But when we love what God wishes us to love, without doubt he will give it to us. This is that one thing, which ought to be loved, that we may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of our life.

**8.** And because in these earthly dwellings men are delighted with diverse delights and pleasures, and each one wishes to dwell in that house where he has nothing to offend the mind, and has many things to delight; but if those things which delighted are taken away, the man wishes to migrate anywhere: let us ask, as it were more curiously, and let this one tell, what we ourselves, what he is about to do in that house, where he desires and wishes, longs and asks this one thing of the Lord, to dwell in it all the days of his life. What do you do there, I ask you? what is it that you desire? Hear what: *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord.* Behold what I love, behold why I wish to dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life. He has there a great spectacle, to contemplate the delight of the Lord himself. He wishes, his night ended, to cling to his light. For then will be our morning, the night being past: whence in another place the psalm says: *In the morning I will stand before you, and contemplate*[^7]. Now for this reason I do not contemplate, because I have fallen: then I shall stand, and contemplate. This is a human voice. For man fell, and one would not be sent to lift him up, unless we had fallen. We fell, he came down. He ascended, we are lifted up: because no one ascends but he who came down. He who fell is lifted up; he who came down ascends. And let us not despair because he alone ascended. For he lifts us up, to whom, as we fell, he came down: and we shall stand, and contemplate, and enjoy great delight. Behold, I have said this, and you have cried out with desire for a certain form not yet seen. Let your heart go beyond all the customary things, and let your intention go beyond all your solemn thoughts that come from the flesh, drawn from the senses of the flesh, and imagining I know not what phantasms. Reject everything from your mind, deny whatever comes to mind: recognize the weakness of your heart, and because even what you could think of comes to mind, say: That is not it; for if that were it, it would already have come to my mind. So you will desire a certain good. What good? The good of all good, whence is every good, the good to which is not added what the good itself is. For a good man is spoken of, and a good field, and a good house, and a good animal, and a good tree, and a good body, and a good soul: you added something, as often as you said: Good. There is a simple good, the good itself by which all things are good, the good itself from which all things are good: this is the delight of the Lord; this we shall contemplate. Now see, brothers, if these good things which are called good delight us, if good things which are not good through themselves delight us (for all changeable things are not good through themselves), what will be the contemplation of the unchangeable good, eternal, always remaining in the same way? Since these things which are called good would in no way delight us, unless they were good; nor would they be good in any other way, unless they were from him who is simply good.

**9.** Behold why I wish to dwell, he says, in the house of the Lord all the days of my life. I have told you why: *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord.* But that I may always contemplate, and no trouble touch me contemplating, no suggestion turn me aside, no one's power take it away, I suffer no enemy in contemplating, and enjoy the delights secure, the Lord my God himself, what will be done for me? He will protect me. Therefore not only do I wish to contemplate the delight of the Lord, he says, but also *to be protected, his temple.* That he may protect me as his temple, I shall be his temple, and shall be protected by him. Is the temple of God such as the temples of idols are? The idols of the gentiles are protected by their temples: the Lord our God will protect his temple, and I shall be secure. I shall contemplate unto delight, and be protected unto salvation. As perfect as that contemplation will be, so perfect this protection; and as perfect that joy of contemplating, so perfect also the incorruption of health. To those two words which he said, *that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord, and be protected, his temple*, pertain those two with which the psalm began: *The Lord is my illumination, and my salvation, whom shall I fear?* Because I shall contemplate the delight of the Lord, he is my illumination: because he will protect me, his temple, he is my salvation.

**10.** [v 5.] But why does he grant us this at the end? *Because he has hidden me in his tabernacle, in the day of my evils.* Therefore I shall have a dwelling in the house all the days of my life, to this end, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord, and be protected, his temple. But that I may arrive there, whence do I promise it to myself? *Because he has hidden me in his tabernacle, in the day of my evils.* Then there will not be the days of my evils, but he saw me in the days of my evils. He, then, who mercifully looked upon me set far off, how will he bless me set with him? Therefore not impudently did I ask that one thing; nor did my heart say to me: What do you ask, or of whom do you ask? For do you dare to ask anything of God, you wicked sinner? do you dare to hope that you will have any contemplation of God, you weak one, with an unclean heart? I dare, he says, not concerning myself, but concerning his delight; not from my presumption, but from his pledge. He who gave so great a pledge to the pilgrim, will he desert him when he arrives? *Because he has hidden me in his tabernacle, in the day of my evils.* Behold, the day of our evils is this life. The day of evils the impious have in one way, the faithful in another. For even believers, but still on pilgrimage from the Lord (*For as long as we are in this body, we are on pilgrimage from the Lord*[^8], this the Apostle said), if they do not pass days of evils, whence the voice of the Lord's prayer: *Deliver us from evil*[^9], if we are not in days of evils? but far more distinctly do those pass days of evils who have not yet believed: nor has he despised them. For Christ died for the impious. Let the human soul, then, dare to presume, and ask that one thing; secure it shall have it, secure it shall possess it. So beloved while foul, how shall it shine when beautiful! *Because he has hidden me in his tabernacle, in the day of my evils: he has protected me in the hidden place of his tabernacle.* What is the hidden place of his tabernacle? what is this? For many as it were members of the tabernacle are seen outside. And there is as it were a sanctuary, which is called the inner secret place of the temple. And what is this? that which only the priest entered. And perhaps the priest himself is the hidden place of God's tabernacle. For he took flesh from this tabernacle, and made for us the hidden place of the tabernacle: so that his tabernacle are the other members, those believing in him, but the hidden place of the tabernacle is he himself. *For you are dead*, says the Apostle, *and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^10].

**11.** [v 6.] Or do you wish to know that he speaks of him? For the rock is Christ. Hear what follows: *Because he has hidden me in his tabernacle, in the day of my evils: he has protected me in the hidden place of his tabernacle.* You were asking what the hidden place of the tabernacle is, hear what follows: *On a rock he has exalted me.* Therefore in Christ he has exalted me. Because on the dust you humbled yourself, on a rock he has exalted you. But Christ is above, while you are still below. Hear what follows: *And now he has exalted my head above my enemies. And now*, before I come to that house, where I wish to dwell all the days of my life, before I come to that contemplation of the Lord: *And now he has exalted my head above my enemies.* Still I suffer the enemies of the body of Christ, still I am not exalted above the enemies; but *he has exalted my head above my enemies.* Now our head Christ is in heaven, still our enemies can rage against us: we are not yet exalted above them; but our head is now there. Whence he said this: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^11] He said that he was in us here below: therefore we too are in him there above, because *and now he has exalted my head above my enemies.* Behold what a pledge we have, whence we too by faith and hope and love are with our head in heaven forever: because he himself too by divinity, goodness, unity, is with us on earth unto the consummation of the world.

**12.** *I have gone around, and have sacrificed in his tabernacle a victim of jubilation*: we sacrifice a victim of jubilation, we sacrifice a victim of gladness, a victim of rejoicing, a victim of thanksgiving, which cannot be unfolded in words. But where do we sacrifice? In his very tabernacle, in the holy Church. What, then, do we sacrifice? A most abundant and unspeakable joy, by no words, by an ineffable voice. This is the victim of jubilation. Whence sought, whence found? By going around. *I have gone around*, he says, *and have sacrificed in his tabernacle a victim of jubilation.* Let your mind go around through the whole creation: from every side the creation will cry out to you: God made me. Whatever delights you in the work of art commends the artist; and the more if you go around the whole, the consideration conceives praise of the artist. You see the heavens, they are great works of God. You see the earth, God made the numbers of seeds, the diversities of sprouts, the multitude of animals. Go around still from the heavens to the earth, leave nothing out: from every side all things resound the creator to you; and the very forms of the creatures are as it were voices of those praising the creator. But who can unfold the whole creation? who can unfold it in praises? who can worthily praise heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them? And these indeed are visible things. Who can worthily praise the Angels, Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, and Powers? Who can worthily praise this very thing that thrives in us, quickening the body, moving the members, putting forth the senses, and embracing so many things in memory, distinguishing so many things by understanding, who can worthily praise it? But if in these creatures of God human speech so labors, in the creator what does it do, except that, speech failing, jubilation alone remains? *I have gone around, and have sacrificed in his tabernacle a victim of jubilation.*

**13.** There is also another sense, which seems to me to pertain rather to the connection of the psalm. For because he had said that he was exalted on a rock, which is Christ; and that his head was exalted above his enemies, which is Christ: he also wished himself, who was exalted on the rock, to be understood as exalted in that same head of his above his enemies, referring this to the honor of the Church, to which the persecution of the enemies yielded: and because this was done through the faith of the world, *I have gone around*, he says, *and have sacrificed in his tabernacle a victim of jubilation*: that is, I have considered the faith of the world, in which my head has been exalted above those who persecuted me; and in his very tabernacle, that is, in the Church spread throughout the whole world, I have ineffably praised the Lord.

**14.** [v 7.] *I will sing and make melody to the Lord*: we shall be secure, and secure we shall sing, and secure we shall make melody, when we shall contemplate the delight of the Lord, and be protected, his temple, in that incorruption, when death will be swallowed up in victory. Now what? Because those joys have already been spoken of, which we shall have, when we have received that one petition. Now what? *Hear, Lord, my voice*: let us groan now, let us pray now. Groaning is only of the wretched, prayer is only of the needy. Prayer will pass, praise will succeed; weeping will pass, joy will succeed. Meanwhile now, while we are in the days of our evils, let not our prayer to God cease, of whom we ask that one thing; and let us not desist from that petition, until, by his giving and leading, we come to it. *Hear, Lord, my voice, with which I have cried to you; have mercy on me, and hear me*: that one thing he asks, so long praying, weeping, groaning, he asks but one thing. He has finished all desires, that one thing remains which he asks.

**15.** [v 8.] Hear that he asks this: *To you my heart has said: I have sought your face.* This, then, is what he said a little before: *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord. To you my heart has said: I have sought your face.* If our joy were in this sun, our heart would not say: *I have sought your face;* but the eyes of our body. To whom does our heart say: *I have sought your face*, but to him who pertains to the eye of the heart? This light the eyes of the flesh seek, that light the eyes of the heart seek. But you wish to see that light which is seen by the eyes of the heart: because that light is God. *For God is light*, says John, *and there is no darkness in him at all*[^12]. Do you wish, then, to see that light? Cleanse the eye whereby it is seen: *Blessed*, for, *are the pure of heart, for they shall see God*[^13].

**16.** [v 9.] *To you my heart has said, I have sought your face, your face, Lord, I will seek*: one thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek, your face: *Do not turn away your face from me.* How he has fixed himself in that one petition! Do you wish to obtain it? Do not ask anything else: be content with the one thing, because the one thing will be enough for you. *To you my heart has said: I have sought your face; your face, Lord, I will seek. Do not turn away your face from me: do not turn aside in anger from your servant.* Magnificently, nothing more divine can be said. Those who truly love perceive this. Another would wish to be happy and immortal in those pleasures of earthly desires which he loves: and perhaps for this reason he would worship God, and pray, that he might live here long in his delights, and that nothing might perish for him which earthly desire possesses, neither gold, nor silver, nor, if any estates delighted his eyes, nor that his friends should die, nor his children, nor his wife, nor his dependents; in these delights he would wish always to live. But because he cannot always, for he knows himself mortal; perhaps for this he worships God, and for this prays to God, and for this groans to God, that all these things may be supplied to him even into old age. And if God said to him: Behold, I make you immortal in these things; he would receive it as a great good, and, congratulating himself with the exultation of joys, would not contain himself. This one does not wish this, who has asked one thing of the Lord. But what does he wish? To contemplate the delight of the Lord all the days of his life. And again, another who would worship God in this way and for this cause, if those temporal things were present to him, would not fear God angry, except lest he take that away. This one does not fear him angry for this reason; since he said even of his enemies: That they may eat my flesh. Whence does he fear him angry? Lest he take away what he has loved. What has he loved? Your face. Therefore he thinks this to be the Lord's anger, if he turn away his face from him. Lord, *do not turn aside in anger from your servant.* It might perhaps be answered to him in this way: Why do you fear, lest he turn aside from you in anger? Rather, if he turns aside from you in anger, he will not take vengeance on you: if you run into him angry, he will take vengeance on you. Wish, then, rather that he turn aside from you in anger. No, he says. For he knows what he desires. His anger is nothing but the turning away of his face. What if he will make you immortal in these delights and the pleasure of earthly joys? Such a lover answers: I do not wish it; whatever is to me besides him is not sweet: whatever my Lord wishes to give me, let him take it all away, and give me himself. *Do not turn aside in anger from your servant.* Perhaps he turns aside from some, not in anger; as from certain ones who say to him: *Turn away your face from my sins*[^14]. When he turns away his face from your sins, he does not turn aside from you in anger. Let him, then, turn away his face from your sins: but let him not turn away his face from you.

**17.** *Be my helper, do not forsake me.* For behold, I am on the way; I have asked of you one thing, to dwell in your house all the days of my life, to contemplate your delight, to be protected, your temple; this one thing I have asked: but that I may arrive at it, I am on the way. Perhaps you will say to me: Strive, walk; I have given you free choice, you are of your own will; pursue the way, seek peace and follow it; do not turn aside from the way, do not remain there, do not look back; persevere in walking, since he who perseveres to the end, he will be saved. Now you, having received free choice, as it were presume whence you may walk: do not presume on yourself; if he forsakes you, on the very way you will fail, you will fall, you will stray, you will remain behind. Say, then, to him: You have indeed given me a free will, but without you my effort is nothing to me: *Be my helper, do not forsake me; nor despise me, O God my savior.* For you help who founded me, you do not desert who created me.

**18.** [v 10.] *Because my father and my mother have forsaken me.* He made himself a little one to God: he made him father, he made him mother. He is father, because he founded, because he calls, because he commands, because he rules: mother, because he cherishes, because he nourishes, because he suckles, because he sustains. *My father and my mother have forsaken me; but the Lord has taken me up*, both to be ruled and to be nourished. Mortal parents begot, children succeeded, mortals to mortals, and for this they were born who should succeed, that those who begot should pass away: he who created me will not pass away, I will not withdraw from him. *My father and my mother have forsaken me: but the Lord has taken me up.* Besides even those two parents, of whose flesh we were born, the father male, and the mother female, as Adam and Eve; besides those two parents, we have here another father, and another mother, or rather we had. The father according to the world is the devil, and he was our father while we were unbelievers; for to unbelievers the Lord says: *You are of your father the devil*[^15]. If he is the father of all the impious, who works in the sons of distrust, who is the mother? There is a certain city, which is called Babylon: this city is the society of all the lost from east to west; it has the earthly kingdom. According to this city a certain commonwealth is named, which you now see growing old and diminishing: this was our first mother, in this we were born. We have known another father, God; we have left the devil. For when does he dare to draw near to those whom he has taken up who overcomes all things? We have known another mother, the heavenly Jerusalem, which is the holy Church, a portion of which is on pilgrimage on earth: we have left Babylon. *My father and my mother have forsaken me*: they no longer have what they may bestow on me; because even when they seemed to bestow on me, you were bestowing, and I imputed it to them.

**19.** For by whom is anything bestowed on a man even according to this world, but by God? Or what is taken away from a man, unless he has commanded or permitted it, who gave it? But vain men think that the demons whom they worship bestow this; and sometimes they say to themselves: God is necessary for eternal life, for that spiritual life; but these powers ought to be worshiped by us, for the sake of these temporal things. O emptiness of the human race! You love more the things for the sake of which you wish to worship these: surely you have to worship these more, not to say more, certainly at least as much. But God does not wish to be worshiped with them, nor even if he himself be worshiped much more, and they much less. What then, you will say, are these too not necessary for these things? No. But it is to be feared lest, angry, they harm. They will not harm unless he permits. These always have the will to harm, nor, if they are appeased, nor if they are entreated, do they cease to wish to harm: for this is proper to their malevolence. Therefore what will you do by worshiping them, but offend him, by whose being offended you will be given over to their power: so that those who could do nothing to you when he is appeased, may do whatever they wish when he is angry. And that you may know how vainly you worship these, as it were for the sake of temporal things, whoever you are who think these things: have all who worship Neptune not been shipwrecked? or have all who blaspheme Neptune not reached harbor? have all women who worship Juno given birth well? or have all who blaspheme Juno given birth badly? From this let your Charity understand, that men are vain who wish to worship these even for the sake of these earthly things. For if they were to be worshiped for the sake of these earthly things, their worshipers alone would abound in all earthly things. And even if this were so, we ought to flee such gifts and ask one thing of the Lord. To this is added, that even these things he gives, who is offended when these are worshiped. Therefore let our father and our mother forsake us: let the devil forsake us, let the city Babylon forsake us: let the Lord take us up, to be consoled with temporal things, to be made blessed with eternal. *Because my father and my mother have forsaken me: but the Lord has taken me up.*

**20.** [v 11.] Now, then, he has been taken up by the Lord, that city and its ruler the devil being deserted, because the ruler of the impious is the devil, the ruler of this world of darkness. Of what darkness? Of sins, of unbelievers. Whence to believers the Apostle now says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^16]. Therefore, now taken up by him, what shall we say? *Establish a law for me, Lord, in your way.* Have you dared to ask for a law? What if he says to you: You will fulfill the law? if I give a law, you will fulfill it. He would not dare to ask, unless he first said: *But the Lord has taken me up.* He would not dare to ask, unless he first said: *Be my helper.* Therefore if you help, if you take me up, give a law: *Establish a law for me, Lord, in your way.* Therefore establish a law for me in your Christ. For the way itself spoke to us, and said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^17]. The law in Christ is a law with mercy. He is the wisdom, of which it is written: *She carries law and mercy on her tongue*[^18]. And if you offend in anything against the law, he who shed his blood for you pardons you when you confess: only do not desert the way; say to him: Be my upholder: *And direct me on the straight path because of my enemies.* Give a law, but do not take away mercy; who said in another psalm: *For he will also give mercy, who gave the law*[^19]. Therefore: *Establish a law for me, Lord, in your way*, this pertains to the precept: to mercy what pertains? *And direct me*, he says, *on the straight path, because of my enemies.*

**21.** [v 12.] *Do not hand me over to the souls of those who trouble me*: that is, lest I consent to those who trouble me. For if you consent to one troubling you, into his soul, in a manner he will not devour your flesh, but with a perverse will will eat your soul. *Do not hand over my soul to those who trouble me.* Hand over, if it pleases, into the hands of those who trouble. For the martyrs said this, and he handed over his own into the hands of those who troubled. But what did he hand over? The flesh. As it is said in the book of Job: *The earth has been handed over into the hands of the impious*[^20]: the flesh has been handed over into the hands of the persecutor. *Do not hand me over*, not my flesh, but me. To you, soul, I speak, to you, mind, I speak; I do not say: Do not hand over my flesh into the hands of those who trouble me; but: *Do not hand me over to the souls of those who trouble me.* And how are men handed over to the souls of those who trouble them? *Because unjust witnesses have risen up against me.* Now because there are unjust witnesses, and they say many evil things about me, and detract from me in many things; if I am handed over to their souls, I too will lie, and I will be their associate, not a sharer in your truth, but a co-sharer in falsehood against you: *Unjust witnesses have risen up against me; and iniquity has lied to itself.* To itself, not to me: to itself let it always lie, but to me let it not lie. If you hand me over to the souls of those who trouble me, that is, if I consent to their wills; iniquity will now have lied not to itself, but also to me: but if they rage as much as they wish, and try to hinder my courses, yet if you do not give me over to their souls; by not consenting to their wills, I shall remain, and shall continue in your truth, and iniquity will lie, not to me, but to itself.

**22.** [v 13.] He returns to that one thing after these dangers, after labors, after difficulties, amid the hands of those persecuting and troubling, seething, panting, laboring, and firm and certain by his taking him up, by his helping, by his leading, by his ruling. Yet after that going around and jubilation, exulting with joy, and groaning in labors, he sighed at the last, and said: *I believe I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.* O good things of the Lord, sweet, immortal, incomparable, everlasting, unchangeable! And when shall I see you, good things of the Lord? I believe I shall see them, but not in the land of the dying. *I believe I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.* The Lord will rescue me from the land of the dying, who for my sake deigned to take up the land of the dying, and to die amid the hands of the dying; the Lord will rescue me from the land of the dying: *I believe I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.* Sighing he said it, laboring he said it, in peril he said it amid a great crowd of temptations: but yet hoping all from his mercy, to whom he said: *Establish a law for me, Lord.*

**23.** [v. 14.] And what does he say, the one who appointed the law for him? Let us hear also the voice of the Lord from above, exhorting us, consoling us; the voice of him whom we have in place of the father and mother who have forsaken us—let us hear his voice. For he has heard our groanings, he has seen our sighs, he has gazed on our longing, and our one petition, our one request, he has gladly received through Christ our advocate; and until we complete this pilgrimage—a delay he intends, not a withdrawal of what he promised—he has said to us: *Wait for the Lord*[^21]. You will not be waiting on a liar, nor on one who can be deceived, nor on one who will fail to find what to give. The Almighty has promised, the sure one has promised, the truthful one has promised: *Wait for the Lord, be strong*[^22]. Do not grow slack: do not be among those to whom it is said, *Woe to those who have lost endurance*[^23]! *Wait for the Lord*[^24]—this is said to all of us, and it is said to one man. We are one in Christ, we are the body of Christ, we who long for that one thing, we who ask that one thing, we who groan in these days of our evils, we who believe that we shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living; to all of us, who are one in the One, it is said: *Wait for the Lord, be strong, and let your heart take courage; and wait for the Lord*[^25]. What else could he say to you than to repeat what you have heard? *Wait for the Lord, be strong*[^26]. So then, whoever has lost endurance has become soft, has lost his vigor. Let men hear this, let women hear this, for in one man there is both man and woman. Such a one in Christ is neither male nor female. *Wait for the Lord, be strong, and let your heart take courage; and wait for the Lord*[^27]. By waiting for the Lord you will have him, you will have the one for whom you wait. Long for something else, if you find something greater, something better, something sweeter.
