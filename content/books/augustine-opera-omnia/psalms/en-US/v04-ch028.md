# Exposition of Psalm 99 (98)

**1.** Brothers, it ought already to be known to your Love, as to children of the Church and to those instructed in the school of Christ through all the writings of our ancient fathers who wrote the words of God and the mighty works of God, that they meant to take counsel for us, who at this time were going to believe in Christ; who came to us at the opportune time, first lowly, afterward to come on high. For first he came to stand before a judge; afterward he is to come as a judge to sit, so that the human race may stand before him according to its merit. Now many heralds went before him, as before a great judge, who was still to come in lowliness. Many heralds went before him while he was still to be born of the Virgin Mary, to be an infant, and to suck milk; many heralds went before the Word of God, through whom all things were made, while he was still to be a little child, and they spoke of these times to come: but they spoke in such a way that they covered their meanings with certain figures of things, and the very veil by which the truth was covered in the books of the ancients would then be taken away, when the truth itself should spring up from the earth. For so it is said in the psalm: *Truth has sprung up from the earth, and justice has looked down from heaven*[^1]. Now therefore our whole intention, when we hear a Psalm, when we hear a Prophet, when we hear the Law—all of which were written before our Lord Jesus Christ came in the flesh—is to see Christ there, to understand Christ there. Let your Love, then, attend with us to this psalm, and let us seek Christ here: surely he will appear to those who seek, who first appeared to those who did not seek; and he will not abandon those who desire him, who redeemed those who neglected him. See, the Psalm has begun with him, it is spoken of him.

**2.** [v. 1.] *The Lord has reigned, let the peoples be angry*[^2]. For our Lord Jesus Christ began to reign, began to be preached, after he rose from the dead and ascended into heaven, after he filled his disciples with the confidence of the Holy Spirit, so that they would not fear the death which he had already slain in himself. The Lord Christ, then, began to be preached, so that those who wished to have salvation might believe in him; and the peoples who worshiped idols were angered. Those who worshiped what they had made were angered, because he was being announced by whom they themselves were made. For he was announcing himself through his disciples, he who wished them to be turned toward him by whom they were made, and to be turned away from those things which they themselves had made. They were angered against their Lord on behalf of their idol, who, if on behalf of their idol they were angered against their slave, would deserve condemnation. For their slave was better than their idol: God made their slave, a craftsman made their idol. They were so angered on behalf of their idol that they did not fear to be angry against their Lord. But *let them be angry* is foretold, not commanded; for in prophecy it is said: *The Lord has reigned, let the peoples be angry*. There is something brought about even by the peoples' anger: let them be angry, and in their anger let the martyrs be crowned. What did they do to those who announced the word of truth, to the clouds of Christ going about the world of the earth and raining down upon the field of God? What did those who were angry do to them, except that in their hands the flesh was afflicted, and in the hands of Christ the spirit was crowned? Nor did that very flesh which the persecutors were able to kill so die that it should perish forever: for it will have its own time when it too shall rise; because the Lord has already shown the resurrection of the flesh in himself. From there he willed to receive it from us, so that we might be able not to despair of our own. Therefore, brothers, the flesh of the servants which the worshipers of idols killed will rise again in its own time: the idols which Christ has broken, the craftsman will never make again. You heard, when Jeremiah was read before the apostolic reading, if you applied your ear; you saw there the present times in which we now live. For he said: *Let the gods who did not make heaven and earth perish from the earth and from under heaven*[^3]. He did not say: Let the gods who did not make heaven and earth perish from heaven and from earth; because they were never in heaven: but what did he say? *Let the gods who did not make heaven and earth perish from the earth.* As if he answered concerning the earth, and what he might answer concerning heaven was lacking, because they were not in heaven: he said "earth" twice, because the earth itself is under heaven. *Let them perish from the earth and from under heaven*, from their own temples. See whether it is not coming to pass, whether it has not already in great part come to pass: for what has remained, or how much has remained? The idols have remained in the hearts of the pagans more than in the places of the temples.

**3.** Therefore: *The Lord has reigned, let the peoples be angry. He who sits upon the Cherubim*: you understand, *has reigned. Let the earth be moved*[^4]. Again he said: *Let the peoples be angry.* For what he said, *the Lord*, this he repeated, *He who sits upon the Cherubim*; and what he said, *has reigned*, he made to be understood in the other verse; and what he said, *Let the peoples be angry*, this he said, *Let the earth be moved.* For what are the peoples, except the earth? Let the earth be as angry as it can against him who now sits in heaven. For the Lord was on earth too, and he took up the earth in which to be on earth. He clothed himself with flesh, and first willed to suffer the angry peoples. So that his servants might not fear the anger of the peoples, he first willed to suffer it; and because the anger of the peoples was necessary for his servants, that through those tribulations they might be cured and healed of all their sins, the physician first drank the bitter cup, so that the sick man might not fear to drink. Therefore: *The Lord has reigned, let the peoples be angry*: let the peoples be angry, because out of their anger God brings about many good things. They are angry, and the servants of God are purified; because they are exercised, they are crowned. *Let the peoples be angry. He who sits upon the Cherubim* has reigned; *let the earth be moved.* The Cherubim is the seat of God, as the Scriptures hand down, a certain lofty heavenly seat which we do not see; but the word of God knows it, knows it as his own seat, and the Word of God himself and the Spirit of God told the servants of God where God sits. Not that God sits as a man does: but if you wish God to sit in you, if you are good, you will be the seat of God; for so it is written: *The soul of the just is the seat of wisdom*[^5]. For a throne is in Latin called a seat. For some who knew that Hebrew tongue have also interpreted what the very word "Cherubim" is said to mean in Latin; for "Cherubim" is said in the Hebrew tongue, and they said that the Cherubim is "fullness of knowledge." Therefore, because God surpasses all knowledge, he is said to sit above the fullness of knowledge. Let there be in you, then, the fullness of knowledge, and you too will be the seat of God. But perhaps you will say: And when will the fullness of knowledge be in me? and who can reach so great a height that the fullness of knowledge should be in him? Do you suppose that God wishes this fullness of knowledge to be in us, that we should know either how many the stars are, or how many the grains—I do not say of sand, but of wheat—or how many fruits hang on a tree? He knows all things; for our hairs are numbered before God. But there is another fullness of knowledge which he willed man to know; the knowledge which he willed you to have pertains to the law of God. And who can, you may perhaps say to me, perfectly know the Law, so as to have in himself the fullness of the knowledge of the Law, and be able to be the seat of God? Do not be troubled; it is told you briefly what you have, if you wish to have the fullness of knowledge and to be the seat of God: for the Apostle says: *The fullness of the Law is love*[^6]. What then? You have lost every excuse. Question your heart, see whether it has love. If love is there, the fullness of the Law is there; God now dwells in you, you have been made the seat of God. *Let the peoples be angry*; what will the angry peoples do to him who has been made the seat of God? You attend to those who rage against you; you do not attend to him who sits within you. You have been made heaven, and do you fear the earth? For Scripture says elsewhere that the Lord our God says: *Heaven is my seat*[^7]. If, then, you too, by having the fullness of knowledge and by having love, have surely been made the seat of God, you have been made heaven. For this heaven which we look up at with these eyes of ours is not very precious to God. The heaven of God is the holy souls; the heaven of God is the minds of the angels and all the minds of his servants. Therefore *let the peoples be angry, let the earth be moved*; what will they do, or what will it do, to the seat of God and to the heaven where God sits?

**4.** [v. 2.] *The Lord is great in Zion, and he is exalted above all peoples*[^8]. The Lord is great and exalted in Zion. See, if it was obscure to you, because it was said, *He who sits upon the Cherubim*, and you did not know what the Cherubim is, and perhaps you pictured to your mind a certain heavenly throne, huge and jeweled, and called that itself the Cherubim, flitting through phantasms with a carnal sense: it has been told you that the Cherubim is the fullness of knowledge; and it has been said that the fullness of knowledge—not of any knowledge whatever, but the fullness of the knowledge of the Law—is useful to man; and lest you despair of that very knowledge of the Law, it has been told you briefly: *The fullness of the Law is love.* Have love, then, toward God and toward your neighbor, and you will be the seat of God; you will belong to the Cherubim. But if you still do not understand, hear what follows: *The Lord is great in Zion.* He whom I called to you the one above the Cherubim is great in Zion. Now ask what Zion is. We know that Zion is the city of God. The city which is Jerusalem was called Zion; and it was called so, receiving its name from a certain interpretation, because "Zion" means "watching"—that is, seeing and contemplating. For to watch is to look ahead, or to behold, or to direct one's gaze so as to see. Now every soul is Zion, if it directs its gaze to see the light that is to be seen. For if it attends to its own light, it is darkened; if it attends to that light of his, it is enlightened. Yet since it is clear that Zion is the city of God, what is the city of God except the holy Church? For men loving one another, and loving their God who dwells in them, make a city for God. Since a city is held together by a certain law, the law of these men is love; and that very love is God: for it is plainly written, *God is love*[^9]. Whoever, then, is full of love, is full of God; and many full of love make a city for God. This city of God is called Zion: therefore the Church is Zion. In her is the great God. Be in her, and God will not be apart from you. But when God is in you, because you have been made of Zion, a member of Zion, a citizen of Zion, belonging to the society of the people of God, God will be exalted in you above all peoples, above those who are angry, or above those who were angry. For do you suppose that they were angry then, and now are not angry? They were angry then: but because they were more numerous, they were openly angry; now, because they have become few, they are angry in secret. For the time being their boldness has been broken; their anger too will be ended.

**5.** For do you suppose, brothers, that those whose musical instruments were sounding yesterday are not angry over our fasts? But let us not be angry at them; rather, let us fast for them. For the Lord our God who sits in us has told us, he himself has commanded us, to pray for our enemies, to pray for those who persecute us[^10]: and when the Church does this, the persecutors are nearly finished. For she was heard when she did this, and she is heard when she does it: they prevailed to their own harm, they have been finished to their own good. Do you wish to know how they have been finished? They have been eaten by the Church. You look for them in themselves, and do not find them; look in her who ate them, and they are found in her bowels. For passing over to the Church, they have been made Christians; the persecutors have perished, the preachers have increased. Therefore on their feast days, because we see that those who are left still rave in their evil and perverse pleasures, we ask God for them, that those who listen with delight to the instrument may listen with greater delight to the voice of God. For that which sounds without reason does not delight the ear, while the word of God does not delight the heart. But for this reason we pray for them, when on their feast days we do not fast, that they may become a spectacle to themselves. For when they see themselves, they will be displeased with themselves; but they are not displeased with themselves for this reason, that they do not attend to themselves. The drunkard is not displeased with himself, but the sober man is displeased with him. Show me a man who already rejoices in God, who lives gravely, who sighs after that eternal peace which God has promised him; and see that when he looks back at a man dancing to the instrument, he grieves over him as one raving more than over a delirious man in a fever. Therefore, if we know their evils, because we too have been freed from those very evils, let us grieve over them; and if we grieve over them, let us pray for them; and that we may be heard, let us fast for them. For we are not celebrating our own fasts on their feast days. There are other fasts of ours which we celebrate during the coming days of the Pasch, and during one and another solemnity that we keep in Christ: but during these days we fast for this reason, that when they themselves rejoice, we may groan for them. For by their joy they remind us of our grief, and they make us recall how wretched they still are. But because we see that many have been freed from there, where we too once were, we ought not to despair of them either. And if they are still angry, let us pray; and if that little portion of the earth which has remained is still moved, let us continue in groaning for them, that God may grant understanding to them too, and that they may hear with us these voices over which we now rejoice. *The Lord is great in Zion, and he is exalted above all peoples*[^11].

**6.** [vv. 3-4.] *Let them confess to your great name*[^12]. Those very peoples, above whom you are great in Zion, let them now *confess to your great name.* Your name was small when they were angry: it has become great; let them now confess. How do we say that the name of Christ was small, before Christ was gloriously made known? Because his name is called his fame. The name was small; but now the name has become great. What nation is there that has not heard the name of Christ? Now, then, let the peoples confess to your great name, who before were angry at your small name: *let them confess to your great name.* Why should they confess? *Because it is terrible and holy*[^13]. Your very name is terrible and holy. He is preached as crucified, preached as humbled, preached as judged, in such a way that he may come on high as well, may come living, may come in power to judge. Now he spares the blaspheming peoples, because the patience of God leads to repentance[^14]. For he who now spares does not have to spare always; nor is he who is now preached that he may be feared not going to come to judge. He is going to come, my brothers, he is going to come: let us fear him, and let us so live that we may be found at his right hand. For he is going to come and to judge, so as to set some on the left, others on the right[^15]. And he will not do that in just any way, so as perhaps to err with regard to men, so that one who is to be set on the right is set on the left, or one who ought to stand on the left is, by God's erring, set on the right: he cannot err, so as to set the evil there where he ought to set the good; nor will he set the good there where he ought to set the evil. If he cannot err, we err, if we do not fear; but if we fear now, then we shall not have anything to fear. *Because it is terrible and holy; and the honor of the king loves judgment*[^16]. So, then, let the peoples fear, that they may correct themselves: not presuming too much on his mercy, letting themselves go and living badly; for he loves mercy, but he also loves judgment. What is mercy? That he now preaches the truth to you, that he now cries out to you to be converted. Is it a small mercy, that, though you have lived in evil deeds, and he has not yet carried you off while you were sinning, he should forgive your sins to you who believe? Is it a small mercy? Do you suppose that mercy will always be such that he punishes no one? Do not think so. Terrible and holy is his name; *and the honor of the king loves judgment.* For judgment is unjust, and is altogether no judgment, unless each one's own deserts are rendered to each, according as each has done in the body, whether good or evil[^17]: *and the honor of the king loves judgment.* Therefore let us fear, therefore let us do justice, therefore let us do equity.

**7.** But who does equity? who does justice? A sinful man, an unjust man, a perverse man, a man turned away from the light of truth? What ought a man to do? Only to turn himself to God, so that he himself may form in him the equity which he himself cannot form, but can only deform. A man is fit to wound himself; is he in any way fit to heal himself? He falls sick when he wills, but he does not rise when he wills. If he wishes, let him live intemperately either in cold or in heat; on the day he wills, he falls sick: but when by living intemperately he has begun to fall sick, let him rise when he wills; let him who lay down when he willed rise, if he can, when he wills. To lie sick, he had his own intemperance as the necessary cause; but to rise, he has need of the physician's medicine. So then, that a man should sin, he is sufficient for himself unto sinning: that he should be justified, he is not sufficient for himself, unless he is justified by him who alone is just. Therefore, so that men might give themselves to him to be formed unto justice, when this psalm had struck terror into the peoples and had said, *Let them confess to your great name, because it is terrible and holy; and the honor of the king loves judgment*, then, as it were to peoples now terrified and seeking how the just ought to live, because in themselves they cannot have justice, it commended to them the very fashioner of their justice, and went on to say: *You have prepared equity; judgment and justice in Jacob you have done*[^18]. For we too ought to have judgment, we ought to have justice; but he does judgment and justice in us, who made us in whom he might do it. How ought we too to have judgment and justice? You have judgment when you distinguish evil from good; and justice when you follow the good and turn aside from evil. By distinguishing, you have judgment; by doing, you have justice. *Turn aside from evil*, he says, *and do good; seek peace, and pursue it*[^19]. First you ought to have judgment, and afterward justice. What judgment? That first you judge what is evil and what is good. And what justice? That you turn aside from evil and do good. But this you will not have from yourself; for see what he said: *Judgment and justice in Jacob you have done.*

**8.** [v. 5.] *Exalt the Lord our God*[^20]. Truly exalt him, rightly exalt him. Let us praise him, let us exalt him who made the very justice that we have; he himself made it in us. For who made justice in us, except he who justified us? And of Christ it is said: *Who justifies the ungodly*[^21]. We, then, are the ungodly, he the justifier, since he himself made in us the very justice by which we may please him, so that he may set us on the right and not on the left: so that he may say to those set on the right, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^22]; and may not set us on the left, among those to whom he is to say, *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^23]. He who is going to crown in us not our merits but his own gifts, how greatly ought he to be exalted? *Exalt the Lord our God.*

**9.** *And adore his footstool, for he is holy*[^24]. What have we to adore? *His footstool.* A footstool is called a footrest. What the Greeks call &#965;&#960;&#959;&#960;&#972;&#948;&#953;&#959;&#957;, the Latins called *scabellum* (footstool); and others called it *suppedaneum* (footrest). But see, brothers, what he commands us to adore. In another place of the Scriptures it is said: *Heaven is my seat, and the earth is the footstool of my feet*[^25]. Does he then command us to adore the earth, because he said in another place that it is the footstool of God's feet? And how shall we adore the earth, when Scripture says plainly: *You shall adore the Lord your God*[^26]? And here it says: *Adore his footstool*; but, explaining to me what his footstool is, it says: *The earth is the footstool of my feet.* I am made uncertain: I fear to adore the earth, lest he condemn me who made heaven and earth; on the other hand, I fear not to adore the footstool of my Lord's feet, because the Psalm says to me: *Adore his footstool.* I ask what his footstool is; and Scripture tells me: *The earth is the footstool of my feet.* Wavering, I turn to Christ, because it is he I am seeking here; and I find how the earth may be adored without impiety, how his footstool may be adored without impiety. For he took up earth from earth; because flesh is from earth, and he took flesh from the flesh of Mary. And because he walked here in that very flesh, and gave that very flesh to us to eat for our salvation—and no one eats that flesh unless he has first adored it—it has been found how such a footstool of the Lord's feet may be adored, and not only do we not sin by adoring, but we sin by not adoring. But does the flesh give life? The Lord himself said, when he was speaking about the very commending of that earth: *It is the Spirit that gives life; the flesh profits nothing*[^27]. Therefore, when you bow down and prostrate yourself before any earth, do not regard it as earth, but as that Holy One whose footstool is what you adore; for it is on his account that you adore. Therefore here too he added: *Adore his footstool, for he is holy.* Who is holy? He in whose honor you adore his footstool. And when you adore him, do not remain in thought upon the flesh, and so fail to be given life by the Spirit: *For it is the Spirit*, he says, *that gives life; the flesh profits nothing.* Now at that time, when the Lord commended this, he had been speaking about his flesh, and had said: *Unless a man eat my flesh, he shall not have eternal life in him*[^28]. Certain of his disciples, about seventy, were scandalized, and said: *This saying is hard; who can understand it?*[^29] And they drew back from him, and walked with him no longer. It seemed hard to them that he said: *Unless a man eat my flesh, he shall not have eternal life.* They took it foolishly, they thought of it carnally, and they supposed that the Lord was going to cut off certain little pieces from his body and give them to them, and they said: *This saying is hard.* They themselves were hard, not the saying. For if they had not been hard, but gentle, they would have said to themselves: He does not say this without cause, except that there is some hidden mystery there. They would have remained with him, gentle, not hard; and they would have learned from him what those who remained learned, when the others departed. For when the twelve disciples had remained with him, after the others drew back, they suggested to him, as if grieving over their death, that they were scandalized at his word and had drawn back. But he instructed them, and said to them: *It is the Spirit that gives life; the flesh profits nothing: the words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life*[^30]. Understand spiritually what I have said: you are not going to eat this body that you see, nor to drink that blood which those who will crucify me are going to shed. I have commended to you a certain mystery; understood spiritually, it will give you life. Although it must be celebrated visibly, it must nevertheless be understood invisibly. *Exalt the Lord our God, and adore his footstool, for he is holy.*

**10.** [vv. 6-8.] *Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those who call upon his name. They called upon the Lord, and he heard them; in the pillar of cloud he spoke to them*[^31]. Those ancients—Moses and Aaron and Samuel, servants of God—were great among the ancients. You know that Moses led the people of Israel out of Egypt through the Red Sea by the power of God, and led them in the wilderness; and how great wonders God did at that time by the hand of Moses, all know who gladly hear these Scriptures in the Church, or read them at home, or have learned them in any way at all. Aaron was his brother, whom he also ordained a priest. And there indeed no one seems to be a priest except Aaron. For in those writings Aaron is plainly named the priest of God[^32]; of Moses it is not said there that he was a priest. But if he was not this, what was he? could he be greater than a priest? This psalm makes clear that he too was a priest: *Moses and Aaron among his priests.* They were therefore priests of the Lord. Samuel is read of afterward in the book of Kingdoms: this is the Samuel of the times of David; for he it was who anointed holy David[^33]. Samuel grew up in the temple from the beginning of his life. His mother was barren: wishing to have a son, she prayed to the Lord with great groaning, and, in asking that God would give her a son, she showed that she did not wish to have carnally the one whom he who willed him to be gave her at his birth. For she vowed him to the Lord God, and said: *If a male be born to me, he shall serve your temple*[^34]: and so she did. Holy Samuel was born and was with his mother at the time of nursing; as soon as she had weaned him, she gave him to the temple, that he might grow up there, be strengthened there in spirit, serve God there: he became a great priest, a holy priest in that time. He mentions these men, and through these he wishes us to understand all the saints. But why has he named them here? Because we said that here we ought to understand Christ. Let your Holiness take note. He said above: *Exalt the Lord our God, and adore his footstool, for he is holy*: commending a certain one—that is, our Lord Jesus Christ—whose footstool is to be adored, because he took up flesh, in which he might appear to the human race; and wishing to show us that the ancient fathers too preached him, because he, our Lord Jesus Christ, is the true priest, he mentioned these men, because in the pillar of cloud God spoke to them. What is it, *in the pillar of cloud*? He was speaking through figures. For if he was speaking in a certain little cloud, those obscure sayings were prefiguring some manifest one. But that "some one" is now not "some one"; because he is known by us, our Lord Jesus Christ. *Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those who call upon his name. They called upon the Lord, and he heard them; in the pillar of cloud he spoke to them.* He who first spoke in the pillar of cloud, he himself has spoken to us in the footstool of his feet; that is, in the earth, in the flesh he took up, from which we adore the footstool of his feet, for he is holy. He himself was speaking from the cloud what was not then understood; he has spoken in the footstool of his feet, and the words of his cloud have been understood. *In the pillar of cloud he spoke to them.*

**11.** Let us attend, then, brothers: see whom and what kind of saints he has named. *They kept his testimonies, and his precepts which he gave them*[^35]. They kept them, certainly; pay attention. *They kept his testimonies, and his precepts which he gave them.* This he says, and it cannot be denied. Did they then have no sin? How could that be? When they kept his precepts, they kept his testimonies. See how he wishes us to be formed, lest we presume as if upon perfect justice. See, Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those who call upon his name, to whom he spoke from the pillar of cloud, heard so plainly those who kept his testimonies and the precepts which he gave them. *O Lord*, he says, *our God, you heard them; O God, you were merciful to them*[^36]. God is not said to be merciful except toward sins: when he grants pardon, then he is said to be merciful. And what did he have in these men that he might punish, so as to be merciful by forgiving? He was merciful in granting pardon for sins, and merciful in punishing too. For what follows? *You were merciful to them, and taking vengeance upon all their inclinations*[^37]. Even in taking vengeance you were merciful: not only in granting pardon for sins, but also in taking vengeance you were merciful. See, my brothers, what he has commended here; take note. God is angry with the man whom he does not scourge while he sins: for to the one to whom he is truly merciful, he not only grants pardon for sins, lest they harm him for the age to come, but also chastises him, lest it ever delight him to sin.

**12.** Come, brothers; if we ask how vengeance was taken upon them, the Lord will be present for me to tell. Let us seek out these three persons—Moses, and Aaron, and Samuel—and how vengeance was taken upon them, because he said: *Taking vengeance upon all their inclinations*: meaning surely those inclinations of theirs which the Lord knew in their hearts, which men did not know. For indeed they lived in the midst of God's people without complaint from men. But what shall we say? that Moses perhaps had a former, sinful life? For after striking a man down, he fled from Egypt[^38]. Aaron too had a former life that displeased God. For he himself permitted an idol to be made for the raving and furious people, and an idol was made for the people of God to adore[^39]. What did Samuel do, given to the temple as an infant[^40]? He spent all his ages amid the holy mysteries of God, a servant of God from the beginning of his life. Nothing was ever said about Samuel, nothing by men. Perhaps God knew there something to purify; for even what already seems perfect to men is, in comparison with that perfection, still imperfect. Craftsmen make many things and show them to the unskilled; and when the unskilled have already judged them to be perfect, the craftsmen polish them, knowing what they still lack, so that men marvel that so great a polishing has come to things which they had already pronounced perfect. This happens in buildings, and in paintings, and in garments, and in nearly every kind of craft. First they judge that thing to be already, as it were, perfect, so that their eyes desire nothing more: but the unskilled eye judges one thing, the rule of the art judges another. So too those saints lived before the eyes of God, as if without fault, as if perfect, as if angels: but he knew what they lacked, he who was taking vengeance upon all their inclinations. And he took vengeance not in anger, but in mercy: he took vengeance for this purpose, that he might perfect what was begun, not condemn what was cast out. God, then, was taking vengeance upon all their inclinations. How did he take vengeance upon Samuel? where is that vengeance itself? This I say, that Christians may know—those who have now come to know Christ here, to whom he came in the footstool of his feet, whom he so loved, for whom he shed his blood—let them know how they are beaten when they have made much progress. We seek vengeance in Moses; he has almost none, except that at the end God says to him: *Go up the mountain, and die*[^41]. He says to an old man: *Die*: he had already spent his ages; was he never going to die? What sort of vengeance is that? Did he show his vengeance there, in saying: *You shall not enter into the land of promise*[^42], into which the people were going to enter? Moses bore a certain figure of certain men. For one who has entered into the kingdom of heaven, would it be a great punishment not to come into that land which was promised for a time, so as to show a shadow and pass away? Did not many faithless men enter into that land? did not many, living in that land, do many evils and offend God? did they not even follow idolatry in that very land? Was it a great thing not to have given that land to Moses? But he willed Moses to bear the figure of those who were under the Law, because through Moses the Law was given[^43]: and he showed that those who would wish to be under the Law and would not wish to be under grace would not enter into the land of promise. Therefore what was said to Moses was a figure, not a punishment. To an old man, what punishment is death? Not to enter into that land into which the unworthy entered—what punishment is that? But of Aaron, what was said? He too died an old man; his sons succeeded him in the priesthood[^44]; his son afterward administered the priesthood: how did he take vengeance upon him too? Samuel too, himself a holy old man, died, leaving his sons as his successors[^45]. What it is that vengeance was taken upon them, I ask, and according to men I do not find: but according to what I know the servants of God suffer, vengeance was taken upon them daily. Read, and see the acts of vengeance, and you who make progress, bear the acts of vengeance. Daily they suffered contradicting peoples, daily they suffered men living unjustly; and they were forced to live among those whose life they daily rebuked. This was the vengeance. He to whom it is small does not yet make progress. For another's injustice torments you just so much as you have withdrawn from your own. For when you have become wheat—that is, good grass from good seed, a son of the kingdom—when you have begun to bear fruit, then the weeds will appear to you: *for when the grass had grown and had borne fruit, then the weeds appeared*[^46]. When the weeds begin to appear, you will see yourself among the wicked. You want, as it were, to separate the wicked from yourself, and to separate from the Church all the wicked; the Lord's sentence will answer you: *Let both grow until the harvest, lest perhaps, while you wish to root up the weeds, you root up at the same time the wheat also*[^47]. By the Lord's sentence it will be necessary to spare the weeds; by the condition of a servant it will be necessary to live among the weeds: you cannot separate them, you must bear with them. See how many wounds you suffer in heart, you who, whole in body, live among the wicked. Whoever you are who make progress, you will prove this; you who have already made progress, prove it. These things, then, must be borne; and perhaps this pertains to that: *The servant who knows his master's will, and does not do what is worthy, shall be beaten with many stripes*[^48]. For in many things, the more the will of God becomes known to us, the more our own guilt becomes known to us; and the more that becomes known to us, the more we go into weeping and tears. For we see how just is what God requires of us, in how great an imperfection we still lie; and there comes about in us what was said: *He who adds knowledge, adds sorrow*[^49]. See, let love abound in you, and you will grieve the more over one who sins. The greater the love in you, the more will the one whom you bear with torment you: it will torment you not as one angry at him, but as one grieving for him.

**13.** See the apostle Paul, what he suffered; see who suffered what: *Besides those things which are without*, he says (for he had spoken of many things which he suffered, and he began to speak of the inner ones, besides those which were without, which he suffered from the wicked persecutors of Christ), *my daily pressure, the care of all the churches*[^50]. And see what kind of care, how fatherly, how motherly; see how he was being beaten, that vengeance might be taken upon all his inclinations: we shall speak too of his inclinations, upon which God took vengeance. *Who is weak*, he says, *and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I am not set on fire?*[^51] The greater the love, the greater the wounds from others' sins. He had indeed also received a thorn of the flesh, an angel of Satan, by whom he might be buffeted. See how God was merciful, taking vengeance upon all his inclinations. What are the inclinations upon which he thus took vengeance? He himself set it forth, he himself said: *Lest I be exalted*, he says, *by the greatness of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me*[^52]. He was so perfect that it had nevertheless to be feared lest he be exalted: for God would not put medicine where there was no wound. And he asked that it be taken away; that sick man asked that the medicine be taken away: *For this reason I three times asked the Lord*, he says, *that he would take it from me*—that is, the thorn of the flesh by which he was being buffeted, perhaps some pain of the body: *I asked*, he says, *that he would take it from me; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you, for strength is made perfect in weakness*[^53]. I know whom I am tending; let not the one who is sick give me counsel. Like a biting plaster, it burns you, but it heals you. The sick man asks the physician to take away the plaster; and he does not take it away, except when the place where he had put it has been healed. *Strength is made perfect in weakness.* Therefore, brothers, whoever of us make progress in Christ, let us not suppose that we shall be without the scourge: because, however much we make progress, he himself knows our sins; sometimes he shows them to us too, and we too see our sins. And when we have begun to live among such men that now men do not find anything in us to rebuke, he who knows all things still rebukes, and takes vengeance upon all our inclinations, because he is merciful to us. For if he should not take vengeance, and should abandon us, we perish. *O God, you were merciful to them, and taking vengeance upon all their inclinations.*

**14.** [v. 9.] *Exalt the Lord our God*[^54]. Again we exalt him: he who is good even when he strikes, how is he to be praised, how is he to be exalted? You can show this to your son, and cannot God? For you are not good when you caress your son, and bad when you beat your son. Both when you caress, you are a father, and when you beat, you are a father: you caress for this reason, lest he fail; you beat for this reason, lest he perish. *Exalt the Lord our God, and adore on his holy mountain: for holy is the Lord our God*[^55]. As above he said, *Exalt the Lord our God, and adore his footstool*, and we understood what it is to adore his footstool: so now too, after the exaltation of the Lord our God, lest anyone exalt him apart from his mountain, he commended his mountain as well. Who is his mountain? We read elsewhere of this mountain that a stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and broke all the kingdoms of the earth, and that very stone grew. This is the vision of Daniel that I am relating. This stone that was cut out of the mountain without hands grew, *and became*, it says, *a great mountain, so that it filled the whole face of the earth*[^56]. On that great mountain let us adore, if we wish to be heard. Heretics do not adore on that mountain; because that mountain has filled the whole face of the earth: they have clung to a part, and have lost the whole. If they would acknowledge the Catholic Church, they would adore with us on that mountain. For that stone which was cut out of the mountain without hands, we already see how greatly it has grown, and how many regions of the earth it has occupied, and to what nations it has come. What is the mountain from which the stone was cut without hands? The kingdom of the Jews: first because they worshiped one God. From there the stone, our Lord Jesus Christ, was cut. He himself was spoken of: *The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner*[^57]. That stone, cut out of the mountain without hands, broke all the kingdoms of the earth: we see all the kingdoms of the earth broken by that stone. What were the kingdoms of the earth? The kingdoms of idols, the kingdoms of demons, have been broken. Saturn reigned in many men: where is his kingdom? Mercury reigned in many men: where is his kingdom? It has been broken; those in whom he reigned have been brought back into the kingdom of Christ. How great was the kingdom of Caelestis at Carthage! where now is the kingdom of Caelestis? That stone has broken all the kingdoms of the earth, the stone cut out of the mountain without hands. What is it, cut out of the mountain without hands? Born of the nation of the Jews without the work of men. For all who are born are born of the work of marriage; but he, born of a virgin, was born without hands; for by "hands" human work is signified: where human hands did not reach, where there was no marital embrace, there nevertheless was an offspring. He was born, then, of the mountain without hands, that stone: he grew, and by growing broke all the kingdoms of the earth. And he became a great mountain, and filled the whole face of the earth. This is the Catholic Church, with which rejoice to be in communion. But those who are not in communion with her, because they adore and praise God apart from that mountain, are not heard unto eternal life; even if they are heard as to certain temporal things. Let them not, then, flatter themselves, because God hears them in certain things: for he hears the pagans too in certain things. Do not the pagans cry out to God, and it rains? Why? Because he makes his sun rise upon the good and the wicked, and rains upon the just and the unjust[^58]. Do not boast, then, pagan, because, when you cry out to God, God rains, who rains upon the just and the unjust. He has heard you as to temporal things; he does not hear you as to eternal things, unless you adore on his holy mountain. *Adore the Lord on his holy mountain, for holy is the Lord our God.*

**15.** Let this much from the Psalm suffice for your Love; we have spoken as much as the Lord has granted. And whatever we speak in the name of God, since it is God's—God speaking through us—it is the rain of God: see what kind of earth you are. For when the rain comes upon the earth, if it is good earth, it brings forth good fruit; if it is bad earth, it brings forth thorns: yet the rain is sweet both upon the fruit and upon the thorns. Whoever, on hearing these words, has been made worse, and has produced thorns from the rain, let him expect fire, let him not accuse the rain: but whoever has been made better, and has brought forth crops from good earth, let him expect the barn, let him praise the rain. Or what are the clouds, or what is the rain, except the mercy of God, who does all things toward those whom he loves, and to whom he has granted that they should love him?
