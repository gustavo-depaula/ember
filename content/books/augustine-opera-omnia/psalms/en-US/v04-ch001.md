# Exposition of Psalm 73

**1.** [v. 1.] The title of this psalm is: *The understanding of Asaph*[^1]. In Latin "Asaph" is rendered "assembly"; in Greek it is called "synagogue." Let us see what this synagogue understood. But let us first understand what a synagogue is; then we shall understand what this synagogue understood. Every assembly is called by the general name "synagogue": there can be an assembly both of cattle and of human beings; but here it is not an assembly of cattle, since we have heard of understanding. For listen to what is said of the human being who, set in a place of honor, has neglected understanding within himself: *Man, though set in honor, did not understand; he is compared to the senseless beasts, and is made like them*[^2]. Since, then, it is not an assembly of cattle, we need not discuss the matter long or commend it more carefully; but since it is an assembly of human beings, we must understand of which human beings it is. It is certainly not of those who, set in honor and not understanding, were compared to the senseless beasts and made like them; rather, it is of those who do understand. For the title of the psalm lays this down, saying: *The understanding of Asaph.* It is therefore a certain understanding assembly whose voice we are about to hear. But since "synagogue" has properly been the name for the assembly of the people of Israel—so that wherever we hear "synagogue" we are now accustomed to understand nothing but the Jewish people—let us see whether perhaps this is its voice in this psalm. But of what kind of Jews, and of what kind of people of Israel? Not the chaff, but perhaps the wheat; not the broken branches, but perhaps the firm ones. *For not all who are of Israel are Israelites; but in Isaac,* he says, *shall your descendants be named: that is, it is not the children of the flesh who are the children of God, but the children of the promise are reckoned as descendants*[^3]. There are, then, certain Israelites, one of whom was the man of whom it was said: *Behold, truly an Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^4]. I do not say this in the sense that we too are Israelites because we too are Abraham's seed; for the Apostle was speaking to the Gentiles when he said: *Then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to the promise*[^5]. In that sense, then, we are all Israelites, we who follow the footsteps of the faith of our father Abraham. But here let us understand the voice of the Israelites in the way the Apostle meant when he said: *For I too am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin*[^6]. Here, then, let us understand what the prophets said: *The remnant shall be saved*[^7]. Let us hear here the voice of the saved remnant; so that the synagogue may speak—the synagogue that had received the Old Testament and was intent on the carnal promises; and from this it came about that its feet wavered. For in another psalm too, which likewise has *Asaph* in its title, what is said? *How good is God to Israel, to the upright in heart! But my feet had almost slipped*[^8]. And as though we were to ask, Why did your feet slip? *My steps,* he says, *had well-nigh given way, because I was envious of sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^9]. For when, in keeping with God's promises belonging to the Old Testament, he was expecting earthly happiness, he noticed that it abounded among the wicked; that in those things he was expecting from God, those flourished who did not worship God; and as though he had served God in vain, his feet wavered. For there he says: *Behold, these are the sinners, and abounding in this world they have obtained riches. Was it then in vain that I justified my heart?*[^10] See how his steps had well-nigh given way, so that the soul now says to itself: What good is it that I serve God? Behold, that man does not serve, and he is happy; I serve, and I labor. After all, suppose that I am happy; since that man too is happy who does not serve, why should I think myself happy on the ground that I serve? Now the psalm whose testimony I have brought forward precedes this one which we now have in hand.

**2.** Now opportunely—not by our arrangement, but by God's—it has come about that we have just now heard from the Gospel: *For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ*[^11]. For if we distinguish the two Testaments, the Old and the New, they have not the same sacraments, nor the same promises; yet they have most of the same precepts. For *You shall not kill, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not steal, Honor your father and mother, You shall not bear false witness, You shall not covet your neighbor's property, You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*[^12] are precepts given to us as well; and whoever does not observe them goes astray, and is by no means worthy to be counted fit to receive God's holy mountain, of which it is said: *Who shall dwell in your tabernacle, or who shall rest on your holy mountain?*[^13] *He who is innocent in his hands and clean of heart*[^14]. When the precepts are examined, then, either they are all found to be the same, or there is scarcely anything in the Gospel that was not spoken by the prophets. The precepts are the same; the sacraments are not the same, the promises are not the same. Let us see why the precepts are the same: it is because by these we ought to serve God. The sacraments are not the same, because the sacraments that give salvation are one thing, and the sacraments that promise the Savior are another. The sacraments of the New Testament give salvation; the sacraments of the Old Testament promised the Savior. Since, then, you now hold the things promised, why do you seek the things that promised them, when you already have the Savior? I say "you hold the things promised" not in the sense that we have already received eternal life, but in the sense that Christ, who was foretold through the prophets, has already come. The sacraments have changed; they have been made easier, fewer, more wholesome, more blessed. Why are the promises not the same? Because the land of Canaan was promised, a plentiful land, fruitful, flowing with milk and honey; a temporal kingdom was promised, worldly happiness was promised, a fruitfulness of children was promised, the subjection of enemies was promised: all these belong to earthly happiness. But why did these have to be promised first? Because *it is not the spiritual that is first, but the animal; afterward,* he says, *the spiritual. The first man was of the earth, earthly; the second man is of heaven, heavenly: as was the earthly, such also are the earthly; and as is the heavenly, such also are the heavenly. As we have borne the image of the earthly, let us also bear the image of him who is from heaven*[^15]. To the image of the earthly belongs the Old Testament; to the image of the heavenly, the New Testament. But lest anyone suppose that the earthly man was made by one being and the heavenly by another, God, showing that he is the creator of both, also willed to be the author of both Testaments; so that he might promise earthly things in the Old Testament and heavenly things in the New. But how long are you the earlier, earthly man? How long do you savor earthly things? When certain childish things are given to a child—playthings to distract the childish mind—are they not, when he grows up, knocked out of his hands, so that he may now handle something more useful, something befitting a grown person? And yet it was you yourself who gave your son both nuts when he was small and a book when he was grown. Therefore, because God through the New Testament knocked those things, as it were the playthings of children, out of his children's hands in order to give them something more useful as they grew, it is not to be supposed that he himself did not give those earlier things: he gave both. But *the law* itself *was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ*[^16]: *grace,* because what was commanded by the letter is fulfilled through love; *truth,* because what was promised is rendered. This, then, is what that Asaph understood. In short, all the things that had been promised to the Jews have been taken away. Where is their kingdom? Where the temple? Where the anointing? Where the priest? Where now among them are the prophets? Since he came who was foretold through the prophets, none of these things any longer exists in that nation; it has now lost earthly things, and does not yet seek heavenly ones.

**3.** You ought not, then, to cling to earthly things, even though God bestows them. Yet it is not the case that, because we ought not to cling to them, we should believe that anyone but God gives them; he himself gives them; but do not look for any great thing from him in a good that he gives even to one who is not good. For if he gave these things as something great, he would not give them to the wicked. He willed to give them to the wicked too precisely so that the good might learn to seek something else from him, something he does not give even to the wicked. But those wretched men, clinging to earthly things and not relying on him who made heaven and earth, who gave them those very earthly things, who even in temporal terms freed them from the captivity of Egypt, who led them through the divided sea, who overwhelmed their pursuing enemies with the waves[^17]—not relying on him who would surely give heavenly things to the grown as he gave earthly things to the little ones, fearing to lose what they had received, they killed the one who had given it. We say these things, brothers, so that you, men of the New Testament, may learn not to cling to earthly things. For if those men clung inexcusably to earthly things, to whom the New Testament itself had not yet been revealed, how much more inexcusably do those pursue earthly things to whom the heavenly things have already been promised and revealed in the New Testament! For recall, my brothers, what was said by those persecuting Christ: *If we let him go, the Romans will come and take away from us both our place and our nation*[^18]. See how, by fearing to lose earthly things, they killed the King of heaven. And what happened to them? They lost even those earthly things; and where they killed Christ, there they were killed: and since, unwilling to lose their land, they killed the giver of life, being slain they lost that same land; and at the very time at which they killed him, so that from the time itself they might be reminded why they suffered these things. For when the city of the Jews was overthrown, they were celebrating the Passover, and the whole nation, in many thousands of men, had gathered for the celebration of that feast. There God—through wicked men, indeed, yet himself good; through unjust men, yet just and justly—so took vengeance on them that many thousands of men were destroyed and the city itself overthrown. This the *understanding of Asaph* bewails in this psalm; and in that very lament, the understanding, as it were, distinguishes earthly from heavenly things, distinguishes the Old Testament from the New Testament, so that you may see what you are passing through, what you should expect, what you should leave behind, and to what you should cling. Thus, then, he began.

**4.** *Why have you cast us off, O God, to the end? You have cast us off to the end*[^19]: this is from the person of the Jewish people, and from the person of the assembly that is properly called the synagogue. *Why have you cast us off, O God, to the end?* He does not reproach, but asks: *Why,* for what cause, on account of what have you done this? What have you done? *You have cast us off to the end.* What does *to the end* mean? Perhaps until the end of the world. Or have you cast us off into Christ, who is the end for everyone who believes[^20]? *Why,* then, *have you cast us off, O God, to the end? Your spirit is angry against the sheep of your flock.* Why are you angry against the sheep of your flock, except because we were clinging to earthly things and did not recognize the shepherd?

**5.** [v. 2.] *Remember your assembly, which you possessed from the beginning.* Can this be the voice of the Gentiles? Did he possess the Gentiles from the beginning? No, but he possessed the seed of Abraham, the people of Israel born according to the flesh from the patriarchs, our fathers; whose children we have become, not by coming from the flesh, but by imitating their faith. But those who were possessed by God from the beginning—what befell them? *Remember your assembly, which you possessed from the beginning. You redeemed the rod of your inheritance.* That very assembly of yours, *the rod of your inheritance you redeemed.* This same assembly he called *the rod of the inheritance.* Let us look back to what was first done, when he willed to possess that assembly by freeing it from Egypt—what sign he gave to Moses, when Moses said to him: *What sign shall I give, that they may believe me, that you have sent me? And God said to him: What are you carrying in your hand? A rod. Throw it on the ground. And he cast the rod on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses was afraid, and Moses fled from it. And the Lord said to him: Take hold of its tail. And he took hold of it; and it returned to its state, becoming a rod, just as it had been a rod before*[^21]. What does this signify? For it was not done in vain. Let us question the Letters of God. What did the serpent persuade man to? Death[^22]. Death, then, is from the serpent. If death is from the serpent, the rod within the serpent is Christ within death. And so too, when in the desert they were bitten and killed by serpents, the Lord commanded Moses to lift up a bronze serpent in the wilderness, and to admonish the people that whoever had been bitten by a serpent should look upon it and be healed[^23]. And so it was done; and so men, bitten by serpents and poisoned, were healed by looking upon the serpent. To be healed by a serpent—a great mystery! What is it to be healed from a serpent by looking upon a serpent? It is to be saved from death by believing in one who is dead. And yet *Moses was afraid and fled.* What is it that Moses fled from that serpent? What, brothers, but what we know happened in the Gospel? Christ died, and the disciples were afraid, and withdrew from the hope in which they had been[^24]. But what was said? *Take hold of its tail.* What is *the tail*? Take hold of the latter parts. This is also what that other passage signified: *You shall see my back parts*[^25]. First he was made a serpent, but when the tail was held it became a rod; first he was slain, afterward he rose again. In the tail of the serpent there is also the end of the world; for thus does the mortality of the Church now walk: some go, others come, through death as through a serpent, because death was sown by the serpent; but at the end of the world, as it were the tail, we return to the hand of God, and are made the established kingdom of God, so that there may be fulfilled in us: *You redeemed the rod of your inheritance.* But since this is the voice of the synagogue—and the redeemed rod of God's inheritance appears more in the Gentiles, while the hope of the Jews is hidden, whether of those who are yet to come to belief, or of those who believed at the time when, the Holy Spirit having been sent, the disciples spoke in the tongues of all nations[^26]. For then several thousands of the Jews themselves, the crucifiers of Christ, came to believe; and because they were found nearby, they believed in such a way that they sold all they had and laid the proceeds of their goods at the feet of the apostles[^27]. Since, then, this was hidden, and the redemption of God's rod was to be more manifest among the Gentiles, he explains the source of what he said: *You redeemed the rod of your inheritance.* This he said not of the Gentiles, among whom it is manifest. But from where? *Mount Zion.* Even Mount Zion can be understood in another way. *This Mount Zion in which you have dwelt*: where the people were of old, where the temple was instituted, where the sacrifices were celebrated, where there was everything necessary at that time, promising Christ. A promise, when the thing is rendered, now becomes superfluous. For before what is promised is rendered, the promise itself is necessary, lest the one to whom it is promised forget what was promised to him and die from not waiting. He must therefore wait, so that he may receive when it comes: and so he ought not to abandon the promise. For this reason the figures were not abandoned, so that when the day came, the shadows might be taken away: *This Mount Zion in which you have dwelt.*

**6.** [v. 3.] *Lift up your hand against their pride to the end.* As you were casting us off to the end, so *lift up your hand against their pride to the end.* Whose *pride*? The pride of those by whom Jerusalem was overthrown. And by whom, if not by the kings of the Gentiles? Well has his hand been lifted up against their pride to the end; for they too have now come to know Christ. *For Christ is the end of the law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^28]. How well does he wish for them? He speaks as though angry, and seems to curse: and would that what he curses might come to pass! Indeed, let us now rejoice that it is coming to pass in the name of Christ. Now those holding the scepter are subjected to the wood of the cross; now is fulfilled what was foretold: *All the kings of the earth shall worship him; all nations shall serve him*[^29]. Now on the foreheads of kings the sign of the cross is more precious than the jewel of the diadem. *Lift up your hand against their pride to the end. How great are the things the enemy has wickedly wrought among your holy things!* Among those things that were your holy things—that is, in the temple, in the priesthood, in all those sacraments that existed at that time—*how great are the things the enemy has wickedly wrought!* Truly the enemy worked then. For the Gentiles, who did this then, worshiped false gods, adored idols, served demons; yet they wrought many evils among the holy things of God. When could they do so, unless they were permitted? And when would they be permitted, unless those holy things—which at first foreshadowed the promise—were no longer necessary, since he who had promised was now held? Therefore, *how great are the things the enemy has wickedly wrought among your holy things!*

**7.** [v. 4.] *And those who hate you have boasted.* Mark the servants of demons, the servants of idols; such were the Gentiles then, when they overthrew the temple and the city of God, *and they boasted. In the midst of your solemn feast.* Remember what I said—that Jerusalem was then overthrown when that very solemn feast was being kept; the feast at which they crucified the Lord: gathered together, they raged; gathered together, they perished.

**8.** [v. 5.] *They set up their standards, standards; and they did not know.* They had standards to set up there—their banners, their eagles, their dragons, the Roman standards; or even their statues, which they first set up in the temple; or perhaps *their standards,* which they had heard of from the soothsayers of their demons. *And they did not know.* What did they not know? That *you would have no power over me, unless it had been given you from above*[^30]. They did not know that the honor was not conferred on them, that they should afflict, take, or overthrow the city; but their impiety was made, as it were, the axe of God: they were made the instrument of one who was angry, not of one appeased into a kingdom. For God does here what a man too often does. Sometimes an angry man takes up a rod lying in his path, perhaps some bit of brushwood, and beats his son with it; and then he throws the brushwood into the fire, and keeps the inheritance for his son: so sometimes God instructs the good through the wicked, and through the temporal power of the damnable trains the discipline of those to be freed. For what? Do you suppose, brothers, that this discipline was truly given to that nation in such a way that it should perish utterly? How many of them afterward came to believe! How many are yet to believe! The chaff is one thing, the wheat another; yet the threshing-sledge passes over both: but under the one sledge the one is crushed, the other purified. How great a good God brought us out of the evil of Judas the betrayer! From the very savagery of the Jews, how great a good was bestowed on the faithful Gentiles! Christ was slain, so that he might be on the cross for the one to gaze upon who had been struck by the serpent[^31]. So, then, these men too had perhaps heard from their soothsayers that they were to go to Jerusalem and take it; and when they had taken and overthrown it, they said to themselves that it had been done by their demons: *They set up their standards, standards; and they did not know.* What did they not know? *As at the going forth from above.* Because, unless the command had gone forth from above, such things would never have been permitted to the raging Gentiles against the people of the Jews. But it went forth from above, as Daniel says: *From the beginning of your prayer the word went forth*[^32]. This too the Lord said to Pilate himself, who was puffing himself up and setting up his standards, standards, and not knowing, and saying to Christ: *Do you not answer me? Do you not know that I have power to kill you, and power to release you?*[^33] And the Lord, pricking the puffed-up man as one pricks a bladder to let out the air: *You would have,* he says, *no power over me, unless it had been given you from above*[^34]. So too here: *They set up their standards, standards; and they did not know.* How did they not know? *As at the going forth from above.* As the going forth from above had been made, that this might be fulfilled, could they thus have known?

**9.** [v. 6.] Let us now run through these verses, Jerusalem being overthrown, both because they are manifest and because it is not pleasant to linger over the punishment of enemies. *As in a forest of trees, with axes they cut down its gates together; with hatchet and crowbar they cast it down*: that is, in concert, steadfastly, *with hatchet and crowbar they cast it down.*

**10.** *They burned your sanctuary with fire; they defiled on the ground the tabernacle of your name.*

**11.** [vv. 8, 9.] *They said in their heart, their kindred together.* What did they say? *Come, let us suppress all the feasts of the Lord from the land. Of the Lord*—this is inserted from the person of this speaker, from the person of Asaph: for those raging men would not call him "Lord," whose temple they were overthrowing. *Come, let us suppress all the feasts of the Lord from the land.* What says Asaph? What does the *understanding of Asaph* say in this? What? Does even the discipline given do no good? Is the perversity of the mind not corrected? All the things that existed of old have been overthrown: nowhere a priest, nowhere an altar of the Jews, nowhere a victim, nowhere a temple. Is nothing else, then, to be acknowledged that has succeeded this thing now passing away? Or indeed would this foreshadowing sign be taken away, unless what was promised had come? Let us see here, then, the understanding of Asaph; let us see whether he profits from his tribulation. Mark what he says: *We do not see our standards, there is no longer a prophet, and he will not yet know us.* Behold these Jews, who say of themselves that they are not yet acknowledged—that is, that they are still in captivity, that they are not yet freed—still waiting for Christ. Christ will come; but he will come as judge: first the one who calls, afterward the one who separates. He will come, because he came, and it is plain that he will come; but now he will come from above. He was before you, O Israel! You were shaken, because you stumbled against him as he lay; but lest you be crushed, watch for the one coming from above. For thus it was foretold through the prophet: *Whoever stumbles against that stone shall be shaken; and on whomever it falls, it shall crush him*[^35]. The little one shakes; the great one will crush. Now you do not see your standards, now there is no longer a prophet; and you say: *And he will not yet acknowledge us*: because you do not yet acknowledge him. *There is no longer a prophet, and he will not yet acknowledge us.*

**12.** [v. 10.] *How long, O God, shall the enemy reproach?* Cry out as one forsaken, as one deserted; cry out like a sick man who preferred to strike the physician rather than be cured: he does not acknowledge you yet. See what he has done, who does not acknowledge you yet. For those to whom nothing was announced concerning him shall see; and those who have not heard shall understand[^36]: and you still cry out: *There is no longer a prophet, and he will not yet know us.* Where is your understanding? *The adversary provokes your name to the end.* To this end the adversary provokes your name to the end, that, being angry, you may take it to heart; and taking it to heart, you may acknowledge to the end; or certainly *to the end,* meaning up to the end. Up to what end? Until you acknowledge, until you cry out, until, the tail being grasped, there is a return to the kingdom.

**13.** [v. 11.] *Why do you turn your hand and your right hand away from the midst of your bosom to the end?* Again another sign that was given to Moses. For as above there was the sign of the rod, so here there is the sign of the right hand. For when that matter of the rod had been done, God gave another sign: *Put,* he said, *your hand into your bosom; and he put it in: Bring it out, and he brought it out; and it was found white*[^37]—that is, unclean. For whiteness in the skin is leprosy, not fairness[^38]. For God's own inheritance—that is, his people—being sent forth from him, was made unclean. But what did he say to him? *Bring it back into your bosom: he brought it back, and it returned to its color*[^39]. When do you do this, says this Asaph? How long do you estrange your right hand from your bosom, so that it remains unclean outside? Bring it back; let it return to its color, let it acknowledge the Savior. *Why do you turn your hand and your right hand away from the midst of your bosom to the end?* These things the blind man cries out, not understanding; and God does what he does. For why did Christ come? *A blindness in part has come upon Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in, and so all Israel might be saved*[^40]. Now, then, Asaph, acknowledge what has gone before, that at least you may follow, if you could not go before. For Christ did not come in vain, nor was Christ slain in vain; nor did the grain fall to the ground in vain, except that it might rise up many-fold[^41]. The serpent was lifted up in the wilderness, that it might heal the one struck by the venom[^42]. Mark what has been done: do not think that his coming was empty; lest evil find you when he comes again.

**14.** [v. 12.] Asaph understood, for in the title of the psalm there is *The understanding of Asaph.* And what does he say? *But God our king before the ages has wrought salvation in the midst of the earth.* With one voice we cry out: *There is no longer a prophet; and he will not yet know us*: but with this other voice we say that *God* is our *king,* who is *before the ages,* because he himself is the Word in the beginning, through whom the ages were made; he *has wrought salvation in the midst of the earth. God,* then, *our king before the ages,* what did he do? *He wrought salvation in the midst of the earth;* and I still cry out as though deserted. He works salvation in the midst of the earth; and I have remained earth. Asaph understood well, for it is *The understanding of Asaph.* For why these things, or what kind of salvation did Christ work, except that men might learn to desire eternal things, and not always to cling to temporal ones? *But God our king before the ages has wrought salvation in the midst of the earth.* While we cry out: *How long, O Lord, shall the enemy reproach to the end? How long does the adversary provoke? How long do you turn your hand away from your bosom?* When we say these things: *But God our king before the ages has wrought salvation in the midst of the earth;* and we are asleep. The Gentiles are now awake; and we are snoring, and, as though God had deserted us, we rave in our sleep. *He has wrought salvation in the midst of the earth.*

**15.** [v. 13.] Now then, Asaph, correct yourself unto understanding; tell us what kind of salvation God has wrought in the midst of the earth. When that earthly salvation of yours was overthrown, what did he do? What did he promise? *You established the sea by your power.* As though the Jewish nation were a dry land, as it were, separated from the waves, the Gentiles were a sea in their bitterness, washing about that land on every side: behold, *you established the sea by your power,* and there remained a land thirsting for your rain. *You established the sea by your power: you crushed the heads of the dragons in the water.* The heads of the dragons—the prides of the demons by whom the Gentiles were possessed—you crushed upon the water; because those whom they possessed, you freed through baptism.

**16.** [v. 14.] What yet, after the heads of the dragons? For those dragons have their prince, and he himself is the first great dragon. And concerning him, what did he do who wrought salvation in the midst of the earth? Listen: *You broke the head of the dragon.* Of what dragon? We understand the dragons to be all the demons soldiering under the devil: what single dragon, then, whose head was broken, ought we to understand but the devil himself? What did he do concerning him? *You broke the head of the dragon.* That head is the beginning of sin. That head is what received the curse, that the seed of Eve should watch the head of the serpent[^43]. For the Church has been admonished to shun the beginning of sin. What is the beginning of sin, as it were the head of the serpent? *The beginning of all sin is pride*[^44]. The head of the dragon, then, has been broken; the pride of the devil has been broken. And what did he do concerning him, who wrought salvation in the midst of the earth? *You gave him as food to the Ethiopian peoples.* What is this? How do I understand the Ethiopian peoples? How, except as all the Gentiles by them? And well by the black ones; for the Ethiopians are black. They are called to faith who were black; they precisely, so that it may be said to them: *For you were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord*[^45]. They precisely are called black; but not that they should remain black: for of these is made the Church, to which it is said: *Who is this that comes up made white?*[^46] For what has been made of her who was black, except what is said: *I am black, and beautiful*[^47]? And how did these receive that dragon as food? I think rather that they received Christ as food: but Christ, by whom they should be perfected; the devil, whom they should consume. For from this also came that calf which the unfaithful, apostate people worshiped, seeking the gods of the Egyptians, forsaking him who freed them from the slavery of the Egyptians; from this that great mystery was set in motion. For when Moses was so angry with those worshiping and adoring the idol, and, inflamed with zeal for God, took temporal vengeance, that he might frighten them away from the eternal death to be shunned; yet he cast the very head of the calf into the fire and destroyed it, ground it small, scattered it in water, and gave it to the people to drink[^48]: thus was a great mystery enacted. O prophetic anger, and a mind not disturbed but enlightened! What did he do? Cast it into the fire, so that the very form might first be confounded; grind it small, so that it might gradually be consumed; cast it into water, give it to the people to drink. What is this, except that the worshipers of the devil had become his body? Just as those who acknowledge Christ become the body of Christ, so that it may be said to them: *Now you are the body of Christ and his members*[^49]. The body of the devil was to be consumed, and this was to be consumed by the Israelites. For from that people were the apostles; from that people was the first Church. And it was said to Peter concerning the Gentiles: *Kill and eat*[^50]. What is *Kill and eat*? Kill what they are, and make them what you are. With this: *Kill and eat;* with this: Grind small and drink: yet both in the same mystery; because it was necessary, and beyond doubt necessary, that the body which belonged to the devil should, by believing, pass over into the body of Christ. Thus the devil is consumed by the loss of his members. This was figured also in the serpent of Moses. For the magicians did likewise, and casting down their rods they produced dragons; but the dragon of Moses swallowed up the rods of all those magicians[^51]. Let the body of the devil, then, be understood even now: this happens; it is devoured by the Gentiles who have believed, it has been made food for the Ethiopian peoples. This too can be understood, *you gave him as food to the Ethiopian peoples*: because now everyone bites him. What is "they bite"? By reproaching, blaming, accusing. As it was said—by way of prohibition, indeed, yet plainly expressed: *But if you bite and devour one another, take heed that you be not consumed by one another*[^52]. What is *you bite and devour one another*? You quarrel with one another, you disparage one another, you cast reproaches at one another. Mark, then, that now the devil is consumed by these bites. Who, angry with his servant—even a pagan—does not say to him, "Satan"? See the devil given as food. This the Christian says, this the Jew says, this the pagan says; the very man adores him, and reviles him.

**17.** [v. 15.] Let us see, then, what remains. Brothers, I beg you, attend: these things are heard with great delight, because what is heard is also recognized throughout the world. When these things were being spoken, they did not yet exist; for then they were being promised, not rendered: but now with what delight are we affected, when we see fulfilled in the world the things we read foretold in the book! Let us see what he did, whom Asaph now understands to have *wrought salvation in the midst of the earth. You broke open the fountains and the torrents*: that they might pour forth the liquid of wisdom, that they might pour forth the riches of faith, that they might irrigate the saltiness of the Gentiles, that they might convert all unbelievers, by their irrigation, into the sweetness of faith. *You broke open the fountains and the torrents.* Perhaps there is a distinction; perhaps they are one, because the fountains were so abundant that they made rivers. *You broke open the fountains and the torrents.* But if it is by way of distinction: in some, the word of God becomes *a fountain of water springing up to eternal life*[^53]; others, however, hearing the word and not holding it in such a way as to live well, yet not keeping silent with their tongue, become torrents. For those are properly called torrents which are not perennial: for sometimes "torrent" is used figuratively for a river, as it is said: *They shall be inebriated with the abundance of your house, and you shall give them to drink of the torrent of your delights*[^54]: for that torrent will never dry up. But torrents are properly those rivers that fail in summer, but in winter are flooded with waters and run. You see, then, a man who is well-faithful, who will persevere to the end, who will not abandon God in any temptation; who endures all troubles for the truth, not for falsehood and error: from what does he flourish thus, except that the word has become in him *a fountain of water springing up to eternal life*? Another, however, receives the word; he preaches, he is not silent, he runs: but the summer tests whether he is a fountain or a torrent. Yet by both the earth is watered, by him who *wrought salvation in the midst of the earth;* let the fountains overflow, let the torrents run: *You broke open the fountains and the torrents.*

**18.** *You dried up the rivers of Etham.* With this hand he breaks open the fountains and the torrents, with this hand he dries up the rivers, so that from here the waters run, and from there the waters are dried up. *The rivers,* he says, *of Etham.* What is Etham? For it is a Hebrew word. What does "Etham" mean? Strong, robust. Who is this strong and robust one, whose rivers God dries up? Who, but that very dragon? For *no one enters the house of the strong man to plunder his goods, unless he first binds the strong man*[^55]. He is the strong one, presuming upon his own power and forsaking God; he is the strong one who says: *I will set my throne in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^56]. From that cup of perverse strength he gave man to drink. They wished to be strong who believed they would become gods from the forbidden food. Adam was made strong, to be mocked: *Behold, Adam has become like one of us*[^57]. The Jews too were strong, presuming upon their own righteousness: *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and seeking to establish their own righteousness,* as though strong, *they did not submit to the righteousness of God*[^58]. See the man who has spent his strength and remained weak, needy, standing afar off, not daring to lift his eyes to heaven; but striking his breast and saying: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^59]. Now he is weak, now he confesses his weakness; he is not strong: he is dry land; let him be watered with fountains and torrents. Those are still strong who presume upon their own power. Let their rivers be dried up: let the teachings of the Gentiles—of the soothsayers, of the astrologers, the arts of magic—make no progress; since the rivers of the Strong One have been dried up: *You dried up the rivers of Etham.* Let that teaching wither; let minds be flooded with the Gospel of truth.

**19.** [v. 16.] *Yours is the day, and yours is the night.* Who does not know this, since he made all these things; because all things were made through the Word[^60]? To him, the very one who *wrought salvation in the midst of the earth,* it is said: *Yours is the day, and yours is the night.* We ought to understand something here that pertains to that very salvation which he wrought in the midst of the earth. *Yours is the day.* Who are these? The spiritual. *And yours is the night.* Who are these? The carnal: *Yours is the day; and yours is the night.* Let the spiritual speak spiritual things to the spiritual; for it is said: *Comparing spiritual things with spiritual, we speak wisdom among the perfect*[^61]. The carnal do not yet grasp that wisdom: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal*[^62]. Therefore, when the spiritual speak to the spiritual: *Day to day pours forth speech*: but when the carnal too do not keep silent about the faith of the crucified Christ, which the little ones can grasp: *Night to night declares knowledge*[^63]. *Yours is the day, and yours is the night.* To you belong the spiritual, to you belong the carnal: the one you illuminate with unchangeable wisdom and truth; the other you console with the manifestation of the flesh, as the moon consoling the night. *Yours is the day, and yours is the night.* Do you wish to hear the day? See whether you grasp it; raise up your mind as much as you can. Let us see whether you belong to the day; let us see now whether your sight does not flicker. Can you see what you just now heard from the Gospel: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God?*[^64] For you do not know how to conceive of words except those that sound and pass away. Can you now grasp the Word, not a sound, but God? Did you not hear there: *And the Word was God*? But you are thinking of these words: *All things were made through him*[^65]: and through him were made those too who make words. What kind of Word, then, is that? Do you grasp it, O carnal one? Answer; do you grasp it? You do not grasp it; you still belong to the night; the moon is necessary for you, lest you die in darkness. For indeed certain *sinners have bent the bow, that they may shoot in the dark moon at the upright in heart*[^66]. For the flesh of Christ was darkened when it was taken down from the cross and laid in the tomb: and those who killed him mocked; and he had not yet risen, and the disciples, upright in heart, were shot at, but in the dark moon. Therefore, that not only day to day might pour forth speech, but also night to night might declare knowledge; since *yours is the day, and yours is the night;* deign to descend, to remain with him from whom you descend, but to come to those to whom you descend. Deign to descend, you who were in this world, and the world was made through you, and the world did not know you[^67]. Let the night too have its consolation; let it have, he says, *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^68]: *Yours is the day, and yours is the night. You made the sun and the moon*: the sun, the spiritual; the moon, the carnal. He is still carnal; let him not be abandoned, but let him too be perfected. *You made the sun and the moon*: the sun, as it were the wise; the moon, as it were the foolish; yet you have not abandoned them. For thus it is written: *The wise man abides like the sun; but the fool changes like the moon*[^69]. What then? Because the sun abides—that is, because *the wise man abides like the sun, the fool changes like the moon*—is the carnal one, the foolish one, still to be abandoned? And where is what was said by the Apostle: *I am a debtor both to the wise and to the foolish*[^70]? *You made the sun and the moon.*

**20.** [v. 17.] *You made all the borders of the earth.* Did he not do so before, when he founded the earth? But how did he make the borders of the earth, he who *wrought salvation in the midst of the earth*? How, except as the Apostle says: *But by grace you have been saved, and this not from yourselves, but it is the gift of God; not from works, lest perhaps anyone be exalted?*[^71] Were there, then, no good works? There were; but how? By the grace of God. Follow on; let us see. *For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus in good works*[^72]. Behold how he made the borders of the earth, he who *wrought salvation in the midst of the earth: You made all the borders of the earth. Summer and spring, you made them.* Those fervent in spirit are summer. You, I say, made those fervent in spirit: you made also the new in faith; that is spring. *Summer and spring, you made them.* Let them not boast as though they had not received: *you made them.*

**21.** [v. 18.] *Be mindful of this your creation.* Of what *your creation? The enemy has reproached the Lord.* O Asaph, grieve in your understanding for your former blindness! *The enemy has reproached the Lord.* It was said to Christ among his own nation: *This man is a sinner; we do not know where he is from: we know Moses, to him God has spoken; this man is a Samaritan*[^73]. *The enemy has reproached the Lord; and a foolish people has provoked your name.* A foolish people then, Asaph; but it was not then the *understanding of Asaph.* What is said in the earlier psalm? *I have become as a beast before you; and I am always with you*[^74]: because he did not go to the gods and idols of the Gentiles. Though he did not understand, like a beast, yet he recognized as a man, for he said: *Always with you, like a beast*: and what comes afterward there in that very psalm where Asaph appears? *You have held my right hand; in your will you have led me, and with glory you have taken me up*[^75]. *In your will,* not in my righteousness; by your gift, not by my work. So too here: *The enemy has reproached the Lord; and a foolish people has provoked your name.* Have they all perished, then? God forbid. Even though some of the branches were broken off, yet some remain into which the wild olive might be grafted[^76]; and the root remains, and from those very branches broken off through unbelief some were recalled through faith: for the apostle Paul himself had been broken off through unbelief, and was restored to the root through faith. Thus plainly *a foolish people has provoked your name,* when it was said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^77].

**22.** [v. 19.] But what of you, Asaph, now in understanding? *Do not deliver to the beasts the soul that confesses to you.* I acknowledge, says Asaph; for, as it is said in another psalm: *I have acknowledged my sin, and I have not concealed my offense*[^78]. Why? Because Peter spoke to the Israelites, who were marveling at the tongues, that they themselves had killed Christ, when Christ had been sent for their sake. *Hearing this, they were pricked in their heart, and said to the apostles: What then shall we do? Tell us. And the apostles said: Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ; and your sins shall be forgiven you*[^79]. Since, then, through penance confession was made: *Do not deliver to the beasts the soul that confesses to you.* Why *that confesses to you*? Because *I was turned in my distress, while the thorn was being fixed*[^80]. They were pricked in their heart; and those who had been glorious in raging were made wretched by repenting. *Do not deliver to the beasts the soul that confesses to you.* To what beasts, except those whose heads were crushed upon the water? For the devil himself is called a beast, a lion, and a dragon[^81]. Do not, he says, give to the devil and his angels the soul that confesses to you. Let the serpent devour me, if I still savor earthly things, if I still desire earthly things, if I still remain in the promises of the Old Testament, the New having been revealed. But when I have now laid aside pride, and acknowledge not my own righteousness but your grace; let not the proud beasts have power over me. *Do not deliver to the beasts the soul that confesses to you. Forget not to the end the souls of your poor.* We were rich, we were strong; but *you dried up the rivers of Etham*: now we no longer set up our own righteousness, but acknowledge your grace; we are poor, hear your beggars. We no longer dare to lift our eyes to heaven, but striking our breast we say: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^82]; *Forget not to the end the souls of your poor.*

**23.** [v. 20.] *Look upon your testament.* Render what you promised: we hold the tablets, we await the inheritance. *Look upon your testament;* not that old one: I do not ask on account of the land of Canaan; not on account of the temporal subjection of enemies, not on account of the carnal fruitfulness of children, not on account of earthly riches, not on account of temporal salvation; *Look upon your testament,* by which you promised the kingdom of heaven. Now I acknowledge your testament; now Asaph has understanding, Asaph is no beast, now he sees what was said: *Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord; and I will make for the house of Israel and the house of Judah a new testament, not according to the testament I made with their fathers*[^83]. *Look upon your testament; for they that are darkened are filled with the earth of unjust houses*: because they had unjust hearts. Our houses are our hearts: there the blessed of clean heart gladly dwell[^84]. *Look,* then, *upon your testament;* and let the remnant be saved[^85]: for many who attend to the earth are darkened, and are filled with earth. Dust has entered their eyes and blinded them, and they have become the dust which the wind drives from the face of the earth[^86]. *They that are darkened are filled with the earth of unjust houses.* For by attending to the earth they were darkened, of whom it is said in another psalm: *Let their eyes be darkened that they may not see, and bow down their back forever*[^87]. With earth, then, *they that are darkened are filled, of unjust houses,* because they had unjust hearts. For our houses, as we said above, are our hearts: there we gladly dwell, if we cleanse them from iniquity. There is the evil conscience, which drives a man back from going where he is bidden to go carrying his pallet—the man whose sins have been forgiven—when the Lord says: *Take up your pallet, and go into your house*[^88]: carry your flesh, and enter into your healed conscience. *For they that are darkened are filled with the earth of unjust houses*: they were darkened, they were filled with earth. Those darkened ones, who are they? Those who have unjust hearts: the Lord renders to them according to their own heart.

**24.** [v. 21.] *Let not the humble be turned away in confusion.* For pride confounded those others. *The needy and the poor shall praise your name.* You see, brothers, how sweet poverty ought to be: you see that the poor and needy belong to God; but the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven[^89]. Who are the poor in spirit? The humble, who tremble at the words of God, who confess their sins; who do not presume upon their own merits, nor upon their own righteousness. Who are the poor in spirit? Those who, when they do anything good, praise God; when they do anything evil, accuse themselves. *Upon whom shall my Spirit rest,* says the prophet, *but upon the humble, and the quiet, and him who trembles at my words?*[^90] Now then, Asaph has understood; now he does not cling to the earth, now he does not seek out the earthly promises of the Old Testament: he has become your beggar, he has become your poor man; he thirsts for your rivers, because his own have been dried up. Since, then, he has become such, let him not be cheated of hope: he has sought with his hands by night before you; let him not be deceived[^91]. *Let not the humble be turned away in confusion: the needy and the poor shall praise your name.* Confessing their sins, they shall praise your name; desiring your eternal promises, they shall praise your name: not those swollen with temporal things, not those lifted up and puffed up into pride over their own righteousness, not they; but who? *The needy and the poor shall praise your name.*

**25.** [v. 22.] *Arise, O Lord; judge my cause.* For I seem to be deserted, because I have not yet received what you promised: and my tears have become my bread day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God? And because I cannot show my God, I am mocked as though I were pursuing something empty. And not only the pagan, or the Jew, or the heretic; but sometimes a brother, a catholic himself, twists his mouth when the promises of God are preached, when the future resurrection is foretold. And even he, though already washed in the water of eternal salvation, bearing the sacrament of Christ, perhaps says: And who has risen here? and: I have not heard my father speaking from the tomb, since I buried him. God gave a law for a time to his servants, by which to call them off: for who returns from the dead? And what shall I do with such people? Show what they do not see? I cannot: for God ought not to be made visible on their account. Let them now do this, if it please them; let them so act, so attempt it: because they are unwilling to be changed for the better, let them change God for the worse. Let him who can, see; let him who cannot, believe, that there is a God. And even though he who is able sees, does he see with his eyes? He sees with his understanding, he sees with his heart. For it was not the sun and moon that he wished to show, who said: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^92]. But an unclean heart is not even fit for faith, so as to believe what it cannot see. I do not see, he says: what am I to believe? Your soul is seen, I suppose! Fool! your body is seen; who sees your soul? Since, then, only your body is seen, why are you not buried? He marvels that I said: If only the body is seen, why are you not buried? And he answers (for he is shrewd enough for this): Because I am alive. How do I know that you are alive, whose soul I do not see? How do I know? You will answer: Because I speak, because I walk, because I work. Fool! from the works of the body I recognize the living man; from the works of the creation you cannot recognize the Creator! And perhaps he who says, When I am dead, afterward I shall be nothing: he has learned letters too, and he learned this from Epicurus, some delirious philosopher—or rather a lover of vanity, not of wisdom—whom the philosophers themselves named a hog: he who said that the pleasure of the body is the highest good, him the philosophers named a hog, wallowing in carnal mire. From him, perhaps, this lettered man learned to say: I shall not exist after I am dead. Let the rivers of Etham be dried up; let those teachings of the Gentiles perish; let the green pastures of Jerusalem sprout: let them see what they can; let them believe in their heart what they cannot see. Surely all these things that are now seen throughout the world, when God was working salvation in the midst of the earth—when these things were being spoken, they did not yet exist: and behold, they were then foretold; now they are shown fulfilled, and still the fool says in his heart: *There is no God*[^93]. Woe to perverse hearts! since the things that remain are to come just as those things came that then did not exist and were foretold as to come. Or has God indeed displayed to us all that he promised, and deceived us only about the day of judgment? Christ was not on earth; he promised, he displayed it: the Virgin had not given birth: he promised, he displayed it: the precious blood had not been shed, by which the bond of our death was to be blotted out; he promised, he displayed it: the flesh had not yet risen to eternal life; he promised, he displayed it: the Gentiles had not yet believed; he promised, he displayed it: heretics armed with the name of Christ did not yet war against Christ; he foretold it, he displayed it: the idols of the Gentiles had not yet been blotted out from the earth; he foretold it, he displayed it: having foretold and displayed all these things, has he lied about the day of judgment alone? It will surely come, just as these things came; for these things too, before they came, were to come, and as to come were foretold beforehand, and afterward they came; it will come, my brothers. Let no one say: It will not come; or: It will come, but it is far off that it will come: but for you it is near that you depart hence. Let the first deception suffice: if we could not at first remain in the precept, let us at least be corrected by the example. There was not yet an example of human downfall when it was said to Adam: *You shall die the death, if you touch it;* and the serpent came sideways, and said: *You shall not die the death.* The serpent was believed, God was despised; the serpent was believed, the forbidden thing was touched, the man died[^94]. Was not rather that fulfilled which God threatened, than what the enemy promised? So it is, surely: we acknowledge this; from that source all are in death: now at least, taught by experience, let us be cautious. For even now the serpent does not cease to whisper, and to say: Surely God will not condemn such great crowds, and free a few? What is this but: Act against the precept; you shall not die? But as it was then, so it is now. If you do what the devil suggests, and despise what God has commanded, the day of judgment will come, and you will find true what God threatened, and false what the devil promised. *Arise, O Lord, judge my cause.* You died, and you are despised; it is said to me: Where is your God? *Arise, judge my cause.* For none will come to judgment except him who rose from the dead. He was foretold as to come; he came, and was despised by the Jews while walking on earth; he is despised by false Christians while seated in heaven. *Arise, O Lord; judge my cause.* Since I have believed in you, let me not perish; because I have believed what I have not seen, let me not be cheated of my hope, let me receive what you promised; *Judge my cause. Remember your reproaches; those that are from the foolish one all the day.* For Christ is still mocked; nor will the vessels of wrath be lacking all the day—that is, until the end of the world. Still it is said: The Christians preach vain things; still it is said: The resurrection of the dead is empty. *Judge my cause: remember your reproaches.* But whose, if not *those that are from the foolish one all the day*? Does the prudent man say this? For "prudent" is so called: looking ahead; if the prudent man looks ahead, it is by faith that he looks ahead; for with the eyes scarcely can one see before one's feet *all the day.*

**26.** [v. 23.] *Forget not the voice of those who beseech you.* Of those who groan, and who now await what you promised concerning the New Testament, and who are walking toward that very faith, *forget not the voice of those who beseech you.* But those others still say: Where is your God? *Let the pride of those who hate you ascend ever up to you*: Do not forget their pride either. Nor does he forget it: he either punishes it altogether, or corrects it.
