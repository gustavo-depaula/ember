# Exposition of Psalm 45

**1.** [v. 1] We now speak to your Charity certain things as most known, in which we ought not to linger: because the things you know ought to be briefly recalled. Let us understand that we are the sons of Korah. For I recall to you who know, that Korah is interpreted "Baldness"; and that our Lord, since he was crucified in the place of Calvary, drew to himself many, like that grain which, unless it had been put to death, would remain alone; and that those who were drawn were called the sons of Korah: this in a mystery. But there were certain sons of Korah at that time, when these things were sung: but the spirit ought to give us life, not the letter to veil us. Let us, then, here understand, and see whether the things that follow, that is, what the context of the psalm itself has, agree with us: and we find ourselves here, if at least we cling to the members of him whose body's head is in heaven, ascending from that passion, that he may draw up with himself in abundance those who lay in humility, bearing fruit in endurance. Now it was said: *To the end, for the sons of Korah, for the hidden things, a psalm*[^1]. It is hidden, then; but that very one who was crucified in the place of Calvary, you know that he tore the veil, that the secrets of the temple might be laid open. Accordingly, because the cross of our Lord was a key, by which the closed might be opened; let us believe that he will be present to us, that these hidden things may be revealed. What it has of *to the end,* we ought always to understand Christ. *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto justice to everyone who believes*[^2]. But it is called the end, not because it consumes, but because it perfects. For we call food finished which was eaten, and a tunic finished which was woven: the former to consumption, the latter to perfection. Because, then, we have nowhere to tend beyond, when we have come to Christ, he himself is called the end of our course. Nor ought we to think that, when we have come to him, we ought to strive for something more, that we may come even to the Father. For Philip too thought this, when he said to him: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us.* When he says, *it suffices us,* he seeks the end of satiety and of perfection. And he: *So long a time,* he says, *am I with you, and you have not known me? Philip, he who sees me, sees also the Father*[^3]. In him, then, we have the Father, because he himself is in the Father, and the Father in him, and he and the Father are one.

**2.** [v. 2] What, then, does he who sings admonish here, where we ought to recognize our voice, if at least we have the affection of this voice? *Our God is a refuge and strength*[^4]. There are certain refuges where there is no strength, to which when anyone flees, he is rather weakened than strengthened. You flee, for example, to someone great in the world, to make a powerful friend for yourself: it seems to you a refuge. Yet so uncertain are the things of this world, and the falls of the powerful so daily increase, that when you have come to such a refuge, you begin to fear there more. For before you feared only for your cause; but when you have fled to such a one, you will fear about him too. For many, when they had fled to such refuges, when those to whom they had fled fell, were sought out themselves too: whom no one would seek, if they had not fled to such things. Our refuge is not such, but our refuge is strength. When we have fled there, we shall be firm.

**3.** *A helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly*[^5]. Many are the tribulations, and in every tribulation one must flee to God: whether the tribulation be in family matters, or in the health of the body, or about the peril of those most dear, or about some matter necessary for the support of this life, there ought to be altogether no other refuge for a Christian man than his Savior, than his God, to whom when he has fled, let him be strong. For he will not be strong in himself, nor will he be strength to himself; but he will be strength to him, who has become his refuge. Yet, dearly beloved, among all the tribulations of the human soul, there is no greater tribulation than the conscience of sins. For if there be no wound there, and that which is called the conscience be sound within the man; wherever else he suffers tribulations, he will flee thither, and there he will find God. But if there is no rest there on account of the abundance of iniquity, since there too God is not; what will the man do? whither will he flee, when he begins to suffer tribulations? He will flee from the field to the city, from the public to the house, from the house into the bedchamber, and tribulation follows. From the bedchamber he now has nowhere to flee, except into his inner chamber. Further, if there is a tumult there, if the smoke of iniquity, if the flame of crime, he cannot flee thither: he is driven from there; and when he is driven from there, he is driven from himself. And behold he finds his enemy where he had fled: himself, whither shall he flee? Wherever he flees, he drags himself after himself: and wherever he drags such a self, he torments himself about himself. These are the tribulations which find a man exceedingly: for more bitter ones there are not: they are so much not more bitter, as they are not more inward. See, dearly beloved, when timbers are cut down and tested by carpenters; sometimes on the surface they seem as it were damaged and rotten: but the carpenter inspects, as it were, the inner marrow of the wood, and if he knows the wood is sound within, he promises that it will last in the building; nor will he be very anxious about the surface damage, when he reports that what is within is sound. Further, in a man inwardly the conscience is found: what, then, does it profit, if what is outside is sound, and the marrow of the conscience is rotted? These are strait, and altogether vehement, and, as the psalm itself says, exceeding tribulations: yet even in these the Lord has been made a helper, by forgiving sins. For the consciences of the iniquitous nothing heals but pardon. For if a debtor of the treasury, having confessed, says he has great tribulations, and looking at the straits of his family means, when he sees that he cannot be solvent; on account of the collectors threatening every year, he says he suffers great tribulations, nor does he anywhere breathe again except in the hope of a pardon of earthly things: how much more the debtor of punishments from the abundance of his sins, when he shall repay what he owes from a bad conscience, when, if he repays, he himself perishes? For to repay this debt is to suffer punishments. It remains, then, that we can be secure about his pardon: if at least, having received pardon, we do not again return to contracting debts.

**4.** These sons of Korah, then, are perhaps understood to be those to whom Peter spoke in the Acts of the Apostles, when they had been made attentive to the wonders of the coming of the Holy Spirit, when all on whom he had come spoke with all tongues. For he announced Christ to them, him who had been able to do such great things by sending the Holy Spirit. They, considering how contemptible he was whom they had crucified with their own hands, when he was killed by them, how high and exalted he had been made with God, who filled the unlearned with the Holy Spirit, and made the tongues of the simple eloquent, compunct in heart, said: *What shall we do?* These were the exceeding tribulations which had found them. For they themselves did not find their sins, but their sins were found in them by the recalling of the apostles. Therefore the tribulations found them, they themselves did not find the tribulations. For when, without anyone's admonition, a man himself considers his deed, and asks God, what does he say? *I found tribulation and grief, and I called upon the name of the Lord*[^6]. There is one tribulation, then, which you find, another which finds you. In both, however, whether that which finds you, or that which you find, that he may drive away both, he is to be asked who is a helper in tribulations. For both that man, when he found, said this: *And I called upon the name of the Lord*: and these in the tribulations by which they said they were found, said this: *Our God is a refuge and strength, a helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly.* But because he was made a helper, whence was he made one? *Compunct,* he says, *in heart, they said: What shall we do?* As though in great despair. So great is he whom we killed, where shall we be? And Peter: *Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, and your sins are forgiven you*[^7]. For nothing graver than this sin could they conceive. What graver sin of the sick, than the killing of the physician? what graver thing can the sick man do, than if he kill his physician? When this is forgiven, what is not forgiven? From him, then, to whom it was said, *A refuge and strength,* they received great security. *Let each one of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*: in the name of him whom you killed, be baptized, *and your sins are forgiven you.* The physician you have recognized even afterward, now secure drink the blood which you shed.

**5.** [v. 3] In short, having received so great security, what do they say? *Therefore we will not fear, when the earth shall be troubled*[^8]. A little before solicitous, suddenly secure, set from exceeding tribulations into great tranquillity. For Christ was sleeping to them, therefore they were troubled: Christ was roused, as we just now heard in the Gospel, he commanded the winds, and they were stilled. Since Christ is in the heart of each one through faith, it is signified to us that his heart, like a ship, is troubled in the tempest of this world, who forgets his faith: as though, Christ sleeping, it is troubled; but, Christ being roused, there comes a calm. In short, the Lord himself, what does he say? *Where is your faith?*[^9] Christ roused, roused the faith: that what was done in the ship, might be done in their hearts. *A helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly*: he brought it about that there should be great tranquillity there.

**6.** See that very tranquillity: *Therefore we will not fear, when the earth shall be troubled, and the mountains shall be transferred into the heart of the sea*[^10]. Then we will not fear. Let us seek the mountains transferred; and if we can find them, it is manifest that this is our security. For the Lord said to his disciples: *If you have faith like a grain of mustard seed, you shall say to this mountain: Be taken up and cast into the sea, and it shall be done*[^11]. Perhaps, *to this mountain,* he said of himself; for he was called a mountain: *In the last times the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest.* But this mountain is set above the other mountains: because the apostles too are mountains, bearing this mountain. Therefore it follows: *In the last times the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest, prepared on the summit of the mountains*[^12]. He transcends, then, the summits of all mountains, and is set on the summit of all mountains; since the mountains are those announcing the mountain. But the sea signifies this world, in comparison with which sea the nation of the Jews seemed as it were land. For it was not covered with the bitterness of idolatry, but was as it were dry land surrounded by the bitterness of the Gentiles as by a sea. It was to come about that the earth would be troubled, that is, that very Jewish nation; and the mountains would be transferred into the heart of the sea, that is, first the great mountain himself, prepared on the summit of the mountains. For he deserted the Jewish nation, and was made known among the Gentiles; he was transferred from the land to the sea. By whom transferring? By the apostles, to whom he had said: *If you have faith in you like a grain of mustard seed, you shall say to this mountain: Be taken up and cast into the sea, and it shall be done*: that is, by your most faithful preaching it shall come about that this mountain, that is, I myself, may be preached among the Gentiles, glorified among the Gentiles, recognized among the Gentiles, and that there may come about what was foretold of me: *A people whom I did not know, served me*[^13]. But when were those mountains too transferred? This too let the Scripture of God show us. When the Apostle preached to the Jews, they rejected the word; and the apostle Paul said: *To you we were sent, but because you have rejected the word of God, we go to the Gentiles*[^14]. The mountains were transferred into the heart of the sea. For truly the Gentiles believed the mountains, so that in the heart of the sea those mountains were: not like the Jews, of whom it was said: *This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me*[^15]. For this the Lord promises even about the New Testament, saying through the prophet: *I will give my laws in their hearts*[^16]. These laws, these precepts, given through the apostles to the faith and belief of all the Gentiles, are called mountains transferred into the heart of the sea. Then we will not fear. Who will not fear? Those of us who were compunct in heart, lest we be made of the number of the reprobate Jews, like broken branches. For certain of them believed, and clung to the preaching apostles. Let those, then, fear whom the mountains deserted: we have not withdrawn from the mountains; and when they were transferred into the heart of the sea, we followed.

**7.** [v. 4] What now follows from the fact that the mountains were transferred into the heart of the sea? Attend and see the truth. For these things, when they were said, were obscure, because they had not yet happened: but now who does not recognize them as done? Let the divine page be your book, that you may hear these things: let the world be your book, that you may see these things. In these codices none read them except those who know letters: in the whole world let even the unlettered read. What, then, came about, while the mountains were transferred into the heart of the sea? *Its waters sounded and were troubled*[^17]. When the Gospel was preached: *What is this? This man seems to be an announcer of foreign demons*[^18]: this the Athenians. But the Ephesians, with what a tumult they wished to kill the apostles, when in the theater for their Diana they made so great a din, that they cried out: *Great is Diana of the Ephesians*[^19]? Among which waves and the sounding of the sea those did not fear who had fled to that refuge. In short, the apostle Paul wished to enter into the theater, and was called back by the disciples, because it was still necessary that he remain in the flesh on their account[^20]. But yet *Its waters sounded and were troubled: the mountains were troubled in his strength.* Whose? Of the sea, or rather of God, of whom it was said: *A refuge and strength, a helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly*? For the mountains were troubled, that is, the powers of this world. For there are some mountains of God, others mountains of the world: the mountains of the world, whose head is the devil; the mountains of God, whose head is Christ. But through these mountains those mountains were troubled. Then they raised their voices against the Christians, when the mountains were troubled by the sounding waves: and the mountains were troubled, and there was made a great earthquake with the moving of the water. But to whom these things? To that city founded on the rock. The waters sound, the mountains are troubled, the Gospel being announced. What of you, city of God? Hear what follows.

**8.** [v. 5] *The rushing of the river gladdens the city of God*[^21]. When the mountains are troubled, when the sea rages, God does not desert his city by the rushing of the river. What is this rushing of the river? That inundation of the Holy Spirit, of which the Lord said: *If anyone thirsts, let him come and drink: he who believes in me, rivers of living water shall flow from his belly.* Therefore these rivers flowed from the belly of Paul, Peter, John, the other apostles, the other faithful evangelists. When these rivers flowed from one river, many *rushings of the river gladden the city of God.* For that you may know this was said of the Holy Spirit, in the same Gospel consequently the evangelist says: *But this he said of the Spirit, whom those were to receive who were to believe in him. But the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^22]. Jesus being glorified after the resurrection, glorified after the ascension, on the day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit came, filled the believers, they spoke with tongues, they began to preach the Gospel to the Gentiles. Hence the city of God was gladdened, while the sea was troubled by the sounding of its waters, while the mountains were troubled, seeking what they should do, how they might drive away the new doctrine, how they might root out the race of Christians from the earth. Against whom? Against the rushings of the river gladdening the city of God. Hence too he showed of what river he spoke, because he signified the Holy Spirit: *The rushing of the river gladdens the city of God.* And what follows? *The Most High has sanctified his tabernacle.* If, then, the name of sanctification follows, it is manifest that those rushings of the river are to be understood of the Holy Spirit, by whom every godly soul believing in Christ is sanctified, that it may become a citizen of the city of God.

**9.** [v. 6] *God is in the midst of it, and it shall not be moved*[^23]. Let the sea rage, let the mountains be troubled: *God is in the midst of it, and it shall not be moved.* What is, *in the midst of it*? As though God stood in one place, and those who believe in him surrounded him. Is God, then, encompassed by place, and are the things that surround him broad, and is he who is surrounded in a strait? Far be it. Think nothing such of God, who is contained by no place, whose seat is the conscience of the godly: and so the seat of God is in the hearts of men, that if a man falls from God, God remains in himself, not as it were falling by not finding where he may be. For he rather lifts you up, that you may be in him, than leans on you, so that, if you withdraw yourself, he should fall. If he withdraws himself, you will fall: if you withdraw yourself, he will not fall. What, then, is, *God is in the midst of it*? This signifies that God is equal to all, and does not accept persons. For as that which is in the middle has equal spaces to all the ends; so God is said to be in the middle, equally caring for all. *God is in the midst of it, and it shall not be moved.* Whence shall it not be moved? Because God is in the midst of it. *God will help it with his countenance.* He is the *helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly. God will help it with his countenance.* What is, *with his countenance*? With his showing. How does God show himself, that we may see his countenance? Now I recall, you have learned God present, we have learned through works. When from him we receive some help, so that we do not at all doubt it has been granted us by the Lord, the countenance of God is with us. *God will help it with his countenance.*

**10.** [v. 7] *The nations were troubled*[^24]. And how troubled? for what were they troubled? That they might cast down the city of God, in whose midst is God? that they might overthrow the sanctified tabernacle, which God helps with his countenance? No. But now the nations were wholesomely troubled. For what follows? *And the kingdoms were bowed down. The kingdoms,* he says, *were bowed down*: now no longer erect, to rage; but bowed down, to adore. When were the kingdoms bowed down? When there came about what was foretold in another psalm: *All the kings of the earth shall adore him, all the nations shall serve him*[^25]. What thing brought it about that the kingdoms were bowed down? What thing, hear: *The Most High gave his voice, and the earth was moved.* The possessed of idols sounded forth like frogs from the marshes, the more tumultuously, the more sordidly from mud and mire. And what is the din of frogs to the thunders of the clouds? For from there *the Most High gave his voice, and the earth was moved*: he thundered from his clouds. What are his clouds? His apostles, his preachers, through whom he thundered by precepts, flashed by miracles. They are the clouds who are also the mountains: mountains on account of their height and firmness, clouds on account of their rain and abundance. For these clouds irrigated the earth, of which it was said: *The Most High gave his voice, and the earth was moved.* For of these clouds he threatens a certain barren vineyard, whence the mountains were transferred into the heart of the sea: *I will command,* he says, *my clouds not to rain a shower upon it*[^26]. This was fulfilled in what we recalled, when the mountains were transferred into the heart of the sea: when it was said: *To you we were sent, but because you have rejected the word of God, we go to the Gentiles*[^27]: it was fulfilled: *I will command my clouds not to rain a shower upon it.* In short, that very Jewish nation has now so remained, like a dry fleece on the threshing-floor. For this too you know happened in a certain miracle. The threshing-floor was dry, the fleece alone was wet: but the rain did not appear on the fleece[^28]. So too the sacrament of the New Testament did not appear in the nation of the Jews. What there was a fleece, here a veil: for the sacrament was veiled in the fleece. But on the threshing-floor, in all the Gentiles, the Gospel of Christ lies open: the rain is manifest, the grace of Christ is bare; for it is not covered with a veil. But that the rain might come out of it, the fleece was wrung. For by their oppression they shut out Christ from themselves, and the Lord now from his clouds rained upon the threshing-floor, the dry fleece remained. From there, then, *the Most High gave his voice,* from these clouds, by which voice the kingdoms were bowed down, and adored.

**11.** [v. 8] *The Lord of powers is with us, our upholder the God of Jacob*[^29]. Not any man, not any power, not even an angel, not any creature, whether earthly or heavenly, but *the Lord of powers is with us, our upholder the God of Jacob.* He who sent the angels, came after the angels, came that the angels might serve him, came that he might make men equal to the angels. Great grace. If God is for us, who is against us? *The Lord of powers is with us.* What Lord *of powers is with us*. If God is for us, I say, who is against us? *He who spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how has he not with him also given us all things?*[^30] Therefore let us be secure, in tranquillity of heart let us nourish a good conscience from the bread of the Lord. *The Lord of powers is with us, our upholder the God of Jacob.* However great your weakness, see who upholds you. Someone is sick, a physician is brought; the physician calls the sick man his charge. Who has taken him up? He. Great hope of health, a great physician has taken him up. What physician? Every physician except him is a man: every physician who comes to the sick man can be sick on another day, except him. *Our upholder the God of Jacob.* Make yourself a little infant altogether, such as are taken up by their parents. For those who are not taken up, are exposed: those who are taken up, are nourished. Do you think God so took you up, as your mother took you up an infant? Not so, but forever. For your voice is in that psalm: *Because my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the Lord has taken me up*[^31]. *Our upholder the God of Jacob.*

**12.** [v. 9] *Come, and see the works of the Lord*[^32]. Now about this upholding what has the Lord done? Consider the world, come, and see. For if you do not come, you do not see; if you do not see, you do not believe; if you do not believe, you stand far off: if you believe, you come; if you believe, you see. For how is one to come to this mountain? With the feet? with ships? with wings? with horses? As far as pertains to the spaces of places, do not be busy, do not be troubled, he himself comes to you. For from a small stone he grew, and was made a great mountain, so that he filled the whole face of the earth. Why, then, do you wish to come to him through lands, who has filled the lands? Behold, he has now come; awake: by growing he knocks even at the sleeping; if at least there is not in them so great a sleep that even against the mountain coming they grow hard, but they hear: *Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you*[^33]. For it was much for the Jews to see the stone. For that stone was still small: deservedly they despised even the small one, by despising they stumbled, and by stumbling they were shattered; it remains that they be ground to pieces. For of that stone it was said: *He who stumbles on that stone shall be shattered; on whom it shall come, it shall grind him to pieces*[^34]. It is one thing to be shattered, another to be ground to pieces: to be shattered is less than to be ground to pieces: but the lofty one coming grinds to pieces no one, except him whom the lowly one lying shattered. For now, before our Lord come, he lay lowly to the Jews, and they stumbled on him, and were shattered: he will come afterward in his judgment bright and high, great and mighty; not weak that he be judged, but strong that he may judge, and may grind to pieces those who were shattered by stumbling on him. For a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal he himself is to those who do not believe[^35]. Therefore, dearly beloved, it is no wonder if the Jews did not recognize him whom, as a small stone lying before their feet, they despised: those are to be wondered at who still do not wish to recognize so great a mountain. The Jews stumbled on the small stone by not seeing it, the heretics stumble on the mountain. For now that stone has grown; now we say to them: Behold, the prophecy of Daniel is fulfilled: that *stone* which was small, *has become a great mountain, and has filled the whole earth*[^36]. Why do you stumble on it, and not ascend onto it? Who is so blind as to stumble on a mountain? As though he came to you for this, that you might have something to stumble on, and not have something to ascend onto. *Come, let us ascend to the mountain of the Lord*[^37]. Isaiah says this: *Come, let us ascend.* What is, *Come, let us ascend*? *Come,* is believe; *let us ascend,* let us advance. But those neither wish to come, nor to ascend, nor to believe, nor to advance. They bark against the mountain. Now by stumbling on it so often they have been shattered, and they do not wish to ascend, always choosing to stumble. Let us say to them: *Come, and see the works of the Lord, the prodigies which he has set upon the earth.* They are called prodigies, because they portend something, those signs of miracles which were done when the world believed. And what came of it, and what did they portend?

**13.** [v. 10] *Taking away wars even to the ends of the earth*[^38]. This we do not yet see fulfilled: there are still wars, there are wars among the nations for the kingdom; among sects, among Jews, pagans, Christians, heretics, there are wars, wars increase; some contending for truth, others for falsehood. Not yet, then, is it fulfilled: *Taking away wars even to the ends of the earth*: but perhaps it will be fulfilled. Or is it fulfilled even now? In certain ones it is fulfilled: in the wheat it is fulfilled, in the tares it is not yet fulfilled. What, then, is this: *Taking away wars even to the ends of the earth*? Does he mean wars by which war is made against God? For who wars against God? Impiety. And what can impiety do to God? Nothing. What does an earthen vessel do, dashed against a rock, however vehemently it be dashed? It comes with so much greater harm to itself, the greater the force with which it comes. These wars were great, were frequent. Against God impiety fought, and the earthen vessels were ground to pieces: men presuming on themselves, prevailing much by their own strength. Of which thing the shield too Job spoke of a certain impious man: *He ran against God on the thick neck of his shield*[^39]. What is, *on the thick neck of his shield*? Presuming too much on his own protection. Were those such, who said: *Our God is a refuge and strength, a helper in the tribulations which have found us exceedingly*? or in another psalm: *For I will not hope in my bow, and my arm will not save me*[^40]? When anyone recognizes that in himself he is nothing, and has no help from himself, his arms are broken in him, the wars are stilled. Such wars, then, that voice of the Most High from the holy clouds blotted out, by which the earth was moved, and the kingdoms were bowed down: it took away these wars even to the ends of the earth. *He will break the bow, and shatter the arms, and burn the shields with fire.* The bow, the arms, the shields, the fire. The bow is treachery; the arms, open assault; the shield, the vain protection of presumption. The fire by which these are burned, is that of which the Lord says: *I came to send fire on the earth*[^41]. Of which fire the psalm says: *And there is no one who hides himself from its heat*[^42]. This fire being fervent, no arms of impiety will remain in us, it is necessary that all be shattered, ground to pieces, burned. May you remain unarmed, having no help of your own: and the more weak you are, having no arms of your own, the more he upholds you, of whom it was said: *Our upholder the God of Jacob.* For you were strong as it were through yourself, you are troubled in yourself. Lose the arms on which you presumed: hear the Lord saying: *My grace is sufficient for you.* Say you too: *When I am weak, then am I strong*[^43]. It is the voice of the Apostle. He had lost all his arms, as it were of his own strength, who said: *But I will not glory, except in my weaknesses*[^44]: as if he said: I do not run against God on the thick neck of my shield, *I who was first a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious; but for this I obtained mercy, that in me Christ Jesus might show all his long-suffering toward those who are to believe in him unto eternal life*[^45]. *Taking away wars even to the ends of the earth.* But when the Lord upholds us, does he dismiss us unarmed? He arms us, but with other arms, evangelical, of truth, of continence, of salvation, of hope, of faith, of love. These arms we shall have, but not from ourselves. But the arms which we had of ourselves, were burned: if at least we have been kindled by that fire of the Holy Spirit, of which it is said: *And he will burn the shields with fire.* You who desired to be mighty in yourself, God made you weak, that he might make you strong from himself, because you were weak from yourself.

**14.** [v. 11] What, then, follows? *Be still.* To what end? *And see that I am God*[^46]. This is: Not you, but I am God: I created, I recreate; I formed, I reform; I made, I remake. If you could not make yourself, how can you remake yourself? This the contentious tumult of the human mind does not see: to which contentious tumult it is said: *Be still,* that is, restrain your minds from contradictions. Do not argue and, as it were, arm yourselves against God: otherwise there live arms not yet burned by that fire. But if they have been burned: *Be still;* because you have nothing wherewith to fight. But if you have been still in yourselves, and have asked all things from me, you who first presumed on yourselves: *Be still, and* you will see *that I am God.*

**15.** [v. 12] *I will be exalted among the nations, and I will be exalted in the earth*[^47]. A little before I had said that by the name of earth the nation of the Jews is signified, by the name of sea the rest of the nations. The mountains were transferred into the heart of the sea: the nations were troubled, the kingdoms were bowed down, the Most High gave his voice, and the earth was moved. *The Lord of powers is with us, our upholder the God of Jacob.* Miracles were done among the nations, the faith of the nations is fulfilled, the arms of human presumption burn: there is stillness in tranquillity of heart, that God may be recognized as the author of all his gifts. And after this glorification, does he desert even the people of the Jews, of whom the Apostle says: *For I say to you, that you be not wise in your own eyes, that blindness in part has happened to Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles should enter*? That is, until the mountains should be transferred here, the clouds should rain here, the Lord should here bow down the kingdoms by his thunder, *until the fullness of the Gentiles should enter.* And what afterward? *And so all Israel should be saved*[^48]. Therefore here too, keeping that very order: *I will be exalted,* he says, *among the nations, and I will be exalted in the earth*: that is, both in the sea and in the earth; that now all may say what follows: *The Lord of powers is with us, our upholder the God of Jacob.*
