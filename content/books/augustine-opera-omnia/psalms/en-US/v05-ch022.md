# Exposition of Psalm 115

**1.** I think it is most well known to your Holiness what the Apostle says: *For faith is not of all*[^1]; and that the multitude of unbelievers is wont to be greater, you are not ignorant: wherefore it is said: *Lord, who has believed our hearing?*[^2] Among whom are numbered too those of whom the same apostle says: *All seek their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's*[^3]: whom in another place he says to preach the word of God not from truth, but from occasion, not chastely, that is, not from a mind of pure and sincere love. For they felt one thing, which appeared in their morals; and preached another, that they might please men by the holy name: for of these he says again: *For neither do these serve God, but their own belly*[^4]. Whom yet he permits to announce Christ. For although they rather believed the things which they themselves did, so as to die: yet they preached the things which, if others believing did them, they would be saved; for they announced nothing beyond the rule of faith. For those the Apostle excludes, saying: *If anyone announces to you anything other than what you have received, let him be anathema*[^5]. Those do not announce Christ who announce falsehood; because Christ is truth. But of these he says that they announce Christ; but not chastely, that is, not with a simple and pure mind, and a sincere faith which works through love: for, consulting earthly desires, they announced the kingdom of heaven, having falsehood in their breast, truth on their tongue. And so the Apostle, knowing that even through Judas evangelizing believers were freed, permits these too thus: *Whether,* he says, *by occasion, or by truth, let Christ be announced*[^6]. For they announced the truth, even if not by truth, that is, not with a true mind. These speak what they do not believe, and therefore are reprobated; although they are useful to those whom the Lord instructs, saying: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not do*[^7]. Whence, except because they do not believe to be useful the things they say? But there are others who believe, and do not speak what they believe, either by laziness, or by fear. For that servant too, although he had a talent, yet because he was unwilling to put it out, hears from the Lord the judge: *Wicked and lazy servant*[^8]. And in another place of the Gospel it was said that many of the princes of the Jews had believed in him, but did not confess him, lest they be expelled from the synagogues; but these too were reproved and disapproved: for the Evangelist goes on, saying: *For they loved the glory of men more than of God*[^9]. If, then, both those who do not believe the truth which they speak, and those who do not speak the truth which they believe, are deservedly reprobated; who will be a servant truly to be called faithful, except him to whom it is said: *Well done, good servant, because you were faithful in a little, I will set you over much; enter into the joy of your lord*[^10]? Such a servant, then, neither speaks before he believes, nor is silent when he has believed; lest he either so put it out that he himself does not have it, or, by not putting out what he has, not have it. For so it was said: *To him who has, it will be given; but from him who has not, even what he has will be taken from him*[^11].

**2.** [v. 10.] Let this good servant, then, say, singing *Alleluia,* that is, sacrificing to the Lord the sacrifice of praise, from whom he is going to hear: *Enter into the joy of your Lord;* let him exult, and say: *I believed, wherefore I spoke*: that is, I believed perfectly. For they do not believe perfectly who are unwilling to speak what they believe: for to faith itself pertains too the believing of that which was said: *He who confesses me before men, I too will confess him before the angels of God*[^12]. For from this that servant was called faithful, not only because he received, but because he expended and gained. So in this place too he does not say: I believed, and spoke; but he says that he spoke for this reason, because he believed. For he believed at once both what reward he ought to hope for by speaking, and what punishment he ought to fear by being silent. *I believed,* he says, *wherefore I spoke. But I was humbled exceedingly.* For he suffered many tribulations, because of the word which he faithfully held, faithfully expended; and was humbled exceedingly: which those feared who *loved the glory of men more than of God.* But what is it: *But I?* He would rather say: I believed, wherefore I both spoke, and was humbled exceedingly: why did he add: *But I,* except because a man can be humbled by those who contradict the truth, not the truth itself which he believes and speaks? Whence the Apostle too, when he spoke of his chain: *But the word,* he says, *of God is not bound*[^13]. So this one too, because it is the one person of the holy witnesses, that is, the martyrs of God: *I believed,* he says, *wherefore I spoke. But I,* not that which I believed, not the word which I brought forth; but *I was humbled exceedingly.*

**3.** [v. 11.] *But I said in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar.* By ecstasy he means the dread which human weakness suffers when persecutors threaten, and the torments of suffering or death are impending. For we understand this, because in this psalm the voice of the martyrs appears. For ecstasy is also said in another way, when the mind is alienated not by dread, but is taken up by some inspiration of revelation. *But I said in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar.* For, terrified, he looked back at his weakness, and saw that he ought not to presume on himself. For as far as concerns the man himself, he is a liar: but by the grace of God he was made truthful, lest, yielding to the pressures of enemies, he should not speak what he had believed, but deny it; as happened to Peter, since he had presumed on himself, and had to be taught that one ought not to presume on man. And if one ought not to presume on man, surely neither on himself; because he is a man. Well, then, did this one see in his dread that every man is a liar; because even those who by no dread grow vain, lest by yielding to the persecutors they should lie, are such by the gifts of God, not by their own strength. Accordingly it was most truly said: *Every man is a liar;* but God is truthful, who says: *I said: You are gods, and all sons of the Most High: but you will die like men, and fall like one of the princes*[^14]. He consoles the humble, and fills them not only with the faith of believing, but also with the confidence of preaching the truth, if they perseveringly submit to God, and do not imitate one of the princes, the devil, who did not stand in the truth, and fell. For if every man is a liar, so far they will not be liars, as far as they will not be men; since they will be gods, and sons of the Most High.

**4.** [v. 12.] And so, considering this, the most devoted people of the faithful witnesses, how the mercy of God does not desert human weakness, in the dread of which weakness it was said: *Every man is a liar;* how he consoles the humble, and fills with the spirit of confidence those who tremble, so that with heart almost dead they revive, and are not trusting in themselves, but in him who raises the dead, and makes the tongues of infants eloquent; who says: *But when they hand you over, do not think how or what you will speak: for it will be given you in that hour what you will speak; for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^15]: considering, then, all these things, he who had said, *I said in my dread: Every man is a liar,* and seeing himself made truthful by the grace of the Lord: *What shall I render,* he says, *to the Lord, for all that he has rendered to me?* He does not say, for all that he has allotted to me; but *for all that he has rendered to me.* What things, then, had preceded on the part of man, that not the allotting, but the recompense of all the gifts of God could be called? what had preceded on the part of man, except sins? God renders, then, good for evil, to whom men render evil for good: for this they rendered who said: *This is the heir; come, let us kill him*[^16].

**5.** [vv. 13-15.] But this one seeks what to render to the Lord, and finds nothing, except from those things which the Lord himself rendered. *I will take,* he says, *the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord.* O man, a liar by your sin, truthful by the gift of God, and therefore now not a man; who gave you the cup of salvation, which, taking, and calling upon the name of the Lord, you will render to him for all that he has rendered to you? who, except him who says: *Can you drink the cup that I am about to drink?*[^17] Who gave you to imitate his passions, except he who first suffered for you? And therefore: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints.* He bought it with his blood, which he first shed for the salvation of his servants, lest the servants should hesitate to shed it for the name of the Lord; which, however, would profit them, not the Lord.

**6.** [v. 16.] So let the slave bought at so great a price confess his condition, and say: *O Lord, I am your servant; I am your servant, and the son of your handmaid.* Therefore he was both bought, and is homeborn. Or was he bought together with his mother? or, because he is homeborn, was he, for the sin of his flight, taken as plunder, and therefore bought, because he was redeemed? For he is the son of the handmaid according to which every creature is subject to the Creator, and owes most true service to the most true Lord: which, when he renders it, she is free, receiving this grace from the Lord, that she may serve him not by necessity, but by will. Therefore this one is the son of the heavenly Jerusalem, which is above, the free mother of us all. And free indeed from sin, but a handmaid of righteousness: to whose children still on pilgrimage it is said: *You were called to liberty;* and again he makes them servants, saying: *But in love serve one another*[^18]: to whom likewise it is said: *When you were serving sin, you were free from righteousness; but now, freed from sin, and made servants to God, you have your fruit unto sanctification, and the end eternal life*[^19]. Let this servant, then, say to God: Many call themselves martyrs, many your servants, because they have your name in various heresies and errors; but because they are outside your Church, they are not the sons of your handmaid: *But I* am *your servant, and the son of your handmaid.*

**7.** [v. 17.] *You have burst my bonds; to you I will sacrifice a sacrifice of praise.* For I found no merits of mine, when you burst my bonds; therefore I owe you a sacrifice of praise: because even if I glory that I am your servant and the son of your handmaid; I will glory not in myself, but in you my Lord, who burst my bonds, that, returning from flight, I might be bound again to you.

**8.** [v. 18.] *I will render my vows to the Lord.* What vows will you render? What victims did you vow? what incenses? what holocausts? Or do you look back to that which you said a little before: *I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord;* and: *To you I will sacrifice a sacrifice of praise?* And truly, whoever thinks well what he may vow to the Lord and what vows he may render, let him vow himself, let him render himself: this is demanded, this is owed. The coin being inspected, the Lord says: *Render to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's*[^20]: his own image is rendered to Caesar, let his own image be rendered to God.

**9.** [v. 19.] But he who remembers that he is not only a servant of God, but also the son of the handmaid of God, sees where he may render his vows, conformed to Christ through the cup of salvation. *In the courts,* he says, *of the house of the Lord.* What is the house of God, this is the handmaid of God: and what is the house of God, except all his people? Therefore there follows: *In the sight of all his people.* And more openly he now names the mother herself. For what else is his people, except what follows: *In the midst of you, O Jerusalem?* For then what is rendered is acceptable, if it is rendered from peace and in peace. But those who are not the children of this handmaid loved war rather than peace. But lest anyone suppose that by the courts of the house of the Lord, and all his people, the Jews are signified, because this psalm so closed that it said: *In the midst of you, O Jerusalem,* in whose name the carnal Israelites glory; hear the following psalm, which is contained in four little verses.
