# Exposition of Psalm 79

**1.** [v. 1.] There are not so many things in this psalm in which our discourse suffers difficulty, or the hearers' attention an impediment to understanding. Accordingly, with the Lord's help, with affection for hearing and seeing now the things that have been prophesied and foretold, as those instructed in the school of Christ, we ought to run through what is plain; so that, if perhaps the obscure things demand the duty of interpreting, those that are plain may demand of me the duty of reading. Here it is sung of the coming of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, and of his vineyard. But that Asaph sings, as it appears, enlightened and corrected, by whose name you know the synagogue is signified. The title of the Psalm, then, is: *Unto the end, for those who shall be changed*: surely for the better, since Christ, the end of the Law, came for this, to change into the better. And he adds, *a testimony to Asaph himself*: a good testimony of the truth. Finally, this testimony confesses both Christ and the vineyard; that is, the head and the body, the king and the people, the shepherd and the flock, and the whole mystery of all the Scriptures, Christ and the Church. And the title of the Psalm concludes, *for the Assyrians.* The Assyrians are interpreted: "those who direct." Now, then, let there not be a generation that did not direct its heart, but a generation now directing. Therefore let us hear what he says in this testimony.

**2.** [v. 2.] *You who feed Israel, attend.* What is: *You who feed Israel, attend, you who lead Joseph like sheep*? He is invoked to come, awaited to come, desired to come. Therefore let him find those who direct: *You who lead,* he says, *Joseph like sheep*: Joseph himself as sheep. Joseph himself is sheep, and Joseph himself is a sheep. On hearing "Joseph," although the interpretation of his name too helps much; for it signifies "increase," and therefore surely he came, that the grain put to death might rise up many-fold[^1], that is, the people of God might be increased: yet from what you already know happened to Joseph, let it come to mind that he was sold by his brothers, that he was dishonored by his own, exalted among foreigners[^2], and you will understand in whose flock we ought to be, together with those who now direct their heart, that the stone rejected by the builders may become the head of the corner, holding two walls coming from different sides, but agreeing in the corner[^3]. *You who sit upon the Cherubim.* The Cherubim is the seat of the glory of God, and is interpreted "fullness of knowledge." There God sits in the fullness of knowledge. Although we understand the Cherubim to be the sublime powers and virtues of the heavens; yet if you will, you shall be Cherubim. For if the Cherubim is the seat of God, hear what Scripture says: *The soul of the righteous is the seat of wisdom*[^4]. How, you say, shall I be the fullness of knowledge? who fulfills this? You have wherewith you may fulfill it: *The fullness of the Law is love*[^5]. Do not go through many things and be stretched out. The spreading of the branches frightens you; hold the root, and do not think of the greatness of the tree. Let there be in you love, and it is necessary that the fullness of knowledge follow. For what does he not know who knows love, since it has been said: *God is love*[^6]?

**3.** [v. 3.] *You who sit upon the Cherubim, appear.* For we erred for this reason, because you did not appear. *Before Ephraim, and Benjamin, and Manasseh.* Appear, I say, before the nation of the Jews, before the people of Israel: for there is Ephraim, there Manasseh, there Benjamin. But let us see the interpretation: Ephraim, "fructification"; Benjamin, "son of the right hand"; Manasseh, "forgotten." Appear, then, before the fructified, before the son of the right hand, appear before the forgotten, that he may no longer be forgotten, but you may come to the mind of him whom you freed. For if the Gentiles will be reminded, and all the ends of the earth will be converted to the Lord[^7]; will the people itself coming from Abraham not have its wall to rejoice in the corner, since it is written: *A remnant shall be saved*[^8]? *Stir up your power.* For you were weak, when it was said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^9]. You seemed to avail nothing: the persecutor prevailed over you; and you showed this beforehand, that Jacob himself too prevailed in the wrestling, a man over an Angel. When, except that the Angel willed it? and the man prevailed, and the Angel was conquered; and the conquering man holds the Angel, and says: *I will not let you go, unless you bless me.* A great mystery! And the conquered one stands, and blesses the conqueror: conquered, because he willed it; in the flesh weak, in majesty strong. And he blessed: *You shall be called,* he says, *Israel.* Yet he touched the broadness of his thigh too, and it withered, and made the same one man both blessed and lame[^10]. You see the Jewish people limped: see there too the blessed line of the Apostles. *Stir up,* then, *your power*: how long will you seem weak? Crucified from weakness, rise again in power[^11]: *Stir up your power, and come, to save us.*

**4.** [v. 4.] *O God, convert us.* For we are turned away from you, and unless you convert us, we shall not be converted. *And enlighten your face, and we shall be saved.* Does he then have a darkened face? He does not have a darkened face, but he set before it the cloud of flesh, and as it were the veil of weakness, and he was not thought to be the one to be recognized when he hung on the wood, who would sit in heaven. For so it was done. Asaph did not recognize Christ present on earth and doing miracles; yet dead, after he rose again and ascended into heaven, he recognized him, was pricked with compunction: and he may have said of him all this testimony which we now recognize in this psalm: *Enlighten your face, and we shall be saved.* You covered your face, and we were sick: enlighten it, and we shall be saved.

**5.** [v. 5.] *O Lord God of hosts, how long will you be angry at the prayer of your servant?* Now *your servant.* You were angry at the prayer of your enemy; you are still angry at the prayer of your servant! You have converted us, we have recognized you, and you are still angry at the prayer of your servant! So plainly you are angry, as a father correcting, not as a judge condemning. So plainly you are angry, because it is written: *Son, when you draw near to the service of God, stand in righteousness and fear, and prepare your soul for temptation*[^12]. Do not suppose that the anger of God has already passed because you are converted; it has passed, but lest it condemn forever. But he scourges, he does not spare, because he scourges every son whom he receives[^13]. If you refuse to be scourged, why do you desire to be received? He scourges every son whom he receives: he scourges everyone, who did not spare even his Only-begotten. But yet *how long will you be angry at the prayer of your servant?* not now your enemy; but *you are angry at the prayer of your servant.* How long?

**6.** [v. 6.] There follows: *You will feed us with the bread of tears, and give us tears to drink in measure.* What is *in measure*? Hear the Apostle: *God is faithful, who does not permit you to be tempted beyond what you can bear*[^14]. That is the measure, according to your strength: that is the measure, that you may be instructed, not that you may be overwhelmed.

**7.** [v. 7.] *You have set us as a contradiction to our neighbors.* Plainly it has been done; for from Asaph were chosen those who would go to the Gentiles, and preach Christ, and it would be said to them: *Who is this announcer of new demons*[^15]? *You have set us as a contradiction to our neighbors.* For they preached him who was contradicted. Whom did they preach? Christ, dead, risen again. Who would hear this? who would know this? A new thing! but signs followed, and miracles made faith in the incredible thing. He was contradicted, but the contradictor was conquered, and from a contradictor was made a believer. Yet there was a great flame there: there the martyrs were fed with the bread of tears, and given tears to drink; but in measure, not more than they could bear, that after the measure of tears might follow the crown of joys. *And our enemies mocked us.* And where are those who mocked? It was long said: Who are these worshiping a dead man, adoring a crucified man? it was long said. Where is the nose of the mockers? Do not those who reprove now flee into caverns, lest they be seen? *And our enemies mocked us.*

**8.** [vv. 8-9.] But you see what follows: *O Lord God of hosts, convert us, and show your face, and we shall be saved. You transferred a vineyard out of Egypt; you cast out the nations, and planted it.* We know it, it was done. How many nations were cast out! The Amorites, the Hittites, the Jebusites, the Girgashites, and the Hivites: who being expelled and conquered, the people freed from Egypt was led into the land of promise. Whence the vineyard was cast out, and where it was planted, we have heard. Let us see what was done next; how it believed, how much it grew, what it occupied. *You transferred a vineyard out of Egypt; you cast out the nations, and planted it.*

**9.** [vv. 10-12.] *You made a way in its sight, and planted its roots, and it filled the earth.* Would it fill the earth, unless a way were made in its sight? What way was made in its sight? *I am,* he says, *the way, the truth, and the life*[^16]. Rightly it filled the earth. This was now said of this vineyard, which was perfected unto the end: but yet what first? *Its shadow covered the mountains, and its branches the cedars of God. You stretched out its shoots even to the sea, and its offshoots even to the river.* This demands the duty of the expounder, the duty of the reader and praiser does not suffice: help me by attending; for the mention of this vineyard in this psalm is wont to make a gloom for those less attentive. For the greatness of this vineyard has already been explained by us; and whence it was made, and whence it was made great, has been foretold. *You made a way in its sight, and planted its roots, and it filled the earth*: this was said of its perfection. But yet the first nation, Judea, was this vineyard. And the Jewish nation reigned as far as the sea, and as far as the river. As far as the sea: it appears in Scripture that the sea is near there. And as far as the river Jordan. For across the Jordan something of the Jews was placed; but within the Jordan, the whole nation. Therefore *as far as the sea, and as far as the river* was the kingdom of the Jews, the kingdom of Israel: but not *from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the earth,* which is now the perfection of the vineyard of which he foretold here: *You made a way in its sight, and planted its roots, and it filled the earth.* When, then, he had foretold its perfection, he returned to the beginning, from which beginning the perfection was made. Do you wish to hear the beginning? *As far as the sea and as far as the river.* Do you wish to hear the end? *He shall have dominion from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the earth*[^17]: this is, *it filled the earth.* Let us see, then, the testimony of Asaph; what happened to the first vineyard, what is to be expected of the second vineyard, rather of the same vineyard: for it is the same; for it is not another. From there is Christ, salvation from the Jews[^18], from there the Apostles, from there the first believers, laying the prices of their goods at the feet of the Apostles[^19]; from there all these things. And if some of the branches *were broken off, they were broken off because of unbelief: but you,* people of the Gentiles, *stand by faith; do not be high-minded, but fear. For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you. But if you are high-minded, you do not bear the root, but the root you*[^20]. What, then, was the vineyard before whose sight a way was made, that it might fill the earth—where was it first? *Its shadow covered the mountains.* Who are the mountains? The Prophets. Why did its shadow cover them? Because obscurely they spoke the things that were announced as to come. You hear from the Prophets: Observe the sabbath: On the eighth day circumcise the infant: Offer the sacrifice of a ram, a calf, a he-goat: do not be moved; it is its shadow covering the mountains of God, after the shadow will come the manifestation. *And its branches the cedars of God*: that is, it covered the cedars of God, the highest, but of God. For there are cedars signifying the proud to be overthrown. The cedars of Lebanon, the heights of the world, this vineyard covered by growing, and the mountains of God, all the holy Prophets, the Patriarchs.

**10.** [v. 13.] But how far *did you stretch out its shoots? Even to the sea, and even to the river its offshoots.* From there, what? *Why have you destroyed its wall?* Now you see that nation of the Jews overthrown; now you have heard from another psalm: *With hatchet and crowbar they cast it down*[^21]. When would it happen, unless its wall were destroyed? What is its wall? Its fortification. For it rose up proud against its planter. The servants sent to it and asking for the fruit, the tenants scourged, beat, killed. The only Son too came; they said: *This is the heir, come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours: they killed him and cast him out of the vineyard*[^22]. Cast out, he rather possessed that from which he was cast out. For thus he threatens it through Isaiah: *I will destroy its wall.* Why? *For I waited that it might make a grape; but it made thorns*: I waited thence for fruit, and I found sin. What, then, do you ask, O Asaph: *Why have you destroyed its wall?* Do you not know why? *I waited that it might do judgment, and it did iniquity*[^23]: was its wall not to be destroyed? And the Gentiles came, the wall destroyed, the vineyard was invaded, and the kingdom of the Jews destroyed. This he first bewails, but not without hope. For he now speaks of the direction of the heart, that is, the Psalm is for the Assyrians, for those directing. *Why have you destroyed its wall; and all who pass along the way pluck it?* What is *passing along the way*? Temporally ruling.

**11.** [v. 14.] *The boar from the wood has laid it waste.* What do we understand by the boar from the wood? To the Jews the pig is hateful, and in the pig they set, as it were, the uncleanness of the Gentiles. But by the Gentiles the Jewish nation was overthrown: but that king who overthrew it was not only an unclean pig, but also a boar. For what is a boar, except a savage pig, a proud pig? *The boar from the wood laid it waste. From the wood*: from the Gentiles. For that was a vineyard; but the Gentiles were woods. But when the Gentiles believed, what was said? *Then shall all the trees of the woods exult*[^24]. *The boar from the wood laid it waste, and the solitary wild beast fed upon it.* What is *the solitary wild beast*? The boar itself which laid it waste, the solitary wild beast. Solitary, because proud. For this says every proud man: I am; I am, and no one else.

**12.** [vv. 15-16.] But to what fruit are these things? *O God of hosts, turn now.* Although these things have been done, *turn now. Look down from heaven, and see, and visit this vineyard. And perfect it which your right hand has planted.* Do not institute another, but perfect this one. For it is the seed of Abraham, it is the seed in which all nations are blessed[^25]: there is the root, where the grafted wild olive is borne. *Perfect this vineyard which your right hand has planted.* But where does he perfect it? *And upon the son of man whom you have confirmed for yourself.* What is more evident? what do you still await that we should expound to you by disputing, and not rather cry out with you in wonder: *Perfect this vineyard which your right hand has planted, and upon the son of man* perfect it? What son of man? *Whom you have confirmed for yourself.* A great firmament! build as much as you can. *For other foundation no one can lay, besides that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^26].

**13.** [v. 17.] *Things kindled with fire and dug out shall perish at the rebuke of your countenance.* What are the things kindled with fire and dug out, which shall perish at the rebuke of his countenance? Let us see and understand what the things are that are kindled with fire and dug out. What did Christ rebuke? Sins: at the rebuke of his countenance sins perished. Why, then, are sins kindled with fire and dug out? Two things make all sins in man; desire, and fear. Think, examine, question your hearts, search your consciences; see whether there can be sins, except either by desiring, or by fearing. A reward is set before you that you may sin—that is, what delights you; you do it for the sake of what you desire. But perhaps you are not induced by gifts, you are terrified by threats; you do it for the sake of what you fear. Someone wishes to corrupt you, that you may say, for example, false testimony. They are innumerable, but I set forth plainer ones, from which you may infer the rest. You attended to God, and said in your soul: *What does it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, but suffer the loss of his soul*[^27]? I am not led by a reward, to lose my soul for the gain of money. He turns himself to striking in fear; he who could not corrupt by a reward, begins to threaten loss, expulsion, perhaps blows and death: there now, if desire did not avail, perhaps fear will avail that you sin. But further, if from the Scriptures against desire there comes to your mind: *What does it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, but suffer the loss of his soul;* let there come also, against fear: *Do not fear those who kill the body; but the soul they cannot kill*[^28]. Whoever wishes to kill you, to the body it is permitted him; to the soul it is not permitted. Your soul will not die, unless you yourself wish to kill it. Let another's iniquity destroy your flesh, while truth preserves your soul. But if you spring back from the truth, what more will the enemy do to you, than you do to yourself? The raging enemy can kill the flesh: but you, by speaking false testimony, kill the soul. Hear the Scripture: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^29]. And so, my brothers, to every rightly-done deed love and fear lead: to every sin love and fear lead. That you may do well, you love God, and fear God: but that you may do ill, you love the world, and fear the world. Let these two be turned to good: you loved the earth, love eternal life; you feared death, fear hell. Whatever the world promises you when you are wicked, can it give as much as God will give to the righteous? Whatever the world threatens you when you are righteous, can it do what God does to the wicked? Do you wish to see what God will give, if you have lived righteously? *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* Do you wish to see what he will do to the impious? *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^30]. You surely want nothing else than that it be well with you. For in that which you love, you want it to be well with you; and in that which you fear, you do not want it to be ill with you: but you do not seek in that region in which it ought to be sought. You are in haste; for you want both to be without need, and without trouble: it is good, what you want, but bear what you do not want, that you may attain what you want. Accordingly, his countenance, blotting out sins, what will it do? what are the sins kindled with fire and dug out? What had evil love done? It had kindled, as it were, a fire. What had evil fear done? It had, as it were, dug out. For love inflames; fear humbles: therefore the sins of evil love are kindled with fire; the sins of evil fear are dug out. Both good fear humbles, and good love kindles; but in one way and another. For the husbandman too, interceding for the tree which gave no fruit, lest it be cut down, said: *I dig around it, and apply a basket of dung*[^31]. The digging signifies the pious humility of one fearing, and the basket of dung the useful filth of one repenting. But of the fire of good love the Lord says: *I came to send fire into the world*[^32]. With which fire let those burn who are fervent in spirit, and blazing with the love of God and neighbor. And through this, as all righteous works are done by good fear and good love; so by evil love and evil fear all sins are committed. Therefore: *Things kindled with fire and dug out,* namely all sins, *shall perish at the rebuke of your countenance.*

**14.** [vv. 18-20.] *Let your hand be upon the man of your right hand, and upon the son of man whom you have confirmed for yourself. And we do not depart from you.* How long, a perverse and embittering generation, and one that does not direct its heart? Let Asaph say: Let your mercy show itself; deal well with your vineyard, perfect it, *since blindness in part has happened to Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter in, and so all Israel might be saved*[^33]. With your face shown *upon the man of your right hand whom you have confirmed for yourself, we do not depart from you*: how long do you rebuke us? how long do you accuse? Do this, *and we do not depart from you: You will give us life, and we will call upon your name.* You will be sweet to us; *you will give us life.* For first we loved the earth, not you; but you have put to death our members which are upon the earth[^34]. For that Old Testament, having earthly promises, seems to persuade this, that God should not be worshiped freely; but, because he gives something on earth, therefore he should be loved. What do you love, that you should not love God? tell me. Love, if you can, something which he did not make. Look around at the whole creation; see whether anywhere you are held by the birdlime of desire, and impeded from loving the Creator, except by that thing which he himself, whom you neglect, created. But why do you love those things, except because they are beautiful? Can they be as beautiful as he by whom they were made? You marvel at these, because you do not see him: but through those things at which you marvel, love him whom you do not see. Question the creation; if it is from itself, remain in it: but if it is from him, it is harmful to its lover for no other reason, except that it is preferred to the Creator. Why have I said this? On account of this verse, brothers. They were dead, then, who worshiped God for this, that it might be well with them according to the flesh: for to savor according to the flesh is death[^35]; and they are dead who do not worship God freely—that is, because he himself is good, not because he gives such goods as he gives even to those who are not good. Do you want money from God? The robber has it too. A wife, fruitfulness of children, health of body, dignity of the world? Mark how many evil men have these. Is this the whole reason for which you worship him? Your feet will waver, you will suppose you worship without cause, when you see those things in those who do not worship him[^36]. Therefore he gives all these things even to the evil; he keeps himself only for the good. *You will give us life*: for we were dead, when we clung to earthly things; we were dead, when we bore the image of the earthly man. *You will give us life*: you will renew us; you will give us the life of the inner man. *And we will call upon your name*: that is, we will love you. You will be sweet to us, the forgiver of our sins; you will be the whole reward of those justified. *O Lord God of hosts, convert us; and show your face, and we shall be saved.*
