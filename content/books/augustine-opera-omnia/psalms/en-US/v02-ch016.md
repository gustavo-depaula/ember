# Exposition of Psalm 44

**1.** [v. 1] This psalm, as we sang it with you with exultation, so I ask that you consider it with us with attention. For it is sung about the holy nuptials, about the bridegroom and the bride, about the king and the people, about the Savior and those who are to be saved. He who comes to the nuptials with a wedding garment, seeking the glory of the bridegroom, not his own, not only gladly hears, which is wont to be the case even with men seeking spectacles, displaying no deeds; but even commits it to the heart, that it may not there be idle, but bud, break forth, grow, be perfected, be taken up. For we who must be those to whom this is sung, ought to be sons of Korah, which the title of the psalm has. For these were certain men: yet every inscription of the divine Letters hints something to those who understand, and desires not only a hearer, but also one who recognizes. For we ask the force of the Hebrew word, what Korah is: and, as the interpretations of all the words set in Scripture have it, it is reported to us that the sons of Korah are interpreted the sons of the Bald one. Which name do not take in mockery, lest we be found in a childish sense, such children as we read in the book of Kings, insulting Elisha the holy prophet, and crying after him: *Go up, bald-head, go up, bald-head.* For such foolishly garrulous children, and cursing to their own ruin, beasts coming out of the wood devoured. This is written, and where it is written we have recalled: those who remember, let them recognize; those who do not remember, let them read; those who have not read, let them believe[^1]. That, then, which that deed prefigured for the future, ought not to lay hold of us. For in those children were signified foolish men, having the sense of ignorance; such as the Apostle does not wish us to be, where he says: *Do not be children in your minds*[^2]. And because the Lord invited us to the imitation of children, when he set a little one before himself, and said: *Unless one becomes as this child, he will not enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^3]; there too the Apostle, cautious, where he calls back from a childish mind, calls again to childlike imitation: *Do not,* he says, *become children in your minds, but in malice be little ones, that you may be perfect in your minds*[^4]. Let him whom it delights to imitate a child, be delighted not by inexperience, but by innocence. But those, out of inexperience, insulted the bald holy man of God, and cried after him: *Bald-head, bald-head.* It came about that they were consumed by beasts: and they figured men in the same childish mind foolishly deriding a certain bald man; because he was crucified in the place of Calvary. Such men, then, were possessed as it were by beasts, that is, by demons, by the devil and his angels, who works in the sons of disobedience. Such were the children who, standing before the consecrated wood, wagged the head, and said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^5]. Of this one we are the sons, because we are sons of the bridegroom: and to us is inscribed this psalm, whose title is said: *To the sons of Korah, for the things that shall be changed.*

**2.** What shall I expound what it is, *for the things that shall be changed*? What shall I say? This everyone who is changed recognizes. He who hears these things, *For the things that shall be changed,* let him see what he was, and what now he is. And first let him see the world itself changed; lately adoring idols, now adoring God; lately serving the things which it made, now him by whom it was made. *For the things that shall be changed,* see when it was said. Now already the remaining pagans dread the things changed: and those who do not wish to be changed, see the churches filled, the temples deserted; here a throng, there a solitude. They marvel at the things changed, let them read the things foretold: let them lend an ear to the promiser, let them believe the one who fulfills. But each one of us too, brothers, is changed from the old man into the new; from unbelieving he becomes faithful, from a plunderer a giver, chaste from an adulterer, beneficent from a malefactor. Therefore let it be sung to us, *For the things that shall be changed*: and so let there begin to be described, through whom they were changed.

**3.** For there follows: *For the things that shall be changed, to the sons of Korah, for understanding, a Canticle for the beloved.* For that beloved was seen by his persecutors, but not unto understanding. For if they had understood, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory[^6]. For this understanding he himself sought other eyes, when he said: *He who sees me, sees also the Father*[^7]. Let the psalm now sound him: let us rejoice in the nuptials, and we shall be with those who become the nuptials who are invited to the nuptials; and those invited are themselves the bride. For the bride is the Church, the bridegroom Christ. Certain songs are wont to be said by the learned for those taking wives and marrying, which are called epithalamia; whatever is sung there, is sung to the honor of the bridegroom and bride: or is it perhaps that even in these nuptials, to which we are invited, there is no bridal chamber? And whence does another psalm say: *In the sun he set his tabernacle, and he himself as a bridegroom coming forth from his bridal chamber*[^8]? The nuptial conjunction, the Word and flesh: the bridal chamber of this conjunction, the womb of the Virgin. For the flesh itself is conjoined to the Word: whence also it is said: *Now not two, but one flesh*[^9]. The Church was taken up from the human race, so that the head of the Church should be that very flesh conjoined to the Word, and the rest who believe should be the members of that head. For do you wish to see who came to the nuptials? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^10]. Let the bride rejoice, beloved by God. When beloved? While still foul. *For all,* says the Apostle, *have sinned, and need the glory of God*[^11]. And again: *For Christ died for the ungodly*[^12]. She was loved foul, that she might not remain foul. For not truly was the foul thing loved, because the foulness was not loved: for if it loved this, it would preserve it: it overthrew the foulness, it formed beauty. To what kind did he come, and what kind did he make? Let him now come in the prophetic words; behold, let the bridegroom himself come forth to us: let us love him; or if we find anything foul in him, let us not love him. Behold, he himself found many foul things, and loved us: if we find anything foul in him, let us not love him. Because even this very thing that he put on flesh, so that of him too it was said, *We saw him, and he had no form nor comeliness*[^13]; if you consider the mercy by which he was made it, even there he is beautiful. But the prophet bore the person of the Jews, when he said: *We saw him, and he had no form nor comeliness.* Why? Because not unto understanding. But to those who understand, even *the Word was made flesh*[^14], there is great beauty. *But far be it from me to glory,* said one of the friends of the bridegroom, *except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^15]. It is too little that you not blush at it, unless you even glory. Why, then, had he no form nor comeliness? Because Christ crucified is to the Jews indeed a scandal, to the Gentiles folly. But why did he have comeliness even on the cross? Because what is foolish of God is wiser than men; and what is weak of God is stronger than men[^16]. To us, then, who now believe, let the bridegroom everywhere meet us beautiful. Beautiful God, the Word with God: beautiful in the womb of the Virgin, where he did not lose divinity, and took humanity: beautiful the Word born an infant; because even when he was an infant, when he was suckled, when he was carried in the hands, the heavens spoke, the angels said praises, the star directed the Magi, he was adored in the manger, food of the meek. Beautiful, then, in heaven, beautiful on earth; beautiful in the womb, beautiful in the hands of his parents; beautiful in miracles, beautiful in scourges; beautiful inviting to life, beautiful not caring for death; beautiful laying down his soul, beautiful taking it up; beautiful on the wood, beautiful in the tomb, beautiful in heaven. Hear the Canticle unto understanding, and let not the weakness of the flesh turn your eyes from the splendor of his beauty. The highest and true beauty is justice: there you will not see him beautiful, where you find him unjust: if everywhere just, everywhere comely. Let him come, then, to be beheld by us with the eyes of the mind, described by a certain praiser of his, a prophet: behold, he begins.

**4.** [v. 2] *My heart has belched forth a good word*[^17]. Who says it, the Father, or the Prophet? For some understood the person of the Father saying: *My heart has belched forth a good word,* commending to us a certain ineffable birth. Lest perhaps you should think something was taken up, whence God might beget the Son; as a man takes to himself something whence he may beget sons, namely a marriage, without which a man cannot procreate offspring: lest, then, you should think that God needed some marriage, whence he might beget the Son: *It has belched forth,* he says, *my heart a good word.* Today your heart, O man, begets a counsel, and does not seek a wife: through the counsel born from your heart you build something; and that building, before it stands in the work, stands in the counsel; and there is already in that through which you are about to make it, what you are about to make: and you praise the building not yet existing, not yet in the appearance of an edifice, but in the bringing-forth of the counsel; nor does another praise your counsel, unless either you have indicated it, or he has seen what you made. Therefore if through the Word are all things, and the Word is of God; inspect the building made through the Word, and from this edifice marvel at the counsel. What a Word it is through which heaven and earth were made, and all the adornment of heaven, all the fecundity of the earth, the spread of the sea, the extension of the air, the gleam of the stars, the brightness of the sun and moon? These things are seen: transcend these too; think of the angels, Principalities, Thrones, Dominions, Powers: all things were made through him. Whence, then, were these good things made? Because that was belched forth through which they should be made, the *good Word.* Therefore the good Word: and to the Word itself it was said: *Good Master.* And the Word itself answered: *Why do you ask me about the good? No one is good, but the one God*[^18]. It was said: *Good Master;* and he says: *Why do you ask me about the good?* He added too: *No one is good, but the one God.* How, then, is he himself too good, except because he is God? But not only God, but with the Father one God. For by saying: *No one is good, but the one God,* he did not separate himself, but united himself. *My heart has belched forth a good word.* Let God the Father have said this about his good Word and beneficent good for us, through which good alone we can somehow be good.

**5.** There follows: *I tell my works to the king*[^19]. Does the Father still speak? If the Father still speaks, let us seek also this, how we may understand it according to the true and catholic faith: *I tell my works to the king.* For if the Father tells his works to his Son our king; what works will the Father tell to the Son, when all the works of the Father were made through the Son? Or does perhaps *I tell my works to the king,* the very *I tell,* signify the generation of the Son? I fear lest this sometimes cannot be understood by the slower: yet I will say it; let him follow who can, lest by my not saying it he who can does not follow. We read it said in another psalm: *Once has God spoken*[^20]. So often he has spoken through the prophets, so often through the apostles, today too he speaks through his saints, and he says: *Once has God spoken.* Whence has he spoken once, except on account of the one Word? But as *My heart has belched forth a good word* we understood there the generation of the Son; it seems to me a repetition is made in the following sentence, so that what was said: *My heart has belched forth a good word,* is repeated in that which he says: *I tell.* For what is, *I tell*? I bring forth the Word. And whence does God bring forth the Word, except from his heart, from his inmost being? You do not speak except what you bring forth from your heart; your word which sounds and passes, is not brought forth from elsewhere: and do you marvel that God so speaks? But the speaking of God is eternal. You say something now, because you were silent a little before: or behold now you do not yet bring forth a word; but when you have begun to bring it forth, you break the silence in a way, and beget a word which before was not. Not so did God beget the Word: the speaking of God is without beginning, and without end; and yet he speaks one Word. Let him speak another, if what he has said has passed. But since both he by whom it is said remains, and what is said remains; and it is said once and is not ended; and that very "once" is said without beginning, nor is it said twice, because what is said once does not pass. This, then, is: *My heart has belched forth a good word,* which is: *I tell my works to the king.* Why, then, *My works do I tell*? Because in the Word itself are all the works of God. For whatever God was to make in the creature, was already in the Word; nor would it be in things, unless it were in the Word: as in you too it would not be in the building, unless it were in the counsel. As in the Gospel it is said: *What was made, in him was life*[^21]. There was, then, what was made, but it was in the Word: and all the works of God were there, and the works were not yet: but the Word was, and this Word was God, and was with God, and was the Son of God, and was with the Father one God. *I tell my works to the king.* Let him hear the one telling, who understands the Word; and let him see, with the Father, the everlasting Word, in which are even the things that are future, in which the things that have passed have not departed. These works of God in the Word, as in the Word, as in the Only-begotten, as in the Word of God.

**6.** What, then, follows? *My tongue is the pen of a scribe writing swiftly*[^22]. What likeness, my brothers, what likeness has the tongue of God with the pen of a scribe? What likeness has the rock with Christ? what likeness has the lamb with the Savior? what likeness has the lion with the strength of the Only-begotten? Yet these things have been said: and unless they were said, in no way would we be informed through these visible things toward the invisible. So, then, this humble likeness of the pen, let us neither compare to that excellence, nor yet reject. For I ask why he said his tongue is the pen of a scribe writing swiftly? But however swiftly a scribe writes, he is not compared to that swiftness, of which another psalm says: *His word runs even unto swiftness*[^23]. But it seems to me, as far as human understanding dares, that this too can be taken as said from the person of the Father: *My tongue is the pen of a scribe.* Because what is said by the tongue sounds and passes; what is written, remains: since, then, God says the Word, and the Word which is said does not sound and pass, but is both said and remains; God preferred to compare this to writings rather than to sounds. But that he added, *writing swiftly,* impelled the mind to understanding: but let it not remain sluggish, by looking back at copyists, or by looking back at any swift shorthand-writers; but if it attends to this, it will remain there. The very *swiftly,* let it think swiftly; and let it see why it was said, *swiftly.* The swiftly of God is such, that nothing is swifter. For in writings letter is written after letter, syllable after syllable, word after word; nor is one passed to the second, unless the first is written out. But there nothing is swifter, where there are not many words, nor yet is anything passed over, since in one are all things.

**7.** [v. 3] Behold now that Word so brought forth, eternal, coeternal from the eternal, the bridegroom will come. *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^24]. *Beyond the sons of men*: why not also beyond the angels? What did he wish to say: *Beyond the sons of men,* except because a man? Lest you should think Christ the man any ordinary man, he said: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men.* A man too beyond the sons of men; even among the sons of men, beyond the sons of men; even from the sons of men, beyond the sons of men. *Grace is poured out on your lips.* The Law was given through Moses; Grace and truth were made through Jesus Christ[^25]. *Grace is poured out on your lips.* Deservedly was I aided, because *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man. But another law in my members fights against the law of my mind, and leads me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members. Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^26]. *Grace,* then, *is poured out on your lips.* He came to us with the word of grace, with the kiss of grace. What is sweeter than this grace? To what does this grace pertain? *Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered*[^27]. If a severe judge came, and did not bring that grace poured out on his lips, who would hope anything about salvation? Who would not fear for himself that which was owed to the sinner? He, coming with grace, did not exact what was owed, he paid what he did not owe. For did not the sinner owe death? Or was anything owed to you, a sinner, except punishment? He forgave your debts, and paid out his own that were not owed. Great grace. Why grace? Because freely given. Therefore it is allowed you to give him thanks, it is not allowed to repay; for you cannot. He sought what he might repay, and said: *What shall I render to the Lord for all the things that he has rendered to me?* And he found as it were something: *I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord*[^28]. Do you repay him this, that you take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord? Who gave you that cup of salvation? He remained in the giving of thanks, for in the repaying he failed. Find what you may give to God, which you have not received from him; and you will have repaid the favor. But beware lest, while you seek what you may repay him which you have not received from him, you find your own sin. This plainly you did not receive from him, but neither ought you to give it to him. This the Jews gave, they repaid evil for good; they received from him rain, and gave not fruit, but the thorns of griefs. Therefore whatever good you wish to give to God in yourself, you do not find that you have received it except from God. This is the grace of God poured out on the lips. He made you, he made you freely. For there was not one to whom he should grant it before he made it. You had perished, he sought you; and, found, he called you back. The past things he did not impute, the future he promised. Truly *grace is poured out on your lips.*

**8.** *Therefore,* he says, *God has blessed you forever*[^29]. It is labored, that this can be understood as still said by God the Father: *Therefore God has blessed you forever.* It seems more aptly taken from the person of the Prophet. And sudden changes of persons, and altogether unforeseen, are found in the holy books of the Scriptures; and if one attends, the divine pages are full of them. *Lord, free my soul from iniquitous lips and from a deceitful tongue;* and at once: *What may be given to you, or what added to you against a deceitful tongue?* One person was there, another here: there of the one asking, here of the one coming to aid. *The sharp arrows of the mighty, with coals that lay waste.* Another person: *What may be given to you, or what added to you;* and in what follows it becomes another: *Woe is me! that my sojourning has been made far off*[^30]. In a few verses so frequent a change of persons admonishes the understanding: it does not express the place where it is changed; it is not said: This a man said, this God said; but from the very words it is made for us to understand what pertains to man, what to God. Now man was saying: *My heart has belched forth a good word, I tell my works to the king.* Man was saying it, he who wrote the psalm was saying it; but from the person of God he was saying it: he begins to say it also from his own: *Therefore God has blessed you forever.* For God had said: *Grace is poured out on your lips,* to him whom he had made beautiful beyond the sons of men, the man too whom, as God before all things, he had brought forth, the eternal the coeternal. The Prophet, then, was filled with a certain ineffable joy, and attending to what God the Father revealed to a man about his Son, he who could say these things even from the person of God: *Therefore,* he says, *God has blessed you forever.* Why? On account of grace. For to what does that grace pertain? To the kingdom of heaven. For the first Testament had promised the land: and there was one reward, or promise, for those set under the Law, another for those under Grace: the land of the Canaanites for the Jews set under the Law, the kingdom of heaven for Christians set under Grace. And so what pertained to those set under the Law, the kingdom, that land passed away: the kingdom of heaven which pertains to those set under Grace does not pass away. Therefore here, *God has blessed you,* not for a time, but *forever.*

**9.** There were not lacking those who preferred that all the earlier words too be taken from the person of the Prophet: and they wished this which was said, *My heart has belched forth a good word,* to be understood from the Prophet, as it were saying a hymn. (For whoever says a hymn to God, his heart belches forth a good word: as he who blasphemes against God, his heart belches forth an evil word.) So that even that which was added, *I tell my works to the king,* they wished to signify that the highest work of man is none other than to praise God. It is his to please you by his appearance, it pertains to you to praise him in thanksgiving. If your works are not the praise of God, you begin to love yourself; and you will pertain to those of whom the Apostle says: *For men will be lovers of themselves*[^31]. Be displeasing to yourself, let him who made you be pleasing to you; because in this you are displeasing to yourself, in that which you yourself made in yourself. Let your work, then, be the praise of God, let your heart belch forth a good word. *Tell,* then, *your works to the king*: because that you might tell them, the king made it, and he himself granted what you might offer. Render to him of his own, lest, having received a part of your patrimony, you wish to go far off and prodigally lose it on harlots, and feed swine. Recall this from the Gospel. But of us too it was said: *He was dead, and lived again; he had perished, and was found*[^32].

**10.** *My tongue is the pen of a scribe writing swiftly*[^33]. There were not lacking those who so understood that the Prophet said the things which he wrote, and therefore compared his tongue to the pen of a scribe. But *writing swiftly* they wished him to mean, that he signified that he was writing things which were swiftly to come, so that to write swiftly is understood to write swift things, that is, to write things that would not delay. For God did not delay to bring forth Christ. How quickly it is felt to be rolled away, what is recognized as accomplished! Recall the generations before you, you will find Adam, as it were, made yesterday. So we read all things done from the very beginning: therefore they were done swiftly. The day of judgment too will be swift; anticipate its swiftness: it will come swiftly, more swiftly do you change. The face of the judge will be present, but see what the Prophet says: *Let us anticipate his face in confession*[^34]. *Grace is poured out on your lips, therefore God has blessed you forever.*

**11.** [v. 4] *Gird your sword about your thigh, most mighty one*[^35]. *Your sword,* what, except your word? With that sword he laid low the enemies, with that sword he divided son from father, daughter from mother, daughter-in-law from mother-in-law. We read these things in the Gospel: *I came not to send peace, but a sword. And there will be in one house five divided against one another, two against three, and three against two will be divided*: that is, *son against father, daughter against mother, daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law*[^36]. By what sword was this division made, except the one which Christ brought? And truly, brothers, even by daily examples we see these things. It pleases some young man to serve God, it displeases his father; they are divided against each other: the one promises an earthly inheritance, the other loves the heavenly; the one promises one thing, the other chooses another. Let not the father think an injury done him; God alone is preferred to him: and yet he quarrels with his son wishing to serve God. But that spiritual sword separating is stronger than carnal nature joining. This happens too with daughter against mother, much more even with daughter-in-law against mother-in-law. For sometimes in one house a daughter-in-law and a mother-in-law are found heretical and catholic. And where this sword is bravely received, we do not fear rebaptism. The daughter could be divided against her mother, and the daughter-in-law cannot against her mother-in-law?

**12.** This was done generally too in the human race, the son was divided against the father. For we were once sons of the devil. To the still unbelieving it was said: *You are of your father the devil*[^37]. And all our unbelief, whence, except from our father the devil? not by his creating, but by our imitating him. Now indeed you see the son divided against the father. That sword comes; he renounces the devil: he finds another father, he finds another mother. The one offering himself for imitation, begot unto destruction: the two parents we have found beget unto eternal life. The son was divided against the father. The daughter was divided against her mother: that people which believed from among the Jews, was divided against the Synagogue. The daughter-in-law too was divided against her mother-in-law: the people coming from the Gentiles is called the daughter-in-law; because the bridegroom Christ is the son of the Synagogue. For whence was the Son of God born according to the flesh? From that Synagogue. He who left father and mother, and clung to his wife, that they might be two in one flesh: not by our conjecture, but with the Apostle attesting, and saying: *This sacrament is great, but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^38]. For he left the father in a way: he did not leave him altogether as for separation, but for the taking up of human flesh. How did he leave? Because, though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant[^39]. How did he leave the mother too? The nation of the Jews, that Synagogue clinging to the old sacraments. To that figure pertains what he said: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?*[^40] For he was teaching within, they were standing outside. See whether the Jews are not now so. Christ teaches in the Church, they stand outside. What, then, is the mother-in-law? The mother of the bridegroom. The mother of the bridegroom our Lord Jesus Christ, is the Synagogue. Accordingly his daughter-in-law, the Church, which, coming from the Gentiles, did not consent to carnal circumcision, was divided against her mother-in-law. *Gird your sword.* About the power of this sword we were speaking, when we said these things.

**13.** *Gird your sword,* your discourse: *about your thigh, most mighty one,* having the sword about the thigh. What is, *about the thigh*? what does it signify by the thigh? The flesh. Whence is that: *There shall not fail a prince from Judah, and a leader from his thighs*[^41]. Did not Abraham himself too, to whom was promised the seed in which all nations should be blessed, when he sent his servant to seek and bring a wife for his son, whence should come that holy seed in which all nations should be blessed; holding by faith that in that, as it were, humility of seed was the greatness of the name, that is, that the Son of God would come from the sons of men through the seed of Abraham, make that very servant whom he was sending swear to him thus? *Put,* he said, *your hand under my thigh, and so swear*[^42]. As if he said: Put your hand to the altar, or to the Gospel, or to the Prophet, or to something holy. Under my thigh, he said, put your hand: having confidence, and not revering the unseemliness, but understanding the truth. Therefore: *Gird your sword about your thigh, most mighty one.* Most mighty one even about the thigh: because what is weak of God is stronger than men. *Most mighty one.*

**14.** [v. 5] *In your beauty and your comeliness*[^43]. Take it as justice, by which you are always beautiful and comely. *And aim, and prosperously advance, and reign.* Do we not see it? Surely it is now done. Attend to the world; he has aimed, prosperously advanced, and reigns; all nations are subjected. What was that to see in the spirit? What now it is to experience in truth. When these things were said, Christ did not yet so reign, had not yet aimed, had not yet advanced: they were foretold; they have been exhibited, now we hold them: in many things we have God the repayer, in few the debtor. *Aim, and prosperously advance, and reign.*

**15.** *On account of truth, and meekness, and justice*[^44]. Truth was rendered, when truth arose from the earth, and justice looked down from heaven[^45]. Christ was presented to the expectation of the human race, that in the seed of Abraham all nations should be blessed. The Gospel was preached; it is truth. What of meekness? The martyrs suffered, and thence much advanced, and the kingdom of God was promoted through all nations: because the martyrs suffered, and neither failed, nor resisted; saying all things, hiding nothing; ready for all things, refusing nothing. Great meekness! The body of Christ did this, it learned it in its head. He first, like a sheep, was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer did not open his mouth: so meek, that hanging on the cross he said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^46]. What on account of justice? He will come too, to judge, and to repay to each one according to his works. He spoke truth, endured iniquity, will bring equity. *And your right hand shall lead you wonderfully.* We are his right hand, he his own right hand. For he is God, we men. By his right hand he was led, that is, by his power. For the power which the Father has, he himself too has, and the immortality of the Father he himself too has; the divinity of the Father he has, the eternity of the Father he has, the virtue of the Father he has. His right hand shall lead him wonderfully, doing divine things, suffering human things, laying low the malices of men by his goodness. Still he is being led even where he is not yet, and his right hand leads him. For this leads him, which he himself granted to his saints. *Your right hand shall lead you wonderfully.*

**16.** [v. 6] *Your arrows are sharp, most mighty*[^47]: words piercing the heart, rousing love. Whence it is said in the Song of Songs: *For I am wounded with love*[^48]. For she says she is wounded with love, that is, she says she loves, she says she burns, sighs for the bridegroom, from whom she received the arrow of the word. *Your arrows are sharp, most mighty*: both piercing, and effective: *sharp, most mighty. The peoples shall fall under you.* Who fell? Those who were struck, and fell. We see the peoples subjected to Christ, falling we do not see. He expounds where they fall: *in the heart.* There they raised themselves against Christ, there they fall before Christ. Saul blasphemed Christ, he was raised up: he supplicates Christ, he fell, he was prostrated: the enemy of Christ was killed, that the disciple of Christ might live. From heaven the arrow was sent forth, Saul was struck in the heart, not yet Paul, still Saul, still raised up, not yet prostrated: he received the arrow, he fell in the heart. For not where he was prostrated in the face, there did he fall, but where he said: *Lord, what do you bid me do?*[^49] Just now you were going to bind the Christians and lead them to punishment; and now you say to Christ: *What do you bid me do?* O sharp arrow, most mighty, by receiving which Saul fell, that he might be Paul! As he, so the peoples too: attend to the nations, see them subjected to Christ. Therefore: *The peoples shall fall under you, in the heart of the king's enemies*: this is, in the heart of your enemies. For him he calls king, him he knows as king. *The peoples shall fall under you, in the heart of the king's enemies.* They were enemies: they received your arrows, they fell before you. From enemies they were made friends: the enemies died, the friends live. This is: *For the things that shall be changed.* We seek to understand the single words, the single verses: yet we so seek, that no one doubt it is said of Christ. *The peoples shall fall under you, in the heart of the king's enemies.*

**17.** [v. 7] *Your throne, O God, is forever and ever*[^50]. Because God has blessed you forever, on account of the grace poured out on your lips. Now there was a throne of the temporal Jewish kingdom, pertaining to those who were under the Law, not to those who were under Grace: he came to free those who were under the Law, and to set them under Grace. *His throne is forever and ever.* Why? Because that first throne was of the temporal kingdom. Whence now a throne forever and ever? Because it is God's. *Your throne, O God, is forever and ever.* O divinity of eternity! For God could not have a temporal throne. *Your throne, O God, is forever and ever. A rod of directing is the rod of your kingdom.* A rod of directing is that which directs men. They were crooked, they were distorted: they desired to reign for themselves, they loved themselves, they loved their own evil deeds; they did not subject their own will to God, but wished to bend the will of God to their lusts. For the sinner and iniquitous man is for the most part angry at God, because he does not rain; and he does not wish God to be angry at him, because he himself does flow. And to this almost men daily sit, to dispute against God: This he ought to have done, this he did not do well. You forsooth see what you do, and he does not know? You are distorted, he is straight. The distorted, when do you join it to the straight? It cannot be made to line up. As if you should set crooked wood on a level pavement; it is not joined, does not cohere, is not fitted to the pavement: the pavement is everywhere level; but that is crooked, it is not fitted to the level. Therefore the will of God is level, yours is crooked: for this reason it seems to you crooked, because you cannot be fitted to it: direct yourself to it, do not wish to bend it to yourself; because you cannot, you try in vain: it is always straight. Do you wish to cling to it? Be corrected. It will be the rod of him who rules you, a rod of directing. Hence a king (rex) too is named from ruling (regere). But he does not rule who does not correct (corrigere). For this our king is the king of the righteous. As a priest from sanctifying us, so a king from ruling us. But what does he say in another place? *With the holy you will be holy, and with the innocent man innocent, and with the elect elect, and with the perverse you will be perverse*[^51]: not because God is perverse, but because the perverse think him perverse. The good pleases you, God is good: it displeases you, God is as it were crooked. God is crooked to you, your own crookedness does this: for his rectitude always remains. Hear in another psalm: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright of heart!*[^52]

**18.** [v. 8] *A rod of directing is the rod of your kingdom. You have loved justice, and hated iniquity*[^53]. See the rod of directing: *You have loved justice, and hated iniquity.* Draw near to that rod, let Christ be king for you: let that rod rule you, lest it break you. For that rod is of iron, inflexible. And what was said? *You shall rule them in a rod of iron, and like a potter's vessel you shall break them*[^54]. Some he rules, others he breaks: he rules the spiritual, he breaks the carnal. Therefore draw near to that rod. What do you fear in it? This is the whole rod: *You have loved justice, and hated iniquity.* What do you fear? But perhaps you were iniquitous: for you hear your king, that he hates iniquity, and you fear. There is something you can do. What does he hate? Iniquity: surely not you? But is iniquity in you? God hates it, do you too hate it: that you both hate one thing. For you will be a friend of God, if you hate what he hates. So too you will love what he loves. Let your iniquity be displeasing to you in yourself, and let his creature be pleasing to you. For you are a man iniquitous. I have said two names; two names, man and iniquitous: in these two names one is of nature, the other of fault; one God made for you, the other you made: love what God made, hate what you made, because he himself too hates this. See how you now begin to be joined to him, when you hate what he hates. He is to punish sin, because a rod of directing is the rod of his kingdom. But will he not punish sin? But he cannot. Sin must be punished: if it were not to be punished, it would not be sin. Anticipate him: you do not wish that he himself punish, do you punish. For therefore he still spares, defers, holds his hand, bends the bow, that is, his threats. Would he cry out that he would strike only, if he wished to strike? He defers his hand, then, from your sins: do not you defer. Turn yourself to punishing your sins, because sins cannot be unpunished. It will have to be punished, then, either by you, or by him: do you acknowledge, that he may pardon. Attend to the example in that psalm of penitence: *Turn away your face from my sins*[^55]. Did he say: From me? For in another place he openly says: *Turn not away your face from me*[^56]. Therefore: *Turn away your face from my sins*: I do not wish you to see my sins. Because the seeing of God is to take notice. Hence even a judge, that which he punishes, is said to take notice (animadvertere), that is, to turn the mind there; to intend, of course, to punishing, because he is a judge. So God too is a judge. *Turn away your face from my sins.* Do not you turn away your face from them, if you wish God to turn his face from them. See how he offers this to God in that same psalm: *My misdeed I,* he says, *acknowledge, and my sin is before me always*[^57]. This he does not wish to be before God, which he wishes to be before himself. *A rod of directing is the rod of your kingdom.* Let no one flatter himself too much about the mercy of God; it is a rod of directing. Do we say that God is not merciful? What is more merciful than he, who spares all sinners, than he who, in all who turn to him, does not heed past things? So love him as merciful, that you wish him to be truthful: for mercy cannot take justice from him, nor justice mercy. Meanwhile, as long as he defers, do not you defer: since a rod of directing is the rod of his kingdom.

**19.** *You have loved justice, and hated iniquity: therefore God, your God, has anointed you*[^58]. Therefore he anointed you, that you might love justice, and hate iniquity. And see how he says: *Therefore God, your God, has anointed you.* O you God, your God anointed you. God is anointed by God. For in Latin the same case of the noun is thought to be repeated: but in Greek there is a most evident distinction, because one is the noun of the one addressed, and the other of the one who addresses: *He anointed you, O God.* O you *God, your God anointed you*: as if he said: Therefore your God anointed you, O you God. So take it, so understand it, so in Greek it is most evident. Who, then, is this God anointed by God? Let the Jews tell us. These Scriptures are common. God was anointed by God: you hear "anointed," understand Christ. For Christ is from "chrism": this name by which he is called Christ, is of anointing. Nor were kings and priests anointed anywhere else, except in that kingdom where Christ was prophesied and anointed, and whence the name of Christ was to come: it is nowhere else at all, in no nation, in no kingdom. God, then, was anointed by God: with what oil, except spiritual? For the visible oil is in the sign, the invisible oil is in the sacrament, the spiritual oil is within. God was anointed for us, and sent for us: and he himself was God, that he might be anointed, he was man: but he was man in such a way that he was God; he was God in such a way that he did not disdain to be man: true man, true God; in nothing deceitful, in nothing false; because everywhere truthful, everywhere truth. God, then, a man, and therefore God anointed, because the God-man, and he was made Christ.

**20.** This was figured in that Jacob set a stone for himself at his head, and slept. The patriarch Jacob set a stone for himself at his head; and sleeping with that stone set at his head, he saw, the heavens opened, a ladder from heaven to earth, and angels ascending and descending: at this sight he awoke, anointed the stone, and departed. In that stone he understood Christ, therefore he anointed it. See from when Christ is foretold. What does that anointing of a stone mean, especially among the patriarchs, who worshiped one God? But it was done in a figure, and they departed. For he did not anoint the stone, and come there always to adore, and to make sacrifices there. The mystery was expressed, sacrilege not begun. And see the stone: *The stone which the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner*[^59]. And because the head of the man is Christ[^60], therefore the stone at the head. Attend to a great sacrament: the stone Christ. *A living stone,* says Peter, *rejected by men, but chosen by God*[^61]. And the stone at the head, because the head of the man is Christ. And the stone anointed, because from "chrism" Christ is named. And the ladder is seen, Christ revealing it, from earth to heaven, or from heaven to earth, and angels ascending and descending. What this is, we shall see better, when we have recalled the gospel testimony from the Lord himself. You know that Jacob himself is Israel. For when he wrestled with the angel and prevailed, and was blessed by him over whom he prevailed, his name was changed, so that he was called Israel: just as the people Israel prevailed over Christ, so as to crucify him; and yet in those who believed in Christ, he was blessed by the very one over whom he prevailed. But many did not believe, hence the lameness of Jacob. A blessing and a lameness. A blessing in those who believed: for afterward we know that many from that people believed. But a lameness in those who did not believe. And because more did not believe, and few believed; therefore, that the lameness might be made, he touched the breadth of his thigh. What is the breadth of his thigh? The multitude of the race. See, then, that ladder: the Lord, when he saw Nathanael in the Gospel, said: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^62]. For so it is said of Jacob himself: *And Jacob was without guile, dwelling at home*[^63]: recalling this, the Lord, seeing Nathanael without guile from that nation and from that people: *Behold a true Israelite,* he said, *in whom there is no guile.* He called him an Israelite, in whom there was no guile, on account of Jacob. And that Nathanael: *Whence do you know me?* And the Lord: *When you were under the fig tree I saw you*: that is, when you were in that people set under the Law, which protected that people with a carnal shadow, there I saw you. What is, there I saw you? There I had mercy on you. But he, recalling that he had been even under the fig tree in truth, marveling, because he thought himself seen by no one when he was there, confesses and says: *You are the Son of God, you are the king of Israel.* Who said this? He who had heard that he was a true Israelite, and there was no guile in him. And the Lord: *Because I said to you: I saw you under the fig tree, you have believed; you shall see greater things than these*[^64]. He speaks with Israel, with Jacob, with him who had set a stone for himself at his head. *You shall see greater things than these.* What greater things? Because now that stone is at the head. *Amen I say to you, you shall see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending over the Son of man*[^65]. Let the angels of God ascend and descend by that ladder, let this be done in the Church, the angels of God are announcers of the truth: let them ascend and see: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* Let them descend, and see, that *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^66]. Let them ascend, that they may raise the great: let them descend, that they may nourish the little. See Paul ascending: *Whether we have been in ecstasy, it is to God.* See him descending: *Whether we are sober, it is for you*[^67]. See him ascending: *We speak wisdom among the perfect.* See him descending: *Milk I gave you to drink, not food*[^68]. This is done in the Church: the angels of God ascend and descend over the Son of man: because the Son of man is above, to whom they ascend with the heart, that is, his head; and below is the Son of man, that is, his body. His members are here, the head is above: there is ascending to the head, descending to the members. Christ there, Christ here. For if there only, and not here; whence that voice: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^69] For who was troublesome to him in heaven? No one, neither the Jews, nor Saul, nor the tempting devil; no one there troublesome to him: but just as in the framework of the human body, when the foot is trodden on, the tongue cries out.

**21.** *You have loved justice, and hated iniquity: therefore God, your God, has anointed you*[^70]. We have spoken of the anointed God, that is, of Christ. The name of Christ could not be said more openly, than that the anointed God should be said. As *beautiful in form beyond the sons of men;* so anointed, *with the oil of gladness beyond his fellows.* For who are his fellows? The sons of men: since he himself, the Son of man, became a partaker of their mortality, that he might make them partakers of his immortality.

**22.** [vv. 9-10] *Myrrh and aloes and cassia from your garments*[^71]. Good odors from your garments. His garments are his saints, his elect, his whole Church, which he displays to himself like a garment, without spot and wrinkle: on account of the spot, washing in his blood; on account of the wrinkle, stretching on the cross. Hence the good odor which is signified by certain named spices. Hear that least one, Paul, the fringe of the garment which the woman in the flux of blood touched, and was healed; hear him saying: *We are the good odor of Christ in every place, both among those who are being saved, and among those who are perishing*[^72]. He did not say: A good odor among those who are being saved, and an evil odor among those who are perishing; but: As far as pertains to us, *we are a good odor, both among those who are being saved, and among those who are perishing.* That a man be saved by a good odor is neither improbable nor incredible: but that a man perish by a good odor, what is the reason? A great force, a great truth: even if it cannot be grasped, it is so. For that you may know it is grasped with difficulty, he at once added: *And for these who is fit?* Who understands that men die by a good odor? Yet I will say something, brothers. Behold Paul himself preached the Gospel; many loved him, the preacher of the Gospel, many envied him: those who loved him were saved by the good odor; those who envied him perished by the good odor. Therefore even to those perishing it is not an evil odor, but a good odor. For they envied him the more, because so good a grace prevailed in him: for no one envies a wretched man. He was glorious, then, in the preaching of the word of God, and living according to the rule of that rod of directing: and those loved him who loved Christ in him, who followed the good odor; the bride herself loved the friend of her bridegroom, she who says in the Song of Songs: *After the odor of your ointments we will run*[^73]. But those, the more they saw him in the glory of preaching the Gospel and in a blameless life, the more they were tormented with envy, and were killed by the good odor.

**23.** [v. 10] *Myrrh and aloes and cassia from your garments, from the ivory houses, from which the daughters of kings have delighted you*[^74]. The ivory houses, the great houses, the royal houses, whichever you choose, from there the daughters of kings have delighted Christ. Do you wish to take the ivory houses spiritually? Take the great houses, and great tabernacles of God, the hearts of the saints, and those very kings ruling the flesh, subjugating to themselves the crowds of human affections, chastising the body, and bringing it into servitude: because from there the daughters of kings have delighted him. For all the souls which were born when those preached and evangelized, are daughters of kings: and the churches, the daughters of the apostles, are daughters of kings. For he is the *King of kings*[^75]: but those are kings of whom it was said: *You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^76]. They preached the word of truth, and begot churches, not for themselves, but for him. To this sacrament pertains what is written in the Law: *If a brother die, let his brother take his wife, and raise up seed for his brother*[^77]. *Let his brother take the wife, and raise up seed,* not for himself, but *for his brother.* Christ said: *Tell my brothers*[^78]. He said in the psalm: *I will declare your name to my brothers*[^79]. Christ died, rose again, ascended, made himself absent in body: his brothers took up his wife, to beget sons by the preaching of the Gospel, not through themselves, but through the Gospel, on account of the name of the brother. *For in Christ Jesus,* he says, *through the Gospel, I begot you*[^80]. And so, raising up seed for their brother, however many they begot, they named not Paulians or Petrians, but Christians. See whether this sense is not awake even in these verses. For when he said, *from the ivory houses,* he said of the royal, ample, beautiful, smooth ones, such as are the hearts of the saints: he added: *From which the daughters of kings have delighted you, in your honor. The daughters* indeed *of kings,* the daughters of your apostles: but, *in your honor,* because they raised up seed for their brother. Therefore those very ones whom Paul had raised up for his brother, when he saw them running to his own name, he cried out: *Was Paul crucified for you?* For what does the Law say? And let the one born have the name of the dead. To the dead let him be born, by the name of the dead let him be called. Paul keeps this law: those wishing to be called by his name, he calls back: *Was Paul crucified for you?* Look to the dead: *Was Paul crucified for you?* And what then? When you begot them, what if you had imposed your name on them? No. For he says: *Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^81] *The daughters of kings have delighted you in your honor.* Hold, keep, *in your honor.* This is to have the wedding garment, to seek his honor, his glory. Understand also the daughters of kings to be cities which believed in Christ, and were founded by kings: and *from the ivory houses,* the rich, the proud, the lofty. *The daughters of kings have delighted you in your honor*: because they did not seek the honor of their fathers, but sought your honor. Let there be shown me at Rome a temple of Romulus in so great honor, as I there show the memorial of Peter. In Peter who is honored, except he who died for us? For we are Christians, not Petrians. Even if born through the dead brother, yet surnamed by the name of the dead. Through him born, but for him born. Behold Rome, behold Carthage, behold other and other cities are daughters of kings; and they have delighted their king in his honor; and from all is made a certain one queen.

**24.** What a wedding song? Behold, amid songs full of cheerfulness, she too, the bride, comes forth. For the bridegroom was coming, he was being described, on him was all our attention: let her too come forth. *The queen stood at your right hand*[^82]. She who is at the left, is not a queen. For there will stand a certain one at the left too, to whom it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire.* But at the right will stand she to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^83]. *The queen stood at your right hand, in a gilded vestment, surrounded with variety.* What is the vestment of this queen? It is both precious, and varied: the sacraments of doctrine in all the various tongues. One tongue African, another Syrian, another Greek, another Hebrew, another this and that: these tongues make the variety of the vestment of this queen. But as all the variety of the vestment agrees in unity, so too all the tongues to one faith. In the vestment let there be variety, let there be no rending. Behold, we have understood the variety from the diversity of tongues, and we have understood the vestment on account of the unity: but in that very variety what is the gold? Wisdom itself. Whatever the variety of tongues, one gold is preached: not different gold, but variety from gold. For the same wisdom, the same doctrine and discipline all the tongues preach. Variety in the tongues, gold in the sentences.

**25.** [v. 11] The Prophet addresses this queen (for he gladly sings to her), and each one of us; if at least we know where we are, and try to belong to that body, and by faith and hope belong to it, united in the members of Christ. For he addresses us: *Hear, daughter, and see*[^84]. He addresses her as one of the fathers, because they are daughters of kings: whether a prophet addresses, whether an apostle addresses, as a daughter (for so we say, Our fathers the prophets, our fathers the apostles: if we are to them as fathers, they to us as sons), and one paternal voice addresses the one daughter. *Hear, daughter, and see.* First hear, afterward see. For it came to us with the Gospel, and there was preached to us what we do not yet see, and by hearing we believed, by believing we shall see; as the bridegroom himself says through the Prophet: *A people whom I did not know, served me; in the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^85]. What is, *in the hearing of the ear*? Because it did not see. The Jews saw, and crucified: the Gentiles did not see, and believed. Let the queen come from the Gentiles in a gilded vestment, surrounded with variety: let her come from the Gentiles, let her come surrounded with all tongues, in the unity of wisdom; let it be said to her: *Hear, daughter, and see.* If you will not hear, you will not see. Hear that you may cleanse the heart by faith, as the Apostle says in the Acts of the Apostles: *By faith cleansing their hearts*[^86]. For for this we hear, that we may believe before we see, that by believing we may cleanse the heart, whence we may be able to see. Hear, that you may believe, cleanse the heart by faith. And when I have cleansed the heart, what shall I see? Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God[^87]. *Hear, daughter, and see; and incline your ear.* It is too little to hear, hear humbly: *Incline your ear. And forget your people and the house of your father.* There was a certain people, and a certain house of your father, in which you were born, the people of Babylon, having the devil as king. From wherever the Gentiles came, they came from the father the devil: but they renounced the father the devil. *Forget your people and the house of your father.* He begot you foul, when he made you a sinner: he regenerates you beautiful who justifies the ungodly woman. *Forget your people, and the house of your father.*

**26.** [v. 12] *Because the king has desired your beauty*[^88]. What beauty, except that which he himself made? *He has desired the beauty.* Whose beauty? Of the sinner, the iniquitous, the ungodly, such as she was with her father the devil, and with her own people? No, but that of which it is said: *Who is this that comes up made white?*[^89] Therefore before she was not white, afterward made white. Because if your sins are like scarlet, I will make them white as snow[^90]. *The king has desired your beauty.* What king? *Because he himself is your God.* Now see whether you ought not to forsake that father of yours, and that people of yours, and come to this king, your God: he is your God, he is your king. Your king, and he himself is your bridegroom. You marry a king, God, dowered by him, adorned by him, redeemed by him, healed by him. Whatever you have whereby you may please him, you have from him.

**27.** [v. 13] *And the daughters of Tyre will adore him with gifts*[^91]. Your very king, your God, *the daughters of Tyre will adore with gifts. The daughters of Tyre,* the daughters of the Gentiles: from a part to the whole. Tyre, neighboring this land where the prophecy was, signified the Gentiles who would believe in Christ. From there was that Canaanite woman, who first was called a dog: for that you may know she was from there, the Gospel speaks thus: *He withdrew to the parts of Tyre and Sidon, and behold a Canaanite woman, coming out from those borders, cried out,* and the rest which is there narrated. She who first was a dog with her father and among her people, by crying and coming to this king, made comely by believing in him, what did she deserve to hear? *O woman, great is your faith*[^92]. *The king has desired your beauty. And the daughters of Tyre will adore him with gifts.* With what gifts? So this king wishes to be come to, and wishes his treasures to be filled: and he himself granted whence they may be filled, and by you let them be filled. Let them come, he says, let them adore him with gifts. What is, with gifts? *Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy, and where thieves dig through and steal: but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither thief nor moth corrupts. For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also*[^93]. Come with gifts: *Give alms, and all things are clean to you*[^94]. Come with gifts to him who says: *I desire mercy rather than sacrifice*[^95]. To that temple which was before a shadow of the future, there was coming with bulls and rams, with goats, with various animals fit for sacrifice, that in that blood one thing might be done, another signified. Now already the blood itself, which all those things figured, has come: the King himself has come, and he himself wishes gifts. What gifts? Alms. For he himself will judge, and he himself will impute the gifts to certain ones. *Come,* he says, *blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* Why? *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was naked, and you clothed me; a stranger, and you took me in; sick and in prison, and you visited me.* These are the gifts with which the daughters of Tyre adore the king: because when they said, *When did we see you?* he who is both above and below, on account of those ascending and descending: *When you did it,* he says, *to one of these least of mine, you did it to me*[^96].

**28.** *The daughters of Tyre will adore him with gifts*[^97]. And who the daughters of Tyre are, and how they will adore him with gifts, he wished to say more plainly: *The rich of the people will entreat your countenance.* These daughters of Tyre adoring with gifts, are the rich of the people, whom that friend of the bridegroom addresses: *Charge the rich of this world not to be high-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches; but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy: let them be rich in good works, let them give easily, let them share.* Let them adore with gifts; but they do not lose them: secure let them put where they may always find them. *Let them treasure up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold on true life*[^98]. By adoring with gifts, *they will entreat your countenance.* For they run together to the Church, and there give alms. Lest they be outside, that is, lest, being set outside, they be made so, let them do it in the Church. For the countenance of this bride and queen will profit those who do it. Therefore those who sold their goods, entreating the countenance of this queen with gifts, came; and the things they carried, they set at the feet of the apostles. Love was fervent in the Church, the countenance of the queen was the Church, the countenance of the queen was the homage of the daughters of Tyre, that is, of the rich adoring with gifts. *The rich of the people will entreat your countenance.* And those who will entreat the countenance, and whose countenance they will entreat, all one bride, all one queen, mother and sons together, the whole pertaining to Christ, pertaining to the head.

**29.** [vv. 14-15] But because these works and these alms are done for the boasting of men, hence the Lord himself says: *Beware of doing your justice before men to be seen by them*[^99]. But how they ought to be done even publicly, on account of the countenance of the bride, he says: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^100]: not that you may seek your own glory in the good works which you do publicly, but the glory of God. And who knows, he says, whether I seek the glory of God, or my own glory? Because I give to the poor, I am seen: with what mind I give, who sees? Let it suffice you that he sees; he sees who will repay. Within he loves who within sees: within he loves, within let him be loved who makes the inner man and that very beauty. Do not, as it were, be delighted with the outer eyes, because you are seen, and because you are praised; attend to what here follows: *All the glory of her, the king's daughter, is within*[^101]. Outwardly there is not only the gilded and varied vestment, but within he who loved her beauty knows her beautiful. What are the inner things of beauty? Of the conscience. There Christ sees, there Christ loves, there Christ addresses, there Christ punishes, there Christ crowns. Let your almsgiving, then, be in secret; because *all the glory of her, the king's daughter, is within. In golden fringes, surrounded with variety.* The beauty is within: but in the golden fringes the variety of tongues, the ornament of doctrine. What do these profit, if there is not that beauty within?

**30.** *Virgins shall be brought to the king after her*[^102]. Truly it has come about. The Church believed, the Church was made throughout all nations. Now how do virgins desire to please that king? Whence are they incited? Because the Church went before. *Virgins shall be brought to the king after her: those near her shall be brought to you.* For those who are brought are not strangers, but those near her, pertaining to her. And because he said, *to the king;* turned to him he said, *to you: those near her shall be brought to you.*

**31.** [v. 16] *They shall be brought in gladness and exultation, they shall be led into the temple of the king*[^103]. The temple of the king is the Church herself, the Church herself enters into the temple. Whence is the temple built? Of men who enter into the temple. What are the living stones, except the faithful of God? *They shall be led into the temple of the king.* For there are virgins outside the temple of the king, heretical nuns: they are indeed virgins, but what will it profit them unless they be led into the temple of the king? The temple of the king is in unity: the temple of the king is not ruinous, not torn apart, not divided. The joining of the living stones is love. *They shall be led into the temple of the king.*

**32.** [v. 17] *For your fathers sons have been born to you*[^104]. Nothing more evident. Attend now to the temple of the king itself, because hence he speaks on account of the unity spread throughout the whole world: because those who wished to be virgins, unless they be led into the temple of the king, cannot please the bridegroom. *For your fathers sons have been born to you.* The apostles begot you: they were sent, they preached, they are fathers. But could they always be with us bodily? Even though one of them said: *I desire to be dissolved, and to be with Christ is much better; to remain in the flesh is necessary on your account*[^105]. He said this indeed, but how long could he remain here? Even to this time? even to a later time? Was the Church, then, deserted by their departure? Far be it. *For your fathers sons have been born to you.* What is, *For your fathers sons have been born to you*? The fathers were the apostles sent; for the apostles sons have been born to you, bishops have been appointed. For today the bishops, who are throughout the whole world, whence are they born? The Church herself calls them fathers, she herself begot them, and she herself set them in the seats of the fathers. Do not, then, think yourself deserted, because you do not see Peter, because you do not see Paul, because you do not see those through whom you were born: from your own offspring fatherhood has grown for you. *For your fathers sons have been born to you: you will appoint them princes over all the earth.* See the temple of the king, how widely spread: that those virgins who are not led into the temple of the king may know that they do not pertain to these nuptials. *For your fathers sons have been born to you: you will appoint them princes over all the earth.* This is the Catholic Church: her sons are appointed princes over all the earth, her sons are appointed in place of the fathers. Let those recognize who have been cut off, let them come to unity, let them be led into the temple of the king. God set his temple everywhere, he firmly fixed everywhere the foundations of the prophets and apostles. The Church begot sons, she appointed them in place of her fathers princes over all the earth.

**33.** [v. 18] *They will be mindful of your name in every generation and generation. Therefore the peoples will confess to you*[^106]. What, then, does it profit to confess, and to confess outside the temple? What does it profit to pray, and not to pray on the mountain? *With my voice,* he says, *I cried to the Lord, and he heard me from his holy mountain*[^107]. From what mountain? Of which it was said: *A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden*[^108]. From what mountain? Which Daniel saw grown from a small stone, and break all the kingdoms of the lands, and fill the whole face of the earth. There let him adore who wishes to receive, there let him ask who wishes to be heard, there let him confess who wishes to be pardoned. *Therefore the peoples will confess to you forever, and unto ages of ages.* Because even in that eternal life there will indeed no longer be the groaning of sins, yet in the divine praises of that supernal and perpetual city there will not be lacking an everlasting confession of so great a felicity. For to that very city, to which another psalm sings: *Glorious things have been said of you, O city of God*[^109]; to the bride herself of Christ, to the queen herself, the daughter of the king, and spouse of the king; because her princes are mindful of her name in every generation and generation, that is, as long as this world, which is carried on through many generations, passes, bearing the care of love for her, that, freed from this world, she may reign together with God forever; on account of this, to her the peoples will confess forever, with the hearts of all there conspicuous and manifest, luminous with perfect love, so that she may know herself wholly most fully, who here in her many parts is hidden from herself. Whence we are admonished by the Apostle to judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, and illumine the hidden things of darkness, and make manifest the thoughts of the heart, and there be praise to each one from God[^110]. For that holy city itself will in a way confess to itself, when its peoples of which it consists will confess to the city itself forever; so that it may be hidden from itself in no part, there being in it no one of whom anything is hidden.
