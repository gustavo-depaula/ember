# Exposition of Psalm 120

**1.** This is the second of those psalms whose title is *A Song of Ascents.* For there are several signifying, as you have already heard in the first of them, our ascent, which is made in the heart to God, from the valley of weeping, that is, from the humility of affliction. For an ascent cannot be useful to us, unless, first humbled, we remember that we must ascend from a valley (for a valley is a low place of earth: as the high places of earth are mountains and hills, so a valley is a low place); lest, while too hastily and in reverse order we seek to be exalted, we do not ascend, but fall. But the Lord himself taught that one must ascend from the valley of weeping, when for us he deigned to be humbled even to the death of the cross, and to suffer. Let us not abandon this example: this valley of weeping the martyrs understood. Whence did they understand it? Whence? Because they too ascended from the valley of weeping, that they might be crowned.

**2.** This psalm, *A Song of Ascents,* agrees also with today: for of them it was said, *Going they went and wept, casting their seeds*[^1]. This is the valley of weeping, where the seeds are cast by those who weep. But what are the seeds? Good works in this earthly tribulation. He who works well in the valley of weeping is like a man sowing through winter. Is he deterred from the work by the cold? So by the pressures of the world we ought not to be deterred from a good work; for you see what follows: *Going,* he says, *they went and wept, casting their seeds.* Very wretched, if they always wept; very wretched, if they were never to be freed from tears. But see what follows: *But coming they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^2].

**3.** By nothing else are we taught in these songs, brethren, than to ascend; but to ascend in the heart, in good affection, in faith, hope, and love, in desire of perpetuity and eternal life. So one ascends. It is fitting that we say how one must ascend. How much more terrible things, read from the Gospel, has your Charity heard! You certainly see *that the hour of the Lord, like a thief in the night, so will come. If the householder knew,* he says, *at what hour the thief would come, amen I say to you, that he would not let his wall be broken through*[^3]. You say now: Who then knows at what hour he will come, since the hour will be like a thief? You do not know at what hour he comes; always keep watch; that, because you do not know when he comes, he may find you ready when he comes. And for this reason perhaps you do not know when he is to come, that you may always be ready. For that hour will suddenly seize that householder who is signified as the proud man. Do not wish to be a householder, and that hour will not suddenly seize you. And what shall I be, you say? Such as you have heard from the psalm: *I am poor and in sorrow*[^4]. For if you are poor and in sorrow, you will not be a householder, whom that hour will suddenly find, and suddenly press. For householders are those who, presuming on their cupidities, and dissolving in the delights of this age, swell up; and are raised against the humble, and insult the saints who understand the narrow way that leads to life. For to such will that hour come suddenly; because such were they too in the days of Noah, of which days you have heard the mention made in the Gospel. *So will the coming of the Son of man be,* he says, *as in the days of Noah. They ate, drank, married, took wives, planted vineyards, built, until Noah entered the ark; and the flood came, and destroyed them all*[^5]. What then? Will all perish who do these things, who marry, who take wives, who plant vineyards, who build? No: but those who presume on these things, but those who prefer these things to God, but those who for the sake of these are quickly ready to offend God. But those who either do not use all these things, or use them as though not using[^6], presuming more on him who gave than on the things that were given, and understanding in them his consolation and mercy, and not occupied with the gifts, lest they fall away from the giver; such men that hour, like a thief, will not find unprepared. To such the Apostle said: *But you are not in darkness, that that day should overtake you like a thief; for you are all sons of light, and sons of the day*[^7]. Therefore the Lord, when he said that the hour, like a thief, was to be feared, set down "night"; and the Apostle says thus: That the hour of the Lord, like a thief in the night, so will come. Do you not wish it to find you? Do not be in the night. What is, Do not be in the night? *You are sons of light and sons of the day; we are not of the night nor of darkness*[^8]. Who are the sons of the night and of darkness? The unjust, the ungodly, the unbelieving.

**4.** [v. 1.] But let these too hear before the hour comes, and let the Apostle say to them: *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^9]. As this psalm has it, let them awake. Now the mountains are enlightened; why do they still sleep? Let them lift their *eyes to the mountains, whence help will come* to them. What is, Now the mountains are enlightened? Now the sun of righteousness has risen, now the Gospel has been preached by the Apostles, the Scriptures have been preached, all the mysteries have lain open, the veil has been torn, the secret of the temple has lain open: now at last let them lift their eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to them. For this the psalm enjoins, the second of those inscribed *A Song of Ascents.* But again let them not presume on the mountains; because the mountains themselves do not shine of themselves, but shine from him of whom it was said, *He was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^10]. The mountains can be understood as great men, illustrious men. And who is greater than John the Baptist? What a mountain he was, of whom the Lord himself says, *Among those born of women none has arisen greater than John the Baptist*[^11]? You certainly see this great mountain shining; hear him confessing. Confessing what? *We all,* he says, *have received of his fullness*[^12]. Of whose fullness, then, the mountains received, from him is help for you, not from the mountains; to which, however, unless you lift your eyes through the Scriptures, you will not be brought near; that you may be enlightened by him.

**5.** [v. 3.] Sing, then, what follows: if you wish to hear how to set your feet most firmly on the steps, lest in that ascent you either be wearied, or, slipping, fall, say what follows: *Do not give my foot to be moved.* Whence are feet moved? Whence the foot was moved for him who was in paradise. But first attend whence the foot was moved for him who was among the Angels, and, his foot being moved, fell, and from an angel was made a devil: for, his foot being moved, he fell. Ask whence he fell: by pride he fell. Nothing, then, moves the foot except pride: nothing moves the foot unto ruin except pride. Love moves it to walk and advance and ascend; pride moves it to fall. Therefore what too does that one say in the psalm? *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^13]. If under the covering, always humble, always hoping in God, always not presuming on themselves. *Under the covering of your wings they will hope:* for they are not satisfied of themselves so as to be blessed. But what follows? *They will be inebriated from the abundance of your house, and with the torrent of your delights you will give them to drink*[^14]. Behold those thirsting, behold them inebriated; behold they thirst, behold they drink: but they do not drink of themselves, nor are they themselves the fountain for themselves. But whence do they drink? *Under the covering of your wings they will hope.* If under the wings, they are humble. Why? *Because with you,* he says, *is the fountain of life*[^15]. The mountains, then, are not watered of themselves, just as the mountains are not enlightened of themselves. For see what follows: *In your light we will see light*[^16]. If, then, in his light we will see light, who falls from the light, except one for whom he himself is not light? And whoever wishes to be light for himself, he himself falls from the light by which he is enlightened. Therefore, knowing that none falls except him who wishes to shine for himself, when he is in himself darkness; he immediately added: *Let not the foot of pride come to me, and let not the hand of sinners move me*[^17]: that is, let not the imitation of sinners move me, that I fall from you. Why did you fear, and say, *Let not the foot of pride come to me?* There follows: *There they fell, all who work iniquity*[^18]. Those whom you now see working iniquity have already been condemned: but that they might be condemned, they fell there, when the foot of pride came to them. Rightly, then, this man, hearing that he should ascend and not fall, that he should advance from the valley of weeping, not fail in the swelling of pride, says to God: *Do not give my foot to be moved.* And God to him: *Nor let him slumber who keeps you.* Let your Charity attend. As it were one sentence has been made out of two voices. The man said, ascending and singing the Song of Ascents, *Do not give my foot to be moved;* and God, as if he said: You say to me, *Do not give my foot to be moved;* add, *Nor let him slumber who keeps you,* and your foot will not be moved.

**6.** [v. 4.] But he would say: Is it in my power that he not slumber who keeps me? I wish that he not sleep, nor slumber. Therefore choose for yourself the one who will not sleep, nor slumber, and your foot will not be moved. God is never sleeping: if you wish to have a keeper who does not sleep, choose God as your keeper. *Do not give my foot to be moved,* you say; well, excellently: but he too says to you, *Nor let him slumber who keeps you.* You perhaps were going to turn yourself to human keepers, and to say: Whom shall I find not sleeping? What man will not slumber? Whom do I find? Where shall I go? Whither shall I turn? This man shows you: *Behold, he will not slumber, nor sleep, who keeps Israel.* Do you wish, then, to have a keeper who neither sleeps nor slumbers? *Behold, he will not sleep, nor slumber, who keeps Israel:* for Christ keeps Israel. Be, then, Israel. What is Israel? Israel is interpreted "Seeing God." And how is God seen? First through faith, afterward through sight. If you cannot yet through sight, see through faith. If you cannot see his face, since there is sight there, see his back parts. This was said to Moses by the Lord: *You cannot see my face; you will see my back parts, when I have passed by*[^19]. You perhaps await that he pass by: he has already passed; do you see his back parts. Where did he pass? Hear John: When the hour had come, he says, *that he should pass from this world to the Father*[^20]. Our Lord Jesus Christ has now made the Pasch. But Pasch is interpreted "Passing-over." For it is a Hebrew word: and men think it is Greek, as though Passion, but it is not. By the more diligent and the more learned it has been found that Pasch is a Hebrew word; and they interpret it not as passion, but as passing-over. For through the passion the Lord passed over from death to life; and made for us, believing in his resurrection, a way, that we too might pass over from death to life. It is no great thing to believe that Christ died: this even pagans, and Jews, and all the unjust believe. This all believe, that he died: the faith of Christians is the resurrection of Christ; this we hold a great thing, that we believe he rose again. Therefore he then wished himself to be seen, when he passed over, that is, when he had risen again. Then he wished to be believed in himself, when he passed over; because he was handed over for our offenses, and rose again for our justification[^21]. And the Apostle especially commended this very faith in the resurrection of Christ: for he says, *If you believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved*[^22]. He did not say: If you believe that Christ died, which even pagans, and Jews, and all his enemies believed: but he says, *If you believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.* To believe this, this is to be Israel, this is to see God: although still his back parts, yet, when you have believed in the back parts, you will arrive at the vision of the face. What is this? When you believe in that which Christ was afterward made for you; when you believe in that which Christ afterward took up. For in the beginning, what is his face? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* What are his back parts? *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^23]. When, then, you believe in this, that the Word was made for you, and rose again in the flesh, lest you despair of your own flesh, you are made Israel. But when you have been made Israel, he will not slumber, nor sleep, who keeps you; because you are now Israel, and you have heard in the psalm: *Behold, he will not slumber, nor sleep, who keeps Israel.* For Christ himself slept, but rose again. For what does he himself say in the psalm? *I slept, and took sleep*[^24]. Did he remain in sleep? *I rose up,* he says, *because the Lord will uphold me.* If, then, he has now risen again, he has now passed over; if he has now passed over, see his back parts. What is, See his back parts? Believe in his resurrection. And because the Apostle says, *And though he was crucified out of weakness, yet he lives by the power of God*[^25]; and again says, *Christ rising from the dead now dies no more, and death will no more have dominion over him*[^26]: rightly it sings to you, *Behold, he will not slumber, nor sleep, who keeps Israel.* You perhaps still ask, with a carnal sense: Who is it that *will not slumber, nor sleep?* And when you seek among men, you are deceived; you will never find. Do not, then, presume on any man: every man sleeps, and will slumber. When does he slumber? When he bears the flesh of weakness. When will he sleep? When he has died. Do not, then, presume on a man. A mortal can slumber: one dying sleeps. Do not seek among men.

**7.** [v. 5.] And who, you say, will keep me, who does not slumber, nor will sleep? Hear what follows: *The Lord keeps you.* Not, then, a man slumbering and sleeping, but the Lord keeps you. How does he keep you? *The Lord is your covering over the hand of your right.* Come, brethren, let us understand, with the Lord's help, what it means: *The Lord is your covering over the hand of your right.* For it seems to me to have a hidden sense, why he did not say, *The Lord keeps you,* absolutely and simply; but added, *over the hand of your right.* For what? Does God keep our right hand, and not keep the left? Did he not make us whole? Did not he who made our right hand also make the left? Finally, if it pleased to say it of the right hand alone, why did he say, *over the hand of your right,* and not simply, over your right hand? Why would he say this, unless he hid here something hidden, to which, by knocking, we might arrive? For he would say either, *The Lord keeps you,* and add nothing; or, if he wished to add "right hand," The Lord keeps you over your right hand; or surely, because he added *hand,* he would say this, The Lord keeps you over your right hand, not, *over the hand of your right.* What the Lord himself deigns to suggest, I will bring forth to you; he who dwells in you, without doubt, makes you approve true to be what I say. For what we are going to say you do not know; but when we have said it, we will not show it true to you from ourselves, but you yourselves will recognize true to be what we say. But whence will you recognize it, except by him showing it who dwells in you, insofar as you are of that number whose voice is, *Do not give my foot to be moved;* and to whom it is said, *Nor let him slumber who keeps you?* For it is necessary that Christ not sleep in you, and you will straightway understand true to be what we say. How, you say? Because if your faith sleeps, Christ sleeps in you. For Christ in your heart is the faith of Christ. The Apostle says: *That Christ may dwell through faith in your hearts*[^27]. In whom faith does not sleep, Christ keeps watch. And if perhaps your faith was sleeping, and therefore you were tossing in this question, like that ship which was suffering a storm, where Christ was sleeping; rouse Christ, and the storms will be stilled[^28].

**8.** Your faith, then, I ask, dearest ones: since you are sons of the Church, and have advanced in the Church, and will advance in the Church, you who have not yet advanced, and you who have already advanced are to advance in the Church, I ask you what you are accustomed to understand by what was said in the Gospel: *Let not your left hand know what your right hand does*[^29]. For when this is understood, you will find what is the right hand, and what is the left: at the same time you will understand both that God made both parts, both the right and the left; and yet the left ought not to know what the right does. Our left is called whatever we have temporally: our right is called whatever the Lord promises us as eternal and unchangeable. But if he who will give eternal life himself also consoles the present life from these temporal things, he made both right and left. The psalm in David says of certain men: *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity*[^30]. He found, then, certain men whom he blames, who held the true right hand for the left, and made the true left their right hand: who these are he expounds in what follows. Every man who thinks that there is no happiness of man except only in these temporal resources and delights, and the affluence and abundance of this world, he is foolish and perverse, who makes the left his right hand: such were those of whom the psalm speaks; not because they had not received from God even what they possessed temporally, but because they thought that alone was the blessed life, and sought nothing else. For hear what he says of them in what follows: *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity.* And there follows: *Whose sons are like new plantings established; their daughters adorned like the likeness of a temple: their storerooms full, overflowing from this to that; their sheep fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth; their oxen fat: there is no falling of the hedge, nor exit; nor cry in their streets*[^31]. He has described the great happiness of certain men. Yet a righteous man too could have this happiness, as Job had: but Job held it for the left, not for the right; for he had no right hand except perpetual and everlasting happiness with God. Therefore the left was permitted to be struck, and the right sufficed for him. How was the left struck? By the temptations of the devil. The devil suddenly took it away; permitted indeed by God, that the righteous man might be proved, the ungodly punished; he took away all: but Job, who knew the left to be the left, and the right to be nothing but the right, how did he hold to the right? He exulted in the Lord, he was consoled for his losses, because he suffered no loss of his inner riches; he had a heart full of God. *The Lord,* he says, *gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^32]. This was his right hand, the Lord himself, eternal life itself, that possession of light, the fountain of life, light in light. *They will be inebriated from the abundance of your house*[^33]: this was the right hand. But the left was for the help of consolation, not for the foundation of happiness. For his true and genuine happiness was God. But those of whom David says that their *mouth has spoken vanity,* and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity; he does not blame them for this, that they abounded in all these things, but because their mouth spoke vanity. For what follows? When he had enumerated their resources, he says: *They called blessed the people that has these things.* This is the vanity which their mouth spoke, that they called blessed the people that has these things. What do you say, you who know what is the left, and what is the right? There follows and he says: *Blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^34].

**9.** Let your Charity attend, then. We have seen the left, we have seen also the right. Hear this confirmed in the Song of Songs: *His left hand,* she says, *is under my head.* The bride says of the bridegroom, the Church of Christ in the embrace of godliness and love. What does she say? *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand will embrace me*[^35]. What, but that the right was above, the left below, and so the bridegroom embraced the bride, setting the left under for consolation, laying the right over for protection? *His left hand,* she says, *is under my head.* God gives that: therefore his left, because all these temporal things God gives. How vain they are, how impious, who ask these things of idols, of demons! How many ask these things of demons, and do not have them? And again, those who do not ask them of demons have them; but they are not given by demons. Likewise many ask of God, and do not have. For he knows how to dispense the left, who calls to the right. Therefore if there is a left, let there be a left; but let it be under the head: above it let your head be, that is, above it let your faith be, where Christ dwells. Do not prefer temporal things to your faith; and the left will not be over your head: but put all temporal things under your faith, and prefer your faith to all temporal things; and the left will be under your head, and rightly his right hand will embrace you.

**10.** Hear this very thing expounded in Proverbs, what is the left, what is the right: when it spoke of Wisdom, *Length,* it says, *of days and years of life are in her right hand; but in her left hand riches and glory*[^36]. That length of days is eternity. For Scripture in a proper way calls "long" that which is eternal: for whatever has an end is short. In another place: *With length,* it says, *of days I will fill him*[^37]. And would it otherwise be said as a great thing: *Honor your father and your mother, that you may be long-lived upon the earth*[^38]? Upon what earth, except that of which it says, *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^39]? To be long-lived there, what is it, except to live there for ever? For to be long-lived here, what is it, except to arrive at old age? Even if the age seems long, when it has arrived, it is convicted of being short; because it is ended. And many cursing their parents grow old on this earth; but many obeying their parents go quickly to the Lord. Is it then fulfilled, that he be long-lived according to this life? But "long-lived" is set down for that eternity. Long life is in her right hand; but riches and glory—that is, the sufficiency of this life, the things esteemed good by men—are the left. I do not know who comes, and wishes to strike your right hand, that is, to take away your faith from you: you have received a blow on the right hand, offer the left, that is, that he may take away what is temporal, and not the things that you have eternal. Hear the apostle Paul doing this. Men persecuted in him that he was a Christian; his right hand is struck, he offered the left: *I am a Roman citizen,* he says[^40]. They despised the right hand, and he terrified them with the left: because they could not fear his right hand; for they had not yet believed in Christ. What, then, if the right hand embraces, the left is under the head; what is, *Let not your left hand know what your right hand does?* That is, when you do a good work, do it for the sake of eternal life. For if you do a good work on earth for this reason, that earthly things may abound for you; your left hand knows what your right hand does, you have mingled the right with the left. Do it only for the sake of eternal life. So do, and you will do securely: for this God has commanded. If for the sake of human things alone and this life alone you do what you do, the left alone works; but if for the sake of eternal life you work, the right alone works: but if you have an intention toward eternal life, but the cupidity of temporal life creeps in upon you, so that you attend to this too when you do a good work, that something here be repaid to you, the left mingles itself with the works of the right: this God forbids.

**11.** Let us come now to that which the psalm says: *The Lord is your covering over the hand of your right.* By "hand" he means power. Whence do we prove it? Because the hand of God too is called the power of God. For the devil, who tempted Job, said this to God: *Send your hand, and touch all that he has; and see if he will bless you to your face*[^41]. What is, *Send your hand,* except, give power? Hear it more evidently, brother, lest perhaps with a carnal sense you still think that God is distinguished by members; hear evidently how a hand is called power. Scripture says in a certain place: *Death and life are in the hands of the tongue*[^42]. We know that tongues are certain pieces of flesh; they are moved in the mouth, and by striking the palate and the teeth distinguish the sounds by which we speak: let the hands of the tongue be shown me. The tongue, then, does not have hands, and yet has hands. What are the hands of the tongue? The power of the tongue. What is, *Death and life are in the hands of the tongue? By your mouth you will be justified, and by your mouth you will be condemned*[^43]. Therefore if a hand is power, what is the hand of the right? I think nothing is understood more fittingly than that we understand the hand of the right to be the power which God gave you, that, if you will, by God's gift, you may be at the right hand. For all the ungodly will be at the left; but all the good sons will be at the right, to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^44]. But that you may be at the right hand—that is, that you may be able to become a son of God—you have received power. What power? Of which John says: *He gave them power to become sons of God.* Whence did you receive this power? *To those who believe,* he says, *in his name*[^45]. If, then, you believe, this very power has been given you, that you may be among the sons of God. But to be among the sons of God is to pertain to the right hand. Therefore your faith is the hand of your right; that is, the power which has been given you, that you may be among the sons of God, is the hand of your right. But what is that power which a man has received worth, unless the Lord protects? Behold, he has believed, now he walks in faith: he is weak, he is shaken among temptations, among troubles, among carnal corruptions, among the suggestions of cupidity, among the wiles and snares of the enemy. What is it worth, then, that he has power, and has believed in Christ, that he may be among the sons of God? Woe to that man, unless the Lord protect even his faith: that is, that he not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear; as the Apostle says, *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear*[^46]. He, then, who does not allow us to be tempted beyond what we can bear, although we are now faithful, although now the hand of our right is in us, God protects us over the hand of our right. It does not suffice us to have the hand of the right, unless he protect that very hand of the right.

**12.** [v. 6.] Behold, this I said about temptations: attend to what follows. *May the Lord protect you over the hand of your right.* I have said it, and, I think, you have recognized it. For if you had not recognized it, and had not recognized it from the Scriptures, you would not signify to me your understanding by your voices. Therefore, because you have understood, brethren, see what follows; why the Lord protects even over the hand of the right—that is, over faith itself, in which we have received power to be sons of God, and to be at the right hand: why is it necessary that the Lord protect? On account of stumbling blocks. Whence are stumbling blocks? From two things stumbling blocks are to be feared, because there are two precepts on which the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets, the love of God and of neighbor. The Church is loved for the sake of the neighbor, but God for the sake of God. In God, the sun is understood, figuratively said: in the Church, the moon is understood, figuratively said. Whoever can err, so as to believe something other about God than he ought, not to believe that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are of one substance, has been deceived by the wiles of heretics, especially the Arians. If he believes something less in the Son, or less in the Holy Spirit, than in the Father, he has suffered a stumbling block in God; he is scorched by the sun. Whoever again supposes that the Church is in one part, and does not know it spread through the whole world, and believes those who say, *Behold, here is Christ, and behold, there*[^47]—as you just heard when the Gospel was read; whereas he bought the whole world, because he gave so great a price: that one is, as it were, made to stumble in the neighbor; he is scorched by the moon. Whoever, then, errs in the very substance of the truth, is scorched by the sun, and is scorched through the day; because he errs in that very wisdom, of which it was said, *Day to day pours forth speech*[^48]. Whence the Apostle too says: *Comparing spiritual things with spiritual*[^49]. *Day to day pours forth speech;* Comparing spiritual things with spiritual. *Day to day pours forth speech;* *We speak wisdom among the perfect*[^50]. What is, And night to night declares knowledge[^51]? To the little ones is preached the humility of Christ, and the flesh of Christ, and the crucifixion of Christ; because that is the milk which suffices the little ones. And therefore the little ones are not left in the night, because the moon too shines even in the night; that is, through the flesh of Christ the Church is preached, because that very flesh of Christ is the head of the Church. Whoever is not made to stumble there, in the Church itself and the flesh of Christ, is not scorched by the moon. Whoever has not been made to stumble in that unchangeable and uncontaminable truth, is not scorched by the sun: not by that sun which the flies and the cattle see with us; but by that sun of which the ungodly say at the end: *What did our pride profit us? And what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All have passed away like a shadow*[^52]. And when they had said these things, *Therefore,* they say, *we erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness did not shine for us, and the sun did not rise for us*[^53]. Does not this sun rise for all the ungodly, by his appointment of whom it was said, *Who makes his sun to rise upon the good and the evil*[^54]? Therefore God made another sun, which rises upon the good and the evil, this one which both the good and the evil see; but that sun is another, not made, but begotten, through whom all things were made, where is the understanding of unchangeable truth: of him the ungodly say, *And the sun did not rise for us.* Whoever does not err in that very wisdom is not scorched by the sun. Whoever does not err in the Church, and in the flesh of the Lord, and in the things that were done temporally for us, is not scorched by the moon. But every man, although he has now believed in Christ, either errs there, or there, if there is not done in him what was said: *The Lord is your covering over the hand of your right.* For therefore, when he had said, *The Lord is your covering over the hand of your right;* as if that one had asked and said: Behold, now there is the hand of my right, now I have chosen to believe in Christ, I have received power to be among the sons of God, why is it that God is my covering still above, that is, *over the hand of* my *right?* there follows: *By day the sun will not scorch you, nor the moon by night.* For this, then, your covering is over the hand of your right, that the sun may not scorch you by day, nor the moon by night. For from this understand, brethren, that it was figuratively said. For indeed, if we think of the visible sun, it scorches by day: does the moon scorch by night? But what is scorching? A stumbling block. Hear the Apostle saying: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I am not scorched*[^55]?

**13.** [v. 7.] *By day, then, the sun will not scorch you, nor the moon by night.* Why? Because *the Lord will keep you from all evil.* From stumbling blocks in the sun, from stumbling blocks in the moon, from all evil he will keep you, who is your covering over the hand of your right, who does not sleep, nor slumber. And to what end? Because we are amid temptations: *The Lord will keep you from all evil. May the Lord keep your soul:* even in the soul itself. *May he keep your going in and your going out, from this time forth and for evermore.* Not, as it were, your body; for in the body the martyrs were destroyed: but *may the Lord keep your soul;* because the martyrs did not yield in the soul. And the persecutors raged against Crispina, whose birthday we celebrate today; they raged against a rich and delicate woman: but she was strong, because the Lord was her covering over the hand of her right, he who kept her. For who, brethren, is there in Africa who is ignorant of her? For she was most illustrious, noble by birth, abounding in riches: but all these things were the left, were under her head. The enemy came who would strike the head, and the left was set against him, which was under the head. The head was above, the right embraced from above. What could the persecutor do, even to a delicate woman? She was indeed weak in sex, and perhaps the more languid for riches, and the more infirm for bodily habit; but what was that to such fortifications? what to that bridegroom setting the left under the head, and embracing with his right? When could the enemy strike one so fortified? And yet he struck, but the body. But what does the psalm say? *May the Lord keep your soul.* The soul did not yield, the body was struck. And the body was struck for a time: for at the end it will rise again. For he himself too, who deigned to be the head of the Church, offered his body to be struck for a time: but he raised up his flesh in three days; he will raise up our flesh at the end. For this reason the head was raised up, that it might watch over the body, that it might not fail. *May the Lord keep your soul.* Let it not yield, let it not be broken by stumbling blocks; in persecutions, in tribulations, let it not yield by failing: as the Lord says, *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul: but fear him who has power to kill both body and soul in the gehenna of fire*[^56]. May the Lord, then, keep this your soul, lest you yield to the evil persuader, lest you yield to one promising false things, lest you yield to one threatening temporal things, and *may the Lord keep your soul.*

**14.** And then: *May the Lord keep your going in and your going out, from this time forth and for evermore.* For attend to your going in for a time. *May the Lord keep your going in and your going out, from this time forth and for evermore.* May he keep likewise your going out. What is *going in?* What is *going out?* When we are tempted, we go in: when we overcome the temptation, we go out. Hear, *going in;* hear, *going out. The furnace tests the vessels of the potter,* says Scripture, *and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men*[^57]. If righteous men are so, like the vessels of the potter; it is necessary that the vessels of the potter go into the furnace. And not when they go in is the potter now secure, but when they have come out. But the Lord is secure, because he knows who are his, and knows which will not crack in the furnace. Those do not crack who do not have the wind of pride. Humility, then, keeps in every temptation: because from the valley of weeping we ascend, singing the Song of Ascents; and the Lord keeps the going in, that we may go in safe. Let us be of sound faith when the temptation befalls, and he keeps the *going out, from this time forth and for evermore.* For when we have come out from all temptation, now for ever no temptation will terrify us, no concupiscence will even solicit us. Hear the Apostle mentioning this very thing, which a little before I mentioned: *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear*[^58]. Behold, your going in is kept; when God does not let befall you a temptation which you cannot bear, he keeps your going in: see whether he keeps the going out too. *But he will make,* he says, *with the temptation also a way out, that you may be able to endure*[^59]. Can we, brethren, interpret it otherwise than the very words of the Apostle have taught? Keep yourselves, then, but not by yourselves, because the Lord is your covering, who keeps you, who does not slumber nor sleep. Once he slept for us: he rose again; now he will not sleep. Let no one presume on himself. From the valley of weeping we ascend, let us not remain on the way. The steps remain on the way; we ought not to remain slothful, we ought not to fall proud: let us say to God, Let not our foot be moved. He will not sleep, who keeps us. It is in our power, by God's gift, if we make our keeper him who neither sleeps nor slumbers, who keeps Israel. What Israel? The one seeing God. So will your help be from the Lord, so will your covering be over the hand of your right; so is your going in and your going out kept, from this time forth and for evermore. For if you presume on yourself, your foot is moved; if your foot is moved, you already think yourself to be on some step: thence you fall, if you are proud; because the humble man in the valley of weeping says, *Do not give my foot to be moved.*

**15.** Although the psalm is short, yet the treatment is long, and the sermon is long. Suppose, brethren, that on the birthday of the blessed Crispina I have invited you, and have been rather immoderate in drawing out the banquet. Could this not be done to you, if some military man invited you, and forced you to drink without measure at the table? Let it be permitted us to do this in the divine sermon, that you may be inebriated and filled; just as the Lord too deigned to water the earth with his timely rain, that he might allow us to go with greater joy to the place of the martyrs, as we had promised yesterday. For those martyrs are here with us without toil.
