# Exposition of Psalm 102

**1.** [v. 1.] In every gift of the Lord our God, in every consolation of his, in every rebuke, in the grace he has deigned to give, in the indulgence by which he has not repaid us what he owed, in all his works, let our soul bless the Lord. For this we have sung; from this begins the psalm of which we will speak, with him granting what we can, whom our soul blesses. Let each one of us rouse and exhort his soul, and say to it: *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* And let all of us, and the brothers who are everywhere in Christ, one man, whose head is in heaven, let that one man exhort his soul, and say to it: *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* It obeys, it complies, it does this, it is persuaded—not from our gifts, but from the gifts of him whom our soul blesses. For this psalm has undertaken to show us why our soul should bless the Lord, as if its soul had answered it: Why do you say to me: Bless the Lord? Let us hear, then, let our soul itself hear, let it consider all the things by which it is roused, lest it be lazy in blessing the Lord; and let it see whether it is just what is said to it: *Bless the Lord, O my soul*: let it see whether it ought to bless anything besides the Lord. *Bless*, he says, *O my soul, the Lord.*

**2.** He repeats this, and says more expressly what he had said: *Bless the Lord, O my soul: and all that is within me, his holy name.* I think he does not speak to the inward parts of the body; I think he does not say this, that our lung and liver, and whatever the intestines of the flesh are, should burst forth into a voice of blessing the Lord. There is indeed the lung of our breast as it were a kind of bellows breathing the reciprocal breath, which breath of the air taken in is pressed out into voice and sound when we form words; nor can any sound of voice come from our mouth except what the lung, pressed, has sent out: but this is not what is meant here; all this is for the ears of men. God has ears, the heart too has a sound. A man addresses his inward parts, that they may bless the Lord, and says to them: All that is within me, bless his holy name. Do you ask what your inward parts are? Your very soul. So what he says, *Bless the Lord, O my soul*; this he says, *all that is within me, his holy name*: for it is understood: bless. Cry out with the voice, if there is a man to hear; be silent in voice, when there is no one to hear; your inward parts never lack one to hear. And so the blessing was sounding a while ago from our mouth, and we sang these very words: *Bless the Lord, O my soul; and all that is within me, his holy name.* We have sung as much as was enough for the time, and we have fallen silent: but ought our inward parts to be silent from blessing the Lord? Let the sound of voices alternate according to the time, let the voice of the inward parts be perpetual. When you come together to the church to say a hymn, your voice sounds the praises of God: you have said as much as you could, you have departed; let your soul sound the praises of God. You do business; let your soul praise God. You take food; see what the Apostle says: *Whether you eat or drink, do all to the glory of God*[^1]. I dare to say: when you sleep, let your soul bless the Lord. Let no thought of disgrace rouse you, let no plotting of theft rouse you, let no corruption perhaps agreed upon rouse you. Your innocence, even in the sleeper, is the voice of your soul. *Bless the Lord, O my soul; and all that is within me, his holy name.*

**3.** [v. 2.] *Bless the Lord, O my soul, and do not forget all his recompenses. Bless*, he says, *O my soul, the Lord.* What is your soul? All that is within you. *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* The repetition is for the sake of exhortation. But that you may always bless the Lord, *Do not forget all his recompenses.* If you forget, you will be silent. But the recompenses of the Lord cannot be before your eyes, unless your sins have been before your eyes. Let not the delight of past sin be before your eyes, but let the condemnation of sin be before your eyes: condemnation by you, remission by God. For these things the Lord recompenses, so that you can say, *What shall I render to the Lord, for all that he has rendered to me?* For considering this, the martyrs, whose memory too we celebrate today, and all the saints whatsoever who scorned this life, and, as you heard in the Epistle of John, laid down their souls for the brothers—which is the perfection of love, as the Lord says, *No one has greater love than to lay down his soul for his friends*[^2]: considering this, then, the holy martyrs scorned their souls here, that they might find them there, following the words of the Lord, who says, *He who loves his soul will lose it; and he who has lost it for my sake will find it unto eternal life*[^3]. For they wished to render: who? and what? and to whom? Men rendered their service, even unto death, to God. What, that he had not given? What did they give, that they had not received? He, then, truly renders, who alone gives: but he did not render according to our sins; for some recompenses were owed to us, and others were rendered. *Do not*, he says, *forget all his recompenses*: not allotments, but *recompenses.* For one thing was owed, and there was rendered what was not owed. Whence he too: *What shall I render to the Lord, for all*, he says, *that he has rendered to me?*[^4] He does not say, that he allotted to me; but, that he recompensed to me. You rendered evil for good; he rendered good for evil. How did you, O man, render to God evil for good? You who were formerly a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and an insulter, rendered blasphemies. For what goods? First, that you exist: but the stone too exists. Then, that you live: but the beast too lives. What will you render to the Lord, for the fact that he made you, above all beasts and above all birds, to his image and likeness? Do not seek what to render him: render him his own likeness, he seeks no more; he demands his own coin. But you, instead of giving thanks, instead of humility, instead of obedience, instead of religious worship—that is, instead of all those goods which you owed to your God, for the goods you received, which I mentioned—you rendered blasphemies. What, then, does he do? Confess, I forgive. I too render, but not what you rendered: you rendered evil for good; I render good for evil.

**4.** Consider, then, O soul, all the recompenses of God, by considering all your evil deeds: for as many as your evil deeds are, so many are his good recompenses. And what perhaps of gifts will you offer him? what of presents? what of sacrifices? Since you will not forget his recompenses, he delights in this sacrifice: *Bless the Lord, O soul. The sacrifice of praise will glorify me: offer to God the sacrifice of praise, and render to the Most High your vows*[^5]. God wishes to be praised; and this, that you may profit, not that he may be exalted. There is nothing at all to render him: and what he demands, he demands not for himself, but for you; it will profit you, it is kept for you. He loves this from you, not because it increases him, but because it leads you to him. Therefore the martyrs sought, and failed in a certain way by not finding, and said, *What shall I render to the Lord, for all that he has rendered to me?* And they did not find what to render, except, *I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord*[^6]. What will you render to the Lord? For you were considering, and not finding: *I will take the cup of salvation.* What? Did not the Lord himself give the *cup of salvation?* Render something of your own, if you can. I would not say it; do not do it, do not render of your own; God does not wish to be rendered of your own. If you render of your own, you render sin. For all that you have, you have from him: only your sin you have of your own. He does not wish to be rendered of your own, but of his own. As with the farmer, from the earth he sowed, if you bring a crop, you have rendered of the farmer's fruit; if thorns, you have offered of your own. Render truth, praise the Lord in truth: if you wish to render of your own, you will lie. *He who speaks a lie speaks of his own*[^7]. If he who speaks a lie speaks of his own; he who speaks the truth speaks of God's. But what is it to take the cup of salvation, except to imitate the passions of the Lord? This the martyrs did. This he said to those who were being proud, and already seeking lofty seats, and shunning the valley of weeping, who wished to sit, one at the right hand, the other at the left. What, then, does he say? *Can you drink the cup that I am about to drink?*[^8] Now, then, the martyr, prepared for the holy victim, says, *I will take the cup of salvation*: I will take the cup of Christ, I will drink the passion of the Lord. Beware lest you fail. But *I will call upon the name of the Lord.* Those, then, who failed, did not call upon the Lord; they presumed on their own strength. Render in such a way that you remember that you have received what you render. So, then, let your soul bless the Lord, so as not to forget all his recompenses.

**5.** [vv. 3-5.] Hear all his recompenses: *Who is gracious to all your iniquities, who heals all your weaknesses. Who redeems your life from corruption, who crowns you in compassion and mercy. Who satisfies your desire in good things: your youth will be renewed like the eagle's.* Behold the recompenses. What was owed to the sinner, except punishment? what was owed to the blasphemer, except the Gehenna of burning fire? He did not recompense these: do not be terrified, do not shudder, do not fear without love. Do not forget all his good recompenses; and change now, lest you experience his recompenses: what shall I say? his bad ones? If they are just, they are not bad. To you, then, bad; but to God, even these evils which you suffer are not evils: for if they are just, they are good; but to you who suffer them these are evils. Do you not wish that the things which are just on God's part be evils to you? Let not your iniquity be an evil in the sight of God. For he did not cease to call, nor neglect to instruct the one called, nor cease to perfect the one instructed, nor neglect to crown the one perfected. What do you say? That you are a sinner? Be converted, and receive these recompenses; *he is gracious to all your iniquities.* After the remission of sins you carry an infirm body: it is necessary that there be certain carnal desires that titillate you, and that suggest to you illicit delights; they come from your weakness. For you still carry infirm flesh, death is not yet swallowed up in victory, this corruptible has not yet put on incorruption; the soul itself is still shaken by certain disturbances after the remission of sins; it is still set among the dangers of temptations, it is delighted by certain suggestions, by certain ones it is not delighted, and in those by which it is delighted, sometimes it consents to certain ones, it is caught. It is a weakness; *he heals* too *all your weaknesses.* All your weaknesses will be healed, do not fear. They are great, you will say: but the physician is greater. To the almighty physician no weakness is incurable: only let yourself be cured, do not push back his hands; he knows what he is doing. Be not only delighted when he soothes, but endure too when he cuts: endure the medicinal pain, thinking of the health to come. See, my brothers, in these weaknesses of the body, how much men endure, that they may live a few days, and die, and these few days uncertain. For many, after enduring great pains, when they were cut by physicians, either died in the hands of the physicians, or, now healthy, were carried off by some sickness rushing in. If they expected death so near to themselves, would they take on those immense pains to be endured? You do not endure for something uncertain: he who promised health cannot be deceived. A physician is sometimes deceived, and promises health of the human body. Why is he deceived? Because he does not heal what he made. God made your body, God made your soul; he knows how to recreate what he created, he knows how to re-form what he himself formed: only be under the hands of the physician; for he hates the one who pushes back his hands. This does not happen in the hands of a human physician. Men wish to be bound, and to be cut; about to give, for uncertain health, certain pain, a great fee. God, whom he made, heals you both with certainty and for free. Bear, then, his hands, O soul, who bless him, not forgetting his recompenses; for *he heals all your weaknesses.*

**6.** *Who redeems your life from corruption.* From this he heals all your weaknesses, because he redeems your life from corruption. Behold, the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul. The soul, then, has life in a corruptible body. What sort of life? It suffers burdens, it bears weights. For thinking of God himself, as it is fitting for God to be thought of by man, how many things impede, as it were interrupting from the necessity of human corruption? how many call it back? how many twist it away from its lofty intention? how many interrupt? what a crowd of phantasms? what a populace of suggestions? All this in the human heart, as it were, teems with the worms of this corruption. We have exaggerated the disease, let us praise the physician too. Will he not, then, heal you, who made you such that you would not be sick, if you had been willing to keep the law of health you received? Did he not both arrange for you and command you what to touch, what not to touch, to retain your health? You were unwilling to hear it for retaining; hear it for recovering. By your weakness you have experienced how true were the things he had commanded. At long last let a man hear at least by experience what, warned, he did not hold. What hardness is it, which not even experience teaches? Will he not, then, heal you, who had made you such that you would never be sick, if you had been willing to keep his precepts? will he not heal you, who made the angels, and who will make you, restored, equal to the angels? will he not heal what was made to his image, who made heaven and earth? He will heal you; you must be willing to be healed. He heals indeed any weak person, but he does not heal the unwilling. And what is happier for you than to have, as it were in your hand, so in your will, your health? If you wished to be in some lofty honor on this earth; if you coveted a dukedom, a proconsulship, a prefecture: could you immediately do as you wished? would power follow your will? Many wish to attain these things, and cannot: but if they attained them, what does honor profit the sick? For who is not sick in this life? who does not drag out a long weakness? To be born here in a mortal body is to begin to be sick. Our needs are propped up by daily remedies, the refreshments of all our needs are daily remedies. Would not hunger kill you, unless you applied its remedy? would not thirst destroy you, unless by drinking you, not entirely extinguished it, but deferred it? For thirst, a little tempered, will return. We temper, then, with these poultices the misery of our sickness. You were wearied by standing, you are refreshed by sitting; the very sitting is medicine for weariness: in this very medicine you are wearied again; you cannot sit long. Whatever it is where weariness is relieved, another weariness is begun. Why, then, do you, weak, desire these things? First think of your health. Sometimes a man is sick in his house, in his bed, with a more manifest sickness; although that too is manifest which men are unwilling to look at: yet with that sickness for which human physicians are sought, anyone is sick in his house, panting with fevers in his bed; he might perhaps wish to think about his household affairs, to order something, whether in the house or on the estate, or to arrange things; immediately, with the care of his people clamoring and murmuring around, he is called back from such cares, and it is said to him: Leave these things, first think of your health. So this is said to you: Every man, if you are not sick, think of other things; if your very weakness convicts you of being sick, first think of your health. Your health is Christ: think, then, of Christ. Take the cup of his salvation: *Who heals all your weaknesses*: if you will this health, you will obtain it. Honors and riches, when you seek them, you will not immediately have, if you will them: this is both more precious, and follows the will. *Who heals all your weaknesses, who redeems your life from corruption.* There every weakness of yours will be healed, when this corruptible will put on incorruption. For your life has been redeemed from corruption; now be secure: a contract of good faith has been entered; no one deceives your redeemer, no one circumvents, no one oppresses. He has carried out the transaction here, he has already paid the price, he has shed his blood. He shed his blood, I say, the only Son of God, for us: O soul, lift yourself up, you are worth so much. *He redeems your life from corruption.* He has shown by example what he promised as reward. He died for our offenses, and rose again for our justification. Let the members hope for what was demonstrated in the head. Will he not care for his members, whose head he raised to heaven? Therefore *he redeems your life from corruption.*

**7.** *Who crowns you in compassion and mercy.* For perhaps you had now begun to be as it were arrogant, when you heard, *he crowns you.* So I am great, so I have wrestled. By whose strength? Yours, but supplied by him. For that you wrestle is manifest; and therefore you will be crowned, because you will conquer: but see who conquered first, see who makes you a conqueror in the second place too. *I*, he says, *have conquered the world, rejoice*[^9]. And whence do we rejoice, if he conquered the world? as if we had conquered? Yes, plainly let us rejoice, because we conquered. We who were conquered in ourselves, conquered in him. So he crowns you, because he crowns his own gifts, not your merits. *I labored more than all of them*, says the Apostle: but see what he adds: *yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^10]. And after all his labors he awaits the crown itself, and says, *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith: for the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge*[^11]. Why? Because *I have fought the fight.* Why? Because *I have finished the course.* Why? Because *I have kept the faith.* Whence did you fight? whence did you keep the faith? *Yet not I, but the grace of God with me.* So that even that you are crowned, you are crowned by his mercy. Be proud nowhere; always praise the Lord, do not forget all his recompenses. It is a recompense, when you, a sinner and impious, were called, that you might be justified. It is a recompense, when you were raised up and steered lest you fall. It is a recompense, when strength was supplied to you, that you might persevere to the end. It is a recompense, that even this flesh of yours, by which you were weighed down, should rise again, and not even a hair of your head perish. It is a recompense, that after the resurrection you be crowned. It is a recompense, that you praise God himself forever without failing. Do not forget all his recompenses, if you wish your soul to bless the Lord, *who crowns you in compassion and mercy.*

**8.** And what shall I do when crowned? Behold, I was helped while I wrestled, when the contest is finished I will be crowned; now there will be no remaining hostile suggestion or corruption with which I might wrestle. For always in this life we wrestle with this corruption: but what is written? *The last enemy, death, will be destroyed.* After the destruction of death, you will fear no enemy: thence *death is swallowed up in victory*[^12]. Then, then, there will be victory, then the crown. After the contest, then, I will be crowned; after the crown, what shall I do? *Who satisfies your desire in good things.* For now you hear "good," and you pant; you hear "good," and you sigh: and this very thing that you perhaps sin in, you are deceived by an eagerness for choosing good things; and you are held guilty in this, that you do not hear the good counsel of God, what is to be scorned and what to be chosen; which perhaps you neglect to learn, if you were deceived in choosing the good. Wherever you sin, you seek as it were a good, you desire as it were a refreshment. These things you seek are good; but they will be evil to you, if you have deserted him by whom the good things were made. Seek your own good, O soul. For one thing is good for one, another for another, and all creatures have a certain good of their own, of their wholeness, and of the perfection of their nature: it matters what is necessary for each imperfect thing, that it may be perfected; seek your own good. *No one is good but the one God*[^13]. The highest good, this is your good. What, then, is lacking to one for whom the highest good is good? For there are also lower goods, which are good for one and another. What is good for a beast, brothers, except to fill its belly, to lack want, to sleep, to frolic, to live, to be healthy, to beget? It is good for it, and up to a certain measure it has the measure of its good allotted, and granted by God the creator of all things. Do you seek such a good? God gives this too; but do not seek it alone. Coheir of Christ, why do you rejoice that you are a partner of the beast? Lift up your hope to the good of all goods. He himself will be your good, by whom you were made good in your own kind, and all things were made good in their own kind. For God made all things very good. So if we say of that good which God is that it is very good, this has already been said of the creation too: *God made all things very good*[^14]. What, then, of that good of which it is said, *No one is good but the one God?* do we say that it is very good? The recollection of all creatures recurs to us, because it was said, *God made all things very good.* What, then, shall we say? We fail in voice, but not in feeling. Let that recent discussion of the Psalm come to mind: we cannot unfold it, let us shout for joy. God is good. What sort of good, who can say? Behold, we cannot say it, and we are not permitted to be silent. So if we cannot say it, and for joy we are not permitted to be silent; let us neither speak nor be silent. What, then, shall we do, neither speaking nor being silent? Let us shout for joy. *Shout for joy to God our salvation*: shout for joy to God, all the earth. What is it, *Shout for joy?* Bring forth the ineffable voice of your joys, and belch out to him your gladness. And what will that belching be after the feasting, if now after these little refreshments our soul is so affected? what will it be, when it happens after the redemption from all corruption, what is said in this psalm, *Who satisfies your desire in good things?*

**9.** And as if you were to ask: When does he satisfy? for now I am not satisfied; to whatever I turn myself, what I have attained becomes cheap to me, although it had kindled me while desired: since all the things which I love while I do not have them, when I have them I scorn—what good will satisfy me? The praise of God. And even that, when the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind thinking of many things, my soul is not filled, is not perfected: other delights of needs, from corruption, twist me away from it. When will my desire be satisfied in good things? when, you ask? *Your youth will be renewed like the eagle's.* You ask, then, when your soul will be satisfied in good things? When your youth will have been renewed. And he added, *like the eagle's.* Surely something lies hidden here: yet what is wont to be said of the eagle we do not pass over in silence, because it is not beside the point to understand this. Let only this be impressed on our hearts, that it was not without cause said by the Holy Spirit, *Your youth will be renewed like the eagle's.* For he signified to us a certain resurrection. And indeed the youth of the eagle too is renewed, but not unto immortality. For the likeness was given, as far as it could be drawn from a mortal thing to signify in some way an immortal thing, not to demonstrate it. It is said that the eagle, when it is pressed by the old age of its body, cannot take food because of the overgrowth of its beak. For the upper part of its beak, which curves over the lower part, when through old age it has grown too much, the length of its growth does not allow it to open its mouth, so that there be some interval between the lower part and the upper hook. For unless some interval lies open, the bite does not have, as it were, pincers, by which it may as it were clip what it passes into its throat. So when the upper part grows, and is curved too much, it cannot open its mouth and take anything. This old age does to it. It is weighed down by the weakness of old age, and grows too weak from the lack of eating; both things befalling it, both of age and of want. And so, in a certain natural manner, in the measure of as it were renewing its youth, the eagle is said to dash and strike against a rock its as it were upper lip, by whose excessive growth the way of eating is closed: and so by grinding it against the rock it knocks it off, and is rid of the burden of the former beak, by which its food was impeded. It comes to its food, and all things are restored: after old age it will be as a young eagle; the vigor of all its members returns, the brightness of its feathers, the rudders of its wings, it flies on high as before, there comes about in it a certain resurrection. For to this end this likeness is set forth: as it is taken of the moon, that the moon, diminished and as it were intercepted, is born again and filled; and it signifies to us the resurrection; but when filled it does not remain; again it is diminished, that it may always signify. So, then, also this that is said of the eagle: the eagle is not restored unto immortality, but we unto eternal life; yet for this reason the likeness was drawn from it, that what impedes us, the rock may take from us. Do not, then, presume on your own strength; the firmness of the rock knocks off your old age: *but the rock was Christ*[^15]. In Christ our youth will be renewed like the eagle's. For we have grown old among our enemies, as the voice of the psalm is known: *I have grown old*, it says, *among all my enemies*[^16]. Whence have we grown old? By mortal flesh, by this hay-like flesh: and therefore, *I am struck like hay, and my heart has withered, because I forgot to eat my bread*[^17]. *I forgot*, he says, *to eat my bread.* The old age grew, it closed the mouth, let it be worn down on the rock.

**10.** So too, then, in this psalm we are dealing with, when he had set down, *Who satisfies your desire in good things;* as if the soul answered: I will not be satisfied with mortal things, I will not be satisfied with temporal things; let him give something eternal, let him grant something eternal: let him give me his Wisdom, let him give me his Word, God with God, and himself God the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit. A beggar, I stand before his door, he whom I call upon does not sleep, let him give me three loaves. Recall the Gospel: behold what it is to know the Scriptures of God; those who have read are moved. For recall that a certain needy man came to the house of his friend, and asked for three loaves. And he, it says, answering him as he slept, said, *I am now at rest, and my children are within sleeping with me*[^18]. The man, persevering in asking, extorted by his persistence what he could not by merit. But God wishes to give; but he does not give except to one who asks, lest he give to one who cannot receive. He does not wish to be roused by your persistence. For when you pray, you are not troublesome as if to one sleeping: *He who keeps Israel will not sleep, nor slumber*[^19]. Once Christ slept, that from his side a spouse might be made: he slept on the cross, it is manifest. For he died, that he might say, *I have slept, and taken sleep.* But shall not he who sleeps add that he rise again? Therefore there follows there: *And I rose up, for the Lord will receive me*[^20]. But what now does the Apostle say? *Christ*, he says, *rising from the dead, now dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^21]. He himself, then, does not sleep; see that your faith does not sleep. Let the soul say, then, now desiring to be satisfied with a certain lofty, unspeakable good, over which one shouts for joy rather than unfolds anything; for now it wills, now it perceives something of it: it sees itself impeded by the weighing-down of the body, that it cannot be satisfied in this life; and as if it answers, and says: Why do you say to me: Your desire will be satisfied in good things? I know the good of him that I desire, I know what suffices me, I see this in Philip: *Show us*, he says, *the Father, and it is enough for us.* He desired only the Father, as it were: the Lord showed three loaves to be desired; he showed that there is one bread, and said, *Have I been with you so long a time, and do you not know the Father? Philip, he who sees me, sees the Father too*[^22]. He promised the Spirit too: *Whom the Father will send*, he says, *in my name;* and likewise he says, *Whom I will send to you from the Father*[^23]: his gift, equal to himself. I know what I desire; but when will I be satisfied with it? Behold, now I think of the Trinity, in whatever way of the Trinity, scarcely in a riddle through a mirror, in part I dare to perceive something; when will I be satisfied? *Your youth will be renewed like the eagle's.* You are not satisfied now, because your soul is not fit for that solid and great food; but with its beak closed, it is not fit. Old age closed your mouth, therefore the rock was given, where, your old age ground away, your youth may be renewed like the eagle's; that you may be able to eat your bread, the one who says, *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^24]. *Your youth will be renewed like the eagle's*: then you will be satisfied in good things.

**11.** [v. 6.] *The Lord doing mercies, and judgment for those who receive injury.* He does them now, brothers, before we come to the renewal like the eagle's, before we are satisfied in good things. For what here, what in this pilgrimage, what in this life? are we deserted? No. *The Lord doing mercies.* And see how he does mercies, not leaving us in the desert, not leaving us in the wilderness, until we reach the homeland: *doing mercies;* but for whom? *Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy*[^25]. You heard just now, brothers, when the Gospel was read. Let no one, then, suppose that the mercy of God will be in him, if he is himself merciless. But hear what the measure of mercy is, lest it be as it were toward a friend, and not toward an enemy. It is said, *Love your enemies*[^26]. You wish to be satisfied with the good things of God; let mercy be satisfied in you. Full mercy is itself perfect mercy; it is the one that loves, that loves even the one who holds it in hatred. For what shall I do, you say? If I begin to love my enemy, I will receive injuries, and bear injuries, and not avenge myself, though there are laws at hand? It is just that you avenge yourself; it is granted, because it is just: see whether you do not have something for which you may be avenged, and avenge. For you say thus: Shall I then not avenge myself? as if God represses the justice of vengeance, and does not extinguish the pride of the avenger. Or indeed was that adulteress not to be stoned? or if she were stoned, would something unjust be done? If it would be done unjustly, it was commanded unjustly; but the Law commanded it, God commanded it: but, O you avengers, see whether you are not sinners. The adulterous woman was brought to be stoned according to the Law, but she was brought to the giver of the Law. You who brought her rage: see who you rage at, and against whom you rage; if a sinner against a woman sinner, cease to rage, first confess: if as a sinner you rage against a woman sinner, leave her; he knows what to think of her, what to judge, how to spare, how to heal. Do you rage from the Law? The giver of the Law, from which you rage, knows better what to do. Now the Lord, at the time when she was brought before him, with bowed head was writing on the ground. Then he wrote on the ground, when he bent down to the ground: before he bent down to the ground, he did not write on the ground, but on stone. The earth was now going to bring forth something fruitful from the Lord's writing. On stone he had written the Law, signifying the hardness of the Jews: on the earth he wrote, signifying the fruit of the Christians. So they came bringing the adulteress, like raging waves against a rock: but they were broken by his answer. For he said to them, *He who among you is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her*[^27]. And again with bowed head, he wrote on the ground. And each one, now questioning his own conscience, they did not appear. Not the weak adulterous woman repelled them, but their adulterated conscience. They wished to avenge, they desired to judge: they came to the rock, their judges were swallowed up beside the rock.

**12.** *The Lord doing mercies*: but for whom? *Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy.* Do mercy to all. What mercy will you do to the just man? Only in bodily needs; and if the supplies for these are lacking from you, they will not be lacking from God. What you do, then, profits you more. You give to a beggar passing by and asking: seek also a just man to give to, through whom you may be received into the eternal dwellings; because *he who receives a just man in the name of a just man will receive the reward of a just man*[^28]. The beggar seeks you, you seek a just man. For of one it is said, *Give to everyone who asks you*[^29]: and of another it is said, Let alms sweat in your hand, until you find a just man to give it to. And if for a long time he is not found; seek for a long time, you will find him. But what will you bestow? Is not more bestowed on you? *If we*, he says, *have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal things?*[^30] Thence too is that which we expounded a while ago, at the Lord's prompting, that the earth brings forth hay for the cattle, that is, carnal things for those who thresh; because, *You shall not muzzle the mouth of the threshing ox*[^31]. Whence we exhorted you to be diligent, careful, sober in this matter; to reckon your works your treasures. But do we, brothers, say these things, that they may so be done toward us? I think that, in the name of the Lord, this voice, though of weak men, can yet be apostolic, but it profits you, as the Apostle himself says, *Not because I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit*[^32]. What alms, then, will you do to the just man? The widow did not feed Elijah, the raven fed him; because the one who made the raven fed him: I mean Elijah. There is not, then, lacking whence God may give to his own: you, see what you buy, when you buy, at what price you buy. For you are buying the kingdom of heaven; and there is no time for buying except in this life. And at how cheap a price you buy, attend. It is worth to you as much as you have been able to have.

**13.** Do mercy to the wicked man, not as to a wicked man. For do not receive the wicked man himself insofar as he is wicked: that is, do not receive him as if with the intention and love of his wickedness. For it is both forbidden to give to a sinner, and to receive sinners. And how, *Give to everyone who asks you?* and how, *If your enemy hungers, feed him*[^33]? They seem as it were contrary, but they are opened in the name of Christ to those who knock, and will be manifest to those who seek. *Do not hand over to a sinner*, and *Do not receive a sinner*[^34]; and yet, *Give to everyone who asks you.* But he who asks me is a sinner. Give, not as to a sinner. When do you give as to a sinner? When it pleases you to give because of that in which he is a sinner. Let your Charity attend a little, until the matter is unrolled with examples too, very useful for understanding. He said this: When someone or other hungers, if you have something to give, give; if you see that it must be given to relieve him, give. Let not the bowels of mercy grow lazy in this, because a sinner has met you: for it is a man, a sinner, who has met you. When I say, A sinful man has met you, I have said two names; these two names are not superfluous: two names, one because man, the other because sinner; because man, it is the work of God; because sinner, it is the work of man: give to the work of God, do not give to the work of man. And how, you say, do you forbid me to give to the work of man? What is it to give to the work of man? To give to a sinner because of sin, to one who pleases you because of sin. And who will do this, you say? Who will do this? Would that no one, would that few, would that not publicly. Those who give to hunters, why do they give, let them tell me? Why does he give to a hunter? He loves in him that in which he is most worthless; he feeds this in him, he clothes this in him, that very public worthlessness, for the spectacles of all. He who gives to actors, who gives to charioteers, who gives to prostitutes, why does he give? Do not they too give to men? Yet there they attend not to the nature of the work of God, but to the worthlessness of the human work. Do you wish to see what you honor in the hunter, when you clothe him? Let it be said to you: Be such: you love him, you delight in him, you wish in a way to strip yourself and clothe him; do not take it as an injury, if it is said to you: Let your sons be such. It is an injury, you say. Why is it an injury, except because that is iniquity? why an injury, except because that is baseness? You do not, then, when you give, give to bravery, but to baseness. So, just as he who gives to a hunter does not give to a man, but to a most worthless craft (for if he were only a man, and not a hunter, you would not give; you honor in him the vice, not the nature): so, on the contrary, if you give to a just man, if you give to a prophet, if you give to a disciple of Christ something he needs, and do not consider there that he is a disciple of Christ, that he is a minister of God, that he is a steward of God; but consider there some temporal advantage, that perhaps when he is needed for your case, he may be for sale to you, because you have offered him something; you have so failed to give to the just man, if you give thus, as that man failed to give to the man, when he gave to the hunter. The matter, then, dearest ones, is set in plain view, and I think that even if it was obscure, it is now manifest. To this the Lord bound you, when he said, *He who receives a just man*: it would suffice. But because a just man can be received with another intention, when he is thought to be able to be of use for something temporal, perhaps to supply cupidity, perhaps to help in circumventing or oppressing a man; because you seek such service from him, perhaps for that reason you receive him: he denied you the reward of a just man, except with an addition. For he said, *He who receives a just man in the name of a just man;* that is, receiving him because he is just: and, *He who receives a prophet;* not only receives a prophet, but, *in the name of a prophet*, honoring in him that he is a prophet: finally, *He who gives a cup of cold water to one of these least ones, only in the name of a disciple*, that is, for this reason because he is a disciple of Christ, for this reason because he is a steward of the sacrament; *amen I say to you, he will not lose his reward*[^35]. As, then, you understand, *He who receives a just man in the name of a just man will receive the reward of a just man;* so understand: He who receives a sinner in the name of a sinner will lose the reward.

**14.** Therefore, brothers, exercise mercy. There is no other bond of love, there is no other vehicle by which we are carried over from this life to that homeland; extend your love even to enemies: be secure. For this Christ came, to whom it was so long before said, *Out of the mouth of infants and sucklings you have perfected praise, that you may destroy the enemy and the avenger*[^36]: which some manuscripts have as *defender*; but more truly, *avenger.* The Lord wished to destroy the avenger, that is, the one who wished to avenge himself, so that his sins should not be forgiven him. What then, you say? will discipline sleep? will all rebuke be taken away? It will not be taken away. For what will you do with a dissolute son? will you not chastise him, will you not beat him? And your very slave, if you see him living badly, will you not restrain him by some punishment, by beatings? Let this be done, let it be done: God permits it, indeed he reproves you if it is not done; but do it with the mind of love, not with the mind of vengeance. But where you have suffered injuries from some more powerful people, where it is not lawful for you to correct by discipline, nor perhaps even to admonish or command; endure, endure securely: hear the Gospel that was just read: *You will be blessed, when men will have persecuted you, and said against you every evil, lying, for my name's sake*[^37]. And there he added for what reason, lest you receive curses by your own desert, not for the cause of the justifications of God. For he who is cursed is not just: but he who is just, and is unjustly cursed; and if he is unjustly cursed, a reward is rendered to him. Therefore, then, be securely merciful, extend your love even to enemies: those who perhaps pertain to your governance, avenge, restrain with love, with charity, attending to eternal salvation; lest, while you spare the flesh, the soul perish. Do this: and you will suffer from many on whom you cannot exercise discipline, because they do not pertain to the rights of your governance; bear the injuries, be secure. *For the Lord will do mercies, and judgment for all who receive injury.* So he will do mercy in you, if you have shown mercy: so will you be merciful, that the injury you suffer not be unpunished: *Vengeance is mine, and I will repay*[^38], says the Lord.

**15.** [v. 7.] *He made his ways known to Moses.* What ways of his did he make known to Moses? Why did he choose Moses? From Moses, understand all the just, all the saints: he set down one, let all come to mind. Yet through Moses the Law was given, and the very handing down of the Law has something obscure. For the Law was given for this purpose, that the sick one might be convicted, and implore the physician. This is the hidden way of God. You had heard a while ago, *Who heals all your weaknesses.* Weaknesses lay hidden in the sick, the five books of Moses were given: the pool was surrounded by five porticoes; it brought forth the sick, that they might lie there, that they might be exposed, not that they might be healed. The five porticoes exposed the sick, they did not cure; the pool cured when one went down, and this when the pool was stirred: the stirring of the pool is in the passion of the Lord. For coming and made unknown, when it is said by some, He is the Christ; by others, He is not the Christ; he is just, he is a sinner; he is a teacher, he is a seducer: he stirred the water, that is, he stirred the people; and in all that disturbance of the water one was healed, because in the passion of the Lord unity is healed. Whoever is outside unity, even if he lies in the porticoes, cannot be healed: even if he holds the Law, he does not reach salvation. So because this mystery is there, therefore he teaches that the Law was given, that sinners might be convicted, and, to receive grace, might call upon the physician. Whence that man was convicted whom the apostle Paul transfigures into himself, saying, *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?*[^39] For through the commandment a certain strife had been shown to him within himself, whence he says, *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive under the law of sin, which is in my members.* He knew himself to be in misery, in groaning, in strife and contention; himself not at one with himself, dissonant from himself, springing back from himself: and what does he say, longing for peace, true peace, supernal peace? *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord.* For *where sin abounded, grace superabounded.* But whence did sin abound? *But the Law entered in, that sin might abound*[^40]. Why, the Law entering in, did sin abound? Because men were unwilling to confess themselves sinners, with the Law added they became transgressors too. For no one is a transgressor, except when he has transgressed a law. The Apostle himself says this: *For where there is no law, there is no transgression*[^41]. Sin abounded, then, that grace might superabound. Therefore, as I had begun to say, because this is the great mystery in the Law, therefore it was given, that, sin increasing, the proud might be humbled, the humbled might confess, the confessing might be healed: these are the hidden ways which he made known to Moses, through whom he gave the Law, by which sin might abound, that grace might superabound. God did not do this cruelly, but with the counsel of medicine. For sometimes a man seems healthy to himself, and is sick; and in that he is sick and does not feel it, he does not seek the physician: the disease grows, the trouble increases, the physician is sought, and the whole is healed. *He made his ways known to Moses; his wishes to the sons of Israel.* To all the sons of Israel? But to the true sons of Israel; or rather to all the sons of Israel. For those who are deceitful, who are treacherous, who are hypocrites, are not sons of Israel. And who are sons of Israel? *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^42]. *His wishes to the sons of Israel.*

**16.** [v. 8.] *The Lord is compassionate and merciful, long-suffering and abounding in mercy.* What is so long-suffering? what so abounding in mercy? One sins, and lives; sins are added, life is increased: he is blasphemed daily, and *he makes his sun rise on the good and the bad*[^43]. He calls from every quarter to correction, he calls from every quarter to repentance, he calls by the benefits of creation, he calls by imparting time for living, he calls through the reader, he calls through the expositor, he calls through inward thought, he calls through the scourge of rebuke, he calls through the mercy of consolation: *long-suffering and abounding in mercy.* But beware lest, by ill use of the length of God's mercy, you store up for yourself, as the Apostle says, wrath on the day of wrath. For this the Apostle says: *Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and long-suffering, not knowing that the patience of God leads you to repentance?* Because he spares you, do you suppose that you please him? *These things you did*, he says, *and I was silent; you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you.* Sins do not please me, but by long-suffering I seek right deeds. If I punished sinners, I would not find confessors. Therefore God, by his long-suffering, in sparing leads you to repentance: but you, when you say daily, "Today is ending, and so I will be tomorrow too, for tomorrow will not be the last"; and on the third day: and suddenly his wrath comes. Brother, do not delay to be converted to the Lord. For there are those who prepare their conversion, and put it off, and there becomes in them the crow's voice, "Tomorrow, tomorrow." The crow sent from the ark did not return. God does not seek delay in the crow's voice, but confession in the dove's groaning. The dove sent returned. How long: Tomorrow, tomorrow? Watch for the last tomorrow: because you do not know which is the last tomorrow, let it suffice that you have lived as a sinner up to today. You have heard, you are often wont to hear, you have heard today too: as daily as you hear it, so daily you are not corrected. *For you, according to your hardness of heart and impenitent heart, are storing up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works*[^44]. Let God not seem to you so merciful as not to seem just. *The Lord is merciful and compassionate.* I hear, and I rejoice: so you say. Hear, and rejoice; he has added still: *long-suffering and very merciful*: and at the end: *And truthful.* You rejoice at the earlier words, at the last tremble. He is merciful and long-suffering in such a way that he is also truthful. When you have stored up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath, will you not experience as just the one you scorned when kindly?

**17.** [v. 9.] *He will not be angry to the end, nor be indignant forever.* For even that we live amid scourges and the corruption of mortality is from his indignation: as punishment we have this from the first sin. My brothers, we ought to think not only how to escape his future threats, but his present wrath too; because this wrath is also his, of whom the Apostle says that both he and we were sons: for he says, *For we too were once by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^45]. So it is from his wrath that man here is a pilgrim, that he labors. Is it not from his wrath, my brothers: *In sweat and in labor you will eat your bread, and the earth will bring forth thorns and thistles for you*[^46]? This was said to our author. Or if our life is something else; if you can, turn to some pleasure where you do not feel thorns. Choose what you will, the greedy man, the dissolute man, to mention only these two; add a third too, the ambitious man: in the desire of honors how many thorns? in the luxury of lusts how many thorns? in the burning of greed how many thorns? How many troubles have base loves? how many anxieties here in this life? I omit the Gehennas. See lest you are already a Gehenna to yourself. All this, then, my brothers, is from his wrath: and when you have been converted, so as to act justly, you will only be able to labor on the earth; and the labor is not finished, except when the way is finished. We must labor on the way, that we may rejoice in the homeland. Therefore he consoles your labor, your sweat, your troubles by his promise, and says to you: *He will not be angry to the end, nor be indignant forever.*

**18.** [vv. 10, 11.] *He has not done to us according to our sins.* Thanks be to God, that he willed this. We did not receive what we deserved: *He has not done to us according to our sins, nor recompensed us according to our iniquities. For according to the height of heaven from the earth, the Lord has confirmed his mercy upon those who fear him. The Lord has confirmed his mercy upon those who fear him*: according to what? *According to the height of heaven from the earth.* What did he say? If at some time the sky can withdraw from the protection of the earth, at some time God will be able not to protect those who fear him. Attend to the sky too: everywhere, on every side it protects the earth, and there is no part of the earth that is not protected by the sky. Men sin under the sky, they do all evils under the sky; yet they are protected by the sky. Thence light to the eyes, thence air, thence breath, thence rain to the earth for the fruits, thence all mercy from the sky. Take away the help of the sky from the earth, it will immediately fail. As, then, the protection of the sky remains over the earth, so the protection of the Lord remains over those who fear him. You fear God, above you is his protection. But perhaps you are scourged, and you suppose that God has deserted you. As if the protection of the sky had deserted the earth: *For according to the height of heaven from the earth, the Lord has confirmed his mercy upon those who fear him.*

**19.** [v. 12.] And what did he do? Because he did not recompense us according to our sins. *As far as the east is distant from the west, he has put our sins far from us.* According to the height of heaven from the earth, the Lord has confirmed his mercy upon us. I have said why: because of the protection. Whence? *As far as the east is distant from the west, he has made our sins to be far from us.* Those who know the sacraments know; yet I say what all can hear. When sin is remitted, your sins set, your grace rises: your sins are as it were in their setting; the grace by which you are freed is in its rising. *Truth has arisen from the earth*[^47]. What is it, *Truth has arisen from the earth?* Your grace is born, your sins set, you are in a way made new. You ought to attend to the rising, you ought to turn away from the setting. Turn away from your sins, be converted to the grace of God; as they set, you rise and make progress. But the part of the sky that rises goes again to its setting. Likenesses cannot be drawn to perfection from every side; the things themselves can be lined up with the things to which they are applied: as he said of the eagle, as of the moon, so here too. One part of the sky sets, another rises: but the part that rises now will set after twelve hours. Not so is the grace that rises for us: both the sins set forever, and the grace abides forever.

**20.** [v. 13.] But why *As far as the east is distant from the west*, so far *he has made our sins to be from us*, that they may set, and grace rise? why do you suppose? *As a father has compassion on his sons, so the Lord has had compassion on those who fear him.* Now let him rage as much as he wishes, he is a father. But he has scourged us, and afflicted us, and crushed us: he is a father. Son, if you weep, weep under a father; do not weep with indignation, do not weep with the pride of arrogance. What you suffer, what you lament, is medicine, not punishment; it is chastisement, not condemnation. Do not push back the scourge, if you do not wish to be pushed back from the inheritance: do not attend to what punishment you have in the scourge, but what place in the testament. *As a father has compassion on his sons, so the Lord has had compassion on those who fear him.*

**21.** [v. 14.] *For he himself knew our formation*: that is, our weakness. He knew what he had made, how it had fallen, how it must be restored, how it must be adopted, how it must be enriched. Behold, we were made of mud: *The first man from the earth, earthly; the second man from heaven, heavenly*[^48]. He sent his Son too, who became the second man, who before all is God. Second in coming, first in returning: he died after many, he rose before all. *He himself knew our formation*: what formation? Us. Whence do you say that he knew? Because he had compassion. *Remember that we are dust.* Turned to God himself, he says, *Remember*, as if God forgets: he so knows, he so is aware, that he does not forget. But what is it, *Remember?* Let your mercy persevere toward us. You knew our formation in a certain way; do not forget our formation, that we may not forget your grace. *Remember that we are dust.*

**22.** [v. 15.] *Man, his days are like hay.* Let him consider what man is; let man not be proud: *his days are like hay.* Why is the hay proud, now flowering, after a little while withering? why is the hay proud, green for a time, and this for a short time, until the sun grows hot? It is good for us, then, that his mercy be upon us, and that he make gold out of the hay. For *man, his days are like hay; like the flower of the field, so will he flower forth.* The whole splendor of the human race; honors, powers, riches, pride, threats, are the flower of the hay. That house flowers, and that great house, that family flowers; and how many flower, or for how many years do they live? Many years to you; a short time to God. God does not count as you count. In comparison with the long and long-living ages, every flower of any house is as the flower of the field. The whole beauty of the year scarcely lasts a year. Whatever there flourishes, whatever there gleams, whatever there is beautiful, does not last the year; rather it cannot be carried through the whole year. In how short a time the flowers pass, and this is the beautiful thing in the plants. That which is very beautiful, this quickly falls. *All flesh is hay, and the glory of man as the flower of hay. The hay has withered, and the flower has fallen: but the Word of the Lord abides forever*[^49]. Because, then, just as the Father knew our formation, that we are hay, and can flower for a time; he sent us his Word, and his Word which abides forever he made a brother to the hay which does not abide forever: by nature the Only-begotten, the only one born of his substance, he made a brother to so many adopted brothers. Do not wonder that you will be a partaker of his eternity; he himself first became a partaker of your hay. Will he deny you what is lofty from you, who took up from you what was lowly? Therefore *man*, as far as concerns man, *his days are like hay; like the flower of the field, so will he flower.*

**23.** [v. 16.] *For the spirit will pass through in him, and he will not be, and he will no longer know his place.* As if a certain perdition, as if a certain ruin. Behold the one who inflates himself, behold the one who swells, behold the one who exalts himself: *The spirit will pass through in him, and he will not be, and he will no longer know his place.* See men dying daily; and this will be the whole, this will be the end. For he does not address the hay, but that for which the Word too became hay. For you are man; but for your sake the Word too became man: you are flesh, and for your sake the Word became flesh. *All flesh is hay, and the Word became flesh.* How great, then, is the hope of the hay, when the Word became flesh? That which abides forever did not disdain to take up the hay, lest the hay despair of itself.

**24.** [v. 17.] Therefore as for what you attend to in yourself, think of your lowliness, think of your dust; do not be exalted: whatever better you will be, you will be it by his grace, you will be it by his mercy. For hear what follows: *But the mercy of the Lord is from age and unto the age, upon those who fear him.* You who do not fear him, you are hay, and you will be in the hay, and in torment with the hay: for the flesh will rise again to torment. Let those rejoice who fear him, because upon them is his mercy.

**25.** [v. 18.] *And his righteousness upon the sons of sons.* He means the recompense, *upon the sons of sons.* How many are the servants of God who have no sons, much less *sons of sons?* But by our sons he means our works; by *sons of sons*, the reward of our works. *His righteousness upon the sons of sons, for those who keep his covenant.* Let them see lest all suppose that what is said pertains to them: let them choose, while it is permitted. *For those who keep*, he says, *his covenant, and retain in memory his commandments, to do them.* You were now arranging to exalt yourself, and perhaps to recite to me the Psalter, which I do not hold by heart, or to pronounce the whole Law from memory. Plainly you are better than I in memory, better than any just man, if the just man does not hold the Law word for word: but see that you keep the precepts. But how should you keep them? Not by memory, but by life. *For those who retain in memory his commandments*: not that they may recite them; but, *that they may do them.* Now perhaps each one's soul is disturbed. Who holds all the commandments of God? who holds all the Scriptures of God? Behold, I wish not only to hold them in memory, but to do them by my works: but who holds all in memory? Do not fear; it does not burden you. *On two precepts the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets*[^50]. But I wish to hold the whole Law. Hold it, if you can, when you can, how you can. Whatever page you question, this will answer you: What you hold, hold; hold love: *The end of the precept is love*[^51]. Do not think of the multitude of branches; hold the root, and the whole tree is in you. *And for those who retain in memory his commandments to do them.*

**26.** [v. 19.] *The Lord has prepared his throne in heaven.* Who, except Christ, has prepared his throne in heaven? He who descended and ascended, who died and rose again, who lifted the manhood he assumed into heaven, he himself has prepared his throne in heaven. A throne is the seat of a judge: observe, then, you who hear, that *he has prepared his throne in heaven.* Let each one do what he wishes on earth, sin will not be unpunished, righteousness will not be unfruitful; because the Lord, who before the throne of a human judge was mocked, has prepared his throne in heaven. *The Lord has prepared his throne in heaven; and his kingdom will have dominion over all.* The kingdom is the Lord's, and he will have dominion over the nations. *And his kingdom will have dominion over all.*

**27.** [v. 20.] *Bless the Lord, all his angels, mighty in strength, doing his word.* So you are not yet just or faithful by the word of God, except when you do it. *Mighty in strength, doing his word, to hear the voice of his words.*

**28.** [v. 21.] *Bless the Lord, all his hosts, his ministers, doing his will.* All the angels, all the mighty in strength, doing his word, all his hosts, all his ministers, doing his will, you, you bless the Lord. For all who live badly, even if they are silent with the tongue, curse the Lord by their life. What does it profit that your tongue sings a hymn, if your life breathes out sacrilege? By living badly you have sent many tongues into blasphemy. Your tongue is free for the hymn, and the tongues of others who look at you are free for blasphemies. If, then, you wish to bless the Lord, do his word, do his will. Build on rock, do not build on sand. To hear and not to do is to build on sand: to hear and to do is to build on rock: neither to hear nor to do is to build nothing. If you build on sand, you build a ruin: if you build nothing, exposed to the rain, the rivers, the winds, you will be snatched away before you stand. Therefore one must not cease, but build: nor build in such a way that a ruin is built; but build on rock, that temptation may not overthrow it. If it is so, bless the Lord: if it is not so, do not flatter your tongue; question your life, let it answer you. You find what evil you are; groan, confess: your confession can bless the Lord; but let your change persevere in the blessing.

**29.** [v. 22.] *Bless the Lord, all his works, in every place of his dominion.* Therefore in every place. Let him not be blessed there where he does not have dominion: *In every place of his dominion.* Lest perhaps someone say: I cannot bless the Lord in the east, because he has set out to the west; or, "I cannot in the west, because he is in the east." *Not from the east and from the west, not from the desert mountains; for God is judge*[^52]. He is everywhere in such a way that he is blessed everywhere: he is on every side in such a way that he is shouted to with joy on every side: he is blessed on every side in such a way that one lives well on every side. *Bless the Lord, all his works.* Because when you have begun, in a good life, to bless the Lord, his works bless him, not your merits. For he works the good through you and in you, as the Apostle says, *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation; for it is God who works in you*[^53]. Therefore, lest you exalt yourself, because you do his word, because you do his will, he willed you to be humbled, by looking back to his grace, whence you obtained this. *In every place of his dominion. Bless the Lord, O my soul.* This last verse is the same as the first: blessing at the head, blessing at the end; from blessing we set out, to blessing let us return, in blessing let us reign.
