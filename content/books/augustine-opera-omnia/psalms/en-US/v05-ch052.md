# On the Same Psalm 118 (27)

**1.** [vv. 137-138.] He who sings this psalm had said above: *Outpourings of waters my eyes have run down, for they did not keep your law;* where he testified that he had wept much for his transgression. Accordingly, as though giving the reason why he ought to weep much and grievously to grieve over his sin: *You are righteous,* he says, *O Lord, and right is your judgment. You have commanded righteousness as your testimonies, and your truth exceedingly.* This righteousness of God, surely, and his right judgment and truth, is to be feared by every sinner: for by it are condemned divinely whoever are condemned; nor is there at all anyone who can rightly complain against the righteous God about his own condemnation. Hence the weeping of the penitent is right; since, if his impenitent heart were condemned, it would surely be most justly condemned. He calls the testimonies of God righteousness, indeed: for God proves himself righteous by commanding righteousness. This too is truth, that God becomes known by such testimonies.

**2.** [v. 139.] But what is it that follows: *My zeal has made me waste away;* or, as other manuscripts have, *your zeal?* Some have also *of your house;* and not *has made me waste away,* but *has consumed me.* Which, as it seems to me, was thought to need correcting from another psalm, where it is written: *The zeal of your house has consumed me*[^1]; which we know is mentioned in the Gospel too[^2]. Yet *has made me waste away* is something similar to what is there, *has consumed me.* But *my zeal,* which more manuscripts have, raises no question: for what wonder is it if anyone wastes away by his own zeal? But what some have, *your zeal,* signifies a man zealous for God, not for himself; yet it is not inconsistent if it is called also *mine.* For what else does the Apostle say: *For I am jealous over you with the jealousy of God?* For by saying, *I am jealous over you,* what does he show, except his own jealousy? But because he said, *of God*—that is, not for himself, but for God—therefore he added, *with the jealousy of God.* For this God inspires by his Spirit into his faithful: for it is of love, not of envy. For what was the care that the Apostle should say this? *For I have betrothed you,* he says, *to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ: but I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so your minds too be corrupted from the simplicity and chastity that is in Christ*[^3]. The zeal of the house of God was consuming him, which, however, he was zealous for unto Christ, not for himself. For the bridegroom is zealous for his bride for himself: but the friend of the bridegroom ought to be zealous for her not for himself, but for the bridegroom. Good, then, is this man's zeal too to be understood: for he adds the cause and says, *Because my enemies have forgotten your words.* They were repaying evil for good, then; since he was zealous for them unto God so vehemently and ardently that he said he had been wasted away by that zeal; but they, on account of this, exercised enmities against him: because surely he wished those to love God whom, by loving, he was zealous for. For not ungrateful for the grace of God, by which he had been reconciled to God from an enemy, he too loved his own enemies, and was zealous for them unto God, grieving and wasting away that they had forgotten his words.

**3.** [v. 140.] Then, himself considering with what flame of love he burned in the words of God: *Your utterance,* he says, *is fiery exceedingly, and your servant has loved it.* Rightly he was zealous, in his enemies, against the impenitent heart of those who had forgotten the words of God: for he burned to lead them to this, which he himself loved most ardently.

**4.** [v. 141.] *I am younger,* he says, *and despised; I have not forgotten your statutes:* not like my enemies, who have forgotten your words. But the younger in age, who has not forgotten the statutes of God, seems to grieve for his enemies who are greater in age and have forgotten. For what is, *I, the younger, have not forgotten,* except: Those greater ones have forgotten? For there is in Greek a word which is also in that place where it was said, *Wherein does a younger man correct his way*[^4]? But this word is comparative, and therefore is well understood in comparison with a greater. Let us recognize here, then, two peoples, who struggled even in Rebecca's womb; when, *not of works, but of him who calls, it was said to her, The greater will serve the lesser*[^5]. But the lesser here says he is despised; therefore he was made the greater: because God chose the ignoble and contemptible things of the world, and the things that are not, as though they were, that the things that are might be brought to nothing[^6]; and behold, those are last who were first, and first who were last.

**5.** [v. 142.] And not without reason did they forget the words of God who wished to establish their own righteousness, ignorant of the righteousness of God; but this younger one has not forgotten, who wished to have not his own righteousness, but God's, of which even now he says: *Your righteousness is righteousness for ever, and your law is truth.* For how is the law not truth, through which is the knowledge of sin, and which bears witness to the righteousness of God? For thus the Apostle says: *The righteousness of God has been manifested, witnessed by the Law and the Prophets*[^7].

**6.** [v. 143.] On account of this the younger suffered persecution from the elder, so that the younger said what follows: *Tribulation and necessity have found me; your commandments are my meditation.* Let them rage, let them persecute; provided only that the commandments of God are not forsaken, and that, out of those very commandments, even those who rage are loved.

**7.** [v. 144.] *Your testimonies are righteousness for ever; give me understanding, and I shall live.* This younger one asks for understanding; which, if he did not have, he would not understand above his elders: but he asks it in tribulation and necessity, that he may understand how to be despised is that which the persecuting enemies can take from him—by whom he says he is despised. Therefore he said, *and I shall live:* because if tribulation and necessity reach so far that, through the hands of persecuting enemies, this life is ended; he will live for ever, who prefers to temporal things the righteousness which abides for ever. Which righteousness, in tribulation and necessity, is the martyria of God, that is, the testimonies, for which the martyrs were crowned.
