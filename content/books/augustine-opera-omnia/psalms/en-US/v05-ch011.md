# Exposition of Psalm 105

**1.** The hundred and fifth psalm too is itself prefaced with *Alleluia*: and this twice. But some say that one Alleluia pertains to the end of the preceding psalm, the other to the beginning of this one. And they assert this, that all the Alleluiatic psalms have Alleluia at the end, not all at the head: whence whatever psalm does not have Alleluia at the end, they hold that it ought not to have it at the head either; but what seems to be at its head pertains to the end of the preceding one. But we, until they persuade us by some certain proofs that this is true, follow the custom of many, who, wherever they read Alleluia, attribute it to that psalm at whose head they find it. For there are very few manuscripts (which indeed I have found in none of the Greek ones I was able to inspect) that have Alleluia at the end of the hundred and fiftieth psalm; after which there is now none that pertains to the same canon. But not even this could prescribe to custom, even if all manuscripts had it. For it could be that, for some reason of the praise of God, the whole book of Psalms, which is said to consist of five books (for where it is written, *So be it, so be it*, there they say are the ends of the books), after all that has been sung, is closed at the last with Alleluia: nor do I see it necessary, because of the end of the hundred and fiftieth psalm, that all the Alleluiatic psalms have Alleluia at the end. But when at the head of a psalm the Alleluia is doubled, why the Lord sometimes once, sometimes twice says *Amen,* and in that way *Alleluia* cannot sometimes once, sometimes twice be said, I do not know: especially since, after the mark of the number by which the psalm is inscribed as to which it is, like this hundred and fifth, both Alleluias are set. But one ought to have been set before the number itself, if it pertains to the end of the preceding one; and after the number of the Psalm the other Alleluia written, which pertains to the Psalm of that number. But perhaps in this too unskilled custom has prevailed, and something can be brought forward which we do not yet know, whence the judgment of truth ought rather to teach us than the prejudice of custom. Now, however, before we learn this thoroughly, wherever after the number of the psalm, whether once or twice, we find Alleluia written, according to the most celebrated custom of the Church, we attribute it to that psalm which is prefaced with the same number; confessing that we both believe the secrets of all the titles which are in the Psalms, and of the order of those same Psalms, to be great, and that we have not yet been able, as we wish, to penetrate them.

**2.** But I see these two, the hundred and fourth and the hundred and fifth, so joined to each other, that in one of them, which precedes, the people of God is commended in his elect, of whom there is no complaint, who I judge were there in whom God was well pleased: but in this one which follows, those are commemorated who in the same people embittered him; and yet not even to them was the mercy of God lacking. But these things are said from the person of those who, converted, pray for pardon; and the examples of those are commemorated to whom, even sinners, the rich mercy of God appeared. This psalm too, then, begins as that one: *Confess to the Lord.* But there follows: *And call upon his name*; but here: *For he is good, for his mercy is unto the age.* Wherefore here indeed even a confession of sins can be understood; for after a few verses there follows too: *We have sinned with our fathers, we have acted unjustly, we have committed iniquity*: but in that he says, *For he is good, for his mercy is unto the age*, it is certainly the praise of God, and in his praise a confession. Although even when each one confesses his own sins, he ought to confess with the praise of God; nor otherwise is the confession of sins pious unless it is not despairing, and asks the mercy of God. It has, then, his praise, whether even in words, when it calls him good and merciful; or in the affection alone, when it believes this. For even that publican, of whom those words alone were commemorated, *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^1], though he did not say, "Because you are good and merciful," or something of the sort; yet he would not say that, unless he believed this; since he prayed with hope, which could not be without that faith. There can be, then, a true and pious praise of God, where there is no confession of sins; which praise is much more frequently called confession in the Scriptures: but there is no pious and useful confession of sins, where God is not praised, whether in heart, or even with mouth and speech. But what other manuscripts have, *For he is good;* others have, *For he is sweet*: so one Greek word, which is chrestos, a different translation has followed. Likewise what was said, *For his mercy is unto the age;* the Greek has eis ton aiona, which can also be translated *forever.* Accordingly, if that mercy is here understood by which no one can be blessed without God; we better take *forever*: but if it is that mercy which is shown to the wretched, that they may either be consoled in their wretchedness, or even be freed from it; better *unto the age*, that is, to the end of the age, in which there will not be lacking wretched ones to whom mercy may be shown. Unless perhaps someone dares to say that even to those who will be damned with the devil and his angels, some mercy of God will by no means be lacking; not such as would free them from that damnation, but that it would be somewhat mitigated for them: and so the eternal mercy of God could be understood over their eternal wretchedness. But that some will receive a more tolerable damnation in comparison with certain others, we read; but who would boldly say that the punishment of anyone to whom he has been handed over is mitigated, or has some pause at certain intervals, since that rich man did not deserve one drop? But of this so great a matter we must discuss more diligently at leisure; now, as far as concerns this psalm, let what has been said of it thus far suffice.

**3.** [vv. 2, 3.] *Who will speak the powers of the Lord?* Filled with the consideration of the divine works, he who begs his mercy: *Who*, he says, *will speak the powers of the Lord, will make heard all his praises?* What was said above is to be understood, so that this sentence too is full: Who *will make heard all his praises?* that is, who is sufficient to make heard all his praises? *Will make heard*, he said, that is, will cause them to be heard; showing that the powers of the Lord and his praises are to be spoken in such a way that they be proclaimed to hearers. But who can do all? Or perhaps, because there follows: *Blessed are those who keep judgment, and do righteousness in all time*, he called those his praises which are understood as his works in his precepts? *For it is God*, says the Apostle, *who works in you*[^2]. And it was said to the seed of Abraham: *Sing to him, and sing psalms to him*: which we understood as said as if it were said, "Both speak and do good things in his praise." With which two words, that is, of singing and singing psalms, the two following verses agree, so that what was said, *Tell all his wonders;* this is, *Sing to him*: but what was said, *Be praised in his holy name*; this is, *Sing psalms to him.* To this seed indeed the Lord himself says: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^3]. Here, then, considering the very precepts of God, the works of which precepts are the praises of him who works in his own, he says: *Who will speak the powers of the Lord?* since he works these things ineffably. Who *will make heard all his praises?* that is, who, when he has heard, does all his praises? which are the works of his precepts. Because, insofar as they are done—even if not all the things that are heard are done—he is to be praised, *who works in us both to will and to work, for his good will*[^4]. Therefore, though he could say, All his commandments, or, all the works of his commandments; he preferred to say, *his praises*: because, as was said, insofar as they are done, he himself is to be praised. Yet these praises, who is sufficient to make heard? that is, when they have been heard, who is fit to do all?

**4.** *Blessed are those who keep judgment, and do righteousness in all time*: certainly from when they begin and live in time. For he who perseveres to the end, he will be saved. But it can seem a repetition of the same sentence, so that to do righteousness is the same as to keep judgment: so that even in the earlier verse is understood, *in all time;* as in the later is understood, *blessed;* and, the understood words being restored, it is said thus: *Blessed are those who keep judgment* in all time, blessed are those who *do righteousness in all time.* But unless there were some difference between judgment and righteousness, it would not be said in another psalm: *Until righteousness be turned into judgment*[^5]. Scripture indeed loves to set these two together: as is, *Righteousness and judgment are the direction of his seat*[^6]; and that: *And he will bring forth your righteousness as light, and your judgment as noonday*[^7]; when there too a repetition of the same sentence seems to appear. And perhaps because of the nearness of signification one can even be set for the other, either judgment for righteousness, or righteousness for judgment: yet if they are spoken properly, I do not doubt that there is some difference, so that he is said to keep judgment who judges rightly, but to do righteousness who acts rightly. Nor do I think it absurd to understand, according to what was said, *Until righteousness be turned into judgment*, that here too those are called blessed who keep judgment in faith, do righteousness in work. For a time will come that the judgment which is now kept in faith will also be exercised in work, when righteousness will have been turned into judgment, that is, when the just will have received the power of judging rightly those by whom they are now not rightly judged. Whence elsewhere the body of Christ itself is understood to say: *When I have received the time, I will judge righteousnesses*[^8]. Which would be said more word for word, I will judge equities. But he did not say: When I have received the time, I will do righteousness; because it is to be done in all time, as he says here too: *Those who do righteousness in all time.*

**5.** [vv. 4, 5.] Then, because God justifies, that is, makes just, by healing them from their iniquities, there follows a prayer: *Remember us, O Lord, in the good pleasure of your people*: that is, that we may be among those in whom you are well pleased; because God is not well pleased with all of them. *Visit us in your salvation.* For he is the Savior, in whom sins are forgiven, and souls healed, that they may be able to keep judgment, and do righteousness; whom, since those who speak these things understood to be blessed, they consequently ask this for themselves in prayer. Of this salvation it is said elsewhere: *That we may know on earth your way*: and as if we were asking on what earth, there follows: *Among all the nations;* again, as if we were asking what way, there follows: *Your salvation*[^9]. For of him the old man Simeon said: *For my eyes have seen your salvation*[^10]; who said of himself: *I am the way*[^11]. *Visit*, then, *us in your salvation,* that is, in your Christ. *To see in the goodness of your elect, to rejoice in the gladness of your nation*: that is, visit us for this in your salvation, that we may see in the goodness of your elect, and rejoice in the gladness of your nation. But what is here set, *in goodness;* other manuscripts have, *in sweetness*: as that, *For he is good*; others have, *For he is sweet.* But it is the very same word in the Greek that is read elsewhere too: *The Lord will give sweetness*[^12]; which some too have translated goodness, some kindness. But what is it, *Visit us, that we may see in the goodness of your elect*, that is, in that goodness which you bestow on your elect; except that we should not remain blind, like those to whom it was said: *But now you say: We see; your sin remains*[^13]? For *the Lord enlightens the blind*[^14], not by their merits, but *in the goodness of his elect,* that is, which he shows or gives to his elect: as *the salvation of my countenance*, not from myself, but *my God*[^15]; and we say *our daily bread,* but yet we add, *give us*[^16]. *Visit*, then, *us in your salvation, to see,* that is, that we may see, *in the goodness of your elect; to rejoice,* that is, that we may rejoice, *in the gladness of your nation.* We ought to understand the one nation of God to be the whole seed of Abraham; but the sons of the promise, not of the flesh. These, then, whose voice it is, desire to have the gladness of the same nation. And what is the gladness of this nation, except its God? To whom it is said: *My exultation, redeem me*[^17]: and to whom it is said: *The light of your countenance is signed upon us, O Lord; you have given gladness into my heart*[^18]; namely, the highest, true, unchangeable, and beatifying good, which God himself is. *That you may be praised with your inheritance.* I wonder that this verse is so translated in many manuscripts, since there is one and the same Greek expression in these three verses, so that if this is rightly said which is read, *That you may be praised with your inheritance*; it could rightly be said, *That you may see in the goodness of your elect, and rejoice in the gladness of your nation*: the whole sense being woven thus: *Visit us in your salvation, that you may see in the goodness of your elect, that you may rejoice in the gladness of your nation, and be praised with your inheritance.* But according to what we said, *Visit us, that we may see in the goodness of your elect, and rejoice in the gladness of your nation;* consequently here too it ought to have been said, *That we may be praised with your inheritance*: to which inheritance it was said, *Be praised in his holy name*[^19]. But further, since this expression seems ambiguous; if that meaning is true by which the translators preferred to say, *That you may be praised*; then the two earlier verses too are to be understood thus, because, as I said, there is one Greek expression in these three verses: so that this whole is taken as said thus: *Visit us in your salvation, that you may see in the goodness of your elect,* that is, for this visit us, that you may make us be there, and there see us; *that you may rejoice in the gladness of your nation,* that is, that you be said to rejoice, when they rejoice from you; *that you may be praised with your inheritance,* that is, that you be praised with it, since it is not praised except for your sake. Whether, then, in that way or in this it is to be understood, what was said, *to see, to rejoice, to be praised*; for this reason they desire to be visited in the salvation of God, that is, in his Christ, that they may not be estranged from his people, and from those in whom God is well pleased.

**6.** [vv. 6, 7.] But what they next confess, let us hear: *We have sinned with our fathers, we have acted unjustly, we have committed iniquity.* What is it, *with our fathers?* Or, as the Epistle to the Hebrews has, that Levi too was tithed with Abraham, since he was in his loins when he gave tithes to the priest Melchizedek; so these too sinned with their fathers, in whose loins they were when those fathers were in Egypt? For those who were at that time when this psalm was written, and especially their descendants (because it could either be said by those who were then, or be prophesied about those who would be afterward), were far from the age of those who sinned in Egypt, not understanding the wonders of God. For this follows, expounding how they sinned with their fathers: *Our fathers*, he says, *in Egypt did not understand your wonders;* and the rest, which he commemorates much of their sins. Or is it rather to be taken thus, what he says, *We have sinned with our fathers*, as if he said: We have sinned as our fathers did, namely by imitating their sins? But if it is so, it must be supported by some example of such an expression: which, when I sought it for the present, did not occur to me, that anyone says he sinned with that man, or did something with that man, whom in a similar deed he imitated even after much time.

**7.** What is it, then: *Our fathers did not understand your wonders;* except, they did not come to know what through those wonders you wished to bestow on them? What, certainly, except eternal life, and the not temporal but unchangeable good, which through patience is awaited? Therefore impatiently they murmured, and were embittered, and sought to be made blessed by present goods, deceitful and fleeting. *They were not mindful of the multitude of your mercy.* He reproves both the understanding, and the memory. For there was need of understanding, that they might think to the eternity of which goods God was calling them through those temporal things: but of memory, that they might at least not forget the wonders done temporally, and faithfully presume that, by the same power they had already experienced, God would free them from the persecution of their enemies; but they forgot what he had bestowed on them in Egypt, to crush their enemies through such great prodigies. *And they provoked, going up in the sea, the Red Sea.* The manuscript I was looking at had it thus: and indeed by those two last words, where it was said, *the Red Sea,* a star had been prefixed; by which are signified the things that are in the Hebrew, and are not in the translation of the Seventy. But more manuscripts, which I was able to inspect, both Greek and Latin, have it thus: *And they provoked,* or, what is more expressed from the Greek: *And they embittered, going up in the Red Sea.* Whoever reads that history, when they went out of Egypt, and crossed through the Red Sea, grieves at their unbelief, in how great trepidation and despair they were, after so many and so great recent miracles in Egypt; of the multitude of whose mercy of God he says they were not mindful. But *they went up* was said for this reason, that such is the position of the land, that going from the land of Canaan into Egypt is called a descent, and from there into it an ascent. It is to be noted indeed how Scripture wished to blame both not understanding what is to be understood, and not remembering what is to be retained in memory: which men are unwilling to be reckoned to their own fault, for no other end than that they may pray less, and be less humble to God, in whose sight they should confess what they are, and, help obtained, be able to be what they are not. For even sins of ignorance or negligence are better accused that they may perish, than excused that they may remain; and are better purged by calling upon God, than confirmed by provoking him.

**8.** [v. 8.] Yet he adds that God did not act according to their unbelief. *And he saved them*, he says, *for his name's sake, that he might make known his power*: not for any good merits of theirs.

**9.** [v. 9.] *And he rebuked the Red Sea, and it was dried up.* We do not read of any voice sent from heaven by which the sea was rebuked; but he called the divine power by which it was done a rebuke: unless perhaps someone says it was rebuked secretly, in such a way that the water could hear, and men could not. Very hidden and abstruse is the force by which God acts, so that even those things which lack perception immediately obey his will. *And he led them out in the deeps, as in a desert.* By deeps he meant a multitude of waters. For some, wishing to translate this whole little verse, said: *And he led them out in many waters.* What is it, then, *in the deeps, as in a desert,* except that it had been made, by dryness, like a desert, where there had been deeps of waters?

**10.** [v. 10.] *And he saved them from the hand of those who hated them.* This verse some translated by a circumlocution, avoiding less Latin words: *And he made them safe from the hand of those who hated them. And he redeemed them from the hand of the enemy.* What price was given in this redemption? Or is it a prophecy, that this was done in figure of Baptism, where we are redeemed from the hand of the devil at a great price, which is the blood of Christ? Whence not by just any sea, but by the Red Sea this was more fittingly figured: for blood has a red color.

**11.** [v. 11.] *And the water covered those who troubled them; not one of them remained*: not of all the Egyptians, but of those who were pursuing the departed, desiring to seize or kill them.

**12.** [v. 12.] *And they believed in his words.* The expression seems less Latin, because he does not say: His words, or, in his words (accusative), but, *in his words* (ablative); yet it is most usual in the Scriptures. *And they praised his praise.* Such are the expressions, when we say: He served this servitude, he lived such a life. But he commemorates that most known praise of God, where it is said: *Let us sing to the Lord; for he is gloriously magnified: horse and rider he cast into the sea*[^20].

**13.** [v. 13.] *They quickly did, they forgot his works.* Other manuscripts have it more intelligibly: *They hastened, they forgot his works: they did not endure his counsel.* For they ought to have considered that God's so great works toward them were not empty, but called to some endless happiness, which is to be endured through patience; but they hastened to be made blessed by temporal things, which for this reason confer true happiness on no one, because they do not extinguish insatiable cupidity: *For whoever drinks*, he says, *of this water, will thirst again*[^21].

**14.** [v. 14.] Finally: *And they desired a desire in the wilderness, and tempted God in the waterless place.* What is it, *in the wilderness;* this was repeated, *in the waterless place,* that is, in a place without water: but what, *they desired a desire;* this, *they tempted God.* Such indeed is the expression: *They desired a desire;* as the earlier: *They praised praise.*

**15.** [v. 15.] *And he gave them their petition*: that is, what they asked by petition. *And he sent fullness into their soul.* Nor did he therefore make them blessed; because that was not the fullness of which it is said: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied*[^22]. And so in this place he said "soul" not according to that by which it is rational, but according to that by which it makes a body an animate animal. To the sustenance of which animal pertain food and drink, according to what is said in the Gospel: *Is not the soul more than food, and the body more than the garment?*[^23] as if to feed pertains to the soul, to be clothed to the body. According to this it is said in Isaiah: *Why is it that we have fasted, and you did not see; we have defrauded our souls, and you did not know?*[^24] The people sinning in the wilderness is afflicted with many plagues.

**16.** [v. 16.] *And they provoked Moses in the camp, Aaron the holy one of the Lord.* What provoking, or, as others more expressly translated, embittering, he means, the consequent things sufficiently indicate.

**17.** [v. 17.] *It was opened*, he says, *and swallowed up Dathan, and covered over the congregation of Abiram.* What is, *swallowed up;* this is, *covered over.* But of both, that is, Dathan and Abiram, there was one cause of a most proud and sacrilegious schism.

**18.** [v. 18.] *And fire blazed up in their synagogue. flame burned up the sinners.* This is not in the Scriptures a usual name for those who, although they live justly and praiseworthily, are not without sin. For rather, just as there is a difference between those who deride and derisers, between those who murmur and murmurers, between those who write and writers, and other similar things; so Scripture is wont to call "sinners" those who are very wicked and burdened with great packs of sins.

**19.** [vv. 19, 20.] *And they made a calf in Horeb; and adored the graven thing. And they changed their glory into the likeness of a calf eating hay.* He does not say, into a likeness (accusative), but, *in the likeness* (ablative). Such is the expression as that where he says: *And they believed in his words.* Elegantly indeed he does not say: And they changed the glory of God, although they did this; as the Apostle too speaks: *And they changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of the image of corruptible man*[^25]: but he said *their glory.* For God was their glory, if they had endured his counsel, and not hastened; to whom it is said: *My glory, and the one who lifts up my head*[^26]. This *their glory*, that is, God, *they changed into the likeness of a calf eating hay,* that they might be eaten by him by whom are eaten those who taste according to the flesh: *For all flesh is hay*[^27].

**20.** [vv. 21, 22.] *They forgot God who saved them.* How did he save them? *Who did great things in Egypt, wonders in the land of Ham, terrible things in the Red Sea.* The things that are wonders, the same are terrible; for there is no wonder without a certain dread: although these things could be called terrible from this too, that they afflicted the adversaries, and showed these people what they ought to fear.

**21.** [v. 23.] *And he said that he would destroy them.* Because they forgot him who made them safe, doing great things, and made and adored the graven thing, by this certainly so monstrous a crime and incredible impiety they were worthy to perish. *He said*, then, *that he would destroy them; if Moses his chosen one had not stood in the breaking in his sight.* He did not say that he stood *in the breaking*, as if to break the wrath of God; but *in the breaking*, that is, in the blow by which they were to be struck: that is, unless he had set himself before them, saying: *If you forgive them their sin, forgive it; but if not, blot me out of your book*[^28]. Where it was shown how much the intercession of the saints avails for others with God. For Moses, secure of the justice of God, by which he could not blot him out, obtained mercy, that he might not blot out those whom he could justly. So he stood *in the breaking in his sight, to turn away his wrath, lest he destroy them.*

**22.** [v. 24.] *And they held the desirable land for nothing.* But had they seen it? How, then, did they hold for nothing what they had not seen, except as there follows: *Nor did they believe in his words?* Surely, unless that land signified something great, which land was called flowing with milk and honey, through which visible sacrament it led to invisible grace and the kingdom of heaven those who understood his wonders; in no way would these be blamed, that they held for nothing that land, whose temporal kingdom we too ought to hold for nothing, that we may love the free Jerusalem our mother, which is in heaven, truly desirable. But rather here unbelief is deservedly reproved, because, in that they held the desirable land for nothing, they did not believe the words of God, leading through certain small things to great, and, hastening to be blessed by temporal things, which they tasted according to the flesh, *they did not endure*, as was said above, *his counsel.*

**23.** [v. 25.] *And they murmured in their tents; they did not hear the voice of the Lord*: vehemently forbidding them from murmuring.

**24.** [vv. 26, 27.] *And he raised his hand over them, to lay them low in the wilderness: and to cast down their seed among the nations, and to scatter them in the regions.*

**25.** [vv. 28, 29.] Here, before he said that anyone interceded against so great an indignation of God, and in some way appeased him, he went on and added. *And they were initiated to Beelphegor;* that is, consecrated to the idol of the gentiles: *And they ate the sacrifices of the dead. And they provoked him in their inventions, and ruin was multiplied among them.* As if he deferred to this the fact that he had raised his hand over them, to lay them low in the wilderness, and to cast down their seed among the nations, and to scatter them in the regions, that, given over to a reprobate sense, they might commit even that, in which by a more monstrous crime they would be punished with evident justice; as the Apostle says: *And as they did not approve to have God in knowledge, God handed them over to a reprobate mind, to do the things that are not fitting*[^29].

**26.** [v. 30.] Finally, so great was their crime, that they were consecrated to an idol, and ate the sacrifices of the dead (that is, because the gentiles sacrificed to dead men as if to gods), that God was unwilling to be appeased otherwise, except in the way that Phinehas the priest appeased him, who killed alike the male and female caught in an adulterous embrace. But if he had done this out of hatred of them, not out of love, while the zeal of the house of God consumed him, it would not be reckoned to him for righteousness. For by this deed, that people of which there was to be a greater destruction, as if one man, he struck as it were with a rod, that he might save his soul from death. The Lord Christ indeed willed the discipline to be milder, the New Testament revealed; but more dreadful is the threat of Gehenna, which then, in those threats of God, we do not read, according to the dispensation of the times. *Ruin was multiplied*, then, *among them,* when for their grave sins they were gravely laid waste. *And Phinehas stood, and appeased, and the shaking ceased.* Briefly he said the whole, because here he does not teach those who do not know, but commemorates to those who know. But what is set here, "shaking," is above the "breaking": for in the Greek it is one word.

**27.** [v. 31.] *And it was reckoned to him for righteousness, unto generation and generation, forever.* God reckoned this to his priest for righteousness, not only as long as there is a generation, but *forever;* who searches the heart, and knows how to weigh with how great a love of the people it was done.

**28.** [vv. 32, 33.] *And they provoked him at the water of contradiction, and Moses was vexed because of them; for they exacerbated his spirit. And he made a distinction with his lips.* What is it, *he made a distinction?* As if God could not do that, who had already done so great things, that water flow forth from the rock. For doubtingly he struck the rock with the rod, and therefore he distinguished this miracle from the other miracles, in which he had not doubted. From this he offended, from this he deserved to hear that he should die, and not enter the land of promise. For, disturbed by the murmur of the unbelieving people, he did not hold the trust such as he ought. To whom yet God, as to his chosen one, even after his death gives good testimony, that we may understand that that wavering of his faith was corrected by this punishment alone, that he was not permitted to enter into that land where he himself was leading the people. But far be it that we believe him estranged from the kingdom of the grace of God, which that land of promise signified, whence milk and honey were said to flow. For this is rather the eternal covenant which he arranged with Abraham, not according to the flesh our father, but according to faith.

**29.** [vv. 34-36.] But those of whose iniquities this psalm speaks, when they had entered into that temporal land of promise, *did not destroy the nations, which the Lord had said to them. And they were mingled among the nations, and learned their works, and served their graven things; and it became a scandal to them.* That they did not destroy them, but were mingled with them, became a scandal to them.

**30.** [vv. 37, 40.] *And they sacrificed their sons and their daughters to demons: and they shed innocent blood, the blood of their sons and their daughters, which they sacrificed to the graven things of Canaan.* That they sacrificed their sons and their daughters to demons and idols, that history does not narrate; but neither can this psalm lie, nor the Prophets, who say this in many places of their rebukes. But that the gentiles had this custom, even their own writings did not keep silent.

**31.** But what is it that follows? *And the land was slain in bloods.* For we would suppose an error of the copyist, and would say that for what is *stained* (infecta) he made *slain* (interfecta), unless we had the benefit of God, who willed his Scriptures to be in many languages; and, the Greek manuscripts inspected, we would see it written thus: *The land was slain in bloods.* What is it, then, *The land was slain*, unless this be referred to the men who dwelt in the land, by a tropical expression, by which is signified, through that which contains, that which is contained, as we call a house bad, in which the bad dwell, and good, in which the good? For they themselves were slaying their own souls by sacrificing their sons, and by shedding the blood of the little ones, who were strangers to the consent of that crime: whence it was said, *They shed innocent blood.* Therefore: *The land was slain in bloods, and was contaminated in their works,* when they themselves were slain in soul, and contaminated in their works. *And they fornicated in their inventions.* These he calls inventions, which the Greeks call epitedeumata: for this word is in the Greek manuscripts both in this place, and above, where it was said, *They provoked him in their inventions*; since both there and here he calls them inventions in which they imitated others. Let us not, then, suppose them so called inventions, as if instituted by themselves, with no preceding example in others which they imitated. Whence other translators of ours preferred to say, not inventions, but "pursuits"; others, "affections," or "affectations," others "pleasures": and the very same who said inventions, in another place set "pursuits." This I wished to commemorate, lest the name of invention raise a question in that matter which they did not devise from themselves, but imitated from others.

**32.** [vv. 40-43.] *And the Lord was angry with fury at his people.* Some of our translators were unwilling to set "anger" for what the Greek has thymos; but some set "mind"; some translated it "indignation," some "spirit." But whichever of these is said, disturbance does not befall God; but, transferred from custom, the power of taking vengeance received this name.

**33.** *And he abominated his inheritance. And he handed them over into the hands of the nations, and those who hated them had dominion over them: and their enemies troubled them, and they were humbled under their hands.* Since he called them the inheritance of God, it is manifest that he abominated them and handed them over into the hands of their enemies not for destruction, but for discipline. Finally there follows: *Often he freed them.*

**34.** *But they exacerbated him in their counsel.* This is what he said above: *They did not endure his counsel.* But the counsel of a man is ruinous to the man himself, by which he seeks the things that are his own, not the things that are God's. In whose inheritance—which is that he himself is for us, when he deigns to offer himself to be enjoyed—we will suffer no narrows of fellowship with the saints, through love of our own thing as it were private. For that most glorious city, having obtained the promised inheritance, in which no one will die, no one will be born, will not have citizens who each rejoice in their own things, because God will be all in all. Whose fellowship whoever in this pilgrimage has faithfully and ardently desired, grows accustomed to prefer common things to private, not seeking his own, but the things that are Jesus Christ's: lest, wise for himself and consulting for himself, he exacerbate God by his counsel; but, hoping for what he does not see, let him not hasten to be made blessed by the things that are seen, and, patiently awaiting that eternal thing which is not seen, let him follow in his promises the counsel of him whose help he prays for in his temptations. So he will be both humble in his confessions, lest he become like those of whom it is said, *And they were humbled in their iniquities.*

**35.** [vv. 44, 45.] Yet God, full of mercy, did not neglect them; *And he saw when they were troubled, when he heard their prayer. And he was mindful of his covenant, and he repented according to the multitude of his mercy.* *He repented* was said, because he changed what he seemed about to do, namely to destroy them. And with God indeed all things are arranged and fixed; nor does he do anything as if by sudden counsel, which he did not foreknow from eternity that he would do: but in the temporal motions of the creation, which he governs wonderfully, he himself not moved temporally, as if by a sudden will, is said to do what, by the ordered causes of things, he arranged by the unchangeableness of his most secret counsel, by which, each thing being known in its own times, he both makes things present, and has already made things future. And for these things who is fit? Let us hear, then, Scripture, humbly saying lofty things, when it both holds out things to be taken for the nourishing of the little ones, and proposes things to be scrutinized for the exercising of the greater. *And he saw when they were troubled, when he heard their prayer; and he was mindful of his covenant*: certainly of the eternal covenant *which he arranged with Abraham*, not of the old which is abolished, but of the new which is hidden even in the old. *And he repented according to the multitude of his mercy.* He did what he had arranged, but he had foreknown that he would grant it to those troubled and praying; because even their prayer itself, when it was not yet, but was to be, was without doubt not hidden from God.

**36.** [v. 46.] *And he gave them over to mercies.* That they might be not vessels of wrath, but vessels of mercy. But I think mercies were said in the plural, into which he gave them, because each one has his own gift from God, one in this way, another in that. *He gave*, then, *them over to mercies, in the sight of all who had taken them captive.* Come now, whoever you are who read these things, and by reading in the apostolic writings, and by scrutinizing in the prophetic, come to know the grace of God by which we are redeemed unto eternal life through our Lord Jesus Christ, and see the Old Testament revealed in the New, the New veiled in the Old; recall whom the apostle Paul called the prince of the power of the air, *who works in the sons of unbelief*[^30], and that where he says of certain men, *that they may recover from the snares of the devil, captured by him according to his will*[^31]: and the words of the Lord Jesus Christ, where, casting him out of the hearts of the faithful, he says, *Now the prince of this world is cast out*[^32]; and likewise the Apostle himself saying: *Who rescued us from the power of darkness, and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of his love*[^33]. Having reasoned these and such things, turn your mind also to the writings of the Old Testament, and see what is sung in that psalm whose title is: *When the house was being built after the captivity*: for there it is said: *Sing to the Lord a new song*[^34]. And lest you suppose it pertains only to the people of the Jews: *Sing*, he says, *to the Lord, all the earth; sing to the Lord, bless his name, announce*, or rather, *announce well,* or rather, that I may translate the very word which is set in the Greek, *evangelize day from day, his salvation*[^35]. For from this the Gospel is named, in which is announced day from day, the Lord Christ, light from light, the Son from the Father. For this is too *his salvation;* because the salvation of God is Christ, as we have already shown above. *Announce among the nations his glory, among all peoples his wonders. For the Lord is great and greatly to be praised, terrible above all gods: for all the gods of the nations are demons*[^36]. These enemies, then, with their king the devil, held the people of God captive. From which captivity, when we are redeemed, and the prince of this world is cast out, the house is built after the captivity: whose cornerstone is Christ, who founded the two in himself, into one new man, making peace, which, coming day from day, he evangelized to those who were near, and to those who were far off, making both one; and bringing other sheep which are not of this fold, that there may be one flock and one shepherd. And so God *gave over to mercies* his predestined ones; because *it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy*[^37]: *in the sight of all who had taken them captive.* These enemies, then, the devil and his angels, had taken captive those predestined to the kingdom and glory of God: but, cast out by our Redeemer, those who were wont to have dominion over the unbelievers within, assail the faithful from without. But they assail, they do not overcome, those who lay hold of the tower of strength from the face of the enemy. But that they may assail, they perceive that there are in us remnants of weakness, on account of which we say, *Forgive us our debts;* on account of which we say, *Lead us not into temptation, but free us from evil*[^38]. These enemies, then, cast out, the Lord Christ accomplished healings in the body, of which the head is the Savior himself of the body, that in that very body of his the third thing might be consummated. For so he said: *Behold, I cast out demons, and accomplish healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am consummated*[^39]; that is, I am perfected, all of us meeting in the perfect man, in the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ.

**37.** [vv. 47, 48.] And so, the demons cast out by whom we were held captive, he accomplishes healings. Therefore here too, when he had said, *And he gave them over to mercies, in the sight of all who had taken them captive;* as if, the demons who had taken them being cast out, a prayer is made that he accomplish healings. *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from the nations,* or, as other manuscripts have, *from the gentiles; that we may confess to your holy name, and glory in your praise.* Then briefly he subjoined the praise itself: *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, from the age and unto the age*: which we understand, from eternity to eternity; because he will be praised without end by those of whom it is said: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house, for ages of ages they will praise you*[^40]. This is the third consummation of the body of Christ, the demons cast out and the healings accomplished, even to the immortality of that very body, the eternal kingdom of those perfectly praising, because perfectly loving: but perfectly loving, because contemplating face to face. For then will be perfected what at the beginning of this psalm was prayed: *Remember us, O Lord, in the good pleasure of your people; visit us in your salvation, to see in the goodness of your elect, to rejoice in the gladness of your nation, that you may be praised with your inheritance.* For he gathers not only from the gentiles the sheep which had perished of the house of Israel, but also those which are not of that fold; that there may be one flock, as was said, and one shepherd. But the Jews, when they suppose this prophecy to pertain to their own visible kingdom, because they do not know how to rejoice in the hope of invisible goods, are going to fall into the snares of him of whom the Lord says: *I came in the name of my Father, and you did not receive me; another will come in his own name, him you will receive*[^41]. Of whom the apostle Paul says: *For the man of sin will be revealed, the son of perdition, who opposes and exalts himself above all that is called God, or that is worshiped, so that he sits in the temple of God, showing himself as if he were God.* And a little after: *Then will be revealed*, he says, *the wicked one, whom the Lord Jesus will slay with the spirit of his mouth, and will make void by the illumination of his presence him whose presence is according to the working of Satan, in all power and signs and lying prodigies, and in all the seduction of iniquity to those who perish, because they did not receive the love of the truth, that they might be saved; and therefore God will send them the working of error, that they may believe a lie, and all may be judged who did not believe the truth, but consented to iniquity*[^42]. Through that apostate, through that one who exalts himself above all that is called God, or that is worshiped, it seems to me that the people of the carnal Israelites will suppose this prophecy to be fulfilled, by which it was said, *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from the gentiles;* because, under his leadership, as it were in the sight of their visible enemies, who had visibly taken them captive, they will have visible glory. So they will believe a lie, because they did not receive the love of the truth, so as to desire not carnal goods, but spiritual. For they were so deceived by the devil that they killed Christ, when they said: *If we let him go thus, all will believe in him, and the Romans will come, and take away both our place and our nation*: when *Caiaphas, one of them, since he was high priest of that year, said to them: You know nothing, nor do you consider that it is expedient for us that one man die for the people, and the whole nation not perish. But this*, as the Evangelist understood, *he did not say of himself, but, since he was high priest of that year, he prophesied that Jesus was to die for the nation; and not only for the nation,* that is, for the sheep which had perished of the house of Israel, *but that he might gather into one the sons of God who were scattered*[^43]. For he had other sheep which were not of that fold: all these sheep, both of the Israelites and of the gentiles, the devil and his angels had taken captive. And so, the dominion of the devil being driven from them, in the sight of the malign spirits who had taken them captive, that they may be saved and perfected forever, their voice is in the prophecy: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from the gentiles.* Not as the Jews suppose it to be fulfilled through the antichrist, but through Christ our Lord coming in the name of his Father, *day from day, his salvation;* of whom here too it was said: *Visit us in your salvation. And let all the people say*: this people of the predestined, of circumcision and of uncircumcision, a holy nation, a people unto adoption: *So be it, so be it.*
