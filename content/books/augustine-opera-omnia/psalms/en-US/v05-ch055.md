# On the Same Psalm 118 (30)

**1.** [v. 161.] What persecutions from the kings of the earth the body of Christ, that is, the holy Church, has borne, we know. Let us recognize here too its words, saying: *Princes have persecuted me without cause, and from your words my heart has feared.* For how had Christians harmed earthly kingdoms, although their king promised them the kingdom of heaven? How, I say, had they harmed earthly kingdoms? Did their king forbid his soldiers to pay out and render the things that are owed to the kings of the earth? Did he not, to the Jews who were contriving a slander against him about this, say, *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's*[^1]? Did he not himself too pay the tribute from the mouth of a fish[^2]? Did not his forerunner, to the soldiers of this kingdom asking what they ought to do for eternal salvation, not say, "Loose your belt, throw down your arms, desert your king, that you may be able to serve the Lord as soldiers"; but say, *Do violence to no one, accuse no one falsely; let your wages be enough for you*[^3]? Did not one of his soldiers and his most beloved companion say to his fellow soldiers, and as it were to Christ's provincials, *Let every soul be subject to the higher powers*[^4]? And a little later he says: *Render to all their dues: to whom tribute, tribute; to whom custom, custom; to whom fear, fear; to whom honor, honor. Owe no one anything, except to love one another*[^5]. Did he not command that the Church pray even for those kings themselves[^6]? In what, then, did Christians offend them? What due did they not render? In what were Christians not obedient to earthly kings? Therefore earthly kings persecuted Christians without cause. But attend to what he added: *And from your words my heart has feared.* They indeed had their threatening words too: I expel, I proscribe, I kill, I torture with claws, I roast with fires, I expose to beasts, I tear the limbs: but your words rather terrified me: *Do not fear those who kill the body, and afterward have nothing they can do; but fear him who has power to destroy both body and soul in hell*[^7]. From these your words my heart has feared; and I despised my human persecutor, and conquered the devil my seducer.

**2.** [v. 162.] Finally there follows: *I will exult over your utterances, like one who finds much spoil.* By those words he conquered, from which he feared. For from the conquered the spoils are stripped; as that one was conquered and despoiled, of whom it is said in the Gospel: *No one enters the house of the strong man, to plunder his goods, unless he first binds the strong man*[^8]. But much spoil was found, when, having marveled at the patience of the martyrs, even those who persecuted believed; and those who sought to do harm to our king by the loss of his soldiers were, over and above, acquired by him. Whoever, then, fears from the words of God, lest he be conquered in the contest, exults as a victor over those same words.

**3.** [v. 163.] For lest we should think that from that fear a hatred of the words of God could have crept in: although he has already said, *I have exulted over your utterances,* which surely he would not say if he hated them; yet he still added, and says: *Injustice I have hated, and abhorred; but your law I have loved.* Surely that fear from his words did not produce a hatred of those same words, but kept love entire. For the law of God is not other than the words and utterances of God. Far be it, then, that love perish by fear, where the fear is chaste. So fathers are both feared and loved by godly sons; so a chaste wife both fears her husband, lest she be forsaken by him, and loves him, that she may enjoy him. If, then, both a father, a man, and a husband, a man, ought to be both feared and loved; how much more our Father who is in heaven, and that Bridegroom, beautiful not in flesh above the sons of men, but in power. For by whom is the law of God loved, except by those by whom God is loved? And what does the father's law have that is grievous to good sons? Or is it because whom he loves he corrects, and scourges every son whom he receives[^9]? But he who rejects these judgments does not receive the promises. Let the fatherly judgments, then, be praised even in the scourge, if the promises are loved in the reward.

**4.** [v. 164.] So plainly does this man do, who says: *Seven times a day I have praised you, over the judgments of your righteousness. Seven times a day,* which he says, signifies "always." For this number is wont to be a sign of totality: on account of which, to the six days of the divine working a seventh of rest was added; and through seven days, running and recurring, all times roll. Nor for any other reason was it said, *The righteous man will fall seven times, and will rise again*[^10]: that is, the righteous man does not perish, though humbled in every way, yet not having transgressed—otherwise he will not be righteous. For in place of every kind of tribulation by which he is cast down in the sight of men, it is put, *he falls seven times;* and in place of the fact that out of all those tribulations he advances, it is put, *he will rise again.* In that book the following sentence sufficiently illustrates the preceding: for there follows, *But the ungodly will be weakened in evils*[^11]. So this is, for the righteous man to fall seven times and rise again: in all evils not to be weakened. Rightly, then, seven times a day did the Church praise God over the judgments of his righteousness; because, when it was time that judgment should begin at the house of the Lord, in all its tribulations it was not weakened, but glorified by the crowns of the martyrs.

**5.** [v. 165.] *Much peace,* he says, *to those who love your law, and there is no stumbling block to them.* Is it that the law itself is no stumbling block to those who love it, or that to those who love it there is no stumbling block from any source whatever? But both are rightly understood. For he who loves the law of God honors even what in it he does not understand; and what seems to him to sound absurd, he judges that he rather does not understand, and that something great lies hidden there: therefore the law of God is no stumbling block to him. But that he may suffer no stumbling block at all, let him not so attend to the men of any holy profession that his faith depends on their morals; lest, when some fall whom he held in high esteem, he himself perish by the stumbling block: but let him love the law of God itself, and there will be to him much peace, and no stumbling block. For he securely loves that in which, even though many sin, it itself does not know how to sin.

**6.** [v. 166.] *I was awaiting,* he says, *your salvation, O Lord, and I have loved your commandments.* For what would it have profited the righteous of old to have loved the commandments of God, unless Christ, who is the salvation of God, had set them free; by whose Spirit too, imparted, they were able to love the commandments of God? If, then, those awaited the salvation of God who loved his commandments; how much more necessary was Jesus, that is, the salvation of God, for making safe those who did not love his commandments? This prophecy can fit also the saints of these times, since, grace having been revealed, the Gospel is preached: for they await Christ who love the commandments of God, that when Christ our life appears, then we too may appear with him in glory[^12].

**7.** [vv. 167-168.] *My soul has kept,* he says, *your testimonies, and I have loved them greatly;* or, as some manuscripts have, *it has loved,* so that *my soul* be supplied. The testimonies of God are kept when they are not denied. This is the office of the martyrs, because testimonies in Greek are called martyria. But because, even to be burned in flames for the testimonies of God without love profits nothing[^13], therefore he added, *And I have loved them greatly.* Above, moreover, he had said, *I have loved your commandments;* then in the following verse, *Your testimonies* both *I have kept* and *I have loved;* afterward, *Both your commandments and your testimonies I have kept;* for he says, *I have kept your commandments and your testimonies.* For he who loves keeps them truly and gladly. But often, while the commandments of God are kept, enemies are made, against whose will they are kept: then indeed the testimonies too must be strongly kept, lest, the enemies persecuting, they be denied.

**8.** When, then, this man had said that he had done both, he attributed to God that whereby he did it, adding and saying: *For all my ways are in your sight, O Lord.* Therefore, he says, *I have kept your commandments and your testimonies; because all my ways are in your sight.* As if he said: If you had turned your face away from me, I would be made troubled, nor would I keep your commandments and testimonies. I have kept them, then, *because all my ways are in your sight.* For by a favorable and helping sight he wished it understood that God sees his ways; as he prayed who said, *Do not turn your face away from me*[^14]. For surely the face of the Lord is also over those who do evil, but to destroy their memory from the earth[^15]. Not so, surely, did this man say his ways are seen by him; but as the Lord knows the ways of the righteous[^16], and as he says to Moses, *I know you above all*[^17]. For if this, that his ways are in the sight of God, did not help the one walking, he would not say that he therefore kept his commandments and testimonies, because all his ways are in the sight of the Lord. For he knows how to hear: *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling: lay hold of discipline, lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you perish from the just way*[^18]; because, unless it were in the sight of the Lord, it would not be the just way. For this fear and trembling the apostle Paul too lays on those to whom he says, *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation*[^19]; and, opening why he said this, *For it is God,* he says, *who works in you both to will and to work, for his good pleasure*[^20]. For this, then, are the ways of the righteous in the sight of the Lord, that he may direct their steps, since they are the ways of which it is written in Proverbs: *But the ways that are on the right the Lord knows: but perverse,* he says, *are those on the left*[^21]; that we might understand that the Lord does not know them, on account of which he is going to say to the perverse, *I do not know you*[^22]. Further, to show with what fruit the Lord knows the ways that are on the right—that is, of the righteous—he immediately added: *For he himself will make your steps straight, and will lead forth your journeys in peace*[^23]. Behold why this man too says, *I have kept your commandments and your testimonies.* And as if we asked whence he could do this: *Because all,* he says, *my ways are in your sight, O Lord.*
