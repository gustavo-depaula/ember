# Exposition of Psalm 56

**1.** We have heard in the Gospel just now, brethren, how greatly our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ loves us, God with the Father, man with us, from our own selves, now at the right hand of the Father; you have heard how greatly he loves us. For the measure of his love, he himself both stated and enjoined upon us, saying that his commandment is that we love one another. And lest we should ask, doubting and burning, how much we ought to love one another, and how great is that perfect measure of love which pleases God—for that is perfect than which there can be none greater—he himself expressed it, taught it, and said: *Greater love than this no one has, than that a man lay down his life for his friends*[^1]. He himself did what he taught, the Apostles did what they learned from him, and preached to us that it is to be done. Let us too do it; because, although we are not what he is according to that whereby he created us, yet we are what he is according to that whereby he was made for our sake. And if he alone had done it, perhaps none of us would dare to imitate it; for he was so a man as also to be God: but in that which is man, the servants imitated the Lord, and the disciples the Master, and they did it who went before us in his household, our fathers indeed, yet our fellow servants; nor would God command us to do this, if he judged it impossible for it to be done by man. But, considering your weakness, do you fail under the precept? Be strengthened by the example. But is even the example too much for you? He who furnished the example is present to furnish help also. Let us hear, then, in this psalm: for it happened opportunely, and by his providence, that the Gospel should accord with it, commending to us the love of Christ, who laid down his life for us, that we too may lay down our life for the brethren. It has accorded and sounded together with this psalm, that we may see how the Lord himself laid down his life for us: for this psalm sings his passion. And since the whole Christ is head and body, which I do not doubt you know well: the head is our Savior himself, who suffered under Pontius Pilate, who now, after he rose from the dead, sits at the right hand of the Father: but his body is the Church; not this one or that, but spread throughout the whole world; nor that which is now in men who lead the present life, but pertaining to it also are those who were before us, and those who will be after us unto the end of the age. For the whole Church, consisting of all the faithful, because all the faithful are members of Christ, has that head placed in the heavens which governs its body; though separated in vision, yet it is joined by love. Since, then, the whole Christ is his head and body; therefore in all the Psalms let us so hear the voices of the head as also to hear the voices of the body. For he was unwilling to speak separately, because he was unwilling to be separated, saying: *Behold, I am with you unto the consummation of the age*[^2]. If he is with us, he speaks in us, speaks of us, speaks through us; because we too speak in him: and therefore we speak truth because we speak in him. For when we wish to speak in ourselves and from ourselves, we shall remain in falsehood.

**2.** [v. 1] Since, then, this psalm sings the Lord's passion, see what title it has: *Unto the end.*[^3] The end is Christ. Why is he called the end? Not one who consumes, but one who consummates. For to consume is to destroy: to consummate is to perfect. For whatever we call finished, we call from "end." But we say it one way: The bread is finished; another way: The tunic is finished: the bread that was being eaten is finished, the tunic that was being woven is finished: the bread, then, is finished so as to be consumed, the tunic is finished so as to be perfected. The end, then, of our purpose is Christ; because however much we strive, in him we are perfected, and by him we are perfected; and this is our perfection, to reach him: but when you have reached him, you seek no further; he is your end. For just as the end of your road is the place to which you tend; where, when you have arrived, you will now remain: so the end of your zeal, your purpose, your effort, your intention, is he to whom you tend; to whom, when you have arrived, you will desire nothing further, because you will have nothing better. He himself, then, both set before us an example of living in this life, and will give the reward of living in the future life.

**3.** *Unto the end, do not corrupt, for David himself, for the inscription of a title, when he fled from the face of Saul into the cave.*[^4] Referring ourselves to the holy Scripture, we find indeed that holy David, that king of Israel, from whom the Davidic Psalter took its name, suffered as persecutor Saul, the king of that same people, as many of you know who have either touched or heard the Scriptures. King David, then, had Saul as persecutor: and whereas the one was most gentle, the other most fierce; the one mild, the other envious; the one patient, the other cruel; the one beneficent, the other ungrateful; he bore with him with such gentleness that, when he had received him into his hands, he did not touch him, did not harm him. For he received power from the Lord God, that, if he wished, David himself might kill Saul; and he chose to spare rather than to kill. But the other was not conquered even by such a benefit, so as to cease persecuting. We find, then, at that time, when Saul was persecuting David, that the future and predestined king, the king now reprobated, that David himself fled from the face of Saul into a cave. What, then, has this to do with Christ? If all that was then done were figures of things to come, we find Christ there, and most of all. For that, *Do not corrupt, for the inscription of a title*, I do not see how it pertains to that David. For no title was inscribed for David himself, which Saul wished to corrupt. But we see that in the Lord's passion a title was written, *King of the Jews*; so that that title might reproach their forehead, that they did not keep their hands from their king. For in those men was Saul, in Christ was David. For Christ, as the apostolic Gospel says, is, as we know, as we confess, *of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^5]; for according to divinity he is above David, above all men, above heaven and earth, above the angels, above all things visible and invisible; because all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made: yet, deigning to become man of the seed of David, he came to us; because he was born of the tribe of David, whence was the virgin Mary who bore Christ. And so that title was inscribed, *King of the Jews.* Saul, as we said, was the people of the Jews; David was Christ: the title there, *King of the Jews.* The Jews were indignant, because the title was inscribed, *King of the Jews*: it shamed them to have a king whom they could crucify. For they did not see that the very cross on which they fixed him was to be on the foreheads of kings. When, then, they were indignant at that title, they went to Pilate the judge, to whom they had handed over Christ to be killed, and said to him: *Do not write thus, King of the Jews; but write that he himself said he was king of the Jews.* And because it had already been sung through the Holy Spirit, *Unto the end; do not corrupt, for the inscription of a title*, Pilate answered them: *What I have written, I have written*[^6]: why do you suggest falsehood to me? I do not corrupt the truth.

**4.** We have heard, then, what it means: *Do not corrupt, for the inscription of a title.*[^7] What, then, is: *When he fled from the face of Saul into the cave*? This indeed that David did: but because in him we do not find the inscription of a title, in this one let us find the flight into the cave. For that cave, with which David covered himself, figured something. But why did he cover himself? That he might be hidden, and not be found. What is it to be covered by a cave? To be covered by earth. For he who flees into a cave is covered by earth, that he may not be seen. But Jesus carried earth, the flesh which he had taken from earth; and in it he hid himself, that God might not be found by the Jews. For if they had recognized him, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. Why, then, did they not find the Lord of glory? Because the cave had covered him, that is, he set before their eyes the weakness of the flesh, but covered the majesty of the divinity in the covering of the body, as in the hidden part of the earth. They, then, not recognizing God, crucified a man. Nor could he die except in a man, nor be crucified except in a man; because he could not even be held except in a man. He set earth before those who sought ill, he kept life for those who sought well. He fled, then, according to the flesh into the cave from the face of Saul. But if you wish to take this too, that the Lord so fled into the cave from the face of Saul, because he suffered; he so hid himself from the Jews that he even died. For however much the Jews raged against him, until he died, they still thought he could be freed, and show by some miracle that he was the Son of God. This had been foretold in the book of Wisdom: *Let us condemn him to a most shameful death; for there will be respect to him from his words. For if he is truly the son of God, he will take him up, and will free him from the hands of his adversaries*[^8]. Since, then, he was being crucified, and was not being freed, they believed he was not the Son of God. Therefore, insulting him as he hung on the wood, and wagging their heads, they said to him: *If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross. He saved others, himself he cannot save*[^9]. Saying these things, as is in that same book of Wisdom, *they thought these things and erred; for their malice blinded them*[^10]. For what great thing was it to come down from the cross, for one to whom it was easy to rise from the tomb? But why did he will to be patient unto death? That he might flee from the face of Saul into the cave. For the cave can be taken as the lower part of the earth. And certainly, what is manifest and sure to all, his body was placed in the tomb, which was cut out of rock. This tomb, then, was a cave: there he fled from the face of Saul. For the Jews persecuted him for just so long, until he was placed in the cave. Whence do we prove that they persecuted him for just so long, until he was placed there? Even dead, hanging on the cross, they wounded him with a lance. But when, wrapped with the funeral spices, he was placed in the cave, they now had nothing they could do to the flesh. The Lord, then, rose again from that cave unharmed, incorrupt, into which he had fled from the face of Saul; hiding himself from the ungodly, whom Saul prefigured, but showing himself to his members. For the members of the risen one were handled by his members: for his members, the Apostles, touched him as he rose, and believed; and behold, Saul's persecution profited nothing. Let us, then, now hear the psalm, because we have spoken enough of its title, as much as the Lord deigned to grant.

**5.** [v. 2] *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me, for my soul trusts in you.*[^11] Christ in the passion says: *Have mercy on me, O God.* God says to God: *Have mercy on me.* He who, with the Father, has mercy on you, cries out in you: *Have mercy on me.* For that he cries about him: *Have mercy on me*, is yours: from you he received this, for the sake of freeing you he was clothed with flesh. The very flesh cries: *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me*: the man himself, soul and flesh. For the Word took up the whole man, and the whole man was made the Word. Let it not therefore be thought that there was no soul there, because the evangelist says thus: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^12]. For "flesh" is said for "man," as in another place Scripture says: *And all flesh will see the salvation of God*[^13]. Surely flesh alone will see, and the soul will not be there? Again the Lord himself says of men: *As you have given him power over all flesh*[^14]. Surely he had received power over flesh alone, and not above all over souls, which he was freeing first? Therefore there was a soul there, there was flesh there, there was the whole man; and the whole man with the Word, and the Word with the man, and man and Word one man, and Word and man one God. Let him say, then: *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me.* Let us not be terrified at the voices of one asking for mercy and showing it. For he asks for this reason, because he shows it: for to this end he is man, because he is merciful; not that he was born by the necessity of condition, but that he might free us from the condition of necessity. *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me, for my soul trusts in you.* You hear the master praying, learn to pray. For to this end he prayed, that he might teach you to pray: because to this end he suffered, that he might teach you to suffer; to this end he rose, that he might teach you to hope for the resurrection.

**6.** *And in the shadow of your wings I will hope, until iniquity passes by.*[^15] This now the whole Christ plainly said: this is also our voice. For iniquity has not yet passed by, iniquity still rages. And at the end the Lord himself stated the future abundance of iniquity: *And because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold; but he who perseveres unto the end, he will be saved*[^16]. But who will persevere unto the end, until iniquity passes by? He who is in the body of Christ, who is among the members of Christ, and has learned from the head the patience of persevering. You pass on, and behold, your temptations have passed; and you go into another life into which the saints have gone, if you are a saint. The martyrs went into another: if you are a martyr, you too go into another life. Surely, because you have passed from here, iniquity has therefore now passed by? Other unrighteous men are born, as other unrighteous men die. As, then, some unrighteous die, and others are born; so some righteous go, and others are born. Unto the end of the age neither will iniquity be lacking to press, nor righteousness to suffer. *And in the shadow of your wings I will hope, until iniquity passes by*: that is, you will protect me, and that I may not wither from the heat of iniquity, you will furnish me a shade.

**7.** [v. 3] *I will cry to God most high.*[^17] If he is most high, how does he hear you crying? Confidence was born from experience: *To God*, he says, *who has done good to me.* If, before I sought him, he did good to me, will he not hear me crying out? For the Lord God did good to us by sending us our Savior Jesus Christ, that he might die for our sins, and rise again for our justification. For what sort of men did he will his Son to die? For the ungodly. But the ungodly were not seeking God, and were sought by God. So, then, he is most high in such a way that our misery and our groaning is not far from him; because the Lord is near to those who have bruised their heart. *I will cry to God most high, to God who has done good to me.*

**8.** [v. 4] *He sent from heaven, and saved me.*[^18] Now the man himself, now the flesh itself, now the Son of God, according to our participation, is manifest that he was saved, and the Father sent from heaven, and saved him; sent from heaven and raised him up: but that you may know that the Lord himself too raised himself up; both are set down in Scripture, both that the Father raised him up, and that he himself raised himself up. Hear that the Father raised him up: the Apostle says: *He became*, he says, *obedient unto death, even the death of the cross: for which reason God also exalted him, and gave him the name that is above every name*[^19]. You have heard the Father raising up and exalting the Son; hear that he himself too raised up his flesh: in the figure of the temple he says to the Jews: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.* But the evangelist explained to us what he was saying: *But this*, he says, *he was saying of the temple of his body*[^20]. Now, then, from the person of the one praying, from the person of the man, from the person of the flesh, he says: *He sent from heaven, and saved me.*

**9.** *He gave over to reproach those who tread me down.*[^21] Those who trod him down, who insulted him dead, who crucified him as a man, because they did not understand God, he gave over to reproach. See whether it has not come about: we do not believe it is to come, but recognize it as fulfilled. The Jews raged against Christ, were proud against Christ: where? In the city of Jerusalem. For where they reigned, there they swelled, there they raised their necks. After the Lord's passion they were uprooted from there; and they lost the kingdom in which they were unwilling to recognize Christ as king. See how they were given over to reproach: they were scattered among all the nations, having nowhere stability, nowhere a fixed seat. But for this reason they are still Jews, that they may carry our Books to their own confusion. For when we wish to show that Christ was prophesied, we bring forth these Scriptures to the pagans. And lest perhaps those hard to faith say that we Christians composed them, so that with the Gospel which we preach we have fabricated the Prophets, through whom it would seem to be foretold what we preach; from this we convict them, that all those very Scriptures by which Christ was prophesied are with the Jews, the Jews have all those very Scriptures. We bring forth the codices from enemies, that we may confound other enemies. In what sort of reproach, then, are the Jews? The Jew carries the codex, from which the Christian may believe. They have been made our librarians, as servants are wont to carry the codices behind their masters, so that they fail in carrying, while the others profit in reading. Into such reproach the Jews have been given; and there has been fulfilled what was foretold so long before: *He gave over to reproach those who tread me down.* But what a reproach it is, brethren, that they read this verse, and themselves, blind, look into their own mirror! For the Jews appear from the holy Scripture that they carry, as the face of a blind man appears from a mirror: it is seen by others, by himself it is not seen. *He gave over to reproach those who tread me down.*

**10.** [v. 4] You might perhaps have asked, when he said: *He sent from heaven, and saved me*: What did he send from heaven? whom did he send from heaven? Did he send an angel, to save Christ, and is the Lord saved through a servant? For all the angels are a creation serving Christ. Angels could be sent for service, sent for ministry, not for help: as it is written, that angels ministered to him, not as merciful ones to one in need, but as subjects to the Almighty. What, then, did he *send from heaven, and save me*? We hear just now in another verse what he sent from heaven: *He sent from heaven his mercy and his truth.* To what end? *And he rescued my soul from the midst of the lions' cubs.* *He sent*, he says, *from heaven his mercy and his truth*: and Christ himself says: *I am the truth*[^22]. Truth was sent, then, to rescue my soul from here, from the midst of the lions' cubs; mercy was sent. We find Christ himself to be both mercy and truth; mercy having compassion on us, and truth repaying us. This, then, is what I said a little before, that he himself too raised himself up. For if truth raised up Christ, and if truth rescued the soul of Christ from the midst of the lions' cubs; as he was merciful to die for us, so he was truthful to rise again to justify us. For he had said he would rise again, and the truth could not lie; and because he is truth and truthful, therefore he showed true scars, because he bore true wounds. These scars the disciples held, handled, made manifest to themselves; he cried out who put his fingers into the pierced side, and said: *My Lord and my God*[^23]. By mercy he had died for him, and by truth he had risen again for him. *He sent from heaven his mercy and his truth: and he rescued my soul from the midst of the lions' cubs.* Who are the lions' cubs? That little people, badly deceived, badly seduced by the chiefs of the Jews: so that they were the lions, these the lions' cubs. All roared, all killed. For we are going to hear here too of their slaughter, just now in the following verses of this psalm.

**11.** [v. 5] *And he rescued*, he says, *my soul from the midst of the lions' cubs.*[^24] Why do you say: *And he rescued my soul*? For what had you suffered, that your soul should be rescued? *I slept, troubled.* Christ expressed his death. Surely of that David we read that he fled into a cave, yet not that he slept in the cave. There is one David in the cave, another David who says: *I slept, troubled.* We see his being troubled; not that he was troubled, but that they troubled him. For he called himself troubled, according to the opinion of those raging, not according to the conscience of the one yielding. They thought they had troubled him, they thought they had conquered: but he slept, troubled. So peaceful was this troubled one that, when he willed, he slept. No one sleeps troubled: all who are disturbed are either awakened from sleep, or not permitted to sink into sleep. But he was troubled, and slept. Great the humility of the troubled one, great the power of the sleeping one. From what power came it that he slept? From that of which he himself says: *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again: no one takes it from me; but I lay it down, and take it up again*[^25]. They troubled, and he slept. Whose figure Adam bore, when God sent upon him a deep sleep, that he might make a spouse for him from his side. For could he not have made a wife for the first man even from the side of one awake? or did he therefore will him to sleep, lest he feel when the rib was drawn from him? Finally, who sleeps so that, with a bone torn out, he does not wake up? He who could take away a rib from one sleeping without pain, could do so also to one awake. But why did he will to do it to one sleeping? Because for the sleeping Christ on the cross a spouse was made from his side. For the side of the one hanging was struck with a lance, and there flowed forth the sacraments of the Church. *I slept*, he says, *troubled.* And in another psalm he makes this manifest, where he says: *I slept and took my rest.* There he expressed his power. He could there too have said: *I slept*, as he said here. But what is, *I slept*? That is, because I willed, I slept. They did not compel me into sleep unwilling; but by my will I slept, according to that: *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again.* Therefore there it follows and says: *I slept and took my rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will uphold me*[^26].

**12.** *I slept, troubled.*[^27] Whence troubled? by whom troubled? Let us see how he brands the evil conscience of the Jews, who wish to excuse themselves from the killing of the Lord. For they handed him over to the judge, as the Gospel says, lest they themselves should seem to have killed him. For when the judge, Pilate, then said to them: *Take him yourselves, and judge him according to your law;* they answered: *It is not lawful for us to kill anyone*[^28]. It is not lawful to kill, is it lawful to hand over to be killed? But who killed him? He who, hearing the outcry, yielded, or those who by crying out wrung from him that he should be killed? Let the Lord himself give testimony by whom he was killed: whether by that Pilate who unwillingly killed him—whence he also scourged him, and clothed him with an ignominious garment, and produced him scourged before their eyes, so that, sated at least with the punishment of the scourges, they might not wring out his being killed. Therefore, when he saw them persist, as we read, he washed his hands, and said: *I am innocent of the blood of this just man*[^29]. You may see whether he is innocent, who at least yielded to those crying out: much more, however, are those guilty who by crying out willed to kill him. But let us ask, and let us hear the Lord, to whom he attributes his death, because he said: *I slept, troubled.* Let us ask him, and say: Since you slept troubled, who persecuted you? who killed you? Was it perhaps Pilate, who gave you to the soldiers to be hung on the wood, to be transfixed with nails? Hear who: *The sons of men.* He certainly means those whom he suffered as persecutors. But how did they kill him, who carried no iron? Who did not draw a sword, who made no assault on him to kill him, whence did they kill him? *Their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword.* Do not attend to the unarmed hands, but to the armed mouth: thence proceeded the sword by which Christ would be killed; as also from the mouth of Christ, whence the Jews too would be slain. For he has a sword sharp on both sides: and rising again he struck them, and divided from them those whom he would make his faithful. They have an evil sword, he a good one: they evil arrows, he good ones. For he too has good arrows, good words, with which he shoots the faithful heart, that he may be loved. So those have one kind of arrows, and he another sword. *The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp blade.* The tongue of the sons of men is a sharp blade, and their teeth weapons and arrows. When, then, did they strike, except when they cried out: *Crucify, crucify*[^30]?

**13.** [v. 6] And what did they do to you, O Lord? Let the Prophet here exult. For above the Lord was speaking all those verses: the Prophet indeed, but from the person of the Lord, because in the Prophet is the Lord. And when the Prophet speaks from his own person, the Lord himself speaks through him, who dictates to him the truth that he speaks. Now, then, hear from the person of the Prophet, my brethren. This prophet saw the Lord in the Spirit humbled, beaten, scourged, struck with fists, slapped with hands, smeared with spittle, crowned with thorns, hung on the wood; those raging, him enduring; those exulting, him as though conquered he saw in the Spirit: and after all that humiliation and their fury, that he had risen again, and that all those things which the raging Jews had done were made empty; and lifted up with joy, as though he saw it happening: *Be exalted*, he says, *above the heavens, O God.*[^31] Man on the cross, and God above the heavens. Let those raging remain on earth, do you in heaven be judging. Where are those who raged? where are *their teeth, weapons and arrows*? Have not *the wounds they made become the arrows of children*[^32]? For in another place a psalm says this, wishing to show that they raged in vain, and were in vain hurled headlong into furies: since they could do nothing to Christ, crucified for an hour, and afterward rising and sitting in heaven, *the wounds they made have become the arrows of children.* How do children make themselves arrows? Out of reeds. But what arrows? or what strength? or what bow? or what blow? or what wound? *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and over all the earth your glory.* Why are you exalted above the heavens, O God? Brethren, God exalted above the heavens we do not see, but believe: but his glory over all the earth we not only believe, but also see. What true madness the heretics suffer, I beg you to attend. They, cut off from the joining of the Church of Christ, and holding a part, losing the whole, are unwilling to be in communion with the world, throughout which the glory of Christ is spread. But we Catholics are in all the earth, because we are in communion with all the earth throughout which the glory of Christ is spread. For we see that what was then sung is now fulfilled. Our God has been exalted above the heavens, and over all the earth his glory. O heretical madness! What you do not see you believe with me, what you see you deny: you believe with me that Christ is exalted above the heavens, which we do not see; and you deny his glory over all the earth, which we see. *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and over all the earth your glory.*

**14.** [v. 7] He returns to the words of the Lord: and the Lord himself begins to recount to us, as though addressing us, while the Prophet too exults and says: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and over all the earth your glory.* He himself too strengthens us, as though saying to us: What could they do to me who persecuted me? But why does he address us? Because they do it to us too. But those do nothing who shall have persecuted us in like manner. For let Your Charity see the Lord addressing us, and exhorting us by his example. *They prepared a trap for my feet, and they bowed down my soul.*[^33] They wished to bring it down, as it were, from heaven, and to press it to lower things: *They bowed down my soul. They dug a pit before my face, and they themselves fell into it.* Did they harm me, or themselves? Behold, that God is exalted above the heavens, and behold, over all the earth is his glory: we see the kingdom of Christ; where is the kingdom of the Jews? Since, then, they did what they ought not to have done, there came about in them what they ought to have suffered: they *dug a pit, and they themselves fell into it.* For that they persecuted Christ did not harm Christ, but harmed themselves. And do not think, brethren, that this befell them alone. Everyone who prepares a pit for his brother must himself fall into it. Attend, my brethren, have Christian eyes, do not be deceived by visible things. For perhaps to someone of you, because I said this, there comes to mind now someone who wished to commit a fraud against his brother, and wished to contrive some snares against him, and contrived them, and accomplished it, and the brother fell into his snares, and was despoiled, or oppressed, whether by prison, or by false testimony, or circumvented by some wicked accusation: but this man seems oppressed, that one to have oppressed; this one conquered, that one to have conquered; and what we said seems false, that whoever prepares a pit for his brother himself falls into it. I ask you as Christians, that you may take an example from things we already know. The pagans persecuted the martyrs, and the martyrs were seized, bound, sent to prison, exposed to beasts, some struck with iron, some burned with fires: did the persecutors conquer, and were the martyrs conquered? Far from it. Seek the glory of the martyrs with God, seek the pit of the pagans in the pierced conscience: for there is the pit into which the ungodly fall, in an evil conscience. Do you think he has not fallen into a pit, who lost the light of Christ and was struck with blindness? If he did not fall into a pit, he would see before him: he does not know where he goes; just as one who walks on a road, when he has fallen into a pit, has lost the road. You see, then, that all evildoers have lost the road, entangled in crime. But now perhaps he has handed you over into the hands of a robber, or into the hands of some unjust judge, or one circumvented by himself: and you are in distress, he rejoices, he exults. Do not, I have already said, do not have the eyes of pagans: have Christian eyes. You see the one who exults: that very exultation is his pit. For the sorrow of one suffering unjustly is better than the joy of one acting unjustly. The very joy of the one acting unjustly, that itself is the pit: when anyone has fallen there, he loses his eyes. You grieve for yourself, because you have lost a garment; and do you not grieve for him, because he has lost faith? Which of you was struck with the heavier loss? Behold, he kills, you are killed: does he live, are you dead? Far from it. Where is the faith of Christians? where is he who dies for a time? Let him hear his Lord: *He who believes in me, though he die, lives*[^34]. Therefore he who does not believe, though he live, is dead. *They dug a pit before my face, and they themselves fell into it.* To all the wicked this must come about.

**15.** [v. 8] But the patience of the good, by the preparation of the heart, takes up the will of God, and glories in tribulations, saying what follows: *My heart is prepared, O God, my heart is prepared; I will sing and play.*[^35] What did he do to me? He prepared a pit, my heart is prepared. He prepared a pit to deceive, shall I not prepare a heart to endure? He prepared a pit to oppress, shall I not prepare a heart to bear? Therefore he will fall into it, but I will sing and play. Hear the prepared heart in the Apostle, because he imitated his Lord: *We glory*, he says, *in tribulations; because tribulation works patience, patience proving, proving hope, and hope does not confound: because the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^36]. He was in distresses, in chains, in prisons, in blows, in hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness, in every devastation of labors and griefs, and he said: *We glory in tribulations.* Whence, except because his heart was prepared? Therefore he sang and played: *My heart is prepared, O God, my heart is prepared; I will sing and play.*

**16.** [v. 9] *Arise, my glory.*[^37] He who had fled from the face of Saul into the cave: *Arise, my glory*, let Jesus be glorified after the passion. *Arise, psaltery and harp.* What does he call, that it should arise? I see two instruments, but I see the body of Christ as one: one flesh rose again, and two instruments arose. One instrument, then, is the psaltery, the other the harp. All the instruments of musicians are called "instruments" (*organa*). Not only that instrument is called an organ which is large and is inflated by bellows; but whatever is fitted for melody, and is corporeal, which instrument the singer uses, is called an organ. But these instruments are distinct from one another; and I wish, as far as the Lord grants, to show you both how they are distinct, and why distinct, and why to both is said: *Arise.* We have already said, one flesh of the Lord rose again; and it is said: *Arise, psaltery and harp.* The psaltery is an instrument that is indeed carried by the hands of the player, and has strings stretched out; but that place from which the strings receive their sound, that hollow wood which hangs and resounds when touched, because it takes in air, the psaltery has in the upper part. But the harp has this kind of hollow and resounding wood in the lower part. And so in the psaltery the strings receive their sound from above; but in the harp the strings receive their sound from the lower part: this is the difference between the psaltery and the harp. What, then, do these two instruments figure for us? For Christ, our Lord God, rouses up his psaltery and his harp, and says: *I will rise at dawn.* I think that here you now recognize the rising Lord. We read in the Gospel: see the hour of the resurrection. How long was Christ sought through the shadows? It dawned, let him be recognized; at dawn he rose again. But what is the psaltery? what is the harp? Through his flesh the Lord wrought two kinds of deeds, miracles and sufferings: the miracles were from above, the sufferings from below. For those miracles which he did were divine; but he did them through the body, did them through the flesh. The divine flesh working, then, is the psaltery: the human flesh suffering is the harp. Let the psaltery sound; let the blind be enlightened, the deaf hear, the paralytics be braced, the lame walk, the sick rise, the dead rise again: this is the sound of the psaltery. Let the harp sound too; let him hunger, thirst, sleep, be seized, be scourged, be mocked, be crucified, be buried. When, then, you see in that flesh that certain things sounded from above, certain from the lower part, one flesh rose again, and in one flesh we recognize both psaltery and harp. And these two kinds of deeds filled the Gospel, and it is preached among the nations; for both the miracles and the sufferings of the Lord are preached.

**17.** [vv. 10-12] So the psaltery and harp rose at dawn, and confess to the Lord: and what does it say? *I will confess to you among the peoples, O Lord, and play to you among the nations: because your mercy is magnified even to the heavens, and your truth even to the clouds.*[^38] The heavens are above the clouds, and the clouds below the heavens: and yet to this nearest heaven the clouds pertain. But sometimes the clouds rest on the mountains, so far do they gather together in the nearest air. But heaven is on high, the dwellings of the angels, of the Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, Powers. Perhaps, then, it might seem that it should have been said: Your truth is magnified even to the heavens, and your mercy even to the clouds. For in heaven the angels praise God, seeing the very form of truth, without any haze of vision, without any interruption of falsehood: they see, they love, they praise, they are not wearied. There is truth: but here, in our misery, certainly mercy. For mercy is to be shown to the wretched. For there is no need of mercy above, where there is no one wretched. I said this for this reason, that it seems it could have been more fittingly said: Your truth is magnified even to the heavens, and your mercy even to the clouds. For by "clouds" we understand the preachers of truth, men bearing this flesh, in a manner hazy, whence God both flashes lightning with miracles, and thunders with precepts: and they themselves are those clouds of which Isaiah says, from the person of the Lord, rebuking a certain evil, barren, thorny vine: *I will command my clouds not to rain rain upon it*[^39]: that is, I will command my Apostles to forsake the Jews, and not evangelize them, but evangelize in the good land of the Gentiles, whence come not thorns, but grapes. We know, then, that the clouds of God are the preachers of truth, the Prophets, the Apostles, all who rightly bring forth the word of truth and have in themselves a hidden light, as the clouds have whence they flash: men, then, are clouds. What, then, does it mean, O Lord: *Because your mercy is magnified even to the heavens, and your truth even to the clouds*? Truth prevails among the angels: but you gave it to men too, and led it down even to the clouds. Again, the angels seem not to need mercy; but because you have mercy on wretched men, and by showing them mercy you make them angels by participation in the resurrection, your mercy is even to the heavens. Glory to our Lord, and to his mercy, and to his truth, because his mercy did not forsake us so as to make us blessed by his grace, nor did his truth defraud us: because first truth, covered with flesh, came to us, and healed by its flesh the inner eye of our heart, that we might afterward be able to see it face to face. Giving thanks, then, to him, let us say with the Psalm itself the last verses, which it has already said long since: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and over all the earth your glory.* For this the Prophet said to him so many years ago: this we now see: this, then, let us too say.
