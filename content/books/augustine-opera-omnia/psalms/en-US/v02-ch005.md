# Exposition of Psalm 35

**1.** [v. 2] Let your Charity attend a little to the text and the mysteries of this psalm; and let us run through it, because in many places it is clear: but where the need of obscurity compels us to linger, you will endure it for the fruit of learning. *The unjust man said within himself that he would transgress: there is no fear of God before his eyes*[^1]. He speaks not of one man, but of the kind of unjust men, who are adversaries to themselves by not understanding so as to live well; not because they cannot, but because they will not. For it is one thing when someone tries to understand something and through the infirmity of the flesh cannot, as Scripture says in a certain place, *Because the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that thinks of many things*[^2]: but it is another thing when the human heart acts more perniciously against itself, so that what it could understand, if good will were added, it does not understand, not because it is difficult, but because the will is opposed. And this happens while they love their sins and hate the precepts of God. For the word of God is your adversary, if you are a friend of your iniquity: but if you are an adversary of your iniquity, the word of God is your friend, and an adversary of your iniquity. If, then, you hate your iniquity, you join yourself to the word of God; and you will be two against destroying it, you and the word of God. For you by yourself, of your own strength, can do nothing: he helps you who sent you his word, and the iniquity is conquered. If you hate it, and God has forgiven, you will be free: but if you love it, it is contrary to you to understand whatever is said against it. Suppose a man seeks how the Son is equal to the Father; he has believed, he seeks to understand, he cannot yet. For it is a great matter, and desires greater strength to be grasped; and there is the beginning of faith which guards the soul until it is strengthened. It is nourished with milk that it may reach the constitution and firmness of more solid food; that it may understand, *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^3]. Before it can do this, it is nourished in faith: and it tries to understand, that it may understand as much as God shall have granted. Does it also understand this with effort: *What you do not wish to be done to you, do not do to another*[^4]; so that, because you do not wish to suffer iniquity, you do not do iniquity; because you do not wish to suffer deceit and treachery, you do not lay snares for another? But when you do not wish to understand this, it is attributed to your will. Therefore *the unjust man said within himself that he would transgress*: he resolved to transgress.

**2.** But does the one who resolves to transgress say this publicly, and not within himself? Why within himself? Because man does not see him. What, then, because man does not see him, in the very heart, where he says to himself that he will transgress, does God not see there? God sees there. But what follows? *There is no fear of God before his eyes.* Before his eyes is the fear of men. For he does not dare publicly to profess iniquity, lest he be either rebuked or condemned by men. But he withdraws from the sight of men: where? To himself: he leads himself within, and no one sees him: where he meditates deceits and treacheries and transgressions, no one sees. He could not even there, within himself, meditate, if he considered that God sees him: but because there is no fear of God before his eyes, when he has withdrawn from the sight of men to his own heart, whom does he fear there? Is not God present there? But there is no fear of God in his sight.

**3.** [v. 3] He meditates, then, deceits: and there follows—(Does it perhaps lie hidden from him that God sees there? Even this is shown, which I had begun to say: it lies hidden from him, but from one who wills it; because he acted against himself by being unwilling to understand.)—*For he acted deceitfully in his sight*[^5]. In whose sight? Of him whose fear is not before the eyes of the one who acted deceitfully. *So as to find out his iniquity, and hate it.* This man acted so as not to find it out. For there are men who, as it were, try to seek out their iniquity, and are afraid to find it; because if they find it, it is said to them: Withdraw from it: this you did before you knew, you did iniquity while you were in ignorance; God grants pardon: now you have come to know it; let it go, that pardon may easily be granted to your ignorance, and that with free brow you may say to God, *Remember not the sins of my youth and of my ignorance*[^6]. For this reason he seeks it, for this reason he is afraid lest he find it: for he seeks deceitfully. When does a man say, "I did not know it was a sin"? When he has seen that it is a sin, and has ceased to do that very sin, which he was doing for this reason, that he was ignorant of it; then he truly wished to know his iniquity so as to find it out, and hate it. But now many act deceitfully, so as to find out their iniquity, that is, they do not from the heart act to find it out and hate it. But because in the very inquiry there is deceit, in the finding there will be a defense of the iniquity. For when he has found his iniquity, behold, it is now manifest to him that it is iniquity. "Do not do it," you say. And he who was acting deceitfully so as to find it out, now has found it, and does not hate it: for what does he say? "How many do this, and who does not do it? And will God destroy them all? Or surely he says this: If God were unwilling that these things be done, would the men who commit them be alive?" You see that you were acting deceitfully to find out your iniquity? For if you were acting not deceitfully, but sincerely, you would already have found it out and hated it: now you have found it out and you defend it: deceitfully, then, were you acting when you sought.

**4.** [v. 4] *The words of his mouth are iniquity and deceit: he was unwilling to understand, that he might act well*[^7]. You see that he attributes it to the will: for there are men who wish to understand and cannot; but there are men who are unwilling to understand, and therefore do not understand. *He was unwilling to understand, that he might act well.*

**5.** [v. 5] *He meditated iniquity on his bed*[^8]. What did he say, *on his bed*? *The unjust man said within himself that he would transgress*: what he said above, *within himself,* this he here said, *on his bed.* Our bed is our heart: there we suffer the tumult of a bad conscience, and there we rest, when there is a good conscience. Let one who loves the bed of his heart do something good there. There is the bed where the Lord Jesus Christ commands us to pray: *Enter into your chamber, and shut your door.* What is *shut your door*? Do not expect from God such things as are outside, but those that are within: *And your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you*[^9]. Who is it that does not shut the door? He who asks such things of God for a great prize, and there sets all his prayers, that he may receive the goods that are of this world. Your door is open, the crowd sees when you pray. What is it to shut your door? That you ask of God what God alone knows how to give you. What is this, for the sake of which you shut the door and ask? *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^10]. And perhaps it has not ascended into your very bed, that is, into your heart. But God knows what he is going to give you. And when will it be? When the Lord is revealed, when the judge appears. For what is more manifest than what he is going to say to those set on the right? *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^11]. Those who will be on the left will hear these things, and will groan with fruitless repentance; because, when they lived thus, they were unwilling to do it fruitfully. How will they groan? Because there is no place of correction for them. But they too will hear: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^12]. This is the evil hearing. For the just will rejoice at the good hearing; for so it is written, *The just shall be in everlasting remembrance; he shall not fear the evil hearing*[^13]. From what evil hearing? Which those will hear: *Go into the eternal fire.* God, then, who can do beyond what we ask or understand, seeks our hidden groaning, that we may please in his sight, and not, as it were, boast of our justice before men. For he who wishes to please men by his justice, not with the end that the men who see him may praise God, but with the intention that he himself be praised, does not shut the door against the noise: because the door is open to that noise, and God does not hear in the way he wishes to hear. Let us labor, then, to cleanse our heart, our bed, where it can be well with us. Your Charity knows how many things many people suffer in public, in the forum, in quarrels, in contentions, in the troubles of business: how each one, wearied by business outside, runs to his house that he may rest there, and takes pains to finish quickly the business that is outside, and to betake himself to rest in his house. For each one has his house for this reason, that he may rest there. But if there too he suffers troubles, where can he rest? What then? It is good that he have rest at least in his own house. But if he suffers enemies outside, within perhaps a bad wife; he goes out in public: when he wishes to rest from the things that are outside, he enters his house: but when neither there does he rest, nor outside does he rest, where will rest be? At least in the chamber of the heart, that you may betake yourself to the inner parts of your conscience. If there perhaps you have found a wife who does not make bitterness for you, the wisdom of God; join yourself with her, rest within in your chamber; let not the smoke of a bad conscience cast you out from there. But that man, in order to meditate deceits, betook himself there of which this Scripture speaks, where men do not see; and there he meditated such things that not even in his own heart did he rest. *He meditated iniquity on his bed.*

**6.** *He stood by every way that is not good*[^14]. What is *he stood by*? He sinned perseveringly. Whence also of a certain godly and good man it is said, *And in the way of sinners he has not stood*[^15]. As that man did not stand, so this man stood by. *But malice he did not hate.* There is the end, there the fruit: if he cannot be without malice, let him at least hate it. For when you hate it, it scarcely creeps up on you to make you do anything evil. For there is sin in the mortal body: but what does the Apostle say? *Let not sin reign in your mortal body, so as to obey its desires*[^16]. When does it begin not to be? When there is fulfilled in us what he says: *When this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality*[^17]. Before this comes to pass, there is the delight of iniquity in the body; but greater is the delight of the pleasure of the word of wisdom, of the precept of God. Conquer sin and its will. Hate sin and iniquity, that you may join yourself to God who with you will hate it. Already joined in mind to the law of God, in mind you serve the law of God. And if in the flesh you therefore serve the law of sin, because there are in you certain carnal delights, then there will be none when you will no longer fight. It is one thing not to fight, and to be in true and perpetual peace; another to fight and conquer; another to fight and be conquered; another not even to fight, but to be dragged. For there are men, indeed, who do not fight, such as is this one of whom he speaks: for when he says, *He did not hate malice*; how does he fight against that which he does not hate? This man is dragged by malice, and does not fight. But there are those who begin to fight; but because they presume on their own strength, in order that God may show them that he himself conquers if a man subjoins himself to God, even fighting they are conquered, and when they have, as it were, begun to hold justice, they become proud, and are dashed down. These fight, but are conquered. But who is it that fights and is not conquered? He who says, *I see another law in my members warring against the law of my mind*[^18]. See one fighting: but he does not presume on his own strength, therefore he will be victor. For what follows? *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^19]. He presumes on him who commanded him to fight, and conquers the enemy aided by the one who commands. But this man *did not hate malice.*

**7.** [v. 6] *O Lord, your mercy is in heaven, and your truth reaches even to the clouds*[^20]. I know not what mercy of his he means, which is in heaven. For there is a mercy of the Lord even on earth. You have it written, *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord*[^21]. Of what mercy, then, does he speak, when he says, *O Lord, your mercy is in heaven*? The gifts of God are partly temporal and earthly, partly eternal and heavenly: he who worships God for this reason, that he may receive these earthly and temporal things, which are available to all, is as yet, as it were, like a beast; he uses indeed the mercy of God, but not that which is set apart, which will be given to none but the just, the holy, the good alone. What are the gifts that abound for all? Of him who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust[^22]. Who does not have this mercy of God, first that he should exist, that he should be distinguished from beasts, that he should be a rational animal, who can understand God, then enjoy this light, this air, rain, fruits, the diversity of seasons, earthly comforts, health of body, the affection of friends, the welfare of his own household? All are good, and are gifts of God. Do not suppose, brothers, that anyone can give these except the one God. Whoever, then, expect these things only from the Lord, there is much difference between these and those who seek them either from demons, or from soothsayers, or from astrologers. For these latter are wretched in two ways, because they both desire earthly goods alone, and do not ask them from him who gives all goods. But those who desire these goods, and wish to be happy in these goods, and ask these alone from God, are indeed better, because they ask from God; but they are still in peril. Someone says: Why are they in peril? Because sometimes they consider human affairs, and see all these earthly goods, which they desire, possessed and abounding among the ungodly and the unjust, and they think that they have lost the reward of worshiping God, because what the wicked have, they too have, though they worship God whom the others do not worship; or sometimes those who worship do not have them, and those have them who blaspheme: therefore they are still in peril.

**8.** But this man truly understood what mercy he was beseeching from God. *O Lord, your mercy is in heaven, and your truth reaches even to the clouds*: that is, a certain mercy which you grant to your saints is heavenly, not earthly; eternal, not temporal. And how were you able to announce it to men? Because *your truth reaches even to the clouds.* For who could know the heavenly mercy of God, unless God announced it to men? How did he announce it? By sending his truth even to the clouds. What are the clouds? The preachers of the word of God. Whence in a certain place God was angry at a certain vineyard. For your Charity understands, I think; you have heard Isaiah the prophet, where he says of a certain vineyard, *I waited that it should bring forth a grape, but it brought forth thorns.* And lest anyone should suppose he was speaking of that visible vineyard, he thus concluded: *But the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel; and the man of Judah is his beloved sprout.* Therefore he was rebuking that very vineyard, which he waited should bring forth a grape, but it brought forth thorns. And what does he say? *I will command my clouds not to rain upon it.* Angry, God said this: *I will command my clouds not to rain upon it*[^23]: and truly it was done. For the apostle-preachers were sent. So we have it in the Acts of the Apostles, that the apostle Paul wished to preach to the Jews, and found there not a grape, but thorns: for they began to repay evil for good, and to persecute. And he said, as though fulfilling what was said, *I will command my clouds not to rain upon it: To you we were sent, but because you have rejected the word of God, behold, we turn to the Gentiles*[^24]. So it was fulfilled: *I will command my clouds not to rain upon it.* Truth came even to the clouds: therefore the mercy of God which is in heaven, and not on earth, could be announced to us. And truly, brothers, the clouds are the preachers of the word of truth. When God threatens through the preachers, he thunders through the clouds. When God works miracles through the preachers, he flashes through the clouds, terrifies through the clouds, and irrigates through rain. These preachers, then, through whom the Gospel of God is preached, are the clouds of God. Let us hope, then, for mercy, but that which is in heaven.

**9.** [v. 7] *Your justice is like the mountains of God: your judgments are like a great deep*[^25]. Who are the mountains of God? Those who were called clouds are themselves also the mountains of God: the great preachers, the mountains of God. And just as, when the sun rises, it first clothes the mountains with light, and from there the light descends to the lowest parts of the earth: so when our Lord Jesus Christ came, he first shone on the height of the apostles, first illumined the mountains, and so his light descended to the valley of the earth. And therefore in a certain place he says in the psalm, *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help shall come to me*[^26]. But do not suppose that the mountains themselves will give you help: for they receive what they give, they do not give of their own. And if you remain in the mountains, your hope will not be firm: but in him who illumines the mountains ought to be your hope and your presumption. But help will come to you from the mountains, because the Scriptures were ministered to you through the mountains, through the great preachers of truth: but do not fix your hope in them. Hear what he says consequently: *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help shall come to me.* What then? Do the mountains give you help? No: hear what follows: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^27]. From the mountains comes help, but not from the mountains. And from whom? *From the Lord, who made heaven and earth.* There were other mountains, by which each one, when he steered his ship, would make shipwreck. For there arose leaders of heresies, and they were mountains. Arius was a mountain, Donatus was a mountain. Maximian just now has, as it were, become a mountain. Many, attending to these mountains, and desiring land, when they wish to be freed from the waves, are driven to the rocks, and have made shipwreck on land. By such mountains he was not seduced who said, *In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul: Migrate to the mountains like a sparrow?*[^28] I will not have my hope in Arius, I will not in Donatus: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth.* Learn how much you should presume on God, and how much you should attribute to men; because cursed is everyone who places his hope in man[^29]. Most modestly and humbly the holy apostle Paul, truly zealous for the Church, but for the bridegroom, not for himself, and shuddering at them when they wished to say, *I am of Paul, I of Apollos*[^30]; took up his own person rather to trample and despise it, that he might glorify Christ: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^31] He thrusts away from himself, but in order to send to Christ. He does not wish to be loved by the bride in place of the bridegroom, nor as the friend of the bridegroom. For the apostles are friends of the bridegroom. For this bridegroom that humble John too, who was thought to be the Christ, was zealous. Whence he says, *I am not the Christ, but he who comes after me is greater than I, the strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to loose*[^32]. Truly, because he humbled himself so much, he showed that he was not the bridegroom, but the friend of the bridegroom; and therefore he says, *He who has the bride is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice*[^33]. And if the friend of the bridegroom is a mountain, yet the mountain does not have light from itself: but it hears, and rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice. *We,* he says, *have received of his fullness*[^34]. Of whose fullness? Of him who *was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^35]. For this bridegroom, then, the Apostle too was zealous for the Church, saying, *So let a man regard us as ministers of Christ, and dispensers of the mysteries of God*[^36]: this is, *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help shall come to me. So let a man regard us as ministers of Christ, and dispensers of the mysteries of God.* But lest again your hope be fixed in the mountains and not in God, hear: *I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase;* and, *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^37]. Now, then, you have said, *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help shall come to me*: but because *neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters;* say, *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*: and, *Your justice is like the mountains of God,* that is, the mountains are filled with your justice.

**10.** *Your judgments are like a great deep.* By the deep he means the depth of sins, into which each one comes by despising God; as he says in a certain place, *God gave them up to the desires of their heart, to do what is not fitting.* Let your Charity attend. It is a great matter, a great matter that is being treated. What is this? *God gave them up to the desires of their heart, to do what is not fitting.* If, then, God gave them up to the desires of their heart, to do what is not fitting, do they therefore do those so great evils? As though someone should pose the question: If God does this, that they do what is not fitting, what did they themselves do? Hidden is what you have heard: *God gave them up to the desires of their heart.* Therefore there was a desire which they were unwilling to conquer, to which they should be handed over by the judgment of God. But that they should be held worthy to be handed over, see what he said above of them: *Who, when they had known God, did not glorify him as God, or give thanks: but became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened.* Whence? From pride. *For saying they were wise, they became fools.* From there now follows: *God gave them up to the desires of their heart*[^38]. Because, then, they were proud and ungrateful, they were held worthy to be handed over to the desires of their heart, and they became a great deep, so that they not only sinned, but even acted deceitfully, lest they understand their iniquity, and hate it. This is the depth of malice, that they were unwilling to find it out and hate it. But how each one comes to this depth, see: *The judgments of God are a great deep.* As the mountains of God are his justice, who through his grace become great: so also through his judgments they become in the depth, who are sunk into the lowest parts. By this, then, let the mountains delight you, by this turn away from the deep, and turn to that which is said, *My help is from the Lord.* But whence? *Because I lifted up my eyes to the mountains.* What is this? Let me say it in plain terms: In the Church of Christ you find a deep, you find also mountains; you find there fewer good ones, because the mountains are few, the deep is wide, that is, many living badly, away from the wrath of God, because they acted so as to be handed over to the desires of their heart, so that now they defend their sins, and do not confess, but say: Why? What have I done? And he did that; and he did this. Now they even wish to defend what the divine word reproves: it is a deep. Therefore in a certain place Scripture says, hear the deep: *The sinner,* it says, *when he has come into the depth of evils, despises*[^39]. Behold, *your judgments are like a great deep.* But you are not yet a mountain, you are not yet a deep: flee the deep, attend to the mountains; but neither remain in the mountains. For your help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth.

**11.** [v. 8] *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord; as your mercy is multiplied, O God*[^40]. Because he said, "Your mercy is in heaven," in order that it may be known to be on earth too, he says, *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord; as your mercy is multiplied, O God.* Great is your mercy, and manifold is your mercy, O God: and this you give both to men and to beasts. For the salvation of men is from whom? From God. Is the salvation of beasts not from God? For he who made man, he himself made the beasts too; he who made both, saves both: but the salvation of beasts is temporal. But there are those who ask of God as a great thing what he gave to beasts. *Your mercy is multiplied, O God,* so that it is given not only to men, but also to beasts—this carnal and temporal salvation which is given to men.

**12.** Do men, then, have something set apart with God, which the beasts do not deserve, and to which the beasts do not attain? They have, plainly. And where is that which they have? *But the children of men shall hope under the cover of your wings*[^41]. Let your Charity attend to a most sweet saying: *Men and beasts you will save.* Now he said *men and beasts,* but then, *the children of men*: as though men were one thing, and the children of men another. Sometimes in the Scriptures men are generally called the children of men, sometimes in a certain way they are properly called the children of men, in a certain proper signification, so that not all men are understood: especially when it has a distinction. For not without cause is it set there: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord: but the children of men*: as though, these being set apart, he guards the children of men separately. Separated from whom? Not only from the beasts, but also from those men who seek the salvation of beasts from God, and desire this as a great thing. Who, then, are the children of men? Those who hope under the cover of his wings. For those men with the beasts rejoice in the thing, but the children of men rejoice in hope: those pursue present goods with the beasts, these hope for future goods with the angels. Why, then, with this distinction are the former called men, and the latter called the children of men? For in a certain place Scripture says thus: *What is man that you are mindful of him, or the son of man that you visit him?*[^42] What is man that you are mindful of him? You are mindful of him as of one absent; but the son of man you visit as present. What is it, you are mindful of man? *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord*: because to the wicked too you give salvation, and to those who do not desire the kingdom of heaven. For he protects them, and does not desert them in his own way, as his cattle: and he does not desert them; yet he is, as it were, mindful of the absent. But the one he visits is a son of man; and it is said to him, *But the children of men shall hope under the cover of your wings.* And if you wish to distinguish these two kinds of men, attend first to two men, Adam and Christ. Hear the Apostle: *For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all shall be made alive*[^43]. We are born of Adam, that we may die: we rise again through Christ, that we may live forever. When we bear the image of the earthly man, we are men: when we bear the image of the heavenly man, we are children of men; because Christ was called the Son of man. For Adam was a man, but was not a son of man: therefore those belong to Adam who desire carnal goods, and this temporal salvation. We exhort them to be children of men, who hope under the cover of his wings, and desire that mercy which is in heaven, and which was announced through the clouds. But if they cannot yet, let them at least, meanwhile, not desire temporal goods except from the one God: so let them serve even in the Old Testament, that they may come to the New.

**13.** For that people too desired earthly goods, and the kingdom of Jerusalem, the subjection of their enemies, abundance of fruits, their own welfare, the welfare of their children. Such things they desired, and such things they received; they were kept under the Law. They desired from God the goods which he gives even to the beasts, because the Son of man had not yet come to them, that they might be children of men: yet they already had clouds announcing the Son of man. The prophets came to them, they announced Christ: and there were certain ones there who understood, and had future hope, that they might receive the mercy which is in heaven. There were there those who desired only carnal things and earthly and temporal happiness. Their feet slipped to making or adoring idols. For when he admonished them, and scourged them in all the things in which they delighted, and took these away, they suffered famine, wars, pestilences, diseases, and they turned to idols. Such goods, which they ought to have desired from God as a great thing, they desired from idols, and forsook God. For they observed those very goods which they sought abounding among the ungodly and the wicked, and they thought they worshiped God in vain, because he did not give an earthly reward. O man! you are a laborer of God; afterward is the time for receiving the reward: why do you now demand the reward before you labor? If a laborer come to your house, will you give the reward beforehand, unless he has completed his work? For you would judge him perverse, if he said: First I will receive the reward, and then I will work. You grow angry. But why are you angry? Because he did not have faith in a lying man. How will God not be angry, when you do not have faith in the truth itself? What he has promised you, he will give: he does not deceive, because he who promised is truth. But you fear lest perhaps he have not what to give? He is omnipotent. Do not fear lest there be no one to give; he is immortal. Do not fear lest a successor come to him; he is everlasting: be secure. If you are willing to presume on your laborer all day, you too believe God all your life; because your life is a moment of time to God. And you will be what? *But the children of men shall hope under the cover of your wings.*

**14.** [v. 9] *They shall be inebriated with the plenty of your house*[^44]. He promises us something great, I know not what. He wishes to say it, and does not say it: can he not, or do we not grasp it? I dare to say, my brothers, that even of the holy tongues and hearts through which the truth was announced to us, neither could what they announced be said, nor be thought. For the matter is great and ineffable: and they themselves saw it in part, in an enigma, as the Apostle says, *We see now in part, in an enigma, but then face to face*[^45]. Behold, seeing in an enigma they thus poured forth. What shall we be, when we shall see face to face what they were in travail with in heart, and could not bring forth with the tongue so that men might grasp it? For what need was there for him to say, *They shall be inebriated with the plenty of your house*? He sought a word from which to speak of human affairs, that he might say something: and because he saw men gorging themselves in drunkenness, taking wine immoderately, and losing their mind; he saw what he might say, that when that ineffable joy has been received, the human mind in a certain way perishes, and becomes divine, and is inebriated with the plenty of the house of God. Whence also in another psalm it is said, *How glorious is your inebriating cup!*[^46] With this cup the martyrs were already inebriated, when, going to their passion, they did not recognize their own. What is so drunken as not to recognize a weeping wife, not children, not parents? They did not recognize them, they did not think them to be before their eyes. Do not wonder; they were drunk. From what were they drunk? See: they had received a cup from which to be inebriated. Whence that man too gives thanks to God, saying, *What shall I render to the Lord for all the things that he has rendered to me? I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord*[^47]. Therefore, brothers, let us be children of men, and hope under the cover of his wings, and be inebriated with the plenty of his house. As I could, I have spoken, and how I can, I see, and as I see, I cannot say. *They shall be inebriated with the plenty of your house: and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight.* A torrent is called water coming with force. There will be a force of the mercy of God, to irrigate and inebriate those who now place their hope under the shade of his wings. What is that delight? As it were a torrent inebriating the thirsty. Now, then, let one who thirsts place his hope: let one who thirsts have hope, inebriated he will have the reality: before he has the reality, let him thirst in hope. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled[^48].

**15.** [v. 10] From what fountain, then, will you be irrigated, and from where flows so great a torrent of his delight? *For with you,* he says, *is the fountain of life*[^49]. What is the fountain of life, but Christ? He came to you in the flesh, that he might bedew your thirsting throat: he will satisfy the one who hopes, who bedewed the one who thirsted. *For with you is the fountain of life, in your light we shall see light.* Here the fountain is one thing, the light another: there it is not so. For what is the fountain, that is also the light: and whatever you wish, that you call it, because it is not what you call it; because you cannot find a fitting name, it does not remain in one name. If you should say that it is only light, it would be said to you: Why then was it said to me that I should hunger and thirst? For who is there who eats light? That plainly was rightly said to me: *Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God*[^50]: if it is light, let me prepare my eyes. Prepare also your throat; because that which is light, is also a fountain: a fountain, because it satisfies the thirsty; light, because it enlightens the blind. Here at times light is in one place, the fountain in another. For sometimes fountains flow even in darkness; and sometimes in the desert you endure the sun, you find no fountain: here, then, these two can be separated, but there you will not be wearied, because it is a fountain; you will not be darkened, because it is light.

**16.** [v. 11] *Extend your mercy to those who know you, and your justice to those who are upright of heart*[^51]. As we have often said, those are upright of heart who follow in this life the will of God. The will of God is sometimes that you be healthy, sometimes that you be sick: if, when you are healthy, the will of God is sweet, and when you are sick, the will of God is bitter; you are not upright of heart. Why? Because you do not wish to direct your will to the will of God, but to bend God's will to yours. His is upright, but you are crooked: your will must be corrected to his, not his bent to you: and you will have an upright heart. It is well with you in this world, let God be blessed who consoles: it is hard in the world, let God be blessed because he amends and tests: and you will be upright of heart, saying, *I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall always be in my mouth*[^52].

**17.** [v. 12] *Let not the foot of pride come to me*[^53]. Surely he has already said: Under the shade of your wings the children of men shall hope, and they shall be inebriated with the plenty of your house. When one has begun to be more abundantly irrigated by this fountain, let him beware lest he grow proud. For it was not lacking to Adam the first man: but the foot of pride came to him, and the hand of the sinner moved him, that is, the proud hand of the devil. As he who seduced him said, *I will set my seat in the North*[^54]: so he persuaded him, *Taste, and you shall be as gods*[^55]. By pride, then, we fell, so that we came to this mortality. And because pride had wounded us, humility makes us sound. God came humble, that he might cure man of so great a wound of pride. He came, because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. He was seized by the Jews, he was insulted. You have heard, when the Gospel was read, what they said, and to whom they said it: *You have a demon*[^56]: and he did not say, You yourselves have a demon, because you are in your sins, and the devil possesses your hearts. He did not say this, which if he said, he would say truly; but it was not the time to say this, lest he seem not to preach the truth, but to return a curse. He let pass what he heard, as though he had not heard it. For he was a physician, and had come to cure a frenzied man. As a physician does not heed whatever he hears from a frenzied man, but how the frenzied man may recover and become sound; nor, if he even receive a blow from him, does he heed it; the frenzied man makes new wounds for the physician, the physician heals the old fever: so the Lord too came to the sick man, came to the frenzied man, that whatever he heard, whatever he suffered, he might disregard, by this very thing teaching them humility, that, taught by humility, they might be healed of pride; from which this man begs to be freed, saying, *Let not the foot of pride come to me; and let not the hand of the sinner move me.* For if the foot of pride comes, the hand of the sinner moves. What is the hand of the sinner? The working of one who counsels evil. Have you become proud? He who counsels evil quickly corrupts you. Humble, fix yourself in God, and do not greatly heed what is said to you. Hence it is that elsewhere it is said, *Cleanse me from my hidden faults, and from the faults of others spare your servant*[^57]. What is *from my hidden faults*? *Let not the foot of pride come to me.* What is *And from the faults of others spare your servant*? *Let not the hand of the sinner move me.* Guard what is within, and you will not fear what is without.

**18.** [v. 13] But why do you fear this so much? As though it were said: *There fell all who work iniquity*[^58]; so that they came to that deep, of which it was said, *Your judgments are like a great deep;* so that they came to that depth where the sinners who despise have fallen. *They fell*: where did they first fall? In the foot of pride. Hear the foot of pride: *Who, when they had known God, did not glorify him as God.* The foot of pride, then, came to them, whence they came into the depth: *God gave them up to the desires of their heart, to do what is not fitting*[^59]. The root of sin, and the head of sin, he feared who said, *Let not the foot of pride come to me.* Why did he call it that foot? Because by being proud he forsook God, and departed: he called the foot, the affection. *Let not the foot of pride come to me; and let not the hand of the sinner move me*: that is, let not the works of the sinner move me away from you, so that I should wish to imitate them. But why does he say this against pride: *There fell those who work iniquity*? Because those who are now unjust fell in pride. Therefore, when the Lord made the Church cautious, he said: *She shall watch your head,* he says, *and you her heel*[^60]. The serpent watches when the foot of pride comes to you, when you slip, that he may cast you down; but you, watch his head: the beginning of all sin is pride[^61]. *There fell those who work iniquity: they were driven out, and could not stand*: first he who did not stand in the truth, then through him those whom God put out of paradise. Whence that humble one who says he is not worthy to loose the strap of the sandal is not driven out, but stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice, and not because of his own, lest the foot of pride come to him, and he be driven out, and not be able to stand.

**19.** And if with labor we have been wearisome to some of you, we have finished the psalm, the weariness has passed, and we shall rejoice that the whole psalm has been expounded. In the middle, now fearing lest I burden you, I was about to leave off: but I considered that our purpose would be cut short, and that we should not return to the half so well as if we ran through the whole; and I preferred to be burdensome to you, rather than, the matter being unfinished, to keep the remainder. For tomorrow's sermon also is owed you: pray for us, that we may be able to render it, and bring hungering throats and devout hearts.
