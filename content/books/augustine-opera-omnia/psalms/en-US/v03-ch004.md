# Exposition of Psalm 54

**1.** [v. 1] The title of this psalm is: *Unto the end, in hymns, the understanding of David himself.*[^1] Who the end is we recall briefly, because you know. *For Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^2]. Let our intention, then, be directed unto the end, directed unto Christ. Why is he called the end? Because whatever we do, we refer to him; and when we have come to him, we shall have nothing further to seek. For there is an end which consumes, and there is an end which perfects. For we understand it one way when we hear: The food that was being eaten is finished; and another way when we hear: The garment that was being woven is finished: in both we hear: It is finished; but the food so that it might no longer be, the garment so that it might be perfected. Our end, then, ought to be our perfection; our perfection is Christ. For in him we are perfected, because we are members of that head. And he was called the end of the Law, because without him no one perfects the Law. When, then, you hear in the Psalms: *Unto the end*—for many psalms are inscribed thus—let your thought not be on consumption, but on consummation.

**2.** *In hymns*: in praises. For whether we are troubled and distressed, or whether we are glad and exult, he is to be praised, who both instructs in tribulations and consoles in gladness. For the praise of God ought not to depart from the heart and mouth of a Christian: not so that he praises in prosperity, and curses in adversity; but as that psalm prescribes: *I will bless the Lord at all times, his praise will always be in my mouth*[^3]. You rejoice; recognize the father coaxing you; you are troubled; recognize the father correcting you. Whether he coaxes or corrects, he is instructing the one for whom he is preparing an inheritance.

**3.** What, then, is: *The understanding of David himself*? David was indeed, as we know, a holy prophet, king of Israel, son of Jesse: but because from his seed came the Lord Jesus Christ according to the flesh for our salvation, often by this name he is figured, and David is set in figure for Christ, on account of the origin of his flesh. For in one respect he is the son of David, in another respect he is the Lord of David: son of David according to the flesh, Lord of David according to divinity. For if all things were made through him, through him too was David himself made, from whose seed he came to men. Accordingly, when the Lord had asked the Jews whose son they said Christ was, *they answered: David's.* He saw that they had remained in the flesh and lost the divinity; and he corrects them with a question put forth: *How, then, does David himself in the spirit call him Lord: The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet? If, then, he in the spirit calls him Lord, how is he his son?*[^4] He put forth a question, he did not deny that he was the son. You have heard "Lord"; say how he is the son: you have heard "son"; say how he is Lord. This question the catholic faith solves. How Lord? Because *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* How son? Because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^5]. Since, then, David in figure is Christ, and Christ, as we have often reminded Your Charity, is both head and body: we ought neither to call ourselves strangers to Christ, whose members we are, nor to reckon ourselves as another; because *the two will be in one flesh. This is a great mystery*, says the Apostle, *but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^6]. Since, then, the whole Christ is head and body; when we hear: *The understanding of David himself*, let us too understand in David. Let the members of Christ understand, and in his members let Christ understand, and let the members of Christ understand in Christ: because head and members are one Christ. The head was in heaven, and was saying: *Why do you persecute me?* We are with him in heaven through hope, he is with us on earth through love. Therefore, *the understanding of David himself.* Let us be admonished when we hear, and let the Church understand: for it pertains to us as a great concern to understand in what evil we now are, and from what evil we desire to be freed, remembering the Lord's prayer, where at the end we say: *Deliver us from evil*[^7]. Amid the many tribulations of this age, then, this psalm laments something concerning understanding. He does not weep with this one who has no understanding. Moreover, dearly beloved, we ought to remember that we were made in the image of God, and nowhere else than in the understanding itself. For in many things we are surpassed by the beasts: but where man knows himself made in the image of God, there he recognizes in himself something more than was given to the cattle. But when all the things man has are considered, he finds himself properly distinguished from the beast in this, that he himself has understanding. Hence the Maker himself rebukes some who despise in themselves what they received as proper and chief from the Creator, saying: *Do not become like the horse and the mule, who have no understanding*[^8]. And in another place he says: *Man, set in honor.* In what honor, except that he was made in the image of God? Therefore, *set in honor, he did not understand*, he says, *he was compared to the senseless beasts, and was made like them*[^9]. Let us, then, recognize our honor, and understand. If we understand, we see that this is not a region of rejoicing, but of groaning; not now of exulting, but still of lamenting. But even if a certain exultation dwells in our hearts, it is not yet in reality, but in hope. We are glad from the promise, because we know that he who promises does not deceive us. But as for the present time, in what evil, in what straits we are, hear; and if you hold this way, recognize in yourselves what you hear. For whoever does not yet hold the path of godliness wonders that the members of David groan such things: for he does not see these things in himself. As long as he does not see these things in himself, he is not yet there: he does not feel what the body feels; because he is outside the body: let him be incorporated, and he will feel. Let him say, then, and let us hear; let us hear, and let us say:

**4.** [vv. 2-3] *Hear, O God, my entreaty, and do not despise my prayer: attend to me, and hear me.*[^10] These are the words of one busied, anxious, set in tribulation. He prays, suffering many things, desiring to be freed from evil: it remains for us to hear in what evil he is; and when he begins to speak, let us recognize that we are there, so that, the tribulation being shared, we may join our prayer. *I was saddened in my exercising, and I was troubled.* Where saddened? where troubled? *In my exercising*, he says. He is about to mention the evil men he suffers, and he called the very suffering of evil men his exercising. Do not think that the wicked are without purpose in this world, and that God does no good through them. Every wicked man either lives in order to be corrected, or lives in order that the good may be exercised through him. Would, then, that those who now exercise us might be converted and be exercised with us: yet as long as they are such as to exercise us, let us not hate them; because, in the fact that one of them is wicked, we do not know whether he is to persevere to the end; and very often, when you think you hate an enemy, you hate a brother, and do not know it. The devil and his angels have been made manifest to us in the holy Scriptures, that they are destined for the eternal fire. Of these alone is correction to be despaired of: against whom we have a hidden struggle, for which struggle the Apostle arms us, saying: *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood*, that is, against the men you see; *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of this world, of this darkness*[^11]. Lest perhaps, when he had said *of the world*, you should understand the demons to be rulers of heaven and earth; he said *of the world, of this darkness; of the world*, he said, of the lovers of the world; *of the world*, he said, of the ungodly and unrighteous; *of the world*, he said, of which the Gospel says: *And the world did not know him*[^12]. For if the world did not know the light, because the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it, the very darkness that did not comprehend the present light is called by the name of "the world"; of this darkness they are the rulers. Of these rulers, then, we have a fixed sentence of the Scriptures, that the return of any of them is by no means to be hoped for. But of the darkness itself, of which they are the rulers, we are not certain, lest perhaps those who were darkness become light. For to the faithful, now made such, the Apostle says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^13]: darkness in yourselves, light in the Lord. Therefore, brethren, all the wicked, when they are wicked, exercise the good. For behold, hear briefly, and understand. If you are good, you will have no enemy except an evil one. Further, this rule of goodness is set before you, that you imitate the goodness of your Father, *who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^14]. For neither do you have an enemy while God does not. You indeed have as an enemy one who was created with you; but he, one whom he created. We often read in the Scriptures of the wicked and unrighteous as enemies of God: and he spares them who has nothing the enemy can charge against him, to whom every enemy is ungrateful; for from him he has whatever good he has. For from him he has mercy, whatever it is even by which he is troubled. For he is troubled for this reason, that he may not be proud; he is troubled for this reason, that, being humble, he may recognize the exalted one. You, then, to your enemy, whom you suffer intolerably, what have you furnished? If he has him as an enemy who furnished him so much, and *makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*; you, who can neither make the sun rise nor rain upon the lands, cannot keep a single thing for your enemy, that there may be peace for you on earth, a man of good will. Therefore, since this rule of love is fixed for you, that, imitating the Father, you love your enemy—*Love*, he says, *your enemies*[^15]—in this precept how would you be exercised, if you suffered no enemy? You see, then, that something profits you: and let the fact that God spares the wicked profit you for having mercy; because you too, perhaps, if you are good, have been made good out of an evil one; and if God did not spare the wicked, neither would you, who give thanks, appear. Let him spare others, then, who has spared you too. For when you have crossed over, the way of godliness is not to be blocked off.

**5.** [v. 4] Whence, then, does this man pray, set among the wicked, by whose enmities he was exercised? what does he say? *I was saddened in my exercising, and I was troubled.*[^16] When he extends his love so as to love his enemies, he was affected with weariness, barked at on every side by the enmities of many, by the rage of many, and through a certain human weakness he gave way. He saw that he was already beginning to be penetrated by the devil's evil suasion, so as to bring in hatred against his enemies: struggling against hatred so as to perfect that very love, in the very fight and wrestling he was troubled. For his is the voice in another psalm too: *My eye was troubled because of anger.* And what follows there? *I have grown old among all my enemies*[^17]. He had begun, as it were, to be sunk in the storm and the waves, like Peter. For he treads upon the waves of this age who loves his enemies. Christ walked upon the sea undismayed, from whose heart the love of his enemy could by no means be taken away: who, hanging on the cross, said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^18]. And Peter wished to walk. He as the head, Peter as the body: because *upon this rock*, he says, *I will build my Church*[^19]. He was bidden to walk, and he walked by the grace of the one bidding, not by his own strength. But when he saw the strong wind, he feared; and he had already begun to be sunk, troubled in his exercising. By what strong wind? *By the voice of the enemy, and by the tribulation of the sinner.* So, as that one cried out among the waves: *Lord, I perish, save me*[^20]; such was the voice that went before this man too: *Hear, O God, my entreaty, and do not despise my prayer: attend to me, and hear me.* Why? what do you suffer? what do you groan over? *I was saddened in my exercising.* You set me indeed to be exercised among the wicked, but they have risen up too much beyond my strength: calm the troubled one, stretch out your hand to the one being sunk. *I was saddened in my exercising, and I was troubled by the voice of the enemy, and by the tribulation of the sinner: because they have turned aside iniquity upon me, and in anger they shadowed me over.* You have heard the waves and the winds: they insulted him as one humbled, and he prayed; on every side they raged at him with the din of insult, but he within called upon the one they did not see.

**6.** When a Christian suffers anything of this kind, he ought not easily to go, as it were in hatred, against the one from whom he suffers, and to wish to conquer the wind; but to turn himself to prayer, lest he lose love. For it is not to be feared that the enemy man may do anything. For what is he going to do? Speak many evils, hurl reproaches, rage in revilings: but what is that to you? *Rejoice*, he says, *and exult, because your reward is great in heaven*[^21]. He doubles his revilings on earth, you your gains in heaven. But let him rage more, let him be able to do something more: what is more secure than you, to whom it was said: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^22]? What, then, is to be feared when you suffer an enemy? Lest the love by which you love the enemy be troubled in you. For that enemy man, flesh and blood, craves what he sees in you. But another, hidden enemy, the ruler of this darkness which you suffer in flesh and blood, seeks your other, hidden self, strives to plunder and lay waste your inner treasures. Set, then, two enemies before your eyes; one open, and the other hidden: the open one, man; the hidden one, the devil. That man is what you are according to human nature, but according to faith and love he is not yet what you are, yet he can be what you are. Since, then, there are two, see the one, understand the other; love the one, beware of the other. For that enemy whom you see wishes to humble in you that by which he is conquered. For example, if he is conquered by your riches, he wishes to make you poor; if he is conquered by your honor, he wishes to make you humble; if he is conquered by your strength, he wishes to make you weak: he attends, then, to those things in you that he wishes either to cast down or to take away, the things by which he is conquered. And that hidden enemy wishes to take from you that by which he is conquered. For, as man, you conquer man by human happiness, but you conquer the devil by love of the enemy. As, then, the man seeks to take from you, and lop off, or overturn, the happiness by which he is conquered, so the devil too wishes to conquer the man by taking away that by which he is conquered. But take care to keep in your heart the love of the enemy, by which you conquer the devil. Let the man rage as much as he can, let him take away whatever he can; if he who openly rages is loved, the one who secretly rages is conquered.

**7.** [v. 5] But this man, troubled and saddened, was praying, as one whose eye was troubled because of anger. But the anger of a brother, if it has grown inveterate, is now hatred. Anger troubles the eye, hatred extinguishes it: anger is a speck, hatred is a beam. Sometimes you hate and rebuke an angry man; in you is hatred, in him whom you rebuke is anger: deservedly it is said to you: *First cast out the beam from your own eye, and then you will see to cast out the speck from your brother's eye*[^23]. For that you may know how great a difference there is between anger and hatred: daily men are angry with their sons; produce those who hate their sons. This man, troubled and saddened, was praying, wrestling against all the revilings of all the revilers; not so as to conquer any of them by returning reviling, but so as not to hate any of them. From this he prays, from this he asks. *By the voice of the enemy, and by the tribulation of the sinner: because they have turned aside iniquity upon me, and in anger they shadowed me over. My heart was troubled within me.*[^24] This is that which is said elsewhere: *My eye was troubled because of anger*[^25]. And if the eye is troubled, what follows? *And the fear of death fell upon me.* Our life is love: if life is love, death is hatred. When a man begins to fear lest he hate the one he loved, he fears death; and a sharper death, and an inner death, by which the soul is killed, not the body. You were attending to the man raging against you: what was he going to do, against whom your Lord had given you security, saying: *Do not fear those who kill the body*? He, by raging, kills the body; you, by hating, have killed the soul; and he another's body, you your own soul. *The fear*, then, *of death fell upon me.*

**8.** [vv. 6-7] *Fear and trembling came upon me, and darkness covered me. And I said.*[^26] He who hates his brother is in darkness still. If love is light, hatred is darkness. And what did he say to himself, set in that weakness, and troubled in that exercising? *Who will give me wings like a dove's, and I will fly, and be at rest?* Either he wished for death, or he desired solitude. As long, he says, as this is done with me, as this is enjoined upon me, that I love my enemies, the revilings of these men, increasing and shadowing me over, disturb my eye, trouble my light, attack my heart, slay my soul. I would wish to go away, but I am weak, lest by remaining I add sins to sins: or at least let me be separated a little while from the human race, lest my wound suffer frequent blows, so that, healed, it may be restored to the exercising. This happens, brethren, and very often there arises in the soul of God's servant a desire for solitude, for no other reason than the throng of tribulations and scandals, and he says: *Who will give me wings?* He sees himself without wings, or rather with wings bound? If they are lacking, let them be given; if they are bound, let them be loosed: because even he who looses the wings of a bird either gives or restores to it its wings. For it did not have them as its own, the wings with which it could not fly. Bound wings make a burden. *Who*, he says, *will give me wings like a dove's, and I will fly, and be at rest?* He will be at rest—where? I said there are two senses here: either, as the Apostle says: *To be dissolved, and to be with Christ, for this is by far the best*[^27]. For he himself too, though strong, though great, though most robust in heart, though an unconquered soldier in Christ, was, in his exercising, as we read, troubled, and says: *Henceforth let no one trouble me*[^28]. As though he were saying, with that psalm: *Weariness held me because of the sinners who forsake your law*[^29]. A man, then, very often tries to correct men who are distorted, depraved, pertaining indeed to his care, but in whom all human diligence and vigilance fails: he cannot correct, he must suffer. And he who cannot be corrected is yours, either by the fellowship of the human race, or very often by ecclesiastical communion; he is within, what will you do? where will you go? whither will you separate yourself, so as not to suffer these things? But be present, speak, exhort, coax, threaten, rebuke. I have done all, I have spent and expressed whatever strength I had, I see that I have profited nothing; all my labor is spent, the grief remains. How, then, will my heart be at rest from such men, except by saying: *Who will give me wings?—like a dove's*, however, not like a raven's. The dove seeks flight away from troubles, but does not lose love. For the dove is set as a sign of love, and in it groaning is loved. Nothing is so friendly to groanings as the dove: day and night it groans, as though set here where one must groan. What, then, does this lover say? I cannot bear the revilings of men, they screech, they are carried by rage, they are kindled by wrath, in anger they shadow me over; I cannot profit them: would that I might rest somewhere, separated from them in body, not in love; lest in me love itself be troubled: by my words and my conversation I cannot profit them, by praying for them perhaps I shall profit. Men say these things, but very often they are so bound that they cannot fly. For perhaps they are not bound by birdlime, but bound by duty. But if they are bound by care and duty, and cannot abandon it, let them say: I desired *to be dissolved, and to be with Christ, for this is by far the best; but to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^30]. The dove, bound by affection, not by desire, could not fly because of the duty to be fulfilled, not because of small merit. Yet there must be a desire in the heart: and no one suffers this desire except one who has begun to walk that narrow way; so that he may know that persecutions are not lacking to the Church, even in this time when the Church seems to be in tranquility from those persecutions, indeed, which our martyrs suffered. But persecutions are not lacking, because that is true: *All who wish to live godly in Christ will suffer persecution*[^31]. You do not suffer persecution: you do not wish to live godly in Christ. Do you wish to prove that what was said is true? Begin to live godly in Christ. What is it to live godly in Christ? That what the Apostle says may pertain to your inmost being: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I do not burn?*[^32] The weaknesses of others, the scandals of others, were persecutions to him. Are these, then, lacking in this time? More abound for those who care for them. And very often a man is seen from afar, and it is said: It is well with him. And the one who says this either tastes his own things, and cannot taste another's; or has within himself nothing to taste, and does not have compassion on another who tastes, indeed who devours. Let him, then, begin to live godly in Christ, and prove what is said: he begins to desire wings, to go far away, to flee, and to remain in the desert.

**9.** [v. 8] For whence do you think, brethren, that the deserts have been filled with the servants of God? If it were well with them among men, would they withdraw from men? And yet what do even they do? Behold, they go far away, fleeing, they remain in the desert: but do they remain singly? Love holds them, so that they remain with many: and out of those very many there arise some who exercise them. For in every gathering of a multitude it is necessary that wicked men be found. For God, who knows that we must be exercised, mingles with us even those who will not persevere, or at least those who are so feigned that they have not even begun what they ought to persevere in. For he knows that it is necessary for us to bear with the wicked, and that our being good may profit; let us love our enemies, rebuke them, chastise them, excommunicate them, with love separate them even from ourselves. For see what the Apostle says: *But if anyone does not obey our word by the letter, note that man, and do not associate with him.* But lest from this anger should creep upon you, and trouble your eye: *Not*, he says, *that you should reckon him an enemy, but rebuke him as a brother, that he may be ashamed*[^33]. From the one from whom he commanded separation, he did not cut off love. That eye lives, your life lives. For love lost is your death. This that man feared to lose who said: *The fear of death fell upon me*[^34]. And so, lest I lose the life of love, *who will give me wings like a dove's, and I will fly, and be at rest?* Where are you going to go? whither are you going to fly? where are you going to rest? *Behold, I have gone far away in flight, and remained in the desert.* In what desert? Wherever you are, the rest will gather, they will seek the desert with you, they will crave your life, you cannot repel the fellowship of the brethren: even the wicked are mingled with you; the exercising is still owed to you. *Behold, I have gone far away in flight, and remained in the desert.* In what desert? Perhaps in conscience, where no man enters, where no one is with you, where you and God are. For if in a desert, in some place, what will you do about those who gather? For you cannot be separated from the human race, as long as you live among men. Attend rather to that consoler, the Lord and king, our commander and creator, created also among us: attend to the fact that he mingled with his twelve one whom he might suffer.

**10.** [v. 9] He says: *Behold, I have gone far away in flight, and remained in the desert.*[^35] Perhaps this man, as I said, has fled to his conscience, has found there some desert where he might rest. But that love troubles him: he was alone in conscience, but not alone in charity; within, conscience was consoling, but outside, tribulations did not leave him. And so, at rest in himself, depending on others, when he was still troubled, what does he say? *I awaited him who would save me from faintheartedness and storm.* There is a sea, there is a storm: nothing remains for you but to cry out: *Lord, I perish*[^36]. Let him stretch out his hand who treads the waves undismayed, let him lift up your trembling, establish your security in himself, address you within, and say to you: Attend to me, what I have borne: perhaps you suffer a wicked brother, or you suffer an enemy outside; whom have I not suffered? Outside the Jews raged, within the disciple was betraying. The storm rages, then, but he saves *from faintheartedness and storm*. Perhaps your ship is troubled for this reason, because he is asleep in you. The sea was raging, the little boat in which the disciples were sailing was troubled, but Christ was asleep: at length it seemed to them that among them the commander and creator of the winds was sleeping; they approached and awakened Christ: he commanded the winds, and there was a great calm. Deservedly, then, perhaps your heart is troubled, because the one in whom you have believed has slipped from you: you suffer intolerably, because it does not come to your mind what Christ has borne for you. If Christ does not come to your mind, he sleeps: awaken Christ, recall faith. For then Christ sleeps in you, if you have forgotten the sufferings of Christ: then Christ is awake in you, if you remember the sufferings of Christ. But when with full heart you have gazed on what he suffered, will you too not bear it with even mind? and perhaps rejoicing, because you have been found in some likeness to the sufferings of your king. When, then, thinking on these things, you have begun to be consoled and to rejoice, he has risen, he has commanded the winds: therefore there has been a calm. *I awaited him who would save me from faintheartedness and storm.*

**11.** [v. 10] *Sink them, O Lord, and divide their tongues.*[^37] He attended to those who troubled him and shadowed him over, and wished this, not out of anger, brethren. Those who have wickedly exalted themselves, it is to their advantage that they be sunk: those who have wickedly conspired together, it is to their advantage that their tongues be divided: let them agree to good, and let their tongues be in concord. But if *all my enemies whispered together against me*[^38], let them lose the togetherness in evil; let their tongues be divided, let them not agree with one another. *Sink them, O Lord, and divide their tongues. Sink them*, why? Because they exalted themselves. *Divide*, why? Because they conspired in evil. Recall that tower of the proud, made after the flood: what did the proud say? Lest we perish by a flood, let us make a high tower. They thought themselves fortified by pride, they built a high tower; and the Lord divided their tongues. Then they began not to understand one another: hence arose the origin of the many tongues. For before, there was one tongue; but one tongue profited those in concord, one tongue profited men: but when that assembly was thrown headlong into the conspiracy of pride, God spared them so as to divide their tongues, lest, by understanding one another, they make a pernicious unity. Through proud men the tongues were divided, through the humble Apostles the tongues were gathered: the spirit of pride scattered the tongues, the Holy Spirit gathered the tongues. For when the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples, they spoke in the tongues of all, were understood by all: the scattered tongues were gathered into one. Therefore, if they still rage and are gentiles, it is to their advantage to have their tongues divided. If they want one tongue, let them come to the Church; because even in the diversity of the tongues of the flesh, there is one tongue in the faith of the heart. *Sink them, O Lord, and divide their tongues.*

**12.** *Because I have seen iniquity and contradiction in the city.*[^39] Deservedly this man sought the desert, because he saw iniquity and contradiction in the city. There is a certain turbulent city: it was the one that built the tower, it was confounded and called Babylon, it was scattered among innumerable nations: from there the Church is gathered into the desert of a good conscience. For he saw contradiction in the city. Christ came. What Christ?—you contradict. The Son of God. And does God have a son?—you contradict. He was born of a virgin, suffered, rose again. And whence can this come about?—you contradict. Attend at least to the glory of his very cross. Already on the forehead of kings is fixed that cross at which the enemies scoffed. The effect proved its power: it subdued the world, not with iron, but with wood. The wood of the cross seemed worthy of insults to the enemies, and standing before that very wood they wagged their heads, and said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^40]. He stretched out his hands to a people not believing and contradicting. For if he is righteous who lives by faith, he is unrighteous who has no faith. What he calls here *iniquity*, then, I understand as faithlessness. The Lord, then, saw in the city iniquity and contradiction, and stretched out his hands to a people not believing and contradicting; and yet, awaiting even them, he said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^41]. Still indeed the remnants of that city rage, still they contradict. From the foreheads of all he now stretches out his hands to the remnants not believing and contradicting. *Because I have seen iniquity and contradiction in the city.*

**13.** [v. 11] *Day and night iniquity and labor will surround it upon its walls. Upon its walls*[^42], upon its defenses, holding, as it were, its heads, its nobles. If that noble were a Christian, no one would remain a pagan. Men often say: No one would remain a pagan, if that man were a Christian. Men often say: And if that man became a Christian, who would remain a pagan? Therefore, that they do not yet become Christians, they are, as it were, the walls of that city not believing and contradicting. How long will these walls stand? They will not stand forever. The ark went around the walls of Jericho: the time will come, at the seventh circuit of the ark, when all the walls of the city not believing and contradicting will fall. Until it comes about, this man is troubled in his exercising; and, bearing with the remnants of the contradicting, he desires the wings of flight, he desires the rest of the desert. Rather let him endure among the contradicting, let him bear the threats, drink the reproaches, await him who will save him from faintheartedness and storm: let him gaze on the head, the example of his life, let him be calmed in hope, even if he is troubled in reality. *Day and night iniquity will surround it upon its walls, and labor in the midst of it and injustice.* And therefore labor is there, because iniquity is there: because injustice is there, therefore labor too is there. But let them hear the one stretching out his hands: *Come to me, all you who labor.* You cry out, you contradict, you revile: he, on the contrary: *Come to me, all you who labor* in your pride, and you will rest in my humility. *Learn from me*, he says, *for I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls*[^43]. For whence do they labor, except because they are not meek and humble of heart? God was made humble; let man be ashamed to be proud.

**14.** [v. 12] *Usury and deceit have not departed from its streets.*[^44] Usury and deceit are not even hidden because they are evil, but rage publicly. For he who does some evil in the house at least is ashamed of his own evil: *in its streets usury and deceit.* Usury even has a profession, usury is even called a craft: it is called a corporation, a corporation as if necessary to the city, and from its profession it pays a tax: so far is in the street what at least ought to have been hidden. There is also another, worse usury, when you do not forgive what is owed you; and the eye is troubled at that verse of the prayer: *Forgive us our debts*[^45]. For what will you do there, when you are going to pray and to come to that very verse? You have heard an insulting word: you wish to exact the penalty of damnation. At least exact only as much as you gave, you usurer of injuries. You have been struck with a fist, you seek a killing. Evil usury. With what face will you go to prayer? If you abandon the prayer, with what face will you go round to the Lord? Behold, you will say: *Our Father, who are in heaven: hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done, as in heaven so on earth.* You will say: *Give us this day our daily bread.* You will come to: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^46]. Even if in that evil city those usuries abound, let them not enter these walls where the breast is beaten. What will you do, since you and that verse are there in the midst? The heavenly jurist composed the prayers for you: he who knew what was done there said to you: Otherwise you will not obtain it. *For amen I say to you, that if you forgive men their sins, they will be forgiven you; but if you do not forgive men their sins, neither will your Father forgive you*[^47]. Who says this? He who knows what is done there, which you, standing by, ask for. See that he willed himself to be your advocate: he himself your jurist, he himself the assessor of the Father, he himself your judge said: Otherwise you will not receive it. What will you do? You will not receive it, if you do not say it; you will not receive it, if you say what is false. So either you will both do it and say it, or you will not merit what you ask; because those who do not do this are in those evil usuries. Let those be there who still either adore or seek idols; do not you, people of God, do not, people of Christ, do not, body of that head. Attend to the bond of your peace, attend to the promise of your life. For what does it profit you that you exact the injuries you have suffered? Does revenge restore you? Will you then rejoice over another's evil? You have suffered evil, forgive; lest there be two of you. *And usury and deceit have not departed from its streets.*

**15.** [vv. 13-15] For this reason, then, you were seeking solitude and wings, for this reason you murmur, you cannot bear these things, the contradiction and iniquity of this city. Rest in those who are within with you, and do not seek solitude. Hear what he says of these too: *For if an enemy had reproached me.*[^48] And indeed above he was troubled in his exercising by the voice of the enemy and by the tribulation of the sinner, perhaps set in that city—that proud city raising up the tower, which was sunk so that their tongues might be divided: attend to what he groans within, because of the dangers from false brethren. *For if an enemy had reproached me, I would surely have borne it: and if he who hated me had spoken great things against me*, that is, had insulted me through pride, magnified himself over me, threatened me with whatever he could, *I would surely have hidden myself from him.* From the one who is outside, where would you hide yourself? Among those who are within. But now see whether there is anything else left, except to seek solitude. *But you*, he says, *a man of one mind, my guide and my acquaintance.* Perhaps once you gave good counsel, perhaps once you went before me, and advised something wholesome: in the Church of God we were together. *But you, a man of one mind, my guide and my acquaintance: who took sweet food together with me.* What are the sweet foods? Not all who are present know them; but let not those who know them grow bitter, so that they can say to those who do not yet know them: *Taste and see, that the Lord is sweet*[^49]. *Who took sweet food together with me. In the house of God we walked with consent.* Whence, then, the dissension? He who was within has been made an outsider. He walked with me in the house of God with consent: he raised up another house against the house of God. Why was that one abandoned, where we walked with consent? why was that one deserted, where we took sweet foods together?

**16.** [v. 16] *Let death come upon them, and let them go down alive to hell.*[^50] How he repeated and made us recall that first beginning of schism, when in that first people of the Jews certain proud men separated themselves and wished to sacrifice outside. A new death came upon them: the earth opened itself, and swallowed them alive. *Let death*, he says, *come upon them, and let them go down alive to hell.* What is *alive*? Knowing that they perish, and yet perishing. Hear them perish alive and be swallowed by the gaping of the earth, that is, be swallowed by the devouring of earthly desires. You say to the man: What do you suffer, brother? We are brothers, we call upon one God, we believe in one Christ, we hear one Gospel, we sing one psalm, we answer one Amen, we resound one Alleluia, we celebrate one Pasch: why are you outside, and I within? Very often, hard pressed and considering how true the things said are: May God, he says, requite our elders. Therefore he perishes alive. Then you add and admonish: At least let the evil of separation alone exist; why do you add also that of rebaptism? Recognize in me what you have; and even if you hate me, spare Christ in me. And this evil very often, and especially, displeases them: Truly, they say, it is wrongly done; would that it could not be done! but what do we do about the statutes of our elders? *Let them go down alive to hell.* If you went down dead, you would not know what you are doing: but since you know that what you do is evil, and yet you do it, do you not go down alive to hell? And why did the gaping of the earth especially swallow those very leaders alive, while fire rushing from heaven consumed the people consenting to them? For this reason this psalm, mentioning this punishment, began with the people, and concluded with the leaders. *Let death come upon them*, he said on account of those upon whom fire came from heaven: and at once he added: *Let them go down alive to hell*, on account of the leaders whom the gaping of the earth swallowed. For how would they go down alive to hell, of whom he had said: *Let death come upon them*? If death had already come upon them, how were they going down alive to hell? Therefore he began with the lesser, he concluded with the greater. *Let death come upon them*, those who consented and followed. What of those leaders and chiefs? *Let them go down alive to hell*: because they themselves handle the Scriptures, and know well, by daily reading, how the catholic Church is so spread throughout the whole world that all contradiction is entirely empty, nor can any testimony be found for their schism, they know it well; therefore they go down alive to hell, because they know that the evil they do is evil. But the fire of divine wrath consumed those others. For, kindled with zeal for contention, they were unwilling to withdraw from their evil leaders: fire came upon fire, upon the burning of dissension the burning of consuming. *Let death come upon them, and let them go down alive to hell. Because wickedness is in their lodgings, in the midst of them. In their lodgings*, where they sojourn and pass through. For they are not always going to be here: and yet for a temporal animosity they so fight. *In their lodgings*, then, *iniquity, in the midst of them* iniquity: nothing is so much the midst of them as their heart.

**17.** [v. 17] *I cried out to the Lord.*[^51] The body of Christ and the unity of Christ, in anguish, in weariness, in trouble, in the disturbance of its exercising; that one man, the unity set in one body, when his soul was weary, crying out from the ends of the earth: *From the ends of the earth I cried to you*, he says, *when my heart was in anguish*[^52]. He himself one, but the unity is one: and he himself one, not one in one place, but from the ends of the earth he cries, one. How would one cry from the ends of the earth, unless he were one in many? *I cried out to the Lord.* Rightly. Cry out to the Lord, not to Donatus; lest there be to you as a Lord a master who was unwilling to be a fellow servant under the Lord. *I cried out to the Lord: and the Lord heard me.*

**18.** [v. 18] *At evening, and morning, and noon, I will recount, and I will announce, and he will hear my voice.*[^53] Evangelize, do not be silent about what you have received, *at evening*, concerning things past; *in the morning*, concerning things to come; *at noon*, concerning things eternal. So to that which he says, *at evening*, pertains what he recounts: to that which he says, *in the morning*, pertains what he announces: to that which he says, *at noon*, pertains the fact that his voice is heard. For the end is at noon, but the noon from which one does not decline toward setting. For at noon the light is high, the splendor of wisdom, the fervor of love. *At evening, and morning, and noon.* Evening, the Lord on the cross; morning, in the resurrection; noon, in the ascension: I recount at evening the patience of the dying one, I announce in the morning the life of the rising one, I will pray that he may hear at noon, sitting at the right hand of the Father; he will hear my voice who intercedes for us. How great is this man's security, how great his consolation, how great his refreshment from faintheartedness and storm, against the wicked, against the unrighteous both outside and within, and among those who are outside, though they had been within!

**19.** [v. 19] And so, my brethren, those whom in this very gathering of these walls you see turbulent, proud, seeking their own, puffed up, not having a zeal of God that is chaste, sound, quiet, but assigning much to themselves; ready for dissension, but not finding an occasion—these are the chaff of the Lord's threshing floor. From here the wind of pride has shaken out those few: the whole chaff will not fly off, except when he winnows at the last. But we, what, except to sing with this one, to pray with this one, to lament with this one, and to say securely: *He will redeem my soul in peace*? Against those who do not love peace: *In peace he will redeem my soul*: because with those who hated peace, I was peaceable. *He will redeem my soul in peace, from those who draw near to me.* For from those who are far from me, the case is easy: he does not so quickly deceive me who says: Come, adore the idol: he is very far from me. Are you a Christian? I am a Christian, he says. From nearby he opposes me, he is near. *He will redeem my soul in peace, from those who draw near to me: because they were with me in many things.* Why did I say, *they draw near to me*? Because *they were with me in many things.* In this verse two meanings present themselves. *They were with me in many things*: we both had Baptism, in that they were with me; we both read the Gospel, in that they were with me; we celebrated the feasts of the martyrs, there they were with me; we frequented the solemnity of the Pasch, there they were with me. But not altogether with me: in the schism not with me, in the heresy not with me. In many things with me, in few things not with me. But in those few things in which they are not with me, the many things in which they are with me do not profit them. For, brethren, see how many things the apostle Paul recounted: he named one; if it is lacking, those are in vain. *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels*, he says, *if I have all prophecy and all faith, and all knowledge, if I move mountains, if I distribute all my goods to the poor, if I deliver my body to be burned*[^54]. How many things he enumerated! Of all these many, let one—love—be lacking: those are more in number, this is greater in weight. Therefore in all the sacraments with me, in the one love not with me: *They were with me in many things.* Again, in another way: *Because they were with me in many things*, those who separated themselves from me were with me, not in few things, but in many. For throughout the whole world the grains are few, the chaff is much. What, then, does he say? In the chaff they were with me, in the wheat they were not with me: and the chaff is near the wheat, comes forth from one seed, is rooted in one field, is nourished by one rain, suffers the same reaper, endures the same threshing, awaits the same winnowing, does not enter into one barn. *Because they were with me in many things.*

**20.** [vv. 20-21] *God will hear me, and humble them, he who is before the ages.*[^55] For they presume on some leader of theirs who began yesterday: *he will humble them who is before the ages.* For although in time Christ is from the virgin Mary, yet before the ages he is in the beginning the Word, and the Word with God, and the Word God. *He will humble them who is before the ages. For there is no change for them*: I speak of those for whom there is no change. He had known that some would persevere, and would die in the perseverance of their wickedness. For we see them, and there is no change for them: those who so die in that very perversity, in that very schism, there is no change for them. God will humble them, will humble them in damnation, because they were lifted up in dissension. There is no change for them, because they are not changed for the better, but for the worse; neither while they are here, nor in the resurrection. *For we shall all rise again, but we shall not all be changed*[^56]. Why? Because *there is no change for them, and they have not feared God.* My brethren, there is one remedy: let them fear God, let them forsake Donatus. You say to him: You are perishing in heresy, in schism; it is necessary that God requite these evils: you will come into damnation, do not flatter yourself with your words, do not follow a blind leader; but a blind man leading a blind man, both fall together into the pit. What is that to me? he says. As I lived yesterday, so also today; what my parents were, this I too am. You do not fear God. Give fear of God: let him consider that all these things that are read are true, that it is the faith of Christ which cannot be deceived; how will he remain in heresy, against such evidence of the holy Catholic Church, which God has spread throughout the whole world; which, before he spread it, he promised, foretold, and so presented as he promised? Therefore let those beware and observe who do not fear God. *He has stretched out his hand in requiting.*

**21.** [vv. 21-22] *They have polluted his covenant.*[^57] Read the covenant they polluted: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^58]. *They have polluted his covenant.* What do you say against these words of the testator? Africa alone has merited this grace of the holy Donatus, in it alone has remained the Church of Christ. Say at least, the Church of Donatus. Why do you add, of Christ, of whom it was said: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed.* Do you wish to go after Donatus? Set Christ aside, and then withdraw. See, then, what follows: *They have polluted his covenant.* What covenant? *To Abraham the promises were spoken, and to his seed.* The Apostle says: *Brethren, yet no one annuls a man's confirmed testament, or adds to it. To Abraham the promises were spoken, and to his seed: it does not say, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^59]. In this Christ, then, what covenant was promised? *In your seed all the nations will be blessed.* You who have abandoned the unity of all the nations, and remained in a part, have polluted his covenant. What, then, has befallen you, that you should be exterminated, that you should be separated from the inheritance, is from the wrath of God. For attend to what follows: *They have polluted his covenant; they were divided by the wrath of his countenance.* What do you wait for, by what greater mark heretics may be shown? *They were divided by the wrath of his countenance.*

**22.** [v. 22] *And his heart drew near.*[^60] Whose, do we understand, except his by whose wrath they were divided? How *did his heart draw near*? So that we might understand his will. For from the heretics the Catholic faith was asserted, and from those who think wrongly were proved those who think rightly. For many things lay hidden in the Scriptures; and when the heretics were cut off, they agitated the Church of God with questions: the things that lay hidden were opened, and the will of God was understood. Hence it is said in another psalm: *A gathering of bulls among the cows of the peoples, that those who are proved by silver may be set apart*[^61]. For he said *set apart*, stand out, appear. Hence in the silversmith's craft too they are called "those who set apart," that is, the ones who, out of a certain confusion of the mass, bring out the forms. Therefore many who could excellently discern and handle the Scriptures lay hidden among the people of God; nor did they assert the solution of difficult questions while no slanderer pressed. For was the Trinity treated perfectly, before the Arians barked against it? was penance treated perfectly, before the Novatianists resisted? So Baptism was not treated perfectly, before the rebaptizers, set outside, contradicted; nor were the things said concerning the very unity of Christ said in detail until that separation began to press the weak brethren, so that now those who knew how to handle and resolve these things, lest the weak should perish, troubled by the questions of the ungodly, might bring the obscure things of the Law into the open by their sermons and disputations. Therefore those men were divided by the wrath of his countenance, and his heart drew near to us for understanding. So understand that which he mentioned in another psalm: *A gathering of bulls*, that is, of proud butting ones, *among the cows of the peoples.* What did he call *cows*? Souls that can be seduced. To what end this? *That those may be set apart*, that is, may appear who lay hidden, *who are proved by silver.* What is *by silver*? By the eloquence of God. *The eloquences of the Lord are chaste eloquences, silver tried by fire, proved of the earth, purified seven times*[^62]. This obscure meaning, see how the Apostle brings into the light: *It is necessary*, he says, *that there be heresies too, so that those who are proved may become manifest among you*[^63]. What is *proved*? *Proved by silver*, proved by eloquence. What is *may become manifest*? *May be set apart.* Why this? On account of the heretics. What is, on account of the heretics? On account of the gathering *of bulls among the cows of the peoples.* So, then, these too *were divided by the wrath of his countenance, and his heart drew near.*

**23.** *His words were made softer than oil, and they themselves are darts.*[^64] For certain things in the Scriptures seemed hard, while they were obscure; when expounded, they were made soft. For even the first heresy among the disciples of Christ arose, as it were, from the hardness of his very word. For when he said: *Unless one eat my flesh and drink my blood, he will not have life in himself*, they, not understanding, said to one another: *This word is hard, who can listen to it?* Saying that this word is hard, they separated themselves from him: he remained with the other twelve. When they had suggested to him that those men had been scandalized by his word: *Do you too*, he says, *wish to go?* And Peter: *You have the word of eternal life, to whom shall we go?*[^65] Attend, we beseech you, and you little ones, learn godliness: Did Peter now understand the secret of that word of the Lord? He did not yet understand it; but he godly believed that the words he did not understand were good. So if a word is hard, and is not yet understood, let it be hard to the ungodly, but let it be softened for you by godliness: because it will at some time be unfolded, and become oil for you, and penetrate even to the bones.

**24.** [v. 23] Accordingly, as if Peter himself, after those men scandalized by the hardness, as they thought, of the Lord's word, said even here: *You have the word of eternal life, to whom shall we go?*—so he added: *Cast your care upon the Lord, and he will nourish you.*[^66] You are a little one, you do not yet understand the secrets of the words: perhaps the bread is hidden from you, and you must still be nourished with milk; do not be angry at the breasts, they will make you fit for the table, for which you are now less fit. Behold, through the division of the heretics many hard things have been softened: his hard words have been softened more than oil, and they themselves are darts. They have armed those who evangelize: and those very words are aimed at the breasts of all the hearers by those who press in season and out of season; with those words, with those sayings as with arrows, the hearts of men are struck unto the love of peace. They were hard, and have been made soft. Softened, they have not lost their strength, but have been turned into darts. *His words were made softer than oil, and they themselves*, the softened words, *they themselves are darts.* But you perhaps are not yet fit to be armed with these darts, and that which is perhaps obscure and hard in the word has not yet shone forth for you; *Cast your care upon the Lord, and he will nourish you.* Cast yourself upon the Lord. Behold, you wish to cast yourself upon the Lord, let no one put himself in the place of the Lord. *Cast your care upon the Lord.* See how that great soldier of Christ was unwilling that the care of the little ones be cast upon himself: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^67] What else was he saying to them, except: Cast your care upon the Lord, and he will nourish you? But now the little one wishes to cast his care upon the Lord, and someone, I know not who, runs up and says: I will take you in. As to a tossing ship he runs up and says: I will take you in. Answer you too: I seek a harbor, not a rock. *Cast your care upon the Lord, and he will nourish you.* And see that the harbor takes you in: *He will not give the just man tossing forever.* You may seem to toss in this sea, but the harbor takes you in. Only, before you enter the harbor, do not be torn from the anchor. The ship tosses at anchor, but is not thrown far from land; nor will it toss forever, even if for a time it tosses. For to the tossing pertain the words above: *I was saddened in my exercising, and I was troubled. I awaited him who would save me from faintheartedness and storm*[^68]. The tossing one speaks, but he will not toss forever; for he is bound by the anchor, the anchor is his hope. *He will not give the just man tossing forever.*

**25.** [v. 24] But of those others, what? *But you, O God, will lead them down into the pit of corruption.*[^69] The pit of corruption is the darkness of drowning. *You will lead them down*, he says, *into the pit of corruption*: because a blind man leading a blind man, both fall into the pit. God leads them down into the pit of corruption, not that he himself is the author of their fault, but that he himself is the judge of their iniquities. For God has handed them over to the desires of their heart: for they loved darkness, and not light; they loved blindness, and not vision. For behold, the Lord Jesus has shone upon the whole world; let them sing in unity with the whole world: for *there is no one who can hide himself from his heat*[^70]. But those who lead themselves over from the whole to a part, from the body to a wound, from life to amputation, what are they going to suffer, except to go into the pit of corruption?

**26.** *Men of blood and deceit.*[^71] He calls them men of blood on account of killings; and would that they were bodily, and not spiritual! For blood coming out of the flesh is seen and shuddered at: who sees the blood of the heart in one rebaptized? Those deaths require other eyes. And yet, even from these visible deaths the armed Circumcellions are not quiet anywhere. And if we attend to these visible deaths, they are *men of blood.* Attend to an armed man, whether he is a man of peace, and not of blood. If only he carried a club; but he carries a sling, he carries an axe, he carries stones, he carries lances: and carrying these, they wander everywhere they can, they thirst for the blood of the innocent. So even from these visible deaths they are *men of blood.* But of those other deaths too let us say: Would that they caused only these, and did not destroy souls. These who are *men of blood and deceit*, let them not think that we wrongly so understand men of blood as those who kill souls; they themselves so understood their own Maximianists. For when they condemned them, in the very sentence of their council they set down these words: *Their feet are swift to shed the blood* of the heralds. *Crushing and calamity are in their ways, and the way of peace they have not known*[^72]. This they said of the Maximianists. But I ask of them when the Maximianists shed bodily blood—not that they themselves would not also shed it, if there were such a multitude as could shed it, but, on account of the timidity of their small number, they rather suffered something from them than ever did such a thing themselves. Therefore I question the Donatist, and say: In your council you set down concerning the Maximianists: *Their feet are swift to shed blood.* Give me one man whom the Maximianists harmed with a finger. What else will he answer me, except what I say? Those who separated themselves from unity, and by seducing destroy souls, shed blood spiritually, not carnally. You expounded it excellently, but in your exposition recognize your own deeds. *Men of blood and deceit.* In fraud is deceit, in pretense, in seduction. What, then, of those very ones who *were divided by the wrath of his countenance*? They themselves are *men of blood and deceit.*

**27.** But what does he say of them? *They will not halve their days.*[^73] What is: *They will not halve their days*? They will not advance as far as they think: within the time they hope for, they will perish. For he is that partridge of which it was said: *In the half of his days they will forsake him, and in his last days he will be a fool*[^74]. They make progress, but for a time. For what does the Apostle say? *But evil men and seducers will advance for the worse, themselves erring, and sending others into error*[^75]. But a blind man leading a blind man, both fall together into the pit. Deservedly they fall *into the pit of corruption.* What, then, does he say? *They will advance for the worse;* yet not for long. For a little before he said: *But they will advance no further*: that is, *they will not halve their days.* Let the Apostle follow, and say why: *For their madness will be manifest to all, as also that of those others was*[^76]. *Men of blood and deceit will not halve their days. But I will hope in you, O Lord.* Deservedly those men will not halve their days, because they hoped in man. But I, from temporal days, have come to the eternal day. Why? Because I have hoped in you, O Lord.
