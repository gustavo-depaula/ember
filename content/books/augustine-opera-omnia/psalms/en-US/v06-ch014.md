# Exposition of Psalm 135

**1.** [v. 1.] It ought to be very sweet to us, and we must rejoice in its sweetness, that to which this psalm exhorts us. For it says: *Praise the name of the Lord.* And the very reason why it is just that we should praise the name of the Lord, it at once added: *Praise the Lord, you servants*[^1]. What is more just? what more worthy? what more pleasing? For if the servants do not praise the Lord, they will be proud, ungrateful, irreligious. And what do they bring about by not praising the Lord, except that they may feel the Lord to be severe? For an ungrateful servant, if he refuses to praise his Lord, does not thereby bring it about that he is not a servant. Whether you praise or do not praise, you are a servant: but if you praise, you will appease him; if you do not praise, you will offend him. So it is a good and useful exhortation; whence we ought rather to be busy about how God is to be praised, than to doubt that he is to be praised. *Praise*, then, *the name of the Lord.* The Psalm exhorts us, the Prophet exhorts us, the Spirit of God exhorts us, finally the Lord himself exhorts us, to praise the Lord. For he does not grow by our praises, but we do. God is neither made better, if you praise him; nor worse, if you blame him: but you, by praising the good, will be better; by blaming, worse: but he remains good, as he is. For if he teaches his servants—those who have deserved well of him, the preachers of his word, the rulers of his Church, the venerators of his name, the obeyers of his command—to have in their conscience the sweetness of their good life, that they may not be corrupted by praises, nor broken by the blame of men; how much more does he himself, unchangeable above all things, who teaches these things, not become greater if you praise him, nor less if you blame him? But since it is expedient for us to praise the Lord, mercifully he commands us to praise him, not arrogantly. Let us hear, then, what he says: *Praise the name of the Lord, praise the Lord, you servants.* For you do nothing unfitting, you servants, by praising the Lord: even if you were always servants only, you ought to praise the Lord; how much more ought you servants to praise the Lord, that you may deserve to be also sons?

**2.** [v. 2.] But since it is written in another psalm: *Praise befits the upright*[^2]; likewise it is written elsewhere: *Praise is not comely in the mouth of a sinner*[^3]; and likewise elsewhere it is said: *The sacrifice of praise will glorify me, and there is the way in which I will show him the salvation of God;* and consequently: *But to the sinner God said: Why do you declare my just decrees, and take up my covenant through your mouth? But you have hated discipline, and cast my words behind you*[^4]: lest perhaps anyone, because it was said: *Praise the Lord, you servants*, even if in this great house there be a bad servant, should think that the praise of the Lord profits him, he at once subjoins and teaches what kind those ought to be who ought to praise the Lord: *You who stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God*[^5]. *You who stand*, not who collapse. But those are said to stand who persevere in his commandments, who serve God in faith unfeigned, and firm hope, and sincere love, and honor his Church, and do not give offense by living badly to those who wish to come, and find on the way stones of offense. So: *You who stand in the house of the Lord, praise the name of the Lord.* Be grateful; you were outside, and you stand inside. Since, then, you stand, is it a small thing for you, where he is to be praised who raised you up as you lay, and made you stand in his house, and acknowledge him, and praise him? is this a small benefit, that we stand in the house of the Lord? Here meanwhile, in this pilgrimage, in this house, which is also called the tabernacle of pilgrimage, because here we stand, should we be ungrateful at all? Should we not consider that here we stand? should we not consider what we had been made? should we not consider where we lay, and to where we were gathered? should we not consider that all the impious did not seek the Lord, and he himself sought them who did not seek him, he himself roused them when found, he himself called them when roused, he himself led them in when called, and made them stand in his house? Whoever considers these things and is not ungrateful, despises himself altogether before the love of his Lord, by whom such great things have been bestowed on him: and since he has nothing to repay God for such great benefits, what remains for him but to give thanks, not to repay? To that very giving of thanks belongs both to take the cup of the Lord, and to call upon his name. For what shall the servant repay the Lord, for all the things which he has repaid to him[^6]? So: *You who stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God, praise the Lord.*

**3.** [v. 3.] What shall I say as to why you should praise? *Because the Lord is good.* Briefly, in one word, the praise of the Lord our God is set forth: *The Lord is good.* But good, not as the things he made are good. For God made all things good[^7]: not only good, but even very good. Heaven and earth and all the things that are in them he made good, and made them very good. If he made all these things good, what kind is he who made them? And yet, although he made good things, and he who made is much better than the things which he made; you find nothing better to say of him, except *that the Lord is good*: if indeed you understand good in the proper sense, from whom the rest are good. For he made all good things: he himself is the good whom no one made. He is good by his own good, not by a good shared from elsewhere: he is good by his very self as good, not by cleaving to another good[^8]. But for me to cleave to God is good, who did not need one by whom he might be made good; but the rest needed him that they might be made good. Do you wish to hear how singularly he is good? The Lord, when asked, said: *No one is good, except God alone*[^9]. This singularity of his goodness I neither wish to pass over briefly, nor am I able fittingly to commend. I fear lest, if I pass over it quickly, I be found ungrateful: likewise I fear lest, when I have undertaken to explain it, I be wearied out under so great a burden of the Lord's praise. Yet so, brothers, receive me both praising and not sufficient, that even if the explanation of his praise is not fulfilled, the devotion of the praiser may be accepted. Let him himself approve that I wished, let him pardon that I did not fulfill.

**4.** I am held by an unspeakable sweetness, when I hear: *The Lord is good;* and when all the things I see outwardly are considered and surveyed, since all things are from him, even when these things please me, I return to him from whom they are, that I may understand *that the Lord is good.* Again, when I have entered to him, as far as I can, I find him more inward and higher to me; because the Lord is good in such a way that he does not need those things whereby he might be good. Finally, those things I do not praise without him; but him I find perfect without those things, not in need, unchangeable, seeking no one's good whereby he might be increased, fearing no one's evil whereby he might be diminished. And what more shall I say? I find in creation heaven good, the sun good, the moon good, the stars good; the earth good, the things that are born on the earth and rest on roots, good; the things that walk and move, good; the things that fly in the air and swim in the waters, good. I call man too good: for *a good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good*[^10]. I call the angel too good, who did not fall by pride and become the devil; but cleaves by obeying to him by whom he was made. All these I call good, but yet with their own names; heaven good, an angel good, a man good: but when I refer myself to God, I think it better to say nothing than 'good.' For the Lord Jesus Christ himself said: *A good man;* and he himself likewise said: *No one is good, except God alone.* Did he not spur us to seek and to distinguish what it is to be good by another good, and good by one's own self? How good, then, is he from whom are all good things? You will find absolutely no good that is not good from him. The good making good things, just as he is in the proper sense, so too is good in the proper sense. For the things he made are not nonexistent; nor is an injury done to him when we say that the things he made are not. For why did he make them, if the things he made are not? or what did he make, if what he made is not? Since, then, even those things which he made exist, yet one comes to his comparison; and as though he alone existed, he said: *I am who am*[^11]; and: *You shall say to the children of Israel: He who is has sent me to you.* He did not say: 'The Lord God, that almighty one, merciful, just': which, if he said it, he would surely say truly. Removing from the midst all the things by which God could be named and called, he answered that he himself is called 'to be'; and as though this were his name: *This you shall say to them*, he says: *He who is has sent me.* For he is in such a way that, in comparison to him, the things that have been made are not. With him not compared, they are; since they are from him: but compared to him, they are not, because true being is to be unchangeable, which he alone is. For 'is' is, just as the good of goods is good. Consider, and see that whatever else you praise, you praise it because it is good. He is mad who praises what is not good. If you praise a wicked man in that very thing in which he is wicked, will you not too be wicked? If you praise a thief in that very thing in which he is a thief, will you not too be a partaker? If you praise a just man in that very thing in which he is just, do you not too have a part therein by praising? For you would not praise the just man, unless you loved him; you would not love him, if you had nothing from it. If, then, whatever else we praise, we praise it because it is good; no greater and better and firmer cause could be given you for praising God, than because he is good. So: *Praise the Lord, because he is good.*

**5.** How long shall we speak of his goodness? Who can conceive or embrace with his heart how good the Lord is? But let us return to ourselves, and acknowledge him in ourselves, and praise the maker in his works; because we are not fit to contemplate him: and if we will sometime be fit to contemplate him, when our heart has been cleansed by faith, that at last it may rejoice in the truth; now, since he cannot be seen by us, let us see his works, that we may not remain without his praise. So I have said: *Praise the Lord, because he is good; sing to his name, because it is sweet*[^12]. Perhaps he would be good and not sweet, if he did not give you the ability to taste. But he showed himself such to men, that he even sent bread from heaven[^13], and gave his equal Son, who is what he himself is, to be made man, and to be slain for men; that through that which you are, you might taste what you are not. For it was much for you to taste the sweetness of God; because it was remote and too high, but you were too cast down and lying in the depths. In this great separation a Mediator was sent. You could not come to God as man; God was made man: that, since as a man you can come to a man, you who could not come to God, might come to God through a man; and there was made a mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus[^14]. But if he were only man, by following that which you are, you would never arrive; if he were only God, by not comprehending that which you are not, you would never arrive: God was made man, that by following the man, which you can, you might come to God, which you could not. He himself is the Mediator, thence he was made sweet. What is sweeter than the bread of angels? How is the Lord not sweet, when man ate the bread of angels[^15]? For man does not live from one thing, and the angel from another. He himself is the truth, he himself is the wisdom, he himself is the power of God: but how the angels enjoy these, you cannot. For how do they enjoy them? As it is: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God, through whom all things were made.* But how do you reach it? Because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^16]. For that man might eat the bread of angels, the Creator of angels was made man. *So sing to his name, because it is sweet.* If you have tasted, sing; if you have tasted how sweet the Lord is, sing; if what you have tasted tastes good, praise. Who is so ungrateful either to the one cooking or to the one feeding, that, when he has been delighted by some dish, he does not return thanks by praising what he tastes? If we are not silent about these things, shall we be silent about him who gave all things? *Sing to his name, because it is sweet.*

**6.** And hear now his works. For perhaps you were straining to see the good of all goods, the good from which are all goods, the good without which nothing is good, and the good which without the rest is good; you were straining to see it, and perhaps in straining the keenness of your mind you were failing. For this I conjecture from myself; so I suffer. But even if there is someone—as can happen, and very much can—stronger than I in the keenness of mind, and who fixes the gaze of his heart long upon that which is; let him praise as he can, and in the way we cannot, let him praise. Yet thanks to him who in this psalm tempered his praise, so that it might be both of the strong and of the weak. For in that sending of his servant Moses, when he said: *I am who am*; and: *You shall say to the children of Israel: He who is has sent me to you*: because for the human mind to grasp that very 'to be' was difficult, and a man was being sent to men, though not by a man; God at once tempered his praise, and said this of himself which could be grasped sweetly; he did not wish to remain in that for which he was praised, which the praiser could not reach. *Go*, he says, *say to the children of Israel, the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob has sent me to you: this is my name forever*[^17]. Surely, Lord, you have that name; because you also said: *I am: He who is has sent me to you*: whence have you now changed the name, so as to say: *The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob*? Does not his reason seem to answer you, and say: What I said: *I am who am*; is true, but you do not grasp it: but what I said: *I am the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob*; is both true, and you grasp it? For that *I am who am* pertains to me, but that *the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob* pertains to you: and if you fail in that which I am to myself, grasp what I am to you. And lest perhaps anyone should think that what God said: *I am who am*; and: *He who is has sent me to you*, is alone his eternal name; but that what he said: *I am the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob*, is a temporal name: God did not take care, when he had said: *I am who am*; and: *He who is has sent me to you*, to say that this is his name forever; because even if he did not say it, it would be understood. For he is, and truly is, and by that very thing that he truly is, he is without beginning and without end. But because that which is for the sake of man is: *I am the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob*, lest there human anxiety should arise, because this is temporal, not everlasting, he made us secure, because from temporal things he leads us through to eternal life. *This*, he says, *is my name forever*, not because Abraham is eternal, and Isaac is eternal, and Jacob is eternal, but because God makes them eternal afterward without end: for they had a beginning, but they will not have an end.

**7.** In Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, think of his whole Church, think of all the seed of Israel: but all the seed of Israel, not only that which is of the flesh, but also that which is of faith. For the apostle was speaking to the Gentiles, to whom he said: *If, then, you are Christ's, then you are the seed of Abraham, heirs according to the promise*[^18]. So we are all blessed in the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob. He blessed a certain tree, and made it an olive, as the apostle said—the holy patriarchs themselves, whence the people of God flowered: but this olive tree was pruned, not cut down, and from it the proud branches were broken off; this is the blasphemous and impious people of the Jews. Yet good and useful branches remained; for thence the apostles. And when useful branches had been left there, by the mercy of God the wild olive of the Gentiles too was grafted in, to which the apostle says: *But you, though you were a wild olive, were grafted in among them, and were made a partaker of the richness of the olive. Do not boast against the branches: but if you boast, you do not bear the root, but the root you*[^19]. This is one tree belonging to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob; and what is more, the wild olive grafted in belongs more to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob than the broken branches. For they, by the breaking off, are no longer there; the wild olive was not there, and is there: they deserved by pride to be broken off, this one deserved by humility to be grafted in; they lost the root, this one held it. When, then, you have heard of the Israel of God, the Israel belonging to God, do not make yourselves strangers to it. You were indeed a wild olive, but you are an olive, partakers of the richness of the olive. For do you wish to know in Abraham and Isaac and Jacob how the wild olive is grafted in, lest you think that you do not belong to this tree, because you do not belong carnally to the seed of Abraham? When the Lord marveled at the faith of that centurion who was not of the people of Israel, but was of the people of the Gentiles: *Therefore*, he says, *I say to you, that many will come from the east and the west*. Behold, now the wild olive is in the hand of the grafter: *Many will come from the east and the west.* We see what he carries to be grafted; let us see where he grafts it: *And they will recline*, he says, *with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven.* We see both what he grafted, and where he grafted it. Of the proud natural branches, what does he say? *But the sons of the kingdom will go into the outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^20]. A thing foretold, a thing fulfilled.

**8.** [v. 4.] So: *Sing to the Lord, because he is sweet.* And attend to what he did concerning us. *Because the Lord has chosen Jacob for himself, Israel for a possession for himself*[^21]. Praise, sing, because he did these things. I speak such things as you can grasp. The other nations he set under angels; *the Lord chose Jacob for himself, Israel for a possession for himself.* He made his own nation a field, which he might cultivate, which he himself might sow: although he himself founded all the nations, the rest he committed to angels, but this one he appointed to be possessed and kept by himself; this people, this Jacob. By his own merit, or by his own grace? Of those not yet born he says: *Because the greater will serve the lesser*[^22]; the apostle said it. What merit could those not yet born have, before any of them had done anything good or evil? So let not Jacob exalt himself, let him not boast, let him not attribute it to his own merits. He was foreknown beforehand, predestined beforehand, chosen beforehand: not chosen by his own merits, but found and made alive by the grace of God. So too all the nations: for that it might be grafted in, what did the wild olive deserve by the bitterness of its berries, by its wild barrenness? For it was wood of the forest, not of the Lord's field; and yet he, by his mercy, grafted in even the wild olive into the olive. But the wild olive was not yet grafted in, when *the Lord chose Jacob for himself, Israel for a possession for himself.*

**9.** [v. 5.] And what does the Prophet say? *Because I have known that the Lord is great*[^23]. With his mind flying to things above, raised from the flesh, transcending creation, he knew that the Lord is great. Not all can know by seeing: let them praise what he did: *He is sweet; the Lord chose Jacob for himself; Israel for a possession for himself.* And from this praise him; for *I too have known that the Lord is great.* The Prophet was speaking, who entered into the sanctuary of God, who perhaps heard unspeakable words which it is not lawful for a man to utter[^24]; who said what could be said to men, and held with himself what could not be said. So let both that which we can be heard, and that which we cannot be believed. As to what we can, let it be heard: *Because the Lord chose Jacob for himself, Israel for a possession for himself;* as to what we cannot, let it be believed, that he himself knew *that the Lord is great.* If we said to him: We ask you, explain his greatness; would he not perhaps answer us: He whom I see is not very great, if he can be explained by me? So let him return to his works, and tell us. Let him have in his conscience the greatness of the Lord, which he saw, which he commended to us to be believed; to which he could not bring our eyes: and let him enumerate certain things which here the Lord did; that even to us, who cannot see his greatness as he saw it, it may grow sweet from his works, which we can grasp. *Because I*, he says, *have known that the Lord is great, and our God is above all gods.* What gods? As the apostle says: *Even if there are those that are called gods in heaven and on earth; as there are many gods and many lords; yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things, and we in him, and one Lord of ours, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we through him*[^25]. Let men be called gods: for it was said: *God stood in the synagogue of gods*[^26]. It was said: *I said: You are gods and all sons of the Most High*[^27]: is he not God above men? But what great thing is it, if God is above men? He is God above the angels too: because the angels did not make God, God made the angels; and it must be that he who made is above all that he made. So this man, knowing the greatness of the Lord, and seeing that he is above all creation, not only bodily, but also spiritual: *A great King*, he says, *above all gods.* He himself is the supreme God who has no god above himself. Let him tell his works, those are grasped.

**10.** [v. 6.] *All things whatsoever he willed, the Lord did, in heaven and on earth, in the sea and in all the deeps*[^28]. But who can comprehend these things? who can enumerate the works of the Lord in heaven and on earth, in the sea and in all the deeps? Yet even if we cannot comprehend all things, we ought unshakenly to believe and to hold that whatever of creatures is in heaven, whatever on earth, whatever in the sea and in all the deeps was made by the Lord; because *all things which he willed, he did in heaven and on earth, in the sea and in all the deeps*, as we have already said. Not all the things he made was he compelled to make, but *all things whatsoever he willed, he did*: the cause of all the things he made is his will. You build a house, because if you were unwilling to build, you would remain without a dwelling: necessity compels you to build a house, not free will. You make a garment, because if you did not make it, you would walk naked: so to make a garment you are led by necessity, not by free will. You plant a hill with vines, you scatter seed, because unless you did it, you would not have food: all these things you do by necessity. God made by goodness, he needed none of the things he made; therefore *all things whatsoever he willed, he did.*

**11.** Do you suppose we too have something which we do by free will? For the things we have spoken of, we do by necessity; because if we did not do them, we would remain in want and need. Do we find something which we do by free will? We do plainly find it, when by loving God we praise him. For this you do by free will, when you love what you praise; for not by necessity, but because it pleases. Whence God pleased the just and holy of God, even while scourging them. When he displeases all the wicked, he pleased them; and under his scourge, set in hardship, in labors, in wounds, in want, they praised God: he did not displease them even while tormenting them. This is to love freely, not as it were with the receiving of a reward set before one; because that reward of yours, the highest, will be God himself, whom you love freely: and you ought so to love that you do not cease to desire him for a reward, who alone can satisfy you; as Philip desired, when he said: *Show us the Father, and it is enough for us*[^29]. Rightly, because we do this by free will, and ought to do it by free will, because we do it being delighted, we do it by loving; because even if we are rebuked by him, he ought never to displease us, who is always just. This that praiser of his said: *In me, O God, are your vows, which I will render of praise to you*[^30]. And in another place: *Freely will I sacrifice to you*[^31]. What is: *Freely will I sacrifice*? Freely will I praise you. Because: *The sacrifice of praise*, he says, *will glorify me*[^32]. If you were compelled to offer to your Lord a sacrifice pleasing and acceptable to him, as formerly sacrifices were vowed in the shadow of things to come; perhaps you would not find in your herd a bull pleasing, and among the goats a he-goat worthy of the altar of the Lord, nor in your fold a ram worthy as a victim for your Lord; and not finding one you would be busy as to what to do, and would perhaps say to God: I wished, and I had it not. Can you say of praise: I wished, and I had it not? To have wished it is to have praised. For God does not seek words from you, but the heart. Finally, you can say: I had no tongue either. If anyone is struck dumb by some illness, he had no tongue, and yet he had praise. For if God had bodily ears, and needed the sound of your body, then when you remained without a tongue, you would remain without praise too: but now, because he seeks the heart, looks at the heart, within is the witness, the judge, the approver, the helper, the crowner; it is enough that you offer the will. When you can, you confess with the mouth unto salvation; but when you cannot, you believe with the heart unto justice[^33]: with the heart you praise, with the heart you bless, with the heart you lay the sacred victims on the altar of conscience; and it is answered to you: *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^34].

**12.** That God, then, who is almighty, *did in heaven and on earth all things whatsoever he willed;* you in your house do not do all the things you wish: he *did in heaven and on earth all things whatsoever he willed;* you, do even in your field all the things you wish. You wish many things, and you cannot in your house do all the things you wish: perhaps your wife contradicts, your sons contradict, sometimes even a little slave by his stubbornness contradicts, and you do not do what you wish. But I do, you say, what I wish; because I take vengeance on the disobedient and the contradicting. Not even this do you do when you wish: sometimes you wish to take vengeance, and you cannot; sometimes you threaten, and before you do what you threaten, you die. Do we suppose you do in yourself what you wish? do you bridle all your desires? Perhaps you bridle them; but do you bring it about that the very desires which you bridle do not arise? Surely you wish this, not to be tickled by the trouble of your desires; and yet *the flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh, so that you do not do the things that you wish*[^35]. You in yourself do not do what you wish; *but our God in heaven and on earth did all things whatsoever he willed.* May he give you grace, that in yourself you may do what you wish; for unless he helps, you do not even in yourself do what you wish. For when even that man did not do in himself what he wished, who said: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, but the spirit against the flesh, so that you do not do the things that you wish;* when he groaned over himself, saying: *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man; but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^36]: because not only in his house, nor only in his field, but in his flesh or in his spirit he did not fulfill the things he wished, he cried out to God, who *did all things whatsoever he willed in heaven and on earth;* and said: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* and that good and that sweet one, as though answering him, he at once subjoined: *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^37]. This sweetness, then, love, this sweetness praise. Understand the God who *did all things whatsoever he willed in heaven and on earth;* he himself will do in you too what you wish, with him helping you will fulfill your will. But while you cannot, confess; when you can, give thanks: as you lie, cry out; when raised up, do not be proud. So he *did in heaven and on earth all things whatsoever he willed, in the sea and in all the deeps.*

**13.** [v. 7.] *Raising up clouds from the end of the earth*[^38]. We see these works of the Lord in his creation: for clouds come from the end of the earth to the middle, and rain; whence they have risen you do not know. So the Prophet points it out: *from the end of the earth*, whether from the lowest, or from the encircling of the ends of the earth, he rouses clouds from where he wills, yet from the earth. *He made lightnings into rain.* For lightnings without rain would terrify you, and give you nothing. *He made lightnings into rain.* There are lightnings, you tremble; it rains, you rejoice. *He made lightnings into rain;* he who terrified, he himself acted that you might rejoice. *Who brings forth winds from his treasuries*: by hidden causes, whence you do not know. For that the wind blows, you feel; by what cause it happens, or from what treasury of reason it has been brought forth, you do not know: yet you owe to God the devotion of believing that it would not blow unless he commanded who made it, unless he brought it forth who created it.

**14.** [vv. 8-12.] So we see these things in this creation; we praise, we marvel, we bless God: let us see what he did among men for the sake of his people. *Who struck the firstborn of Egypt.* For those things were said as divine which you would love, and there were things said which you would fear: attend, that even when he is angry, he does what he wills. *He struck the firstborn of Egypt, from man even to beast. He sent signs and wonders into your midst, O Egypt, against Pharaoh and against all his servants*[^39]. You know, you have read, how great things the hand of the Lord did through Moses in Egypt, to terrify, to crush, to cast down the proud Egyptians. *Against Pharaoh and against all his servants.* It is too little in Egypt; what after the people were led out from there? *Who struck many nations*: which possessed the land that God wished to give to his people. *And slew strong kings; Sihon king of the Amorites, and Og king of Bashan, and all the kingdoms of Canaan*[^40]. All these things, as the Psalm mentions them briefly, so we read them in other books of the Lord, and great is the hand of the Lord there. When you see what was done against the impious, take care lest it be done against you: for it was done against them for this, that you might pass over, and not imitate them, and not suffer such things. Yet see that the scourge of the Lord is over all flesh. Do not think that you are not seen when you sin, do not think that you are despised, do not think that the Lord sleeps: attend to the examples of the benefits of God, when you recall them; and when you recall the vengeance of God, fear. He is almighty, both to console and to chastise. Therefore these things are useful when they are read. But when the devout man sees what the impious man suffered, he purges himself from all impiety, lest he too come into such punishment and such vengeance. So you have rightly received these things. What then did God do? He drove out the impious: *And gave their land for an inheritance, an inheritance for Israel his servant*[^41].

**15.** [v. 13.] Then the exultation of his praise: *O Lord, your name is forever*[^42]; after all these things which you did. For what do I see of the things you did? I look upon your creation which you made in heaven, I look upon this lower part where we dwell; and from here I see your benefits, of the clouds, the winds, the rains. I attend to your people: you led them out of the house of slavery, and did signs and wonders among their enemies, you punished those whom they endured as troublesome, you cast the impious out of their land, you slew their kings, you gave their land to your people; I have seen all things, and, filled with praise, I said: *O Lord, your name is forever.*

**16.** [v. 6.] These things, indeed, as they were written according to the letter, we see, we know, we praise. But if they signify something, let me not be burdensome, while I explain it as I can. Behold, in men themselves I can recognize that *in heaven and on earth he did all things whatsoever he willed.* For by the heaven, the ethereal heaven, I understand spiritual men; by earth, carnal men: of these two, as of heaven and earth, the Church of God consists; and to the spiritual belongs preaching, to the carnal belongs compliance. For *the heavens declare the glory of God, and the firmament announces the works of his hands*[^43]. But if the people of God were not the earth of God, the apostle would not say: *You are God's building, God's tillage: as a wise architect I laid the foundation; another builds upon it*[^44]. So we are the building of God, and we are the field of God. *Who*, he says, *plants a vineyard, and does not partake of its fruit*? *I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase*[^45]. So both in his Church, and in his preachers, and in his peoples, as in heaven and on earth, he did all things whatsoever he willed. It is too little in them: *In the sea and in all the deeps he did all things whatsoever he willed.* The sea is all the unbelievers, all who do not yet believe, and in them *he did all things whatsoever he willed.* For the unbelievers do not rage, except by permission; nor is vengeance taken on them, when they are perverse, unless he commanded who made all the nations. Suppose that it is sea, and not earth; is it therefore alien from the power of almighty God? *And in the sea and in all the deeps he did what he willed.* What are the deeps? The hidden hearts of mortals, the deep thoughts of men. How does God do there too what he wills? Because *the Lord questions the just and the impious: but he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^46]. And where does he question him? Elsewhere it is written: *In the thoughts of the impious there will be questioning*[^47]. So, *and in all the deeps he did all things whatsoever he willed.* The good heart is hidden, the evil heart is hidden; there is a deep both in the good heart and in the evil heart: but these are bare to God, from whom nothing is hidden. He consoles the good heart, he torments the evil heart. So *he did all things which he willed in heaven and on earth, in the sea and in all the deeps.*

**17.** [v. 7.] *Raising up clouds from the end of the earth.* What clouds? The preachers of the word of his truth. Of which clouds, in another place, being angry at his vineyard, he says: *I will command my clouds not to rain rain upon it*[^48]. And it is too little to have roused clouds from Jerusalem or from Israel, which he sent to preach his Gospel in the whole world; of which clouds it was said: *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^49]; this is too little: but because the Lord himself says: *This Gospel of the kingdom will be preached in the whole world, for a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come*[^50]; he rouses clouds from the end of the earth. For, the Gospel growing, whence will there be preachers of the Gospel at the ends of the earth, unless he rouses clouds there from the end of the earth? But of those clouds, what does he work? *He made lightnings into rain*: he bent threats to mercy, from terrors he watered. How did he water from terrors? When God threatens you through a prophet and through an apostle, and you fear; did not the flash terrify you? But when by repenting you are corrected, and acknowledge that this is done by mercy, the terror of the lightning is turned into rain. *Who brings forth winds from his treasuries.* The same preachers I think to be both clouds and winds: clouds, on account of the flesh; winds, on account of the spirit. For clouds are seen, winds are felt and not seen. Finally, since we see that the flesh is of the earth: *The clouds*, he says, *he rouses from the end of the earth.* He had expressed whence he rouses the clouds: he comes to the winds, because the spirit of man is not known whence it comes: *Bringing forth*, he says, *winds from his treasuries.* Attend a little, and let us see the rest.

**18.** [v. 8.] *Who struck the firstborn of Egypt from man even to beast.* May our firstborn be safe to the Lord, since he himself gave them to us. But a troublesome punishment it is, and an exceedingly terrible blow, the death of the firstborn. What are our firstborn? These our morals, by which we now serve God, these are our firstborn. For we have as firstfruits faith itself, from which we begin. For to the Church it was said: *You will come, and pass over from the beginning of faith*[^51]: and no one begins to live well, except from faith. So our faith is in our firstborn. When our faith is guarded, the rest can follow. For that men are purified daily by making progress to the better, and by living better, the inner man himself being renewed day by day, as the apostle says: *Even though our outer man is corrupted, yet the inner is renewed day by day*[^52]; this comes about because the firstborn faith lives, of which firstborn faith the apostle too says: *And not only that, but we ourselves too, having the firstfruits of the spirit;* that is, now giving to God the firstfruits of our spirit, that is, faith itself as our firstborn; *yet we groan within ourselves, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^53]. If, then, it is a great grace of God that our faith be preserved; it is a great punishment to kill the firstborn, when men, set in the affliction of the Church, lose faith. For they afflict the Church, that they may destroy faith: for Egypt itself is interpreted 'affliction.' Whoever, then, afflict the Church, whoever bring scandals into the Church, even if they are called Christians, their firstborn die. They will be unbelievers, they will be empty, having the name only and the sign: but in the heart they have buried their firstborn; so much so, that when you say something to such a one, for the sake of a good life, for the sake of the hope of eternal life, and on account of the fear of eternal fire, he sneers within himself; or if he is such as to dare to do so before you, he twists his mouth and says: Who has come back here from there? men say to themselves what they wish. And he is a Christian; but because, by afflicting, his firstborn has been slain, his faith is dead: and this *from man even to beast.* I will say, brothers, what I think. I understand men to be spiritually signified as the learned, on account of the rational soul, which is man: but beasts, the unlearned; yet having faith, for otherwise they would not have firstborn. There are learned men who afflict the Church, by making schisms and heresies. Accordingly you do not find faith in them either, since they have become Egypt, that is, affliction to the people of God. Their firstborn have been slain: they drag after themselves the unlearned crowds; these are the beasts. So in this affliction by which the Church is afflicted, faith dies in those who afflict: the firstborn die both in the learned themselves and in the unlearned; because God slew the firstborn of the Egyptians *from man even to beast.*

**19.** [v. 9.] *He sent signs and wonders into your midst, O Egypt, against Pharaoh and against all his servants.* Pharaoh is the king of the Egyptians. Attend to the name, and see how God does these things. The king in every nation is first; Egypt is 'Affliction,' Pharaoh is 'Dissipation.' So Affliction has Dissipation for its king; because those who afflict the Church afflict her, having been dissipated. For in order to afflict, they are dissipated: since the king leads, the people follow; dissipation goes before, affliction follows. Hear, hear these names, full of typical interpretation and wisdom: you do not find one of these names which interprets anything good, where God exercised his anger.

**20.** [vv. 10-11.] *He struck many nations, and slew strong kings.* Say what kings and what nations? *Sihon king of the Amorites.* Hear names pregnant with mysteries. *Sihon the king*, he says: *of the Amorites* he slew. He slew indeed; and now too let him slay from the hearts of his servants, and from the temptations of his Church, nor let his hand cease in slaying such kings and such peoples: for 'Sihon' is interpreted 'Temptation of the eyes'; the Amorites are interpreted 'Embittering ones.' Now here see whether we can understand how the embittering ones have temptation of the eyes for their king. The temptation of the eyes is nothing but a lie: it has color, it does not have truth. Now what wonder is it if the embittering ones have such a king, a lying king? For unless lying and pretense go before, there are no embittering ones in the Church; for they embitter precisely because they feign. The temptation of the eyes goes before, embittering follows: and in the devil himself it went before; for the temptation of the eyes is that he *transfigures himself as an angel of light*[^54]. May the hand of the Lord slay both him and them; him, that he may not lead astray; them, that they may be corrected. For in each man that king is slain, when he condemns pretense, and loves truth. The hand of God does not cease to do this. For just as he did literally then, so he does now spiritually, that he may fulfill what then he prophetically foretold. He slew also another king and another people of his: *And Og king of Bashan.* And how evil is this one too! 'Og' is interpreted 'shutting off'; 'Bashan' is interpreted 'confusion.' That king is evil who shuts off the way to God. For this the devil does; always by setting up his fictions, by setting up his idols, by setting up himself as necessary through the possessed, fortune-tellers, augurs, soothsayers, magicians, the rites of demons, he shuts off the way. Just as through Christ it is brought about that the way may be open, which had been shut off; for through him a certain redeemed man says: *And in my God I shall leap over the wall*[^55]: so the devil does nothing else but shut off the way, that there may be no belief in God. For if there is belief in God, the way is open; and Christ himself is the way[^56]: but if there is no belief in God, the way is shut off. But if it has been shut off, because there is no belief; what remains, except that when he comes in whom they did not believe, those who did not believe should be confounded? Why? Because the shutting off goes before, and confusion follows: the shutting off goes before as king, confusion follows as the people. Those whom he now shuts off, that they may not believe in Christ, when Christ has appeared, all will be confounded, and their iniquities will set themselves against them face to face. Then the confounded impious will say: *What has pride profited us*[^57]? Great mysteries, my brothers. Dissipation is the king of affliction; they are dissipated that they may afflict. Great mysteries. Temptation of the eyes, that is, deceit, is the king of the embittering ones; for they deceive that they may embitter. Shutting off is the king of confusion; for they are shut off that they may not pass over to faith, and when he comes in whom we believe, they will be confounded. *And all the kingdoms of Canaan* God slew. 'Canaan' is interpreted 'prepared for humiliation.' Humility as it were signifies something good, but only if the humility be useful: for evil humiliation is penal. For if humiliation did not pertain to punishment, it would not be said: *He who exalts himself will be humbled*[^58]: for no benefit is bestowed on him, when he is punished that he may be humbled. So Canaan is now proud. Every impious man, every unbeliever exalts his heart; he does not wish to believe in God. But this exaltation is prepared for humiliation on the day of judgment; then he will be humbled, when he does not wish: for they are vessels of wrath, which have been completed for destruction[^59]. Now let them exalt themselves, chatter, stretch themselves over the faithful, mock the faithful, blaspheme Christians; let them say: These things they say about the day of judgment are old wives' tales. This their elation is prepared for humiliation. When the judge shall come, who now, being announced, is mocked; then he who now is proud, not usefully, but penally will be humbled. But now he is not humbled, but is prepared for humiliation, that is, prepared for damnation, prepared as a victim.

**21.** [v. 13.] All these things, then, God overthrew; bodily then, when our fathers were led out of the land of Egypt; he overthrows spiritually now, nor does his hand cease until the end. Therefore, lest you think these powers of God ceased, accomplished then: *O Lord, your name*, he says, *is forever.* That is, your mercy does not cease, your hand does not cease forever to do these things, which then you foretold by figuring them: *For all those things happened to them in figure; but they were written for our correction, upon whom the ends of the ages have come*[^60]. *O Lord, your memorial is from generation to generation*[^61]. This generation and another generation: the generation by which we are made faithful, and are reborn through Baptism; the generation by which we shall rise from the dead, and, joined to the angels, shall live forever. Your memorial, O Lord, both over this generation and over that generation: because neither now has he forgotten us, so as to call us; nor then will he forget us, so as to crown us. *Your memorial, O Lord, is from generation to generation.*

**22.** [v. 14.] *Because the Lord has judged his people.* He certainly fulfilled all those things in the people of the Jews. Have his works now remained over, after he led his people into the land of promise? Plainly he will still judge: *The Lord has judged his people, and he will be entreated in his servants*[^62]. He has already judged that very people; apart from the judgment to come, the people of the Jews has been judged. What is, has been judged? The just have been separated from there, and the unjust have remained. But if I lie or am thought to lie, because I said: It has already been judged; hear the Lord saying: *For judgment I have come into this world, that those who do not see may see; and those who see may become blind*[^63]. The proud have been blinded, the humble have been enlightened. *He has judged*, then, *his people.* That very judgment Isaiah spoke: *And now you house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord.* This is too little, but what follows? *For he has sent away his people, the house of Israel*[^64]. The house of Jacob is the house of Israel; for he who is Jacob is himself Israel. You know the holy Letters, and I think it comes to your mind that Jacob himself, when he saw the angel wrestling with him, then received the name to be called Israel[^65]. He is one man, whether Jacob or Israel; he is one person: the house of Jacob and the house of Israel, one nation, one people; this he invites, and this he sends away. And now indeed you have already slain Christ, O house of Jacob; you have already slain Christ, you have already wagged your head before the cross, you have already mocked him hanging, you have already said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^66]. The physician has already prayed for the frenzied: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^67]. Surely you have already done all these things, and now believe in him whom you slew, drink the blood which you shed. And now you house of Jacob, by the testimony of Isaiah I wish to expound what he said here: *Because the Lord has judged his people, and he will be entreated in his servants.* For he is understood to have judged his people, by separating in his own people the good from the evil, the faithful from the unbelieving, the apostles from the lying Jews. This he signified, as I had begun to say, saying through the Prophet: Now after all those evil things of yours, *O you house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord.* Why do I say to you: *Come, let us walk in the light of the Lord*? Lest, by remaining in Judaism, you do not come to Christ. For why? Was not Christ always prophesied there? But now *he has sent away his people, the house of Israel.* Come, house of Jacob, because he has sent away his people the house of Jacob; come, house of Israel, because he has sent away his people the house of Israel. Which has come, and which has been sent away, except that this is the judgment, *that those who do not see may see; and those who see may become blind*[^68]? *The Lord*, then, *has judged his people.* So he separated, and will he not find there those whom he may restore into his kingdom? Plainly he will find them: *And he will be entreated in his servants.* *He has not rejected*, says the apostle, *his people, whom he foreknew.* And whence does he prove it? *For I too am an Israelite*[^69]. So *the Lord has judged his people*, by separating the good from the evil: this is: *And he will be entreated in his servants.* By whom? By the Gentiles. For how many nations have come by believing! how many estates, how many deserted places now come! There come from there I know not how many, they wish to believe, we say to them: What do you wish? They answer: To know the glory of God. Believe me, brothers, that we marvel and rejoice at such a voice of country folk. They come from I know not where, roused by I know not whom: what do I say, by I know not whom? Rather I know by whom; because: *No one comes*, he says, *to me, unless the Father has drawn him*[^70]. They come suddenly from the forest, from the desert, from the most remote and steep mountains to the Church, and most of them, and nearly all of them, have this voice, that we may see God truly teaching within: the prophetic Scripture is fulfilled by which it was said: *They will all be teachable by God*[^71]. What do you desire, we say to them? And they: To see the glory of God. *For all have sinned, and need the glory of God*[^72]. They believe, they are consecrated, they demand that clergy be ordained for them. Is it not fulfilled: *And he will be entreated in his servants*?

**23.** [vv. 15-17.] Finally, after all this arrangement and dispensation, the Spirit of God turns himself to reproaching and mocking the idols, which are now mocked even by their own worshippers: *The idols of the nations are silver and gold*[^73]. With God doing all these things, who in heaven and on earth did all things whatsoever he willed, who judged his people, and will be entreated in his servants; what remains for the figment, except to be laughed at, not adored? He was perhaps about to say 'the idols of the nations,' that we might despise all those things; was he about to say the idols of the nations are stones and wood, plaster and earthenware? I do not say these things; the material is cheap: that which they greatly love, or which they greatly honor, I say: *The idols of the nations are silver and gold.* Surely it is gold, surely it is silver; is it because silver shines, gold shines, that they therefore have eyes and see? Just as it is silver, just as it is gold, useful perhaps to the avaricious, not to the religious; rather, not even useful to the avaricious, but useful to one who uses it well, and by spending it acquires a heavenly treasure: yet now, since these things are senseless, why do you men make gods of silver and gold? The gods you make, do you not see that they do not see[^74]? *They have eyes, and they will not see; they have ears, and they will not hear; they have nostrils, and they will not smell; they have a mouth, and they will not speak; they have hands, and they will not work; they have feet, and they will not walk*[^75]. All these things the craftsman, the silversmith, the goldsmith was able to make—both eyes, and ears, and nostrils, and a mouth, and hands, and feet; but he could give neither light to the eyes, nor hearing to the ears, nor voice to the mouth, nor sense to the nostrils, nor motion to the hands, nor a path to the feet.

**24.** [v. 18.] O man, you laugh now, without doubt, at what you made, if you have come to know him by whom you were made. But of those who have not come to know him, what is said? *Let all who make them become like them, and all who trust in them.* And do you believe, brothers, that there is expressed in them a certain likeness of the idols, not indeed in their flesh, but in their inner man? For they have ears, and do not hear: for to them God cries: *He who has ears to hear, let him hear*[^76]. They have eyes, and do not see: for they have the eyes of the body, but do not have the eyes of faith. Finally, this prophecy is fulfilled throughout all the nations: see how it was said by the prophet. I mention nothing allegorical, nothing figured: hear the proper, express, simple, manifest prophecy foretold, see it fulfilled. *The Lord prevailed*, he says, *against them;* the prophet Zephaniah says it. Against those who resist, against those who rebel, and by the slaying of the faithful make martyrs, though without knowing it; *the Lord prevailed against them.* And how did he prevail? In his Church we see that he prevailed against them. They wished to extinguish, to kill a few Christians; they shed blood: from the blood of the slain so many rose up, by whom those killers of the martyrs were overcome. Yet even now they seek where to hide their idols, who at first butchered Christians on account of idols. Did not the Lord prevail against them? See whether he does what follows: *The Lord prevailed against them.* And what did he do? *He destroyed all the gods of the nations of the earth; and they will adore him, each one from his own place, all the islands of the nations*[^77]. What is this? Was it not foretold? was it not fulfilled? is it not seen as it is read? And those who have remained have eyes, and do not see; nostrils, and do not smell. They do not perceive that fragrance: *We are the good fragrance of Christ*, as the apostle says, *in every place*[^78]. What does it profit, that they have nostrils, and do not smell the so sweet fragrance of Christ? Truly it has come to pass in them, and truly it was said of them: *Let all who make them become like them, and all who trust in them.*

**25.** [vv. 19-20.] But daily they believe through the miracles of Christ the Lord, daily the eyes of the blind and the ears of the deaf are opened, the nostrils of the senseless are inspired, the mouths of the dumb are loosed, the hands of the paralyzed are bound up, the feet of the lame are set straight, from these stones sons of Abraham are raised up[^79]: to all of whom let it now be said: *House of Israel, bless the Lord.* All are sons of Abraham; and if from these stones sons of Abraham are raised up, it is plain that those are more the house of Israel who belong to the house of Israel, who belong to the seed of Abraham, not by flesh, but by faith: *House of Israel, bless the Lord.* But grant that it is said of that house, and that the people of Israel is called; from there the apostles believed, and thousands of the circumcised. *House of Israel, bless the Lord; house of Aaron, bless the Lord; house of Levi, bless the Lord*[^80]. Bless the Lord, you peoples, that is, *the house of Israel* in general; bless, you prelates, that is, *the house of Aaron*; bless, you ministers, that is, *the house of Levi.* What of the other nations? *You who fear the Lord, bless the Lord*[^81].

**26.** [v. 21.] So let us all with one voice say what follows: *Blessed be the Lord out of Zion, who dwells in Jerusalem*[^82]. Out of Zion and Jerusalem. Zion is 'Watching,' Jerusalem is 'Vision of peace.' In which Jerusalem is he now about to dwell? In that one which fell? No, but in that mother of ours, which is in the heavens, of which it was said: *Many are the sons of the deserted, more than of her who has a husband*[^83]. So now the Lord is out of Zion, because we watch until he comes: yet now, as long as we live in hope, we are in Zion. The way being finished, we will dwell in that city, which will never collapse; because both the Lord dwells in it, and guards it: which is the vision of peace, the eternal Jerusalem, of that peace, my brothers, for the praise of which the tongue does not suffice, where we will feel no enemy any more, neither in the Church, nor outside the Church, nor in our flesh, nor in our thought. Death will be swallowed up in victory, and we will have leisure to see God in eternal peace, made citizens of Jerusalem, the city of God[^84].
