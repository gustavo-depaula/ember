# Exposition of Psalm 47

**1.** [v. 1] The title of the psalm is, *A Praise of a Canticle, to the sons of Korah, on the second day of the sabbath*[^1]. Of this which the Lord deigns to grant, receive it as the sons of the firmament. For on the second day of the sabbath, that is, after the first day, which we call the Lord's Day, which is also called the second weekday, was made the firmament of heaven, rather the firmament heaven. For God called the firmament heaven. But on the first day he had made the light, and divided it from the darkness; and had called the light, day, and the darkness, night[^2]. But as the context of this psalm indicates, God said something even beforehand in that work of his, which should be fulfilled in us: and according to the condition of this creature the ages have run. For not in vain does the Lord say of Moses: *For he wrote of me*[^3]: except that all things that are written, even when God founded the creature, can be interpreted to the signification of things to come; so that you may understand that God made the light, when Christ rose from the dead. For then truly that light was divided from the darkness, when immortality was separated from mortality. What, then, follows, except that for the head there should be made the body too, which is the Church? In short, there is also a psalm on the first day of the sabbath, in which most openly the resurrection of the Lord is declared: for there it is said: *Lift up the gates, you princes, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the King of glory shall enter in*[^4]. What more evident than that Christ is the King of glory? of whom it was said: *If they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^5]. The second day of the sabbath, then, we ought to understand as nothing but the Church of Christ: but the Church of Christ in the saints, the Church of Christ in those who are written in heaven, the Church of Christ in those who do not yield to the temptations of this world. For these are worthy of the name of firmament. Therefore the Church of Christ in those who are firm, of whom the Apostle says: *But we who are firm ought to bear the weaknesses of the weak*[^6], is called the firmament. Of this it is sung in the psalm: let us hear, recognize, be associated, glory, reign. For hear and recognize that it is called firmament even in the apostolic Letters: *Which is,* he says, *the Church of the living God, the pillar and firmament of truth*[^7]. Of this firmament it is sung to the sons of Korah, whom you know to be the sons of the bridegroom crucified in the place of Calvary. For Korah is interpreted "Baldness." It follows in this psalm, which is inscribed, *on the second day of the sabbath.*

**2.** [v. 2] *Great is the Lord, and exceedingly to be praised*[^8]. Behold *the Lord is great, and exceedingly to be praised*: but do the unfaithful praise the Lord? Do even those who believe and live badly praise the Lord, through whom it comes about that the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles? do they praise the Lord? Or indeed, even if they praise, is their praise accepted, when it is written: *Praise is not comely in the mouth of a sinner*[^9]? You have said, then: *The Lord is great, and exceedingly to be praised*: but here, where. *In the city of our God, in his holy mountain.* Of this it is said elsewhere: *Who shall ascend the mountain of the Lord? The innocent of hands and clean of heart*[^10]. In these *the Lord is great, and exceedingly to be praised*: that is, *in the city of our God, in his holy mountain.* This is the city set on a mountain, which cannot be hidden: this is the lamp which is not hidden under the bushel, known to all, spread abroad to all[^11]. But not all are its citizens, but those in whom *the Lord is great, and exceedingly to be praised.* What, then, is this city, let us see, lest perhaps, because it was said: *In the city of our God, in his holy mountain,* we ought to seek this mountain, where we too may be heard. For not in vain is it said in another psalm: *With my voice I cried to the Lord, and he heard me from his holy mountain*[^12]. This mountain, then, helped you that you might be heard. For if you did not ascend it, lying below you could cry, but could not be heard. Who, then, is this mountain, brothers? With great care to be inquired into, with great solicitude to be investigated; with labor too to be occupied and ascended. But if it is in some part of the lands, what shall we do? Shall we go on pilgrimage from our land, that we may be able to come to that mountain? Rather then we go on pilgrimage, if we are not in it. For it is our city, if we are members of the king, who is the head of that city. Where, then, is this mountain? If it has held some region, we must labor, as I said, to come to it. But why are you busy? Would that you be not slothful to ascend the mountain, as the mountain was not slow to come to the sleeper. For there was a certain cornerstone, contemptible, on which the Jews stumbled, cut from a certain mountain without hands, that is, coming from the kingdom of the Jews without hands, because human work did not approach Mary, of whom Christ was born. But if this stone, where the Jews stumbled on it, there remained, you would have nowhere to ascend. But what came about? What does the prophecy of Daniel say? What, except that this stone grew, and was made a great mountain? How great? So great that it filled the whole face of the earth. Therefore this mountain, by growing and filling the whole face of the earth, came to us. Why, then, do we seek the mountain as though absent, and not now ascend onto it as present, that in us may be *the Lord great, and exceedingly to be praised*?

**3.** [v. 3] In short, lest you should not recognize this mountain in this psalm too, and should think it to be sought in some part of the lands, see what follows: when he had said: *In the city of our God, in his holy mountain,* what did he add? *Spreading wide the exultations of the whole earth, the mountains of Zion*[^13]. Zion is one mountain: why, then, *mountains*? Or is it because to Zion pertained even those who came from a different quarter, that they might meet one another in the cornerstone, and there should be made those two walls like two mountains, one from the circumcision, the other from the uncircumcision; one from the Jews, the other from the Gentiles: now no longer adverse; even if diverse, because from a diverse quarter, now in the corner not even diverse? *For he himself,* he says, *is our peace, who made both one*[^14]. That very cornerstone, *the stone which the builders rejected, has become the head of the corner*[^15]. The mountain joined in himself two mountains. One house, and two houses: two on account of those coming from a diverse quarter, one on account of the cornerstone, in which both are joined to one another. Hear this too: *The mountains of Zion; the sides of the north, the city of the great king.* For you had understood Zion as a certain one place, where Jerusalem was founded, and in it there met you only the people from the circumcision; which indeed was gathered from the remnants by Christ, but for the greater part was, as it were, winnowed away like chaff. For it is written: *The remnants shall be saved*[^16]. But attend to the Gentiles too, see the wild olive too grafted into the fatness of the olive[^17]. Behold the Gentiles: *The sides of the north*: the sides of the north have been joined to the city of the great king. The north is wont to be contrary to Zion: for Zion is in the south, the north over against the south. Who is this north, except he who said: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^18]? The devil had held the kingdom of the ungodly, and had possessed the Gentiles serving idols, adoring demons: and the whole, whatever there was of the human race everywhere through the world, by clinging to him, had been made the north. But since he who binds the strong man, takes away his vessels, and makes his vessels his own, men, freed from the unbelief and superstition of demons, believing in Christ, were aligned to that city, met in the corner that wall coming from the circumcision, and there was made the city of the great king what had been the sides of the north. Therefore in another Scripture too it is said: *From the north clouds of golden color; in these is the great glory and honor of the Almighty*[^19]. For great is the glory of the physician, when from despair the sick man recovers. From the north clouds, and not black clouds, not dark, not foul, but of golden color. Whence, except from grace illuminating through Christ? Behold: *The sides of the north, the city of the great king.* Sides indeed, because they had clung to the devil. For whoever cling to anyone are called his sides. For of some men too we are wont to speak thus: He is a good man, but he has bad sides: that is, he himself excels in probity, but they who are joined to him are malign. The sides of the north, then, were those who clung to the devil: whence came that son too, of whom we just now heard, that he was dead, and lived again; he had perished, and was found. For by going into a far country, he had come even to the north, and there, as you heard, he had clung to one of the princes of that region[^20]. He was made, then, a side of the north, by clinging to the prince of that region: but because the city of the great king is gathered even from the side of the north, returning to himself, he said: I will rise, and go to my father. And his father met him, who says of him: He was dead, and lived again; he had perished, and was found. That fatted calf was the cornerstone. In short, the elder son too, who was unwilling to feast, exhorted by the father, entered: and the two walls now, like those two sons coming to the calf, made the city of the great king.

**4.** [v. 4] Let this psalm follow, then, and say: *God shall be known in its houses*[^21]. Now in houses on account of the mountains, on account of the two walls, on account of the two sons. *God shall be known in its houses.* But he commends grace, therefore he added: *When he shall uphold it.* For what would that city be, unless he had upheld it? Would it not at once fall, unless it had such a foundation? For other foundation no one can lay, except that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus[^22]. Let no one, then, glory in his own merits; but he who glories, let him glory in the Lord. Because then it is that great city, then in it the Lord is recognized, when he shall uphold it: as the physician upholds the sick man to be cured, not as he is to be loved. For the physician hates the fever. The physician does not love the sick man, and the physician loves the sick man: if he loved the sick man, he would always wish him sick; again, if he did not love the sick man, he would not come to the sick man: but he loves the sick man, to make him well. Therefore the Lord upheld this city, and was recognized in it, that is, his grace was recognized in that city; because whatever that city which glories in the Lord has, it does not have from itself. For on account of this it is said: *For what do you have that you have not received? But if you have received, why do you glory as if you had not received?*[^23] *God shall be known in its houses, when he shall uphold it.*

**5.** [vv. 5-7] *For behold the kings of the earth have been gathered together*[^24]. Now behold those sides of the north, see how they come, see how they say: *Come, let us ascend to the mountain of the Lord: for he has announced to us his way, that we may enter into it*[^25]. *Behold the kings of the earth have been gathered together, and have come together into one.* Into what one, except into that cornerstone? *They themselves, seeing, so marveled.* After the wonder of the miracles and glory of Christ, what followed? *They were troubled, they were moved, trembling laid hold of them.* Whence did trembling lay hold of them, except from the conscience of their sins? Let the kings, then, run after the king, let the kings recognize the king. Hence elsewhere: *But I,* he says, *have been set king by him over Zion his holy mountain, preaching the precept of the Lord: The Lord said to me: You are my Son, this day have I begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and your possession the ends of the earth; you shall rule them with a rod of iron, and like a potter's vessel you shall break them*[^26]. The king set on Zion was heard, then, and possession was handed over to him even to the ends of the world of the earth. The kings ought to fear, lest they lose the kingdom, lest the kingdom be taken from them, as the wretched Herod feared, and for the sake of a little one killed the little ones. But fearing to lose the kingdom, he did not deserve to recognize the king. Would that he too had adored the king with the Magi! that, not by seeking the kingdom badly, he had lost the innocents, and, guilty, perished. For as far as pertained to him, he lost the innocents: but as far as pertained to Christ, even as a little one he crowned the little ones dying for him. Therefore the kings ought to have feared when it was said: *But I have been set king by him,* and an inheritance even to the ends of the earth he who set me king will give me. But why do you envy, kings? See, do not envy. For that king is far otherwise who said: *My kingdom is not of this world*[^27]. Do not, then, fear lest the kingdom of this world be taken from you: a kingdom will be given you, but of the heavens, where that king is. Therefore what follows there? *And now, kings, understand.* You were now preparing to envy: *understand;* it is said of another king, whose kingdom is not of this world. Deservedly, then, *the kings were gathered together into one, they were troubled, trembling laid hold of them.* Hence to them too this is said: *And now, kings, understand, be instructed, all you who judge the earth. Serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling*[^28]. And what did they do? *There griefs as of one giving birth.* What are *griefs as of one giving birth,* except the griefs of one repenting? See that very conceiving of grief and giving birth: *From your fear,* says Isaiah, *we conceived, and gave birth to the Spirit of salvation*[^29]. So, then, the kings conceived from the fear of Christ, that by giving birth they might bring forth salvation by believing in him whom they feared. *There griefs as of one giving birth.* Where you hear of one giving birth, await the offspring. The old man gives birth, but the new man is born. *There griefs as of one giving birth.*

**6.** [v. 8] *With a violent wind you will shatter the ships of Tarshish*[^30]. Briefly it is understood, you will overthrow the pride of the nations. But whence from this history is the overthrow of the pride of the nations said? On account of *the ships of Tarshish.* The learned have sought what city Tarshish is, that is, what city is signified by this name: and to some it seemed that Cilicia was called Tarshish, from the fact that its metropolis is called Tarsus. Of which city was the apostle Paul too, born in Tarsus of Cilicia. But some understood it to be Carthage: perhaps once so named, or by some manner of speaking so signified. For in the prophet Isaiah it is found thus: *Howl, you ships of Carthage*[^31]. But in Ezekiel by different interpreters, by some it was interpreted Carthage, by others Tarshish; and by this diversity of interpreters it can be understood that this was called Tarshish which was called Carthage. But it is manifest that the beginnings of the kingdom of Carthage flourished with ships, and so flourished, that among the other nations it excelled in trafficking and seafaring. For when Dido, fleeing her brother, came down to the lands of Africa, where she founded Carthage, she had taken the ships which were prepared for trade in her region for her flight, the princes of that region consenting with her: and those very ships, even when Carthage was founded, did not cease from trafficking. And hence that city became too proud, so that fittingly by its ships is understood the pride of the nations, presuming on uncertain things as on the blasts of the winds. Let it now not presume on its sailing, and as it were on the prosperity of this world, as of the sea: let our foundation be in Zion; there we ought to be made firm, not blown about by every wind of doctrine[^32]. Whoever, then, had swelled from the uncertain things of this life, let them be overthrown; and let all the pride of the nations be subjected to Christ, *who shatters with a violent wind the ships of Tarshish*: not of any city, but *of Tarshish.* How *with a violent wind*? With a most powerful fear. For so all pride trembled at him about to judge, that it believed in the humble one, lest it dread the exalted one.

**7.** [v. 9] *As we have heard, so also we have seen*[^33]. O blessed Church! at one time you heard, at one time you saw. It heard in the promises, it sees in the fulfillments: it heard in prophecy, it sees in the Gospel. For all the things that are now fulfilled, were before prophesied. Lift up your eyes, then, and spread them through the world; see now the inheritance even to the ends of the world of the earth: see now fulfilled what was said: *All the kings of the earth shall adore him, all the nations shall serve him*[^34]. See fulfilled what was said: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and your glory over all the earth*[^35]. See him whose feet and hands were fixed with nails, whose bones hanging on the wood were numbered, over whose garment the lot was cast: see reigning him whom they saw hanging: see sitting in heaven him whom they despised walking on earth. See from this fulfilled: *All the ends of the earth shall be reminded, and shall be converted to the Lord, and all the families of the nations shall adore in his sight*[^36]. Seeing these things cry out with joy: *As we have heard, so also we have seen.* Deservedly the Church herself from the nations is so called: *Hear, daughter, and see, and forget your people and the house of your father*[^37]. Your father was the North, come to the mountain of Zion. Hear, and see: not see, and hear; but hear, and see: first hear, afterward see. You hear first what you do not see, you will see afterward what you heard. *A people,* he says, *whom I did not know, served me, in the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^38]. If in the hearing of the ear it obeyed, then it did not see. And where is: *Those to whom it was not announced of him, shall see; and those who have not heard, shall understand*[^39]? Those to whom the prophets were not sent, they first heard and understood the prophets: those who first did not hear, afterward hearing marveled. Those remained to whom they were sent, carrying the codices, not understanding the truth; having the tablets of the Testament, and not holding the inheritance. But we, *as we have heard, so also we have seen. In the city of the Lord of powers, in the city of our God.* There we have heard, there too we have seen. He who is outside it, neither hears nor sees; he who is in it, is neither deaf nor blind. *As we have heard, so also we have seen.* And where do you hear? where do you see? *In the city of the Lord of powers, in the city of our God. God has founded it forever.* Let not the heretics, cut up into parts, insult: let not those exalt themselves who say: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there*[^40]. He who says: *Behold, here it is, behold, there,* leads to parts. Unity God promised: the kings were gathered into one, they were not scattered through schisms. But perhaps this city which held the world, will sometime be overthrown. Far be it: *God has founded it forever.* If, then, God founded it forever, why do you fear lest the firmament fall?

**8.** [v. 10] *We have received, O God, your mercy in the midst of your people*[^41]. Who received, and where did they receive? Did not your people itself receive your mercy? If your people received your mercy, how *have we received your mercy, and in the midst of your people*? as though they are others who received, and others in whose midst they received. A great sacrament, but yet known: when from this too, that is, from these verses, what you know has been carved out and dug out, it will not be cruder, but sweeter. For now indeed all are reckoned the people of God who bear his sacraments, but not all pertain to his mercy. For all who receive the sacrament of the baptism of Christ are called Christians; but not all live worthily of that sacrament. For there are certain ones of whom the Apostle says: *Having the form of godliness, but denying its power*[^42]. Yet on account of that very appearance of godliness they are named among the people of God: just as to the threshing-floor, as long as it is being threshed, not only the grains, but also the chaff pertain. Will it pertain to the granary too? But in this midst of the bad people is the good people, who received the mercy of God. He lives worthily of the mercy of God, who hears and holds and does what the Apostle says: *Charging you, then, we beseech you, that you receive not the grace of God in vain*[^43]. He, then, who does not receive the grace of God in vain, himself receives both the sacrament and also the mercy of God. And what does it harm him, that he is in the midst of a disobedient people, until this threshing-floor be winnowed, until the good be separated from the bad? what does it harm him to dwell in the midst of the people? Let him be of those who are called the firmament, receiving the mercy of God; let him be a lily in the midst of thorns. For that even those thorns pertain to the people of God, do you wish to hear? So is the very likeness set: *Like a lily,* he says, *in the midst of thorns, so is my beloved in the midst of daughters*[^44]. Did he say, in the midst of strangers? No; but, *in the midst of daughters.* There are, then, bad daughters, and among them is the *lily in the midst of thorns.* And so those who have the sacraments, and do not have good morals, are both called of God and not of God; and are called his and strangers: his, on account of his sacrament; strangers, on account of their own vice. So too the strange daughters: daughters, on account of the form of godliness; strangers, on account of the loss of power. Let there be a lily there, let it receive the mercy of God, let it hold the root of the good flower, let it not be ungrateful for the sweet rain coming from heaven. Let the thorns be ungrateful, let them grow from the showers; they grow for the fire, not for the granary. *We have received, O God, your mercy in the midst of your people.* In the midst of your people not receiving your mercy we have received your mercy. For he came into his own, and his own did not receive him. In the midst of them, however, as many as received him, he gave them power to become sons of God[^45].

**9.** Now here it occurs to everyone considering: And what? This people which in the midst of the people of God receives the mercy of God, what a number does it have? How few they are! scarcely is anyone found: will God be content with these, and lose so great a multitude? They say this who promise themselves what they have not heard from God promising. And truly if we live badly, if we enjoy the delights of this world, if we serve our own lusts, will God lose us? For how many are those who seem to keep the precepts of God? Scarcely is one or two or the fewest found: will God free these alone, and damn the rest? Far be it, they say: when he comes and sees so great a multitude at the left, he will have mercy, and grant pardon. This plainly that serpent too promised to the first man: for God had threatened death, if he tasted. But he: *Far be it,* he said, *you shall not die the death*[^46]. They believed the serpent, they found it true what God threatened, false what the devil had promised. So now too, brothers, set before your eyes the Church in the likeness and similitude of paradise: the serpent does not cease to suggest what it then suggested. But the fall of the first man ought to avail us for the experience of being cautious, not for the imitation of sinning. Therefore he fell, that we might rise. Let us answer such suggestions what Job answered. For him too the devil tempted through the woman as through Eve, and was conquered on the dunghill, who had conquered in paradise. Therefore let us not hear such voices, nor think these to be few: they are many, but they lie hidden among the more. For we cannot deny that the bad are more, and so much more, that among them the good do not at all appear, just as the grains do not appear on the threshing-floor. For whoever sees the threshing-floor, can think that there is only chaff. Give an inexperienced man, and he thinks it is done in vain that the oxen are set on, that men there sweat under the heat to thresh the chaff; but there is also the mass to be purged by winnowing. Then the abundance of grain will come forth, which lay hidden in the abundance of chaff. And now do you wish to find the good? Be one, and you will find them.

**10.** [v. 11] Against this despair, then, see what follows in this psalm. For when he had said: *We have received, O God, your mercy in the midst of your people,* he signified that there is a people not receiving the mercy of God, in whose midst certain ones receive the mercy of God: and lest it should occur to men, that they are so few as to be almost none; how did he console with the following words? *According to your name, O God, so also your praise unto the ends of the earth*[^47]. What is this? *Great is the Lord and exceedingly to be praised, in the city of our God, in his holy mountain*[^48]; nor can there be praise of him, except in his saints. For those who live badly do not praise him; but even if they preach with the tongue, they blaspheme by their life. Because, then, his praise is only in his saints, let not the heretics say to themselves: In us his praise has remained, because we are few, and separated from the crowd; we live justly, we praise God, not only by speaking, but also by living. It is answered them from this psalm: Why do you say that you praise God in a part, to whom it was said: *According to your name, O God, so also your praise unto the ends of the earth*? That is, as you are known through all lands, so you are also praised through all lands; nor are there lacking those who now praise you through all lands. But those praise who live well. *For according to your name, O God, so also your praise,* not in a part, but *unto the ends of the earth. Your right hand is full of justice*: that is, there are many also of those who will stand at the right hand. Not only will those be many who will stand at the left, but there too will be the fullness of the mass set at the right: *Your right hand is full of justice.*

**11.** [v. 12] *Let mount Zion rejoice, and let the daughters of Judah exult, on account of your judgments, O Lord*[^49]. O mount Zion, O daughters of Judah, you labor now among the tares, among the chaff, among the thorns you labor; but exult on account of the judgments of God. God does not err in judging. Live discerned, even if you are born commingled; not in vain did that voice go out from your mouth and from your heart: *Do not destroy my soul together with the ungodly, and my life with the men of blood*[^50]. He, so great a craftsman, will winnow, he will carry the winnowing-fan in his hand, so that not one grain of wheat fall into the heap of chaff to be burned, nor one ear of chaff pass over to the mass to be stored in the granary. Exult, daughters of Judah, on account of the judgments of God who does not err; and do not now rashly judge. Let it pertain to you to gather, let it pertain to him to separate. *Let mount Zion rejoice, and let the daughters of Judah exult, on account of your judgments, O Lord.* But do not think the daughters of Judah to be the Jews. Judah is "Confession." All the sons of confession are the sons of Judah: because, *Salvation is from the Jews*[^51], is nothing else than Christ from the Jews. This too the Apostle says: *For not he who is a Jew in the open, nor that which is in the open in the flesh is circumcision; but he who is a Jew in secret, and the circumcision of the heart, in spirit, not in letter, whose praise is not from men, but from God*[^52]. Be such a Jew: glory in the circumcision of the heart, even if you do not have the circumcision of the flesh. *Let the daughters of Judah exult, on account of your judgments, O Lord.*

**12.** [v. 13] *Surround Zion, and embrace her*[^53]. Let it be said to those who live badly, in whose midst is that people which received the mercy of God: In the midst of you is a people living well: *Surround Zion.* But how? *Embrace her.* Do not surround her with scandals, but surround her with love; that you may imitate those who live well in the midst of you, and by imitating them may be incorporated into Christ, whose members they are. *Surround Zion, and embrace her. Tell it in her towers.* In the height of her fortifications preach her praises.

**13.** [v. 14] *Set your hearts on her power*[^54]. Not that you should have the form of godliness, and deny its power; but *set your hearts on her power.* What is the power of this city? He who wishes to understand the power of this city, let him understand the force of love. It is the power which no one conquers. Its fire no waves of the world, no rivers of temptation extinguish. Of it was said: *Love is strong as death*[^55]. For as death, when it comes, cannot be resisted, with whatever arts, with whatever medicines you meet it; he who is born mortal cannot avoid the violence of death: so against the violence of love the world can do nothing. For from the contrary the likeness is given of death; for as death is most violent to take away, so love is most violent to save. For through love many have died to the world, that they might live to God. Kindled by this love the martyrs, not feigned, not blown about by vain glory, not such as those of whom it was said: *If I deliver my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^56], but such as the love of Christ and of truth truly led to the passion; what did the temptations of the raging do to them? The eyes of their weeping families had greater violence than the persecutions of those assailing them. For how many did sons hold back lest they suffer! at how many's knees did wives throw themselves, lest they be left widows! how many parents did their children try to prevent from dying, as we know and read in the passion of blessed Perpetua! These things were done. But the tears, however great and with however great force they flowed; when would they extinguish the ardor of love? This is the power of Zion, to which elsewhere too it is said: *Let there be peace in your power, and abundance in your towers*[^57]. *Tell it in her towers: Set your hearts on her power, and distribute her houses.*

**14.** What do we understand here: *Set your hearts on her power; and distribute her houses*[^58]? That is, distinguish house from house, do not confuse them. For there is a house having the form of godliness, and not having godliness: but there is also a house having both the form and godliness. *Distribute,* do not confuse. But then you distribute and do not confuse; when you set your hearts on her power, that is; when you have been made spiritual through love. Then you will not rashly judge; then you will see that the bad in no way harm the good, as long as we are in this threshing-floor: *Distribute her houses.* There can be another understanding too. Those two houses, one coming from the circumcision, the other from the uncircumcision, it was said to the apostles that they should distribute them. For when Saul was called, and made the apostle Paul, coming into the unity of his fellow apostles, he had such an agreement with them, that they should go to the circumcision, he to the uncircumcision. By this dispensation of his apostolate they distributed the houses of the city of the great king, and agreeing in the corner, they divided the Gospel by dispensation, joined it by love. And truly this is rather to be understood. For it follows, and shows that this was said to the preachers: *And distribute her houses: that you may tell it in another generation,* that is, that even to us who would be afterward the dispensation of their Gospel should come. For they did not labor only for those with whom they lived on this earth; nor the Lord only for those apostles, to whom he deigned to show himself alive even after the resurrection, but for us too. For he spoke to them, and signified us, when he said: *Behold, I am with you all the days, even to the consummation of the world*[^59]. Were they going to be here even to the consummation of the world? Likewise he says: *I do not pray for these only, but for those who shall believe in me through their word*[^60]: therefore he attended to us, because he suffered on our account. Deservedly, then, it is said: *That you may tell it in another generation.*

**15.** [v. 15] What shall you tell? *That this is our God*[^61]. He seemed earth, the founder of the earth was not seen: the flesh was held, but God in the flesh was not recognized. The flesh was held by those from whom that very flesh had been taken, for the Virgin Mary was from the seed of Abraham: they remained at the flesh, they did not understand the divinity. O apostles, O great city! preach it in the towers, and say: *This is our God.* So, so, as he was despised, as he lay a stone before the feet of those stumbling, that he might humble the hearts of those confessing; so *this is our God.* Surely he was seen, as it was said: *After this he was seen on earth, and conversed with men*[^62]. *This is our God.* And he is a man, and who is it that shall know him? *For this is our God.* But perhaps for a time, like the false gods. For because they can be called gods, but cannot be such, for a time they are even called so. For what does the prophet say to them, or what does he advise to be said to them? This you shall say to them: What? *Let the gods who did not make heaven and earth perish from the earth; and from under these things which are under heaven*[^63]. This is not such a God, since our God is above all gods. Above what all gods? *Because all the gods of the nations are demons, but the Lord made the heavens*[^64]. He, then, is our God, *this is our God*: How long? *Forever and unto ages of ages; he himself shall rule us forever.* If he is our God, he is also our king; he protects us, because he is God, lest we die; he rules us, because he is king, lest we fall. But by ruling us he does not break us; for those whom he does not rule, he breaks. *You shall rule them,* he says, *with a rod of iron, and like a potter's vessel you shall break them*[^65]. But there are those whom he does not rule: these he does not spare, breaking them like a potter's vessel. From him, then, let us choose to be ruled and freed: because *this is our God forever, and unto ages of ages; and he himself shall rule us forever.*
