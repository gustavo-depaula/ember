# On the Same Psalm 68

**1.** [vv. 17-18] The latter part of the psalm of which we spoke yesterday to Your Charity had remained today to be explained by us. And I see it is time to repay the debt, if only its length does not leave us debtors even today. And so I say this beforehand, and ask that you not expect a lengthy discourse on the things that are manifest. For so we can necessarily linger over the more obscure, and perhaps supply what we owe, so that on other days we owe other things, and repay other things. Let us see, then, what follows. After he said: *Nor let the pit close its mouth over me;* which yesterday we commended to Your Charity, that with all intention of mind and faith of godliness we should beware lest this curse come upon us. For then the pit closes its mouth over a man, that is, the depth of iniquity, when he not only lies sunk in sins, but even loses the entrance of confession. But when a man says: I am a sinner; even the depth of the pit is lit up by some light. There follows, then, our Lord Jesus Christ crying out amid sufferings, head and body; as we have commended, that in certain places you recognize the words of the head; but those that are said in such a way that they cannot suit the head, you refer to the body. For so Christ speaks as one; because he is also truly one of whom it was said: *They will be two in one flesh*[^1]. For if in one flesh, why do you marvel that he is also in one voice? There follows, then: *Hear me, O Lord, because your mercy is sweet.*[^2] He asserted this cause why he ought to be heard, because the mercy of God is sweet. Was it not rather consequent that he should say: Hear me, O Lord, that your mercy may be sweet to me? Why, then: *Hear me, O Lord, because your mercy is sweet!* The sweetness of the mercy of the Lord he commended from his tribulation in a manner with other words, when he said: *Hear me, O Lord, because I am troubled.* For truly he who says: *Hear me, O Lord, because I am troubled,* states the cause why he asks to be heard: but to a man set in tribulation, it is necessary that the mercy of God be sweet. Of this sweetness of the mercy of God see what Scripture says in another place: *As rain in dryness, so beautiful is the mercy of God in tribulation*[^3]. What there he says, *beautiful;* this he here says, *sweet.* Nor would bread be sweet, unless hunger went before. Therefore even when the Lord permits or causes us to be in some tribulation, even then he is merciful; for he does not withdraw the nourishment, but stirs up the desire. And so what does he now say: *Hear me, O Lord, because your mercy is sweet*? Now do not defer the hearing; I am in so great tribulation, that your mercy is sweet to me. For this reason, then, you deferred to come to aid, that what you came to aid might be sweet to me: now, then, there is no further reason for you to defer; my tribulation has come to a certain measure of calamity, let your mercy come to do the work of goodness. *Hear me, O Lord, because your mercy is sweet. According to the multitude of your compassions look upon me*: not according to the multitude of my sins.

**2.** [v. 18] *Do not turn away your face from your boy.*[^4] And this too is a commendation of humility, *from your boy,* that is, from the small one; because I have now lost pride through the discipline of tribulation: *Do not turn away your face from your boy.* This is that beautiful mercy of God which he mentioned above. For in the following verse he expounds what he said: *Because I am troubled, hear me quickly.* What is, *quickly*? Now there is no reason to defer: I am troubled; my affliction has gone before, let your mercy follow.

**3.** [v. 19] *Attend to my soul, and redeem it.*[^5] It does not need exposition: let us see, then, what follows. *For the sake of my enemies rescue me.* This is plainly a wonderful petition, neither to be touched on briefly, nor to be skipped over in passing; altogether wonderful: *For the sake of my enemies rescue me.* What is: *For the sake of my enemies rescue me*? That they may be confounded, that they may be tormented by my liberation. What then, if there were not those who would be tormented by my liberation, ought I not to be helped? and is your liberation acceptable to you then, when it will be another's damnation? Behold, there are no enemies, who would either be confounded, or tormented by your liberation: will you so remain? will you not be freed? Or is it to this end, that it may avail your enemies, that they too may be converted by your liberation? But this too is a wonder, whether it has some cause for the petition. For surely is a servant of God freed by the Lord his God for this, that others may profit? What, if there were lacking those who would profit, was that servant of God not to be freed? Wherever, then, I turn myself, whether to the punishment, or to the liberation of the enemies, I do not see the cause of this petition: *For the sake of my enemies rescue me*: unless we understand something, which when I have said it with the Lord's help, he will judge in you who dwells in you. There is a certain hidden liberation of the saints: this comes about for their own sake. There is a certain public and manifest one: this comes about for the sake of their enemies; whether to be punished, or to be freed. For certainly God did not free the Maccabee brothers from the fires of the persecutor, against whom Antiochus, raging, even employed their mother, that by her coaxings they might be turned to the love of life, and, by loving to live for men, might die to God. But that mother, now not like Eve, but like mother Church, those whom she had borne with grief that she might recognize them alive, she saw with joy dying; and exhorted them to this, that they should choose rather to die for the paternal laws of the Lord their God, than to live against them. What here shall we believe, brethren, except that they were freed? But hidden was their liberation: finally Antiochus himself, by whom they were killed, seemed to himself to have done something which his cruelty dictated, or rather incited. But the three young men were openly freed from the furnace of fire; because their body too was rescued, their salvation was public. The former, then, were crowned in secret, the latter freed in the open; yet all were saved. But what is the fruit of the liberation of the three young men? Why was their crown deferred? Nebuchadnezzar himself was converted to their God, and preached him, because he rescued his servants; whom he had despised, when he had sent them into the furnace. There is, then, a hidden liberation; there is a manifest liberation. The hidden liberation pertains to the soul, the manifest liberation pertains also to the body. For in secret the soul is freed, in the open the body. Further, if it is so, in this psalm let us recognize the voice of the Lord: to the hidden liberation pertains what he said above: *Attend to my soul, and redeem it.* There remains the liberation of the body: because, when he rose again, and ascended into the heavens, and sent the Holy Spirit from above, those were converted to his faith who had raged at his death; and from enemies were made friends by his grace, not by their own righteousness. Therefore there followed: *For the sake of my enemies rescue me. Attend to my soul;* but this in secret: *But for the sake of my enemies rescue* my body too. For it will profit my enemies nothing, if you free the soul alone: they will believe that they have done something, that they have accomplished something. What profit is there in my blood, while I go down into corruption? Therefore, *attend to my soul, and redeem it;* which you alone know: then also *for the sake of my enemies rescue me,* that my flesh may not see corruption.

**4.** [v. 20] *For you know my reproach, and my confusion, and my shame.*[^6] What is reproach? what is confusion? what is shame? Reproach is what the enemy lays to one. Confusion is what bites the conscience. Shame is what makes an honest forehead blush even at the laying of a false charge. It is not a crime; or even if it is a crime, it is not his to whom it is laid: but yet the weakness of the human mind is very often ashamed, even when something false is laid; not because it was laid, but because it was believed. All these are in the body of the Lord. For confusion could not be in him, in whom no fault was found. The crime was laid to Christians, this very thing, that they were Christians. That indeed was glory: the strong received it gladly, and so received it, that they did not blush at all about the name of their Lord. For shamelessness had covered their face, who had the forehead of Paul saying: *For I do not blush at the Gospel; for it is the power of God unto salvation to everyone who believes*[^7]. O Paul, are you not an adorer of the Crucified? It is too little, he says, not to blush about it; nay, in this alone I glory, in which the enemy thinks I should blush. *For far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world*[^8]. To such a forehead, then, only reproach could be laid. For neither could there be confusion in a conscience now healed, nor shame in a forehead so free. But when it was laid to certain ones that they had killed Christ; deservedly they were pricked by an evil conscience, and savingly confounded and converted, that they could say: *You knew my confusion.* You, then, O Lord, know, not only my reproach, but also my confusion; in certain ones also shame, who, although they believe in me, yet blush publicly to confess me before the ungodly, the human tongue prevailing with them more than the divine promise. See them, then: and such are commended to God, not that he should so leave them, but that by helping them he should perfect them. For a certain believing and wavering one said: *I believe, Lord; help my unbelief*[^9]. *In your sight are all who trouble me.* Why it is a reproach to me, you know; why a confusion, you know; why a shame, you know: therefore rescue me for the sake of my enemies, because you know these things of mine, they do not know them; and through this, because they themselves are in your sight, not knowing these things they cannot either be confounded or corrected, unless you manifestly rescue me for the sake of my enemies.

**5.** [v. 21] *My heart expected reproach and misery.*[^10] What is, *expected*? He foresaw these things as future, he foretold these things as future. For he came for no other purpose. If he were unwilling to die, he would not wish to be born either: the cause of the resurrection did both. For two things in the human race were known to us, but one was unknown. For we knew that men are born and die; that they rise again and live forever we did not know. That he might show us what we did not know, he took on the two things that we knew. To this end, then, he came. *My heart expected reproach and misery.* But the misery of whom? For he expected misery, but rather of those crucifying, rather of those persecuting; that in them there should be misery, in him mercy. For having compassion on their misery even while hanging on the cross: *Father,* he says, *forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^11]. *My heart expected reproach and misery: and I waited for one who would be sad together with me, and there was none.* What, then, did it profit that I expected it? that is, what did it profit that I foretold it? what did it profit that I said I had come for this? It came about, that what I said might be fulfilled: *I waited for one who would be sad together with me, and there was none; and for those who would console me, and I did not find them;* that is, there was none. For what in the earlier verse he said: *I waited for one who would be sad together with me;* this in the following verse, *and for those who would console.* But what in the earlier verse, *and there was none;* this in the following verse, *and I did not find.* Therefore not another sentence is added, but the earlier one repeated. Which sentence, if we examine it, some question can arise. For surely his disciples were not saddened, when he was led to the passion, when he was hung on the wood, when he died? So saddened, that that Mary Magdalene, who first saw him, rejoicing announced to those mourning what she had seen. The Gospel says these things; it is not our presumption, not our suspicion: it is established that the disciples grieved, it is established that they mourned. Outside women wept, when he was led to the passion; to whom, turning, he said: *Weep; but for yourselves, not for me*[^12]. How, then, did he wait for one who would be sad together with him, and there was none? We attend, and we find them sad, and mourning, and lamenting; whence this sentence seems to us wonderful: *I waited for one who would be sad together with me, and there was none; and for those who would console me, and I did not find them.* Let us attend more diligently, and we shall see that he waited for one who would be sad together with him, and there was none. For they were saddened carnally about the mortal life, which was to be changed by death, and restored by resurrection: hence was that sadness. For it ought to be about those who, being blind, killed the physician, who, like frenzied men perniciously fevered, did injury to him from whom salvation had been brought to them. He wished to cure, they to rage: hence sadness to the physician. Seek whether he found a companion of this sadness. For he does not say, I waited for one who would be sad, and there was none; but, *one who would be sad together with me,* that is, would be sad over that matter over which I was, *and I did not find him.* Peter certainly loved very much, and without doubt cast himself to tread the waves, and was freed at the voice of the Lord: and following him led to the passion with the boldness of love, yet, troubled, denied him three times. Whence, except because it seemed evil to him to die? For he avoided that which he thought evil. This, then, he grieved over in the Lord too, which he himself avoided. Therefore he had also said before: *Far be it from you, Lord; be merciful to yourself, this shall not happen*: when he merited to hear, *Satan;* after he had heard, *Blessed are you, Simon Bar Jonah*[^13]. Therefore in the sadness which the Lord had over those for whom he prayed: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^14], he found no companion. *And I waited for one who would be sad together with me, and there was none.* Altogether there was none. *And for those who would console, and I did not find them.* Who are those who console? Those who make progress. For they themselves console us, they themselves are a solace to all preachers of truth.

**6.** [v. 22] *And they gave gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.*[^15] It was indeed done to the letter, and the Gospel indicates this to us. But it is to be understood, brethren, this very thing that I did not find those who console, this very thing that I did not find one who would be sad together with me, this was my gall, this bitter to me, this was vinegar: bitter on account of grief, vinegar on account of their oldness. For we read that gall was indeed offered to him, as the Gospel says; but for drink, not for food. But yet it is to be taken thus, and fulfilled, what here had been foretold: *They gave gall for my food*: and in this very deed, not only in this saying, we ought to seek the mystery, to knock at the secrets, to enter through the rent veil of the temple, to see there the sacrament, either of that which was so said, or of that which was so done. *They gave,* he says, *gall for my food.* That which they gave was not itself food; for it was drink: but *they gave it for food;* because the Lord had already taken food, and into it the gall was thrown. But he had taken sweet food, when he ate the Passover with his disciples: there he showed the sacrament of his body. Into this food so sweet, so delightful, of the unity of Christ, which the Apostle commends, saying: *Because there is one bread, we many are one body*[^16]; into this sweet food who is it that puts gall, except the gainsayers of the Gospel, like those persecutors of Christ? For the Jews sinned less, crucifying him walking on earth, than those who despise him sitting in heaven. What, then, the Jews did, that into the food which he had already taken they gave that bitter drink to drink, this they do who, by living badly, bring scandal to the Church: this the embittering heretics do; but let them not be exalted in themselves. They give gall upon so delightful food. But what does the Lord do? He does not admit it to his body. In this sacrament the Lord himself, when they offered him gall, tasted it, and was unwilling to drink. If we did not suffer them, we would not taste it at all: but because it is necessary to suffer them, it is necessary to taste it. But because such cannot be in the members of Christ, they can be tasted, they cannot be received into the body. *And they gave gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.* I thirsted, and received vinegar; that is, I desired their faith, and found oldness.

**7.** [vv. 23-24] *Let their table become before them a trap.*[^17] Such a trap as they presented to me, by giving me such a drink, let such a trap be to them. Why, then, *before them*? It would suffice: *Let their table become a trap.* There are such who know their iniquity, and most obstinately persevere in it: before them their trap comes about. Exceedingly perilous are they themselves, who go down into hell alive. Finally of the persecutors what was said? *Unless the Lord were in us, perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive*[^18]. What is, *alive*? Consenting to them, and knowing that we ought not to consent to them. Therefore before them their trap comes about, and they are not corrected. Or, because the trap is before them, that they may not fall into it? Behold, they know the trap, and put in their foot, and submit their necks to be held. How much better it was to be turned away from the trap; to recognize the sin, to condemn the error, to be free from the bitterness, to pass over into the body of Christ, to seek the glory of the Lord! But so great is the presumption of mind, that the trap is before them, and they fall into it. *Let their eyes be darkened, that they may not see,* follows here; that, since without cause they saw, it may come about for them not to see either. *Let,* then, *their table become before them a trap. Before them let it become a trap,* is not of one wishing, but of one prophesying; not that it may come about, but because it will come about. This we have often commended, and you ought to remember, lest what the presaging mind says in the Spirit of God seem to imprecate out of ill will. Let it come about, then; nor can it be otherwise, except that these things befall such men. And since we see through the Spirit of God that such things are said to befall the evil; let us understand these things in them for this purpose, that we may avoid such things: for this it profits us to understand, and to profit from the enemies. Let it become to them, then, *both for a requital, and for a scandal.* And surely this is not unjust? It is just. Why? Because *for a requital*: for nothing would befall them which was not owed. *For a requital* it comes about, *and for a scandal,* because they are a scandal to themselves.

**8.** *Let their eyes be darkened, that they may not see; and bow down their back always.*[^19] This is consequent. For of those whose eyes have been darkened, that they may not see, it follows that their back is bowed down. Whence this? Because when they have ceased to know the things above, it is necessary that they think of the lower things. He who hears well: Lift up the heart, does not have a bowed back. For with upright stature he awaits the hope laid up for him in heaven; especially if he sends before his treasure, whither his heart follows. But those who, now blinded, do not understand the hope of the future life, think of the lower things: and this is to have a bowed back; from which disease the Lord freed that woman. For Satan had bound her for eighteen years; and her, bowed down, he set upright, and because he did it on the sabbath, the Jews were scandalized: well were they scandalized at her set upright, themselves bowed. *And bow down their back always.*

**9.** [v. 25] *Pour out upon them your wrath, and let the indignation of your wrath seize them.*[^20] These are plain: but yet, *let it seize them,* we recognize as it were fleeing. But whither will they flee? Into heaven? You are there. Into hell? You are present. They are unwilling to receive their wings, to fly straight: *let the indignation of your wrath seize them;* let it not permit them to escape.

**10.** [v. 26] *Let their dwelling become deserted.*[^21] This now in the open. For as he commended his liberation not only as hidden, saying: *Attend to my soul, and redeem it;* but also as manifest according to the body, adding: *For the sake of my enemies rescue me*; so to these too he foretells certain future calamities as hidden, of which he was speaking a little before. For how few are there who understand the unhappiness of a man whose heart is now blind? Let the eyes of his body be taken away; all men call him wretched: let him lose the eyes of the mind, but yet let him flow with all abundance of things; they call him happy, but those who have likewise lost the eyes of the mind. Therefore now what is openly done, that it may appear to all that vengeance has been taken on them? For the blindness of the Jews is a hidden vengeance: but what is the manifest one? *Let their dwelling become deserted, and let there be no one to dwell in their tents.* This was done in that very city of Jerusalem, in which they seemed to themselves powerful, by crying out against the Son of God: *Crucify, crucify*[^22]; and by prevailing, because they could kill him who raised the dead. How powerful, how great they seemed to themselves! There followed afterward the vengeance of the Lord; the city was stormed, the Jews subdued, I know not how many thousands of men killed. No one of the Jews is now permitted to come there; where they could cry out against the Lord, there they are not permitted by the Lord to dwell. They lost the place of their fury: and would that even now they would recognize the place of their rest! What did it profit them, Caiaphas saying: *If we let this man go thus, the Romans will come, and take from us both our place and our kingdom*[^23]? Behold, both they did not let him go alive, and he lives; and the Romans came, and took from them both place and kingdom. Just now we were hearing, when the Gospel was read: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, how often I have willed to gather your children, as a hen her chicks under her wings; and you would not! Behold, your house will be left to you deserted*[^24]. This too is said here: *Let their dwelling become deserted, and let there be no one to dwell in their tents. Let there be no one to dwell,* but of their number. For all those places are both full of men, and empty of Jews.

**11.** [v. 27] Why this? *Because they have persecuted him whom you struck, and they have added upon the grief of my wounds.*[^25] What, then, did they sin, if they persecuted one struck by God? What is imputed to their mind? Malice. For that was done in Christ which it behooved. He had come to suffer, and he punished the one through whom he suffered. For Judas the traitor was punished, and Christ was crucified; but he redeemed us by his blood, and punished him from his own price. For he threw back the price of silver for which the Lord had been sold by him, nor did he recognize the price for which he himself had been redeemed by the Lord. This was done in Judas. But since we see that there is a certain measure of requital in all, and that no one can be permitted to rage more than he has received in power; how did they add, or what is this striking of the Lord? Doubtless he spoke from the person of him from whom he had taken his body, from whom he had assumed flesh, that is, of the human race, of that very Adam who was first struck with death on account of his sin. Mortal, then, men are here born with punishment: to this punishment they add, whoever men have persecuted. For now this man would not die, unless God had struck him: why, then, do you, O man, rage more? is it too little for a man, that he is at some time to die? Each one of us, then, bears his own punishment: to this punishment they wish to add who persecute us. This punishment is the striking of the Lord. For the Lord struck man by a sentence: *On the day you touch it,* he says, *you will die the death*[^26]. Of this death he had taken on flesh, and our old man was crucified together with him. From his voice he said these things: *Him whom you struck, they have persecuted, and they have added upon the grief of my wounds.* Upon what grief of wounds? Upon the grief of sins they added: for he called his sins wounds. But do not look to the head; attend to the body, according to whose voice it was said by him in that psalm, in which he showed his voice, because its first verse he cried from the cross: *O God, my God, look upon me; why have you forsaken me?* There, following, he says: *Far from my salvation are the words of my offenses*[^27]. These are the wounds inflicted by the robbers on the way, on him whom he lifted up onto his beast; whom the priest and Levite passing by had found and despised, by whom he could not be cured; but the passing Samaritan had mercy on him; he drew near, and lifted him onto his own beast. Samaritan is interpreted in Latin "Guardian": but what guardian, except the Savior our Lord Jesus Christ? Who, since he rose from the dead now no more to die, neither slumbers nor sleeps who keeps Israel. *And they have added upon the grief of my wounds.*

**12.** [v. 28] *Add iniquity upon their iniquity.*[^28] What is this? Who would not be terrified? It is said to God: *Add iniquity upon their iniquity.* Whence will God add iniquity? Has he iniquity to add? For we know it to be true what was said through the apostle Paul: *What then shall we say? Is there iniquity with God? Far be it*[^29]. Whence, then: *Add iniquity upon iniquity*? how are we to understand this? Let the Lord be present that we may say it, and, on account of your weariness, may be able to say it briefly. It was their iniquity, that they killed a just man: another was added, that they crucified the Son of God. Their raging was as it were against a man: but if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. They by their iniquity willed to kill him as it were a man: iniquity was added upon their iniquity, that they crucified the Son of God. Who added this iniquity? He who said: *Perhaps they will reverence my Son; him I will send*[^30]. For they were wont to kill the servants sent to them, the collectors of the rent and reward. He sent his Son himself, that they might kill him too. He added iniquity upon their iniquity. And did God do this by raging, or rather by justly requiting? *Let it become to them,* he says, *for a requital, and for a scandal.* They had deserved to be so blinded, that they did not recognize the Son of God. And this God did, adding iniquity upon their iniquity; not by wounding, but by not healing. For as you increase a fever, you increase a disease, not by applying the disease, but by not coming to aid: so, because they were such as did not deserve to be cured, in that very malice they made progress in a manner, as it was said: *But the evil and criminal advance for the worse*[^31]; and iniquity was added upon their iniquity. *And let them not enter into your righteousness.* This is plain.

**13.** [v. 29] *Let them be blotted out of the book of the living.*[^32] Were they then sometime written there? Brethren, we ought not to take it thus, that God writes anyone in the book of life, and blots him out. If a man said: *What I have written, I have written,* of the title where it was written: *King of the Jews*[^33]; does God write anyone and blot him out? He is foreknowing; he predestined all who would reign with his Son in eternal life before the foundation of the world. These he wrote down; the book of life contains these very ones. Finally, in the Apocalypse what does the Spirit of God say, when the same Scripture speaks of the pressures to come from the Antichrist? *All those will consent to him,* it says, *who are not written in the book of life*[^34]. Accordingly, without doubt, those will not consent who are written. How, then, are these blotted out from there, where they were never written? This was said according to their hope, because they themselves thought they were written. What is: *Let them be blotted out of the book of life*? Let it also be established to them that they are not there. From this manner of speaking it was said in another psalm: *A thousand will fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand*[^35]; that is, many will be scandalized, and from that number who hoped they would sit with you, and from that number who hoped they would stand at your right hand, separated from the goats on the left: not because, when anyone has stood there, he will afterward fall, or when anyone has sat with him, he will be cast away; but because many were going to fall into scandal, who already thought themselves to be there; that is, many who hoped they would sit with you, many who had hoped they would stand at the right, they themselves are going to fall. So, then, here too those who hoped, as it were by the merit of their righteousness, to be written in the book of God, to whom it is said: *Search the Scriptures, in which you think you have eternal life*[^36]: when their damnation has been brought even to their own knowledge, they will be blotted out of the book of the living, that is, they will know that they are not there. For the verse that follows expounds what was said: *And let them not be written with the just.* I said, then, *Let them be blotted out,* according to their hope; but according to your equity what do I say? *Let them not be written.*

**14.** [v. 30] *I am poor and grieving.*[^37] Why this? Was it that we might recognize that out of bitterness of mind this poor man curses? For he said many things which may befall them. And as though we said to him: Why such things? do not so much; he answers: *I am poor and grieving.* They brought me to want, they put me down to this grief; therefore I say these things. Yet it is not the indignation of one cursing, but the foretelling of one prophesying. For of his poverty and his grief, certain things which he says afterward, he is going to commend to us, that we may learn to be poor and grieving. *For blessed are the poor, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven;* and: *Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be consoled*[^38]. This, then, he himself first shows us; and therefore: *I am poor and grieving.* His whole body says this. The body of Christ on this earth is poor and grieving. But let Christians be rich. Plainly if they are Christians, they are poor; in comparison with the heavenly riches which they hope for, they reckon all their gold as sand. *I am poor and grieving.*

**15.** [v. 31] *And the salvation of your face, O God, has upheld me.*[^39] Is this poor man forsaken? When do you deign to bring a ragged poor man to your table? But this poor man the salvation of the face of God has upheld; in his own face he has hidden his want. For of him it was said: *You will hide them in the hiding of your face*[^40]. But in that face do you wish to know what the riches are? These riches furnish you this, that you dine on what you wish, when you wish; but those, that you never hunger. *I am poor and grieving; and the salvation of your face, O God, has upheld me.* To what end? That I may no longer be poor, no longer grieving. *I will praise the name of God with a song, I will magnify him in praise.* It has already been said; this poor man praises the name of God with a song, magnifies him in praise. When would he dare to sing, unless he were refreshed from hunger? *I will praise the name of God with a song, I will magnify him in praise.* Great riches! What gems of the praise of God he brought forth from the inner treasure! *I will magnify him in praise.* These are my riches. *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away.* Did he then remain wretched? Far from it. See the riches: *As it pleased the Lord, so it is done; blessed be the name of the Lord*[^41]. *I will praise the name of God with a song, I will magnify him in praise.*

**16.** [v. 32] *And it will please God,* this that I will praise him, it will please, *more than a young calf, bringing forth horns and hoofs.*[^42] More pleasing to him will be the sacrifice of praise, than the sacrifice of a calf. *The sacrifice of praise will glorify me; and there is the way in which I will show him the salvation of God. Sacrifice to God the sacrifice of praise, and pay to the Most High your vows*[^43]. Therefore I will praise God; and it will please him more than a young calf, bringing forth horns and hoofs. More, then, will his praise going forth from my mouth please God, than a great victim brought to his altar. Or is something to be said about the horns and hoofs of this calf? He who is well instructed, and opulent with the praise of God, ought to have both horns with which to toss the adversary, and hoofs with which to stir up the earth. For you know that growing calves do this, and growing into bullish boldness. For therefore he is "young," on account of the new life. Some heretic, then, perhaps contradicts; let him be tossed with the horns. Another does not contradict, but yet relishes the earthly basely; let him be stirred up with the hoofs. Therefore over this calf my praise will please you; now that praise, after the poverty and grief, in the eternal fellowship of the angels, where there will be neither an adversary to be tossed in combat, nor a lazy one to be stirred up from the earth.

**17.** [v. 33] *Let the needy see, and be glad.*[^44] Let them believe, and rejoice in hope. Let them rather be needy, that they may deserve to be filled; lest, when they belch out the fattening of pride, the bread by which they may savingly live be denied them. *Seek the Lord,* you needy; hunger, and thirst: for he himself is the living bread which came down from heaven. *Seek the Lord, and your soul will live.* You seek bread, that your flesh may live; seek the Lord, that your soul may live.

**18.** [v. 34] *Because the Lord has heard the poor.*[^45] He heard the poor; nor would he hear the poor, unless they were poor. Do you wish to be heard? Be poor: let grief cry out of you, not disdain. *Because the Lord has heard the poor; and his prisoners in chains he has not despised.* Offended by his servants, he made them prisoners in chains; but crying out from the chains he did not despise. What are these chains? Mortality, the corruptibility of the flesh, are chains by which we are bound. And do you wish to know the heaviness of these chains? Hence it is said: *The body that is corrupted weighs down the soul*[^46]. When men wish to be rich in the age, they seek rags for these chains. But let the rags of the chains suffice: seek only as much as is enough for driving off necessity. But when you seek superfluous things, you desire to load your chains. In such custody, then, let the chains alone at least remain. Let the day's own malice suffice for it. Of this malice we cry out to God: *Because the Lord has heard the poor; and his prisoners in chains he has not despised.*

**19.** [v. 35] *Let heaven and earth praise him, the sea and all creeping things in them.*[^47] The true riches of this poor man are these, to consider the creature and to praise the Creator. *Let heaven and earth praise him, the sea and all creeping things in them.* And this creature alone praises God, when, it being considered, God is praised.

**20.** [vv. 36-37] Hear another thing too: *Because God will save Zion.*[^48] He repairs his Church: he incorporates the faithful nations into his Only-begotten; he does not defraud those believing in him of the reward of his promise. *Because God will save Zion: and the cities of Judah will be built.* These are the Churches. Let no one say: When will this be, that the cities of Judah are built? O if you would recognize the structure, and be a living stone, that you may enter into it! Even now the cities of Judah are being built. For Judah is interpreted "Confession." Of the confession of humility the cities of Judah are built; so that outside them the proud remain, who blush to confess. *Because God will save Zion.* What Zion? Hear in what follows: *And the seed of his servants will possess it; and those who love his name will dwell in it.*

**21.** The psalm is finished; but let us not leave these two verses for a little while: for they admonish us something, lest by despairing we not enter into that structure. *The seed,* he says, *of his servants will possess it.*[^49] Now, then, who are *the seed of his servants*? Perhaps you say, the Jews born of Abraham: but we who are not born of Abraham, how shall we have this city? But they are not the seed of Abraham, those Jews to whom it was said: *If you are sons of Abraham, do the deeds of Abraham*[^50]. *The seed,* then, *of his servants,* the imitators of the faith of his servants, *will possess it.* Finally the last verse expounds the earlier. For as though troubled, lest you think this is perhaps said of the Jews: *And the seed of his servants will possess it,* saying: We are the seed of the Gentiles, who worshiped idols, and served demons; what, then, is to be hoped for us in this city? he at once added, that you may presume and hope: *And those who love his name will dwell in it.* For this is *the seed of his servants, those who love his name.* For because his servants loved his name; whoever do not love his name, let them not call themselves the seed of his servants; and those who love his name, let them not deny themselves to be the seed of his servants.
