# On the Same Psalm 118 (6)

**1.** [v. 17.] If you remember the earlier parts of this psalm, dearest ones, they ought to help us to understand the following. For the things which speak as it were from the person of one man are the members of Christ, and pertain to one head as one body. For above he had said: *By what does the younger one correct his way? By keeping your words.* Behold, now more openly, that he may do it, he asks help: *Render,* he says, *to your servant; I will live, and keep your words.* If he asked good things to be rendered to himself for good, he had already kept the words of God. But he did not say: Render to your servant, because I have kept your words; as though he demanded a good reward for the good of obedience: but he said: *Render to your servant; I will live, and keep your words.* Which is nothing else than to say that the dead cannot keep them: namely the unbelievers, of whom it is said: *Let the dead bury their dead*[^1]. Wherefore if we understand the dead as unbelievers, but the living as believers; since the just lives by faith, nor can the words of God be kept except by faith, which works through love: this one asks for himself who says: *Render to your servant; I will live, and keep your words.* And since before faith nothing is owed to man except evil for evil, but God renders through undue grace good for evil; this recompense he asks who says: *Render to your servant; I will live, and keep your words.* For there are four recompenses: either evil is rendered for evil, as God is going to render eternal fire to the impious; or good for good, as he is going to render the eternal kingdom to the just; or good for evil, as Christ through grace justifies the impious; or evil for good, as Judas and the Jews through malice persecuted Christ. Of these four recompenses, the two former pertain to justice, that evil be rendered for evil, good for good: the third pertains to mercy, that good be rendered for evil: the fourth God does not know; for he renders evil for good to no one. But this which I set in the third place is first necessary: for unless God rendered good for evil, there would in no way be those to whom he could render good for good.

**2.** See that Saul, afterward Paul: *Not from works,* he says, *of righteousness which we did, but according to his mercy he made us safe through the washing of regeneration*[^2]. And again: *I who was formerly a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and an insulter; but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief*[^3]. And again: *But I give counsel, as one who has obtained mercy from the Lord to be faithful*[^4]: that is, to live; because the just lives by faith. He was dead, then, first through his own unrighteousness, before he lived through the grace of God. But his very death he thus confesses: *But when the commandment came,* he says, *sin revived, but I died, and the commandment which was unto life was found to be unto death for me*[^5]. God rendered him, then, good for evil, that is, life for death; namely such a recompense as is here asked, where it is said: *Render to your servant; I will live, and keep your words.* And he lived, and kept his words, and began to pertain to that other recompense, in which good is rendered for good: for which he says: *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; for the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord will render to me on that day, the just judge*[^6]. Certainly *just,* by rendering good for good; because first merciful, by rendering good for evil. Although even that very justice, by which good is rendered for good, is not without mercy; because this too is written: *Who crowns you in compassion and mercy*[^7]. For he who said: *I have fought the good fight,* when would he conquer, except with him giving of whom he says: *Thanks to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*[^8]? And he who finished the course, when would he run, when arrive, except with him helping of whom he says: *Therefore it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy*[^9]? And he who kept the faith, when would he do this, except, as he himself says, having obtained mercy to be faithful?

**3.** Let human pride, then, nowhere exalt itself: by his own gifts God renders good rewards. But this one who now prays and says: *Render to your servant, I will live;* if he were utterly dead, would not pray: but from him he received the beginning of good desire, from whom he asks the life of obedience. For those had some faith who said: *Lord, increase our faith*[^10]. But that one both confessed his unbelief, and did not deny his faith, who, when he was asked whether he believed, said: *I believe, Lord; help my unbelief*[^11]. Now certainly beginning to live, he asks for life, who, believing, prays for obedience: not a reward for it kept; but, that it may be kept, help. For with life growing he grows alive every day, who is renewed from day to day.

**4.** [v. 18.] But knowing that the words of God cannot be kept by obedience, unless they are seen by understanding, he adds this too to the prayer and says: *Reveal my eyes, and I will consider the wonders of your law.* To this also pertains what he adds: *I am a sojourner on earth*: or, as some manuscripts have: *I am a stranger on earth; do not hide from me your commandments.* For what he said above: *Reveal my eyes;* this he said afterward, *do not hide from me*: and what he had said there, *the wonders of your law;* this, repeating it in another way, he says, *your commandments.* But nothing is more wonderful in the commandments of God than: *Love your enemies*[^12]; that is, render good for evil. But of this sojourning or dwelling-as-a-guest the sermon must not be confined; and therefore not this one about this matter, but another must be awaited, and, with the Lord helping, rendered.
