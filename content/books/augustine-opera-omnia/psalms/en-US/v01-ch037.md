# On the Same Psalm 30 (2)

**1.** Let our attention return to the remainder of the psalm, and let us recognize ourselves in the words of the Prophet. For if we examine ourselves in the time of tribulation, we shall rejoice in the time of recompense. I had commended to your Charity, when I expounded the first parts of this psalm, that Christ speaks; and how the whole Christ is to be understood, with head and body, I had not been silent: I had confirmed it also by testimonies of the Scriptures, as far as it seems to me, sufficiently apt and clear; so that it could not at all be doubted that Christ is head and body, bridegroom and bride, the Son of God and the Church, the Son of God made the son of man for our sake, that he might make the sons of men sons of God; and so the two are in one flesh in a great mystery, who are recognized in the Prophets as two in one voice. The thanksgiving expressed above was his, saying: *You have looked upon my lowliness, you have saved my soul from its necessities, and you have not shut me up in the hands of the enemy, you have set my feet in a spacious place*: it is the thanksgiving of a man freed from tribulation, of the members of Christ freed from affliction and ambush. And again he says: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am in tribulation*. In tribulation there is surely a narrowness: how then, *You have set my feet in a spacious place*? If he is still in tribulation, how are his feet in a spacious place? Is it perhaps that there is indeed one voice, because there is indeed one body; but in some members spaciousness is felt, in some narrowness—that is, some feel the ease of righteousness, others toil in tribulation? For if it were not that some members suffer this and others that, the Apostle would not say: *If one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice with it*[^1]. Some Churches, for example, have peace; some are in tribulation: in those that have peace, the feet are in a spacious place; those that are in tribulation suffer narrows: but the tribulation of the latter saddens the former, and the peace of the former consoles the latter. For so it is one body, that there be no rending; but nothing makes a rending except dissension. But love makes the joining, the joining embraces unity, unity preserves love, love arrives at glory. Let it say, then, from certain members: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am in tribulation; my eye is troubled in anger, my soul and my belly*.

**2.** We ask whence this tribulation comes, since a little before he seemed, when freed, to rejoice, a certain righteousness being poured into him abundantly by the gift of God, and from that spaciousness made for his feet in the breadth of love. Whence, then, does this tribulation arise, unless perhaps from that which the Lord says: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*[^2]? For first, a fewness of saints being commended, then, as nets being cast, the Church was multiplied, and innumerable ones were caught, of whom it had been foretold: *I have announced and spoken, they were multiplied beyond number*[^3]; ones who even weighed down the boats and broke the nets, as is set down in that first fishing before the Lord's passion. From these multitudes, then, were heaped up those by whom the churches are so crammed at Easter that the narrowness of the very walls cannot hold their crowds. But how is this man not in tribulation over this multitude, when he sees those filling the theaters and amphitheaters who a little before filled the churches? the same in wickednesses, who a little before were in the praises of God? the same blaspheming God, who used to answer Amen to God? Let him remain, let him endure, let him not fail even in the abundant multitude of the wicked, because not even the grain fails in the multitude of chaff, until after the winnowing it is put into the granary, and is there in the fellowship of the saints, lest, being troubled, it suffer some of the dust. Let him endure, then, because the Lord too, when he had said, *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*, lest at this foretold abundance of iniquity our feet should slip and waver, at once added what might raise up the faithful, what might console and strengthen, saying: *He who endures to the end, this one will be saved*[^4].

**3.** Attend, then, to this man, as far as it seems to me, set in this tribulation. Since indeed, set in tribulation, he ought as it were to grieve (for tribulation has a fitting grief), he calls himself angry in tribulation, and says: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am in tribulation; my eye is troubled in anger*. If you are in tribulation, why are you angry? This man is angry at others' sins. Who would not be angry, seeing men confessing God with the mouth, denying him by their ways? Who would not be angry, seeing men renouncing the world in words and not in deeds? Who is there who would not be angry, seeing brothers laying snares for brothers, not keeping the faith of the kiss they fix in the mysteries of God? And who could enumerate all the things at which the body of Christ is angry, which lives within by the Spirit of Christ, which groans like a grain among the chaff? For these who so groan, who are so angry, scarcely appear: just as the grains scarcely appear when the threshing floor is threshed. He who does not know how many ears were put on it thinks the whole is chaff: and from the fact that the whole is thought chaff, from there a great mass will be cleansed. At these, then, who do not appear and who groan, he is angry, who says in another place: *The zeal of your house has consumed me*[^5]. He says elsewhere too, since indeed he sees many doing evil things: *Weariness has held me, because of the sinners who forsake your law*[^6]. He says in another place too: *I saw the senseless, and I was wasting away*[^7].

**4.** But this anger—it must be feared—lest it be so great that it turn into hatred. For anger is not yet hatred: for you are angry at a son, you do not hate the son; you keep the inheritance for him who feels you angry; and you are angry for this reason, lest by living badly with depraved ways he lose what you have kept. Therefore anger is not yet hatred: we do not yet hate those at whom we are angry; but this anger, if it remains, and is not quickly torn out, grows and becomes hatred. Therefore, that anger may be torn out while fresh, and not be turned into hatred, Scripture teaches us this, saying: *Let not the sun go down upon your wrath*[^8]. But sometimes you find a brother holding hatred, and he rebukes one who is angry: in him there is hatred, and in another he blames anger; he himself has a beam in his eye, and rebukes a speck in his brother's eye. But that speck and twig, unless quickly torn out, is to become a beam. He does not say, then: My eye is extinguished by anger; but *troubled*: for if it is extinguished, it is now hatred, not anger. And see that it is extinguished. Hence John says: *He who hates his brother is in darkness even until now*[^9]. Before one goes, then, into darkness, the eye is troubled in anger: but it must be guarded against, lest anger turn into hatred, and the eye be extinguished. This man, then, says: *My eye is troubled by anger, my soul and my belly*; that is, my inner parts are troubled. He put "belly" for the inner parts. For sometimes it is permitted to be angry at the wicked and perverse and those straying from the law and living badly, but not permitted to cry out. When we are angry and cannot cry out, our inner parts are troubled. For so great sometimes is the perversity, that it cannot even be rebuked.

**5.** [v. 11.] *For my life has failed in sorrow, and my years in groanings. My life has failed*, he says, *in sorrow*. The Apostle says: *Now we live, if you stand in the Lord*[^10]. Whoever are perfected out of the Gospel and the grace of God do not live here except for the sake of others; for their life in this world is no longer necessary to them. But because their stewardship is necessary to others, there comes about in them what the same Apostle says: *Having the desire to be dissolved, and to be with Christ; for that is by far the best: but to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^11]. But when a man sees that, from his stewardship, from his labors, from his preaching, men do not make progress, the life of man is weakened in want. Truly a wretched want and hunger: since indeed those whom we gain for the Lord the Church in a manner eats. What is "eats"? It passes them into its own body: for whatever we eat, we pass into our body. This the Church does through the saints: it hungers for those it wishes to gain, and those it has in some way gained, it in a manner eats. Whose person Peter bore, when from heaven a dish was let down to him full of all four-footed animals, serpents, and birds: by which kinds all the nations are signified. The Lord was prefiguring the Church, that it was about to swallow down all the nations, and turn them into its own body; and he said to Peter: *Kill, and eat*[^12]. O Church (that is, O Peter, because upon this rock I will build my Church), *kill, and eat*: first kill, and so eat; slay what they are, and make them what you are. When, then, the Gospel is preached, and he who preaches sees that men do not make progress, why should he not cry out: *For my life has failed in sorrow, and my years in groanings: my vigor has been weakened in want, and my bones have been troubled?* These years of ours, which we lead here, are in groanings. Whence? Because iniquity abounded, the love of many grows cold. In groanings, not in loud voices: when the Church sees many going wrong, it devours its groanings within itself, so as to say to God: *My groaning is not hidden from you*[^13]: it is said in another psalm, but it fits here; and this is to say: My groaning, even if it is hidden from men, is not hidden from you. *My vigor has been weakened in want, and my bones have been troubled*: of this want we spoke above: but by "bones" are understood the strong ones of the Church, who, even if they are not troubled by the persecutions of strangers, are nonetheless troubled by the iniquities of brothers.

**6.** [v. 12.] *Above all my enemies I have become a reproach, and to my neighbors exceedingly, and a fear to my acquaintances. Above all my enemies I have become a reproach*: who are the enemies of the Church? Pagans, Jews? Worse than all live the evil Christians. Do you wish to see how, worse than all, the evil Christians live? Of such the prophet Ezekiel says that they are compared to useless branches. Suppose the pagans to be forest wood outside the Church: something can still be made of it; just as out of carpenter's wood there is wood fit for the carpenter, and though still knotty and crooked and barky, yet because it is hewn, dressed, planed, it can come to some construction of human use: but of cut-off branches carpenters can make nothing; fire alone awaits them. Attend, brothers. Although everywhere a branch remaining in the vine is preferred to forest wood, because the branch gives fruit, that wood gives no fruit: yet if forest wood is compared to a branch cut off from the vine, it is understood to be better; because of it a carpenter can make something; but this no one seeks, except him who serves the hearth. Attending, then, to the multitude of those living badly in the Church, *Above all*, he says, *my enemies I have become a reproach*. The evil, he says, live worse in my mysteries than those who never came to them. Why should we not say it openly in plain language, at least when we expound a psalm? And if at other times we perhaps do not dare to say it, at least let the necessity of expounding have the liberty of rebuking. *Above all*, he says, *my enemies I have become a reproach*. Of such the apostle Peter says: *Their latter state has become worse than the former: for it were better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, knowing it, to turn back from the holy commandment delivered to them*. When he says, *It were better for them not to have known the way of righteousness*, did he not judge enemies set outside to be better than those living badly within, by whom the Church is pressed and burdened? *It were better*, he says, *for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, knowing it, to turn back from the holy commandment delivered to them*. In short, see to what a horrible thing he compared them: *What is in the true proverb has happened to them: A dog returned to its own vomit*[^14]. When, then, the churches are full of such people as these, do not the few there—nay rather, the Church itself out of the voice of the few—say truly: *Above all my enemies I have become a reproach, and to my neighbors exceedingly, and a fear to my acquaintances*? To my neighbors I have become exceedingly a reproach, that is, to those who were already drawing near to me to believe: this is, my neighbors were too greatly frightened off by the evil life of evil and false Christians. For how many do you think, my brothers, wish to be Christians, but are offended by the evil ways of Christians? These are the neighbors who were already drawing near, and to whom we seemed exceedingly a reproach.

**7.** [v. 12.] *I have become a fear to my acquaintances*. What so much to be feared? *I have become a fear*, he says, *to my acquaintances*. What so much to be feared as when a man sees many living badly, and those of whom good was hoped found in many misdeeds? He fears lest all whom he thought good be such, and nearly all the good come into evil suspicion. What a man! how he has fallen! how he has been found in that baseness, in that crime, in that evil deed! do you not think all are such? This is *a fear to my acquaintances*, that even to those to whom we are known we often come into doubt. And unless what you are consoles you, if you are anything, you do not believe another to be such. A man's conscience, such as it is, consoles him, so that a man who lives well says to himself: O you who now fear lest all be such, are you such? The conscience answers: I am not. Then, if you are not such, are you alone? See lest this pride be worse than that wickedness. Do not call yourself alone. For Elijah too once, out of weariness at the multitude of the ungodly, said: *They have killed your prophets, they have dug down your altars, and I am left alone, and they seek my life. But what does the divine answer say to him? I have left for myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee before Baal*[^15]. Therefore, brothers, amid these scandals there is one remedy: do not think ill of your brother. Be humbly what you wish him to be, and you will not think him to be what you are not. But yet let there be also a fear to acquaintances, even to those who are tried.

**8.** [v. 13.] *Those who saw me fled outside from me*. It would be excusable if those who did not see me had fled outside from me: but even those who saw me fled outside from me. But if those who did not see me fled outside from me (nor should it be said, fled outside, because they were not within: for if they had been within, they would have seen me, that is, they would have known the body of Christ, would have known the members of Christ, would have known the unity of Christ): this is the more to be groaned over, this is altogether intolerable, that many who saw me fled outside from me, that is, those who knew what the Church was, went outside, and made heresies and schisms against the Church. Today you find a man, for example, born in the party of Donatus, who does not know what the Church is, who holds what he was born in: you do not tear from him the habit he sucked with the milk of his nurse. But give me that other, who is daily turning over the Scriptures, who reads, who preaches: does he not after all see there: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession*[^16]? does he not see there: *All the ends of the earth will remember, and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will worship in his sight*[^17]? If you see there the unity of the whole world, why do you flee outside, so that not only do you yourself suffer, but you also make blindness for others? *Those who saw me*, that is, who knew what the Church is, who beheld it in the Scriptures, *fled outside from me*. For do you think, my brothers, that all those who made heresies in their places and parties did not know in the Scriptures of God that the Church was foretold only as spread through the whole world? Truly I say to your Charity: surely we are all Christians, or all are called Christians, and we are all signed with the sign of Christ: the Prophets spoke more obscurely of Christ than of the Church; I think for this reason, because they saw in the Spirit that men were going to make factions against the Church, and would not have such a great quarrel about Christ, but would stir up great contentions about the Church. Therefore that about which the greater quarrels were to come was more plainly foretold and more openly prophesied, so that it might count against them at judgment—those who saw, and fled outside.

**9.** For example I will recall one thing. Abraham was our father, not by reason of the propagation of the flesh, but by reason of the imitation of faith: righteous and pleasing to God, by faith he received the son promised to him, Isaac, from Sarah his barren wife in his old age: he was commanded to offer that same son in sacrifice to God, and he did not doubt, nor dispute, nor argue about God's command, nor think evil what the Best One could command; he led his son to be sacrificed, laid upon him the wood of the sacrifice, came to the place, raised his right hand to strike, and at his prohibiting laid it down which he had lifted at his commanding; he who had obeyed to strike obeyed to spare; everywhere obedient, nowhere fearful: yet, that the sacrifice might be fulfilled, and there be no departing without blood, a ram was found caught by its horns in a thicket, it was sacrificed, the sacrifice was carried out. Ask what this is: it is a figure of Christ wrapped in mysteries. In short, that it may be seen, it is examined; that it may be seen, it is handled; that what is wrapped up may be unfolded. Isaac, as the beloved only son bearing the figure of the Son of God, carrying the wood for himself, just as Christ carried the cross. That ram, finally, signified Christ himself. For what is it to be caught by the horns, except in a manner to be crucified? This is a figure concerning Christ. The Church too was at once to be foretold; the head being foretold, the body too was to be foretold: the Spirit of God began, God began to wish to foretell the Church to Abraham, and took up a figure. He foretold Christ figuratively, the Church he foretold openly; for he said to Abraham: *Because you have obeyed my voice, and have not spared your beloved son for my sake, blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven, and as the sand of the sea, and in your seed all the nations of the earth will be blessed*[^18]. And almost everywhere Christ was foretold by the Prophets under some wrapping of mystery, the Church openly: so that even those who were to be against it might see it, and there might be fulfilled in them that wickedness which the psalm foretold: *Those who saw me fled outside from me*. *They went out from us, but they were not of us*: this the apostle John said of them.

**10.** [v. 13.] *I am forgotten, as one dead, from the heart*. I am forgotten, I have come into forgetfulness, those who saw me have forgotten me: they have forgotten me, and so forgotten me as though I were dead from their heart. *I am forgotten, as one dead, from the heart: I have become like a broken vessel*. What is this, *I have become like a broken vessel*? This man was toiling, and profiting no one: he saw himself to be a vessel, and to profit no one, and he calls himself as a broken vessel.

**11.** [v. 14.] *For I have heard the reproach of many who dwell round about*: many dwell round about me, and reproach me daily. How many evil things they say against the evil Christians, which curses reach all Christians! For does he who curses, or who rebukes the Christians, say: Behold what the not-good Christians do? But: Behold what the Christians do—he does not separate, he does not distinguish. Yet those say these things who dwell round about, that is, who go around, and do not enter. Why do they go around, and not enter? Because they love the wheel of time: they do not enter to the truth, because they do not love eternity; given over to temporal things, as though bound to a wheel, of whom it is said elsewhere: *Make their princes like a wheel*[^19]; and elsewhere: *The ungodly walk round about*[^20]. *While they gathered together against me, they took counsel to take away my soul*. What is, *they took counsel to take away my soul*? That I should consent to their depravities. For to those who curse, and do not enter, it is too little that they do not enter; they wish also to drive you out from here by reproach. If they have driven you out of the Church, they have taken away your soul, that is, they have held your consent; and you will be round about, not in the abode.

**12.** [v. 15.] But I, amid these reproaches, amid these scandals, amid these evils, amid these seductions, iniquities without and perversities within, when I attended to and sought righteous men, and there were lacking those whom I might imitate, what did I do? what counsel did I find? *But I have hoped in you, O Lord*. Nothing more wholesome, nothing more secure. You wished to imitate someone or other, you found him not good: take away this imitation. You sought another, something or other displeased you: you sought a third, and he too did not please: are you, because this one and that one displeased, also going to perish? Take away hope from man, for cursed is everyone who puts his hope in man. If you still attend to man, and when you seek to imitate him and to depend on him, you still wish to be nourished with milk; and you will become a "breast-clinger," as those boys are called who suck long, which is unseemly. For to use milk, as though wishing food to be passed to oneself through flesh, this is the same as to live through man. Be fit for the table, take from there whence he took, or perhaps did not take. Perhaps usefully you have even fallen upon one you thought good and found evil, so that at the breast, as though maternal, you found bitterness, and were driven back by that offense, and were invited to stronger food. For nurses do this to breast-clingers, that they put something bitter on their nipples, by which the little ones, offended, spring back from the breast, and gape after the table. Therefore let him say: *But I have hoped in you, O Lord, I said: You are my God*. You are my God: let Donatus withdraw, let Caecilian withdraw; neither this one nor that one is my God. I do not walk to the name of a man, I hold the name of Christ. Hear Paul himself saying: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^21] I would perish if I were of Paul's party; how do I not perish if I am of Donatus's party? Let human names utterly withdraw, human crimes, human fabrications. *In you, O Lord, I have hoped: I said: You are my God*. Not any man, but you are my God. One fails, one makes progress: my God neither fails nor makes progress; nor has he anywhere to advance, being perfect, nor anything from which to fail, being eternal. *I said to the Lord: You are my God*.

**13.** [v. 16.] *In your hands are my lots*: not in the hands of men, but in your hands. What are these lots? why lots? Hearing the word "lots," we ought not to seek soothsayers. For a lot is not anything evil: but it is a thing indicating the divine will in human doubt. For the apostles too cast lots, when Judas, having betrayed the Lord, perished, and, as it is written of him, *went to his own place*: it began to be asked who should be ordained in his place, two were chosen by human judgment, and one of the two was chosen by divine judgment: concerning the two God was consulted, which of them he willed to be, *and the lot fell upon Matthias*[^22]. What, then, is *In your hands are my lots*? By "lots" he meant, as far as I judge, the grace by which we have been saved. Why does he call the grace of God by the name of a lot? Because in a lot there is no choice, but the will of God. For where it is said, This one acts, this one does not act, merits are considered; and where merits are considered, it is choice, not lot: but when God found no merits of ours, by the lot of his will he saved us, because he willed it, not because we were worthy. This is the lot. Rightly that tunic of the Lord, woven from the top, which signifies the eternity of love, when it could not be divided by the persecutors, the lot was cast over it: those to whom it came signified those who are seen to come to the lot of the saints. *By grace you have been saved through faith*, says the apostle Paul: *By grace you have been saved through faith, and this not from yourselves* (see the lot), *and this not from yourselves; but it is the gift of God. Not of works* (as though you had done well, that you might be worthy to come to this), *not of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus in good works*[^23]. This, in a manner, is the lot, the hidden will of God; in the human race it is a lot, a lot coming from the hidden will of God, with whom there is no iniquity: for he does not accept persons, but his hidden righteousness is a lot to you.

**14.** Let your Charity attend, then, see how this very thing is confirmed by the apostle Peter. When that Simon the magician, baptized by Philip, was cleaving to him, believing because of the divine miracles done in his sight; the apostles came to Samaria, where that magician too had believed, and where he had been baptized, and the apostles laid hands on the baptized men, and they received the Holy Spirit, and began to speak with tongues. He marveled, and was astonished at so great a divine miracle, that at the laying on of human hands the Holy Spirit came and filled men; and he desired this—not the grace, but the power; not whereby he might be freed, but whereby he might be exalted: but when he desired this, and pride filled his heart, and a diabolical impiety and loftiness to be cast down, he said to the apostles: How much money will you take from me, that at the laying on of my hands too men may receive the Holy Spirit? He who sought worldly things, who dwelt round about, thought he could buy the gift of God with money. He who thought to purchase the Holy Spirit with money supposed the apostles too to be avaricious, just as he himself was ungodly and proud. At once Peter: *May your money*, he said, *be with you unto perdition, because you have thought that the gift of God can be purchased with money. You have no part nor lot in this faith*[^24]; that is, you do not belong to this grace, which we all receive freely, because you think to buy with money what is given freely. But from the fact that it is given freely, it is called a lot: *You have no part nor lot in this faith*. I have said these things, lest we be terrified at his saying: *In your hands are my lots*. For what are the lots? The inheritance of the Church. How far is the inheritance of the Church? within what bounds? Unto all the bounds: I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession. Let no man, then, promise me some little portion or other: my God: *In your hands are my lots*. Now let this suffice your Charity: what remains, in the name of the Lord, with his own help, we shall render tomorrow.
