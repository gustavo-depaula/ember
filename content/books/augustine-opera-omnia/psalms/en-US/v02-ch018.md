# Exposition of Psalm 46

**1.** The Lord our God has, through the holy Books, the holy Scriptures, in manifold and various ways spread abroad to us the faith in which we live, and from which we live; varying indeed the sacraments of words, yet commending one faith. For one and the same thing is for this reason said in many ways, that by the very manner of saying it the distaste may be varied, but by the concord one thing may be held. And so in this psalm which we heard sung, to which by singing we responded, we are to say the things which you know: and yet perhaps, the Lord aiding and granting, we shall bring to you some sweetness, when the things you knew in this place and that, you also, being admonished, chew over again. For by that very rumination, in which God signifies the clean animals, he wished to insinuate this, that every man ought so to put what he hears into his heart, that he be not afterward slothful to think of it: so that when he hears, he is like one eating; but when he recalls the things heard into memory, and reconsiders them with most sweet thought, he becomes like one ruminating. In another way, then, the same things are said, and make us sweetly think of what we know, and gladly hear the same things; because the manner of saying is varied, and the old matter is by the very manner of saying renewed.

**2.** [v. 1] The title of the psalm runs thus: *To the end, for the sons of Korah, a psalm of David himself*[^1]. These sons of Korah some others of the titles of the Psalms have too, and they indicate a sweet mystery, they insinuate a great sacrament: where let us both gladly understand ourselves, and recognize in the title ourselves who hear and read, and, as in a mirror set before us, behold who we are. Who are the sons of Korah? There was a certain man Korah by name, for so some man was called: yet when the things that are written are read, and the divine word is found speaking to certain ones, who are not well understood to be sons of that one man who was called Korah; the mind recurs to the mystery, that it may seek what Korah signifies. For because it is a Hebrew word, it is of course spoken and interpreted into the Greek tongue and into the Latin tongue. And it has now been done; many Hebrew names have been interpreted for us: and we inspect, and find Korah interpreted "Bald." Much more have you attended. It was obscure when the sons of Korah were said, is it not more obscure when the sons of the bald one are said? Who are these sons of the bald one? Or perhaps the sons of the bridegroom? Because the bridegroom was crucified in the place of Calvary. Recall the Gospel, where they crucified the Lord, and you will find him crucified in the place of Calvary. Accordingly those who deride his cross are devoured by demons as by beasts. For a certain Scripture signified this too. When the prophet of God Elisha was going up, children cried out after him, deriding: *Go up, bald-head, go up, bald-head*: but he, not so much cruelly as mystically, made those children be devoured by bears coming out. If those children were not devoured, would they live even now? or could they not, born mortal, be cut off even by a fever? Yet the mystery would not be shown in them, whereby the later ones might be terrified. Let no one, then, deride the cross of the Lord: the Jews were possessed by demons and devoured. For in the place of Calvary, crucifying Christ, and lifting him on the cross, as it were they themselves said with a childish sense, not understanding what they said: *Go up, bald-head.* For what is, *Go up*? *Crucify, crucify*[^2]. For childhood is set forth for imitation, the humility; and again childhood is set forth for avoiding, the foolishness. Childhood is set forth for imitating the humility by the Lord, when he called children to himself, and when they were forbidden by him, he said: *Let them come to me, for of such is the kingdom of heaven*[^3]. The example of childhood for avoiding foolishness is set forth by the Apostle: *Brothers, do not be children in your senses.* And again he sets it forth for imitation: *But in malice be infants, that you may be perfect in your minds*[^4]. *For the sons of Korah.* The psalm is sung: therefore it was sung for Christians. Let us hear it as the sons of that bridegroom, whom senseless children crucified in the place of Calvary. For they deserved to be devoured by beasts, we to be crowned by angels. For we recognize the humility of our Lord, and do not blush at it. We do not blush at his being mystically called bald, on account of the place of Calvary. For by that very cross on which he was insulted, he did not allow our forehead to be bald, because he marked it with his wood. In short, that you may know that these things are said to us, see what is said.

**3.** [v. 2] *All you nations, clap your hands*[^5]. Was the people of the Jews all the nations? But blindness in part happened to Israel, that senseless children might cry, *Bald-head, bald-head;* and so the Lord was crucified in the place of Calvary, that by his blood shed he might redeem the nations, and there might be fulfilled what the Apostle says: *Blindness in part happened to Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter*[^6]. Let the vain and dull and senseless insult, then, and say: *Bald-head, bald-head:* but you, redeemed by his blood, which was shed in the place of Calvary: *All you nations, clap your hands,* that the grace of God has reached you. *Clap your hands.* What is, *clap*? Rejoice. But why *with your hands*? Because by good works. Do not rejoice with the mouth, and be idle with the hands. If you rejoice, *clap your hands.* Let the hands of the nations be seen by him who deigned to grant joys. What is, the hands of the nations? The deeds of those who work well. *All you nations, clap your hands: shout to God with the voice of exultation.* Both with the voice, and with the hands. If only with the voice, not well; because the hands are slothful: if only with the hands, neither this well; because the tongue is mute: let the hands and tongue agree; let the one confess, the others work. *Shout to God with the voice of exultation.*

**4.** [v. 3] *For the Lord is high, terrible*[^7]. That high one, as it were laughable by descending, by ascending into heaven was made terrible. *A great king over all the earth.* Not only over the Jews: for over them too he is king. For from there the apostles too believed, from there too many thousands of men sold their goods, and set the prices at the feet of the apostles: and there was fulfilled in them what was inscribed in the title of the cross: *King of the Jews*[^8]. For he is king even of the Jews. But it is too little of the Jews. *All you nations, clap your hands: for he is king of all the earth, God.* For it does not suffice him to have one nation under him: therefore he gave so great a price from his side, that he might buy the whole world. *A great king over all the earth.*

**5.** [v. 4] *He has subjected the peoples to us, and the nations under our feet*[^9]. Whom has he subjected, and to whom? Who are they who speak? Perhaps the Jews? Plainly if the apostles, plainly if the saints. For to these God has subjected peoples and nations, so that those are today honored among the nations, who deserved to be killed by their own fellow citizens: just as their Lord was killed by his fellow citizens, and is honored among the nations, crucified by his own, adored by strangers, but bought by his deeds. For he bought us for this, that we should not be strangers to him. Do you think, then, that they are the voices of the apostles: *He has subjected the peoples to us, and the nations under our feet*? I do not know. It would be a wonder if the apostles spoke so proudly, as to rejoice that the nations are set under their feet, that is, Christians under the feet of the apostles. For they rather rejoice that we are with them under the feet of him who died for us. For under the feet of Paul there were running those who wished to be of Paul; and he said to them: *Was Paul crucified for you?*[^10] What, then, here? what are we to receive? *He has subjected the peoples to us, and the nations under our feet.* All who pertain to the inheritance of Christ are among all the nations, and all who do not pertain to the inheritance of Christ are among all the nations: and you see the Church of Christ so exalted in the name of Christ, that all not yet believing in Christ lie under the feet of Christians. For how many not yet Christians now run to the Church, ask the help of the Church; they wish to be aided temporally, even if they do not yet wish to reign with us forever. Since all seek the help of the Church, even those who are not yet in the Church, has he not *subjected the peoples and the nations under our feet*?

**6.** [v. 5] *He has chosen for us his inheritance, the beauty of Jacob which he loved*[^11]. A certain beauty of Jacob he has chosen for us, his inheritance, Jacob. Two brothers were Esau and Jacob; in the mother's womb both struggled, and from the struggle the mother's bowels were shaken: and there, since there were two, the younger was chosen, and set before the elder, and it was said: *Two peoples are in your womb, and the elder shall serve the younger*[^12]. Through all the nations the elder, through all the nations the younger: but the younger in the good Christians, the elect, the godly, the faithful; the elder in the proud, the unworthy, the sinners, the obstinate, defending their sins rather than confessing them; such as was the very people of the Jews too, ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish their own. But because it was said: *The elder shall serve the younger,* it is manifest that the ungodly will be subjected to the godly, and the proud will be subjected to the humble. First was born Esau, and afterward was born Jacob: but he who was born later was set before the firstborn, who through gluttony lost his birthright. So you have it written: *He desired lentils, and his brother said to him: If you wish that I give it to you, give me your birthright.* He loved more what he had carnally coveted, than what he had spiritually deserved by being born first: and he laid down his birthright, that he might eat lentils. But we find lentils to be the food of the Egyptians; for there it abounds in Egypt. Whence the Alexandrian lentil is magnified, and comes even to our lands, as though lentils were not born here. Therefore by desiring Egyptian food, he lost the primacy. So too the people of the Jews, of whom it was said: *They were turned back in heart into Egypt*[^13], in a way desired lentils, and lost the primacy. *He has chosen for us his inheritance, the beauty of Jacob which he loved.*

**7.** [v. 6] *God has ascended in jubilation*[^14]. That very God of ours, the Lord Christ, *has ascended in jubilation. The Lord in the voice of the trumpet. He has ascended*: whither, except whither we know? Whither the Jews did not follow him, not even with their eyes. For exalted on the cross they derided him, ascending into heaven they did not see him. *God has ascended in jubilation.* What is jubilation, except the wonder of joy, which cannot be unfolded in words? How the disciples, rejoicing, wondered, seeing him whom they had bewailed dead go into heaven: truly for this joy words did not suffice, it remained to jubilate what no one could unfold. There too was the voice of the trumpet, that voice of the angels. For it was said: *Like a trumpet lift up your voice*[^15]. The angels preached the ascension of the Lord: they saw the disciples clinging, the Lord ascending, wondering, astonished, saying nothing, but jubilating in heart: and now the voice of the trumpet in the clear voice of the angels: *Men of Galilee, why do you stand? This is Jesus.* As though they did not know that it was Jesus. Had they not seen him before themselves a little before? had they not heard him speaking with them? Surely they not only saw the appearance of the one present, but also handled the members. Did they, then, not know that it was Jesus? But by that very wonder, for the joy of jubilation, as it were with minds estranged, the angels speak: *It is Jesus himself.* As if: If you believe him, it is he by whose crucifixion your feet tottered, by whose killing and burial you thought you had lost your hope; behold it is Jesus himself. He has ascended before you; *so will he come, as you see him going into heaven*[^16]: the body indeed is taken from your eyes, but God is not separated from your hearts: see him ascending, believe in him absent, hope for him coming; but yet through his hidden mercy perceive him even present. For he who ascended into heaven, that he might be taken from your eyes, promised you, saying: *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the world*[^17]. Deservedly the Apostle too addressed us thus: *The Lord is near, be anxious about nothing*[^18]. Christ sits above the heavens, and the heavens are far off, and he himself who sits there is near. *The Lord in the voice of the trumpet.* Therefore you, sons of Korah, if you have now understood yourselves, and behold here yourselves, you too rejoice, because you see here yourselves.

**8.** [v. 7] *Sing to our God, sing*[^19]. Him whom, as a man, those derided who are alienated from God, *sing to our God.* For he is not only man, but also God. Man from the seed of David, God the Lord of David, having flesh from the Jews: *whose are the fathers,* says the Apostle, *and from whom is Christ according to the flesh.* From the Jews, then, is Christ, but according to the flesh. But who is this Christ, who is from the Jews according to the flesh? *Who is over all things God blessed forever*[^20]. God before the flesh, God in the flesh, God with the flesh. And not only before the flesh God, but before the earth God, whence the flesh was made; nor only before the earth God, whence the flesh was made, but also before heaven God, which was made first; before the day God, which was made first; before all the angels God, Christ himself God: *since in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^21]. He is before all things, through whom all things were made. *Sing,* then, *to our God, sing.*

**9.** [v. 8] *For God is king of all the earth*[^22]. What then? Even before, was he not God of all the earth? Was he not God both of heaven and earth, since surely through him all things were made? Who could say he was not his God? But not all men recognized their God; and where he was recognized, as it were there only God was. God was known in Judea[^23]: it was not yet said to the sons of Korah: *All you nations, clap your hands.* For that God known in Judea is the God of all the earth. Now he is recognized by all, because there has been fulfilled what Isaiah says: *He, your God, who delivered you, shall be called the God of the whole earth*[^24]. *For God is king of all the earth: sing with understanding.* He teaches us and admonishes us to sing with understanding: let us not seek the sound of the ear, but the light of the heart. *Sing,* he says, *with understanding.* The nations, whence you were called to be Christians, adored gods made with hands, and sang to them; but not with understanding. If they sang with understanding, they would not adore stones. When a man of sense sang to a senseless stone, did he sing with understanding? But now, brothers, we do not see with our eyes whom we adore, and yet, corrected, we adore. Much more is God commended to us, because we do not see him with our eyes. If we saw him with our eyes, perhaps we would despise him. For the Jews too despised Christ seen, the Gentiles adored him unseen. For to them it was said: *Sing with understanding.* *Do not be like the horse and mule, in which there is no understanding*[^25].

**10.** [v. 9] *The Lord will reign over all the nations*[^26]. He who reigned over one nation, *will reign,* he says, *over all the nations.* When these things were said, God reigned over one nation: it was a prophecy, the thing was not yet shown. Thanks to God, now we see fulfilled what was foretold before. God wrote us a bond before the time, the time being fulfilled he repaid us. *God will reign over all the nations,* it is a promise. *God sits upon his holy seat.* What was then promised as future, is now recognized and held as fulfilled. *God sits upon his holy seat.* What is his holy seat? Perhaps the heavens; and it is well understood. For Christ ascended, as we know, with the body in which he was crucified, and sits at the right hand of the Father: thence we await him to come to judge the living and the dead. *He sits upon his holy seat.* Are the heavens his holy seat? Do you too wish to be his seat? Do not think you cannot be: prepare for him a place in your heart; he comes, and gladly sits. He at least is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God. And what does Scripture say of Wisdom herself? The soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom. If, then, the soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom; let your soul be just, and you will be the royal chair of wisdom. And truly, brothers, all men who live well, who act well, who converse according to godly love, does not God sit in them, and himself command? The soul obeys God sitting in it, and itself commands the members. For your soul commands your member, whither the foot is to move, whither the hand, whither the eye, whither the ear, and itself commands the members as its servants: but it too serves within the Lord sitting in it. It cannot well command the inferior, unless it has not disdained to serve the superior. *God sits upon his holy seat.*

**11.** [v. 10] *The princes of the peoples have come together with the God of Abraham*[^27]. The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob. It is true, God said this, and the Jews grew proud over it, and said: *We are the sons of Abraham.* Growing proud over the paternal name, bearing his flesh, not holding his faith; clinging by seed, degenerating in morals. In short, the Lord, what does he say to them growing so proud? *If you are the sons of Abraham, do the deeds of Abraham*[^28]. Likewise what did John say to them coming and trembling, certain of them, who wished to correct themselves by repenting? *Brood of vipers.* For they were iniquitous, they were lost, they were sinners, they were ungodly: they came to the baptism of John: and what does he say to them? *Brood of vipers.* They called themselves sons of Abraham, and he called them sons of vipers. Was Abraham a viper? But because by living badly they had imitated demons, and had been made their sons, whom by imitating they lived badly: *Brood,* he says, *of vipers, who has shown you to flee from the wrath to come? Make, then, fruit worthy of repentance: and do not say among yourselves: We have Abraham for father,* and as it were grow proud over the race of Abraham; *for God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*[^29]. For Abraham will not remain without sons, if God condemns you: for he is able both to condemn those he hates, and to render to him those he promised. And whence will he render him sons, if he condemns the Hebrews begotten of his flesh? *From these stones.* He showed them the stones in the desert. Who were the stones, except the Gentiles, who adored stones? Why stones? By adoring stones, they had been called stones: because the psalm had foretold: *Let those who make them become like them, and all who trust in them*[^30]. Yet from those very stones he rendered sons to Abraham: now all who adored stones, turned to the Lord have been made sons of Abraham; not by drawing flesh from him, but by imitating his faith. *The princes,* then, *of the peoples have come together with the God of Abraham. The princes of the peoples*: the princes of the Gentiles, not the princes of one people, but the princes of all the peoples, *have come together with the God of Abraham.*

**12.** Of these very princes was that Centurion too, of whom you heard just now when the Gospel was read. For he was a Centurion having honor and power among men, he was a prince of the princes of the peoples. To Christ coming to him he sent his friends, rather indeed to Christ passing by he sent his friends; and he asked that he cure his servant, who was perilously sick: and when the Lord himself wished to come, he charged him this: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word, and my servant will be healed. For I too am a man set under authority, having soldiers under me.* See how he kept the order: first he recalled that he was under another, and afterward that others were under him. I am under authority, and in authority; I am both under someone, and over some. *And I say to this one: Go, and he goes; and to another: Come, and he comes; and to my servant: Do this, and he does it.* As though he said: If I, set under authority, command those who are under me, you who are set under no one's authority, cannot you command your creature, when all things were made through you, and without you was made nothing? *Say,* then, *the word,* he says, *and my servant will be healed. For that you should enter into my house, I am not worthy.* He feared to bring Christ within his walls, and Christ was already within his heart: now his soul was the seat of him, now there sat he who sought out the humble. *Turned,* finally, *Christ marveled, and said to those following him: Amen I say to you, I have not found so great faith in Israel*[^31]. And as another evangelist narrates the same thing, the Lord follows, and says: *Therefore I say to you, that many shall come from the East and the West, and shall recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven.* For this Centurion was not of the people of Israel. For in the people of Israel the proud repelled God from themselves: among the princes of the Gentiles was found a humble man, who invited God to himself. Jesus, marveling at his faith, reproves the unbelief of the Jews. For they seemed to themselves healthy, when they were more perilously sick, when, not recognizing the physician, they killed him. When, then, he reproved and repudiated their pride, what does he say? *Therefore I say to you, that many shall come from the East and the West,* not pertaining to the kinship of Israel: many shall come to whom he said: *All you nations, clap your hands; and they shall recline with Abraham in the kingdom of heaven.* Abraham did not beget them by his flesh; and coming they shall recline with him in the kingdom of heaven, and shall be his sons. Why his sons? Not born of flesh, but by following his faith. *But the sons of the kingdom,* that is, the Jews, *shall go into the outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^32]. Those born of the flesh of Abraham shall be damned into the outer darkness, and those who imitated the faith of Abraham shall recline with him in the kingdom of heaven. Deservedly, then, here too: *The princes of the peoples have come together with the God of Abraham.*

**13.** And what of those who pertained to the God of Abraham? *For the strong gods of the earth have been exceedingly lifted up*[^33]. Those who were gods, that people of God, that vine of God, of which it is said: *Judge between me and my vineyard*[^34], shall go into the outer darkness, shall not recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, do not come together with the God of Abraham. Why? *Because the strong gods of the earth.* Those who were strong gods of the earth, presuming on the earth. On what earth? on themselves: because every man is earth. For to man it was said: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^35]. But a man ought to presume on God, and thence hope for help, not on himself. For the earth does not rain for itself, nor shine for itself: but as the earth awaits from heaven rain and light, so a man ought to await from God mercy and truth. Those, then, *strong gods of the earth, have been exceedingly lifted up,* that is, they grew exceedingly proud: they did not think a physician necessary for themselves, and therefore they remained in their sickness, and by that very sickness were led to death. The natural branches were cut off, that the humble wild olive might be grafted in. *Because the strong gods of the earth have been exceedingly lifted up.* Let us, then, brothers, hold humility, love, godliness; because we were called when they were reproved, and by their very example let us fear to grow proud.
