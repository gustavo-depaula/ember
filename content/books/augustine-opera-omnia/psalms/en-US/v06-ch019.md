# Exposition of Psalm 140

**1.** The lords my brothers, and in them the Lord of all, have commanded that I bring this psalm to you to be understood, as far as the Lord grants. Let him help by your prayers, that I may say the things which it behooves me to say, and you to hear; that the divine word may be useful to us all. For it is not useful to all; because not all have faith[^1]. But faith is in the soul as a good root which leads the rain into fruit: but faithlessness, and diabolical error, and cupidity, the evil root of all evils[^2], like the root of thorns, turns even sweet rain into pricks.

**2.** What this psalm has, I believe you noticed when it was sung; that the body of Christ, set among the evil, complains and groans, and pours out a prayer to God: for it is its voice in all such prophecy, as of that needy one, that is, one in want, not yet satisfied, hungering and thirsting for justice[^3], for whom a certain fullness, promised, is kept at the end. Meanwhile here let it now hunger and thirst, groan and knock and seek. Let it not be delighted by the enticements of pilgrimage, let it not think this world its homeland, from which Christ came that it might be freed: because Christ willed to be our head, the head, namely, of a certain body. For it cannot be called a head, where there is no body of which it is the head. Accordingly, if Christ is the head; he is also the head of some body. The body of that head is the holy Church, in whose members we are, if we love our head. Let us, then, hear the voices of the body of Christ, that is, our voices, if we are in the body of Christ; for whoever is not there, will be among those among whom that body groans. Accordingly, either you will be in that body, to groan among the evil; or you will not be in that body, and will be among those, among the evil, among whom the body groans, which groans among the evil: either a member of Christ, or an enemy of the body of Christ. Nor are these enemies and adversaries of the body of Christ understood in one way, or do they act in one way. For cunning is he who reigns in them, and who uses them as his vessels. But many are freed from him, and pass over into the body of Christ; and who they are, and how many they will be, he knows who redeemed them by his blood while they knew it not. But there are certain ones who will persevere in their malice, not belonging to the body of Christ; and these too are surely known to him, to whom nothing is unknown. Meanwhile those who are now framed together with his members, not yet having the future resurrection, in which all groaning perishes, and praise succeeds, in which all tribulation will die, and there will be exultation without end; not yet, then, having this in reality, but yet holding it in hope, groan from a certain desire, and pray to be freed from evil men, among whom it is necessary that even the good live: for separation is not yet safe for anyone. For the separation will be his who does not know how to err. What is, who does not know how to err? That he neither casts the evil to the right hand, nor the good to the left. But in this life it is difficult that we know ourselves; how much less ought we to bear a hasty sentence about anyone? For if today we know an evil man, tomorrow what he will be we do not know; and perhaps the one we vehemently hate is our brother, and we do not know it. Securely, then, let us hate in the evil the malice, and love the creature; that we may love what God made there, and hate what the man himself made there. For God made the man himself; but the man made the sin. Love what God made, hate what the man made: for so you will persecute what the man made, that what God made may be freed.

**3.** [v. 1.] *To the end, a Psalm of David himself.* By 'the end' do not understand anything other than what was set before you by the apostle: *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto justice for everyone who believes*[^4]. So when you hear the Psalm say: *To the end*, let your hearts be turned to Christ. For the title of the Psalm is as it were the herald of the Psalm, as though saying: Behold, he will come; thence I am about to speak, about to sing of Christ. For 'of David himself' too I understand only as him who was made of the seed of David according to the flesh[^5]. Meanwhile, on account of his lineage, the name fits. His fleshly lineage is from David; his spiritual lineage above David. And not only before David, but before Abraham; nor before Abraham only, but before Adam; nor before Adam only, but before heaven and earth, before all the angels, before all Powers and Virtues, before all things visible and invisible. Why? Because that these might be, *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^6]. So because he is of the seed of David, not according to the divinity, by which he is the Creator of David himself, but according to the flesh; he deigned to be called David in prophecy: look toward that very end, because the Psalm is sung *to David himself*; and hear the voice of his body, and be in his body. Let the voice which you heard become yours, and pray, and say what follows.

**4.** [v. 1.] *Take me out, O Lord, from the evil man*[^7]: not from one, but from the whole kind; nor from the vessels only, but from the prince himself, that is, from the devil himself. For why *from the man*, if from the devil? Because he too is called a man in a figure: *An enemy man came, and oversowed weeds;* and when the servants had said to the head of the household: *Did you not sow good seed? Whence have weeds appeared?* the answer was given: *An enemy man did this*[^8]. From this evil man, then, pray, as much as you can, that you may be freed: *For you have not a wrestling against flesh and blood; but against the principalities and powers, and rulers of this world of darkness*[^9], that is, the rulers of sinners. Which indeed we too were; for we have just now heard the voice of the apostle: *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^10]. Now, then, made light, not in ourselves, but in the Lord, let us pray not only against the darkness, that is, against the sinners, whom the devil still possesses; but also against their prince himself the devil, who works in the sons of unbelief. *From the unjust man rescue me.* This is, *from the evil man;* which is, *from the unjust man.* For he said 'evil' precisely because 'unjust'; lest perhaps you should think some unjust man to be good. For there are many unjust men as it were not harmful; they are not savage, are not harsh, are not persecuting and inflicting pressures on men: but yet they are unjust for this, because in some other way they are luxurious, drunken, given over to pleasures. How does he harm no one who does not spare himself? For he is innocent who does not harm; but not he who harms himself. But how can one who harms himself not harm you? But you answer: In what does he harm me? For he has not invaded my property, or attacked my safety: he feeds on his own luxury, is delighted by his own pleasures; but if he has defiled pleasures, he himself is defiled; what does it matter to me, to whom he is not troublesome? He harms you at least even by example, because he lives with you, and invites you to what he does. When you see him perhaps prospering in those filthy things, are you not drawn by the delight of such deeds? Even if you do not consent, at least you have found something to overcome. How, then, did he not harm you, you who labor to conquer what he made in your heart? So every unjust man is evil, and must necessarily be harmful, whether he be smooth, or whether he be fierce. Whoever runs into him, whoever is caught in his snares, will find how harmful is what he thought gentle. For indeed, brothers, even thorns do not prick at their roots. Pull thorns from the earth, handle their roots, and see whether you feel pain: yet that from which you feel pain on the surface, proceeded from that root. And so let men not please you as it were gentle and smooth, yet lovers of carnal pleasures, and followers of defiled desires; let them not please you. Even if they still seem gentle, they are the roots of thorns. For by such deeds, through luxury, they very often pour out what they had: and how do they seek to fill up what they poured out? Do they now spare from plunder and thoughts of fraud, from seeking out every kind of malice? Now you see the evil man, whom you before thought gentle. When you saw him get drunk, he was good: now you see a thief, you fear a robber; the thorns have come forth from those roots. When you felt the gentle roots of thorns, then, if you could, you would burn them, and there would be nothing from which would come forth what now pricks you. Accordingly, my brothers, body of Christ, or members of Christ, groaning among such evil men; whomever you find prone to be carried into evil lusts and pernicious pleasures, then rebuke, and then chastise, then burn. Let the root be burned, and there is nothing from which a thorn may come forth. But if you cannot, be sure that you will have them as enemies. They can be silent, can hide their enmities, cannot love you. But since they cannot love you, and it is necessary that those who hate you seek evils for you; let not your tongue and heart be idle to say to God: *Take me out, O Lord, from the evil man: from the unjust man free me.*

**5.** [v. 2.] *Who have thought injustices in their heart*[^11]. For what, because they do not dare to bring it out on the tongue, and keep it in the heart? For he said it on account of those who very often speak good things on their lips. You hear the voice of a just man; but it is not the heart of a just man. For what did it profit to add this, that he said: *Who have thought injustices in their heart*? From these free me; there let your most powerful hand be to rescue me. For it is easy to avoid open enmities; it is easy to be turned away from a ready and manifest enemy, whose iniquity is now even on his lips: he is troublesome, he is hidden, he is hard to avoid, who carries good things on his lips, and hides evils in his heart: *Who have thought injustices in their heart. All the day they set up wars.* What is, *wars*? Against which I would fight all the day they set up for me. For from such hearts is born whatever there is against which the Christian fights. If sedition, if schism, if heresy, if turbulent contradiction, this bursts forth only from those thoughts which were covered: and when the lips spoke good things, *all the day they set up wars.* You hear peaceable words; but the setting-up of war does not depart from their hearts. For what was said, *all the day*, signifies without intermission, that is, all the time.

**6.** [v. 3.] *They have sharpened their tongues like serpents*[^12]. If you still seek the man, look at the likeness. In a serpent there is especially cunning and the guile of harming; therefore it also creeps. For it does not even have feet, that its footsteps might be heard when it comes. In its going there is as it were a smooth trail, but it is not straight. So, then, they crawl and creep to do harm, having hidden venom and under a smooth touch. And therefore it follows: *The venom of asps under their lips.* Behold that 'under their lips'; so that we may notice one thing under the lips, another on the lips. Whom he also openly makes plain in another place, where he says: *Who speak peace with their neighbor; but evils in their hearts*[^13].

**7.** [v. 4.] *Preserve me, O Lord, from the hand of the sinner; from unjust men rescue me*[^14]. They are the same, they are known: here there is no need to understand, but to act; there is need to pray, not to seek out who they are. But how you may pray against men of this kind, he opens to you in what follows. For many pray against the evil unskillfully. *Who have thought*, he says, *to supplant my steps.* This can still be taken carnally. Each one has an enemy; in business he thinks to do him fraud, and to take from him money, who is engaged in common business: each one has a neighbor as enemy, and thinks to bring evil upon his house, to diminish something of his property; and surely he thinks it by guile, thinks it by fraud, with the devil's machinations he is eager to fulfill these very things; no one doubts it. But yet it is not on account of these things that these men are to be guarded against, but lest by lying in wait for you they lead you to themselves; that is, separate you from the body of Christ, and make you of their own body. For as Christ is the head of the good, so the devil is their head. *Who have thought*, he says, *to supplant my steps.* What is, *to supplant my steps*? Not as though that you should err in the business which you have with him, and he deceive you in the case which you have in common with him in the forum. He has supplanted your steps, if he has hindered you in the way of God, so that what you intended rightly may totter, or slip from the way, or fall in the way, or withdraw from the way, or remain in the way, or turn back to where it came from. Whatever such thing he does to you, he has supplanted you, deceived you. Against snares of this kind pray, lest you lose your heavenly patrimony, lest you lose Christ your coheir, because you will live forever with him who made you an heir. For he did not make you an heir to one whom, when dead, you may succeed, but with whom you may live forever.

**8.** [v. 5.] *The proud have hidden a trap for me*[^15]. He has briefly unfolded the whole body of the devil, when he said, *the proud.* Hence it is that they very often call themselves just, when they are wicked. Hence it is that nothing is so harmful to them as to confess their sins. They are the ones who, since they are false-just, must necessarily envy the true-just. For no one envies another in that which he does not wish either to be or to seem. Another envies you because you are rich; either he wishes to be rich that he may envy you, or he wishes to be thought rich: another envies you because you are illustrious and noble; either he aspires to be this, or he desires to be thought this. And so all things that seem and are thought good in this world; what each one wishes to have, and in what he wishes to excel, or the fame of which thing he catches at, in that he envies you. But those who are false-just wish to seem just, when they are not; and it is necessary that whomever they see truly just, they envy him, and do this with him, how he may lose that in which he glories. Hence come all the seductions and supplantings. This the devil himself first willed, who, falling, envied the man who stood: and because he himself lost the kingdom of heaven, he was unwilling that man should arrive there, and is unwilling; and he does this now, that man may not arrive there, from which he himself was cast down[^16]. Since, then, he himself is proud, and therefore envious because proud, his whole body is a body of such men. But let us pray against him, who cannot be corrected, and for those who can, that we may say to the unjust man: Why do you envy the just, O unjust man? Because you wish to seem just? Quickly do what is better, and you will more easily be what you wish to seem. Be just: so you will love the one you envied; because what you grieve that he is, you too will be, and you will love yourself in him, and him in yourself. For if you envied a rich man, it would not be in your power to be rich; if you envied some honorable noble senator, it would not be in your power to be noble and illustrious; if you envied a handsome man, you would never make yourself handsome; if you envied a strong and vigorous man, you would never give yourself strength: if you envy a just man, the matter is in your will; be what you grieve that another is. For you are not about to buy what you are not and another is; it is free, it costs little: *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^17].

**9.** But those *proud have hidden a trap*: they sought to supplant my steps. And what did they do? *And they have stretched out ropes as traps for my feet*[^18]. What ropes? This word is well known in the Scriptures, and we find elsewhere what ropes signify. The Lord made a scourge of cords, with which he drove out of the temple[^19] those who were behaving badly there; and he signified to us how we should understand ropes: because: *Each one is bound by the cords of his own sins*[^20], says another Scripture; and plainly Isaiah: *Woe to those who drag sins like a long rope*[^21]! But why is it called a rope? Because every sinner persevering in his sins adds sins to sins; and when he ought to be corrected by the accusing of his sin, by defending it he doubles what by confessing he could take away, and very often wishes to be fortified by other sins on account of those he committed. He has committed adultery, and lest he be killed, he prepares murder; he adds sin to sin. Again, if it happens to him to commit murder, now he who feared one crime, fears two: and surely, when he sees himself fearing more things than before he feared, he does not think to diminish what he has done, but to add what he has not yet done; he seeks perhaps even sorcery. Behold, he has three. From there, who could now reckon? who could finish the rope of sins? And it is excellently called a rope: for that a rope may be made, more is added; and straight threads are not added, but twisted. Crookedness, connected with itself, is led out in length, and he does not think to cut off what he wove badly; but to add, to prolong, to stretch out in length: so that he may have at the end something with which his hands and feet may be bound, and he be cast into the outer darkness[^22]. But these their sins they stretch out for the just, when they persuade them to do the evil things which they themselves do. Therefore he said: *They have stretched out ropes as traps for my feet;* that is, they wished to cast me down by their own sins. And where this? *Beside the paths they have set a scandal for me*: not in the paths, but *beside the paths.* Your paths are the precepts of God. They set scandals beside the paths; you, do not withdraw from the paths, and you will not run into the scandals. Nor do I wish you to say: God should forbid them to set scandals for me beside the paths, and they would not set them there. On the contrary, God permitted them to set scandals beside the paths, and you, do not withdraw from the paths. *Beside the paths they have set scandals for me.*

**10.** [v. 6.] And what remains? what remedy amid such great evils, in these temptations, in these perils? *I said to the Lord, You are my God*[^23]. They are men, and not mine; you are both God, and mine. *I said to the Lord, You are my God.* A great voice of prayer; it stirs confidence. Is he not their God too? For whose God is he not, who is the true God? But yet he is properly theirs, who enjoy him, who serve him, who are gladly under him. For the evil too, although unwilling, are under him. They call upon God, by whom they may be crowned; under him they flee, by whom they may be condemned. And the wicked man who does not wish to have the Lord his God, where will he flee from the God of all? It is good for him, then, to be converted to the God of all, and by conversion to make him his own; and, set among such sinners, seducers, hypocrites, the proud, let him say to God, whom by his conversion he made his God: *I said to the Lord, You are my God. Receive in your ears, O Lord, the voice of my supplication.* It is indeed a simple sentence, and easy to understand: but yet it is perhaps pleasant to consider why he did not say: Receive in your ears my supplication; but, as it were more evidently expressing the affection of his soul, he said, *the voice of my supplication*, the life of my supplication, the soul of my supplication, not what sounds in my words, but that from which my words live. For other noises without a soul can be called sounds, cannot be called voices: a voice properly belongs to the living, belongs to those who live. But how many entreat God, and do not feel God, nor think well of God? They can have the sound of supplication, cannot have the voice; because life is not there. Of this man who was living, because he understood his God, and saw from whom he would be freed, and felt from whom he would be freed, that was the voice of his supplication.

**11.** [v. 7.] Commending it to the ears of God, let him say: *O Lord, Lord*[^24]. You *O Lord, Lord*, that is, you most truly Lord, not such lords as men, not such lords as those who buy with a money-bag; but such a Lord as bought with blood. *O Lord, Lord, the strength of my salvation*: that is, you who give strength to my salvation. What does it mean, *the strength of my salvation*? He was complaining of the scandals and snares of sinners, of the malicious men, vessels of the devil, barking around and lying in wait around, of the proud envying the just, among whom it is necessary to lead our life, while we live here in this our pilgrimage. But the Lord foretold that such scandals would abound, and said: *Iniquity will abound, and because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold.* But he at once added a consolation: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^25]. This man attended, and feared; and, troubled by the abundance of iniquities, he looked to hope: because *he who perseveres to the end, he will be saved.* He stretched himself to persevere, and saw a long way; and because to persevere is a great thing and difficult, he prayed for the perfection of his perseverance to him by whom it was commanded him to persevere. Surely I will be saved, if I persevere to the end; but perseverance belongs to strength, that I may deserve salvation: you are the strength of my salvation, you make me persevere, that I may come to salvation. *O Lord, Lord, the strength of my salvation.* And whence do I hope that you are the strength of my salvation? *You have overshadowed over my head in the day of war.* Behold, now I still fight: I fight outside against the feigned good, I fight within against my desires; since *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members. Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God, through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^26]. So, laboring in this war, he looked to the grace of God; and because he had now begun to glow and grow dry, he found as it were a shade under which to live: *You have overshadowed over my head in the day of war;* that is, in the heat, lest I be wearied, lest I grow dry.

**12.** [v. 8.] *Do not hand me over, O Lord, from my desire to the sinner*[^27]. Behold for what your shade will avail me, that I may not suffer heat from myself. And what would that sinner do to me, however much he raged? For the wicked raged against the martyrs; they dragged them, bound them with chains, shut them in prisons, struck them with the sword, exposed them to beasts, consumed them with fires: all these things they did; but God did not hand them over to the sinners, because they were not handed over from their own desire. So pray for that, as much as you can, that God may not hand you over from your desire to the sinner. For you, by your desire, give place to the devil. For behold, the devil has set forth gain, and invited to fraud: you cannot have gain, unless you commit fraud. But gain is the bait, fraud the snare. So attend to the bait in such a way that you see also the snare: because you cannot attain the gain, unless you commit fraud; but if you commit fraud, you will be caught. I do not say, you will be caught, because you will be found out: for sometimes you will not be found out, at least by men; but by God? You will be caught, and dragged off, and slain. For everyone who does such things destroys himself. There, then, is the bait, there too is the snare; bridle your desire, and you will not fall into the snare: but if the desire of the bait conquers you, it puts your neck into the snare, and the fowler of souls will catch you. *Do not hand me over from my desire to the sinner.* Hence he is overshadowed in the day of war. For desire makes the heat; but the Lord's shade tempers desire, that we may be able to restrain that to which we were being swept, that we may not so glow as to be led to the snare. *They have thought against me; do not abandon me, lest perhaps they be exalted.* You have in another place: *Those who press me will exult if I am moved*[^28]. Such are these too, because such is the devil himself. When he has seduced a man, he rejoices, he triumphs over him; he himself is exalted, because the other has been humbled. But why has the other been humbled? Because he was evilly exalted: and he who triumphs over him will be humbled. For such are all who rejoice over evil; they seem to themselves for a time to glory, to be proud, to raise the neck. Let not their exaltation delight you; they have the bait in their throats and the hook: there is that by which they are delighted, there is that by which they are dragged off. *Do not abandon me, lest perhaps they be exalted*: that is, that they may not triumph over me, may not rejoice over me.

**13.** [v. 9.] *The head of their circuit, the labor of their lips will cover them*[^29]. Me, he says, the shade of your wings will cover: for you have overshadowed me in the day of war. Them, what will cover? *The head of their circuit*: that is pride. What is, *their circuit*? That they go around, and do not stand; that they go in a circle of error, where the journey is without end. For he who goes in length begins somewhere, ends somewhere: he who goes in a circle ends nowhere. This is the labor of the impious, which is shown more evidently in another psalm: *The impious walk in a circuit*[^30]. But the head of their circuit is pride; because the beginning of all sin is pride[^31]. But whence is pride *the labor of their lips*? For every proud man is feigned, every feigned man a liar. Men labor to speak a lie: for they would speak the truth with all ease. For he labors who feigns what he says. For he who wishes to speak the truth does not labor: for the truth itself speaks without labor. So of the man he said this to God: Your shade will protect me; their own lie will cover them: but their lie itself is the labor of their lips. *Behold, he has been in travail with injustice, has conceived sorrow, and brought forth iniquity*[^32]. For in every evil work there is labor, and every evil work, thought out, has a lie as its leader. For there is no truth, except in good work. And therefore, because all labor in a lie, what did Truth cry out? *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you*[^33]. It is the voice crying to those who labor in another psalm: *Sons of men, how long heavy of heart? why do you love vanity, and seek a lie*[^34]? Hear plainly in another place the labor in a lie: *They have taught their tongues to speak a lie; to act wickedly they have labored*[^35]. *The head of their circuit, the labor of their lips will cover them.*

**14.** [v. 10.] *Coals of fire will fall upon them on the earth, and you will cast them down*[^36]. What is, *on the earth*? Here, still in this life; here *coals of fire will fall upon them, and you will cast them down.* What are *coals of fire*? We know these coals. Or are these others, and others those of which we are about to speak? For these I see avail for punishment; but those which I am about to mention, for salvation. For it was said of certain coals, when a man asked for help against a deceitful tongue: *What may be given to you, or what may be added to you against a deceitful tongue? The sharp arrows of the mighty one, with desolating coals*[^37]: that is, the words of God piercing the heart, and destroying the oldness, and begetting love; and the examples of men who were dead and came to life again, and were black, and were made shining. For coals are darkness; the color shows it. But when the flame of love has approached them, and they have come to life again from the dead, let them hear from the apostle: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^38]. These are the coals, brothers, which we look upon, when, pierced by the arrow of God, we wish to change our life, and the evil tongues of men hinder us, of which kind he was here just now complaining; and they wish to seduce us from the way of truth, and rather to lead us into their errors, and to say to us that if we profess it, we will not fulfill it. We attend to those coals. He who was yesterday a drunkard, today is sober; he who was yesterday an adulterer, today is chaste; he who was yesterday a plunderer, today is a giver: all these are coals of fire. The examples of the coals come to the wound of the arrows (for I am not afraid to say wound, when the spouse herself cries out: *I am wounded by love*[^39]), and there comes about a wasting of the hay; on account of which they are called desolating coals. The hay is wasted, but the gold is purified; and the man changes life from death, and begins to be himself too a glowing coal: such a coal as was the apostle, who was first a persecutor and blasphemer and injurious[^40], black and extinguished; but having obtained mercy, he was kindled from heaven: the voice of Christ kindled him; all his blackness perished in him, he began, fervent in the spirit by which he was kindled, to kindle. Are such, then, the coals of fire that we are to understand here too, which fall upon these evil men, and cast them down? Plainly we are not forbidden to have this understanding. I see here shining for us a not improbable and not reprehensible opinion: I understand those coals to fall upon these, that they may be cast down. For upon some they come, that they may be kindled; upon others, that they may be cast down: for the coal itself said: *To some we are the fragrance of death unto death, to some we are the fragrance of life unto life*[^41]. For they see the just glowing in spirit, white with light, and by envying them they fall; this is what it means that coals of fire will come upon them on the earth, and they will be cast down. What is, *on the earth*? They are still here in this life; apart from that punishment which is kept for the impious, these coals cast them down, before the eternal fire comes: *Coals of fire will fall upon them on the earth, and will cast them down. In miseries they will not subsist.* Misery comes to them and they do not endure it: but the just man subsists; as he subsists who says: *But we also glory in tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, patience proof, proof hope; but hope does not confound: because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^42]. But when upon them there has fallen some pressure, some misery, they do not subsist; they fall. For when such men suffer pressures of this kind, they are not able to endure, they fall into evil iniquities; because they are handed over from their own desire to the sinner.

**15.** [v. 11.] *A man full of tongue will not be set straight upon the earth*[^43]. A man full of tongue loves lies. For what is pleasure to him, except to talk? For he does not attend to what he speaks, so long as he speaks. It cannot be that this man be set straight. What, then, ought the servant of God to be like, kindled by those coals, and himself made a wholesome coal? what ought he to be like? That he wish rather to hear than to speak, as it is written: *But let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak*[^44]: and, if it can be, let him desire this, not to have the necessity of speaking, and saying, and teaching. For behold, I say to your charity: my brothers, we now speak to you, that we may teach you something: how much better would it be if we all knew, and no one taught another; so that there were not one speaking, another hearing, but all hearing that one to whom it is said: *To my hearing you will give exultation and gladness*[^45]? Whence too that John did not so rejoice because he preached, and because he spoke; but he rejoiced because he heard: for he says: *But the friend of the bridegroom stands, and hears him, and rejoices with joy on account of the voice of the bridegroom*[^46]. And so, brothers, I would quickly say to your charity, wherein let each one prove himself: not that he should not speak, but that he should demand of himself the office of speaking; but let him have the joy of silence in his will, the voice of teaching in necessity. For when is there need of the voice of teaching? When you have an unskilled man, when you have an unlearned man. If it always delights you to teach, you always wish to have an unskilled man whom you may teach: but if you are well-disposed, and wish all to be learned, you do not wish always to have those whom you may teach; and the exercise or proof of your teaching will not be in your will, but in necessity. Let your joy be in the hearing of God, let your necessity be in your speaking; and you will not be a man full of tongue, that you may not fail to be set straight. Why do you wish to speak, and not wish to hear? You always go outside, you refuse to return within. For he who teaches you is within: when you teach, you as it were go outside to those who are outside. For from the inner one we hear the truth, and to those who are outside our heart we speak. For that we are said to have in our heart those of whom we think, is said according to a certain image which we have impressed of them. For if they were altogether within, they would surely know what was in our heart; and so there would be no need for us to speak to them. But if this delights you, what you do outside; see lest you swell outside, and be unable to return through the narrow gate[^47], and your God be unable to say to you: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^48]: but say to you, because what you loved was outside: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness*[^49]. For where he shows that it is evil to be cast outside, there he shows that it is good to enter within. For to the good servant what did he say? *Enter into the joy of your Lord.* But to the evil servant: *Cast him into the outer darkness.* So let us not love the outer things more, but the inner: let us rejoice in the inner; but in the outer let us have necessity, not will. *A man full of tongue will not be set straight upon the earth.*

**16.** *Evils will hunt the unjust man to destruction*[^50]. Evils come, and he does not subsist; therefore he said: *They will hunt to destruction.* For to many good men, to many just men evils have happened; and evils as it were found them. For therefore he said: *They will hunt*, because each one wishes to hide himself from evil: but when he is found by evil, he is as it were made into a hunt. But do only the evil flee from evils, when they are sought by evils? Was it not also said to the good: *If they persecute you in this city, flee to another*[^51]? So when the evil persecuted the good, that is, our martyrs, when they seized them, they hunted them; but not to destruction. For the flesh was pressed, the soul was crowned; and the soul was cast out of the flesh, nor was anything done to the flesh that would harm it afterward. Let the flesh be burned, let the flesh be struck, let the flesh be torn; was it therefore withdrawn from the Creator, because it was given into the hands of the persecutor? Will he who created it from what was not, not restore it better than it was? So whenever the just were captured, the evil indeed hunted them, but not to destruction. But those who will not be set straight and are full of tongue, evils will hunt them to destruction. Why? Because *in miseries they will not subsist.*

**17.** [v. 12.] *I have known that the Lord will do the judgment of the needy*[^52]. This needy man is not full of tongue: for he who is full of tongue wishes to abound, knows not how to hunger. But those are needy to whom it is said: *Knock, and it will be opened to you; seek, and you will find; ask, and it will be given to you*[^53]: that one is needy, of whom it is said: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they will be satisfied*[^54]. They groan among the scandals of the evil, they pray to their head, that they may be rescued from the evil man, and freed from the malicious man, and rescued from the hands of the unjust. They, then, are those whose cause the Lord will not neglect: and if now they suffer pressures, their glory will appear, when their head has appeared. For to such men, set here, it is said: *You are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^55]. So we are poor, our life is hidden; let us cry out to the bread: for there is a living bread which came down from heaven[^56]; and he who refreshes us on the way, will satisfy us in the homeland. For now, that we may endure, we are refreshed: for it is necessary that we hunger, until we are satisfied. *I have known that the Lord will do the judgment of the needy.* This man was sure *that the Lord will do the judgment of the needy, and the cause of the poor.* He will show the unjust how he loves his just; he will show the rich how he loves his poor. By the rich he meant the proud; by the poor he meant the humble: by the rich he meant those who, through abundance, do not seek; by the poor he meant those who, through desire, sigh. The Lord will do their cause.

**18.** [v. 13.] *But yet the just will confess to your name*[^57]. And when you do their cause, and when you do their judgment, they will confess to your name: they will attribute nothing to their merits, will attribute the whole only to your mercy. *But yet the just will confess to your name.* And because they will confess to your name, so that however just they have been, they take nothing to themselves of their own, attribute nothing to themselves of their own; what will come to pass, that they may set their heart straight? Because when they bend toward themselves, they twist the heart; but when they intend toward the Lord, they set the heart straight. And where will pleasure be, where rest, where joy, where blessedness? In themselves? No; but in him in whom they are light. *But now light in the Lord*[^58], he said. Therefore see what follows, see with what he concludes: *The upright will dwell with your countenance.* For it went ill with them in their own countenance; it will go well with them with your countenance. When they loved their own countenance, in the sweat of their countenance they ate bread[^59]. Let them return, and, the sweat wiped away, their labors ended, their groaning perishing, your countenance will come to them abounding in sufficiency. They will seek nothing further, because they have nothing better; they will no longer desert you, nor be deserted by you. For after the resurrection, what was said of the Lord? *You will fill me with gladness with your countenance*[^60]. Without his countenance he would not give us gladness. For this we cleanse our countenance, that we may rejoice at his countenance. For *we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when it appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^61]; because *the upright will dwell with your countenance.* Do we suppose with the countenance of the Father, and not with the countenance of the Son? or with the countenance of the Son, and not with the countenance of the Father? or is the countenance of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit in a certain way one? Let us see whether the Son himself does not promise us his countenance, with which he will make us glad. Now the Lord God inspired that this chapter in the Gospel be read, which would bear witness to this psalm. For the Lord himself says: *He who hears my precepts, and keeps them, he it is who loves me: and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will show myself to him*[^62]. What reward did he promise, dearly beloved? Did they not see him, to whom he promised to show himself? was he not before them? was not the face of his flesh present to their eyes? What is it that he wished to show to those who saw him? But because the disciples saw such a one as the Jews crucified; but God was hidden in that flesh; men could see the man, but could not see God, although in the man; because *blessed are the clean of heart, for they will see God*[^63]; he gave the form of man both to the devout and to the impious; he kept the form of God for the cleansed and devout, that we may rejoice in him, and it may go well with us forever with his countenance.
