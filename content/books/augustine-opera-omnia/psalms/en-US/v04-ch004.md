# Exposition of Psalm 76

**1.** [v. 1.] The threshold of this psalm is inscribed thus: *Unto the end, for Jeduthun, a Psalm of Asaph himself.* What *unto the end* is, you know: *For Christ is the end of the law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^1]. Jeduthun is interpreted "one who leaps over them"; Asaph is interpreted "assembly." Here, then, an assembly speaks that leaps over, so as to arrive at the end which is Christ Jesus. What things, then, must be leapt over, that we may be able to arrive at that end, where we shall no longer have anything to leap over, the text of the Psalm itself shows. For we must leap over whatever impedes us, whatever ensnares us, whatever binds us with a kind of birdlime, and weighs down our flight with a burden, until we arrive at that which suffices, beyond which there is nothing, beneath which are all things, and from which are all things. For Philip wished to behold the Father himself, and said to the Lord Jesus Christ: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^2]; as though he had to leap over whatever else there was, until he arrived at the Father, where he might now stand secure, and have nothing further to seek: for this is *It suffices.* But he who had most truly said: *I and the Father are one*[^3], admonished Philip, and taught every man who would understand Christ that in him too he has the end, because he and the Father are one: *So long a time,* he says, *I am with you, and you have not seen me? Philip, he who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^4]. Whoever, then, wishes to feel, to imitate, to hold the spirit of this psalm, let him leap over all carnal desires, let him trample the pomp and the enticement of this world, and set before himself nothing else in which to rest, except that from which are all things: in all which things he himself too labors until he arrives at the end. What, then, does this leaper indicate to us?

**2.** [v. 2.] *With my voice,* he says, *I cried to the Lord.* But many cry to the Lord for acquiring riches and avoiding losses, for the welfare of their families, for the stability of their house, for temporal happiness, for worldly dignity; finally, even for the very health of the body, which is the patrimony of the poor man. For these and such things many cry to the Lord; scarcely anyone for the sake of the Lord himself. For it is easy for a man to desire anything whatever from the Lord, and not to desire the Lord himself; as though what he gives could be sweeter than he himself who gives. Whoever, then, cries to the Lord for any other thing, is not yet a leaper. But what does this leaper say? *With my voice I cried to the Lord.* And lest you think that the voice with which he cried to the Lord was uttered for the sake of anything other than the Lord himself, he goes on to say: *And my voice to God.* For a voice is uttered by which we cry to God, and that voice is for the sake of something else, not for God. For a voice exists for the sake of that for which the voice is uttered. But this man, who loved God freely, who sacrificed to God willingly[^5], who had leapt over whatever is below, and had seen nothing else above himself into which to pour out his soul, except him from whom, and through whom, and in whom he was created, to whom he had cried with his voice, had made his very voice to be unto him: *My voice,* he says, *to God.* And was it without cause? See what follows: *And he attended to me.* Truly he attends to you then, when you seek him, not when you seek something else through him. It is said of certain men: *They cried, and there was none to save, to the Lord, and he did not hear them*[^6]. Why? Because their voice was not to the Lord. Scripture expresses this in another place, where it says of such men: *They did not call upon the Lord.* They did not cease to cry to him; and yet *they did not call upon the Lord.* What is *they did not call upon the Lord*? They did not call the Lord into themselves: they did not invite the Lord to their heart; they refused to be inhabited by the Lord. And therefore what befell them? *There they trembled with fear, where there was no fear*[^7]. Therefore they trembled at the loss of present things, because they were not full of him whom they did not call upon. They did not love freely, so as to be able, when temporal things were lost, to say: *As it pleased the Lord, so has it been done; blessed be the name of the Lord*[^8]. Therefore this man says: *My voice to the Lord, and he attended to me.* Let him teach us how this comes about.

**3.** [v. 3.] *In the day of my tribulation I sought out God.* Who are you, who do this? In the day of your tribulation, see what you seek out. If a prison makes the tribulation, you seek out release from prison: if a fever makes the tribulation, you seek out health: if hunger makes the tribulation, you seek out fullness: if losses make the tribulation, you seek out gain: if pilgrimage makes the tribulation, you seek out the city of your flesh. And why should I recount all things, or when could I recount all things? Do you wish to be a leaper? In the day of your tribulation seek out God: not something else through God, but God out of the tribulation; so that God removes the tribulation to this end, that you may cling to God in security. *In the day of my tribulation I sought out God*: not anything else, but *I sought out God.* And how did you seek him out? *With my hands by night before him.* Say this again: let us see, let us understand, let us imitate, if we can. In the day of your tribulation what did you seek out? *God.* How did you seek out? *With my hands.* When did you seek out? *By night.* Where did you seek out? *Before him.* And with what fruit did you seek out? *And I was not deceived.* Let us see all things, then, brothers, let us consider all things, let us question all things; both what the tribulation is in which this man sought out God, and what it is to seek out God with the hands, and what it is by night, and what it is before him: and there follows what all understand: *And I was not deceived.* For what is *And I was not deceived*? I found what I was seeking.

**4.** The tribulation is not to be thought of as this one or that one. For everyone who is not yet a leaper does not yet think there is any tribulation, except what has befallen this life from some sorrowful occasion: but that leaper reckons this whole life as his tribulation. For he so loves the heavenly homeland that the earthly pilgrimage itself is the greatest tribulation. For how is this life not a tribulation, I ask you? How is that not a tribulation which is said to be wholly a temptation? You have it written in the book of Job: *Is not the life of man on earth a temptation?*[^9] Did he say: The life of man is tempted on earth? Life itself *is a temptation*: if, then, a temptation, surely a tribulation. In this tribulation, then—that is, in this life—this leaper sought out God. How? *With my hands,* he says. What is *with my hands*? With my works. For he was not seeking something corporeal, so as to find by handling what he had lost; so as to seek with his hands a coin, gold, silver, clothing, or whatever such thing can be held with the hands. Although our Lord Jesus Christ himself willed to be sought out with the hands, when he showed his scars to the doubting disciple. But when that one, touching the scars of his wounds, exclaimed: *My Lord and my God!* did he not hear: *Because you have seen, you have believed; blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed*[^10]? If, then, that one, seeking Christ with his hands, deserved to hear this, so that it was a reproach to him to have sought thus; we who have been called blessed who have not seen and yet believe, what then—does it not pertain to us to seek with the hands? It does pertain, as I said, to seek with works. When does this happen? *By night.* What is *by night*? In this world. For it is night before the day shines forth in the glorified coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. For do you wish to see that it is night? Unless we had a lamp here, we would remain in darkness. For Peter says: *And we have the more sure prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the day-star arises in your hearts*[^11]. The day, then, is to come after this night; meanwhile in this night let not the lamp be lacking. And this perhaps is what we are now doing: by expounding these Letters, we bring in the lamp, that in this night we may rejoice. And it ought indeed to be kindled always in your houses. For to such it is said: *Do not quench the Spirit*[^12]. And as if expounding what he said, he goes on: *Do not despise prophecy*[^13]: that is, let the lamp shine always in you. And this light indeed, in comparison with a certain ineffable day, is called night. For the very life of the faithful, in comparison with the life of the unbelieving, is day. But how it is night, we have already said, and shown by the testimony of the apostle Peter: who indeed also named a lamp, and admonished us concerning that lamp to attend to it, that is, to the prophetic word, *until the day dawns, and the day-star arises in our hearts.* But how the life of the faithful is already day in comparison with the life of the wicked, Paul shows: *Let us cast off,* he says, *the works of darkness, and put on the armor of light; let us walk honorably as in the day*[^14]. Therefore, living honorably, in comparison with the life of the wicked, we are in the day. But this day of the life of the faithful does not suffice for this Jeduthun; he wishes to leap over this day too, until he comes to that day where he will dread no temptation of night at all. For here, although the life of the faithful is day, *the life of man on earth is a temptation*[^15]. Night, and day: day in comparison with the unbelieving; night in comparison with the angels. For the angels have a day which we do not yet have: we already have one which the unbelieving do not have; but the faithful do not yet have what the angels have: but then they will have it, when they will be equal to the angels of God; which was promised them in the resurrection[^16]. In this day, then, and still night; night in comparison with the future day which we desire, day in comparison with the past night which we have renounced: in this night, then, I say, let us seek God with our hands. Let works not cease; let us seek God, let the desire not be empty. If we are on the way, let us pay out our expenses, that we may be able to arrive: let us seek God with our hands. And even if by night we seek him whom we seek with our hands, we are not deceived, because we seek *before him.* What is *before him*? *Do not do your righteousness before men, to be seen by them; otherwise you will have no reward with your Father. When, then, you give alms,* he says—those are the hands seeking God—*do not sound a trumpet before you, as the hypocrites do: but let your almsgiving be in secret, and your Father who sees in secret will repay you*[^17]. Therefore: *With my hands by night before him, and I was not deceived.*

**5.** Yet how much this Jeduthun endured on this earth, and in this night, and how he had, in a certain way, the necessity of leaping over, with tribulations striking and pricking him downward, so that it was necessary to leap over, let us hear most attentively. *My soul refused to be comforted.* So great a weariness occupied me here, that my soul closed itself against all comfort. To such a one, whence the weariness? Perhaps because the vineyard was struck by hail, or because the olive did not come forth, or because the vintage was cut off by the rain. Whence the weariness to this man? Hear it from another psalm. For there too it is his voice: *Weariness held me, because of sinners forsaking your law*[^18]. So great a weariness, then, this man says he was affected with from an evil of this kind, that his soul refused to be comforted. Weariness had nearly swallowed him up, and sadness had altogether and irreparably sunk him; he refuses to be comforted. What, then, remained?

**6.** [v. 4.] First see whence he is comforted. Had he not waited for someone to be sorrowful together with him, and not found one[^19]? For where would he turn for comfort, whom weariness had occupied over sinners forsaking the law of God? Where would he turn? To any man of God? In many, perhaps, this man had already experienced great tribulation, the more he had presumed upon some delight in them. For righteous men are sometimes seen, and one rejoices over them; and it is necessary that one rejoice, since love cannot be such without joy: but in those over whom a man has rejoiced, if perhaps something perverse happens, as often happens, as great as was the gladness there, so great a grief comes on; so that afterward a man now fears to loosen the reins of his joys, fears to commit himself to gladness, lest, the more he had been gladdened, the more, if anything happens, he should waste away. Struck, then, by abounding scandals, as by many wounds, he closed himself against human comfort, and his soul refused to be comforted. And whence life? whence breath? *I was mindful of God, and I was delighted.* Not in vain had the hands worked; they had found a great comforter. Not by resting, *I was mindful of God, and I was delighted.* God is therefore to be proclaimed, of whom this man, being made mindful, was delighted, and comforted in a certain sadness, and refreshed when, in a way, salvation had been despaired of: God is to be proclaimed. Finally, that this man was comforted, he goes on to say: *I chattered.* In that very comfort, being made mindful of God, delighted, I chattered. What is *I chattered*? I was glad, I exulted in speaking. For those are properly called "chatterers" who by the common people are called wordy, when, gladness coming on, they are neither able nor willing to be silent. This man became such. And again what does he say? *And my spirit failed.*

**7.** [v. 5.] He had wasted away from weariness, he had been delighted by recalling God, again he had failed by chattering; what follows? *All my enemies anticipated the watches.* They watched over me, all my enemies: they watched more than I did; by watching they forestalled me. Where do they not set snares? Did not all my enemies anticipate the watches? For who are these enemies, except those of whom the Apostle says: *Yours is not a wrestling against flesh and blood; but against the principalities, and powers, and rulers of the world of these shadows, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places*[^20]? Therefore we exercise enmities against the devil and his angels. He called them rulers of the world, because they rule the lovers of the world: for they do not rule the world as though they were rulers of heaven and earth; but by "world" he means sinners. *And the world did not know him*[^21]: such a world they rule, which does not know Christ. Against these we have perpetual enmities. For whatever enmities you have against a man, you think to end them; either by his giving satisfaction, if he wronged you; or by yours, if you wronged him; or by mutual satisfaction, if you wronged each other; you labor to give satisfaction and be reconciled: but with the devil and his angels there is no reconciliation. They themselves envy us the kingdom of heaven. They cannot grow gentle toward us at all, because *all my enemies anticipated the watches.* They watched more to deceive than I to guard myself: *All my enemies anticipated the watches.* For how did they not anticipate the watches, who set scandals everywhere, snares everywhere? Weariness is in the heart; it is to be feared lest sadness swallow you up: in gladness it is to be feared lest by chattering your spirit fail: *All my enemies anticipated the watches.* Finally, in that very chattering, while you speak, and speak in security, how many things are often found which enemies wish to seize and reprove, from which they wish even to accuse and slander: Did he say this, did he mean that, did he speak this? What is a man to do, except what follows? *I was troubled, and I did not speak.* Troubled, then, lest in his chattering the enemies, anticipating the watches, should seek and find slanders, he did not speak. Yet this leaper would never rest in himself: and if perhaps he ceased from the chattering, by which it had crept upon him from that very speaking to please men; yet he did not desist, he did not cease to try to transcend even this. And what does he say?

**8.** [v. 6.] *I thought upon the ancient days.* Now this man, as one who had been beaten outside, took himself within, he acts in the inner chamber of his mind. And what he acts there, let him tell us: *I thought upon the ancient days.* It is well with him. See what he thinks, I ask you. He is within, with himself he thinks upon the ancient days. No one says to him: You spoke ill; no one says to him: You spoke too much; no one says to him: You thought perversely. So may it be well with him in himself, may God help him: let him think upon the ancient days, and tell us in that inner chamber of his what he did, whither he arrived, what he leapt over, where he remained: *I thought upon the ancient days, and I was mindful of the eternal years.* What are the eternal years? A great thought! See whether this thought wishes anything but great silence. From all outward din, from all tumult of human affairs, let him rest within, who wishes to think upon those eternal years. Are the years in which we are eternal, or those in which our forebears were, or those in which our descendants will be? Far be it that they be deemed eternal. For what of these years remains? Behold, in speaking we say: This year; and what do we hold of this year, except the one day in which we are? For the earlier days of this year have already passed, and are not held; but the future ones have not yet come. We are in one day, and we say: This year: rather say: Today, if you wish to say something present. For of the whole year what present thing do you hold? Whatever of it is past, is now not; whatever of it is future, is not yet: how, then, This year? Correct your speech: say, Today. You speak truly: Today, I will now say. Again, attend also to this, that of today the morning hours have already passed, the future hours have not yet come. Correct this too: say, This hour. And of this hour what do you hold? Certain moments of it have already passed; those that are future have not yet come. Say, This moment. What moment? While I speak syllables, if I say two syllables, the second does not sound until the first has passed: finally that very one syllable, if it have two letters, the latter letter does not sound until the former has departed. What, then, do we hold of these years! These years are changeable: the eternal years are to be thought of, the years that stand, which are not carried out by days coming and departing; the years of which Scripture says to God in another place: *But you are the same, and your years shall not fail*[^22]. These years this leaper thought upon, not in chattering outside, but in silence: *And I was mindful of the eternal years.*

**9.** [v. 7.] *And I meditated by night with my heart.* No slanderer lays for him the little traps of words; in his heart he meditated. *I chattered.* Behold that chattering. Observe again, lest your spirit fail. No, he says: I did not chatter as it were outside; in another way now. How now? *I chattered, and I searched my spirit.* If this man were searching the earth, to find veins of gold, no one would say he was foolish; rather, many would call him wise, who wished to come at gold: how many things has a man within, and does not dig? This man searched his spirit, and spoke with his own spirit, and in that very speaking chattered. He questioned himself, examined himself, was a judge within himself. And it follows: *I searched my spirit.* It is to be feared lest he remain in his own spirit: for he chattered outside; and because all his enemies anticipated the watches, he found sadness there, and his spirit failed. He who chattered outside, behold, began to chatter within in security, where alone in silence he thinks upon the eternal years: *And I thoroughly searched,* he says, *my spirit.* And here too it is to be feared lest he remain in his own spirit, and not be a leaper. Yet he now acts better than he acted outside. He has transcended something: and from here let us see whither. For this leaper does not cease, until he comes *to the end* from which the Psalm has its title: *I chattered,* he says, *and I searched my spirit.*

**10.** [v. 8.] And what did you find? *God will not cast off forever.* He had found weariness in this life; nowhere a faithful, nowhere a secure comfort. To whatever men he turned, he found scandal in them, or feared it. Nowhere, then, secure. To be silent was an evil for him, lest he be silent about good things: to speak and chatter outside was troublesome, lest all his enemies, anticipating the watches, seek slanders in his words. Greatly straitened in this life, he thought much of another life, where this is not a temptation. And when does one arrive there? For it is not uncertain that what we here suffer is the anger of God. This is said in Isaiah: *I will not be an avenger upon you forever, nor will I be angry with you for all time.* And he says why: *For the spirit shall go forth from me, and every breath I have made. On account of sin I grieved him a little, and struck him, and turned my face away from him; and he went away sorrowful, and walked his own ways*[^23]. What then, will this anger of God be always? This man did not find this in silence. For what does he say? *God will not cast off forever; and he will not add further that it should please him still*: that is, that it should please him still to cast off, and to cast off forever he will not add. It is necessary that he recall his servants to himself, it is necessary that he receive the fugitives returning to the Lord, it is necessary that he hear the voice of the fettered: *God will not cast off forever; and he will not add further that it should please him still.*

**11.** [vv. 9-10.] *Or will he cut off his mercy unto the end, from generation to generation? or will God forget to be merciful?* In you, from you, toward another there is no mercy, unless God grants it to you: and will God himself forget mercy? The stream runs: will the fountain itself be dried up? *Or will God forget to be merciful? or will he restrain his compassions in his anger?* that is, will he be so angry that he does not have mercy? More easily will he restrain anger than mercy. This he had also said through Isaiah: *I will not be an avenger upon you forever, nor will I be angry with you for all time.* This too, after he had said: *He went away sorrowful, and walked his own ways. His ways,* he says, *I saw, and I healed him*[^24]. When this man knew this, he transcended even himself, delighted in God, that where he was, and in his works he might rather chatter; not in his own spirit, not in what he was, but in him by whom he had been made. And from here, then, the leaper transcended. See the leaper, see whether he remains anywhere until he arrives at God.

**12.** [v. 11.] *And I said.* Now leaping over himself, what did he say? *Now I have begun*: when I had gone beyond even myself. *Now I have begun.* Here there is now no danger: for to remain even in myself was a danger. *And I said: Now I have begun; this is the change of the right hand of the Most High.* Now the Most High has begun to change me; now I have begun something in which I may be secure, now I have entered some hall of joys, where no enemy is feared, now I have begun to be in that region, where all my enemies do not anticipate the watches: *Now I have begun; this is the change of the right hand of the Most High.*

**13.** [v. 12.] *I was mindful of the works of the Lord.* Now see him roaming among the works of the Lord. For he chattered outside, and, being saddened thereby, his spirit failed: he chattered within with his heart, and with his spirit, and having thoroughly searched that same spirit of his, he was mindful of the eternal years, mindful of the mercy of the Lord, because the Lord will not cast off forever; and he now began to rejoice securely in his works, to exult securely. Let us now hear those works themselves, and let us too exult: but let us too leap over with our affections, and not rejoice over temporal things. For we too have our chamber. Why do we not enter there? why do we not act in silence? why do we not thoroughly search our spirit? why do we not think upon the eternal years? why do we not rejoice in the works of God? So let us now hear, and be delighted while he speaks, that even when we have departed from here, we may do what we were doing while he spoke; if at least we do that beginning of his which he said: *Now I have begun.* To rejoice in the works of God is to forget even yourself, if you can be delighted in him alone. For what is better than he? Do you not see that, when you return to yourself, you return to a worse state? *I was mindful of the works of the Lord: for I will be mindful from the beginning of your wondrous works.*

**14.** [v. 13.] *And I will meditate on all your works, and on your affections I will chatter.* Behold a third chattering. He chattered outside, when he failed; he chattered within in his spirit, when he made progress; he chattered in the works of God, when he arrived whither he had advanced. *And on your affections I will chatter*: not on my affections. Who lives without affections? And do you suppose, brothers, that those who fear God, worship God, love God, have no affections? Truly, will you suppose this, and dare to suppose it, that the game-board, the theater, hunting, fowling, fishing have their affections, and the works of God do not? and that meditation on God does not have certain inner affections of its own, when the world is beheld, and the spectacle of the nature of things is set before the eyes, and in these the Maker is sought, and is found nowhere displeasing, and above all things pleasing?

**15.** [v. 14.] *O God, your way is in the Holy One.* He now beholds the works of God's mercy concerning us, from these he chatters, and in these affections he exults. He began first from here: *Your way is in the Holy One.* What is your way in the Holy One? *I am,* he says, *the way, the truth, and the life*[^25]. Return, then, O men, from your affections. Where do you go? where do you run? whither do you flee, not only from God, but even from yourselves? Return, transgressors, to the heart[^26], search your spirit, recall the eternal years, find the mercy of God concerning you, attend to the works of his mercy: *His way is in the Holy One. Sons of men, how long heavy of heart?* what do you seek in your affections? *Why do you love vanity, and seek a lie? And know that the Lord has magnified his Holy One*[^27]: *Your way is in the Holy One.* Let us, then, attend to him, let us attend to Christ; there is his way: *O God, your way is in the Holy One. What God is great, like our God?* The nations have affections concerning their gods, they adore idols; they have eyes, and see not; they have ears, and hear not; they have feet, and walk not[^28]. Why do you walk to a god who does not walk? I do not, he says, worship those things. And what do you worship? The divine power that is there? Surely you worship what is said elsewhere: *For the gods of the nations are demons*[^29]. Either you worship idols, or demons. Neither idols, nor demons, he says. And what do you worship? The stars, the sun, the moon, those heavenly things. How much better is he who made both earthly and heavenly things! *What God is great like our God?*

**16.** [v. 15.] *You are the God who alone does wonders.* You are truly the great God, doing wonders in body, in soul, alone doing them. The deaf have heard, the blind have seen, the sick have grown strong, the dead have risen, the paralyzed have been knit together[^30]. But those miracles were then in bodies: let us see in the soul. They are sober, who a little before were drunkards; they are faithful, who a little before were adorers of idols; they give their own goods to the poor, who before seized the goods of others: *What God is great, like our God? You are the God who alone does wonders.* Moses too did them, but not alone: Elijah too did them, Elisha too did them, the apostles too did them; but none of them alone. That they might do them, you were with them; you, when you did them, they were not with you. For they were not with you when you did, when you made even them. *You are the God who alone does wonders.* How alone? Is it perhaps the Father, and not the Son? or the Son, and not the Father? Rather, the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit. *You are the God who alone does wonders.* For not three gods, but one God alone does wonders, even in this leaper. For that he might even leap over, and arrive at these things, was a wonder of God: when within he chattered with his spirit, that he might leap over even his own spirit, and be delighted in the works of God, he himself did wonders there. But what did God do? *You made your power known among the peoples.* Hence this assembly, Asaph the leaper, because he made his power known among the peoples. What power of his did he make known among the peoples? *But we preach Christ crucified: to the Jews indeed a scandal, but to the Gentiles foolishness; but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the Power of God, and the Wisdom of God*[^31]. If, then, Christ is the Power of God, he made Christ known among the peoples. Or do we not yet recognize even this? and are we so foolish, do we lie so far below, do we leap over nothing so, that we do not see this? *You made your power known among the peoples.*

**17.** [v. 16.] *You have redeemed with your arm your people. With your arm:* that is, with your power. And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed[^32]? *You have redeemed with your arm your people, the sons of Israel, and Joseph.* How, as it were two peoples, *the sons of Israel, and Joseph*? Are not the sons of Joseph among the sons of Israel? Yes, plainly. This we know, this we read, this Scripture cries out, this the truth shows, that Israel, the same as Jacob, had twelve sons, among whom one was Joseph; and of the twelve sons of Israel, as many as were born, belong to the people of Israel. How, then, does he say: *The sons of Israel and Joseph*? He admonished us, I know not how, that something here is to be distinguished. Let us search our spirit: perhaps God placed something there, whom we ought to seek even by night with our hands, that we be not deceived; perhaps we too shall find in this distinction *the sons of Israel, and Joseph.* By Joseph he wished another people to be understood; he wished the people of the Gentiles to be understood. Why the people of the Gentiles by Joseph? Because Joseph was sold into Egypt by his brothers[^33]. That Joseph whom his brothers envied, and sold into Egypt, sold into Egypt, labored, was humbled; recognized and exalted, he flourished, he ruled. And by all these things what did he signify? What, except Christ sold by his brothers, cast out of his own land, as it were into the Egypt of the Gentiles? There first humbled, when the martyrs suffered persecutions: now exalted, as we see; since it has been fulfilled in him: *All the kings of the earth shall adore him; all nations shall serve him*[^34]. Therefore Joseph is the people from the Gentiles; but Israel is the people from the nation of the Hebrews. God redeemed his people, *the sons of Israel and Joseph.* Through what? Through the cornerstone, in which two walls are joined.

**18.** [v. 17.] And he follows out how: *The waters saw you, O God.* What are the *waters*? Peoples. What these *waters* are, is said in the Apocalypse; the answer is given, Peoples: there we find most plainly the waters set as a figure of peoples[^35]. But above he had said: *You made your power known among the peoples.* Rightly, then: *The waters saw you, O God; the waters saw you, and they feared.* Therefore they were changed, because they feared. *The waters saw you, O God, and they feared, and the depths were troubled.* What are the *depths*? The deeps of the waters. Who is not troubled among the peoples, when his conscience is struck? You seek the depth of the sea: what is deeper than the human conscience? This depth was troubled, when God redeemed his people with his arm. How were the depths troubled? When they poured out all their consciences by confessing: *And the depths were troubled.*

**19.** [v. 18.] *A multitude of the sound of waters.* In the praises of God, in the confessions of sins, in hymns and canticles, in prayers, *a multitude of the sound of waters. The clouds gave voice.* Hence this sound of waters, hence the troubling of the depths, because *the clouds gave voice.* What clouds? The preachers of the word of truth. What clouds? Those of which God threatens a certain vineyard, which made thorns instead of grapes, and says: *I will command my clouds not to rain rain upon it*[^36]. Finally, the apostles, leaving the Jews, went to the Gentiles: in all the nations *the clouds gave voice;* by preaching Christ, *the clouds gave voice.*

**20.** [v. 19.] *For your arrows passed through.* Those same voices of the clouds he again called arrows. For the words of the Evangelists were arrows. For they are likenesses. For properly neither is rain an arrow, nor an arrow rain; but the word of God is both an arrow, because it strikes, and rain, because it waters. Let no one now wonder, then, at the troubling of the depths, when *your arrows passed through.* What is *they passed through*? They did not remain in the ears, but pierced the hearts. *The voice of your thunder in the wheel.* What is this? how are we to understand it? May the Lord help. *The voice of your thunder in the wheel.* As boys we used to suppose, when we heard thunders from the sky, that as it were vehicles had come out of a stable: for thunders have a certain shaking like vehicles. Are we to return to these childish notions, in order to understand *the voice of your thunder in the wheel*; as though God had some vehicles in the clouds, and the passing of vehicles stirred up that din? Far be it. This is childish, vain, trifling. What, then, is *the voice of your thunder in the wheel*? Your voice rolls. Neither do I understand this. What shall we do? We question Jeduthun himself, in case he himself expounds what he said: *The voice,* he says, *of your thunder in the wheel.* I do not understand: let me hear what you say: *Your lightnings appeared to the world.* Say it, then: I had not understood. The world (orbis) is a wheel: for the circuit of the world is rightly called orbis too; whence a little round thing, a rotella, is called orbiculus. *The voice of your thunder in the wheel: your lightnings appeared to the world.* Those clouds went around the world in the wheel; they went around thundering and flashing, they moved the deep, they thundered with precepts, they flashed with miracles: for into all the earth their sound went out, and to the ends of the world their words[^37]. *The earth was moved, and made to tremble*: that is, all who dwell in the earth. But by a likeness the earth itself is the sea. Why? Because all the nations are named with the name of the sea, because human life is bitter, and subject to storms and tempests. And if you attend to this, that men devour one another like fishes, when the greater swallows the lesser: this, then, is the sea; thither the Evangelists went.

**21.** [v. 20.] *Your way is in the sea.* A while ago *your way is in the Holy One,* now *your way is in the sea,* because the Holy One himself is in the sea, and rightly even walked upon the waters of the sea[^38]. *Your way is in the sea*: that is, among the Gentiles your Christ is preached. For in another psalm it is said thus: *May God have mercy on us, and bless us; may he make his face to shine upon us, that we may know your way on earth.* Where *on earth*? *Your salvation among all nations*[^39]: this is: *Your way is in the sea. And your paths in many waters*: that is, in many peoples. *And your footsteps shall not be known.* He touched I know not whom, and it would be a wonder if not the Jews themselves. Behold, the mercy of Christ has now been so spread abroad to the Gentiles, that *your way* is *in the sea, and your paths in many waters; and your footsteps shall not be known.* By whom shall they not be known, except by those who still say: Christ has not yet come? Why do they say: Christ has not yet come? Because they do not yet recognize him walking upon the sea.

**22.** [v. 21.] *You led your people like sheep, by the hand of Moses and Aaron.* Why he added this is somewhat difficult to track down. Help us, then, with your attention, because after these two verses there will be an end both of the Psalm and of the sermon; lest perhaps, when you think something remains, from fear of labor you attend less to the present matter. When he had said: *Your way is in the sea,* which we understand as, among the Gentiles; *and your paths in many waters,* which we understand as, in many peoples, he added: *And your footsteps shall not be known.* And we were asking by whom they would not be known; and he straightway subjoined: *You led your people like sheep, by the hand of Moses and Aaron*: that is, by this your people which was led by the hand of Moses and Aaron, your footsteps shall not be known. Why, then, was it set down, except for the sake of rebuking and reproaching: *Your way is in the sea*? Whence is *your way in the sea,* except because it was shut out from your land? They expelled Christ, the sick refused to have him as their Savior: but he began to be among the Gentiles, and in all nations, in many peoples. The remnant of that people too was saved. The ungrateful multitude remained outside, and the broad part of Jacob's thigh, limping[^40]. For the broadness of the thigh is understood as the multitude of the lineage, and there was made, in the greater part of the Israelites, a certain vain and foolish crowd, which would not recognize the footsteps of Christ in the waters. *You led your people like sheep,* and they did not know you. So great goods you did with them! you divided the sea, you led them across between the waters by the dry ground, you covered their pursuing enemies with the waves, you rained manna on them needy in the desert, leading them *by the hand of Moses and Aaron;* and they expelled you from themselves, so that your way was in the sea, and they would not know your footsteps.
