# On the Same Psalm 118 (12)

**1.** [v. 41.] To the former sermon, which we recently had on the psalm that is the longest of all, this one must be joined, about its words which follow. These are the words: *And let your mercy come upon me, O Lord.* Which sentence seems annexed to the earlier: for he does not say: *Let it come upon me;* but he says: *And let it come.* But that earlier one is: *Behold, I have coveted your commandments; in your righteousness give me life*: then there follows: *And let your mercy come upon me, O Lord.* What, then, does he ask here, except that he may do the commandments which he coveted, through the mercy of him who commanded? For he expounds in a way what he said: *In your righteousness give me life;* when he subjoins: *And let your mercy come upon me, O Lord, your salvation according to your eloquence*: that is, according to your promise. Whence the Apostle wishes us to be understood sons of promise; lest we suppose that what we are is our own, but ascribe the whole to the grace of God. For Christ *became for us wisdom from God, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption; that, as it is written: He who glories, may glory in the Lord*[^1]. What, then, he says: *In your righteousness give me life;* he certainly desires to be given life in Christ, and this is the mercy which he asks to come upon him. Christ himself is also the *salvation* of God; by which word he expounded what mercy he meant where he said: *And let your mercy come upon me, O Lord.* If, then, we ask what this mercy is, let us hear what follows: *Your salvation according to your eloquence.* For this was promised by him who *calls the things that are not, as though they were*[^2]. For they were not yet to whom it was promised, lest anyone glory in merits. And those to whom it was promised, they themselves too were promised; that the whole body of Christ may say, *by the grace of God I am what I am*[^3].

**2.** [v. 42.] *And I will answer,* he says, *those who reproach me a word.* Whether, *those who reproach me a word;* or, *a word I will answer,* is ambiguous: but whichever of them sounds Christ. For they reproach us with him, to whom he is, crucified, either a scandal, or foolishness: not knowing that the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us; which Word was in the beginning, and was with God, and was God. But even if they themselves do not reproach the Word which lies hidden from them, because his divinity is not known by those by whom his weakness on the cross is scorned; let us yet answer the Word, and not be frightened or confounded by the reproach. For if they had known the Word, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory. But he answers the Word to those who reproach, upon whom the mercy of God comes; that is, his very salvation comes to protect, not to grind to powder. For upon certain ones to be ground to powder he is going to come, who now, while they scorn his lowliness, by striking against him are shattered. For so he says in the Gospel: *He who falls upon this stone will be shattered; but on whomever it falls, it will grind him to powder*[^4]. Those, then, who reproach us, strike against and fall upon him. But we, lest we strike against and fall, let us not fear their reproaches, but answer them the Word. This is the word of faith which we preach. For if you believe, he says, in your heart that Jesus is Lord, and confess with your mouth that God raised him from the dead; you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation[^5]. It is too little, then, to have Christ in the heart, and be unwilling to confess while reproach is feared: but to those who reproach the Word must be answered. But that the martyrs might be able to do this, it was promised to them, and said: *For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father, who speaks in you*[^6]. Therefore this one too, when he had said: *I will answer those who reproach me a word;* immediately there follows: *Because I have hoped in your words*: which is certainly, in your promises.

**3.** [v. 43.] But because very many, although they belong to that body whose words these are, when the heavy weight of persecution pressed on them could not endure the reproach, and by falling away denied Christ, there follows: *And take not the word of truth utterly out of my mouth*[^7]. He says *out of my mouth* because it is the unity of the body that speaks, among whose members are reckoned even those who fell away for an hour by denying, but who afterward revived by repenting, or even regained, by a restored confession, the palm of martyrdom they had lost. Therefore the word of truth was not taken *utterly,* or, as some manuscripts have it, not *altogether,* that is, not in every way, out of the mouth of Peter, in whom there was a figure of the Church; for although for an hour he denied, troubled by fear, yet by weeping he was restored, and by confessing he was afterward crowned. And so the whole body of Christ speaks, that is, the whole company of the holy Church; and in this whole body—whether because, when very many denied, there remained the strong who would strive for the truth even to death, or because even of those who had denied many were restored—the word of truth was not taken *utterly* out of its mouth. But when he says *take not,* we must understand it as: do not allow it to be taken; on account of which we say in prayer, *Lead us not into temptation*[^8]. And the Lord himself says to Peter, *I have prayed for you, that your faith may not fail*[^9]; that is, that the word of truth may not be taken *utterly* out of your mouth. There follows: *For I have hoped in your judgments;* or, as some have more carefully rendered from the Greek, *I have hoped beyond hope;* a word which, though formed in a less usual way, nevertheless fulfills the need of interpreting the truth. We must therefore search the sense of this passage more attentively, so that we may understand, as far as God helps us, what it means: *In your words I have hoped; in your judgments I have hoped beyond hope. I will answer,* he says, *those who reproach me with a word, for I have hoped in your words;* that is, because you yourself promised me this. *And take not the word of truth utterly out of my mouth, for in your judgments I have hoped beyond hope;* that is, because your judgments, by which you correct and scourge me, not only do not take away my hope but even increase it, since whom the Lord loves he corrects, and scourges every son whom he receives. For behold, the holy and the humble of heart, by presuming on you, did not fail in persecutions; and behold, even those who failed by presuming on themselves, yet belonged to that body, wept when they came to know themselves, and found your grace more solidly because they lost their pride. Therefore *take not the word of truth utterly out of my mouth, for in your judgments I have hoped beyond hope.*

**4.** [v. 44.] *And I shall keep your law always;* that is, if you do not take the word of truth out of my mouth, *I shall keep your law always. For ever and ever:* he shows what he meant by *always.* For sometimes *always* is understood as: as long as one lives here; but this is not *for ever and ever.* Indeed it has been better rendered thus than, as some manuscripts have it, *for eternity and for ever and ever,* because they could not say "and for eternity of the eternal." This law, then, is to be understood as the one of which the Apostle says, *Love is the fulfilling of the law*[^10]. For this law will be kept by the saints, out of whose mouth the word of truth is not taken, that is, by the very Church of Christ, not only in this age, that is, until this age is ended, but also in the other, which is called the *age of the age.* For we are not going to receive there, as here, precepts of the law to keep, but we shall preserve that very fullness of the law, as I have said, without any fear of sinning; because we shall both love God more fully when we see him, and love our neighbor, since God will be all in all; nor will there be any place for a false suspicion about one's neighbor, where no one will be hidden from anyone.
