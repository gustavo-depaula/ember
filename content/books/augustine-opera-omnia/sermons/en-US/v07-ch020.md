# Sermon 247: In the Days of Easter

**1.** The resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ according to the truth of the four Evangelists seems to have been completed yesterday. For on the first day the resurrection was read according to Matthew, on another day according to Luke, on the third day according to Mark, on the fourth, that is yesterday, according to John. But since John and Luke wrote very many things about the resurrection itself and what happened after the resurrection, which cannot be recited in one reading; yesterday too we heard something according to John, and today, and still other readings remain. Today, then, what have we heard? That on the very day on which he rose, that is, the Lord's day, when it had become late, and the disciples were in one place, and the doors were shut for fear of the Jews, the Lord appeared in their midst. On that very day, then, as the Evangelist John witnesses, he appeared to his disciples twice, in the morning and in the evening. From the fact that he appeared in the morning, that reading too was recited: but that on the same day he appeared again in the evening, we have just now heard as it was recited. There was no need for these things to be recalled by me, but that they should be noted by you: yet because of the lesser understanding or greater negligence of certain people, it was fitting to recall them, that you may know not only what you have heard, but also from what Scripture is read to you what you have heard.

**2.** Let us see, then, what is proposed to us to speak about by today's reading. For the reading itself admonishes us, and in a way speaks to us, that we should say something about how the Lord, who rose in that solidity of body, so that he was not only seen by the disciples, but even touched, could appear to them with the doors shut. For some are so moved about this matter that they almost come into danger, bringing against the divine miracles the prejudgments of their own reasonings. For thus they argue: If it was a body, if it was flesh and bones, if this rose from the tomb, which hung on the wood; how could it enter through shut doors? If it could not, they say: It did not happen. If it could, how could it? If you grasp the manner, it is not a miracle: and if it does not seem to you a miracle, you are close to denying that he even rose from the tomb. Look back from the beginning at the miracles of your Lord, and give me a reason for each. A man did not approach, and a virgin conceived. Give a reason how a virgin conceived without a male. Where reason fails, there is the building up of faith. See, you have one miracle in the Lord's conception: hear another in the birth too. A virgin gave birth, and remained a virgin. Already then, before he rose, the Lord was born through shut doors. You ask of me and say: If he entered through shut doors, where is the measure of the body? And I answer: If he walked on the sea, where is the weight of the body? But the Lord did that as Lord. So when he rose, did he cease to be Lord? What of the fact that he even made Peter walk on the sea? What divinity could do in him, faith fulfilled in the other. But Christ, because he could; Peter, because Christ helped him. If, then, you begin with human sense to discuss the reason of miracles, I fear lest you lose faith. Do you not know that nothing is impossible to God? Whoever, then, says to you, If he entered through shut doors, it was not a body: answer him on the contrary, On the contrary, if he was touched, it was a body; if he ate, it was a body: and he did it by a miracle, not by nature. Is not the daily course of nature itself to be marveled at? All things are full of miracles: but through their constant occurrence they have grown cheap. Give me a reason: I ask something about the customary and usual things: give a reason why the seed of so great a fig tree is so small that it can scarcely be seen, and the lowly gourd produces so large a seed. Yet in that tiny grain of seed, scarcely visible, if you consider with the mind, not the eyes; in that smallness, those straits, both the root lies hidden, and the wood is set within, and the future leaves are bound up, and the fruit that will appear on the tree is already sent ahead in the seed. There is no need to run through many things: of daily things no one gives a reason, and you demand of me a reason about miracles. Read, then, the Gospel, and believe the deeds that are marvelous. Greater is what God did, and you do not marvel at what exceeds all works: it was nothing, and there is a world.

**3.** But the mass of the body could not, you say, pass through the doors, which were shut. How great was that mass, I ask you? As great surely as it is in all men: surely not as great as it is in a camel? Surely not so great. Read the Gospel, hear him: when he wished to show the difficulty of a rich man entering the kingdom of heaven, he said: *It is easier for a camel to enter through the eye of a needle than for a rich man into the kingdom of heaven.* On hearing this the disciples, considering that it could in no way happen that a camel should enter through the eye of a needle, were saddened within themselves, saying: *If it is so, who then can save himself?* If it is easier for a camel to enter through the eye of a needle than for a rich man into the kingdom of heaven; in no way can a camel enter through the eye of a needle; so none of the rich can be saved. The Lord answered: *The things that are impossible to men are easy to God*[^1]. God can both pass a camel through the eye of a needle, and bring a rich man into the kingdom of heaven. Why do you slander me about the shut doors? Shut doors have at least a crack: compare the crack of the doors to the eye of a needle, compare the mass of human flesh to the size of camels; and do not slander the divinity of the miracles.

[^1]: Matt 19:24-26
