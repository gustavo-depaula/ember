# Sermon 114

**1.** The holy Gospel, which we have just now heard recited, admonished us about the remission of sins. Hence you are to be admonished by our sermon. For we are ministers of the word, not our own, but surely of God and our Lord, whom no one serves without glory, whom no one despises without punishment. Therefore our Lord Jesus Christ himself, who, abiding with the Father, made us, and, made for us, remade us; that same Lord our God says to us, as we have heard: *If your brother sins against you, rebuke him; and if he repents, forgive him: and if he sins against you seven times in a day, and coming says: I repent, forgive him* [^1]. By *seven times in a day* he wished nothing else to be understood than however often; lest perhaps he sin eight times, and you refuse to pardon. What, then, is *seven times?* Always, however often he sins and repents of it. For this is: *Seven times a day I will praise you* [^2]; which is in another psalm: *His praise shall always be in my mouth* [^3]. And why *seven times* is set for that which is always, there is a most certain reason; for by seven days coming and returning the whole of time is turned.

**2.** Whoever, then, you who think of Christ, and desire to receive what he promised, do not be slothful to do what he commanded. For what did he promise? Eternal life. And what did he command? Give your brother pardon. As though he said to you: You, a man, give a man pardon, that I, God, may come to you. But, to omit, or rather for the time being pass over, those higher divine promises, in which our Creator will make us equal to his angels, that we may live with him and in him and from him without end: to say not this for the time being, this very thing which you are bidden to give your brother, do you not want to receive from your God? This very thing, I say, which you are bidden to give your brother, do you not want to receive from your Lord? Tell me if you do not want it, and do not give. What is this, except that you pardon the one asking, if you ask pardon for yourself? Or if you have nothing for which you should be pardoned, I dare say: Do not pardon. Although I ought not to say even this. Even if you have nothing for which you should be pardoned, you ought to pardon; because God too pardons, who has nothing for which he should be pardoned.

**3.** You will say: But I am not God, I am a sinful man. Thanks be to God, that you confess you have sins. Therefore pardon, that they may be forgiven you. Yet God himself exhorts us to imitate him. First the Lord Christ himself, of whom the apostle Peter said: *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that you should follow his footsteps* [^4]. And surely he himself had no sin, and died for our sins, and shed his blood for the remission of sins. He took on for us what he did not owe, that he might free us from the debt. He did not owe death, nor we to live. Why? Because we were sinners. Neither was death owed to him, nor life to us: what was not owed to him he received, what was not owed to us he gave. But since the matter is the remission of sins, lest you think it too much for you to imitate Christ, hear the Apostle saying: *Forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you* [^5]. *Be therefore* (they are the Apostle's words, his, not mine) *be therefore imitators of God* [^6]. Is it presumptuous to imitate God? *Imitators of God*. Surely it is presumptuous. *As most beloved sons* [^7]. You are called a son: if you refuse the imitation, why do you seek the inheritance?

**4.** I would say this if you had no sin which you desired to be relaxed for you. But now whoever you are, you are a man: be you just, you are a man; be you a layman, you are a man; be you a monk, you are a man; be you a cleric, you are a man; be you a bishop, you are a man; be you an apostle, you are a man. Hear the voice of the apostle: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves* [^8]. Who said it? That John, that John the evangelist, whom the Lord Christ loved above the rest, who reclined upon his breast [^9], he himself says: *If we say*: he did not say: If you say that you have no sin; but: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us* [^10]. He joined himself in the fault, that he might be joined also in the pardon. *If we say*: see who says it: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. But if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity* [^11]. How does he cleanse? By pardoning, not as though not finding what to punish, but finding what to forgive. So if we have sins, brothers, let us pardon the penitent. Let us not retain enmities in our heart. For those who retain enmities harm rather their own heart.

**5.** I want you, then, to be one pardoning, because I hold you asking pardon. You are asked, pardon: you are asked, and you will ask; you are asked, pardon; just as you too ask that you be pardoned. Behold, the time of praying will come: in the words you are going to say, I will hold you. You are going to say: *Our Father who are in heaven* [^12]. For you will not be in the number of the sons, if you are not going to say: *Our Father*. So you are going to say: *Our Father who are in heaven*. Continue: *Hallowed be your name*. Say still: *Your kingdom come*. Continue still: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so on earth*. See what you add: *Give us this day our daily bread*. Where are your riches? Behold, you beg. Yet, of what we are speaking, say still after: *Give us this day our daily bread*. Say still what follows: *Forgive us our debts*. You have come to my words: *Forgive*, he says, *us our debts*. Do, then, what follows. *Forgive us our debts*. By what right? by what pact? by what agreement? by what guarantee recited? *As we too forgive our debtors* [^13]. It is too little that you do not forgive: you even lie to God besides. The condition is set, the law is fixed: Forgive, as I forgive. So he does not forgive, unless you forgive. Forgive, as I forgive. You want it to be forgiven you when you ask, forgive the one asking. These prayers the heavenly Jurist composed: he does not deceive you; ask according to the heavenly law: say: *Forgive, as we too forgive*; and do what you say. He who lies in his prayers lacks the benefit: he who lies in his prayers both loses the cause and finds the penalty. And if someone lies to the Emperor, when he comes, he is convicted that he lies; but when you in praying lie, you are convicted in the very prayer. For God does not seek witnesses against you to convict you. He who composed the prayers for you is your advocate: if you lie, he is your witness; if you do not correct yourself, he will be your judge. Therefore both say it, and do it. Because if you do not say it, asking against the law you do not obtain; but if you say it and do not do it, you will even be guilty of a lie. There is no way to pass over this verse, except that what you say be fulfilled. Can we blot out this verse from our prayer? Or do you want that to be there: *Forgive us our debts*: and blot out what follows: *As we too forgive our debtors*? You will not blot it out, lest you be blotted out first. In prayer, then, you say: *Give*; you say: *Forgive*: that you may receive what you do not have, and what you have offended in be relaxed for you. You want to receive, give; you want it to be forgiven you, forgive. It is a brief connection. Hear Christ himself in another place: *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given you* [^14]. *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you*. What will you forgive? What others have sinned against you. And what will be forgiven you? What you have sinned. And you: *Give, and it will be given you*. You who desire eternal life, support the temporal life of the poor, sustain the temporal life of the poor, and for that so small and earthly seed you will receive a harvest, eternal life. Amen.
