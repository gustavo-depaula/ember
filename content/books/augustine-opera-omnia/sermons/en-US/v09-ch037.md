# Sermon 327: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1.** We have sung to God with the voice of the martyrs: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^1]. It is the voice of the martyrs. Who would dare to say: *Judge me, O God,* except one who has the best cause? The soul is tempted by promises and threats, it is soothed by enticements, it is tortured by pains: all were conquered for Christ by the unconquered martyrs. The promising world was conquered, the raging world was conquered. Pleasure did not hold them, torment did not terrify them. Gold purged in the furnace does not fear the fire of Gehenna. So, as if purged by the fire of tribulation, the most blessed martyr securely says: *Judge me, O God.* Whatever good you find in me, judge. You gave me what may please you; find it in me, and judge me. The sweetness of the world did not hold me, the tribulation of the world does not twist me away from you. *Judge me, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy.* Many suffer tribulations; they have an equal punishment, but they do not have an equal cause. Adulterers suffer many evils, evildoers suffer many evils, robbers and murderers suffer many evils, all the wicked suffer many evils, many evils, he says, I too your martyr suffer: but *distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy,* of robbers, of murderers, of all the wicked. They can suffer such things as I: they cannot have such a cause. I am purged in the furnace; they turn to ash. Heretics too suffer, and more from themselves; and they want to be called martyrs. But against them we have sung: *Distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy.* It is not the punishment that makes the martyr, but the cause.

**2.** In the passion of the Lord there were three crosses; one punishment, but an unequal cause. At the right one thief, at the left the other thief: in the middle the judge, hanging on the cross between both, as though pronouncing from the tribunal, heard one saying: *Free yourself, if you are righteous;* heard the other reproving his fellow and saying: *Do you not fear God? We suffer these things for our evils; but this man is righteous*[^2]. He had a bad cause, and he distinguished the cause of the martyrs. For what else is: *We suffer these things for our evils; but this man is righteous?* Who would distinguish the cause of the martyrs from the cause of the impious suffering punishment? This man, he says, is acknowledged righteous; we suffer for ourselves, we suffer for our evils. *Lord:* see what he says to the companion of his punishment. Christ was hanging equally; but he was not equally cheapened. The Lord was acknowledged by the hanging man. There was one fellowship of the cross; there was not one reward. What do I say? Do you give Christ a reward, who is the giver of rewards? *Lord,* he says, *remember me, when you come into your kingdom*[^3]. He saw him hanging, saw him crucified; and he hoped he would reign. *Remember me,* he says, not now, but *when you come into your kingdom.* I have done many evils, he says, I do not hope for quick rest for myself: let my torments suffice until your coming. Now let me be tortured; when you come, then spare me. He deferred himself; but Christ offered paradise to one not asking for it. *Remember me:* but when? *When you come into your kingdom.* And the Lord: *Amen I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise*[^4]. My disciples have forsaken me, my disciples have despaired of me; and you on the cross knew me, you did not despise me dying, you hoped I would reign: *Today you will be with me in paradise.* I do not withdraw from you. The cause was distinguished; was the punishment? So it is a good voice: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy.* Let all of us who live in this world labor to have a good cause: that if anything befalls us in this world, we may go out from here with a good cause.

[^1]: Ps 43:1 (42:1)
[^2]: See Luke 23:39-41
[^3]: Luke 23:42
[^4]: Luke 23:43
