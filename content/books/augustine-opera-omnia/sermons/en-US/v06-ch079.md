# Sermon 229/J: A Tractate on the Wednesday of Easter

**1.** The Lord Christ deigned to make his resurrection certain and true by many and various proofs, for the sake of building up faith, and driving faithlessness from the heart, and removing all doubt about his resurrection. It was too little to offer himself to be seen with the eyes, if he did not also offer himself to be handled with the hands. What many impious men and the Manichean heretics suspect and believe about Christ, namely, that there was no true flesh, but a spirit having the image of flesh, not to instruct faith but to deceive the eyes—so that he was not a man, but was thought to be one; that he was not flesh, but only seemed to be so—what the Manicheans, then, believe, and from it made a dogma, and confirmed their error, this first thought arose in the hearts of the Apostles. And those Manicheans indeed never believe that Jesus was a man; they fear to give flesh to the Word, and do not fear to set falsehood against the truth. He has true flesh, by which the truth may show falsehood, and may build up the truth in the hearts of men. Those men, then, never believed that the Lord Jesus Christ was a man; but the disciples knew him as a man, with whom they had conversed for so long a time. They saw him walking, sitting, sleeping, eating, drinking; they knew it all, they knew that he had sat down wearied at the well[^1]. From this long association they knew him to be a true man; but after he died, that which they knew, how could they believe could be raised up, when it could die? He appeared, then, to their eyes such as they had known him; and not believing that on the third day true flesh could rise from the tomb, they thought they were seeing a spirit. This error of the Apostles is the sect of the Manicheans.

**2.** But when these things are objected to them, they are accustomed to answer thus: What evil do we believe, in believing that Christ God was a spirit? We believe he was a spirit, we do not believe he was flesh: spirit is better than flesh. What is better, we believe; what is worse, we are unwilling to believe. What evil do we do? If there is nothing evil in this saying, let Jesus leave his disciples in this error. What evil did they believe? The disciples too believed Christ to be a spirit; for they thought he was not himself, but a spirit. Let the Lord leave it to them, let him confirm in them what they rightly believed; finding them in falsehood, let him not teach them the truth. Hear what evil it is, to believe thus. Hear the Lord. Do you think you are in peril from a slight illness? Hear the physician's verdict. What the disciples thought, that they were seeing a spirit, this you too think, O Manichean. The physician came then to the disciples: he found them believing what you now believe. If he left them uncured, then go astray in security; but if he deigned to heal them, what pleasure do you take in being sick? Hear the Lord: *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts rise up in your heart?*[^2] What sort of thoughts, surely, but false, sickly, ruinous ones? For Christ would lose the fruit of his passion, if there were no truth of the resurrection. *Why are you troubled, and why have thoughts risen up in your heart?* As though a good farmer were to say: What I planted there, I will find; not the thorns, which I did not plant. Into your heart faith descended, for it is from above; but these thoughts did not descend from above, but sprang up in the heart itself like evil weeds. But he does not abandon it; he uproots the ill-born weeds, cleanses the field, sows the good. For when he says, *Why are you troubled?* it is because they were troubled, not set in order. *Why do thoughts rise up in your heart? See my hands and my feet.* If it is too little to see, *handle*; if you do not believe your eyes, believe your hands. *Handle, and see, that a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see that I have*[^3].

**3.** *And while they still were trembling for joy*[^4]. They both rejoiced and doubted; they both saw and touched, and scarcely believed. Great is the gracious favor of grace upon us; we neither saw nor touched, and we believed. *But while they were still trembling for joy, he said: Have you anything here to eat?*[^5] At least then you believe that I am alive, if I dine with you. *They offered him* what they had, *a piece of broiled fish*[^6]. The broiled fish is the martyrdoms, faith tested by fire. Why, then, a piece? Because, he says, if *I hand over my body to be burned, but do not have love, it profits me nothing*[^7]. Make, then, the whole body, as it were, of martyrs; some suffer from love, others suffer from boasting. Take away the part of boasting, take the part of love. This is the food of Christ; give Christ his own part; Christ loves the martyrs who have suffered from love. He ate, and it was he himself, the very one who was seen and hung; the very one who was seen, he was touched, food was offered to him, before the eyes of the disciples he took and ate; and all these things were too little. Pardon me, Apostles, who after these things are still hardened. But their correction is our building up; for see what the Lord went on to do, how he removed all the windings. He was seen, he was touched, he ate: it was certainly he himself.

**4.** Yet, lest in any way he should seem to have deceived the human senses, he sent them to the Scriptures. Let the pagans say what they will: He was a magician, he could show himself. Could a magician, before he was born, prophesy of himself from the Scriptures? See, what you see was provided beforehand, and what you discern was foretold beforehand. *Hear, O daughter, and see*[^8]. O holy Church, hear and see: hear the things foretold, see the things fulfilled. It was the Head that persuaded, the Lord Christ; it was the Head of the Church that persuaded that he was alive, true, whole, certain, and led to faith those who believed. What, then, did he say of the Scriptures? *Do you not know that all things must be fulfilled which are written in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms concerning me? Then he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures. And he said to them: Thus it is written, and thus it behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day*[^9]. Behold, the deed, as it was written; behold, fulfilled, what was foretold; behold, displayed, what was read. Hear the words, discern the deeds; the truth is full, the faith is certain; let heretical faithlessness now at last perish. Behold, thus it is written: *Thus it behooved*—what? *Christ to suffer,* it was foretold. *To rise from the dead on the third day,* it was foretold. The Jews had read these things: they read, and did not see; and so that there might be something for others to believe, they struck against the stone lying in their path. *For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^10]; *if they had never crucified the Lord of glory,* the gentiles would not have believed in him as born and as having suffered. Therefore, that these disciples might be set apart from the Jews, whose heart was closed so that they did not understand the Scriptures, the grace of the Lord made the separation. O Apostle, O Peter, O Matthew, O Thomas, O the rest, *for who distinguishes you?*[^11] Perhaps you say: My faith. I believe it; but if he had not given it, you would not have it. Your faith distinguishes you. *But what do you have that you have not received? And if you have received it, why do you boast as if you had not received it?*[^12] Behold grace, behold he rises, behold he shows himself to the eyes of the Apostles, he who did not deign to show himself to the eyes of the Jews. Behold, he offers himself to be seen with the eyes, he offers himself to be handled with the hands. That is too little: he reads, he calls the Scriptures to mind. And this too is too little: he opens the understanding, that you may understand what you read.

**5.** He persuaded that he is the Head. What of us? What of the body? The Head is Christ, the body the Church; the Apostles saw the Head, but they did not see the future Church. Attend: they saw the Head, they touched the Head, they embraced the Head, they conversed with the Head; the future Church they did not see. We, then, who . . . In a way, in those marriage tablets he ought to have named and expressed both the bridegroom and the bride. But because he has now shown the bridegroom, and kept silent about the bride, the marriage is but half. Let the heavenly vows be fulfilled: the bridegroom has appeared, let the bride also appear; he present, she future; he in the resurrection, she in the preaching; let him be seen, let her be believed. How was he seen? *See that a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see that I have*[^13]. Then he opened their understanding. *It behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise on the third day.* Now we see the Lord, now we know the Lord, we touch, we hear, we believe; what of the Church? *That penance and the forgiveness of sins should be preached in his name.* Where? How far? Let no strange woman come forth here out of a corner and put herself in place of yours. Where? How far? *Through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^14]. Behold, you have heard the Church designated. When the disciples heard these things, they did not see the Church throughout all nations; they saw one thing, they believed another; they saw the Head, of the body they believed. We see the body; of the Head let us believe.
