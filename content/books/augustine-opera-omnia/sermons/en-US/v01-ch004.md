# Sermon 4: On Esau and Jacob

**1.** We remember that we are debtors of yesterday's reading. But as we owe a sermon, so you also owe a hearing. And that reading indeed sounds fleshly. But whoever has received the Spirit of God is wise spiritually. For the Apostle said, *To be wise according to the flesh is death*[^1]. And for this the Lord promised the Paraclete, the Spirit of truth. Therefore, as he promised and sent him, so that each one who has now received that Spirit should not be a slave to temporal pleasures, but, made master of his body and a servant of the Creator, should direct himself in the way of God's commandments. Let neither his steps falter nor his eyes flutter, but let him advance with the intent of faith, that he may come to that *which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^2]—which is believed before it is seen, so that when it comes, he who has believed may not be confounded. Let him therefore press on, walking in hope, hoping for what he does not yet hold, believing what he does not yet see, loving him to whom he does not yet cleave. And the exercising of the soul in faith, in hope, and in love makes it fit to grasp what is to come.

**2.** When, therefore, Peter was still wise in a fleshly way, troubled at the questioning of a servant girl, he denied the Lord three times. For the physician had foretold to the sick man what was going to happen in him. The sick man did not know the falls of his own sickness, and presumed too much about himself as a sick man, but the true physician saw. For he had said that he would die with the Lord and for the Lord. But he could not yet, because he was weak. But when the Holy Spirit, sent from heaven, came afterward and confirmed those into whom he came, that man, filled with spiritual confidence, now began to be truly ready to die for him whom he had earlier denied. Filled with this confidence, all the martyrs, holding the right faith, not dying or suffering for a false faith, for an empty phantasm, for a vain hope, for an uncertain thing, but for the promise of truth, holding it certain that he who promised is able to perform, despised all things present and burned for things to come, which, when they have become present to them, will not be past.

**3.** Remember, then, you who were present yesterday, the two sons of Isaac, Esau and Jacob; how the younger is preferred to the elder, so that you may belong to Jacob and not love Esau. Now Esau will be whoever wishes to live in a fleshly way, or to hope for fleshly things in the age to come. Whether, then, living in a fleshly way and rejoicing in such things in this time, and hoping for such things from God as the wicked also have, and placing his whole happiness in that very thing in which the unjust also rejoice, or, certainly despising this present life and hoping for such a life in the future, he is fleshly, having a fleshly faith, a fleshly hope, a fleshly love. But faith is spiritual: to believe that your Lord is a protector for a time, so that you may come to that which will not be temporal, and to hope that you will have the life of the angels, not in the corruptions of the flesh, not in pleasures and enticements, not in fornications and drunkenness and the joy of fleshly feasts, not in the pride of possessions of earthly dominion, but only as the angels live.

**4.** Now the angels live in the joy not of the creature, but of the Creator. For the joy of the creature is whatever is seen. The joy of the Creator is what is not seen with the eyes of the body, but with the purified gaze of the mind. *Blessed are the pure in heart*. For what vision are they blessed? *For they shall see God*[^3]. For do not think, brothers, that the angels rejoice because they see earth or heaven or whatever is in them. They do not rejoice because they see heaven and earth, but because they see him who made heaven and earth.

**5.** But he who made heaven and earth is neither heaven nor earth, nor can anything earthly be conceived of him, nor anything heavenly, nor will you be able to conceive anything corporeal or spiritual. God is not this. Do not make for yourself some large and beautiful man. God is not bounded by a human form. He is not contained in a place, not held by space. Do not make for yourself, as it were, a golden God. God is not this. For the gold from which you wish to make a God, God himself made, and made it lowly because it is on the earth. Do not set before yourself that God is something such as you see in heaven, whether moon or sun or stars or whatever shines and gleams in heaven. God is not this. But again, do not for that reason suppose that God is the sun, because the sun is, as it were, a kind of wheel, not an immense expanse of light; nor say to yourself, "Therefore God is of infinite and immense light," so as to stretch out the sun itself, as it were, and make it have no end, neither this way nor that, neither above nor below, and yet set before yourself an immense light as God. This too is not God. God indeed *dwells in inaccessible light*[^4]. But such light is not a wheel, nor can it be known by the eyes of the flesh.

**6.** But if you can see what truth is, what wisdom is, what justice is—as it was said, *Come to him and be enlightened*[^5]—how that is the true light of which John says, *It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^6], how John the Baptist himself was not the true light. For John the Evangelist says, *He was not the light, but came to bear witness to the light*[^7]. Now not only was John the Baptist not the true light, but neither was Paul the true light, nor Peter the true light, nor were any of the Apostles the true light. For the true light is that *which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^8]. But they were lights, because they were enlightened. For the eyes of our flesh too are called lights, and each one swears, "By my lights." What are these lights? If the sun is lacking, if moon or lamp is lacking, surely they remain in darkness. Where are the lights? Let them see before themselves, let them lead the feet, if they are lights. And yet they are lights. Why are they lights? Because they can receive light. For when light has been brought in, your forehead does not perceive the light itself, nor your ear, nor your sense of smell, nor your hand, nor your foot. Only the members in you that are called eyes alone perceive the light brought in. With the light absent they are darkened, but with the light brought in they alone are enlightened, that is, because they alone perceive the light. Your other members too are indeed enlightened, but so that they can be seen, not so that they can see. For the light that rises or that is brought in floods all the members: the eyes that they may see, the rest that they may be seen. So all the saints were enlightened that they might see, and that they might proclaim what they saw. Therefore it was said to them, *You are the light of the world*[^9]. But light, not the true light. Why? Because *it was the true light, which enlightens every man*[^10]. He said *every man*. If he were speaking of this sun, he would not say *every man*, because this sun is not seen by men alone. The beasts also see it, and the tiniest animals. And flies see this sun. But that light which is God no one sees, except those of whom it was said, *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^11].

**7.** Try to conceive, brothers, the light of truth, the light of wisdom, how it is everywhere present to all. Try to conceive the light of justice: for it is present to everyone who thinks. For what is it that he thinks? Whoever wishes to live unjustly sins, abandons justice. Is it diminished? He has turned toward justice. What? Is it increased? He abandons it, leaves it whole. He turns toward it, finds it whole. What, then, is the light of justice? Does it rise from the East and go to the West? Or is there some other place from which it rises or to which it comes? Is it not everywhere at hand? A man certainly who is in the West, if he wishes to live justly, that is, according to justice—does that which he may gaze upon and see fail him, according to that very justice? Again, one placed in the East, if he wishes to live justly, that is, according to the same justice—does it fail him? For justice is there; it is at hand to one living justly. According to its rule even those see how they may live justly. As the just by living well see it, so the unjust by living badly do not see it. For the one lives justly, since he sees it, and that one sees it itself in order to direct his acts according to it, because unless he directs his acts according to the rule of justice, he is dashed into the error of iniquity. Therefore, because justice could be at hand also for this man standing there, it is in no place and is everywhere. So justice, so wisdom, so truth, so chastity. Try, then, to see such a light. But you cannot. The gaze of the mind flutters; let it be purified that it may see. And that it may be purified and may see, let it believe, that it may merit to be purified. What, then, you cannot see, defer, that we may be cured and may be able to see.

**8.** Yet do not conceive in the age to come anything such as you now see. For if you conceive something such, and love something such, you wish to go out of the world together with the world itself, you wish to take the world with you. These things will not be there. There will be a certain light there, from which this something—I know not what—distills, which we now understand and rejoice in. But if we have a blessing *from the dew of heaven*, we have *abundance from the fertility of the earth*[^12]: for so was Jacob blessed. Let us belong to him and not live in a fleshly way. Because each one begins by living in a fleshly way, Esau was therefore called the elder. Two Testaments are spoken of in the Law, one the Old, the other the New. The Old had temporal promises, but spiritual significations. Let your Charity attend. If a "land of promise" was promised to the Jews, the land of promise signifies something spiritually. If a city of peace, Jerusalem, was promised to the Jews, the name of the city Jerusalem signifies something spiritually. If circumcision of the flesh was given to the Jews, it signifies some spiritual circumcision. If the Jews were commanded to observe one sabbath day out of seven, it signifies a spiritual rest, which has no evening. For in those very seven days, in Genesis, on all the days it is said, *Evening was made*[^13]; on the seventh day it is not said, "Evening was made." On the seventh day, which has no evening, there is signified to us an everlasting rest, where there is no setting. If fleshly sacrifices were given to the Jews, through the victims of animals they all signify spiritual sacrifices. Therefore those who so understood as though something present were given to them as a great thing, and sought no future thing, and were not able to understand spiritually the things that were done in a fleshly way, belong to the elder son, belong to the Old Testament.

**9.** For the Old Testament is a promise figured. The New Testament is a promise understood spiritually. For since the Jerusalem that was on earth pertains to the Old Testament, it has an image to the Jerusalem that is in heaven, and pertains to the New Testament. Fleshly circumcision pertains to the Old Testament; circumcision of the heart pertains to the New Testament. According to the Old Testament the people is freed from Egypt; according to the New Testament the people is freed from the devil. The Egyptian persecutors and Pharaoh pursue the Jews going out of Egypt; their own sins and the devil, the prince of sins, pursue the Christian people. But just as the Egyptians pursue the Jews as far as the sea, so sins pursue Christians as far as baptism. Attend, brothers, and see: the Jews are freed through the sea, the Egyptians are overwhelmed in the sea; Christians are freed into the remission of sins, sins are blotted out through baptism. They go out after the Red Sea and walk through the wilderness; so too Christians after baptism are not yet in the land of promise, but are in hope. Now this world is a wilderness, and truly for the Christian it is a wilderness after baptism, if he understands what he has received. If not only bodily signs are done in him, but if also there is a spiritual effect in the heart, he understands that this world is a wilderness for him, he understands that he lives in a pilgrimage, that he longs for his homeland. But as long as he longs, he is in hope. *For in hope we have been saved. But hope that is seen is not hope. For what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^14]. That patience in the wilderness makes one hope for something. If he already thinks himself in his homeland, he does not arrive at his homeland. If he already thinks himself in his homeland, he remains on the way. But that he may not remain on the way, let him hope for his homeland, let him long for his homeland, lest he stray. For temptations come to meet him. And as in the wilderness temptations come to meet one, so after baptism they come to meet. For just as the Egyptians were not the only enemies of the Jews, who pursued them from Egypt (for they were past enemies, just as each one is pursued by his past life and his past sins with their prince the devil), but there arose also in the wilderness those who would hinder the way, and it was fought against them, and they were conquered: so after baptism, when the Christian has begun to walk the way of his heart in the hope of God's promises, let him not stray. For temptations come to meet him, suggesting something else (the delights of this world, some other life), to twist each one off the way and lead him from his purpose. But if you overcome these desires, these suggestions, the enemies on the way are conquered, and the people is brought through to its homeland.

**10.** Hear the Apostle, that these were figures of us: *For I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, that all our fathers were under the cloud*[^15]. If they were under the cloud, they were under the gloom. What is it, they were under the gloom? Because they did not understand spiritually the things that were done with them in a fleshly way. *And all passed through the sea, and all were baptized in Moses, and all ate the same spiritual food*[^16]. For manna was given to them in the desert, as the sweetness of the Scriptures is given to us, that we may endure in this wilderness of human life. And Christians know what manna they receive, to whom the psalm itself said, *Taste and see how sweet the Lord is*[^17]. *And all,* he says, *ate the same spiritual food*. What is *the same*? Signifying the same thing. *And all drank the same spiritual drink*[^18]. And note how he expounded one thing and was silent about the rest: *For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed them; and the rock was Christ. But these things were figures of us*[^19]. They were displayed to them, but they were figures of us, because to them they were displayed in a fleshly way, but to us they were signified spiritually. Therefore those who held them in a fleshly way belonged to the Old Testament.

**11.** Now see that Isaac had grown old. Whose person did Isaac bear, when he wished to bless his elder son? He had now grown old. Where there is old age, there is the old. For by old age I understand the old, and by the old I understand the Old Testament. This Old Testament, then, because those who were under the cloud did not understand it, is why Isaac's eyes are said to have grown dim. The dimness of Isaac's bodily eyes signifies the dimness of the minds of the Jews. Isaac's old age signifies the oldness of the Old Testament. What then, brothers? He wishes nevertheless to bless even his elder son Esau. The mother loved the younger and the father the elder, as the firstborn. For the justice was equal toward both, but there was greater love toward the firstborn. He wishes to bless the elder, because the Old Testament made its promises to the first people. It speaks its promises only to the Jews. To them it seems to promise, to them it seems to pledge everything. They are called from Egypt, freed from enemies, led through the sea, fed with manna, they receive the Testament, they receive the Law, they receive the promises, they receive the very land of promise. No wonder that he wished to bless the first son. But under the figure of the elder, the younger is blessed. For the mother bears the type of the Church. But understand the Church, brothers, not in those alone who began to be holy after the Lord's coming and birth, but all, however many were holy, pertain to that same Church. For father Abraham does not fail to pertain to us because he existed before Christ was born of the Virgin, and we so long afterward, that is, after Christ's passion, became Christians, since the Apostle says that we are *sons of Abraham*[^20] by imitating Abraham's faith. Therefore are we to be admitted to the Church by imitating him, and are we to exclude him himself from the Church? This Church is signified in Rebecca, the wife of Isaac. This Church was in the holy Prophets too, who understood the Old Testament, because those fleshly promises signified some spiritual something. If spiritual, then the spiritual belong to the younger son, because the fleshly is first, and afterward the spiritual.

**12.** We told your Holiness yesterday that Esau is called the elder son because no one becomes spiritual except from being fleshly. But if he perseveres in the prudence of the flesh, he will always be Esau. But if he becomes spiritual, he will be the younger son. Yet that younger one will be the greater: for the one precedes in time, the other in virtue. For when Jacob had cooked lentils, Esau desired to receive them before he came to that blessing. And Jacob said to him, *Give me your birthright, and I will give you the lentils I have cooked*[^21]. He sold his birthright to the younger. The one took away temporal pleasure, the other took away everlasting dignity. Therefore those who serve temporal pleasures in the Church eat lentils. Which indeed Jacob cooked, but Jacob did not eat. For idols had flourished more in Egypt. Lentils are the food of Egypt. By lentils all the errors of the gentiles are signified. Because, then, the Church—more eminent and more manifest—was signified as going to come in the younger son from the gentiles, Jacob is said to have cooked the lentils, and Esau to have eaten them. For the gentiles abandoned the idols they had worshiped. But the Jews served idols. For, converted in heart to Egypt, they were led through the wilderness. And when their enemies had been slain in the sea, and their foes overwhelmed by the waves, they desired to make an idol, because they did not see Moses. And they did not understand that God was present with them, but, having their hope in the presence of a man, when they did not see the man with their eyes, they began to think that God was not there, although he alone bestowed such great things through Moses. They sought a man with the eyes of the flesh, because they did not have the eyes of the heart by which to see God in Moses. Therefore they lost their primacy, because, converted in heart to Egypt, they ate lentils. Take note. There is a Christian people. But within that Christian people those hold the primacy who belong to Jacob. But those who live in a fleshly way, believe in a fleshly way, hope in a fleshly way, love in a fleshly way, still belong to the Old Testament, not yet to the New. They are still in the lot of Esau, not yet in the blessing of Jacob.

**13.** Let your Holiness attend. Old Isaac, then, with dim eyes wished to bless his elder son, because the Old Testament was directed to the Jews. Because that Old Testament was not understood by them, his eyes are therefore called dim. As I said, brothers, he speaks to the elder, the blessing comes to the younger. For this mother, who is understood in all the saints, that is, the Church, who understood the prophecy, herself gives counsel to the younger son, and says to him, *I myself heard your father saying to your brother: Go, and bring me game, that I may eat and that my soul may bless you before I die. Now therefore, son, hear me*[^22]. And she gave him counsel to go and bring two kids from the nearby flock, and that the mother should prepare them as his father delighted in, and that he should eat and bless his younger son in the absence of the elder. But he was afraid and said, *My brother is hairy, but I am smooth, lest my father touch me and feel me, and understand that I am Jacob, and I shall gain not a blessing but a curse*[^23]. But she said, *Go, son, hear me; let your curse be upon me*[^24]. He went on, brought two kids. They were prepared, he set them before his father. And as she had foretold, since the father did not recognize him by his voice, he touched him, found the hairs, because the mother had wrapped his arms in the skins of the kids. He believed it was the elder, and blessed him. In the blessing he intended the elder, and the blessing came to the younger. What, then, is the meaning that under the appearance of the elder the younger is blessed, except that under the figures of the Old Testament promised to the people of the Jews, the spiritual blessing came to the people of the Christians? Attend, brothers. They hear of the land of promise, we also hear of it. As though to the Jews Scripture seems to speak of the land of promise, and to us comes the understanding of the land of promise, who say to God, *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^25]. But that we should say this, the mother taught us, that is, the Church herself in the holy Prophets teaches us how we should understand spiritually the very fleshly promises.

**14.** But the blessing could not come to us unless, already cleansed from sins by the new birth of regeneration, we bear the sins of others through forbearance. For the mother bore both sons. Attend, brothers. She bore one hairy, the other smooth. The hairs signify sins, the smoothness gentleness, that is, cleanness from sins. Two sons are blessed, because the Church blesses two kinds. As Rebecca bore two, so two are begotten in the womb of the Church, one hairy, the other smooth, whose difference we have expounded. For there are people who even after baptism do not wish to put away their sins, and wish to do the things they did before. For example: if they were committing frauds, they wish to defraud again; if they swore falsely, they still wish to perjure themselves; if they circumvented the innocent with deceits, they still wish to circumvent; if they were plotting murders, they plot the same; if they fornicated, if they got drunk, they do those very things no less. Behold Esau born hairy. What does Jacob do? It is said to him by his mother, "Go, let your father bless you." And he says, "I am afraid, I will not approach." For there are people in the Church who fear to be mingled with sinners, lest, as it were, through fellowship with sinners they be stained in the unity, and through heresies and schisms perish.

**15.** What, then, is said to hairy Esau, who did not conduct himself well in the house? For this too is said of them. *He was a wild hunter, but Jacob conducted himself without guile in the house*[^26]. And therefore the mother loved him, whose sweet conduct she felt. This is the Jacob who was afterward called Israel, when he wrestled with the angel: and this in a great mystery. Having been blessed, he was called Israel, and this because he was without guile. Attend, my brothers, and see how he was without guile. When the Lord saw Nathanael, because he knew what kind of man he was, he said, *Behold, truly an Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^27]. If, then, this man is an Israelite because there is no guile in him, in Israel himself surely there was no guile. What, then, is the meaning of what is said, *Your brother came with guile, and took away the blessing*[^28]? Scripture commends him as conducting himself without guile in the house. The Lord too bears witness that he was without guile, when he says of Nathanael, *Behold, truly an Israelite, in whom there is no guile*. What, then, is the meaning, when it is said, *He approached with guile, and took away the blessing?*

**16.** Meanwhile let us attend to what guile signifies, and let us see what Jacob ought to do. He bears the sins of others, and bears them with forbearance, though they are the sins of others. For this is what it means to have the goatskins, that is, patiently to bear the sins of others, not to cling to his own. So all who, for the unity of the Church, bear with the sins of others imitate Jacob. For Jacob himself too is in Christ, since Christ is in the seed of Abraham. For it was said, *In your seed shall all nations be blessed*[^29]. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, who committed no sin, bore the sins of others. And does he to whom sins are forgiven disdain to bear the sins of others? If, then, Jacob crosses over to Christ, he bears the sins of others, that is, the goatskins. And what is guile?

**17.** For he comes late, and brings what his father had commanded, and finds his brother blessed in his stead, and is not blessed with another blessing. Because those two men were two peoples. One blessing signifies the unity of the Church. But they are two peoples, which is also Jacob. Yet in another way figured are the two peoples pertaining to Jacob. For our Lord Jesus Christ, who had come to the Jews and the gentiles, was rejected by the Jews, who belonged to the elder son. Yet he chose certain ones who belonged to the younger son, who had begun spiritually to desire and understand the Lord's promises, not receiving in a fleshly way the other land they desired, but spiritually desiring that city where no one is born in a fleshly way; for there no one dies in a fleshly way, no one in a spiritual way.

**18.** When, then, they began to desire this, they began to belong to Jacob, those who believed in Christ, and there came to be a flock of the Lord in Judea itself. But what does the Lord say of that flock? *I have other sheep, which are not of this fold; I will go, I will bring them, and there shall be one flock and one shepherd*[^30]. What other sheep does the Lord Jesus Christ have, except from the gentiles? The sheep from the gentiles were joined to the Jewish sheep. For from the Jews were the Apostles. From there too were the five hundred brothers who saw the Lord after the resurrection. From there was Nathanael himself, to whom the Lord bore witness that there was no guile in him. From there were the one hundred and twenty who, when they were in the house, the Holy Spirit came to flood them, whom he had promised to the disciples. From there were the many thousands of people whom we read in the Acts of the Apostles were baptized in the name of Christ, even those who had crucified Christ. Therefore from there were the sheep, and many sheep. But not alone. The Lord had others from the gentiles. These two peoples, as though coming from different directions, are signified also by two walls. For the Church of the Jews came from circumcision. The Church of the gentiles came from uncircumcision. Coming from different directions, they were joined together in the Lord. Therefore the Lord is called the cornerstone. For the psalm says, *The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner*[^31]. And the Apostle says, *Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone*[^32]. Where there is a corner, two walls are joined; in a corner two walls do not come together unless from different directions; for if they come from one side, they do not make a corner. The two kids, then, are the two peoples, they are the two folds, they are the two walls, they are the two blind men who sat by the way, they are the two boats into which the fish were lifted. In many places of the Scriptures two peoples are understood, but they are one in Jacob.

**19.** Why kids, someone will ask? You know that kids are sinners, for the kids will be on the left, and the lambs on the right. But those who persevere as kids, they will be on the left. For unless they had first been kids, the Lord would not say, *I came not to call the righteous, but sinners*[^33]. For when the Lord was associating with sinners and eating with tax collectors, the Jews—as though lambs, that is, as though righteous, and through pride rather goats—objected to the Lord as a charge, indeed said to his disciples, *Why does your master eat with tax collectors and sinners?*[^34]. Answering, how did the Lord defend himself? *Those who are well,* he said, *have no need of a physician, but those who are sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners*[^35]. He calls the kids, then, but so that they may not persevere as kids. For Jacob slew them and prepared from them a feast for his father, that is, to the spiritual understanding, which was to be understood in that blessing, although it was figured in the elder son. For this the kids were slain, and eaten, and carried over into one body. So sins are slain in sinners, and, slain, they pass over into the one body of the Church, whose figure Peter bore, when it was said to him, *Kill and eat*[^36]. Therefore the one was wild, the other in the house, gentle; the one the elder, the other the younger; the blessings seemed to look toward the one, they came to the other. They looked toward the one, because fleshly promises were promised to the Jews; they came to the other, because they were to be understood spiritually, and to be received by Christians. To the latter the blessing would not have come unless he bore the sins which he himself no longer committed.

**20.** Therefore let your Holiness understand how sins are to be borne. For there are those who seem to themselves to bear sins, and are silent toward sinners. Now that pretense is to be detested. Bear the sinner, not so as to love the sin in him, but so as to pursue the sin for his sake. Love the sinner, not insofar as he is a sinner, but insofar as he is a man. As if you love a sick man, you pursue the fever; for if you spare the fever, you do not love the sick man. Say, then, what is true to your brother, do not be silent. For what else are we doing than telling you what is true? Do not do it deceitfully: with open truth say what is true, but until he is corrected, let him be borne. At different times might have happened the slaying of the kids and the carrying of the skins; yet they signify a matter that can take place at one time. For the just man at once both reproves sinners, which is to slay the kids, and mercifully tolerates their sins, which is to bear the skins. As far as in him lay, he slew the kids, he slew the sinners. But he bore the sins of others, and bore them with forbearance. He deserved to be blessed, because *love bears all things*[^37]. That love was in the mother, and the mother bore the figure of that love. Which figure was of all the saints, she bore the figure of love, because there are no saints except those who have love. For what will it profit, *if I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love? I have become sounding brass or a clanging cymbal. And if I have all faith, so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing. And if I know all mysteries, and all prophecy, and hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^38]. What kind of thing, then, is love, which alone profits much, without which the rest profit nothing! Therefore love itself gives the counsel, and the son of love obeys.

**21.** What counsel does it give? That he take the goatskins and approach his father. The father seeks the elder, and blesses the younger. The Old Testament regards the Jews through the letter, and through its spiritual understanding Christians are blessed in it. Let your Holiness attend to a great mystery, a great hidden meaning. Isaac says, *Your brother came with guile*[^39], of a man without guile. Without doubt Isaac, as he was of a prophetic spirit, knew what was being done, and he himself was acting figuratively. He sets everything in the great depth of mysteries. For if he did not know what he was doing, he would be angry at the son deceiving him. The elder comes and says, *Behold, father, eat; as you commanded me, I have done*[^40]. He says, *Who are you?* He answered, *I am your elder son Esau. And who is he,* he said, *from whom I have already eaten, and have blessed him, and he shall be blessed?*[^41] He seemed to be angry. Esau awaited from his mouth some curse upon his brother. While Esau awaits the curse, Isaac confirms the blessing. O to be angry, O to be indignant! But he knew the mystery. And the dimness of his bodily eyes signified the dimness of the mind of the Jews. But the eyes of his heart saw the depth of the mysteries.

**22.** *And your brother came,* he says, *with guile, and took away the blessing*[^42]. We were saying that we should see what *with guile* is. This guile is not guile. How is guile not guile? How is a rock not a rock? How is something called sea and is not sea, because it signifies something else? So something is called earth and is not earth, because it signifies something else. So something is called a rock and is not a rock, because it signifies something else. So something is called a mountain and is not a mountain. So the Lord Jesus Christ is called a lion of the tribe of Judah and the Lord Jesus Christ is not a lion. So he is called a lamb and is not a lamb. So he is called a sheep and is not a sheep. So he is called a calf, and it is something else. So guile is spoken of and is not guile. Why, then, is it called guile? Let us seek. Why all those things were said, let us seek. Why was he called a lion? Because of his strength. Why was he called a rock? Because of his firmness. Why was he called a lamb? Because of his innocence. Why was he called a calf? Because of the victim. Why was he called a mountain? Because of his greatness. Why manna? Because of its sweetness. Why, then, guile? Let us now see what guile is, and we find why it was called guile. For we know what a rock is; and yet a man who is absurd and hard is called a rock, and a man who is robust and immovable is called a rock. And from it you have drawn firmness for praise, and hardness for reproach. We know firmness in a rock, and we have taken Christ as a rock: *And the rock was Christ*[^43]. We know strength in a lion, and yet the devil too is named a lion. In guile, what do we know, so that we may take guile too as a figure, just as a mountain is taken, just as a lion is taken, just as a lamb is taken, just as a rock is taken, and the rest?

**23.** What, then, is guile? Guile is when one thing is done and another pretended. So when one thing is in the intention, another in the deeds, it is called guile. Therefore guile in its proper sense is reprehensible, as a rock in its proper sense. Whoever says that the rock was Christ in the proper sense blasphemes, as whoever says that the calf was Christ in the proper sense blasphemes. A calf in its proper sense is an animal, in figure a victim. In its proper sense a stone is hardened earth, in figure firmness. Guile in its proper sense is fraud, in figure the figure itself. For every figurative and allegorical reading or speech seems to sound one thing in a fleshly way, to intimate another spiritually. This figure, then, he called by the name of guile. What, then, is, *He came with guile, and took away your blessing?*[^44] Because what was being done was figurative, therefore it was said, *He came with guile*. For he would not confirm the blessing to a deceitful man, to whom a just curse was due. Therefore that was not true guile, especially because he did not lie in saying, *I am your elder son Esau*[^45]. For he had already made a pact with his brother and had sold his birthright. He said he had this from his father, which he had bought from his brother; what the one had lost had passed over to the other. For the honor of the firstborn had not been shut out of Isaac's house. The honor of the firstborn was there. It was not in him who had sold it. Where was it, if not in the younger? Therefore, knowing this in the mystery, Isaac confirmed the blessing and says to his son, *What shall I do for you?* And he, *Bless me also, father: for you have not one blessing only*[^46]. But Isaac knew of one.

**24.** Why one? The Spirit will be present that I may declare it and you may understand. Let us see the blessings themselves, what kind of blessing Jacob received, and what kind of blessing Esau received. Isaac himself to Jacob: *Are you my son Esau? He answered: I am*[^47]. And he said, *Bring it to me, and I will eat of your game, son, and my soul shall bless you before I die; but bring your kiss near to me*[^48]. He did not kiss the other. From peace his blessing began. Why did he confirm peace with a kiss? Because he too for the sake of peace was bearing the sins of others. *And he approached, and kissed him. And he smelled the smell of his garments*[^49]. For he had his brother's robe. That is, the dignity of the firstborn which the other had lost, this man had. In him it now smelled good, which the other had wickedly lost. *He smelled the smell of his garments, and blessed him, and said: Behold the smell of my son, like the smell of a full field which the Lord has blessed*[^50]. He received the smell of the garment, and he spoke of the smell of a field. Understand Christ in the inner mystery, and understand the garment of Christ to be the Church.

**25.** Let your Holiness understand. For one thing is signified in many ways. That is, the Church, which those two kids signify, this garment signifies, because one thing is signified in many ways, which is none of these in plain reality, all of them in figure. A lamb cannot be a lion; a lion cannot be a lamb. But our Lord Jesus Christ could be both a lion and a lamb. But because he is neither a lion nor a lamb in plain reality, he is both a lion and a lamb in figure. So kids cannot be a garment, and a garment cannot be kids. But the Church, because it is neither kids nor a garment in plain reality, is both kids and a garment in figure. And whatever else can be said.

**26.** He smelled *his garments, and said: Behold the smell of my son, like the smell of a full field which the Lord has blessed*[^51]. This field is the Church. Let us prove that the field is the Church. Hear the Apostle saying to the faithful, *You are God's tillage, God's building*[^52]. Not only is the Church a field, but God is also the farmer. Hear the Lord himself: *I am the vine, you the branches, and my Father is the farmer*[^53]. In this field, then, the laborer toiling and hoping for an eternal reward, the Apostle himself claims nothing for himself except as much as befits a laborer. *I,* he says, *planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. So neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the growth*[^54]. How he preserved humility, that he might belong to Jacob, that he might belong to that very field, that is, the Church, lest he lose the robe whose smell was like the smell of a full field, and pass over to the pride of Esau, understanding in a fleshly way and full of conceit! The field, then, smells from the garment of the son. But that field is not anything of itself. Therefore he added, *which the Lord has blessed*. *And the Lord shall give you of the dew of heaven from above, and of the richness of the earth, and abundance of grain and wine. And nations shall serve you, and you shall be lord of your brother, and the sons of your father shall worship you. Whoever curses you shall be cursed, and whoever blesses you shall be blessed*[^55]. This is the blessing of Jacob. If Esau too were not blessed, there would be no question. He too is blessed, but not with the same blessing, nor yet wholly different from it.

**27.** Let us hear, then, how Esau is blessed, and let us see what difference there is between the spiritual sons of the Church and the fleshly; between those who bear the sins of others, and those who carry their own; between those who always live spiritually, and those who always delight in fleshly joys. Answering, Isaac said to Esau, *Who then is it that hunted game for me, and brought it to me? Let him be blessed. And it happened, when Esau heard the words of his father Isaac, he cried out with a loud voice and said: Bless me also, father. And he said to him: Your brother came with guile, and received your blessing*[^56].

**28.** And Esau said, *Rightly is his name called Jacob*[^57]. For Jacob means "supplanting." And is that supplanting itself empty, since it is taken in figure as guile is? For there was not now such malice in the brother that he should wish to supplant his brother. For he was called the supplanter then, when at his birth he held his brother's foot with his hand: then he was called the supplanter. But the supplanting of the fleshly is the life of the spiritual. For all the fleshly, when in the Church they envy the spiritual, are supplanted, and become worse. Hear the Apostle saying this very thing, especially because there he mentioned the smell, which here Isaac expressed, saying, *Behold the smell of my son, like the smell of a full field which the Lord has blessed*[^58]. The Apostle says, then, *We are the good odor of Christ in every place*, and he says, *to some indeed an odor of life unto life, to others an odor of death unto death. And who is sufficient for these things?*[^59]—that is, to understand how we are an odor of death unto the death of men, through no fault of ours. For they walk their spiritual ways, they know nothing else than to live well. And those who envy those living innocently commit grave sins, for which God punishes them. And that odor becomes for them unto death, which is for others unto life. For the Lord himself was first made a good odor unto life for those who believed, and a bad odor unto death for those who persecuted. For because many had believed in him, the Jews envied, and committed so great a crime by killing the innocent one, the Holy of holies. But if they had not done this, the good odor of Christ would not have availed for them unto death. Therefore Esau was supplanted in his father's blessing.

**29.** Now Isaac answered and said to him, *I have made him your lord*[^60]. For Esau could receive nothing else than what was said: *And all his brothers shall serve him; but what shall I do for you, son? And Esau said to his father: Bless me nevertheless. When Isaac was strangled*: that is, when he was constrained. A great matter, a great mystery: would that we might grasp it! Constrained, he blesses, and yet he blesses. And what he blesses is true, but nevertheless he blesses constrained. What this is, attend. Let us see the blessing itself, and let us understand what it is to bless constrained.

**30.** Now Isaac answered—the father indeed did not kiss Esau—and said to him, *Behold, from the fertility of the earth shall be your dwelling, and from the dew of heaven above*[^61]. He said this also to the other, *From the richness of the earth, and from the dew of heaven*: this, then, is common to Jacob and Esau. What is proper to Jacob? *Nations shall serve you*. What is proper to Jacob? *All your brothers shall serve you, and whoever blesses you shall be blessed, and whoever curses you shall be cursed*. This man too has something—I know not what—proper to himself, which was not said to Jacob: *And you shall live by your sword, and you shall serve your brother*[^62]. But lest it should take away free choice—as we said yesterday too on this point—he added, *But it shall be, when you shall lay aside and loose the yoke from your neck*[^63]. What is, *But it shall be, when you shall lay aside and loose the yoke from your neck*? You are free, if you will, to be converted; not as though you will be two, but one Jacob. For all who are converted from Esau belong to Jacob. Likeness makes one, unlikeness makes difference. What, then? *From the dew of heaven, and from the fertility of the earth*, both have. *Let nations serve you and your brothers and the sons of your father*, Jacob alone has. But *you shall live by your sword*, Esau alone. They have something in common, certain things each one alone.

**31.** There are wicked people in the Church belonging to Esau, because they too are children of Rebecca, children of the mother Church, born of her womb, and hairy in persevering in fleshly sins, yet born of her womb. They have, then, from the dew of heaven and from the fertility of the earth: from the dew of heaven all the Scriptures, all the divine word; but from the fertility of the earth, all the visible sacraments: for a visible sacrament pertains to the earth. All these the Church has in common, both the good and the wicked. For they too have and share in the sacraments, and—as the faithful know—of the wheat and the wine. And they have of the dew of heaven, because over all comes down the word of God from heaven. The word of God comes and waters. But who waters, and what he waters, attend. For he waters both these and those, that is, the wicked and the good. But those wicked ones turn the good rain into the root of thorns; but those good ones draw the rain to good fruit. For the Lord rains at once upon the grainfield and upon the thorns: but for the grainfield he rains unto the barn, for the thorns unto the fire; and yet the rain is one. So the word of God rains upon all. Let each one see what kind of root he has; let each one see where he draws the good rain. If he draws it so as to bring forth thorns, is the rain of God to be accused on that account? Before it comes to the root, that rain is sweet. The word of God is sweet, until it comes to an evil heart, and turns the rain of God to its own fraud, turns it to hypocrisy, turns it to the roots of evil desires, to its own perversities and depravities. It indeed begins to bring forth thorns, but from good rain: for it has of the dew of heaven. And since not all the wicked are excluded from the sacraments of God, it has also of the richness of the earth, which those know who have already wished to be partakers of the mysteries of the faithful.

**32.** Although these things pertain to both, yet "all nations" pertains only to the spiritual, because they pertain to the Church that has filled the whole world. Attend, brothers, and discern as much as you can, or as much as the Lord gives you. Every spiritual person sees that the Church throughout the whole world is one, true, catholic. And it claims nothing for itself, and tolerates the sins of people whom it cannot purge from the Lord's threshing floor before that last winnower comes, who cannot be deceived, to purge his threshing floor and put the wheat into the barn, but give the chaff to be burned. For he it is who is to exclude the chaff and separate it from the wheat, and prepare the barn for the wheat, the fire for the chaff. And so, because he knows, he tolerates the sinners to be separated at the end. Throughout all nations sinners and all the fleshly are mixed with the spiritual, and serve them. But the spiritual do not serve, because they profit from them when they themselves fail. Attend, my brothers. I will speak if I can, and not be afraid. I will not be silent. For I am pressed to speak. And if perhaps some are angry with me, let them grant pardon. For I am afraid, as I said. Let them grant pardon to my fear. Christ feared no one. But we, fearing Christ, do not spare, lest, while we are unwilling to grieve such people, he not spare us. What I wish to say, deign to hear, and attend most intently. Both Jacob and Esau received of the dew of heaven and of the richness of the earth. Both have that which we have already said, which we know, which you know. But that nations should serve him, only Jacob received, because the fleshly in the Church throughout the whole world do not serve any but the spiritual. Why? Because the spiritual profit from them. For this reason fleshly people are called servants. And even if they do what they do not wish, the evil conduct of the fleshly nevertheless profits the spiritual, because they both profit from that conduct, and are crowned through their forbearance.

**33.** Let your Holiness attend to what we say. But to Esau the nations were not given, because all the fleshly who are in the Church are either divided off or are easily inclined to division. Behold, the party of Donatus arose from there, is from there, from those very fleshly people thinking in a fleshly way. They were fleshly, but because they sought their own honor, or lost their patience, they found an entrance, and were divided off. They loved their own honor, attributed much to it, swelled with the conceit of pride, did not have forbearance, that is, did not have love. For it is written, *Love bears all things, endures all things. It is not jealous, it is not puffed up, it does not act wrongly*[^64]. And so, whatever else they had, because they did not have that through which whatever is had is profitable, they divided themselves off. And however many heresies or cuttings-off and schisms have arisen, they have arisen through these fleshly people. For either they thought in a fleshly way, and made for themselves images of their phantasms, and erred, and the Catholic faith reproved them; and when they were rebuked, they were thrust out by their own weight. Or, finding for themselves quarrels and feuds with people, they divided themselves off. Who, then, divided themselves off, but those to whom belongs that sword of which it was said, *You shall live by your sword*[^65]? Not that the sword is not taken in a good sense. For just as those things above were said, that is, as that stone is taken both for firmness on account of Christ, and for reproach on account of the fool; as Christ is called a lion for one reason, and the devil is called a lion for another: so the sword is taken sometimes in a good sense, sometimes in a bad. But here, not without cause, it was not given to Jacob but given to Esau, unless something of mystery is signified in the bad sense. As that servitude too is not foreign to the mystery; that is, what was said, *You shall serve your brother*[^66], for this too was said in a great mystery.

**34.** Therefore, brothers, those who divide themselves off, they have the sword of division, and they die by their sword, and they live by their sword. But because the Lord had spoken the truth, *Whoever strikes with the sword shall die by the sword*[^67], look at those, my brothers, who have cut themselves off from unity, in which they were cut off in vain. You know how many parts the party of Donatus has made, and I do not think it is hidden from your Holiness that whoever strikes with the sword perishes by the sword. It was said to him, *You shall live by your sword*[^68]. So too those who have not departed from the Church, yet are such as they would be if they were outside. For those who love their own honors in the Church are altogether such as those who love their worldly conveniences in the Church. And they too are chaff. But the wind is lacking, and therefore they do not fly off the threshing floor. This is what I say briefly: temptation is lacking, for they would fly off the threshing floor. Then, when the Church has done anything against them, how easily they cut themselves off! How easily they gather a congregation outside, and yet are unwilling to let go of their offices! How they are willing to die for that very office! How they wish to hold the people under them, and not release the people to the unity of Christ! How they wish to make them their own sheep, whom they did not buy with their own blood, and therefore they hold them cheap, because they did not buy them! What need is there to dispute on this longer? Look at them throughout the universal Church, see such people, both those who are within, and those who, finding an occasion, have flown off the threshing floor and wish to drag the grains with them. But the true and full grains tolerate the chaff and remain on the threshing floor until the end, until the last winnower comes: as Jacob with the goatskins tolerated the sins of others, and deserved to receive the father's blessing.

**35.** But why did Isaac bless when *importuned*[^69]? For now of necessity, now constrained, his father said to him, *Behold, from the richness of the earth shall be your dwelling, and from the dew of heaven*[^70]. And lest you think yourself good on that account: *You shall live by your sword, and you shall serve your brother*[^71]. But lest you despair of yourself, because you can correct yourself: *But it shall be, when you shall lay aside and loose the yoke from your neck*[^72]. Behold, of the richness of the earth and of the dew of heaven he shall receive. But Isaac, importuned, throws the blessing to him, does not give it. Does it not happen even now in the Church to wicked people who wish to disturb the Church that they are tolerated for the necessity of its very peace, that they are admitted, that they have the sacraments in common? And sometimes it is known that they are wicked, and perhaps they cannot be convicted. That they may be amended and degraded, that they may be excluded, that they may be excommunicated, they cannot be convicted. If someone presses the matter, sometimes it comes to ruptures of the Church. The governor of the Church is constrained, as it were, to say: "Behold, you shall have of the richness of the earth and of the dew of heaven; use the sacraments; you eat judgment for yourself, you drink judgment for yourself: *For whoever eats and drinks unworthily eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^73]. You know that you are admitted out of the necessity of the Church's peace; you have nothing in your heart but disturbances and divisions. Therefore you shall live by your sword. For in that which you receive of the dew of heaven and of the richness of the earth, in that you will not live. For this does not delight you, nor is the Lord sweet to you." For if this delighted you, and the Lord were sweet to you, you would imitate the humility of the Lord, not the pride of the devil. Although, then, he may receive the mystery of the Lord's humility, of the dew of heaven and of the richness of the earth, he nevertheless does not lay aside the pride of the devil—to whom I will do nothing—who always rejoices in seditions and dissensions. Although you have this communion of the dew of heaven and of the richness of the earth, yet you live by your sword, and over seditions and dissensions you either rejoice or are terrified. Change yourself, then, and take the yoke from your neck.

**36.** These things have been said to you, brothers, few perhaps for the greatness of the mysteries, yet many for the time and for our strength and for yours. And if perhaps this question has not been more thoroughly discussed, there are nevertheless in part great mysteries which, if treated later, are found out. Grant pardon both to the narrowness of the time and to our strength and to your capacity. Do you wish to grasp more? Grow. Do you wish to grow? Live well. For whoever does not wish to live well does not wish to grow. May the holy Lord our God have set out these feasts for you on the birthday of his martyr Vincent. The name is of victory. Love, then, that you may conquer. For persecution is not lacking. The devil is a persecutor; the occasion of a crown is never wanting. Only let the soldier of Christ understand the battle and know whom he conquers. Is it that, because no manifest enemy of the body presses you, no hidden persecutor presses you with the enticements of the flesh? How many evils he suggests, how many through desire, how many through fear! With what enticements he persuades you to go to soothsayers, to astrologers, when your head aches. Those who abandon God and go to diabolical amulets have been conquered by the devil. On the contrary, if it is suggested to someone that he might perhaps work remedies of the devil for the body, by which another is said to have been healed—because, the devil having received his sacrifice from him, he stops persecuting the body since he has obtained the heart—therefore, whoever has these wicked remedies suggested to him and says, "I would rather die than use such remedies; if God wills, he scourges and frees me; if he knows it is necessary, let him free me; but if he knows that I must go out of this life, whether I am saddened or glad, I will follow the will of the Lord; for behold, after a little time, with what face am I to go out to the Lord; for the remedies of the devil do not bestow on me what God bestows, eternal life; why do I damn my soul, and buy a few days for my body?"—whoever says this, and does not go nor apply his heart to work the evil remedies, certainly conquers. I have said one thing by way of example. You see surely now how many things the devil suggests. But where is he? You see someone certainly languishing, you see him panting in his bed, you see him scarcely moving his limbs, scarcely moving his tongue: this weary man conquers the devil. Many have been crowned in the amphitheater fighting against beasts. Many in their beds are crowned, conquering the devil. They seem unable to move, and within in the heart they have such great strength, they wage such a battle! But where the battle is hidden, there the victory is hidden.

**37.** Why have we said these things, brothers? That when you celebrate the birthdays of the martyrs, you may imitate the martyrs, and not think that the occasions of a crown can be lacking to you because such persecutions are now lacking. For daily persecutions from the devil are not lacking even now, whether through suggestion or through some afflictions of the body. Only know that you have a commander who has already gone ahead into heaven. He has given you a way by which to follow; hold yourself to him. When you have conquered, do not attribute it to yourself through pride, as though you had striven by your own strength. But presume upon him who gave you strength to conquer, because he himself conquered the world. And you will always be crowned and go out from here a martyr, if you overcome all the temptations of the devil.
