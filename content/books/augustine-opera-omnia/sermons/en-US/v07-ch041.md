# Sermon 261: A Sermon Given at Carthage in the Basilica of Faustus on the Fortieth Day, the Ascension of the Lord Jesus Christ

**1.** The resurrection of the Lord is our hope; the ascension of the Lord is our glorification. For today we celebrate the solemnity of the Ascension. If, then, we rightly, if faithfully, if devoutly, if holily, if piously celebrate the ascension of the Lord, let us ascend with him, and let us have our *heart on high.* But ascending let us not be lifted up. For we ought to have our *heart on high*, but *to the Lord.* For to have the heart on high not to the Lord is called pride; but to have the heart on high to the Lord is called a refuge. For to him who ascends we say: *Lord, you have become a refuge for us*[^1]. For he rose, that he might give us hope, because what dies rises; lest by dying we should despair, and think our whole life ended by death. For we were anxious about the soul itself, and he by rising gave us security about the flesh too. He ascended, then, who? He who descended. He descended, to heal you; he ascended, to lift you. You will fall, if you lift yourself; you will remain, if he lifts you. *Heart on high*, then, but *to the Lord*, is a refuge; heart on high, but not to the Lord, is pride. Let us, then, say to him rising: *Since you, Lord, are my hope*; but to him ascending: *You have made the Most High your refuge*[^2]. For how, having the heart on high to him, will we be proud, who for our sake was made humble, lest we should remain proud?

**2.** God Christ, this always: he will never cease this, because he never began. For if through his grace something begins that will never cease; how will he cease, who never began? What is it that begins, and never ceases? Our immortality will have a beginning, it will not have an end. For we do not yet have what, when we have begun to have it, we will not lose. Therefore God always Christ. And what kind of God? You ask what kind? Equal to the Father. Do not, then, seek in eternity quality, but happiness. What kind of God Christ is, grasp, if you can. See, I say it, I will not defraud you. You ask what kind of God Christ is? Hear me, rather hear with me; together let us hear, together let us learn. For not because I speak and you hear, do I therefore not hear with you. You ask, then, when you hear: Christ is God, what kind of God is Christ? Hear with me: not, I say, hear me, but with me. For in this school we are all fellow students: heaven is the chair of our master. Hear, then, what kind of God Christ is. *In the beginning was the Word.* Where? *And the Word was with God.* But words we are wont to hear daily. Do not think so, as you are wont to hear: *God was the Word*, what kind I ask. For see, I already believe him God: but what kind of God he is, I ask. *Seek his face always*[^3]. Let no one fail in seeking, but make progress. He who seeks makes progress, if piety seeks, not vanity. How does piety seek, how vanity? Piety seeks by believing, vanity by quarreling. For if you wish to quarrel with me, and to say to me: What God do you worship? what kind of God do you worship? show me what you worship, I will answer: Even if there is what I might show, there is no one to whom to show it.

**3.** Nor do I dare to say that I have already grasped what you seek. For I enter, as far as I can, after the footsteps of that great athlete Christ, namely the apostle Paul who says: *Brothers, I do not consider that I myself have grasped it. I myself. I*, what is it, and *myself?* I who *labored more than all of them?* I know, Apostle, how you say: *I.* It is emphasis, not exaltation. For do you wish to hear how he says: *I?* When he had said: *I labored more than all of them*, he took the I from himself. *I labored*, he says, *more than all of them.* And as though we to him: Who? and he to us: *Yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^4]. He, then, with whom was so great a grace of God that, called later, he labored more than those who preceded, yet says: *Brothers, I do not consider that I myself have grasped it.* There *I*, where he does not grasp. For it belongs to human weakness not to grasp. But where he was lifted *into the third heaven, and heard ineffable words, which it is not lawful for a man to speak, he did not say: I. But what did he say? I know a man fourteen years ago*[^5]. *I know a man*: and he himself who spoke was a man, and what was done in him, he did as it were another, therefore he did not fail. Do not, then, contend, do not quarrel, demanding of me what kind of God I worship. For it is not an idol, and I extend my finger, and say to you: See the God whom I worship; or it is some constellation, or some star, or the sun, or the moon; and I extend my finger to heaven, and say: See what I worship. There is nothing to which the finger may be extended, but there is something to which the mind may be extended. See him not grasping, and yet seeking, following, gaping, sighing, desiring; see him, see to what he stretches toward his God, whether a finger, or rather the mind. What does he say? *I do not consider that I have grasped it. But one thing, forgetting the things behind, stretched out to the things that are before, I follow according to the intention toward the prize of the upward calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^6]. *I follow*, he says, I walk, he says, I am on the way. Follow, if you can: together let us come to the homeland, where you will not seek of me, nor I of you. Together, then, now by believing let us seek, that together afterward by seeing we may rejoice.

**4.** For who has shown you what kind of God Christ is? See what he condescended to say through his servant, let him say also through this servant of his, to my fellow servants, his servants. It was said to you: *In the beginning was the Word.* You were asking where he was, the answer was: *The Word was with God.* And lest you despise the word from the custom of human speech, you heard: *God was the Word.* Do you still ask what kind of God? *All things were made through him*[^7]. Love him: whatever you love is from him. Let us not love the creation, neglecting the Creator; but let us attend to the creation, and praise the Creator. I cannot show you my God: I show what he made, I recall what he made. *All things were made through him.* He made new things, not being new; he made temporal things, being everlasting; he made changeable things, who knows no change. Inspect the things made, praise the maker: believe, that you may be cleansed. For do you wish to see? A good thing, a great thing you wish; I urge you to wish it. Do you wish to see? *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God*[^8]. First, then, think about cleansing the heart: have this as your business, call yourself away to this, press at this work. What you wish to see is clean, that with which you wish to see is unclean. You think of God as some immense or manifold light of these eyes, you increase the spaces for yourself as great as you wish: you do not set an end where you do not wish, you set one where you wish. These are phantasms of your heart, this is the uncleanness of your heart. Take it away, cast it out. If earth fell into your eye, and you wished me to show you the light; your eyes would first seek a cleanser. So great is the uncleanness in your heart; there avarice is no small uncleanness. You heap up what you will not take away with you. Do you not know that when you heap up, you draw mud to your heart? From where, then, will you see what you seek?

**5.** You say to me: Show me your God. I say to you: Attend a little to your heart. Show me, you say, your God. Attend, I say, a little to your heart. Whatever you see there that displeases God, take it away from there. God wishes to come to you. Hear the Lord Christ himself: *I and the Father will come to him, and will make our dwelling with him*[^9]. See what God promises. If, then, I promised that I would come into your house, you would clean it; God wishes to come into your heart, and are you slow to clean the house for him? He does not love to dwell with avarice, with an unclean and insatiable woman, whom you served at her command, and sought to see God. What have you done that God commanded? What have you not done that avarice commanded? What have you done that God commanded? I show what your heart dwells with, you who wish to see God. For this I had said: There is what to show, but there is no one to whom. What God commanded, what have you done? What avarice commanded, what have you delayed? God commanded that you clothe the naked, you trembled; avarice commanded that you strip the clothed, you raged. If you had done what God commanded, what shall I say to you, you would have this and that? You would have God himself. If you had done what God commanded, you would have God. You did what avarice commanded, what do you have? I know, you are going to say to me: I have whatever I took away. So you have by taking away. Do you have something with you, who have lost yourself? I have, you say. Where, where, I ask you? Surely either in the bedroom, or in the little bag, or in the chest: I do not want to say more. Wherever you have it, surely now you do not have it with you. Surely now you think you have it in the chest; perhaps it has perished, and you do not know; perhaps when you return, you do not find what you left. I seek your heart: there, what you have, I ask. See, you have filled your chest, and broken your conscience. See the full man, learn to be full: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it was done; blessed be the name of the Lord*[^10]. Surely he had lost everything. From where, then, did he bring forth these gems of praise to the Lord?

**6.** Cleanse, then, your heart, as much as you can; do this, work at this. And that he may cleanse it, where he may dwell, ask, beseech, humble yourself. You do not grasp: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing. What was made in him was life, and the life was the light of men. And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not grasp it*[^11]. See why you do not grasp: *The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not grasp it.* What is the darkness, except bad works? What is the darkness, except bad desires, pride, avarice, ambition, envy? All these are darkness: therefore you do not grasp. For the light *shines in the darkness*; but provide one to grasp it.

**7.** See, then, lest perhaps you can in some way receive this: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^12]. Through the man Christ you tend to God Christ. God is far from you: but God became man. What was far from you, through the man was made near to you. Where you may dwell, is God: by which you may go, is man. The same Christ himself is both by which you go, and to which you go. He himself, then, *the Word, was made flesh and dwelt among us.* He took up what he was not, he did not lose what he was. The man appeared, and God lay hidden. The man was killed, and God was offended: but the man rose, and God was found. Consider, then, how many things he did as God, how many he suffered as man. He was killed, but not in his divinity: Christ himself was killed. For there are not two, God and man; so that we now make or know not a Trinity, but a quaternity. The man indeed is man, and God is God; but the whole Christ is man and God: he himself, then, Christ, is man and God. As you, a man, are body and soul; so the whole Christ is man and God. Therefore the whole Christ is flesh, soul, and God. The same one says something that pertains to God; says something that pertains to the soul; says something that pertains to the flesh: the whole pertains to Christ. What does he say as God? *As the Father has life in himself, so he gave the Son to have life in himself. Whatever the Father does, these same things the Son also does likewise*[^13]. *I and the Father are one*[^14]. What does Christ say according to his soul? *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^15]. What does Christ say according to the flesh? *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^16]. *Handle and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^17]. These are the *treasures of wisdom and knowledge*[^18].

**8.** Surely the whole law hangs on two precepts: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, with your whole soul, with your whole mind; and you shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two precepts hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^19]. In Christ you have the whole. Do you wish to love your God? You have it in Christ: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^20]. Do you wish to love your neighbor? You have it in Christ: *The Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^21].

**9.** Let his grace cleanse us: let him cleanse us by his helps and consolations. My brothers, through him and in him *I beseech you*, abound in good works, in mercy, goodness, kindness. Quickly forgive what is sinned against you. Let no one hold anger against another, lest he block off for himself his prayer to God. For all these things, because we are in this age, because even if we make progress, even if we live justly, we do not live here without sin. For sins are not only those which are called crimes, *adulteries, fornications*, sacrileges, *thefts*, robberies, *false testimonies*; these alone are not sins. To attend to something that you ought not, is a sin; to hear willingly something that ought not to be heard, is a sin; to think something that ought not to be thought, is a sin.

**10.** But our Lord, after that washing of regeneration, gave other daily remedies. Our daily cleansing is the Lord's Prayer. Let us say, and let us say truly, because this too is an alms: Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors. Give alms, and all things are clean to you. Remember, brothers, what he is going to say to those who will stand on the right. He will not say: you did this great thing and that; but: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat.* To those who will stand on the left he is not going to say: you did this evil and that; but: *I was hungry, and you did not give me to eat.* Those, for their alms, *into eternal life: those*, for their barrenness, *into eternal fire*[^22]. Now choose either the right or the left. For I ask you, what hope of salvation can he have who is slack in remedies, frequent in diseases? But they are small diseases. Heap them up, and they press. The sins I have are smaller. Are they not many? For how are those small which press, which overwhelm? What is tinier than raindrops? Rivers are filled. What is tinier than grains of wheat? Barns are filled. You attend to the fact that they are smaller, and you do not attend to the fact that they are many. You know how to attend: count them, if you can. But plainly God gave a daily remedy.

**11.** Great is the mercy of him who *ascended on high, and led captivity captive.* What does it mean: *led captivity captive?* He killed death. Captivity was taken captive: death died. What then? Did he who ascended on high, and led captivity captive, do only this? Did he then abandon us? *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the age*[^23]. Therefore attend to that: *He gave gifts to men*[^24]. Open the bosom of piety, receive the gift of happiness.
