# Sermon 77/B: On the Canaanite Woman

**1.** You know from the Gospel, brothers, how the Canaanite woman by persevering wrung out what by asking once she could not; and the Lord, in order to put her off, exercised her desire, did not deny the benefit; for he knew to what she was going to come by asking, because he himself was teaching her to this end. First she was called a dog by him; afterward, *woman, great is your faith*[^1]. Having received the benefit she departed glad; but first she was changed, and then gladdened. How much changed? From a dog made a woman. And what kind of woman? Whose faith was great. Much did she advance: in one moment how much she profited! On account of this the Lord put her off, who said: *One must always pray, and not faint*[^2]. For it is the Lord's sentence, exhorting us to prayer. Daily men pray, the religious do not interrupt the times of prayers. For the Apostle too says: *Always rejoicing, praying without ceasing*[^3]. This is: *One must always pray, and not faint.* In another place the Lord himself: *Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you*[^4]. This the Canaanite woman did: she asked, sought, knocked, received. But she did this, that her daughter might be healed of a demon, and she merited it; her daughter was made whole from that hour. Was she, her daughter made whole, going to ask this again? Until she received, she sought, asked, knocked; she received, was gladdened, and departed. There is something, rather something great, on account of which one must always pray, and not faint. It is more than the health of a daughter, the immortality of life: this is that for which one must always pray to the end, as long as one lives here, until one lives without end, where there will no longer be petition, but exultation.

**2.** So now *one must always pray, and not faint*[^5]. One prays this, another that; your prayers are diverse, because your desires are diverse. All groan, as it were, equally: but he who hears apportions the wills. One asks God, as that Canaanite woman, that his son who is sick may be well; a wife asks for her husband, a husband for his wife; all ask for the sick: the prayer is not to be reproved. Others pray, groan, appeal, seek, ask, knock, that they may become rich; and the greater the craving is, the more ardent and frequent is such prayer. And some think that the Lord said even for this: *Ask, and you will receive; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you; for everyone who asks receives, and the seeker will find, and to the one who knocks it will be opened*[^6]. The avaricious man hears this, and daily asks nothing else, except to become rich. But, to be silent about the avaricious man, the poor man hears this; he prays, asks, seeks, knocks, that he may become rich; and sometimes he spends much time, and finds no hour in receiving, and says to himself: What is it that the Lord wished to say: *Ask and you will receive, seek and you will find, knock and it will be opened to you; for everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened?* What have I, wretched, done, that I ask and do not receive? What shall we say to such a one? Did the Lord lie? did Scripture lie? Far be it. This not even avarice itself dares to say: what, then, craving does not say, will godliness say?

**3.** What, then, shall we say to that man? Ask still, press on still, knock still; because not without cause was it said: *Everyone who asks receives.* Pray as much as you can, ask, knock, persevere, and you will be rich. He prolongs the time by praying, he expires in poverty; he received nothing, he found nothing to leave to his sons. Did all the labor of asking, seeking, knocking perish, then? It did not perish. So to such a man, while he lives, what must be said, except: Change your prayer? And why do you ask, seek, knock, that you may become rich from poor? Have you not heard the Apostle saying: *Those who wish to become rich fall into temptation, and many desires, foolish and harmful, which plunge a man into ruin and perdition*[^7]? Behold what you were asking: but your Father, of whom you asked this, mercifully denied what you asked, lest you come into ruin and perdition. Behold, change your prayer. For you too do not give your son what he asks. For what if your son asks a little knife, by which he may wound himself, or by weeping dash himself, that you lift him onto a horse, will you do it? will you dare it? Is it not better that he weep healthy, than that he be wept over disabled? If, then, you, although you are greater, know what is good to give to your son, how much more does the Father, who is always good, furnish to you, when he does not give, and you do not know it? It is necessary that you be a son; he does not despise you, be a secure son. The apostle Paul, when he was buffeted by the angel of Satan, lest he be exalted, as he himself confesses—for when would we dare to say such a thing of the Apostle?—says of the angel of Satan: *On account of which I asked the Lord three times that he take him from me; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness*[^8]. Why do you ask, O Paul? is it not that it may be well with you? Let the craftsman do, as you do not know, that it may be well with you. Does the plaster harm you when it bites? It is for you, because it heals. If, then, the apostle Paul asked, and was not heard, why are you sad, when you are not heard? For perhaps you ask what it is not expedient for you to receive.

**4.** It is a wondrous thing, my brothers: the Apostle asked, and did not receive; the devil asked, and received: the Apostle was not heard, and the devil was heard. O justice! Rather, great justice. And where, he says, was the devil heard? Have you not read, or not heard, that the demons were heard, and permitted to go into the swine[^9]? Or have you not read, or not heard, that the devil asked of the Lord his servant Job to be tempted, and received it[^10]? A wondrous thing! The devil asked, and Job is handed over. He is handed over, but to be proved; handed over, but to be tempted; handed over, but to be examined, and to be commended to posterity. The devil received him, but to be confounded. You see, that it is not always good to receive what you ask. Change, then, your prayers, that you may securely receive what is good; change your prayers, amend your desires. I say this to those who desire temporal things, that they may become rich.

**5.** See what Jeduthun asked, whose words we sang: *Hear*, he says, *my prayer, with your ears receive my tears*[^11]. Did he ask for riches? Did he have a wound in his body, and ask this to be healed? And where do we find what he asked, or in what desires he exercised his prayers, or for what desires he poured out the tears which he wished to be heard? Where do we find his desire? where, except in his words? *My substance*, he says, *is as nothing before you. Yet truly all is vanity, every living man*[^12]. *And now what is my expectation? Is it not you, O Lord? And my substance is before you always*[^13]. A little before he said: *And my substance is as nothing before you.* My mortal substance is as nothing before you; but because after this life I am going to receive immortality, on account of which I wish you to hear my tears, my substance will be immortal before you always. Behold the desire, behold what you should ask, behold what you should desire, behold what you should pray; behold what one must always pray, until we go out from here, and not faint.

**6.** Perhaps some of you may say: What did Jeduthun wish to say: *With your ears receive my tears?* For tears are seen, not heard; tears flow, they do not sound. Yet indeed they have their voices, just as the blood of Abel had its voice[^14]. If the blood of the slain had a voice to the Lord, the tears of the one praying have one too, they have it altogether. For tears too are the blood of the heart. So, when you ask for eternal life, when you say: *Your kingdom come*[^15], where you may live secure, where you may live always, where you never grieve over a friend, never fear an enemy, when you ask this, weep, pour out the inner blood, sacrifice to your God your heart; this is what one must pray, and not faint; this is what the Lord's Prayer teaches: *Hallowed be your name, your kingdom come, your will be done as in heaven and on earth*[^16], that we may be equal to your angels[^17]. O desire! What man would dare to desire, if God had not deigned to promise? Pray: great is what you pray, but greater is he who promised. It is difficult what he promised, that a man become an angel, nothing more difficult: but all things are possible to God[^18]. Do you think it a great and difficult thing, that a man become an angel; does that not seem to you difficult, and much more difficult and much more incredible, that the only one of God became a man? Does a man doubt that a man will be an angel, for whose sake God became man! Do you doubt that you receive what you ask, when you hold such a pledge, who deigned to make himself your debtor freely? You did not lend, or advance any money, or furnish any benefit to God. Is it not that whatever you have, you have from him, and what you are going to receive, you are going to receive from him? He deigned to make himself a debtor, and what kind of debtor! He made a bond, he gave a pledge. His bond is the divine Scripture; his pledge is the death of Christ, his promise is the death of Christ. He who gave the death of his Son to the impious, did he deny the death of his Son to the godly and the faithful?

**7.** Be secure, brothers, you will receive: ask, seek, knock, you will receive, find, it will be opened to you. But do not ask, seek, knock only with voices, but also with conduct; do good works, without which this life ought not to be lived at all; by daily good works blot out sins. Nor are the light sins themselves to be despised; for although they are not great, they are heaped up, they make a pile; they are heaped up and make a mass. Do not despise them because they are small, but fear them if they are many. What is smaller than drops of rain? And by many of these the fields are satisfied, the rivers are filled. Do not despise your light and small sins, lest, a pile being made, they press you. Behold, the water of the sea seeps little by little through the cracks of the ship, and yet fills the bilge; if it is not drained, it sinks the ship. But if a wave be huge, it comes like a mountain, and at once overwhelms the ship, and destroys it; so are murders, that is, grave sins are so, adulteries, fornications, blasphemies, perjuries; they are grand sins, they overwhelm at once. But those small sins, without which human life cannot be, enter little by little through the cracks of human frailty, and flow together into the bilge. Imitate the sailors, whose hands do not cease, and nothing remains in the bilge; let them not cease, I say, from good works. But again they flow together to the bilge, because the cracks of human frailty remain; and therefore let the bilge be drained again. For if your hands do not cease draining by good works, that day will find you pure; and you will come secure to that life which Jeduthun desired, when he said: *With your ears receive my tears.*
