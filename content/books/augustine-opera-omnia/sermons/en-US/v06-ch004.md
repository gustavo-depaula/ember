# Sermon 187: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1.** *My mouth will speak the praise of the Lord*[^1]: of that Lord through whom *all things were made*[^2], and who was made among all things: who is the revealer of the Father, the creator of his mother: the Son of God from the Father without a mother, the son of a man from a mother without a father: the great day of the angels, a small one in the day of men: the Word, God before all times, the Word made flesh at the opportune time: the founder of the sun, founded beneath the sun: ordering all the ages from the bosom of the Father, consecrating this present day from the womb of his mother: abiding there, proceeding from here: the maker of heaven and earth, risen under heaven upon the earth: ineffably wise, wisely an infant: filling the world, lying in a manger: ruling the stars, suckling at the breast: so great in the form of God, so small in the form of a servant; that neither was that greatness diminished by this smallness, nor was this smallness pressed down by that greatness. For when he took up human limbs, he did not abandon his divine works: nor did he cease to *reach mightily from end to end and order all things sweetly*[^3], when, clothed in the weakness of flesh, he was received, not enclosed, in a virginal womb; so that the food of wisdom might not be withdrawn from the angels, and we might taste how sweet the Lord is.

**2.** Why do we marvel at this concerning the Word of God, when this speech which we bring forth flows so freely into the senses that the hearer both receives it and does not enclose it? For unless it were received, it would instruct no one: if it were enclosed, it would not come to others. And surely this speech is divided into words and syllables: yet you do not, as from food for the belly, take away from it single particles; rather, all of you hear the whole, each of you grasps the whole. Nor do we fear, while we speak, that one might consume the whole by hearing it, and another not be able to have what he takes: but we wish you to be so attentive, defrauding no one's ear and mind, that each of you may both hear the whole and leave the whole to be heard by the rest. Nor does this happen at alternating times, so that when the speech that is uttered first enters into you, it goes out from you, that it may enter another: but it comes to all at once, and the whole reaches each. And if the whole could be held in memory, then, just as you all came to hear the whole, so each of you would return with the whole. How much more the Word of God, through whom *all things were made*[^4], and which, remaining in itself, makes all things new; which is neither enclosed in places, nor stretched out in times, nor varied by short and long intervals, nor woven of voices, nor ended by silence; how much more could this Word, so great and such as it is, by taking up a body make fruitful the womb of a mother, and not depart from the bosom of the Father? go forth from here to human eyes, illuminate angelic minds from there? proceed to the earth from here, stretch out the heavens from there? become man here, make men there?

**3.** Let no one, then, believe that the Son of God was turned and changed into the son of a man: but let us rather believe that, the divine substance not being consumed and the human being perfectly taken up, the Son of God, remaining so, was made the son of a man. For it is not because it is said, *God was the Word*, and *the Word was made flesh*[^5], that the Word was so made flesh as to cease to be God: since in that very flesh, that the Word was made flesh, *Emmanuel* was born, *God with us*[^6]. Just as the word that we bear in the heart becomes a voice when we bring it forth from the mouth, yet that word is not changed into this voice, but, remaining whole, takes up the voice in which it may proceed, so that what is understood remains within, and what is heard sounds without; yet the same thing is brought forth in sound that had before sounded in silence; and thus the word, when it becomes a voice, is not changed into a voice, but, remaining in the light of the mind and taking up the voice of the flesh, proceeds to the hearer and does not desert the thinker. This is not when the voice itself is thought in silence, which is either Greek, or Latin, or of some other tongue: but when, before all diversity of languages, the very thing that is to be said is still in some way bare in the chamber of the heart to one who understands, which, that it may proceed from there, is clothed with the voice of the speaker. Yet both of these, both what is thought in understanding and what sounds in speaking, is changeable and unlike: neither will that remain when you have forgotten it, nor this when you have fallen silent: but *the Word of the Lord remains forever*[^7], and remains unchangeably.

**4.** And when he took up flesh in time, that he might proceed to our temporal life, he did not lose eternity in the flesh, but rather bestowed immortality even on the flesh. So he himself, *like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, rejoiced like a giant to run his course*[^8]. *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*: but that for our sake he might become what he was not, *he emptied himself*; not losing the form of God, but *taking the form of a servant*: and through this *being made in the likeness of men*; not by his own substance, but *found in fashion as a man*[^9]. For all this that we are, whether in soul or in body, is our nature, his fashion: we, unless we were this, would not be; he, if he were not this, would still be God. And when he began to be what he was not, he was made man while remaining God: so that not one of these, but both, might most truly be said; both, on account of which he was made man, *For the Father is greater than I*[^10]; and, on account of which he remained God, *I and the Father are one*[^11]. For if the Word were changed and turned into flesh, that is, God into man, only the one would be true, *The Father is greater than I*, because God is greater than man: but that other would be false, *I and the Father are one*, because God and man are not one. But perhaps he might say: I and the Father are not one, but were one. For what was and ceased to be, surely is not, but was. But now, both on account of the true form of a servant which he had taken up, he truly said, *The Father is greater than I*; and on account of the true form of God in which he remained, he truly said, *I and the Father are one*. He emptied himself, then, among men, not so made what he was not as to cease to be what he was: but hiding what he was and showing what he had been made. Accordingly, because the Virgin conceived and bore a son, on account of the manifest form of a servant, *a child is born to us*[^12]. But because the Word of God, which remains forever, was made flesh, that it might *dwell among us*[^13], on account of the form of God hidden but remaining, as Gabriel announced, we call *his name Emmanuel*[^14]. For he was made man, remaining God, so that the son of a man might rightly be called *God with us*: not one God, another man. So let the world rejoice in those who believe, for whose salvation he came through whom the world was made. The maker of Mary, born of Mary: the son of David, the Lord of David: the seed of Abraham, who is before Abraham: the maker of the earth, made in the earth: the creator of heaven, created under heaven. He himself is the day that the Lord has made, and the day of our heart is the Lord himself. *Let us walk in his light*[^15], let us rejoice and be glad in him.
