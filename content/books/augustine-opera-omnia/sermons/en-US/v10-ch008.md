# Sermon 346: On Our Pilgrimage in This Life, by Faith

**1.** Recall with us, most beloved brothers, that the Apostle said: *As long as we are in the body, we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord; for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^1]. For our Lord Jesus Christ, who said, *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^2], willed us to walk both through himself and to himself. For by what do we walk, except by the way? And to what do we walk, except to the truth and to the life, namely to eternal life, which alone is to be called life? For this mortal life, in which we now are, by comparison with that life is proved to be death; it is varied by such great changeableness, and is established by no stability, and is bounded by a very short course. And therefore the Lord answered that rich man who had said to him, *Good teacher, what shall I do that I may obtain eternal life?* *If you wish to come to life, keep the commandments*[^3]. He was surely in some life; for he was not speaking to a corpse, and not to a living man. But because that man had asked about obtaining eternal life, the Lord did not say: If you wish to come to eternal life; but: *If you wish*, he said, *to come to life, keep the commandments*: clearly wishing this to be understood, that what is not eternal life is not even to be called life; because the true life is none but the eternal. Hence the Apostle too, when he admonished that the counsel of almsgiving should be given to the rich, said: *Let them be rich in good works, let them give easily, share, lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of true life*[^4]. What did he call true life, except eternal life, which alone is to be called life, because it alone is blessed? For surely those rich men, to whom he said it should be commanded that they lay hold of true life, had this life in the abundance of their riches: which, however, if the Apostle judged it to be true life, he would not say: *Let them lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of true life*: admonishing nothing else, except that the life of the rich is not true life; a life which by fools is named not only true, but even blessed. But how is that blessed which is not true? Therefore the blessed life is none but the true life; nor is the true life any but the eternal life, which the rich are understood not yet to hold through any delights; and therefore through almsgiving they are admonished to lay hold of it, that in the end they may hear: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world: for I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^5]. For that this kingdom itself is eternal life, the same Lord shortly afterward consequently shows, saying: *Those shall go into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^6].

**2.** Until we lay hold of this life, *we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord; since we walk by faith, not by sight*[^7]. For he says: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^8]. In faith he is the way for us, but in sight the truth and the life. *We see now through a mirror in an enigma*, and this is faith: *but then face to face*[^9], and that will be sight. Likewise he says: *That Christ may dwell in the inner man through faith in your hearts*[^10]: this is the way, where we know in part. But a little later he says: *to know also the surpassing knowledge of the love of Christ, that you may be filled unto all the fullness of God*[^11]: that will be sight, when in that fullness, when that which is perfect has come, that which is in part shall be taken away[^12]. Likewise he says: *For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*: this is faith. Then he adds: *When Christ your life appears, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^13]: that will be sight. John too says: *Most beloved, now already we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be*: this is faith. Then he subjoins: *We know that when he appears, we shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is*[^14]: that will be sight. Wherefore the Lord himself, who said, *I am the way and the truth and the life*, when he was speaking to the Jews, among whom were those who had already believed in him, directing his words now to them: *If you remain*, he said, *in my word, you will truly be my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*. These had already believed: for the Evangelist says thus: *But Jesus said to those who had believed in him: If you remain in my word, you will truly be my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*[^15]. They had already believed, then, and as on the way had already begun to walk in Christ. He exhorts them, therefore, that by remaining they may arrive. Where may they arrive, except at that which he said: *The truth will set you free*? What is that liberation, except from all the changeableness of vanity, from all the corruption of mortality? Therefore that itself is the true life, the eternal life, which we have not yet laid hold of, as long as we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord: but we are going to lay hold of it, because in the Lord himself we walk by faith, if we most constantly remain in his word. For according to what he said, *I am the way*, according to this he said: *If you remain in my word, you will truly be my disciples*. And according to what he said, *and the truth and the life*, according to this he said: *And you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*. In this pilgrimage, then, and on this way, that is, in faith, to what do I exhort you, brothers, except in the words of the Apostle who says: *Having therefore these promises, dearest ones, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting sanctification in the fear of the Lord*[^16]? For those who, before they believe, seek that the light of that most pure and unchangeable truth be supplied to them, when they cannot behold it except through faith with the heart cleansed—for *blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^17]—are like blind men, who first desire to see the bodily light of this sun, that they may be healed of their blindness; when they cannot see it unless they are first healed.
