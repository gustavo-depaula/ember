# Sermon 229/G: On the Tuesday of Easter

**1.** You have heard what the Lord said to his disciples after his resurrection, when he was no longer to die, and was taking away the fear of death from those who were to die . . .

**2.** For that Christ is God, not even those deny who refuse to confess that he is of one substance with the Father; they confess that he is God, but they deny that he is equal; therefore they make two gods. But we say that the Son is equal to the Father in such a way that, if it be asked of us singly whether the Father is God, we confess that he is God; whether the Son is God, we confess that he is God; whether the Father and the Son are gods, or God, we confess that he is God. This weak and human hearts do not grasp; this thing so great and divine, since it is about God that we speak, human weakness cannot conceive; but it can believe, so that afterward there may come a fitting thought. For it was said to the Lord: *Show us the Father, and it is enough for us*[^1]. He who said this did not recognize the Father in the Son; for he saw that which the Jews were able to crucify; he did not see him whom the Jews, hidden, struck against. And he thought that all that Christ was, was what he gazed upon with his eyes; and therefore it was not enough for him, because he did not see the whole. And because he believed there was nothing more in Christ, he sought the Father, who would be enough for them. As if to say: You indeed we know, you indeed we see; but we desire still something greater. *Show us the Father, and it is enough for us*; for there will be nothing further for us to seek, once we have come to know the Father. And he, willing to show himself equal to the Father, said: *So long a time I have been with you, and you have not known me?*[^2]

**3.** *Philip, he who has seen me has also seen the Father*[^3]. What does it mean, *He who has seen me has also seen the Father,* except that you do not see me, because you think the Father greater? *He who has seen me has also seen the Father.* Not without cause do you think the Father would be enough for you, except that you do not yet see me. *Show us the Father, and it is enough for us.* See me, and I am enough for you. And yet he said this to one who was seeing him: seeing, and not seeing; seeing the form of a servant, not seeing the form of God. For if he saw the form of God, there he would see the Son equal to the Father; for *though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^4]. Because it was his nature, it was not robbery. He for whom it was robbery fell; and from where he fell, from there he cast down. For the devil willed to share with the man whom he cast down that very robbery, that he might have as companion in punishment the one whom he willed to have as partner in sin. For what else did he say to human beings, but: *Taste, and your eyes will be opened, and you will be like gods*[^5]? This is the robbery. That pride is robbery, it is usurpation, not a gracious gift. For if we do not usurp, we shall be by grace what by pride we shall not be; for to grace pertains: *I have said: You are gods, and sons of the Most High, all of you*[^6]. But *I have said*: what I have said, he himself accomplishes. But what sort of gods will men be, what sort of gods? Equal to the angels of God[^7]. This is what is promised; let us seek no further; for we shall not be equal to God, but, made equal to the angels, we shall on earth, as we believe in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, so also see them. Therefore, because it was too much for Philip to see what the Lord said (for he was still weighed down by a corruptible body; that had not yet come which the Apostle speaks of: *We see now through a mirror in an enigma, but then face to face*[^8])—because it was too much for him to think upon what he heard from the Lord: *He who has seen me has also seen the Father* . . . Consider that it was not said in vain, *has also seen the Father.* He did not say: He who has seen me has seen the Father; lest he give occasion to the Sabellians, who are called Patripassians, who say that the very one who is the Son is the Father; but when he wills, he is the Father; when he wills, he is the Son. Lest he give occasion to this other error, he did not say: He who has seen me has seen the Father, but, *has also seen the Father*: thus both the Father and the Son. Therefore the Father and the Son are two. Two what? If you ask me, the Father and the Son are two.

**4.** Whatever you say again, I answer again. The Father and the Son, what? Gods? No, two. What then? One God. I do not understand, he says. The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Father: they are two. The Father is God, the Son is God: and are they not two gods? I do not understand. What, then, am I to say to you who do not understand? Hear the Prophet: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^9]. You do not understand, so as to believe; but you believe, so as to understand; faith is the work, understanding the reward. *Unless you believe, you will not understand.* Yet hear the Lord himself, that you may learn what to believe. *If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I*[^10]. Now there has arisen, as it were, one who understands. Behold, he says, now I understand. *The Father is greater than I.* The form of a servant is speaking: seek the form of God. This, then, he said: *If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father, for the Father is greater than I.* Now, in that you see me, there you see me where I am less. Since, then, you see me there where I am less, if you love me, let me go there where I am equal. Do you wonder that the Son is less than the Father in the form of a servant? I tell you that he is less even than himself, for *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^11]. But if you understand that it was said of him: *He was made a little less than the angels*[^12]; then, if you have applied yourself to the form of a servant, do not remain there, rise from there, confess Christ equal to the Father. Why do you gladly hear: *The Father is greater than I*? Hear more gladly: *I and the Father are one*[^13]. Behold the catholic faith, sailing as it were between Scylla and Charybdis, as one sails in that strait between Sicily and Italy; on one side ship-wrecking rocks, on the other a ship-devouring whirlpool. If a ship runs upon the rocks, it is broken; if it runs into the whirlpool, it is swallowed up. So too Sabellius: He is one, he said, they are not two, the Father and the Son. Observe the shipwreck. Likewise the Arian: They are two, one greater, the other less, not of equal substance. Observe the whirlpool. Sail between the two, and hold the straight course. For not without cause are the catholics named orthodox: *orthodoxon* in Greek is in Latin "straight." If, then, you hold the straight line, you run neither upon Scylla nor into Charybdis. Hold fast, then, you: *I and the Father are one.*

**5.** *I and the Father*: let the Arian hear *one,* and let Sabellius hear *are.* When you hear *one,* you will keep clear of Charybdis; when you hear *are,* you will save yourself from Scylla. Say *We are one,* and you will sail rightly. Behold, we have heard *we are one*; one, because of the same substance; one, because not of a dissimilar and unequal nature; one, because of the highest equality, in nothing discordant, in nothing diverse. Behold why *one*; why *are*? Because the Father and the Son are one God. Why, then, is the one God not "we are"? For the one God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, is one God; they are three, and there is one God. The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Father, the Holy Spirit is neither the Father nor the Son, but the Spirit of the Father and the Son: and all this is one God. Why one God? Because there is there such great love, such great peace, such great concord, in nothing any dissonance. Now I will tell you whence you may believe what you cannot understand unless you believe. How many souls, tell me, in the *Acts of the Apostles,* believed when they beheld the miracles of the Apostles? I mean those Jews who had crucified the Lord, who brought bloody hands to it, who had sacrilegious ears, whose tongue was compared to a sword: *Their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword*[^14]. Yet because not without cause had Christ poured out a prayer for them, nor without cause had he said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^15], very many of that number believed; and, as we read: *They believed that day,* it is said: *three thousand souls*[^16]. Behold, there are thousands of souls, and behold so many thousands of souls; and yet the Holy Spirit came into them, through whom love is poured forth in our hearts[^17]. And what is said of so many souls? *They had one soul and one heart*[^18]. So many souls, one soul; not by nature, but by grace. If so many souls, by that grace coming from above, were made one soul, do you wonder that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God? And so, my brothers, hold the strongest and most whole and catholic faith. When you hear or read in the Scriptures where the Father is shown to be greater, recall the form of a servant; but where you read that the Father and the Son are one, believe the divinity.

**6.** And if you are not capable of conceiving it, yet hold the faith; faith itself leads through, faith itself causes your good works to be accepted by God. *For everything that is not of faith is sin*[^19]. Someone says to you: Did Christ die? Confess that he died, but in the flesh. And who died? The only Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ. Did the divinity, then, die? The divinity does not die. Did not Christ, then, die? Christ God died. How did Christ God die, if the divinity does not die? In the way that the Apostles too died in the flesh, not in the soul. Where, then, the Apostle died in the flesh, so then Christ died in the flesh. Yet Christ died; though in respect of that part by which he could die, yet not in respect of his divinity could he die. For *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^20]. Where is death? Nowhere. In the beginning the Word was not made, as *in the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^21]: this the Word made. What was in the beginning? Therefore *the Word was, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* Seek death, you do not find it; there is no where, there is no whence, there is no who. There is no where, because it is not among the hands of the Jews; there is no whence, because it has no flesh and blood; there is no who, it is God, it is equal to the Father. Whence, then, did he die? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^22].
