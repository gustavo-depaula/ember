# Sermon 306: On the Feast of the Martyrs of the Massa Candida

**1. 1.** As we have heard, and as we have answered in our singing: *Precious is the death of the saints of the Lord*[^1], but *in his sight*[^2], not in the sight of the foolish. For *in the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die, and their departure was accounted a malice*[^3]. "Malice" in the Latin tongue does not usually have the meaning that it has in the language in which Scripture spoke. For in the Latin language "malice" is usually the word by which men are called evil; but in that language "malice" also means the evil that men suffer. So "malice" in this passage is understood as punishment. This, then, is what was said: *In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die, and their departure was accounted a punishment: but they are in peace. And though in the sight of men they suffered torments,* this is the malice, *their hope,* it says, *is full of immortality; and having been chastened a little, they shall be well rewarded in much*[^4]. *For the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory that is to come, which shall be revealed in us*[^5]. But until it is revealed, it is hidden. And because it is hidden, therefore *in the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die*[^6]. But surely, because it is hidden, is it also hidden from God, in whose sight it is precious? Therefore *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^7]. To this hidden mystery, then, we owe the eyes of faith, that we may believe what we do not see, and that, having suffered evils unjustly, we may bear them bravely.

**2. 2.** Let the cause be a chosen one for us, lest the punishment harm us. For a bad cause has no reward, but only a just torment. It is not, then, in a man's power by what kind of end he finishes this life; but it is in a man's power how he lives, so that he may finish his life secure. And not even this would be in our power, had not the Lord *given the power to become sons of God*[^8]. But to whom? *To those who believe in his name*[^9]. This is the first cause of the martyrs, this is the white throng of the martyrs. If the cause is white, the throng too is white. For it was called a "throng" (massa) from the great number, and "white" (candida) from the splendor of its cause. So many companions did not fear robbers. But even had they walked one by one, they would have been fortified against robbery, because the way itself was a fortification. *Beside the path,* it says, *they laid snares for me*[^10]. Therefore he who does not turn aside from the way does not fall into the snare. And we have the supreme and faithful promise of our Lord Jesus Christ, who says: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^11].

**2. 3.** Now every man, whatever he may be, wants to be happy. There is no one who does not want this, and who does not want it in such a way that he wants it above all else; indeed, whoever wants other things wants them for the sake of this one thing.

**3. 3.** Men are carried away by various desires, and one desires this, another that: there are different ways of living in the human race; and in the multitude of ways of living, one man chooses and pursues one thing, another another; yet there is no one, whatever way of life he has chosen, who does not desire the happy life. The happy life, then, is the common possession of all; but the question is by what road one comes to it, in what direction one tends, by what path, once taken, one arrives. And so, if we seek the happy life on earth, I do not know whether we can find it: not because what we seek is evil, but because we do not seek it in its proper place. One says: Happy are those who serve as soldiers. Another denies it, and says: Happy, but rather those who till the field. And another denies this too, and says: Happy are those who are engaged in the forum with popular renown, who plead cases, who govern by their tongue the life and death of men. And another denies this, and says: Happy, but rather those who judge, who have the power of hearing and discerning. Another denies this, and says: Happy are those who sail the seas, who learn of many regions, who gather many profits. You see, dearly beloved, that in all this multitude of ways of living not one pleases all; and yet the happy life pleases all. What is this, that while no particular way of life pleases all, the happy life pleases all?

**4. 4.** Let us define, then, if we can, the happy life, about which all will answer: This I want. For since there is no one who, when asked whether he wants the happy life, says: I do not, and yet we are asking what that happy life itself is, we ought to define something to which every sense assents, and which no one says of: I do not want it. What then, my brothers, what is the happy life, which all want and not all have? Let us seek it, then. If someone is asked, Do you want to live? does he hear it in the way he would if he were asked: Do you want to be a soldier? For in that question, which is, Do you want to be a soldier? some would say to me, I do; and perhaps more, I do not. But if I say, Do you want to live? I think there is no one who says, I do not. For all have it implanted by nature to want to live and not want to die. Likewise, if I say, Do you want to be healthy? I think there is no one who says, I do not; for no one wants to suffer pain. Health is precious even in the rich man, and certainly in the poor man it is all he has. But what good is wealth to the rich man, if there is no health there, which is the poor man's patrimony? The rich man would gladly exchange his silver bed for the poor man's sackcloth, if sickness could depart along with the bed. See, the sense of all has agreed with me on these two things, life and health. Has the sense of all agreed on soldiering? Has the sense of all on farming? Has it on seafaring? The sense of all agrees on life and health.

**5. 4.** When, then, a man is alive and healthy, does he seek nothing more? If he is wise, perhaps he ought to seek nothing more. For where there is perfect life and perfect health, if more is sought, what will it be but a vicious desire?

**5. 5.** The wicked will have life amid torments. For *the hour will come,* as the Gospel says, *when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice: and those who have done good will come forth to the resurrection of life; but those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^12]. So the one group goes to reward, the other to torment; and both live, nor can any of them die. Those who live in reward embrace a most sweet life; but those who live in torment desire, if it could be done, to end such a life; and no one grants them destruction, just as no one takes away their torment. But see how Scripture speaks and distinguishes: it did not deign to call such a life "life." Amid torments, amid sufferings, amid everlasting fires, it would not call it "life," so that the very name of life might be praise and not grief; so that wherever you hear "life," you should not think of torments. For to be forever amid torments is everlasting death, not any kind of life. The Scriptures call it the second death, after this first death, which we all owe to the human condition. And the second death is both called death, and no one there dies. I might more fittingly and better say, no one there lives. For to live amid sufferings is not to live. And from where do we prove that Scripture so spoke? See from where: from this testimony which I have just recalled: *For they will hear his voice,* it says, *and those who have done good will come forth to the resurrection of life*[^13]. It did not say: of the happy life; but, *to the resurrection of life*[^14].

**6. 5.** The name of life alone draws happiness with it. For unless the name of life carried happiness as its consequence, it would not be said to God: *For with you is the fountain of life*[^15]. For it is not said there either, For with you is the fountain of the happy life. It did not add "happy"; it said only *of life,* so that you might understand "of the happy life." Why? Because if it were miserable, it would no longer be life at all.

**6. 6.** Here is another testimony. We have already given two. For it was said: *Those who have done good, to the resurrection of life*[^16]; likewise it was said: *With you is the fountain of life*[^17]. Nowhere was "happy" added: but only that life is understood which is happy; and that which is not happy is not life either. Take yet another from the Gospel. That rich man who did not want to let go of what he had, and was indignant at the loss of his possessions, which by dying he was compelled to let go: I believe that, while he rejoiced in that most overflowing abundance of great but nevertheless earthly things, he was interrupted by the fear of death, and his heart said to him, as it were: See, you rejoice in your goods, and you do not know when a single fever may come. You gather, you acquire, you obtain, and you keep, and you rejoice: your soul is required of you; these things which you have prepared, whose will they be? When, as far as we can understand, he was often pricked by this thought as by certain goads of fear, he came to the Lord and said to him: *Good Teacher, what shall I do to obtain eternal life?*[^18] He feared to die, and was compelled to die. There was nowhere he might go to avoid perishing. Hemmed in by the necessity of dying and the desire of living, he came to the Lord and said: *Good Teacher, what shall I do to obtain eternal life?*[^19]

**7. 6.** Among other things, he heard—rather, let us say what pertains to the present matter: *If you would enter into life, keep the commandments*[^20]. This is what I had said I would prove. Nor did the one who asked say: What shall I do to obtain the *happy* life; but he said only *eternal life*[^21]. By not wanting to die, he sought a life that has no end. And does not, as I said, even the life of the wicked amid torments have no end? But this he did not call life. He knew that what would be amid sufferings and torments was not life: he knew it should rather be called death. Therefore he sought eternal life: so that where life is heard, there should be no doubt about happiness. And the Lord did not say to him: If you would enter into the happy life, keep the commandments; but he too named life alone, and said: *If you would enter into life, keep the commandments*[^22]. So that life amid torments is not life; and that alone is life which is happy; nor can it be happy unless it is eternal. For this reason that rich man, knowing that he was daily interrupted by the fear of death, sought eternal life. For the happy life, as it seemed to him, he already had. For he was rich and healthy, and I believe he said to himself: I want nothing more, if only it could be perpetual. For he had pleasures, as it were lovable ones, because he was satisfying foolish desires. Accordingly the Lord, by the one name of life—if he understood it—corrected him. He did not say: If you would enter into eternal life, which the man sought, as though already having the happy one; nor did he say: If you would enter into the happy life, knowing that if it is miserable it should not be called life either: but he said: *If you would enter into life;* there it is eternal, there happy; *If you would enter into life, keep the commandments*[^23]. So it is life, which is eternal and happy: because if it is not eternal, it is not happy; but if it is eternal in punishments, it is not life.

**8. 7.** What is it, brothers? When I asked whether you wanted to live, you all answered that you did; whether you wanted to be healthy, you all answered that you did. But if health and life are feared lest they should end, then it is no longer life. For it is not to live forever, but to fear forever. If to fear forever, then to be tormented forever. If the torment is everlasting, where is the eternal life? We hold for certain that there is no happy life except the eternal; indeed, that there is no happy life unless there is life: because if it is not eternal, and if it is not with perpetual satisfaction, then beyond doubt it is neither happy nor life. We have found it; all agree. We have found it plainly in thought, not yet in possession. This is the possession that all seek: there is no one who does not seek it. Be he evil, be he good, he seeks it: but the good man confidently, the evil man shamelessly. Why do you, evil man, seek the good? Does not your very request answer how dishonest you are, when you, an evil man, seek the good? Are you not seeking something that belongs to another? If, then, you seek the highest good, that is, life, be good, so that you may arrive at the good. *If you would enter into life, keep the commandments*[^24]. But when we have arrived at life, why should I add "eternal"? why should I add "happy"? once for all, "life," because that itself is life which is both eternal and happy: when we have arrived at life, it will be certain to us that we shall be in it forever. For if we are there, and are uncertain whether we shall be there forever, even there there will be fear. And if there is fear, there will be torment, not of the flesh, but, what is worse, of the heart. But where there is torment, what happiness is there? It will, then, be certain to us that we shall be in that life forever, and shall not be able to end it: because we shall be in the kingdom of him of whom it was said: *And of his kingdom there will be no end*[^25].

**9. 7.** And when Wisdom set forth the glory of the saints of God, whose death is precious in his sight, she said, as you heard at the end of the reading: *And the Lord will reign over them forever*[^26]. We shall be, then, in a great and everlasting kingdom; and great and everlasting because it is just.

**9. 8.** There no one deceives, no one is deceived: there is no place there for you to suspect evil of your brother. For most of the evils of the human race arise from nowhere else than from false suspicions. You believe of a man that he hates you, when perhaps he loves you; and through a wicked suspicion you become the bitterest enemy of your dearest friend. What is he to do, whom you do not believe, and who cannot show you his heart? He speaks to you, saying, I love you. But because he could say this to you even while lying (for the words of a liar are the same as those of one who speaks the truth), by not believing him you have hated him all the same. Therefore he wanted to make you secure against that sin, who said to you: *Love your enemies*[^27]. Christian, love even your enemies, lest unawares you hate even your friends. So we cannot see one another's hearts in this life, *until the Lord comes, and brings to light the things hidden in darkness, and discloses the purposes of the heart; and then every man will have his praise from God*[^28].

**10. 9.** So if someone were now to say to us—someone whom we would beyond doubt believe, if a prophet said it, if God said it in whatever way he willed and through whomever he willed—Live secure, all things will abound for you, none of you will die, none will fall sick, none will suffer pain; I have taken away death from the human race, I do not want anyone to die—if he said this, we would exult as if made secure, and would ask for nothing more. So it seems to us altogether. If we heard this, we would at once want this also to be added for us: that we might see one another's hearts, and not envy; that we might see not by human suspicion but by divine truth: that I might not be anxious about my friend, about my neighbor, lest he hate me, lest he wish me ill, and lest by that very anxiety I do the evil before I suffer it. Without doubt we would seek this; we would seek a sure life, and the mutual knowledge of one another's hearts. For now you understand what life I mean; lest by commending it too often I dull rather than instruct. So we would want truth to be added to life, that we might know one another's hearts, and not be deceived by our suspicions: and that we might be certain of that perpetual life itself, that we should not fall from it. Add truth to life, and you find the happy life. For no one wants to be deceived, just as no one wants to die. Show me a man who wants to be deceived. How many are found who want to deceive: but no one wants to be deceived. Settle it with yourself. You do not want to be deceived, so do not deceive: what you do not want to suffer, do not do. You want to come to a life where you are not deceived, so live now a life where you do not deceive. You want to come to a life where you are not deceived? who would not? The reward delights you; do not disdain the work whose reward it is. Live now a life where you do not deceive; and you will come to a life where you are not deceived. To the truthful, truth will be given as a reward, and to one who lives well in time, eternity will be given as a reward.

**10. 10.** So we all want this, brothers, life and truth. But by what road do we come, by what do we go? For where we are coming to, even though we do not yet hold it in possession, yet already in thought and reason we believe and see: we tend toward life and truth. Christ himself is this. By what way do you seek to go? *I am,* he says, *the way*[^29]. Where do you seek to go? *And the truth and the life*[^30].

**11. 10.** See what the martyrs loved, and therefore they despised present and passing things. Do not marvel at their courage: love conquered pain. Let us celebrate the solemn feast of the Massa Candida with a white conscience; and following the footsteps of the martyrs and gazing upon the head of the martyrs and of us, if we long to come to so great a good, let us not fear the hard road. He who promised is truthful, he who promised is faithful, he who promised cannot deceive. Let us say to him, then, with a white conscience: *Because of the words of your lips I have kept hard ways*[^31]. Why do you fear the hard ways of sufferings and tribulations? He himself passed along them. You answer, perhaps: But it was he. The apostles passed along them. You still answer: But they were the apostles. I accept that. Answer: many men passed along them afterward too. Blush: women passed along them too. Did you come to suffering as an old man? do not fear death, if only because you are near to death. Are you young? young men passed along them too, who still hoped for life: boys passed along them too, girls passed along them too. How is the road still rough, which so many have smoothed by walking? This, then, brothers, is our solemn and constant reminder to you: that we celebrate the feasts of the martyrs not with an empty solemnity; but that we should not be afraid to imitate, by a like faith, those whom we love in their feasts.
