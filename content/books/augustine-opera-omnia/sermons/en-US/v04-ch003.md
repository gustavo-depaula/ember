# Sermon 96

**1.** It seems hard and heavy, what the Lord has commanded: that if anyone wants to follow him, he should deny himself [^1]. But what he commands is not hard or heavy, since he himself helps so that what he commands may be done. For that too is true which is said to him in the Psalm: *Because of the words of your lips I have kept hard ways* [^2]. And that is true which he himself said: *My yoke is easy, and my burden is light* [^3]. For whatever is hard in the commandments, love makes it easy. We know how much love itself accomplishes. Very often even a base and wanton love accomplishes much: how many hard things men have endured, how many unworthy and unbearable things they have borne, to reach what they loved—whether it be a lover of money, who is called greedy; or a lover of honor, who is called ambitious; or a lover of beautiful bodies, who is called wanton? And who could enumerate all the loves? Yet consider how all lovers labor, and do not feel that they are laboring: and they labor most when anyone is kept from their labor. Since, then, men are of as many kinds as their loves, and there should be no other concern about how one lives than that what is to be loved be chosen rightly—why are you surprised, if the one who loves Christ and wants to follow Christ, by loving him, denies himself? For if a man perishes by loving himself, surely he is found by denying himself.

**2.** The first perdition of man was love of self. For if he did not love himself, but set God before himself, he would want to be always subject to God: he would not turn to neglecting God's will and doing his own. For this is what it is to love oneself: to want to do one's own will. Set God's will above these things: learn to love yourself by not loving yourself. For that you may know it is a vice to love oneself, the Apostle says: *For men will be lovers of self* [^4]. And does not the one who loves himself trust in himself? For he begins, having forsaken God, to love himself, and is driven away from himself toward loving the things that are outside himself: so much so that when the same Apostle had said, *Men will be lovers of self*, he immediately added, *lovers of money*. Now you see that you are outside yourself. You began to love yourself: stand within yourself, if you can. Why do you go outside? Have you been made rich with money, lover of money? You began to love what is outside yourself: you have lost yourself. So when a man's love proceeds even from himself to the things that are outside, he begins to vanish away with vain things, and to expend his strength prodigally, as it were. He is emptied out, poured out, made destitute, and feeds swine: and laboring in feeding swine, he at last remembers and says: *How many hired servants of my father eat bread, and here I am perishing with hunger* [^5]? But when he says this, what was told of that very son who poured out everything on harlots, who wanted to have in his own power what was being kept well for him with his father; who wanted to have those things at his own disposal, poured them out, and was made destitute—what is said of him? *And returning to himself* [^6]. If he returned to himself, he had gone out of himself. Because he had fallen away from himself and gone out of himself, he first returns to himself, so as to return to him from whom he had fallen away from himself. For just as by falling away from himself he remained in himself, so by returning to himself he ought not to remain in himself, lest he go out of himself again. Returning to himself, so as not to remain in himself, what did he say? *I will arise and go to my father* [^7]. See, the place from which he had fallen away from himself—he had fallen away from his father; he had fallen away from himself, he had gone out of himself toward the things that are outside. He returns to himself, and goes on to the father, where he may keep himself most safely. If, then, he had gone out of himself, and away from him from whom he had gone out, then by returning to himself, so as to go to the father, let him deny himself. What is it to deny himself? Let him not presume upon himself, let him sense that he is a man, and let him look to the prophetic saying: *Cursed is everyone who sets his hope in man* [^8]. Let him withdraw himself from himself, but not downward. Let him withdraw himself from himself, so as to cling to God. Whatever good he has, let him attribute to him by whom he was made; whatever evil he has, he himself made it for himself. The evil that is in him, God did not make; let him destroy what he made, since by it he fell away. *Let him deny himself*, he says, *and take up his cross, and follow me* [^9].

**3.** Where is the Lord to be followed? We know where he went: a very few days ago we celebrated that same feast. For he rose again, and ascended into heaven: there he is to be followed. Surely there is no cause for despair, since he himself promised—not because man can do anything. Heaven was far from us before our head had gone into heaven. Now why should we despair, if we are members of that head? There, then, he is to be followed. And who would not want to follow him to such a throne? Especially since on earth there is much labor amid fears and griefs. Who would not want to follow Christ there, where there is the highest happiness, the highest peace, perpetual security? It is good to follow him there: but we must see by what way. For the Lord Jesus was not speaking these words at the time when he had already risen from the dead. He had not yet suffered, he was about to come to the cross, about to come to dishonor, to insults, to scourges, to thorns, to wounds, to mockeries, to reproaches, to death. The way is, as it were, made rough: it makes you sluggish; you do not want to follow. Follow. What man made for himself is rough, but what Christ trod down by returning is worn smooth. For who would not want to go to exaltation? Loftiness delights everyone: but humility is the step. Why do you stretch your foot beyond yourself? You want to fall, not to climb. Begin from the step, and you have climbed. This step of humility those two disciples were unwilling to heed, who said: *Lord, command that one of us may sit at your right hand in your kingdom, and the other at your left* [^10]. They sought loftiness, they did not see the step. But the Lord showed the step. For what did he answer? *Can you drink the cup that I am about to drink* [^11]? You who seek the summit of loftiness, can you drink the cup of humility? Therefore he did not simply say, *Let him deny himself, and follow me*; but he added: *Let him take up his cross, and follow me* [^12].

**4.** What is it: *Let him take up his cross?* Let him bear whatever is burdensome: thus let him follow me. For when he begins to follow me in my conduct and my precepts, he will have many who contradict, many who forbid, many who dissuade, and this from those who are, as it were, Christ's companions. They walked with Christ, those who forbade the blind men to cry out. So whether it be threats or flatteries or any kind of hindrance, if you want to follow, turn it into a cross; bear it, carry it, do not give way. By these words of the Lord martyrdoms seem to be exhorted. If there is persecution, should not everything be despised for Christ? The world is loved: but let him by whom the world was made be preferred. The world is great: but greater is he by whom the world was made. The world is beautiful: but more beautiful is he by whom the world was made. The world is pleasant: but more delightful is he by whom the world was made. The world is evil: and good is he by whom the world was made. How shall I be able to unravel and explain what I have said? Let God help. For what have I said? what have you applauded? Behold, here is a question, and yet you have already applauded. How is the world evil, if he by whom the world was made is good? Did not God make all things, and behold, very good? Does not Scripture testify of each thing that God made it good, by saying: *And God saw that it was good?* And at the end it concluded the whole in this way, that God made all things, *and behold, very good* [^13].

**5.** How then is the world evil, and good is he by whom the world was made? How? Because *the world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^14]. The world was made through him—heaven and earth, and all that is in them: the world did not know him—the lovers of the world; the lovers of the world, and despisers of God: this world did not know him. So then the world is evil, because those are evil who prefer the world to God. And good is he who made the world, heaven and earth and sea, and even those who love the world. For only this, that they love the world and do not love God, he himself did not make in them. But themselves, as far as their nature is concerned, he himself made: as far as their fault is concerned, he himself did not make. This is what I said a little while ago: let man blot out what he himself made, and he will be pleasing to him who made him.

**6.** For there is also a good world in men themselves; but made out of an evil one. For the whole world, if you take men to be the world—apart from what we call the world, namely heaven and earth and all that is in them—if you call men the world, the one who first sinned made the whole world evil. The whole mass is corrupted at the root. God made man good: so Scripture has it: *God made man upright, and men themselves have devised many schemes* [^15]. From the many run to the one, gather the scattered into one: flow together, be fortified, remain with the one; do not go into the many. There is blessedness. But we flowed away, we went on into perdition: we were all born with sin, and to what we were born we also added by living badly, and the whole world became evil. But Christ came, and chose what he made, not what he found: for he found all evil, and by his grace made them good. And another world was made: and the world persecutes the world.

**7.** Which is the world that persecutes? The one of which it is said to us: *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. Whoever loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. For all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age, which is not from the Father but from the world. And the world passes away, and its desire. But whoever does the will of God abides forever, as God also abides forever* [^16]. Behold, I have spoken of both worlds, both the one that persecutes and the one that is persecuted. Which is the world that persecutes? *All that is in the world, the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age, which is not from the Father but from the world: and the world passes away*. Behold, this is the world that persecutes. Which is the world that is persecuted? *Whoever does the will of God abides forever, as God also abides forever*.  
But behold, the one that persecutes is called the world: let us prove whether the one that suffers persecution is also called the world. Or are you really deaf to the voice of Christ saying, or rather to the holy Scripture testifying: *God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself* [^17]? *If the world hates you*, he says, *know that it hated me first* [^18]. Behold, the world hates. Whom, except the world? Which world? *God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself*. The condemned world persecutes; the reconciled world suffers persecution. The condemned world is whatever is outside the Church; the reconciled world is the Church. *For the Son of man did not come*, he says, *to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him* [^19].  
But in this holy, good, reconciled, saved world—or rather, to be saved, but now saved in hope: *For in hope we were saved* [^20]—in this world, then, which is the Church, which as a whole follows Christ, he said universally: *Whoever wants to follow me, let him deny himself* [^21]. For this is not something that virgins ought to hear and married women ought not; or that widows ought and married women ought not; or that monks ought and the married ought not; or that clergy ought and laity ought not: rather, the whole Church, the whole body, all the members distinguished and distributed by their proper offices, should follow Christ. Let the whole follow, she the only one, let the dove follow, let the bride follow, let her follow who was redeemed and endowed by the blood of the bridegroom. Virginal integrity has its place there; widowed continence has its place there; conjugal chastity has its place there: adultery has no place there; unlawful and punishable wantonness has no place there. But those members which have their place there, each in its kind, and in its place, and in its measure, let them follow Christ; let them deny themselves, that is, let them not presume upon themselves; let them take up their cross, that is, let them endure in the world for Christ whatever the world brings on. Let them love him who alone does not deceive, who alone is not deceived, who alone does not deceive: let them love him, because what he promises is true. But because he does not give it at once, faith wavers. Endure, persevere, bear, carry the delay, and you have borne the cross.

**8.** Let the virgin not say: I alone will be there. For Mary will not be there alone, but Anna the widow will also be there. Let the married woman not say: A widow will be there, not I. For Anna will be there, and Susanna will not fail to be there. But let those who are to be there clearly prove themselves from this: that those who here have the lower place should not envy, but should love in others the better place. For example, my brothers, that you may take note: one has chosen the married life, another has chosen the continent life; if the one who has chosen the married life desires adulteries, he has looked back; he has desired what is unlawful. But the one who afterward wants to return from continence to marriage has looked back: he chose what is lawful, and he has looked back. Are marriages then to be condemned? No. Marriages are not to be condemned: but see where the one who chose them had reached. He had already gone forward. When he was living wantonly as a young man, marriage was before him; he was making for it: but when he chose continence, marriage is behind him. *Remember*, the Lord says, *Lot's wife* [^22]. Lot's wife, by looking back, remained fixed [^23]. So let each one fear to look back from where he was able to arrive: and let him walk on the way, let him follow Christ; forgetting what is behind, stretching out to what is ahead, let him follow, according to his inward purpose, to the prize of the calling of God in Christ Jesus [^24]. Let the married set the unmarried before themselves; let them confess that these are better: let them love in them what they themselves do not have; and let them love Christ in them.
