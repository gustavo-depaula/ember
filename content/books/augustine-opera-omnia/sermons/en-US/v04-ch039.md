# Sermon 123

**1.** You know, brothers: for believing in Christ you have learned it, and constantly we too in our ministry commend it to you; that the medicine for the swelling of man is the humility of Christ. For man would not have perished, unless he had swelled with pride. *For the beginning*, as Scripture says, *of all sin is pride* [^1]. Against the beginning of sin, the beginning of righteousness was necessary. If, then, the beginning of all sin is pride; from where would the swelling of pride be healed, unless God deigned to become humble? Let man blush to be proud; since God was made humble. For when it is said to a man to humble himself, he disdains it; and that men want to be avenged when they are hurt, pride makes them. When they disdain to be humbled, they want to be avenged; as though another's penalty could profit anyone. The one hurt and having suffered injury wants to be avenged; from another's penalty he seeks a medicine for himself, and acquires a great torment. Therefore in all things the Lord Christ deigned to be humbled, offering us the way: if only we deign to walk by it.

**2.** Behold, among other things the Son of the Virgin came to a wedding; he who, when he was with the Father, instituted weddings. As the first woman, through whom sin came, was made from a man without a woman; so the man, through whom sin was blotted out, from a woman without a man. Through that one we fell, through this one we rise. And what did he do at that very wedding? From water, wine. What is more powerful? He who could do such things deigned to be in need. He who made wine from water, could make bread too from stones. It was the same power: but then the devil tempted, therefore Christ did not do it. For you know that when the Lord Christ was tempted, the devil suggested this to him. For he was hungry, because he deigned even this, because even this pertained to humility. The bread hungered, as the Way failed, as Health was wounded, as Life died. When, then, he was hungry, as you know, the tempter said to him: *If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become loaves*. And he answered the tempter, teaching you to answer the tempter. For the commander fights for this, that the soldiers may learn. What did he answer? *Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*. And he did not make loaves from stones, who surely could make them so, as wine from water. For it is of the same power to make bread from stone; but he did not do it, that he might despise the will of the tempter. For the tempter is not conquered except by being despised. And when he had conquered the devil the tempter, the angels came, and ministered to him [^2]. He, then, who could do so much, why did he not do that, and did this? read, rather recall, what you just now heard, when he did this, that is, wine from water; what did the Evangelist add? *And his disciples believed in him* [^3]. Was the devil then going to believe?

**3.** He, then, who could do so much, was hungry, thirsted, was wearied, slept, was seized, beaten, crucified, killed. This is the way: walk by humility, that you may come to eternity. Christ God is the homeland to which we go; Christ man is the way by which we go. To him we go, through him we go; why do we fear lest we go astray? He did not depart from the Father, and came to us. He sucked the breasts, and held the world. He lay in the manger, and fed the angels. God and man, the same God who is man, the same man who is God. But not from what makes him man is he God. God, because the Word: man, because the Word was made flesh; both by remaining God, and by taking on the flesh of man; by adding what he was not, not losing what he was. So even now, having now suffered through that very humility, now dead, now buried, now he has risen, now ascended into heaven, there he is and sits at the right hand of the Father: and here he is needy in his poor. Yesterday too I commended this to Your Charity, on account of what he said to Nathanael: *You will see greater things than these. For I tell you, you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending to the Son of man* [^4]. We asked what it was, and spoke long; must we today too repeat the same things? Let those who were present recall: yet I recall it briefly.

**4.** He would not say: *Ascending to the Son of man*, unless he were above; he would not say: *descending to the Son of man*, unless he were also below. He himself above, he himself below; above in himself, below in his own; above with the Father, below in us. Whence too that voice to Saul: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me* [^5]? He would not say: *Saul, Saul*, except because he was above. Saul was not persecuting him above; he who was above would not say: *Why do you persecute me?* unless he were also below. Fear Christ above, acknowledge him below. Have Christ above bestowing, here acknowledge him in need. Here he is poor, there he is rich. That Christ is poor here, he himself speaks for us: *I was hungry, I thirsted, I was naked, I was a guest, I was in prison*. And to some he said: *You ministered to me*; to some he said: *You did not minister to me* [^6]. Behold, we have proved Christ poor: Christ rich, who does not know? And here too it pertained to his very riches, that he turned water into wine. If he is rich who has wine, what kind of rich is he who makes wine? So Christ is both rich and poor: as God rich, as man poor. Now too the man himself, rich, has ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father: yet still here, poor, he hungers, thirsts, is naked.

**5.** What are you? Rich, or poor? Many say to me: I am poor; and they say truly. I acknowledge a poor man having something, I acknowledge also one in need. But someone has much gold and silver. O that he might acknowledge himself poor! He acknowledges himself poor, if he acknowledges the poor man next to him. For what? However much you have, whoever you are who are rich, you are a beggar of God. We come to the hour of prayer, and there I prove you. You ask. How are you not poor, who ask? I add more: You ask for bread. Or are you not going to say: *Give us this day our daily bread* [^7]? You who ask for daily bread, are you poor, or rich? And yet Christ says to you: Give me from what I have given you. For what did you bring, when you came here? All things which I created, created yourself you found here: you brought nothing, you will take nothing hence. Why do you not give me from my own? Because you are full, and the poor man is empty. Attend to your beginnings: you were both born naked. You too, then, were born naked. Many things here you found: did you bring anything with you? I ask for my own: give, and I repay. You had me as a bestower, make me quickly a debtor. It is too little what I said: You had me as a bestower, make me a debtor; let me have you as a moneylender. You give me few things, I will repay more. You give me earthly things, I will repay heavenly. You give me temporal things, I will restore eternal. I will give yourself back to you, when I have given you back to me.
