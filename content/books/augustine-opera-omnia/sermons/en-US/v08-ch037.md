# Sermon 299/C: A Treatise on the Birthday of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul

**1.** The blessed apostles Peter and Paul were called at different times and crowned on one day. The Lord called Peter before all, Paul after all. Of the apostles Peter is first, Paul last: the first and the last brought them to one day. A most beautiful wholeness endures when the last are in concord with the first. The apostle Peter wavered on the sea: Peter alone wavered, but he alone walked on the sea. He alone denied the Lord out of fear, but he alone followed the Lord out of love even to peril. Blessed John was there too, but he relied on his friendship with the chief of the priests; for John was a friend of the chief of the priests, to whose house the Lord was led. Peter followed out of love: weakness trembled, but love wept, and weakness received pardon. But Paul, formerly Saul, was even an enemy of Christ; he persecuted the Christians most savagely. When the first martyr, holy Stephen, suffered, he was there; when he was being stoned, he guarded the garments of all who stoned. For it seemed too little to him to stone with his own hands alone; he was in the hands of all whose garments he guarded. After this, when the most blessed Stephen had been killed and crowned as the first—for his very name indicates a crown by its Greek word—this most savage enemy received letters from the chief priests, that whomever he found following that way he might lead off bound to punishment. So he went to Damascus, raging, breathing slaughter, thirsting for blood. He who dwells in the heavens, according to the psalm, laughed at him, and the Lord mocked him[^1]. Why do you breathe savagery, you who will soon suffer it yourself? How small a thing it was for the Lord Christ to save his enemy, to lay the persecutor low by a single voice from above, to raise up a preacher! *Saul*, he says, *Saul*—still Saul—*why do you persecute me?*[^2]

**2.** How great a condescension, my brethren! In the Lord's voice let us recognize ourselves. Who could still persecute Christ, seated in heaven at the right hand of the Father? But there the head reigned, here the members still labored. The blessed apostle Paul, our teacher of the gentiles, taught us what we are in relation to Christ: *You*, he says, *are the body of Christ and his members*[^3]. So the whole Christ is head and members. See it in our own body, take the likeness. If perhaps you are jostled in a crowd, and someone treads on your foot, the head cries out on behalf of the foot. And what is it that it cries? You are treading on me. *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* For when Saul was persecuting the evangelists, through whom the Lord was being carried throughout the world, the feet of Christ were being trodden on by him; for in them Christ was going to the gentiles, in them Christ was running everywhere. He who was to be the feet of Christ was treading on the feet of Christ: about to carry the Gospel of the Lord to men throughout the whole earth, he was treading on what he himself was going to be. *How beautiful are the feet*, the prophet says, and the teacher himself recalls it, *of those who announce peace, who announce good things!*[^4] This too we sang in the psalm: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth*[^5]. Do you wish to see where Christ has reached through these feet? *And their words to the ends of the world*[^6].

**3.** Then, when the Lord spoke to Ananias, when he sent him to baptize Saul, he heard from Ananias himself: *Lord, I have heard about this man, that everywhere he persecutes your servants*[^7]. As if to say: Why do you send a sheep to a wolf? For Ananias is a Hebrew word, which in Latin is interpreted as sheep. But about Saul who would become Paul, the persecutor who would become a preacher, the prophet had foretold: *Benjamin, a ravening wolf*[^8]. Whence Benjamin? Hear Paul himself: *For I too am an Israelite, of the race of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin*[^9]. A ravening wolf: *In the morning he will seize, in the evening he will divide the food*[^10]; first he will consume, afterward he will feed. For that preacher was already dividing the food, he knew what to give to whom: what nourishment to administer to the sick and weak, with what food to feed the strong. For dividing the food—dividing, not scattering at random—dividing the food he said: *And I, brethren, could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal; as to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not solid food; for you were not yet able, but neither are you able even now*[^11]. I divide, I do not scatter at random. So Ananias the sheep had heard the name of this wolf, and trembled in the hands of the shepherd; but he is frightened by the wolf, while by the shepherd he is consoled, strengthened, preserved. About this very wolf he hears things incredible, yet true and trustworthy because the Truth was speaking. For what did the Lord answer Ananias trembling at Paul's reputation, what did he answer? *Let him be now, this man is a vessel of election to me, to carry my name before gentiles and kings. I will show him how much he must suffer for my name*[^12]. *I will show him*: it is the voice of one threatening, but preparing a crown. Then what did he endure, made a preacher out of a persecutor? *In perils on the sea, perils on rivers, perils in the city, perils in the desert, perils among false brethren; in toil and hardship, in many sleepless nights, in hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness, in deaths often, besides those things that are external, the daily pressure upon me, the care of all the churches. Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn?*[^13] Behold that persecutor. Suffer, endure: you suffer more than you did; but do not be angry, you have received interest. But what was he attending to, when he endured such things? Hear it in another place: *For*, he says, *this slight momentary affliction of ours*. All this is now slight—why? *Is working for us beyond measure an eternal weight of glory, since we look not at the things that are seen, but at the things that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporal; but the things that are not seen are eternal*[^14]. So he was on fire with love of things eternal, when he bravely endured evils that, though bitter and most grievous, were yet temporal. Every punishment is light when it has an end, where a reward is promised without end.

**4.** And yet, when he endured, was it not he who endured in him and with him, who did not fail? I dare to say outright, it was not he who endured. And he did endure, because he willed it faithfully; he did not endure, because the power of Christ dwelt in him. Christ reigned, Christ supplied the strength, Christ did not abandon him, Christ ran in the one who ran, Christ led him to the palm. So I do no wrong when I say, it was not he who endured. I say it outright, I say it with confidence, and I confirm my words with his own testimony; I do not allow the holy apostle to be vexed with me, when I recite his own words to him. Say it, Paul, say it, holy one, say it, apostle; let my brethren hear you, for I have done you no wrong. What then does he himself say, comparing himself in labors with his fellow apostles? He was not afraid to say: *I labored more than all of them*[^15]. Already here it is answered me: Surely it was not he. Say then what follows, lest this delay of mine become a swelling. I labored more than all of them. You had already begun to be angry with me: but he intercedes for me, and in a way addresses you. Do not be angry: *yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^16]. So too, speaking of his impending suffering, whose most solemn day is kept today, what did he say? *For I am now being offered up, and the time of my dissolution is at hand. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith. For the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me on that day*[^17]. He will render: for there is one to whom he renders it. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith. What is rendered is owed: but there would be no one to whom it was owed, unless what was not owed had first been granted him. Surely you hear him now presuming on God's debt, you hear Christ rendering: hear through Paul himself the granting of what was not due. *I am not*, he says, *fit to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God*[^18]. Now attend to what was owed to him for whom you see a crown already being prepared; first attend to him, and see whether you do not find him deserving of punishment by his deeds. Persecuting the Church of God, of what cross is he not worthy? what torments would suffice to punish him? *I am not*, he says, *worthy to be called an apostle*. I know what was owed me: an apostolate for me, who *persecuted the Church of God!* Whence then an apostle? *But by the grace of God I am what I am*[^19]. O grace freely given! It found what to punish, but did what would crown. See what follows. *By the grace of God*, he says, *I am what I am*. For I too am not fit to be an apostle, because I *persecuted the Church of God*; I was expecting punishments, I find rewards. Whence is this to me? Because *by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace in me was not in vain, but I labored more than all of them*. Have you begun to lift yourself up again? *Yet not I, but the grace of God with me*. Well, most excellently, no longer Saul but Paul, no longer proud but small. Saul was a name of pride, because that tall king, the more envious the more exalted, was called Saul, who persecuted holy David. From that Saul this Saulus had taken his name, a name fitting for a persecutor. What afterward? Paul. What is Paul? Modest, least. Recall the word, you who know letters; recall also the custom, you who do not know the books called liberal. *Paulus* is modest. After a little while I will see you, a little after I will see you: a little after, a slight while after.

**5.** So behold Paul, not long ago Saul, thirsting for blood and breathing slaughter, but now Paul. I am the least of the apostles; least indeed, but most wholesome. Perhaps in the Lord's garment this least one was the fringe: this the woman touched, and she was freed from her flow of blood, in whom there was a figure of the Church of the gentiles. For Paul the modest was sent to the gentiles with healing. Then know this: the very woman who touched the fringe of the Lord was unknown to the Lord, but the Lord's ignorance is a figure. For what did he not know? And yet, because that woman signified the Church of the gentiles, where the Lord was not present in bodily presence, but through his disciples was there where his fringe was touched, he said: *Who touched me?*[^20] And the apostles: *The crowds press upon you, and do you say, Who touched me?*[^21] And he: *Someone touched me*[^22]. The crowds press, faith touches. Brethren, be among those who touch, not those who press. *Who touched me?* and *Someone touched me*. Christ was like one who did not know, signifying: not lying, but signifying—signifying what? *A people whom I did not know served me*[^23]. Say then, apostle, your suffering near, you who spend your labor and now exact your crown, say: *For I am now being offered up, and the time of my dissolution is at hand. I have fought the good fight*[^24]. What would the fight profit, unless victory followed? You say that you fought, say whence you conquered. Ask in another place. *Thanks be to God, who gave us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*[^25]. *I have finished the course*. You finished the course? Recall this: *it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy*[^26]. Say the other, *I have kept the faith*. You kept it, you guarded it; but: *Unless the Lord guard the city, in vain do they keep watch who guard it*[^27]. So that you might keep the faith, he helped you, he kept it in you, who said to your fellow apostle, with whom you have one day of suffering, what you read in the Gospel: *I have prayed for you, Peter, that your faith may not fail*[^28]. Demand it then, the reward is prepared. Say: *I have fought the good fight*, it is true; *I have finished the course*, it is true; *I have kept the faith*, it is true. *For the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me on that day*[^29]. Demand what is owed: your crown is prepared, fully prepared: but remember that your merits are the gifts of God.
