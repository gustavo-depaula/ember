# Sermon 167

**1. 1.** You heard the Apostle when he was read; rather, we all heard him saying to us, *See how you walk cautiously; not as the unwise, but as the wise; redeeming the time, for the days are evil*[^1]. Two things make the days evil, brothers: malice and misery. Through the malice of men and the misery of men the days are led as evil. Otherwise these days, so far as concerns the spaces of the hours, are ordered; they lead their turns, they conduct the seasons; the sun rises, the sun sets, the seasons pass. To whom are the seasons troublesome, if men are not troublesome to one another? Therefore two things make the days evil, as I said: the misery of men and the malice of men. But the misery of men is common; malice ought not to be common. For from the time Adam fell, and was expelled from paradise, there were never days but evil. Let us question these children who are born, why they begin from weeping, who could also laugh. He is born, and at once he weeps; after I know not how many days he laughs. When he wept being born, he was a prophet of his own calamity; for tears are witnesses of misery. He does not yet speak, and already he prophesies. What does he prophesy? That he will be in labor, or in fear. And if he lives well and is righteous, surely, set in the midst of temptations, he will always fear.

**2. 2.** What does the Apostle say? *All who wish to live devoutly in Christ will suffer persecution*[^2]. Behold, because the days are evil, the righteous cannot live here without persecution. Those who live among the evil suffer persecution. All the evil persecute the good—not with sword and stones, but by their life and morals. Did anyone persecute holy Lot in Sodom? No one was troublesome to him; and yet he lived among the ungodly, and among the unclean, proud, blasphemers, he suffered persecution, not by being beaten, but by their living evilly. Whoever you are who hear me, and do not yet live devoutly in Christ, begin to live devoutly in Christ, and you prove what I say. Finally, when the Apostle mentioned his perils: *In perils*, he says, *at sea, in perils on rivers, in perils in the desert, in perils from robbers, in perils from false brothers*[^3]. The other perils can be quiet, but perils from false brothers have not known how to be quiet until the end of the age.

**2. 3.** Let us redeem the time; for the days are evil. Perhaps you await from me to know what it is to redeem the time. I am going to say what few hear, few bear, few approach, few do; yet I will say it, since the very few who are going to hear me live among the evil. To redeem the time is this: when someone brings a suit against you, lose something, that you may be free for God, not for suits. Lose, then; from what you lose is the price of the time. Surely when, for your necessities, you go forth to the marketplace, you give coins, and you buy yourself bread, or wine, or oil, or wood, or some furnishing; you give and you receive, you lose something, you gain something; this is to buy. For if you lose nothing, and have what you did not have, either you found it, or received it as a gift, or acquired it by inheritance. But when you lose something to have something, then you buy; what you have is bought; what you lose is the price. As, then, you lose coins to buy yourself something; so lose coins to buy yourself quiet. Behold, this is to redeem the time.

**3. 4.** There is a well-known Punic proverb, which I will indeed say to you in Latin, because you do not all know Punic. For it is an old Punic proverb: The plague seeks a coin; give it two, and let it take itself off. Does this proverb not seem born of the Gospel? For what else did the Lord say than: *Redeeming the time*, when he said, *If anyone wishes to contend with you in judgment, and to take away your tunic, let him have your cloak too*[^4]? *He wishes to contend with you in judgment, and to take away your tunic*, he wishes to call you away from your God by suits; you will not have a quiet heart, you will not have a tranquil mind, you will be overturned by your thoughts, you will be irritated against your very adversary. Behold, you have lost the time. How much better, then, that you lose a coin, and redeem the time? My brothers, in your cases and your business, when they come to us to be judged, if I say to a Christian man that, for redeeming the time, he should lose something of his own; with how much greater care and confidence ought I to say that he should give back what is another's? For I hear both as Christians. Now that slanderous man, who wishes to make a case against another, and to take from him either as a settlement, rejoices at these words. The Apostle said: *Redeeming the time, for the days are evil*. So I make a slander against that Christian: willy-nilly, he gives me something to redeem the time, because he has heard the bishop. Tell me, if I am going to say to him: Lose something, that you may be at peace; shall I not say to you: Slanderer, lost son of the devil, why do you strive to take away others' goods? You have no case, and you are full of slander. If, then, I say to him: Give him something, that he may withdraw from the slander; where will you be, who will have money from slander? That one, who redeems the time from you to avoid slander, here endures evil days; but you who feed on slanders, here will have evil days, and after these you are going to have worse ones on the day of judgment. But perhaps you laugh at this, because you seize money. Laugh, laugh, and despise; let me pay out, there will come one to exact.

[^2]: 2 Tim 3:12
[^3]: 2 Cor 11:26
