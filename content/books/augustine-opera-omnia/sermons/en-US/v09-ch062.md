# Sermon 339: On the Day of His Ordination

**1.** This day, brothers, admonishes me to think more attentively of my burden, about whose weight, even though I must think day and night, yet in some way this anniversary day drives it home to my senses, so that I cannot dissemble at all from thinking about it. And as much as the years come on—indeed, go off—and make us nearer to the last day, which without doubt is at some time surely to come, the keener and the fuller of goads is my thought, what account I can render to the Lord our God for you. For this is the difference between each of you and us, that you are going to render an account almost of yourselves alone; but we both of ourselves, and of all of you. Therefore the burden is greater, but well carried it acquires greater glory, but unfaithfully borne it casts one down to the most monstrous punishment. What, then, must I do most of all today, but commend to you my danger, that you may be my joy? But my danger is, if I attend to how you praise, and dissemble how you live. But he knows, under whose eyes I speak, indeed under whose eyes I think, that I am not so much delighted by popular praises, as goaded and tormented, how those who praise me live. But to be praised by those living badly I do not want, I shrink from it, I detest it, it is to me grief, not pleasure; but to be praised by those living well, if I say I do not want it, I lie; if I say I want it, I fear lest I be more eager for emptiness than for solidity. So what shall I say? I neither fully want it, nor fully do not want it. I do not fully want it, lest I be in danger in human praise; I do not fully reject it, lest those to whom I preach be ungrateful.

**2.** But my burden is what you just now heard, when the prophet Ezekiel was read. For it is too little that the day itself admonishes us to think of that burden; moreover such a reading is recited, as may strike great fear into us, that we may think what we carry; because unless he who imposed it carries it with us, we fail. Behold you heard: *The land,* he says, *over which I shall bring the sword, and it sets for itself a watchman who sees the sword coming, and speaks, and announces it; but when the sword comes, that watchman is silent, and the oncoming sword kills the sinner; the sinner indeed will die for his iniquity, but his blood I will require from the hand of the watchman. But if he sees the sword coming, and sounds the trumpet, and announces it, and the one to whom he announces it does not heed it, that man indeed will die in his iniquity, but the watchman has freed his soul. And you, son of man, I have set you a watchman to the children of Israel*[^1]. He expounded what he meant by the sword, he expounded what he meant by the watchman, he expounded what death he meant; he did not permit us in the obscurity of the reading to excuse our negligence. *I have set you, then,* he says, *a watchman. If I say to the sinner: By death you will die, and you are silent, and he dies in his sin, he indeed will die in his sin worthily and justly, but his blood I will require from your hand. But if you say to the sinner: By death you will die, and he does not heed himself, he will die in his iniquity, but you have freed your soul*[^2]. And he adds the words which he wanted said to the people of Israel: *So you will say to the children of Israel: What is this that you say among yourselves: our iniquities are upon us, in our sins we waste away, how can we live? Thus says the Lord: I do not want the death of the impious, so much as that the impious turn back from his perverse way, and live*[^3]. This he willed that we announce to you. If we do not announce this, we are going to render a bad account of this watch. But if we announce it, we have done what pertains to us. Do you see to it; we are now secure. But how secure, while you are in danger and to die? We do not want our glory to be with your punishment. Security indeed is given, but love makes us anxious. Behold we say it and you know that I have always said it, you know that I have never been silent. *Thus says God: I do not want the death of the impious, so much as that the impious turn back from his worst way, and live.* What is it that the impious said? He spoke the words of the impious and the wicked: *Our iniquities are upon us, in our sins we waste away, how can we live?* The sick man despairs, but the physician promises hope. The man said to himself: *How can we live?* God says: You can live. If *every man is a liar*[^4], God alone is truthful: let him blot out what the man said, and write what God said. Do not despair, you can live, not from your past evils, but from your future goods; you will blot out your evils, if you withdraw from evil. All things, whether good or bad, are blotted out by change. From a good life you bent yourself to a bad one, you blotted out the good. See where you attend, what you take in; two treasures are prepared for you; what you send, this you will find; God is a faithful keeper, he will render to you what you have done.

**3.** But there are others, who perish not by despair; they do not say to themselves: *Our iniquities are upon us, in our sins we waste away, how can we live?* but in another way they deceive themselves: they flatter themselves about the excessive mercy of God, so that they are never corrected. For they say this: even if we do evil things, even if we commit iniquities, even if we live luxuriously and wickedly, even if we despise the poor and needy, even if we exalt ourselves in pride, even if we have no grief of heart for our evils, is God going to destroy so great a multitude, and free a few? So there are two dangers: one which we just now heard from the prophet, the other which the Apostle did not pass over in silence. For against those who by despairing die, like gladiators as it were destined for the sword, gaping after pleasures and living wickedly, despising their souls as if already condemned, he says what they say to themselves: *Our iniquities are upon us,* and *in our sins we waste away, how can we live?* But the other, whence the Apostle says: *Or do you despise the riches of his goodness and mercy and long-suffering?*[^5] Against those who say: God is good, God is merciful, he will not destroy so great a multitude of sinners and free a few; for surely if he did not want them to exist, they would not even live: when they do such great evils and live, surely if it displeased God, he would at once take them from the earth. Against these the Apostle: *Are you ignorant,* he says, *that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But you according to the hardness of your heart and your impenitent heart are storing up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath and the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works*[^6]. To whom does he say this? To those who say: God is good, he will not render. *He will render* indeed *to each according to his works.* What do you do? *You store up.* What? *Wrath.* Add wrath upon wrath, increase the treasure; what you store up will be rendered to you, he to whom you entrust it does not defraud you. But if you cast into another treasure good works, the fruits of righteousness, whether continence, or virginity, or marital chastity; if you are a stranger to fraud, to murder, to crime; if you remember the needy, because you too are needy; if you remember the poor, because you too are poor, however much you abound in riches, you are clothed in rags of flesh; if, thinking these things and doing these things, you cast into the good treasure, through the day of judgment, he who defrauds no one, and will render to each according to his works, will say to you: Take what you sent, because it abounds; when you sent it, you did not see, but I was keeping it as I was going to render it. Because truly, brothers, each one, what he sends into the treasure, because he sends it knows, nor sees when he has sent it. Put a treasure buried in the earth, having one opening or little crack, through which you may send it: gradually whatever you acquire you send, and you do not see it; if what you have sent and have not seen the earth keeps for you, will not he who made heaven and earth keep it for you?

**4.** Lighten, then, brothers, lighten my burden, and carry it with me: live well. We have today to feed our fellow poor, and with them humanity is to be shared; but for you my dishes are these words. To feed all with handleable and visible bread I am not able; I feed from where I am fed; I am a minister, not the householder; I set before you from where I too live, from the Lord's treasure, from the banquet of that householder, who *for our sake became poor, though he was rich, that by his poverty we might be enriched*[^7]. If I set bread before you, the bread broken, you would each take away your own morsels; and if I set out much, very little would reach each one. But now what I say, both all have the whole, and each one has the whole. For surely you did not divide the syllables of my word among yourselves? Surely you did not carry off the single words of the spoken sermon? Each one of you heard the whole. But let him see how he heard it, because I am a dispenser, not an exactor. If I do not dispense, and keep the money, the Gospel terrifies me. For I could say: What is it to me, to be a trouble to men? to say to the wicked: Do not act wickedly, live thus, act thus, cease to act thus? What is it to me, to be a burden to men? I have received how I may live: let me live as I am commanded, as I am ordered. Let me assign what I have received: why should I render an account of others? The Gospel terrifies me. For to that most leisured security no one would defeat me: nothing is better, nothing sweeter, than to search the divine treasure with no one making a din; it is sweet, it is good; but to preach, to convict, to rebuke, to build up, to busy oneself for each one, is a great burden, a great weight, a great labor. Who would not flee this labor? But the Gospel terrifies me. A certain servant came forward, and said to his lord: *I knew you to be a troublesome man, reaping where you did not sow*[^8]; I kept your money, I refused to dispense it: take what is yours; if anything is less, judge; if it is whole, do not be a trouble to me. But he said: *Wicked servant, out of your own mouth I condemn you*[^9]. Why this? Because you called me greedy, why did you neglect my gains? But I feared to give, lest I lose. Do you say this? For for the most part it is said: Why do you rebuke? what you say is lost on that man; he does not hear you. And I, he says, refused to give, lest I lose your money. He said to him: *You should have given my money, and I, coming, would have exacted it with interest*[^10]; I had set you as a dispenser, he says, not an exactor; you would exercise the dispensing, you would leave to me the exacting. Fearing this, then, let each one see how he receives. If I, dispensing, fear, should he who receives be secure? Let him who was bad yesterday be good today. This is my dispensing: let him who was bad yesterday be good today. He was bad yesterday, and is not dead; if he had died bad, he would have gone where he would not return from. He was bad yesterday, he lives today; let it profit him that he lives, let him not live badly. Why then does he want to add to yesterday's evil today's? Do you want to have a long life, and not want a good one? Who would bear a long evil, even a dinner? Has the blindness of the mind grown so callous, is the inner man so deaf, that he wants to have all good things, except himself? Do you want to have an estate? I deny that you want to have a bad estate. You want to have only a good wife, only a good house. Why should I run through each thing? You do not want to have a bad boot, and you want to have a bad life? As if a bad boot would harm you more than a bad life. When a bad and tight boot has hurt you, you sit, you take it off, you throw it away or mend it or change it, lest you hurt your toe, and you put it on. A bad life, by which you lose your soul, you do not care to correct. But this plainly I see whence you are deceived: a harmful boot causes pain, a harmful life causes pleasure: the one harms, the other pleases; but what pleases for a time, afterward hurts the worse; but what hurts for a time healthfully, afterward gladdens with infinite pleasure and abundant joy.

**5.** See the one enjoying and the one grieving, that rich man enjoying, and that poor man grieving: the one feasted, the other was tormented; the one was honored, surrounded by his household, the other was licked by dogs; the one was made over-full by his banquets, the other was not satisfied even with crumbs. The pleasure passed, the need passed; the good things of the rich man went by, the evils of the poor man; evils succeeded for the rich man, good things for the poor man. The things that passed by were not recalled; the things that succeeded were not lessened. The rich man burned in hell, the poor man rejoiced in the bosom of Abraham. The poor man had earlier desired a crumb from the rich man's table, the rich man afterward desired a drop from the poor man's finger. The want of the one was ended by the limit of satiety, the pleasure of the other was carried out without end by succeeding grief. To the banquets succeeded thirst, to pleasure grief, to purple fire. This is the dinner which Lazarus was seen to have in the bosom of Abraham; this we want all of you to have, this we want us to have together. For what would the dinner be that I might offer you, if I invited all of you, and this church were full of tables of feasters? These things are passing. Think on these things which I say, that you may come to those banquets which you will never end. For there no one is undigested by feasting; nor are those banquets such that they feed us by their corruption, and refresh us by their failing; and they will be whole, and we are refreshed by them. If our eye is fed by light, and the light does not fail, what will those banquets be, in the contemplation of truth, in the sight of eternity, in the praises of God, in the security of happiness, with the mind stable, the body immortal, our flesh wasting with no old age, no hunger wearying our soul? There no one grows, no one decreases; there no one is born, because no one dies; there you are not compelled to do any works, which now we exhort you to do.

**6.** For just now you heard the Lord saying, he said it to all of us: *When you make a feast, do not call your friends*[^11]—he showed where you should be generous: do not your kinsmen, who have whence to repay you—*but call the poor, the weak, the blind, the lame*[^12], the needy, who have not whence to repay you. And surely you will not lose? *It will be repaid to you in the repayment of the righteous*[^13]. Do you dispense, he says, I receive, I note it down, I render it. God said this, and he exhorted us to do these things, and to render it to us. When he has rendered it to us, who will take it from us? *If God is for us, who is against us?*[^14] We were sinning, and he gave us the death of Christ; we live justly, and will he deceive us? For *Christ* died not for the righteous, but *for the impious*[^15]. If he gave to the wicked the death of his Son, what does he keep for the righteous? What does he keep for them? He has nothing better to keep for them, than what he gave for them. What did he give for them? *He did not spare his own Son*[^16]. What does he keep for them? The Son himself, but as God to be enjoyed, not as man to die. Behold where God calls you. But as you attend to where, deign to attend to by what way too, deign to attend to how too. But surely, when you have come, will this be said to you: Break your bread for the hungry; if you see one naked, clothe him? Surely this chapter will be recited to you: *When you make a feast, call the lame, the blind, the needy, the destitute?* There no one will be destitute, there no one lame, no one blind, no one weak, no one a stranger, no one naked; all healthy, all vigorous, all abounding, all clothed with eternal light. Whom do you see there a pilgrim? It is itself our homeland: here we are pilgrims, let us desire it. Let us do the commands, that we may exact the promises, let us take the things freely given. For if we exact them, as though God did not want to give: he will plainly give them, he will defraud no one. Consider this, my brothers, see how great goods the Lord our God gives to the bad: light, life, health, fountains, fruits, generations, for the most part honors, heights, powers; all these good things he gives both to the good and to the bad. Do we think there is nothing he keeps for the good, who gives such great good things even to the bad? Let no one bring this into his heart. My brothers, great good things God keeps for the good, but *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^17]. You cannot think it before you receive it. You can see it when you have received it; to think it before you receive it you cannot. What is it that you want to see? . . . It is no lyre, no flute, no sound, that makes pleasure in the ears. What do you want to think? *It has not ascended into the heart of man.* And what do I do? I neither see, nor hear, nor think, what do I do? Believe. A great means, a great vessel, where you can receive a great gift, is faith. Prepare yourself a vessel, because you are to approach a great fountain: prepare yourself a vessel. What is: Prepare? Let your faith grow, let your faith be increased, let your faith be strengthened; let your faith not be slippery and muddy, let your faith not be broken by the tribulations of this world but baked by them. But when you have done this, and have faith as a fit, capacious, firm vessel, God will fill it. For he will not say to you, as men say to one begging and saying: Give me some wine, I beg you; and he: I give it, come and I do it. The other brought a jar, and says: As you had bidden I came; and he: I thought you were going to bring one little flask: what have you brought, or where have you come? I am not able to give so much: take back the larger vessel you brought, and bring something small; bring me something to which my poverty can suffice. God does not say this: he is full, you will be full, and when he has filled you, he himself will have as much as he had before he filled you. Abundant are the gifts of God, you find no such on earth; believe, and you will prove it, but not now. And when, you will say? *Wait for the Lord, act manfully, and let your heart be strengthened*[^18]; that when you have received it you may say: *You have put gladness into my heart*[^19].

**7.** *Wait for the Lord, act manfully, and let your heart be strengthened, and wait for the Lord*[^20]. What is: *Wait for the Lord?* That you may receive when he gives, not exact when you want. The time of giving is not yet: he has waited for you, do you wait for him. What is it that I said: He has waited for you, do you wait for him? If you now live justly, if you are now turned to him, if your past deeds displease you, if you have now been pleased to choose a good new life, do not hasten to exact; he waited for you, that you might change your bad life; do you wait for him, that he may crown the good life. If he did not wait for you, there would be no one to whom he might give; so wait, because you have been waited for. But you who do not want to be corrected, O whoever you are here who still do not want to be corrected—as if there were one! rather I ought to have said: Whoever you are here, if indeed you are here, who have resolved to be corrected; yet I will so speak as though to one—whoever does not want to be corrected, what do you promise yourself? Do you perish by despairing, or by hoping? Whoever you are who perish by despairing, you say this in your soul: My iniquity is upon me, in my sins I waste away, what hope is there for me of living? Hear the prophet saying: *I do not want the death of the impious, so much as that the impious turn back from his worst way, and live*[^21]. Do you perish by hoping? What is, you perish by hoping? You say this in your soul: God is good, God is merciful, he forgives all things, he will not render evil for evil. Hear the Apostle saying: *Are you ignorant that the patience of God leads you to repentance?*[^22] What then remains? As if you have done something if what I said has entered your heart. I see what is answered me: It is true, and I do not despair, so as to perish by despairing; nor do I hope wrongly, so as to perish by hoping. I do not say to myself: My iniquity is upon me, I now have no hope; nor do I say that to myself: God is good, he will render evil to no one; I say neither this nor that; the prophet presses me, the Apostle presses me. And what do you say? Still a little time I will live as I want; afterward, when I have corrected myself—surely it is true what the prophet says: *I do not want the death of the impious, so much as that the impious turn back from his worst way, and live*—when I am converted, he will blot out all my evils: why do I not add something to my pleasures, and live as long as I want, as I want, intending afterward to turn to God? Why do you say this, brother, why? Because God promised pardon, if I change. I see, I know, he promised pardon; through the holy prophet he promises it, and through me his least servant he promises it. It is true what he promises: he promised it through his Only-begotten. But why do you want to add bad days to bad days? Sufficient for the day is its own evil: yesterday was a bad day, today is bad too, tomorrow will be bad too. Or do you think they are good days, when you satisfy your pleasures, when you nourish your heart in luxury, when you plot against another's chastity, when you sadden your neighbor by fraud, when you deny things entrusted to you, when you swear falsely for a coin, when you set out for yourself a good dinner? Do you think because of this that you lead a good day? How can it come about that a day is good, when the man is bad? Do you want to add bad days to bad days? I ask, he says, that a little be remitted to me. Why? Because God promised me pardon. But that you will live tomorrow no one has promised you. Or read to me, as you read to me the prophet, the Gospel, the Apostle, that when you are converted, God blots out all your iniquities: read to me where tomorrow is promised you, and live badly tomorrow. Although, my brother, I ought not to have said this to you. Perhaps your life will be long; if it will be long, let it be good; why do you want to have a long life, and a bad one? Or it will not be long: and that long life ought to delight you, which has no end. Or it will be long: and what evil will there be, that you have lived well a long time? You want to live badly a long time, you do not want to live well; and yet no one has promised you tomorrow. Correct yourself: hear from Scripture. Lest you despise me as a man making a birthday speech, I speak to you from Scripture: *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord*[^23]. These words are not mine, but they are mine too; if I love them, they are mine; love them and they are yours. This sermon, which I now speak, is Holy Scripture: if you despise it, it is your adversary. But hear the Lord saying: *Agree with your adversary quickly*[^24]. What is this terrible sermon? You came to gladness, today is called the bishop's birthday: should I set out anything by which I sadden you? Indeed I set out this, that the lover may rejoice, the despiser be angry; it is better for me to sadden the despiser, than to defraud the faithful.

**8.** Let all hear, I recite words from Scripture. O bad delayer, O bad seeker of tomorrow, hear the Lord saying, hear Holy Scripture foretelling. From this place I am a watchman. *Do not delay to be converted to God, nor put it off from day to day*[^25]. See whether he did not see those, see whether he did not look upon those, who say: Tomorrow I live well, today I will live badly. And when tomorrow comes, you will say this. *Do not delay to be converted to God, nor put it off from day to day; for suddenly his wrath will come, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^26]. What have I done? Can I blot it out? I fear to be blotted out. Can I be silent about it? I fear to be silent. I am compelled to preach, terrified I terrify. Fear with me, that you may rejoice with me. *Do not delay to be converted to God.* Lord, see that I say it: Lord, you know that you terrified me, when your prophet was read. Lord, you know in that chair my trembling, when your prophet was read. Behold I say: *Do not delay to be converted to God, nor put it off from day to day; for suddenly his wrath will come, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you.* But I do not want him to destroy you; I do not want you to say to me: I want to perish; because I do not. It is better: I do not want my own, than: I want yours. If your lethargic father were sick in your hands, and you, a young man, were present to the sick old man, and the physician said: Your father is in danger, this sleep is a kind of deadly heaviness: watch him, do not let him sleep; if you see him sleeping, rouse him; if it is too little to rouse him, pinch him; and if this too is too little, goad him, lest your father die; you would be present, you would be a young man burdensome to the old man. He, dissolving into a sweet sickness, would close his eyes pressed by that heaviness; you, on the contrary: Do not sleep. And he: Let me be, I want to sleep; and you: But the physician said: If he wants to sleep, let him not sleep. And he: I beg, let me be, I want to die. But I do not want it, you the son say to your father. To whom? Surely to one wishing to die. And yet you want to defer the death of your father, and to live somewhat longer with your dying old father. The Lord cries out to you: Do not sleep, lest you sleep forever; wake up, that you may live with me, that you may have a Father whom you never carry to the grave. And you are deaf.

**9.** What have I done, the watchman? I am free, I do not burden you. I know some will say: What did he want to say to us? He terrified us, burdened us, made us guilty. Rather, I wanted to free you from guilt. It is foul, it is base, that I should deceive you, if God does not deceive me. The Lord threatens death to the impious, the most wicked, defrauders, the criminal, adulterers, seekers of pleasures, his despisers, those murmuring about the times, and not changing their conduct; the Lord threatens them death, threatens hell, threatens everlasting destruction. What do they want, that I should promise what he does not promise? Behold the steward will give you security: what does it profit you, if the householder does not accept it? I am a steward, I am a servant. Do you want me to tell you: Live as you want, the Lord will not destroy you? The steward gave you security: the security of the steward is worth nothing. Would that the Lord gave it to you, and I made you anxious! For the security of the Lord is worth it, even if I am unwilling; but mine is worth nothing, if he is unwilling. But what is security, brothers, either mine or yours, except that we attentively and diligently hear the commands of the Lord, and faithfully await his promises? In those things in which we are wearied, because we are men, let us implore his help, to him let us groan; let our prayers not be for worldly things, passing, transitory and vanishing like vapor; but let our prayers be for fulfilling righteousness itself, and for sanctification for the name of God; not for conquering a neighbor, but for conquering lust; not for satisfying, but for taming avarice. From this let our prayers be: let them help us within as we struggle, and crown us as we conquer.
