# Sermon 130/A: (Dolbeau 19)

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, who called himself bread, seeks the hungry. But nothing hungers for this bread except the health of the mind, that is, of the inner belly. From this visible bread take a likeness. Since sick men, who through disease have contracted a distaste, can praise the best bread but cannot eat it, so too the inner languor of the inner man is not moved to eat this heavenly bread, since it is pressed by distaste, and, although it praises it, is not delighted to take it. But the Lord himself says, as we have heard: *Work for the food that does not perish, but that remains unto eternal life*[^1], distinguishing it from this visible and bodily food, of which in another place: *Everything that enters the mouth goes into the belly and is cast out into the privy*[^2]—therefore it perishes. *Work,* then, *for the food that does not perish,* he says, *but that remains unto eternal life.* This food he called bread and showed that he himself was the bread. But what is it to work for this food, but to eat this food? For if this food is bread, likewise it is Christ. Who of us works Christ, who of us makes Christ, but by doing what Christ commanded? *You are,* says the apostle, *the body of Christ and its members*[^3]. Let us work Christ, that is, let us work this food.

**2.** Well did those who asked and were listening hear for us and ask for us. *What shall we do,* they say, *that we may work the work of God?* And with that wonderful and great brevity: *This is,* he says, *the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*[^4]. This is brief: if you weigh it, it is great. It is quickly said, not easily accomplished. Someone of so great a multitude of those standing around says to me, he says: "Who of us is there who has not believed in Christ?" If, then, we have all believed in Christ, we no longer find what to be admonished about. Why? Because *this is the work of God.* Do you seek anything else but to do the work of God and to await the reward of God? Why, then, do you trouble yourself, why do you busy yourself through the great pages of Scripture and labor by running through them to seek and find how you may do the work of God? See, you have, in your Lord speaking to you, truth and brevity: do not stretch yourself out, do not sweat, do not labor, do not grow hot. *This is the work of God, that you believe,* he says, *in him whom he sent.* Where, then, is what James admonishes, saying: *Do you believe that God is one? You do well. The demons too believe and tremble?*[^5] Do not think, he says, that you do something great by believing that God is one: *The demons too believe and tremble.* But perhaps the demons have no belief about the Son of God? And what is it that they say to him: *We know who you are?*[^6] Or do they perhaps say it lying? do they say what they do not know? See, in another place more plainly: *You are the holy one of God,* they say, even *You are the Son of God* they say. When Peter had said this, he heard from the Lord: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven. And I say to you, Simon Bar-Jona, that you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church*[^7]. On account of what was all this? Because he said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^8]. This the demons too said, to whom it was said: *Be silent*[^9]. Who would easily distinguish these things, why an equal voice is not praised, but because the heart was unequal? Understand, then, dearly beloved, as we admonish, what you daily read and say. The demons too believed that Christ existed and was the Son of God. They believed Christ, but did not believe in Christ. Now the whole intent of our sermon rests on explaining, as much as we can, if with the Lord's help we can, what it is to believe in Christ.

**3.** For *this is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent.* He did not say "believe him" or "believe him to be," but *believe in him.* We have heard the words of the prophets: we believe them, but we do not believe in them. We have heard the apostles preaching: we believe their preaching, but we do not believe in them. We do not believe in Paul, but we believe Paul. For he repels from himself certain men who wished to place their hope in him and as it were to believe in him, that they should not believe in him, but he with them in Christ. *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in his name?*[^10] Finally, not only the apostles and holy teachers, but even we ourselves, not to be compared even to the least footprints, daily say: "Believe me," but we never dare to say: "Believe in me." "Believe me": who does not say it? "Believe in me": who says it, or who is not mad who says it? In whom, then, must one believe? Of whom the same Paul says: *To the one who believes in him who justifies the impious, his faith is reckoned unto justice*[^11]. Therefore Paul does not *justify the impious,* for then you would believe in him and your *faith* would be reckoned *unto justice: in him,* namely, you would believe *who justifies the impious.* For when you do not believe in him—for Paul does not *justify the impious,* nor Elijah, nor any angel, but the just one of the just, the holy one of the holy, of whom it is said: *That he himself be just and justifying the one who is of faith*[^12]. Of you it can be said that you be just; it is never heard that you also be justifying. For what is justifying, but making just? As vivifying is making alive, as saving is making safe, so justifying is making just. Who is it that makes just? He who came just without sin. Who is it that makes just? He who was not made just here, but came just. See in whom we believe, that we may do the work of God, because *this* itself *is the work of God,* to believe *in him who justifies the impious.*

**4.** "It suffices me, then," someone says; "I have believed in Christ: why do you admonish me further? *This is the work of God:* let him who promised the reward exact nothing else of me. For he who promises a reward commands me what to do, and pledges what I shall receive. He circumscribes, then, the boundary of the work, when I have burned with love of the reward. He himself says to me: 'Do you wish to live forever?', on account of the one who asked: *What shall I do, that I may have eternal life?*[^13], 'do you wish to live forever? *Do this, and you will live*[^14].' When I ask: *What shall I do?*, he answers most briefly: '*This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent.*'" Why then to that rich man: *If you wish to come to life, keep the commandments*[^15], and when he said: "*Which commandments?*", the precepts of the law were enumerated: *You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false witness, honor your father and mother, and you shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^16]? Could he not have answered him too with that most brief summary, when he said *What shall I do, that I may have eternal life?*: "Believe in me"? For *this is the work of God,* since he defined it above, to which he added nothing. *This is the work of God*—what more do you seek?—*that you believe in him whom he sent.* To that man commandments are given, and many; to us it is said: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent.* Or did the Lord perhaps deal graciously with us; did he wish to burden that man, not relieve him? Far from it! Let us, then, understand, if we can—which we promised we would explain, as we can—what it is to believe in Christ, and let us do this, let us work this, let us daily make progress in this, let us approach this from day to day, until by approaching we arrive. For this we promised even at the beginning of our faith, that we should begin in some part, and, when we have completed it, nothing more will be exacted of us. For *this is the work of God,* nor is there any other work of God, but *that we believe in him whom he sent.*

**5.** And since a little before we distinguished that it is one thing to believe him, to believe him to be, and to believe in him—to believe him is to believe that the things he speaks are true, to believe him to be is to believe that he himself is the Christ, to believe in him is to love him—now examine these things of yours: to believe that the things he speaks are true, to believe that he himself is the Christ, to love Christ. To believe that the things he speaks are true, many even of the bad can do. For they believe that they are true and are unwilling to do them: they are sluggish to act. But to believe that he himself is the Christ, this even the demons could do. Peter, then, said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,* by loving; the demons said this by fearing. But not everyone who fears loves; everyone who loves fears. But perhaps—indeed not perhaps, but surely—everyone who loves fears, but with a chaste fear—for *the fear of the Lord is chaste, remaining forever and ever*[^17]—not with a servile fear. For *perfect love casts out fear*[^18]. It is one thing to fear lest he come, another to fear lest he forsake. For to fear lest he come was in those who said: *We know who you are. Why have you come to destroy us before the time?*[^19] But the fear lest he forsake was in the one who had said: *I will be with you unto death*[^20]. When, then, you hear: "Believe in Christ," let it be too little for you to believe Christ, that is, that the things Christ speaks are true; let it be too little for you to believe Christ to be, that is, that he himself is the one whom God foretold through the prophets; but believe in Christ, that is, love Christ. When you have fulfilled this, nothing more will be exacted of you, since *love is the fullness of the law*[^21]. When you have so believed in Christ that you love Christ with that ardor, see whether these will not be your words: *Who shall separate us from the love of God?*[^22] Do not, then, long seek how to do what Christ commands: you cannot fail to do it, if you love Christ. Love, and you do it. As much as you love, so much you do; as much as you do less, so much you love less. Fulfill love, and you complete the working. See how truly it was said: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent,* that is, that by loving you may go into him, that is, be incorporated into him.

**6.** For someone can answer me and admonish me to speak more cautiously: "You said that to believe in Christ is to love Christ, and you said that we ought to believe in Christ, but not to believe in Paul; ought we not, then, to love Paul?" Therefore, just as we distinguished between believing and believing, so this loving too must be distinguished. I love Paul, and I do not believe in Paul. I do not love Christ, unless I have believed in Christ. For I love Paul, but I do not—by loving—go into Paul: I shall be with Paul, I shall not be in Paul. *For what is Apollos, what is Paul? Ministers through whom you believed*[^23], not in whom you believed. Therefore, brethren, it is a work to believe in God, by believing and loving to go into Christ. *Believe in God,* says Christ himself, *and believe in me*[^24]. Which prophet dared to say this, which patriarch, which apostle, which martyr, which angel? Therefore let us believe in him, let us love him, but by loving let us be incorporated into him. Let us understand that our God was even made man for us, and that from the fact that God was made man he justifies us. For if he were only a man, he would have to be justified along with us, he would not be the justifier. But now, because he is our God and on account of nature was made what he was not, on account of possession remained what he was, so that in him we might have both whereby to go and whither to come. Let us believe in him, that is, let us love him as God, so that by loving we may go into him, from whom by neglecting we had withdrawn.

**7.** But if this be in us, the bread delights: there is now no distaste; we do not praise and abhor, but we eat in silence even the praised bread, because we love it, the distaste removed, because the languor healed. For what will our soul render him, the soul that says: *What shall I render to the Lord?*, that says: *Bless the Lord, O my soul, and forget not all his recompenses, who is gracious to all your iniquities?*[^25] This was done when we were washed. But what follows is done: *Who heals all your weaknesses*[^26]. For as the weaknesses are healed, so much does the heavenly food delight; so much does the bread of life that came down from heaven delight, as our weaknesses are healed. But by whom are they healed, but the one to whom we said: *Convert us, God of our salvations?*[^27] *Convert us:* you convert us, because, before you heal, we are averse and unwilling to touch the praised bread out of distaste. We are averse when languid, we are converted when healed. *Convert us,* then, *God of our salvations, and turn away your anger from us*[^28]. For the languor itself is the anger of God. *He who believes in the Son has eternal life*[^29]. Why? Because he has worked the work of God. *For this is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent. But he who is unbelieving toward the Son will not have life, but the anger of God remains upon him*[^30]. It does not come upon him, but *remains upon him.* He is forsaken, not healed. Attending, then, the one who said: *Convert us, God,* because we cannot be converted except insofar as we are healed, added: *of our salvations.* For this bread does not delight one who is not healthy. As if, then, he brought forward a reason whence we may be converted. What is: *Convert us?* Heal us. For because we are not healthy, we are less converted to the bread of salvation. We praise, but we are not delighted. Who does not approve, who does not say: "It is true," when I have said: *What you do not wish to be done to you, do not do to another?*[^31] "It is true": you cry out, and you cry out nothing truer. Eat what you praise. You wish to deceive a man, you wish to be deceived by no one. You wish to grow rich from another's evil, you do not wish anyone to grow rich from your evil. Do you not set up a law against yourself, and is your heart not prescribed by which you may be punished? Why do you not wish it to be done to you, if it is good? Why do you do it, if it is evil? What will you answer, what will you say? "It delights me to do it and it delights me to do it," "this delights and this delights": these are the words of one languishing. Cry out: *Convert us, God of our salvations, and turn away your anger from us.*

**8.** For in that very psalm this too was said: *The Lord will give sweetness, and our earth will give its fruit*[^32]. *But the fruit of the Spirit is love*[^33]. Whence this fruit, unless the Lord give sweetness, by which *the love of God has been poured out in our hearts?* Not from ourselves, but *by the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^34]. You see that the Lord will give sweetness, that our earth may give its fruit. You see that our earth, that is, our heart, our soul, our earth, does not give its fruit, unless God rains. *The earth was moved*[^35]. It was moved to conceive and bring forth. But why moved? *For the heavens dropped down* not of themselves, but *from the face of God*[^36]. *For what is Apollos, what is Paul? Ministers of God through whom you believed*[^37]. They are clouds of God: unless they are filled, they do not pour. *The earth,* then, *was moved*—with God *of our salvations* converting it—*the earth was moved, for the heavens dropped down from the face of God.* By God it was moved, for it would not be moved, except by the *voluntary rain,* as there follows. After it was said: *The earth was moved, for the heavens dropped down from the face of God,* lest you think that very rain of the heavens was not God's, *a rain,* he says, *voluntary,* not owed—for what did he owe us, but punishment? *A voluntary rain,* then—because *with the shield,* he says, *of your good will you have crowned us*[^38], not by the brilliance of our merits, but *with the shield of your good will—a voluntary rain,* then, *setting apart, God, for your inheritance, and it was weakened*[^39]. She who gives birth is also weakened. *For the earth was moved* to bring forth, nor would she bear, unless weakness preceded. *But you perfected her*[^40].

**9.** What is *it was weakened?* She did not presume on herself. What is *it was weakened?* She hoped wholly in you. What is *it was weakened?* *When I am weak, then I am powerful*[^41]. Therefore *it was weakened,* she understood that it was of the grace of God, not of her own merits, of her own strength; she understood, *it was weakened,* and lost presumption, that she might receive the blessing. *It was weakened.* Let her, then, not presume on herself, let her, weak, cry to the Lord: *Convert us, God of our salvations.* There follows, then: *And it was weakened, but you perfected her.* Why *you perfected her?* Because she herself *was weakened,* because she understood that she could not be perfected by herself. Therefore because *she was weakened, you perfected her.* That very earth cries out in Paul: *On account of which I asked the Lord, that he would take it from me*[^42], namely the corrective sting of my flesh. For why did he say it was applied to him, except for the sake of restraining his own presumption and strength? *For by the greatness of the revelations,* he says, *lest I be exalted, there was given to me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me*[^43]. Therefore be weakened. The voluntary rain has been set apart for you, not owed. Cry out from weakness, because *I am not worthy to be called an apostle*[^44]. Therefore be weakened, that he may perfect you. For he said to you as you prayed: *My grace is sufficient for you, strength is perfected in weakness*[^45].

**10.** So the same apostle himself, in the reading we heard, concluded thus: *That I may be found in him.* How *in him?* *Not having my own justice*[^46]. For if your own, not *in him.* What is *not having my own?* As if gained by me, because fulfilled by my own strength. *Not having my own justice which is from the law, but that which is through faith*[^47]. How *my own,* if it is *from the law?* A troublesome question, but we are now wearied. Accordingly, if I say anything too little, let him whose we are complete it. *And may I be found in him not having my own justice.* If he did not add: *which is from the law,* who would not think that he was saying this: *Not having my own justice,* as if that which comes from human teaching? Whence the Lord Christ said in another place: *My teaching is not my own*[^48], that is, human, it is not of this visible substance which you see. For how is it not his, since the teaching of the Father is his Word? And who but Christ is his Word? Would men, then, think the apostle said this, when he says: *May I be found in him not having my own justice,* unless he added: *which is from the law, but the justice which is from the faith of Jesus Christ?*[^49] Why? Did not Christ also give the law? If you had the *justice which is from the law,* how would you have your own, since you did not give yourself the law, but received it from Christ, from the Son of God? For Christ did not begin to be from the virgin, but from the Father before the mother whom he created so that he might be created from her, when he made that from which he might be made. *For "Mother Sion," said a man, "and a man was made in her." And*—he who *was made a man in her*—*the Most High himself founded her*[^50]. He himself, then, made her, in whom he might be made. He himself, then, was in the time of Moses, the one who gave the law, because surely Moses, with many intervening steps, was from Abraham; but Christ says: *Before Abraham, I am*[^51]. It is too little to say *before Abraham,* before all the angels, before heaven and earth, before all creation: *for all things were made through him*[^52].

**11.** What, then, is *that I may be found in him not having my own justice,* and he added: *which is from the law?* I will say it, if I can briefly; the giver of grace will perfect it in your hearts. What is it, but that the Jews so had that very law that they did the works of the law out of fear of damnation, not out of the fire of love? Therefore, because they did it out of servile fear, they were servants. But if you begin to do out of love what is written in the law: *You shall not covet,* whence is this to you, but that *the love of God has been poured out* in your heart, not from yourself, but *by the Holy Spirit who was given* to you? Since *setting apart a voluntary rain, God, for your inheritance, and it was weakened,* it would hope nothing of itself. For it was said to it: "Do," and it did; "Do not kill," and it did not kill; "Do not commit adultery with another's wife," and it did not commit adultery. It was said: "Do," and it did. It was said: "Love what you do," *and it was weakened.* You do not kill your enemy out of fear: if impunity were granted, would you spare him? Would you do what David did, to whom the Lord so gave his enemy into his hands that he might do to him what he wished? He chose to spare, he chose to spare the one whom he could kill with impunity. If, then, impunity were granted you for killing your enemy, would you spare him? How would you spare him from dying, of whom you say: "O that he would die"? Therefore the law imposed on one fearing servilely forbade the evil deed, it did not root out the evil desire. You restrain yourself from the deed, but you have the desire of doing it. Why have you the desire of doing it? Because you have not the love of justice. Therefore there is nothing more to be forbidden you. Love. What shall I do, that I may love? &lt;...&gt; Therefore by love, because, if you do it out of fear, you do not love. It will be your justice, although from the law, because you do not do what the law forbids, yet your own: for you do not do it out of fear, not out of love. When will it not be your justice? If it be from the faith of Christ. For by believing in him you have obtained what he commands.

**12.** This the Scripture of the present psalm attends to. Does not God say: *Be converted to me?* The Scriptures are full of it. *Be converted to me, be converted to me.* For the languor began to be moved. For what is: *Be converted to me?* Not—which is easily done—that, you who were attending to the west, attend to the east. Would that you might do this within, because this is not easy! You turn the body from hinge to hinge: turn the heart from love to love. *Be converted to me:* God cries out. The earth feared, lest, unconverted, it be condemned; it moved itself, that it might be converted: the languor did not follow, the bread did not delight the sick. *Be converted to me.* The languor saw it could not do what was commanded, and cried out through the prophet: *Convert us, God;* faith entered the heart; it asked that what was commanded be done, it said: *Convert us, God.* Because *it was weakened, but you perfected her, that I may be found in him not having my own justice which is from the law, but* the justice of Christ *which is from faith.* What is *from faith?* That which it obtained by asking, as much as it could not under the one commanding. *For everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^53]. This is the justice of faith which we preach; *this is the living bread that came down from heaven*[^54], healing that it may be eaten, strengthening because it is eaten, fattening because it is desired, since of it it was said to our soul that *he satisfies your desire with good things*[^55].
