# Sermon 287: On the Birthday of Saint John the Baptist, Which Is June 24

**1. 1.** The account is long, but the listener’s effort is repaid by the sweetness of the truth. When the holy Gospel was read, we heard of the illustrious birth of most blessed John, Christ’s herald and forerunner. Let Your Charity consider from this how great a man was born. The Church has celebrated the birthday in the flesh of none of the prophets, none of the patriarchs, none of the apostles. It celebrates only two birthdays: John’s and Christ’s. The very times at which the two were born foreshadow a great mystery. John was a great man, but a man. He was so great a man that whoever was greater than he must be God. *He who comes after me is greater than I*. John said this: *He is greater than I.* If he is greater than you, how is it that we heard him, the one greater than you, say, *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*? If no human being is greater than you, what is he who is greater than you? Do you want to hear who he is? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.*

**1. 2.** And how did God the Word of God, through whom all things were made, who was begotten without a beginning in time, through whom time was made, find a birthday within time? How, I ask, did the Word through whom time was made find a birthday within time? Do you ask how? Hear the Gospel itself: *The Word became flesh and dwelt among us*. Christ’s birthday is the birthday of his flesh, not of the Word; yet it is the Word’s birthday precisely because *the Word became flesh.* The Word was born, but was born in the flesh, not in himself. In himself he is indeed from the Father, but he has no birthday within time.

**2. 3.** John was born; Christ too was born. John was announced by an angel; Christ was announced by an angel. Each was a great miracle. A barren woman bears the servant and forerunner by an aged husband; a virgin bears the Lord and master without a husband. John was a great man, but Christ was more than a man, because he was both man and God. John was a great man, but man had to be humbled so that God might be exalted. Since man had to be humbled, listen to the man himself: *I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandal*. If he had said he was worthy, how greatly he would have humbled himself! Yet he said he was not even worthy of this. He prostrated himself completely, and prostrated himself beneath the rock. For he was a lamp, and he feared being extinguished by the wind of pride.

**3. 4.** Finally, both their birthdays and the manner of their sufferings showed that every human being, and therefore John too, had to be humbled before Christ, and that Christ, God and man, had to be exalted. John was born today; from today the days grow shorter. Christ was born on December 25; from that day the days grow longer. In his suffering John was diminished by the loss of his head; Christ was raised up on the wood. How well he was announced to the Virgin Mary, how truthfully, how holily! *How will this happen, since I do not know a man*? She believed, but asked about the manner. And what did she hear? *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High*—the Holy Spirit himself, that is—*the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore the holy one to be born of you will be called the Son of God*. *The power of the Most High will overshadow you.* There will be conception within you, but no lust of concupiscence. There will be no heat where the Holy Spirit casts his shade. But because our bodies feel the heat, let this suffice for Your Charity. Well considered, these few words will yield much more.
