# Sermon 53/A: On the Eight Sayings of the Beatitudes from the Gospel

**1.** Your love has heard the holy Gospel with us: as we speak to you of that very chapter which was read, may the Lord help us, that the things we say may be fit for you and bear fruit in your ways. For every hearer of the word of God ought to think that what he hears ought to come about; nor let him seek to praise the word of God with the tongue and despise it in his life. For if the things said are sweet when heard, how much sweeter ought they to be when done? For we are like sowers, and you are God's field: let the seeds not perish, let the harvest bear fruit. You heard the Lord Christ with us, when his disciples had come to him: *Opening his mouth, he taught them, saying: Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^1], and the rest. As his disciples came to him, then, the one and true Master taught these things, saying what we have briefly mentioned. And you have come to us, that with his help we may speak to you, and teach you. What better do we do than when we teach the things which so great a Master, expounding, said?

**2.** Be, then, poor in spirit, that yours may be the kingdom of heaven. Why do you fear to be poor? Think of the riches of the kingdom of heaven. Poverty is feared: let it be feared, but iniquity. For after the poverty of the just there will come a great happiness, because full security; but here, the more the riches that are so called and are not are increased, both fear is increased and desire is not ended. You can give me many rich men: can you give me one secure man? He burns to acquire; he trembles lest he lose. When is such a servant free? He is a servant who serves any mistress whatever: and is he free who serves avarice? *Blessed*, then, *are the poor in spirit.* What is *poor in spirit*? Poor in their wills, not in their means. For he who is poor in spirit is humble: and God hears the groans of the humble, and does not despise their prayers. From there the Lord began to commend his sermon, from humility, that is, from poverty. You find a religious man, abounding in these earthly things, yet not puffed up with pride. You find a needy man, having nothing, and sitting in nothing. This one has no more hope than that one: for that one is poor in spirit, because he is humble; but this one is poor, and not in spirit. Therefore the Lord Christ, when he had said: *Blessed are the poor*, added: *in spirit.* Whoever of you, then, have heard us and are poor, do not seek to be rich.

**3.** Hear the Apostle, not me; see what he said: *But godliness with contentment*, he says, *is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world, nor can we take anything away; having food and covering, let us be content with these. For those who wish to become rich*—he did not say "those who are," but "those who wish to become"—those, *then, who wish to become rich, fall into temptation*, he says, *and into a snare, and many foolish and harmful desires, which plunge men into ruin and perdition. For avarice is the root of all evils, which some craving have erred from the faith, and entangled themselves in many sorrows*[^2]. As though "riches" were a sweet name when heard. *They fall into temptation*: is that a sweet name? *Many foolish and harmful desires*: is that a sweet name? *Ruin and perdition*: is that a sweet name? To be entangled in many sorrows: is that a sweet name? Let not one false good seduce you, so that you cling to so many true evils. But because by these words the blessed Apostle did not address those who are rich, but those who are not, lest they wish to be what they are not, let us see also what suits those whom he finds already rich. We have told you what had to be said, you who are poor have heard; but if any of you here are rich, hear the same blessed Apostle.

**4.** Writing to Timothy his disciple, among the other things he admonished him, he said this too: *Charge the rich of this world.* Now the Word of God finds them rich: for if it had found them poor, it would have said those things which I have already mentioned. *Charge*, then, *the rich of this world not to be proud-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy. Let them be rich in good works, give readily, share, lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^3]. Let us consider a little these few words. Above all, he says, *charge the rich not to be proud-minded.* For nothing so begets pride as riches. A rich man, if he is not proud, has trodden riches underfoot, has suspended himself toward God; the proud rich man does not possess, but is possessed. The proud rich man is like the devil. What does the proud rich man have, when he does not have God? He added also: *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches.* So he ought to have riches that he know what he has can perish. Let him have, then, what he cannot lose. So when he had said: *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*, he added: *but in the living God.* For riches can perish: and would that they so perish as not to destroy you. The psalm addresses and mocks the rich man hoping in riches: *Although man walks in the image of God.* For man was made to the image of God; but let him acknowledge what he was made, lose what he himself made, and let what God made him remain. *Although*, then, *man walks in the image of God, yet he is troubled in vain*[^4]. What is *troubled in vain? He stores up, and does not know for whom he gathers it*[^5]. The living attend to these things about the dead: they see that the goods of many dead men are not possessed by their sons, but either lose by luxury what was left to them, or by false accusations let it go; and, what is graver, while what one has is sought after, the one who has it perishes too. Many are killed because of their riches. Behold, what they had here they left behind: since what he commanded they did not do with it, with what face have they gone out to him? Let your riches, then, be true: God himself, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy.

**5.** *Let them be rich*, he says, *in good works.* There let the riches appear, there let them sow. For the same Apostle was speaking of such works where he says: *But in doing good let us not grow weary: for in due time we shall reap*[^6]. Let them sow: he does not yet see what he is going to acquire: let him believe and scatter. For does the farmer, the sower, already see the harvest gathered? With so much labor and so much care he casts out and throws away the grain that was stored up. He entrusts his seeds to the earth: do you not entrust your works to him who made heaven and earth? Therefore: *Let them be rich*, but *in good works. Let them give readily, share.* What is *share*? Let them not have alone. You have spoken, O Apostle, and taught us to make the sowing: show us the harvest too. He shows it. Hear the harvest too. O greedy man, do not be slow to sow: hear, I say, the harvest too. For he adds, when he had said: *Let them be rich in good works, give readily, share*; because he said only this, that they scatter, he ought to say what they gather. *Let them lay up*, he says, *for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^7]. The false life, where riches delight, will pass away. So after this life one must come to the true life. You love what you have: put it in a safer place, lest you lose it. Surely your whole anxiety, whoever you are who love riches, is nothing but lest you lose what you have. Hear the counsel of your Lord. There is no safe place on earth: migrate to heaven. You wished to entrust what you gathered to your most faithful servant: entrust it to your faithful Lord. Your servant, however faithful to you, can lose it even unwillingly; your God can lose nothing: whatever you entrust to him, you will have with him, when you will have him too.

**6.** Because I said, migrate and put it in heaven, let not a carnal thought creep upon you and say to you: "And when do I dig out of the earth, or take up from the earth what I have, and put it in heaven? How do I ascend? By what machines do I lift up there what I have?" Attend to the hungry, attend to the naked, attend to the destitute, attend to the pilgrims, attend to the captives: they will be your porters as you migrate to heaven. Perhaps here you think, and say to yourself: "How will these be porters? As I was thinking how I might lift to heaven what I had, and did not find a way, so I think how those to whom I give may lift it to heaven, and likewise I do not find a way." Hear, then, what Christ says to you: "Make a transfer; give it to me there, and I will repay you here." Christ says: "There, on earth, where you have it, give to me, but here I repay you." Even here you are about to say: "How do I give to Christ? Christ is in heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father: when he was here in the flesh, he deigned for our sake to hunger, to thirst, to need lodging: all these things were furnished him by the devout, who were worthy to receive into their house their Lord; now Christ needs no one, he has placed his incorruptible flesh at the right hand of the Father. How do I give here to him who is not in need?" It has slipped your mind that he said: *What you have done for one of the least of mine, you have done for me*[^8]. The head is in heaven, but he has members on earth: let a member of Christ give to a member of Christ, let him who has give to the needy. You are a member of Christ, and you have what you may give; he is a member of Christ, and he is in need that you may give. You both walk one road, you are both companions: the poor man is light on the shoulders, you the rich are weighed down with burdens: give of that by which you are pressed, of that which loads you give to the needy; and you both relieve yourself and lift up your companion. Holy Scripture says: *The poor man and the rich man met each other: but the Lord made them both*[^9]. A most sweet saying, *the poor man and the rich man met each other.* Where did they meet each other, except in this life? That one is well clothed, this one ragged, but it was when they met each other. Both are born naked, for the rich man too is born poor: let him not attend to what he found, but look to what he brought. What did the wretch bring when he was born, except nakedness and tears? Therefore the Apostle says: *We brought nothing into this world, nor can we take anything away from here*[^10]. Let him, then, send on ahead of himself what he may find when he goes out. There is a poor man, then, there is a rich man too, and they met each other; but the Lord made them both: the rich man, that through him he might help this one; the poor man, that through him he might prove that one. *Blessed*, then, *are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^11]. Let them have riches, let them not have riches: let them be poor, and theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

**7.** *Blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the earth by inheritance*[^12]. The meek. Those not resisting the will of God, they are the meek. Who are the meek? Those who, when it is well, praise God, and when it is ill, do not blaspheme God; in their good works they glorify God, in their sins they accuse themselves. *They shall possess the earth by inheritance.* What earth, except that of which the psalm says: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^13]?

**8.** *Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be consoled*[^14]. My brothers, mourning is a mournful thing, when it is the groan of one repenting. For every sinner ought to mourn. Who is mourned, except the dead? And what is so dead as the wicked man? A great thing: let him mourn himself, and he comes to life again: let him mourn in penance, and he will be consoled by pardon.

**9.** *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^15]. This is in this our earth, to hunger for justice. The satiety of justice will be in another place, where no one will sin, such as is the satiety of justice in the holy angels. But we, who hunger and thirst for justice, let us say to God: *Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven*[^16].

**10.** *Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy*[^17]. In the best order, when he had said: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*, he added: *Blessed are the merciful, for God will have mercy on them.* For you hunger and thirst for justice. If you hunger and thirst, you are a beggar of God. You stand, then, a beggar before the door of God; another beggar stands too before your door: what you do with your beggar, this God does with his.

**11.** *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^18]. Let him do all those things which are said above, and his heart is cleansed. He has a clean heart who does not feign friendships and hold enmities in his heart. For where God sees, there he crowns. Whatever it is that there delights you in your heart, let it not be approved, let it not be praised: and if an evil desire titillates, let it not be consented to: and if it burns much, let God be entreated against it, that something be done within, and the heart be cleansed, where God himself is entreated. For surely, when you wish to entreat God in your chamber, cleanse your chamber, that God may hear; cleanse the inner chamber. Sometimes the tongue is silent, and the soul groans, and God is surely entreated within, in the chamber of the heart: let there be nothing there that offends the eyes of God, let there be nothing there that displeases God. But perhaps you labor in cleansing your heart: invoke him who will not disdain to cleanse a place for himself, and will deign to dwell with you. Or do you fear to receive one so great, so powerful, lest he disturb you; as middling and narrow men are wont to fear, lest they be compelled to receive some greater men passing into their house? Surely nothing is greater than God: do not fear the narrowness, receive him, and he enlarges you. You do not have what to set before him? Receive him, and he feeds you; and, what you may hear more sweetly, he feeds you with himself. He will be your food, because he himself said: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^19]. Such bread refreshes, and does not fail. Therefore *blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.*

**12.** *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God*[^20]. Who are the peacemakers? Those who make peace. Do you see men at variance? Be a minister of peace among them. Speak good things to this one of that one, and to that one of this one. Do you hear evil things of one of them from the other, as from one angry? Do not betray it: suppress the reproach you hear from the angry one, give faithful counsel for concord. But also, if you wish to be a peacemaker between two friends of yours at variance, begin to be a peacemaker with yourself: you ought to pacify yourself within, where perhaps you quarrel with yourself in daily combat. Did he not have a quarrel within himself who said: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; for these are opposed to each other, so that you do not do the things you wish*[^21]? These are the words of the holy Apostle. *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man; but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and taking me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^22]. If, then, there is in the very inner man a certain daily quarrel, and a praiseworthy fight does this, lest the higher things be overcome by the lower, lest lust conquer the mind, lest desire conquer wisdom, that itself is the right peace which you ought to make in yourself, that what is better in you may command the lower things. That is the better thing in you, where the image of God is. This is called the mind, is called intelligence; there faith burns, there hope is made firm, there love is kindled. But does your mind wish to be fit to conquer your lusts? Let it be subject to the greater, and it will conquer the lower; and there will be in you a true peace, certain, most ordered. What is the order of this peace? God commands the mind, the mind the flesh: nothing more ordered. But the flesh still has its weaknesses. It was not so in paradise: by sin it was made such, on account of sin it has a bond of discord against us. One came without sin to make our soul concord with our flesh, and deigned to give us the pledge of the Spirit. *For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God*[^23]. Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God. But all this fight, which wearies us by our weakness—and when we do not consent to evil desires, yet by the very battle we are in some way occupied, we are not yet secure—all this fight will not be then, when death is swallowed up in victory. Hear how it will not be: *The corruptible body must*—this the Apostle says—*put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality. But when this mortal has put on immortality, then will come about the word that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory*[^24]. The war is finished, and concluded in peace. Hear the voice of those triumphing: *Where, O death, is your strife? Where, O death, is your sting?*[^25] Now this is the voice of those triumphing: no enemy at all will remain, neither the wrestler within nor the tempter without. *Blessed*, then, *are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God.*

**13.** *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for justice's sake*[^26]. This addition distinguished the martyr from the robber: for the robber too suffers persecution for his evils, and seeks no crown, but pays the punishment he owes. It is not the punishment that makes a martyr, but the cause: let him first choose the cause, and securely bear the punishment. There were three crosses in one place when Christ suffered: he himself in the middle, on this side and that two robbers. Attend to the punishment, nothing more alike: yet one of the robbers found paradise on the cross. He in the middle, judging, condemns the proud one, helps the humble one. That wood was a tribunal for Christ. What does he do, about to judge, who could do these things while being judged? To the robber confessing he says: *Amen I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise*[^27]. For that man was putting it off. For what had he said? *Remember me, Lord, when you come into your kingdom*[^28]. "I know," he says, "my evils: surely let me be tortured up to here, until you come." And because everyone who humbles himself shall be exalted, at once he brought forth the sentence, gave the indulgence: *Today*, he says, *you will be with me in paradise.* But was the whole Lord buried that same day? According to the flesh he was to be in the sepulcher; according to the soul he was to be among those below, not to be bound, but to loose the bound. If, then, that same day, according to the soul he was to be among those below, according to the flesh he was to be in the sepulcher, how did he say: *Today you will be with me in paradise?* But is the whole Christ soul and flesh? It has slipped your mind, *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^29]. It has slipped your mind, that *Christ is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God*[^30]. Where, then, is the Wisdom of God not? Was it not said of it: *She reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly*[^31]? Therefore according to the person of the Word he said: *Today you will be with me in paradise.* "Today," he says, "according to the soul I descend to those below, but according to my divinity I do not depart from paradise."

**14.** All the beatitudes of Christ I have expounded to your love, as far as I could. I see you so eager that you still wish to hear more. Your love has provoked us to say many things, and perhaps we could say others: but it is better that you ruminate well on what you receive, and digest it wholesomely.
