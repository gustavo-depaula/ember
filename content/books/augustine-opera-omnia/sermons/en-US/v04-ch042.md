# Sermon 125a

**1.** It is often asked, how it is written in the book of the law called Genesis, where is the beginning of the holy Scriptures, why God completed all his works on the sixth day, and on the seventh day, that is, the Sabbath day, rested from all his works, when his Son, through whom all things were made, says: *My Father works until now, and I work* [^1]. For saying this he refuted the Jews, who calumniated him, that on the Sabbath day he said to a man: *Rise, take up your pallet, and walk* [^2]; for it is not lawful for Jews to carry burdens on the Sabbath. What, then, does he mean? Is the Son contrary to the Word of the Father, when he himself is the Word of the Father? We ought, then, to understand, lest we remain like the Jews; for they took the Sabbath so carnally, that they thought God to be at leisure from that Sabbath day. Or if they did not think this, perhaps they believed that he too works in his creation through six days, and on every Sabbath rests and is at leisure for himself, and after the manner of children rejoices at the holidays. So, then, this question is resolved, because it is true that God in six days filled and completed his works by founding creatures, but on the Sabbath rested, not from administering creatures, but from founding them. For this worldly mass, that is, heaven and earth and all that is in them, if it is not ruled by him, falls. But he rules the world without labor, not as that man carried his pallet. And if it is well understood, God at the same time both rests and acts; for everyone who acts without labor, rests even in the work itself. If you ask me whether God is at leisure, would we be living if he were at leisure? Again if you ask me whether God has rest, I will answer you: And what is it that he will give us, if he himself does not have it? Therefore I will answer: He both has rest, and works until now. This you cannot do; but he is God, which you are not. The time had now come, then, when the shadows should pass, and the day breathe, as it is written in the Song of Songs: *Until the day breathe, and the shadows be removed* [^3].

**2.** When, then, the Lord Jesus bade that sick man, whom he made healthy, take up his pallet, he was removing the old shadows. The time had now come, then, that there should come to pass what the Apostle says: *Let no one, therefore, judge you in food and drink, or in respect of a feast day, a new moon, and Sabbaths, which is a shadow of things to come* [^4]. Those Jewish observances commanded by God were, then, shadows of things to come; and the very things which had been to come, Christ coming began to make present; what was awaited had arrived; what was signified was seen. Let not the shadows hinder, then, let them be removed, let us see the light. In whom? In Christ. For this reason he says: *In your light we shall see light* [^5]; and: *To those who sat in the shadow of death, light has risen for them* [^6]. The man is healthy, and do you calumniate about the pallet? He who told the sick man to carry the wood was the one who for him was to hang on the wood. The foolish impiety of the Jews! Whom you saw lying, you see walking; and do you accuse him for carrying? He who gave health to the one walking, gave strength to the one carrying. Be a Christian, O Judea, and understand the Sabbath; as long as you are a Jew, you can observe the Sabbath, you cannot understand it. Unless you pass over to the truth, you cannot have what you celebrate.

**3.** What is it that I said: You cannot have what you celebrate? For you can by the Sabbath leisure celebrate rest; but unless you pass over to Christ, you cannot come to eternal rest; you will remain in shadow, without light. Pass over, then, to Christ, that the veil may be taken away. For the Apostle says this: *To this day, as long as Moses is read, a veil is set upon their hearts; which is not unveiled, because in Christ it is done away with*. The veil, not Moses, is done away with; the veil, not the law. Attend how the Lord came, and the veil is done away with; when he hung on the wood, the veil was torn. O great mystery! O ineffable sacrament! The transgressors of the law crucified the author of the law, and the secrets of the law lay open. Was not that cross a key? It held the Lord, and unlocked the closed things. But the Jews, even with the veil torn, have a covered face: *But we*, says the Apostle, *with unveiled face contemplating the glory of the Lord, are transformed into the same image, from glory to glory, as by the Spirit of the Lord* [^7]. They could have the law in stone; O if they had it in the heart, they would be with us. But we, brothers, let us have the law in our hearts; let us show it not by praises of word, but by good works. Alms must be given, it is time; let your fruits be seen; let me rejoice over my labors. You cannot say to the sick man: Rise and walk; you can say: Until you rise, lie and eat. You cannot heal the sick, you can clothe the naked. Do what you can; God does not demand of you what you cannot.

**4.** Concerning the very doing of mercy you have heard what holy Tobit admonished his son Tobias: *As you have substance, son, so do; if you have much, do much; if little, share from that little* [^8]. The widow, who brought two mites, how little she had! And the Lord saw her [^9]. Surely if they neglected her, because she brought no more than two mites, the heart of the wise one could see what she gave. Who put more into the gifts of God, than she who left herself nothing? Tobit did not admonish his son this: *From what you have*, he says, *share*; he did not say: Give all. Do this which Tobit admonished. Many did even that; they left all their own, gave all their own to the poor, kept nothing for themselves. Nothing, do we think? And where is God? For what does the poor man not have, if he has God? Or what does the rich man have, if he does not have God? Do it, then, and marvel at the words of Scripture. The blind father spoke to the seeing son, admonished him to give alms; who among other things said: *For alms frees from death*. This is no wonder; for though the blind man spoke to the seeing, yet a living man to a living. What follows is a wonder. For when he had said of alms: *It frees from death*, he added, *and does not allow to go into the darkness* [^10]. O father, you always gave alms; why have you come to this darkness of blindness? The son, then, could have said this to his father; but he knew what he was saying, and the son understood what he heard. There are other darknesses, into which alms do not allow those to enter who love to give them. What are those darknesses? Of which the Lord himself says: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth* [^11]. These are called the outer darkness. Why? Because they are outside God; he who comes to them, and who enters into them, departs far from God. In short, he sends the wicked servant into the outer darkness; but to the good he says: *Enter into the joy of your Lord* [^12]. It is evil to go out into darkness; it is good to enter into light, where there is no darkness at all, neither of heart nor of flesh.

**5.** So in this Tobit there was blindness of the flesh, but a great light of the heart. The son held the father's hand, lest he stumble against a place; the father showed the son the way of life, lest he offend God. The one held the hand; the other showed the way, where it is truly perilous to stumble. In what light was he, when he said these things? Surely his eyes were closed, and yet he said: *Son, give alms; alms free from death*. Did he see nothing, who said this? Rather he saw, not white and black, but just and unjust; he discerned, not colors, but conduct. Blessed son, who heard a blind man seeing, blind in the flesh, seeing in the heart! For his blindness passes, God cured him, he received his eyes; but even if he did not receive his eyes in this flesh, would not those eyes one day be closed in death? For all the saints, when they go out from here, come to light; cheap to those seeing God is the light of this sun. The father exhorted the seeing son, and admonished him about alms; he said many things about alms, almost this alone he admonished his dearest and only son. How great is the force in them, how great the power! What is given, and what is received? What is spent, and what is bought? Is it not said to us: Your fathers bought the kingdom of heaven with alms? Both they bought it, and left it for us to buy. Let all buy it, let all possess it; no one will suffer straits; we shall be the possession of God, our possession will be God. For we worship God, and God worships us. But what I said: We worship God, all accept; what I said: God worships us, perhaps I offended some. Do we worship him, not he us? It is good for us that he worship us; for unless he cultivate this field, it will be filled with thorns. Who is the husbandman, except he who cultivates the field? Hear, then, the Lord Christ, and do not take fright, when it is said to you, God cultivates you: *I am*, he says, *the vine, you the branches, and my Father the husbandman* [^13]. Therefore we worship God that we may have fruit, and he cultivates us; both come to be for the sake of our fruit. For from barren we are made fruitful and fruit-bearing; dry and thirsting we are filled with him; but the fountain does not know how to be dried up. The whole, then, comes to be for our sake. Let us give thanks to him who created us, and called us to reign with him.
