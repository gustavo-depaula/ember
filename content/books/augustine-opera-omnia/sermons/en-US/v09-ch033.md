# Sermon 323: Delivered After the Booklet on Saint Stephen

**1. 1.** By the mercy of God, brothers, as is to be believed, all those brothers, whom one anger of God, the maternal blow, struck, will someday come to the health of this man over whom we rejoice. Nevertheless let children learn to obey, let parents fear to grow angry. It is written: *The blessing of the father strengthens the houses of the children: the curse of the mother uproots the foundations*[^1]. Now these men are not in the foundations of their homeland throughout the lands: they offer a spectacle everywhere, they set forth their punishment; they offer their misery to the eyes, they terrify the pride of others. Learn, children, what Scripture says, to render the honor due to parents. But you too, parents, when you are offended, remember that you are parents. The mother prayed against her children, she was heard; because God is truly just, because she had truly suffered an injury. One of them had both laid on insulting words and laid on hands; and the rest bore their mother's injury patiently, nor answered one word against their brother on her behalf. Just is God, who heard her praying, heard her grieving. But what of that wretched woman? Was she not, from where she was the sooner heard, thereby the more punished? Learn to ask this of God, where you need not fear to be heard.

**2. 2.** But let us, brothers, strive to give thanks to the Lord our God for the one who has been healed; and for her who is still held, let us pour out prayers. Let us bless God, because he held us worthy to see this. For what are we, that I appeared to these men without knowing it? For they saw me, and I did not know it: and they were admonished to come to this city. Who am I? I am one man of many, not of the great. And truly, that Your Charity may hear, I marvel much, and rejoice that it was granted to us: since this man could not be healed even at Ancona; indeed he could, but for our sake it was not done, because it could most easily have been done. For many know how many miracles are done through the most blessed martyr Stephen in that city. And hear what you may marvel at: his memorial was there of old, and it is there. But perhaps you say: His body had not yet appeared, from where was the memorial there? The cause indeed lies hidden: but what report has brought to us, I will not keep silent to Your Charity. When holy Stephen was being stoned, some even innocent men, and especially of those who had already believed in Christ, were standing around: it is said that a stone came against the elbow, and, struck off from there, came before a certain religious man. He took it, and kept it. He was a man of the seafarers, the lot of his voyage brought him to the shore of Ancona, and it was revealed to him that that stone ought to be laid there. He obeyed the revelation, and did what was commanded: from that the memorial of holy Stephen began to be there, and there was a rumor that the arm of holy Stephen was there, men not knowing what had happened. But it is rightly understood that it was therefore revealed there, that he should lay there the stone which was struck off from the elbow of the Martyr, because in Greek "elbow" is called ἀγκών. But let those who know what miracles are done there teach us. Those miracles did not begin to be done there, except after the body of holy Stephen appeared. Behold, there this young man was not healed, that he might be kept for our eyes.

**2. 3.** At Uzalis, where my brother Evodius is bishop, ask how many miracles are done there, and you will find out. But, passing over the others, I make known to you one which was done there, that you may see how great is the presence of majesty there.

**3. 3.** A certain woman suddenly lost in her lap her sick son, a catechumen, whom by hastening she could not help: who, crying out, said, My son the catechumen is dead.

**3. 4.** And while Augustine was saying these things, the people from the memorial of holy Stephen began to cry out, Thanks be to God! Praise to Christ! In which continuous shouting, the girl who was healed was led to the apse. When she was seen, the people, with joy and weeping, with no words interposed, but only with an interposed clamor, prolonged the shouting for some time: and when silence was made, Augustine the bishop said: "It is written in the Psalm: *I said, I will declare against myself my offense to the Lord my God, and you forgave the impiety of my heart*[^2]. *I said, I will declare:* I had not yet declared: *I said, I will declare, and you forgave.* I commended this wretched woman—rather, this woman once wretched—I commended her to your prayers. We resolved to pray, and we were heard. Let our joy be thanksgiving. Mother Church was the sooner heard, than was, to her ruin, that cursing mother." Turned to the Lord, etc.

[^1]: Sir 3:9
[^2]: Ps 32:5 (31:5)
