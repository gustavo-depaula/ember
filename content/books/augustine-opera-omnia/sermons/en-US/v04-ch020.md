# Sermon 110

**1.** Hear what the Lord has granted concerning the fig tree, which had three years and bore no fruit, and concerning the woman who had eighteen years in infirmity. The fig tree is the human race; the three years are three times: one before the law, the second under the law, the third under grace. And it is not unfitting that the fig tree be understood as the human race. For the first man, when he sinned, covered his shame with fig leaves; he covered those things from which we are born; the members, which before the sin were to be gloried in, after the sin were made shameful. Indeed: *They were naked, and were not ashamed* [^1]; there was nothing for which to blush, when sin had not gone before; nor could they blush at the works of their Creator, because to the good works of their Creator they had mixed no evil work of their own. From there, then, was born the human race: man from man, the liable from the debtor, the mortal from the mortal, the sinner from the sinner. These, then, he calls in this tree, who through all time refused to bear fruit; and the axe hung over the roots of the unfruitful tree. The vinedresser intercedes: the punishment is deferred so that help may be applied. And that the vinedresser intercedes—every holy person within the Church prays for those who are outside the Church. And what does he pray? *Lord, let it alone this year too* [^2]. That is, in this time under grace spare the sinners, spare the unbelievers, spare the barren, spare the unfruitful. *I will dig around it, and apply a basket of dung; if it bears fruit, well; if not, when you come you will cut it down* [^3]. When you come, when? In judgment. When you come, when? *From there he is to come to judge the living and the dead* [^4]. Meanwhile it is now spared. But what is it, to dig a trench around it, except to teach the humility of penitence? For a trench is lowly ground. Understand the basket of dung in a good sense. It is filth, but it gives fruit. The filth of the cultivator is the grief of the sinner; those who do penance, do it in filth, if only they understand, and do it truly. To this tree, then, it is said: *Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near* [^5].

**2.** What of that woman, having eighteen years? Recall. In six days God completed his works. Three times six, eighteen. What, then, the three years signified in the tree, this the eighteen years signified in that woman [^6]. She was bent over, she could not look up, because she was hearing in vain, *Lift up your heart*. But the Lord raised her up. There is hope, but for sons. So then, until the day of judgment comes, a man gives himself much credit. And what is man? Not even the just man is anything, as far as pertains to the man himself. For a just man is a great something, a man, but yet a just man is a just man by the grace of God. For: *What is man, that you are mindful of him* [^7]? Do you want to see what man is? *Every man is a liar* [^8]. We have sung: *Arise, Lord, let not man prevail* [^9]. What is: Let not man prevail? Were the Apostles not men? Were the martyrs not men? Jesus himself deigned to be a man. What, then, is: *Arise, Lord, let not man prevail*, if *every man is a liar?* Arise, truth, let not falsehood prevail. So a man, if he wants to be something, let him not be of his own; if he wants to be of his own, he will be a liar; if he wants to be truthful, he will be of God, not of his own.

**3.** Therefore: *Arise, Lord, let not man prevail* [^10]. So great did the lie prevail before the flood; after the flood eight men remained; through them the earth was filled with lying men. The people of God was chosen; so great miracles were done, divine benefits granted beforehand. He was led to the land of promise, freed from the slavery of the Egyptians; Prophets were sent to him, he received a temple, he received a priesthood, he received an anointing, he received a law; but: *Alien sons have lied to me* [^11]. At last the promised one was sent. *Let not man prevail*, or because God has now become man. But even he, doing divine things, was despised; bestowing so great benefits, he was seized, scourged, hung up. So far did man prevail, to seize the Son of God, to scourge the Son of God, to crown the Son of God with thorns, to hang the Son of God on the wood. So far did man prevail: but to the point that, taken down from the wood, he was laid in the tomb. If he had remained there, man would have prevailed. But this prophecy addresses even him: Lord, you deigned to come in the flesh, the Word flesh, the Word above us, the flesh before us, the Word flesh between God and man; you chose a Virgin from whom to be born according to the flesh, you found a Virgin to be conceived in, born, you left her a Virgin. But you were not recognized: you appeared, and lay hidden; weakness appeared, power lay hidden. All this, that you might pour out your blood, our price. You did so great wonders, you bestowed benefits on the infirmities of the sick; you received evils for goods, you were insulted; you hung on the wood, the head was wagged by the impious before you, and it was said: *If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross* [^12]. Had you then lost your power, or were you rather showing patience? And yet they insulted you, yet they mocked you, yet they departed, you slain, as though victors. And behold, you lie in the tomb. *Arise, Lord, let not man prevail* [^13]; let not the impious enemy prevail, let not the blind Jew prevail. For when you were crucified, the blind Jew seemed to himself to have prevailed: but you rose again and he was made guilty. *Arise, Lord, let not man prevail*. And it was done. And what remains, except that: *The nations be judged in your sight* [^14]? For he rose again, as you know, ascended into heaven, from there to come to judge the living and the dead.

**4.** Come now, unfruitful tree, do not mock, because you are spared; the axe is deferred, do not be carefree: it will come, you will be cut down. Believe, he is going to come. All these things that you see were not. The Christian people throughout the whole world once was not; in prophecy it was read, on earth it was not seen; but now it is both read and seen. The Church herself was thus addressed; it was not said to her: See, daughter, and hear, but: *Hear, and see* [^15]; hear the things foretold, see the things fulfilled. So then, dearly beloved, Christ was not born of the Virgin: he was promised and born; he had not done wonders: they were promised, and he did them; he had not yet suffered: it was promised, and it was done; he had not risen: it was foretold, and it was fulfilled; he had not ascended into heaven: it was foretold, and it was fulfilled; his name was not throughout the whole world: it was foretold, and it was fulfilled; idols were not destroyed and broken: and it was done; heretics attacking the Church were not: it was foretold, and it was fulfilled; so the day of judgment is not yet, but, because it was foretold, it will be fulfilled. He who in so great things appeared truthful, will he be a liar in the day of judgment? He made us a bond of his promises; for by promising, God made himself a debtor, not by receiving a loan. Can we say to him: Repay what you received? *Who first gave to him, and it shall be repaid to him* [^16]? We cannot say: Repay what you received; but we plainly say: Repay what you promised.

**5.** He promised to our fathers: but he made a written guarantee, which we too might read. If he reckons accounts with us, he who deigned to make a guarantee, and says: Read my debts, that is, of my promises, and reckon what I have repaid; reckon, see what I owe: how much I have repaid! It is little that I owe: from the little that remains, do you think me an unfaithful promiser? Therefore let him who is barren do penance, and bear fruit worthy of penance [^17]. He who is bent over, and gazes on the earth, rejoices in earthly happiness, thinks this alone to be the life where he can be blessed, does not believe in another; whoever is so bent over, let him be raised up; if he cannot of himself, let him call upon God. For was even that woman raised up of herself? Woe would be to her, if he had not held out his hand.
