# Sermon 266: On the Vigils of Pentecost, Against the Donatists

**1.** Among the other divine words which we heard when the Psalm was sung, it pleases me, with the Lord's help, to discuss and treat especially this sentence, by which it was said: *The just will amend me in mercy, and reprove me; but let the oil of the sinner not fatten my head*[^1]. For some have believed that the oil of the sinner is the oil of a man; because *every man is a liar.* But the oil of Christ, because he had no sin at all, even if it is administered through a sinner, is not the oil of a sinner. Since three are to be considered in mind: by whom it is given, to whom it is given, through whom it is given: let us not fear the oil of the sinner, because the intermediary minister does not intercept the benefit of the bestower.

**2.** For surely we now celebrate the solemnity of the coming of the Holy Spirit: for on the day of Pentecost, which day has now begun, there were in one place one hundred twenty souls, among whom were the apostles and the mother of the Lord and others of both sexes praying and awaiting the promise of Christ, that is, the coming of the Holy Spirit. The hope of those awaiting was not empty, because the promise of the one promising was not deceptive: what was awaited came, and found clean vessels by which to be received. *There appeared to them divided tongues, as of fire, which also settled upon each of them; and they began to speak in tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter*[^2]. Each man spoke in all tongues, because the future Church in all tongues was foretold. One man was the sign of unity: all tongues in one man, all nations in unity. Those who were full spoke: and those who were empty marveled; and what is more blameworthy, they marveled and slandered. For they said: *These are drunk and full of new wine*[^3]. How foolish and slanderous a rebuke! A drunk man does not learn another tongue, but loses his own. Nevertheless through the ignorant and slandering the truth spoke. For now they were full of new wine, because they had been made new wineskins. But the old wineskins marveled at the new wineskins, and by slandering were neither renewed nor filled. But the slander being at last repressed, soon, when they offered their ears to the apostles speaking and giving an account and preaching the grace of Christ, by hearing they were pricked, by the pricking they were changed, changed they believed; believing, they merited to receive what they marveled at in others.

**3.** Then the Holy Spirit began to be given through the ministry of the apostles. They laid on hands, and he came. But this was not of men: let the minister not arrogate to himself more than that he is a minister. One is the giver, another the minister. For the Spirit attested this, lest men arrogate to themselves what was of God. For from this Simon wished to be puffed up, who, thinking this was to be granted by men, promised money to the apostles, that the Holy Spirit might come also at the laying on of his hands. He did not know grace. For if he recognized grace, he would have it freely. Therefore, because he wished to buy the Spirit, he did not merit to be redeemed by the Spirit. What are you, O man, that you want to puff yourself up? It is enough for you to be filled, not to be puffed up. He who is filled is rich: he who is puffed up is empty. But through men, they say, it was given. Was it therefore of men because it was given? But it could not be given, they say, except through holy men. Surely it did not come into them through men? The apostles laid on hands, and the Holy Spirit came: when he came to them, who laid hands on them?

**4.** Receive and hold the divine examples: but they are the words of God, the authority of Scripture, the trustworthiness of the words, the truth of the examples. We read the whole, let us believe the whole. Through the laying on of the hands of the apostles the Holy Spirit was given to many: but those through whom it was given had received it. When had they received it? When in one chamber there were one hundred twenty men: each of them prayed, none laid on a hand; he came upon them as they prayed, filled them as they prayed, made the filled into ministers, and through them gave his own. Hear still more. Philip the evangelist, who preached the Gospel in Samaria, was one of the seven deacons: for in the need of ministry seven deacons had been added to the twelve apostles; of whom one, as I said, was Philip, who, because of his ready eloquence of preaching, properly merited to be called an evangelist. Although all did this; he, as I said, preached the Gospel in Samaria: many believed in Samaria, believing they were baptized. But when the apostles heard, they sent to them Peter and John, that they might lay hands on the baptized, and by laying hands on them obtain the Holy Spirit by invoking him. Simon, marveling at so great a grace of the apostles, wished to give money, as if that which was invoked were offered for sale: but he was rejected, and found unworthy of so great a grace. Those, then, received the Holy Spirit through the hands of the apostles. Then, therefore, because that Simon had thought the gift of God to be of men, lest this suspicion be confirmed among the weak; afterward a certain eunuch of Queen Candace was coming from Jerusalem, where he had gone to pray, and sitting in his chariot was reading the prophet Isaiah. Then the Holy Spirit said to Philip that he should approach the chariot. He who had baptized in Samaria, and had laid hands on no one, and had reported to the apostles, that through their coming and laying on of hands those baptized by him might merit to receive the Holy Spirit, approaches the chariot, asks the eunuch whether he understood what he was reading. He answers that he can understand, if he has an expounder: he asks Philip to come up into the chariot; he came up, sat with him, found him reading in the prophet Isaiah what had been foretold of Christ: *Like a sheep he was led to be sacrificed*, and the rest that are around the same reading. Then, asking whether the prophet was speaking of himself, or of another; the door of opportunity being opened, he preached Christ the door of salvation. While these things are done on the journey, they came to water, and the eunuch said to Philip: *See, water, who forbids me to be baptized?* Philip says: *If you believe, it can be done.* And he: *I believe that Jesus is the Son of God.* They went down into the water, Philip baptized him. After they came up from the water, the Holy Spirit came upon the eunuch[^4]. See, Philip was there, who had baptized in Samaria, and had led the apostles to those baptized, he baptized, and did not lay on hands: but, to show that Simon had not suspected the truth, that the Spirit of God was the gift of men, he came into the man freely, and made him free. He came as God, and filled him: as the Lord came, and redeemed.

**5.** Perhaps someone of the contentious may say that Philip who baptized in Samaria was not a deacon, but was an apostle; because both Philip is named among the apostles, and the one who was properly called the evangelist is one of the seven deacons. But let them suspect what they will, I quickly solve the question. Whether he was an apostle, or a deacon, which the reading was silent about, let this be uncertain. Yet this is written, that as soon as he came up from the water, the Holy Spirit came upon the eunuch. No one there mentions a laying on of hands. Perhaps even this is too little: for he says, Surely someone laid a hand on him, but the Scripture was silent about it.

**6.** So what do you say? This, he says, I say, that upon those first one hundred twenty indeed, because then for the first time the Holy Spirit was coming, he came without the laying on of hands; but from then on he came upon no one, unless a hand had been laid. You have forgotten Cornelius the centurion: read diligently, and understand prudently. Cornelius the centurion, as is read in the same book of the Acts of the Apostles, where the coming of the Holy Spirit too is proclaimed. To Cornelius the centurion an angel was sent, announced to him that his alms had been accepted, his prayers heard: and accordingly that he ought to send to Peter, who was dwelling in Joppa in the house of Simon the tanner, and that he was to be summoned. But then a great question was being debated between the Jews and the gentiles, that is, between those who had believed from the Jews, and those from the gentiles, whether the Gospel was to be administered to the uncircumcised. There was great hesitation about this, when Cornelius sends. Meanwhile Peter is admonished, the business of the kingdom of heaven is carried on, both here and there, by him who is everywhere. For while these things are done at Cornelius', meanwhile Peter too in Joppa grew hungry, went up to pray when refreshment was being prepared for him; the mind of the one praying was carried away; but from the lowest to the heights; not that it might stray, but that it might see. There came to him a dish let down from heaven, as it were a heavenly course for the hungry. But this dish was bound by four lines, having all kinds of animals, clean and unclean, and the hungry one was struck by the heavenly voice: *Peter, rise; slay, and eat.* He attended, saw in the dish unclean animals, which he was not accustomed to touch, and answered the voice: *Far be it from me, Lord: never has common and unclean entered my mouth.* And the voice to him: *What God has cleansed, do not call unclean.* But this was done three times, and the vessel was received into heaven[^5]. An evident sacred sign. The dish is the whole world. The four lines holding the dish, the four cardinal points of the world, which the Scripture mentions, saying: *From the East and West, and from the North and the Sea*[^6]. The animals, all nations. The dish let down three times, the commendation of the Trinity. Peter, the Church; Peter hungering, the Church desiring the faith of the gentiles. The heavenly voice, the holy Gospel. *Slay, and eat*: kill what they are, make them what you are. While Peter was at variance over the command, suddenly it was announced that certain soldiers sent by Cornelius wished to see him. And the Holy Spirit to Peter: *Go with them; I sent them.* Peter proceeds, now not hesitating about the vision, but certain: and, as is read, it is announced to Cornelius, he meets him humbly, prostrates himself humbly: he is lifted more humbly. They reach the house, find many others gathered. It is told Peter what the reason was for sending to him, and thanks are given that Peter has come. Therefore, opening his mouth, he began to preach to the uncircumcised gentiles, about which that great question was being debated, the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ. There were certain ones with Peter, who had believed from the Jews, who could be moved if the uncircumcised were baptized: there plainly Peter says: *You know, brothers, how abominable it is for a Jew to approach or join himself to a gentile; but God showed me to call no man common or unclean*[^7]. That hungry one looked to the dish.

**7.** Where are those who said—for I narrated the whole for this reason, for what I want to say: where are those who said that the Holy Spirit is given by the power of a man? While Peter was preaching, Cornelius and all who were with him, a gens, that is, gentiles, believed; and suddenly, before they were baptized, they were filled with the Holy Spirit. What does human presumption answer here? Not only before hands were laid, but before Baptism itself the Holy Spirit came; of power, not of necessity. He came before the washing of baptism, that he might take away the controversy of circumcision. For it could be said to Peter by the slanderous or those not understanding: You did badly to give the Holy Spirit. See, it is fulfilled, see, it is demonstrated, what the Lord says: *The Spirit breathes where it wills*[^8]. See, it is fulfilled, see, it is shown how truly the Lord said: *The Spirit breathes where it wills.* And yet the proud heretic does not yet breathe out the spirit of arrogance. He still says: It is mine; do not receive from him, but from me. You answer: I seek what is of God. He: Have you not read: *But let the oil of the sinner not fatten my head*[^9]? So the oil is yours? If it is yours, I do not want it: if it is yours, it is bad. But if it is of God, even through you the bad one it is good. Mud does not stain the ray of the sun, and do you stain the oil of God? But you have it to your harm, because you have what is good while bad; what is of God, you received while bad: because, separated, you did not gather, but scattered. Those who eat unworthily, eat and drink judgment to themselves: because they eat unworthily, do they not eat? Christ gave the morsel to the unworthy Judas, and he received it unto judgment. Surely he did not receive from one who was bad? surely he did not receive something bad? But he is guilty for this reason, that, being bad, he received the good from the good. The oil of the sinner, then, is not the oil of salvation. Let it be well received, and it is good: even if it is badly received, it is good. Woe to men receiving the good badly!

**8.** See, however, the sense of the Scripture, lest perhaps it suggest something that lies open to a better understanding. *The just will amend me*, he says, *in mercy*: even if he strikes, the reprover loves, esteems; the flatterer deceives: the one has mercy, the other circumvents. Hard is the rod of the one striking, soft is the oil of the one fawning. For all flatterers anoint the head, do not heal the inner parts. Love the reprover, beware of the flatterer. For if you love the truthful reprover, and beware of the deceitful flatterer, you can say what was sung: *The just will amend me in mercy, and reprove me, but the oil of the sinner*, that is, the flattery of the fawner, *let it not fatten my head.* A fat head is a big head: a big head is a proud head. Better is a sound heart than a big head: but a sound heart the rod of the reprover makes; a big head the oil of the sinner makes, that is, the assent of the flatterer. If you have made your head big, beware of the weight of the head, lest you be led to a fall. These things, as far as I judge, for the time we have spoken sufficiently about this one sentence of the Psalm, the Lord helping, and building up your hearts in secret.
