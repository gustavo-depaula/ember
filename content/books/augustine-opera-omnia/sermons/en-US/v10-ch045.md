# Sermon 363: On the Canticle of Exodus 15:1-21

**1.** Our understanding, dearest brothers, in considering and treating the holy Scriptures, ought to be governed by the most manifest authority of those same Scriptures; so that from those things which were openly said for our nourishment, those things which were said more obscurely for our exercise may faithfully be discussed. For who would dare to expound the divine mysteries otherwise than was preached and prescribed by the heart and mouth of the apostle? Now the apostle Paul says: *I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized in Moses, both in the cloud and in the sea; and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. But they drank of the spiritual rock following them; but the rock was Christ. But with most of them God was not well pleased. For they were laid low in the desert. But these things were made figures of us, that we should not be desirers of evil things, as they too desired*[^1]. And a little after: *All these things*, he says, *happened to them in a figure; but they were written for our correction, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^2].

**2.** Hence, therefore, dearest ones, let no one of the faithful doubt that the passage of that people through the Red Sea was a figure of our Baptism: that, freed by Baptism from the devil and his angels, who, like Pharaoh and the Egyptians, were wearing us down, subject to the mud of the flesh as it were with the works of bricks, with our Lord Jesus Christ as leader, whose figure Moses then bore, *Let us sing to the Lord: for he has been gloriously magnified; horse and rider he has cast into the sea*[^3]. For they are dead to us, who can no longer have dominion over us: since our very sins, which had made us subject to him, with us freed by the washing of holy grace, are as it were submerged and blotted out in the sea. *Let us sing*, then, *to the Lord: for he has been gloriously magnified; horse and rider he has cast into the sea*, pride and the proud one he has blotted out in Baptism. For now the humble man subject to God sings this canticle. For to the proud man seeking his own glory, and magnifying himself, the Lord has not been gloriously magnified. But the impious man justified, by believing in him who justifies the impious, so that his faith may be reckoned to him for justice[^4], so that the just man may live by faith[^5]; lest, ignorant of the justice of God, and wishing to establish his own, he be not subject to the justice of God[^6]; most truly sings the Lord his helper and protector unto salvation, his God, whom he honors. For he is not of those exalted ones, who, knowing God, did not honor him as God[^7]. He says, then: *The God of my father*[^8]. For he is the God of Abraham our father, who believed God, and it was reckoned to him for justice[^9]. And therefore we little ones, presuming not on our own justice, but on his grace, magnify the Lord, since he himself *crushes battles*, who is our peace. And therefore *the Lord is his name*: to whom we say in Isaiah: *Possess us*[^10]. *The Lord is his name.* We were not, and he made us: we had perished, and he found us: we had sold ourselves, and he bought us. *The Lord is his name. The chariot of Pharaoh and his army he has cast into the sea*[^11]. And the worldly exaltations, and the troops of innumerable sins, which were soldiering for the devil in us, he has blotted out in Baptism. He had placed *three drivers* on the chariots, who terrified us by persecuting us with the fear of pain, the fear of humiliation, the fear of death. All these things were submerged in the Red Sea, because we were buried together with him through Baptism into death[^12], who for us was scourged, dishonored, and slain. So he covered all the enemies with the Red Sea, who by the bloody death, by which our sins were consumed, consecrated Baptism. But if our enemies have gone down into the deep like a stone, he possesses only them, and only with them is the hardness of the devil, of whom it is written: *The sinner, when he has come into the depth of evils, despises*[^13]. For they do not believe that what they have done can be forgiven them: and by that despair they are more grievously and more deeply submerged. But: *Your right hand, O Lord, has been glorified in strength; the right hand of your hand, O Lord, has shattered the enemies; and in the multitude of your majesty, O Lord, you have crushed the adversaries. For you sent your wrath, and it consumed them like stubble.* For we feared you being born, and we believed in you, and all our offenses were consumed. For why *by the spirit of the Lord's wrath was the water divided, and the waves froze like a wall, the waves froze in the midst of the sea*; when in this division of the water, with the waves freezing, a way lay open to the freed people? Why, then, was the water not rather divided by the spirit of the Lord's mercy; except because the terror of the wrath of God, which that sinner despises who comes to the depth of evils, itself compels to Baptism, that through the water not drowning, but those passing through the way, we may be freed? *The enemy said: I will pursue, and overtake; I will divide the spoils, and fill my soul; I will slay with my sword, my hand will have dominion.* Nor does the enemy understand the power of the Lord's sacrament, which is in the saving Baptism, to those who believe and hope in him: and still he thinks that sins can have dominion even over the baptized, since they are tempted by the fragility of the flesh, not knowing where, and when, and how the full renewal of the whole man is perfected, which is begun in Baptism, and foresignified, and now held by most certain hope. For then both this mortal will put on immortality, and, with every principality and power utterly emptied out, God will be all in all[^14]. But now, as long as the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul[^15], the enemy says: *I will pursue, and overtake. But you sent your spirit, and the sea covered them.* Now it is not called the spirit of the wrath of God, when the sea covered the enemies: and a little before it was said: *By the spirit of your wrath the water was divided*; when rather by this the people of God passing through was freed. But surely God seems not to be angry with one to whom sins are unpunished, and the more he grows heavy. Whence, compared to lead, he goes down into the deep, the more so the more he sees those who have been justified by faith, and for the hope of the future life endure present evils, living in labors, in bearing which the Spirit of God strengthens them. God, then, sent his Spirit to console and exercise the just in labors; and the sea covered the impious, who not only thought there was no difference between themselves and them; but rather judged that God was angry with those who were afflicted with such great tribulations, but favorable to themselves, who rejoiced in such great prosperities. So *they went down like lead into the most mighty water. Who is like you among the gods, O Lord? who is like you? glorious in the saints*, who do not glory in themselves: *wonderful in majesties, who do prodigies.* For these things which were then done foretold something future, since they were figures of us. *You stretched out your right hand, the earth swallowed them up*[^16]. Surely no chasm of the earth swallowed up any of the Egyptians at that time; they were covered with water, they perished in the sea. What is it, then: *You stretched out your right hand, the earth swallowed them up*? Or do we rightly understand the right hand of God to be him of whom Isaiah says: *And the arm of the Lord, to whom has it been revealed?*[^17] For he is the only Son, whom the Father did not spare, but handed him over for us all[^18]. And so on the cross he stretched out his right hand, and the earth swallowed up the impious, when they thought themselves victors, and him contemptible. For *the earth was handed over into the hands of the impious one, and he covered the face of its judge*[^19], that is, his divinity. So the Lord governed his people, as it were carried by that wood, where the earth, that is, the flesh of the Lord stretched out, swallowed up the impious. For the people did not cross the sea by ship, so that "you governed" might be said properly. But *you governed by your justice your people*, not presuming of his own justice, but living by faith under your grace: *this your people, whom you freed*[^20]. For the Lord knows who are his[^21].

**3.** *You have exhorted in your strength*, that is, in your Christ. Because what is weak of God is stronger than men[^22]. And if he was crucified out of weakness, yet he lives by the power of God[^23]. *You have exhorted in your strength, and in your holy refreshment.* For in that the mortality of the flesh was refreshed in him through the resurrection, and in him this corruptible already put on incorruption, we have been exhorted hoping for the future, and on account of this enduring all present things. For after Baptism there remains a passage through the desert, through a life which is lived in hope, until we come to the land of promise, the land of the living where the Lord is our portion, to the eternal Jerusalem; until we come to which, this whole life is a desert for us, and a whole temptation. But in him who conquered the world, the people of God conquers all things. For just as in Baptism, like enemies pursuing from behind, past sins are blotted out: so after Baptism, on the journey of this life, when we eat spiritual food, and drink spiritual drink, we overcome all things opposing us. For the name of our Emperor terrified the enemies of our way. For first the wrath of the nations rose to destroy the Christian name; but where wrath could do nothing, it was turned into grief, and, with faith more and more growing and occupying all things, grief was turned into fear; so that even the proud of this world, like the birds of heaven, seek refuge and protection under the shade of that herb, which from a most minute grain of mustard grew much[^24]. So too in this canticle, where those things are mentioned which then happened in figure in them, the order of the wrath, grief, and fear of the nations was kept. *The nations*, he says, *heard, and were angry; pains seized the inhabitants of Philistia. Then the leaders of Edom hastened*, that is, were troubled, *and the princes of the Moabites: trembling seized them: all the inhabitants of Canaan melted away. Let trembling fall upon them, and the fear of the greatness of your arm. Let them become like stones, until your people pass over, O Lord, until this your people pass over, whom you acquired*[^25]. So it was done, so it is done. Astonished with wonder the enemies of the Church become like stones, until we pass over to the homeland. But those too who try to resist, as then Amalek with the hands of Moses stretched out[^26], so now in the sign of the Lord's cross are overcome. And so we are brought in, and planted in the mountain of the inheritance of the Lord, which from the small stone which Daniel saw, grew and filled the whole earth[^27]. This is the prepared dwelling of the Lord. For the temple of God is holy, and the sanctification of his house which is from him. *For the temple of God is holy*, says the Apostle, *which you are*[^28]. And lest anyone should look back to the earthly Jerusalem, where this temple bore a certain figure temporally, as was fitting, he signified that he was speaking of the eternal kingdom, which is the eternal inheritance of God, the eternal Jerusalem. For he says, following: *Which your hands prepared, O Lord, who reign always, and forever, and still*[^29]. Is there anything beyond *forever*? Who would say so? Why, then, did he add: *and still*? Perhaps because "forever" is wont to be understood also for a too-long time, for this reason it was added: *and still*, that the true "forever," which is without end, might be understood. Or because God reigns *always* in the heavenly things, which he established unto the age of the age, he set a precept and it will not pass away[^30]; *and forever* in those to whom, from the transgression of the precept afterward converted, he forgave their sins, and acquired them from time, and granted them blessedness without end; he reigns *and still* over those whom under the feet of his people he ordained in most just punishments? For no one is withdrawn from his kingdom, by whose eternal law in the distribution of giving and rendering both by the merits of rewards and punishments, and by the most just ordering, the whole creation is restrained. For God resists the proud, but to the humble he gives grace[^31]. *Because the cavalry of Pharaoh entered with chariots and riders into the sea; and the Lord brought upon them the waters of the sea. But the children of Israel walked on dry ground, through the midst of the sea*[^32].

**4.** This Moses and the children of Israel sang, this Miriam the prophetess and the daughters of Israel with her, this we too now, whether men and women, or our spirit and flesh. For *those who are of Jesus Christ*, says the Apostle, *have crucified their flesh with its passions and concupiscences*[^33]. This is fittingly understood to be signified by the timbrel which Miriam took up, to be in harmony with this canticle. For on wood the flesh is stretched out, that a timbrel may be made: and from the cross they learn to confess the sweet sound of grace. Made humble, then, through Baptism by the piety of grace, and with our pride there extinguished, through which the proud enemy had dominion over us, so that now he who glories may glory in the Lord[^34], *Let us sing to the Lord: for he has been gloriously magnified; horse and rider he has cast into the sea*[^35].
