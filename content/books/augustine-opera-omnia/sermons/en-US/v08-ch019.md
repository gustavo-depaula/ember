# Sermon 289: On the Birthday of John the Baptist

**1.** The occasion of our festive gathering today is the birthday of John the Baptist, whose wondrous conception and birth we heard about when the Gospel was read. A great mystery, my brothers and sisters! John’s mother was both barren and elderly, and his father was old: for both, all hope of offspring was gone. But because nothing is impossible for God, a son was promised to a man who did not believe. The father who lacked faith lost his voice, for it had already been written, *I believed, therefore I spoke*. He did not believe, and he did not speak. Meanwhile a virgin also conceived, a sublime miracle, far more excellent. A barren woman conceives the herald; a virgin conceives the judge. John is born of a man and a woman; Christ of a woman alone. Is John perhaps to be compared with Christ? Certainly not. Yet it was not for nothing that so great a man preceded one so great. For if, by the gracious gift of the Lord our God himself, who supplies strength for my effort, I can explain what I understand, my lowliness will not be abandoned and your expectation will not be disappointed. But if I am less able to explain what I understand, the Lord our God will supply in your hearts what he may withhold from me because of my weakness. I have said this beforehand because I know what I want to say and you do not, and I already feel how difficult it is to explain. Yet it needed to be commended to you so that, in your very attentiveness, you might pray for us.

**2.** Elizabeth conceived a man; Mary conceived a man. Elizabeth was John’s mother; Mary was Christ’s mother. But Elizabeth conceived only a man, while Mary conceived God and man. It is a wondrous thing: how could a creature conceive the Creator? What are we to understand, then, my brothers and sisters, except that he who made the first man without father or mother made flesh for himself from a mother alone? Our first fall occurred when the woman through whom we died conceived the serpent’s poison in her heart. For the serpent urged her to sin, and the evil counselor was admitted. If our first fall occurred when a woman conceived the serpent’s poison in her heart, it is no wonder that our salvation came about when a woman conceived the Almighty’s flesh in her womb. Both sexes had fallen; both had to be restored. Through a woman we had been sent to destruction; through a woman salvation was restored to us.

**3.** What, then, does John signify? Why does he come between? Why is he sent ahead? I will say, if I can. Our Lord Jesus Christ said of John, *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*. If John is compared with other human beings, that man surpasses them all; only God made man surpasses him. John was sent ahead of the Lord. There was such excellence in him, such grace, that he himself was thought to be the Christ. For the Jews were awaiting the Christ, since Christ had been foretold in the very prophets whom they too read. They awaited him when absent and killed him when present. Thinking that he was not the one, they fell away, while he remained. Yet not all fell away; many of the Jews believed. Thus, since Christ was expected, see John’s glory: when such grace was noticed in him, as he baptized for repentance and prepared the Lord’s way like an advance messenger marking out the route, the Jews sent to him and said, *Who are you? Are you Elijah, or the prophet? Or are you the Christ? I am not,* he said, *the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet. Then who,* they asked, *are you? I am,* he said, *the voice of one crying in the wilderness*. John answered the Jews who asked who he was and were already beginning to think he was the Christ, *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness.* If you were attentive, you heard the prophetic reading that was read first. There it is written, *The voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight. Every valley will be filled, and every mountain and hill brought low; crooked places will become straight and rough places level roads, and all flesh will see God’s salvation*. Then the Lord said through the prophet, *Cry out! And I said, What shall I cry out?* And there in the prophet the Lord says, *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of flesh is like the flower of grass. The grass has withered and the flower has fallen; but the word of the Lord remains forever*. John said, *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the way for the Lord.* That is to say, “The prophet foretold of me that I would be the one crying in the wilderness.” Thus it belongs to John to say, *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of flesh is like the flower of grass. The grass has withered and the flower has fallen; but the word of the Lord remains forever.* The Word is conceived in the Virgin’s womb; in the wilderness the voice proclaims the Word. If a voice contains no word, it is perhaps noise in the ears—though perhaps it could not even be called that. Every word is a voice; not every voice is a word. If a man opens his mouth and shouts as loudly as he can, that is a voice, not a word. What kind of voice, then, is called a word? Where something is understood, a meaningful voice is a word. But look: it has not yet sounded, and I want to say something; already the word is in my heart. There is a word in the heart and not yet a voice in the mouth. Thus there can be a word without a voice, and there can be a voice without a word. Add a voice to the word, and the word comes forth to be known. What, then, is Christ in Mary? The hidden Word. A voice was sent ahead to precede the Word. What is John? *The voice of one crying in the wilderness.* What is Christ? *In the beginning was the Word*. What are you, voice? What are you, human being? *All flesh is grass, and all human glory is like the flower of grass. The grass has withered and the flower has fallen; but the word of the Lord remains forever.* Hold fast to the Word, for the Word took on grass for your sake. Christ is the Word made flesh. But *all flesh is grass, and all the honor of flesh is like the flower of grass.* Let us despise present things and hope for things to come. *Every valley will be filled:* all humility will be exalted. *And every mountain and hill will be brought low:* all pride will be cast down. Lower the mountains, fill the valleys, and the plain is made level. Show me people rich and honored through the flower of grass; let them hear, *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*. Show me poor people in despair, conscious of their weakness; let them not despair, but believe in him who came for all. Let these be raised up and those brought down. Let the one who is coming find a plain, not a stone against which to strike his foot. For that is why John himself said, *Prepare the way for the Lord:* not for me but for the Lord, by whom I was sent, not for what I am.

**4.** But the Jews say, *Are you the Christ*? If he had been not a valley to be filled but a mountain to be brought low, he would have found an opportunity to deceive. For they wanted to hear from him something they could believe. They so admired his grace that they would undoubtedly believe whatever he said. Here was an opportunity to deceive the human race: if he said, “I am the Christ,” they would believe him. If he boasted in another’s name, he would lose his own merit. If he boasted as though he were Christ, should he not answer himself, “Why are you exalting yourself? *All flesh is grass, and its glory is like the flower of grass. The grass has withered, the flower has fallen.* Understand what remains forever: *But the word of the Lord remains forever.*” He recognized himself; rightly the Lord called him a lamp. The Lord said this of John: *He was a burning and shining lamp, and you wished to rejoice for a time in his light*. But what does John the Evangelist say of him? *There was a man sent from God whose name was John. He came as a witness, to bear witness to the light; he was not the light.* Who? John the Baptist. Who says this? John the Evangelist: *He was not the light, but came to bear witness to the light.* You say, *He was not the light,* of the man of whom the light itself says, *He was a burning and shining lamp.* “But I know,” he says, “what light I mean; I know the light in comparison with which a lamp is not light.” Hear what follows: *He was the true light that enlightens every person coming into this world*. John does not enlighten every person; Christ enlightens every person. And John recognized that he was a lamp, so that he would not be extinguished by the wind of pride. A lamp can be both lit and extinguished. God’s Word cannot be extinguished; a lamp always can.

**5.** Thus the greatest man was sent to bear witness to one who was more than a man. For when he than whom no greater has arisen among those born of women says, *I am not the Christ,* and humbles himself before Christ, Christ must be understood as something more than a man. For if you seek the greatest man, it is John; Christ is more than a man. Understand the forerunner in such a way that you seek the judge; hear the herald in such a way that you fear the judge. He was sent and foretold the one who would come. And what testimony does John bear to Christ? Hear what kind: *I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandal*. Did you understand, man, what you were doing? *Everyone who humbles himself will be exalted*. What, then, of Christ? *We have all received from his fullness*. What does *we all* mean? All the patriarchs, prophets, and holy apostles, whether sent ahead before the Incarnation or sent by the incarnate one: *we have all received from his fullness.* We are vessels; he is the fountain. Thus, if we have understood the mystery, my brothers and sisters, John is a man, Christ is God: let man be humbled and God exalted. So that man might be humbled, John was born on the day when the days begin to grow shorter. So that God might be exalted, Christ was born on the day when the days begin to grow longer. A great mystery! That is why we celebrate John’s birthday as we celebrate Christ’s, because the birth itself is full of mystery. What mystery? That of our exaltation. Let us decrease in the human and increase in God. Let us be humbled in ourselves, so that we may be exalted in him. The mystery of this great reality was fulfilled in the sufferings of both. So that man might be diminished, John lost his head; so that God might be exalted, Christ was hung on the wood. John was sent for this purpose: that we might imitate him and hold fast to the Word. However much human pride boasts of any excellence in holiness, who will be what John was? Whoever you are who think yourself great, you will not be what John was. He was not yet born, and already, leaping for joy in the womb, he foretold the Lord who was to be born. What is more excellent than this holiness? Imitate him; hear what he says of Christ: *We have received from his fullness.* A lamp in the night points out the fountain to you, and he himself drinks from it: *For we,* he says, *have all received from his fullness. We all:* he is the fountain, we are vessels; he is the day, we are lamps. Great is human weakness: through a lamp we seek the day.

**6.** But the apostles too, my brothers and sisters, are lamps of the day. Do not suppose that John alone is a lamp and the apostles are not. The Lord says to them, *You are the light of the world.* And lest they think they were light in the same sense as the one called light, of whom it was said, *He was the true light that enlightens every person coming into this world*, he immediately taught them about that true light itself. After saying, *You are the light of the world,* he added, *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel.* “When I called you light, I called you a lamp. Do not exult in your pride, lest the little flame be extinguished. I do not put you under a bushel; you will be on a lampstand, so that you may shine.” What is the lamp’s stand? Hear about the lampstand: be lamps, and you will have a lampstand. Christ’s cross is the great lampstand. Whoever wishes to shine must not be ashamed of the wooden lampstand. Listen, so that you may understand that the lampstand is Christ’s cross: *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, so that it may shine for everyone in the house. Let your light so shine before people that they may see your good deeds and glorify*—not as you seek to be glorified, for that is to seek extinction—*glorify your Father who is in heaven*. Through your good works let them glorify your Father. To become lamps, you could not light yourselves or place yourselves on the lampstand. Let him who granted you this be glorified. Hear, then, the Apostle Paul; hear a lamp exulting on the lampstand. *But for me,* he says—those who know what follows are calling it out—*but for me.* What about you? *Far be it from me to glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ.* “I glory in the lampstand; if the lampstand withdraws, I fall. *Far be it from me to glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me and I to the world*.” You have praised and applauded. Let the world be crucified to you, and be crucified to the world. What does this mean? Do not seek happiness from the world; keep yourselves apart from worldly happiness. The world flatters: beware the corrupter. The world threatens: do not fear the attacker. If the world’s good things have not corrupted you, if the world’s evils have not corrupted you, the world is crucified to you and you to the world. Glory in the lampstand. Lamp, always preserve humility on the lampstand, so that you may retain your brightness; take care not to be extinguished by pride. Preserve what you have been made, so that you may glory in your maker. For what were you, human being? Every human being, consider how you were born: even if you were born noble, you were born naked. What is nobility? The birth of the poor and the rich is the same nakedness. Or perhaps, because you were born noble, you live as long as you wish? You entered without knowing when; you leave when you do not wish. Finally, let the graves be examined, and let the rich people’s bones be identified.
