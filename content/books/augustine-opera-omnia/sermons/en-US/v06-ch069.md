# Sermon 229: On the Sacraments of the Faithful

**1.** This which you see, dearest ones, on the table of the Lord, is bread and wine; but this bread and this wine, the word being added, becomes the body and blood of the Word. For that Lord, who *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^1], on account of his mercy, by which he did not despise what he created in his own image, *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^2], as you know; for the Word itself took up man, that is, the soul and flesh of a man, and was made man, remaining God. On account of this, because he also suffered for us, he commended to us in this sacrament his body and blood, which he also made us ourselves. For we too have been made his body, and through his mercy, what we receive, we are. Recall what this creature once was in the field, how the earth bore it, the rain nourished it, brought it to the ear; then human labor carried it to the threshing floor, threshed it, winnowed it, stored it, brought it out, ground it, kneaded it, baked it, and scarcely at last brought it to bread. Recall yourselves too: you were not, and you were created, you were carried to the Lord's threshing floor, you were threshed by the labors of oxen, that is, of those announcing the Gospel. When as catechumens you were deferred, you were kept in the granary. You gave your names; you began to be ground by fasts and exorcisms. Afterward you came to the water, and were kneaded, and were made one. With the fervor of the Holy Spirit coming, you were baked, and made the Lord's bread.

**2.** Behold what you have received. As, then, you see that what was made is one, so be you one too, by loving one another, by holding one faith, one hope, an undivided love. The heretics, when they receive this, receive a testimony against themselves; because they seek division, while this bread indicates unity. So too the wine was in many grapes, and now is one; it is one in the sweetness of the cup, but after the pressing of the winepress. And you too, after those fasts, after labors, after humility and contrition, have now in the name of Christ come, as it were, to the cup of the Lord; and there you are on the table, and there you are in the cup. With us you are this: for together we take it, together we drink, because together we live.

**3.** You are about to hear what you also heard yesterday, but today there is set out for you what you heard, and what you answered—or perhaps, when the answer was given, you kept silent, though yesterday you learned what was to be answered today. After the greeting that you know, that is, *The Lord be with you,* you heard: *Lift up your heart.* The whole life of true Christians is: *Lift up your heart*; not of Christians in name only, but of Christians in the very reality and truth, the whole life is: *lift up your heart.* What is it to *lift up your heart*? It is to set your hope in God, not in yourself; for you are below, God is above. If you have your hope in yourself, your heart is below, it is not lifted up. Therefore, when you have heard from the priest, *Lift up your heart,* you answer: *We have lifted it up to the Lord.* Strive to answer truly, for you answer in the presence of the acts of God; let it be as you say, let not the tongue make the sound and the conscience deny it. And because it is God who grants you this very thing, that you may have your *heart* lifted *up,* not your own strength, therefore, when you have said that *you* have *your heart lifted up* to the Lord, the priest goes on and says: *Let us give thanks to the Lord our God.* Why should we give thanks? Because we have our *heart lifted up,* and unless he had raised it, we would be lying on the ground. And from there proceed the things that are done in the holy prayers that you are about to hear, that by the coming of the word the body and blood of Christ may be made present. For take away the word, and it is bread and wine; add the word, and now it is something else. And that something else, what is it? The body of Christ, and the blood of Christ. Take away the word, then, and it is bread and wine; add the word, and it becomes a sacrament. To this you say: *Amen.* To say *Amen* is to subscribe. *Amen* in Latin means "true." Then the Lord's Prayer is said, which you have already received and given back. Why is it said before the body and blood of Christ are received? Because, such is human frailty, if perhaps our thought has conceived something improper, if the tongue has poured out something it ought not, if the eye has looked upon something it ought not, if the ear has heard something too flattering that it ought not, if perhaps such things have been contracted from the temptation of this world and the frailty of human life, they are wiped away by the Lord's Prayer, where it is said: *Forgive us our debts*[^3], that we may approach in security, lest we eat and drink unto judgment for ourselves what we receive. After this is said: *Peace be with you.* A great sacrament, the kiss of peace; kiss in such a way that you love. Do not be a Judas: Judas the betrayer kissed Christ with his mouth while plotting against him in his heart. But perhaps someone has a hostile mind against you, and you cannot convict him or correct him: you are compelled to bear with him. Do not render him evil for evil in your heart[^4]; let him hate, but you love, and kiss in security. You have heard few things, but great ones; let them not seem cheap because they are few, but be dear for their weight. At the same time you are not to be overburdened, so that you may hold fast what has been said.

[^1]: John 1:1
[^2]: John 1:14
[^3]: Matt 6:12
[^4]: See Rom 12:17
