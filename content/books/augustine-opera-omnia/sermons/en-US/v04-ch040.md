# Sermon 124

**1.** Most recently the gospel reading sounded in our ears, and made us attentive to learn what the things read meant. This, I judge, is expected of me, this I promise, with the Lord helping, to expound according to my strength. For without doubt those miracles were not done in vain, and figured something for us toward eternal salvation. For the health of the body that was restored to the man, for how long a time was it? *For what is your life*, says holy Scripture? *It is a vapor*, it says, *appearing for a little while; thereafter it will be wiped out* [^1]. That health was restored to the body of that man for a time, some duration was restored to a vapor. Therefore it is not to be held as a great thing: *Vain is the salvation of man* [^2]. And recall, brothers, that prophetic and gospel saying, because in the Gospel this testimony is read: *All flesh is grass, and all the honor of the flesh like the flower of grass; the grass withered, the flower fell; the Word of the Lord abides forever* [^3]. The Word of the Lord gives honor even to grass, and an honor not transitory: for it gives the flesh too immortality.

**2.** But first the tribulation of this life passes, in which he furnishes us help, to whom we said: *Give us help from tribulation* [^4]. For this whole life, to those who understand, is a tribulation. For there are two torturers of the soul, not torturing at the same time, but alternating the torment. The names of these two torturers are fear and grief. When it is well with you, you fear; when it is ill, you grieve. Whom does the prosperity of this world not deceive, whom does its adversity not break? In this grass and in the days of grass the safer way must be held, the Word of God. For when it was said: *All flesh is grass, and all the honor of the flesh like the flower of grass; the grass withered, the flower fell*; as though we ask: What hope for grass? What stability for the flower of grass? but *the Word*, it says, *of the Lord abides forever* [^5]. And whence, you say, comes the Word of the Lord to me? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us* [^6]. For the Word of the Lord says to you: Do not spurn my promise, because I did not spurn your grass. This, then, which the Word of the Lord bestowed on us, that we should hold to it, lest we pass away with the flower of grass; this, then, which it bestowed on us, that the Word should become flesh, taking on flesh, not changed into flesh; remaining and assuming, remaining what it was, assuming what it was not; this, then, which it bestowed on us, that pool too signifies.

**3.** Briefly I say: that water was the Jewish people; the five porticoes, the Law. For Moses wrote five books. So the water was surrounded by five porticoes, as that people was constrained by the law. The troubling of the water is, in that people, the Lord's passion. He who went down was healed, only one: because that is unity. To whomever this passion of Christ is displeasing, they are proud; they refuse to go down, they are not healed. And I, he says, am I to believe God in the flesh, God born of a woman, God crucified, scourged, dead, wounded, buried? Far be it that I believe this of God; it is unworthy. Let the heart speak, not the neck. To the proud the humility of the Lord seems unworthy, therefore from such health is far off. Do not lift yourself up: if you want to be healed, go down. Devotion ought to take fright, if Christ were said to be changeable in the flesh. But now the truth commends to you an unchangeable Christ, as far as pertains to the Word. *For in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*; not as though it sounded, and passed away; because *God was the Word* [^7]. Your God, then, remains unchangeable. O true devotion: your God remains; do not fear, he does not perish; and through him you too do not perish. He remains, he is born of a woman; but in the flesh. But the Word also made the mother. He who was before he came to be, made for himself her in whom he might come to be. He was an infant; but in the flesh. He sucked, grew, took food, ran through the ages, came to youth; but in the flesh. Wearied, he slept, but in the flesh. He suffered hunger and thirst; but in the flesh. Seized, bound, scourged, afflicted with insults, at last crucified, killed; but in the flesh. Why do you take fright? *The Word of the Lord abides forever* [^8]. He who spurns this humility of God does not want for himself health from the deadly swelling of pride.

**4.** So the Lord Jesus Christ bestowed through his flesh hope on our flesh. For he took on what we knew in this earth, what here abounded, to be born and to die. To be born and to die, here abounded; to rise again and to live forever, was not here. He found here cheap earthly wares, he brought heavenly wares from a foreign land. If you take fright at death, love resurrection. He gave you help from his tribulation: for your salvation had remained vain. So, brothers, let us acknowledge and love the salvation that is a foreigner in this world, that is, everlasting, and in this world let us live as foreigners. Let us think that we pass on, and we shall sin less. Let us rather give thanks to the Lord our God, that he willed the last day of this life to be both brief and uncertain. From first infancy to decrepit old age is a brief span. What it would have profited Adam to have lived so long, if he had died today? What is long, where there is an end? No one calls back yesterday: today is pressed by tomorrow, to pass on. In that small span let us live well, that we may go there from where we do not pass on. Even now, while we speak, surely we pass on. The words run, and the hours fly; so our age, so our acts, so our honors, so our misery, so this happiness of ours. The whole passes, but let us not take fright: *The Word of the Lord abides forever* [^9]. Turned toward the Lord, and so on.
