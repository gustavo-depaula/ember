# Sermon 301/A: On the Feast of the Holy Maccabees

[Delivered at Bulla Regia]

**1.** The Gospel and the living word of God, penetrating the marrow of the soul and searching out the hinge of the heart, is wholesomely set forth to us all, and flatters no one, if a man does not flatter himself. Behold, there is set before us a kind of mirror, in which we may all look at ourselves, and, if anything spotted on our face happens to meet our gaze, let us wipe it away with careful attention, lest we blush again when we look into the mirror. For the crowd was following the Lord, as we heard when the Gospel was read, and turning he spoke to those who were following him. For if he had spoken what he said to those twelve apostles alone, each one of us could say: He said it to them, not to us. One thing seems to pertain to the shepherds, another to the flocks. He spoke to the following crowds: therefore he spoke to us all and to you all. We must not think it was not said to us because we did not yet exist then; for we too believe in him whom they saw; him we hold by faith whom they beheld with their eyes. For it was no great thing to see Christ with the eyes of flesh; if this had been a great thing, the Jewish nation would have found salvation first. For surely they too saw, and yet despised him, and over and above killed him whom they saw and despised; but we surely did not see, and yet we believe, and have received in our heart him whom we did not behold with our eyes. So he said to a certain one of his, who was then among the twelve: *Because you have seen, you have believed: blessed are those who do not see, and believe*[^1]. For if our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ were now present in the flesh, and stood in silence, what would it profit us? But if by speaking he profited, he speaks even now, when the Gospel is recited. Yet by his presence too he furnishes much, as God. But where is God not? or when is God absent? Be you not absent from God, and God is with you; especially because he himself promised, and we hold his promise as a kind of bond written out: *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age*[^2]. But he foresaw us, he was promising to us.

**2.** Let us return, then, and hear what he said, and, as I said, let us look at ourselves, and whatever we find lacking in us, let us cultivate with all diligence toward the form of beauty that pleases his eyes. And, since we are not sufficient, let us call him to our help. Let him reform who formed, let him recreate who created, that he may restore us perfect who founded us. So he said this: *What man, wishing to build a tower, does not first sit down and reckon whether he has the means to finish it? lest perhaps he begin to build, and not complete it, and men passing by say: Behold a man who began to build, and was not able to finish. Or again, what king, going to wage war against another king, does not first consider whether he is fit to meet with ten thousand him who has twenty thousand? Otherwise, while the other is still far off, he sends envoys to seek peace.* And to these two likenesses he concluded thus: *So, whoever does not renounce all that he has, cannot be my disciple*[^3]. If only those disciples had been addressed, it would not have been said to us; but since, as Scripture testifies, all Christians are disciples of Christ—*For one*, he says, *is your master, Christ*[^4]—let only that man deny himself to be Christ's disciple who denies Christ to be his master. Nor are we therefore your masters because we speak to you from this higher place: for he is the master of all whose chair is above all the heavens; under him we come together into one school, and both you and we are fellow disciples; but we admonish you, as greater schools are wont. The tower and the means are faith and patience; faith is the tower, patience the means. If anyone is impatient in enduring the evils of this world, he has failed of the means. The evil king with twenty thousand is the devil, the king with ten thousand is the Christian. The single against the double, truth against falsehood, because simplicity against duplicity. Be of a simple heart: do not be a hypocrite, showing one thing and hiding another; and you will conquer that double one, who transforms himself as an angel of light[^5]. Whence are those means? Where is that perfect simplicity, altogether stable and unshakeably persevering? In what follows, which seems hard. This is what I said, that the word of God flatters no one. *So*, he says, *whoever does not renounce all that he has, cannot be my disciple*. Many have done this, and examined themselves, before the time of persecution pressed, and renounced all that is of the world, and followed Christ. Of these were the apostles, who said: *Behold, we have left all, and have followed you*[^6]. Nor did they themselves leave anything great, because they were all poor; but they are judged to have left great means who have conquered all covetousness.

**3.** Then the disciples said this to the Lord, when that rich man went away sad, who had heard the counsel of eternal life, as he had asked, from the most truthful master. For a certain rich young man came to the Lord, and said to him: *Good master, what good shall I do, that I may obtain eternal life?*[^7] I believe that amid the most abundant delights of his riches he was pricked by the goad of his coming death, and was pining away; and because he knew that he could carry none of the things he possessed with him to the underworld, his soul, amid the great abundance of the flesh, groaned in want. He was saying, as we must suppose, within himself, surrounded by the abundance of his riches: They are good, they are beautiful, they are delightful, they are sweet; but when that last hour comes, all must be left behind. None of them must be carried away from here. There remains life and conscience alone; there remains, after the body, the life of the soul and conscience alone. And if this will be not now life but a second death, and worse to be called: for nothing is worse than that death, where death does not die. Because he was thinking this amid his delights, having such great goods, he came to the Lord. For he said to himself: After such great goods, if I shall have eternal life too, what is happier than I? Therefore, since he was anxious, he asked, and said: *Good master, what shall I do, that I may obtain eternal life?* The Lord answered him first: *Why do you ask me about the good? No one is good but the one God*[^8]. This is to say: No one makes you blessed but the one God. The things the rich have are good indeed: but those good things do not make men good. For, if those good things made men good, each one would be the better the more he abounded in them. But since we see that many, the more abundant, are the worse, without doubt other goods are to be sought, which make men good. They are the goods that cannot be had by the evil: justice, piety, temperance, religion, love, the worship of God, finally God himself. To that good we ought to run: him, unless these are despised, we shall not attain.

**4.** Shall I flatter you, when the Gospel flatters neither you nor us? So I exhort your love, brethren, as the apostle says: *The time is short. It remains*, he says, *that those who have wives be as though they had none, and those who weep as though they wept not, and those who rejoice as though they rejoiced not, and those who buy as though they bought not, and those who use this world as though they used it not*[^9]. So the apostles then let go of what they had, and therefore Peter said: *Behold, we have left all*[^10]. What did you leave, Peter? One little boat, and one net? He would answer me: I left the whole world, I who left nothing for myself. The poverty of all, that is, every poor man, has small means, but has great covetousness. God does not attend to what one has, but to what one wills; the will is judged, which is invisibly searched out by him who is not seen. So they left all, and indeed left the whole world, because they cut off whatever they hoped for in this world, and followed him by whom the world was made, and believed his promises; and afterward many did this. And it is a wonder, my brethren: who did this? Those very ones did it who killed the Lord. There in Jerusalem, when the Lord had ascended into heaven, and after ten days, with the Holy Spirit sent, fulfilled his promise, the disciples, filled with the Holy Spirit, spoke in the tongues of all nations[^11]. Then many Jews who were in Jerusalem, hearing and alarmed at the gift of the Savior's grace, marveling, and astonished, doubting among themselves whence it was, received an answer from the apostles, that he had furnished this by his Spirit whom they had killed, and they sought the counsel of salvation. For they had despaired, and did not think so great a crime could be forgiven them, who had killed the Lord of all creation; and they received consolation from the apostles. With pardon promised, with impunity promised, they believed; and selling all that they had, they laid the prices of their goods at the feet of the apostles[^12]—the more terrified, the more good. Greater fear wrung out their delights. Those did this who killed the Lord; many did it afterward, and many do it. We know it, we behold the examples, in many we are consoled, in many we delight, because the word of God is not idle in those who faithfully hear. But some did not do it, and when persecution came they were tested, because they used the world as though they used it not. Not commoners only, not any sort of craftsmen, not the poor, not the needy, not the middling, but many even great and rich men, senators, most noble women too, when persecution came, renounced all that was theirs, that they might finish the tower, and by the simplicity of fortitude and piety conquer the double and deceitful devil.

**5.** So, exhorting to martyrdom, the Lord Christ said to all: *So, whoever does not renounce all that he has, cannot be my disciple*[^13]. I ask you then, O Christian soul. If I say to you what was said to the rich man: *Go, sell* you too *all that you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven, and come, follow* Christ[^14], will you too perhaps go away sad? For so that young man too went away sad: yet these words a Christian can only hear. When the Gospel was read, could you shut your ears against your own salvation? You heard: *Whoever does not renounce all that he has, cannot be my disciple*. Think within yourself: you have been made one of the faithful, you have been baptized, you have believed, you have not let go your means. But I ask your faith: how did you believe? Behold, peril comes to your faith; it is said to you: If you persist, I take away what you have. I ask your soul. If you say in your soul: Let him take away what I have, I do not let go my faith; then you both hold and have renounced. Because you hold, you are not held. For it is not evil to hold: to be held is the evil. But persecution is lacking, and there is no way to prove what you promised the Lord. Daily affairs prove men. For what if at some time someone calls you to false witness, and he is powerful who can be feared for a time, and, if he threaten, can for a time do harm, and he persuades you to bear false witness? He does not say to you: Deny Christ; for against this you were preparing yourself. In another way that double one has crept in, against what you were not pondering, what you were not setting before yourself. Bear, he says, false witness: if you do not, I will do this and that. He threatens proscription, he threatens death. There prove yourself, there attend to yourself. Do you bear false witness? You have let go Christ, for he himself said: *I am the truth*[^15]. You bore false witness, you acted against the truth: therefore you let go Christ. And what was he going to do to you by threatening proscription, by making you needy? What would you lack, with whom God was? But he threatened more. What is that more? He threatened to kill your flesh. Your soul? You attend to what he threatens: you do not attend to what you do. He threatens to kill the flesh: *But the mouth that lies kills the soul*[^16]. You are two, the enemy and you; yet he too a man, and you: both corruptible according to the flesh, both immortal according to the soul, both to pass on for a time, and in this land guests and pilgrims. He threatens death, not knowing whether he may die before he fulfills what he threatens; but yet suppose that he fulfills what he threatens; I sift you, let us see who is the worse enemy to you, whether he or you. He draws his sword to kill your flesh; you draw your false tongue to slaughter your soul. Who struck more grievously? Who killed worse? Who penetrated more inwardly? He as far as the bones, he as far as the inner parts; you as far as the heart. You left nothing whole for yourself, when you lost your heart: *The mouth that lies kills*, he said, not the flesh, but the soul.

**6.** Such are the daily temptations of men. When it comes to iniquity, that you either do iniquity, or suffer what God wills you to suffer for a time, there now attend to that double one, there now attend to the means of that tower. But if you fail in thinking, call upon him who commanded. Let him help his own commands in you, and he will render to you his promises from himself. For what does God promise us? My brethren, what shall I say, that we may desire it? What shall I say? Is it gold? Is it silver? Are they estates? Are they honors? Is it anything we know on earth? It is cheap. *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love him*[^17]. Briefly I say, not his promises, but himself. Greater than all is he who made all things; more beautiful than all is he who formed all things; mightier than all is he who gave power to all. So whatever we love on earth, in comparison with God is nothing. It is little: it is nothing that we love; and we ourselves are nothing. The lover himself, in comparison with the thing that is to be loved, ought to become cheap in his own eyes. This is that love which is commanded *with the whole heart, with the whole soul, and with the whole mind*. But he added and said: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets*[^18]; so that, when you love the Lord, you may know that then you love yourself, if you love the Lord. But if you do not love God, neither do you love yourself. So when you have learned to love yourself by loving God, draw your neighbor to God, that you may enjoy the good together, and so great a good, which is God.

**7.** Just now we beheld the great contest of the seven brothers and their mother. What a contest, my brethren, if our minds know how to behold it! Compare with this holy spectacle the pleasures and delights of the theaters. There the eyes are defiled, here hearts are cleansed; here the spectator is praiseworthy, if he becomes an imitator; there, however, the spectator too is shameful, and the imitator infamous. In short, I love the martyrs, I behold the martyrs: when the sufferings of the martyrs are read, I behold them. Say to me: Be such, and you have praised. You, behold the mime, behold the pantomime; I will say to you: Be such, and do not be angry. But if I say to you: Be such, and you are angry, it is not my words but your anger that makes you guilty. By being angry you judge about yourself: behold what you love, what you dread to be. Opportunely, from the spectacle of the holy Maccabees, whose victories we commemorate today, your love seemed to me to need admonishing about theatrical spectacles. O brethren of Bulla, all around, in nearly all the cities near you, the wantonness of impiety has fallen silent. Are you not ashamed that among you alone shameful filth has remained for sale? Or does it please you, amid grain, wine, oil, animals, cattle, and whatever is sold in the Roman markets or fairs, to buy and sell filthiness too? And perhaps strangers come here for such trafficking, and it is said: What do you seek? mimes, harlots? You have them at Bulla. Do you think this glory? I do not know whether there is greater infamy. Altogether, my brethren, out of grief I say it, the neighborhood of the other cities condemns you in the sight of men and in the judgment of God. Whoever wishes to imitate evil sets you up as the model. To our Hippo, where now such things have almost ceased, these shameful persons are brought from your city. But perhaps you say: We are like Carthage. As at Carthage there is a holy and religious people, so there is so great a crowd in the great city that all excuse themselves by reference to others. Pagans do it, Jews do it—this can be said at Carthage; here, whoever do it, Christians do it. With great grief we say these things to you: would that one day by your correction the wound of our heart might be healed! We say to your love: We know, in the name of God, both your city and those near you, how great is the multitude here, how great the people; can you all be other than known to him who has been appointed your steward of word and sacrament? Who is excused from this filthiness? Behold, there are the games: let Christians not go, and let us see whether there will not be such a solitude that the very filthiness will blush for itself. Let us see whether the shameful persons themselves will not either be converted to the Lord and be set free, or, if they remain in their filthiness, will migrate from this city. Do this for yourselves, you Christians: do not enter the theaters.

**8.** But I see few of you. Behold, the day of Christ's suffering will come, behold, Easter will come, and these spaces will not hold your multitude. So will they fill these places, who now have filled the theaters? At least compare the places, and beat your breasts. Perhaps you say: It is well that you abstain from these things, you who are clergy, who are bishops, but not we laity. Does this saying really seem just to you? For what are we, except for your sake? It is one thing what we are for our own sake; another, what we are for your sake. We are Christians for our own sake, clergy and bishops only for your sake. The apostle was not speaking to clergy, not to bishops and presbyters, when he said: *You*, he says, *are members of Christ*. He was speaking to the people, to the faithful, to Christians: *You are members of Christ*[^19]. Attend to what body you are members in, attend under what head you live in one structure of the body; see the one Spirit, which you have received from him. I repeat the apostle's own words: *Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them members of a harlot?*[^20] And do our Christians not only love, but even set up, harlots? They not only love those who were already such, but set up those who were not; as if they too did not have souls, as if the blood of Christ were not poured out for them too, as if it had not been said: *Harlots and tax collectors go before you into the kingdom of heaven*[^21]. So, when we ought to gain them, one chooses rather to perish with them. And this is done by Christians; I will not say, even by the faithful. Perhaps a catechumen despises himself. I am, he says, a catechumen. You are a catechumen? A catechumen. Your forehead received the sign of Christ, and do you carry it to the theater? Do you wish to go? Change your forehead, and go. So do not lose the forehead you cannot change. The name of God is invoked over you, Christ is invoked over you, God is invoked over you, the sign of the cross of Christ is painted and fixed on your forehead. I exhort all, I address all; you will see how much more honorable you will be in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**9.** I dare to say: Imitate your neighboring city, imitate the neighboring city of Simitthu. Nothing else do I say to you. More openly I say to you in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ; no one there enters the theater, no shameful person has remained there. An envoy there wished to carry on filthiness of this kind; no leading citizen, no commoner entered, no Jew entered. Are they not themselves honorable? Is that not a city? Is it not a colony, the more honorable the more empty of these things? We would not say these things to you, if we heard good things of you; but if we were silent, I fear lest we be judged together with you. So God willed, my brethren, that I should pass this way. My brother held me, ordered, asked, compelled me to speak a word to you. Whence should I do it, except whence I most fear? Whence should I do it, except whence I most grieve? Do you not know that I and all of us must render a most grievous account to God for your praises? Do you think those praises honor us? They burden, they do not honor. A very grievous account is rendered for those praises; I greatly fear lest Christ say to us at his judgment: Evil servants, you gladly received the praises of my people, and kept silent to them about their death. But the Lord our God will grant that henceforth we hear good of you, and in his mercy are consoled by your correction: for the joy will be the greater, the greater is the sadness now.
