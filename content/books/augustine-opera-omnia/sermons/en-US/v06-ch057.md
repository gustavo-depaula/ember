# Sermon 223/G: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** This holy celebration, brothers, which has taken night out of night, putting the darkness to flight by these lamps, and gladdening our faith as the day of the heart, is carried on, as you know, in memory of the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ. For what could be more fitting than that our vigil should frequent his waking from the dead, that the members, although still about to sleep, should by watching meanwhile sound in unison with their already ever-waking Head, and they too likewise about to awake, and with him about to reign in a perpetual vigil without any sleep? For fittingly, at a certain time, so great a festivity signifies what eternity will have without end of time. Let us watch, then, for the watching Christ, and for a little while, as much as we can, let us abstain from sleep, in honor of him whom no sleep holds. Let us be in his keeping, according to the spirit, the true Israel: *For he will not slumber nor sleep, who keeps Israel*[^1]. Watching for this most vigilant keeper by a yearly solemnity, let us bind our heart in his hand by the bond of faith, that, suspended by this binding from him who knows not how to sleep, it may not fall; until, whole and entire, mortality and corruption being consumed, we are gathered into his framework, where neither can we any longer sleep or slumber.

**2.** This is the fruit of our vigils, this the end of the intent eyes not of the flesh but of the spirit, this the just and holy purpose of reining in and restraining sleep, this the incorruptible reward of the labor borne and the love roused: that he for whom, struggling a little against earthly torpor, we watch, may give us life, where there is a vigil without labor, a day without night, rest without sleep. Not, then, is it praiseworthy to watch, for robbers too watch; but to this end, that, lying in wait for the sleep of husbands, they may come to their wives, the night acting as pander. The followers of magic arts too watch; but to this end, that they may serve demons, and by their aid commit nefarious things. It is long, and not necessary, to recall the vigils of all the wicked. But, to speak of certain even innocent vigils, craftsmen watch, farmers, sailors, fishermen, travelers, merchants, administrators of whatever affairs, judges, advocates, buyers and sellers of letters, those endowed with power, those subject to powers, and whatever there is of arts or industry by which human life is got through; but to this end, that the earth may be inhabited either more conveniently or more becomingly by its sojourners about to pass on with vaporous speed. Finally, the end of all who so watch, the unlawful, is damned by eternal death, and the lawful is consumed by temporal death. *But the end of the law is Christ unto righteousness to everyone who believes*[^2], in regard to whom we watch, who is the end of perfection, claiming us from the end either of damnation or of consumption. And so those, watching either harmfully or harmlessly, yet behold and seek an end about to perish; but our end has no end. Finally, those watch, in that thing to which they desire to come, not about to have a remaining; we watch and pray, lest we enter into temptation. For so we conquer the one who lies in wait for our journey; so we lay hold of him with whom we will remain, the Savior.

[^1]: Ps 121:4 (120:4)
[^2]: Rom 10:4
