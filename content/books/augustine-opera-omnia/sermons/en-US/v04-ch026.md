# Sermon 113a

**1.** The faith of Christians, which is mocked by the impious and unbelieving, is this: that we say there is another life after this life, and that there is a resurrection from the dead, and that there is, after this age has passed, at the end a judgment. Since this was not believed among human affairs, but was preached and announced by the Prophets, the servants of God, and by the law that was given through Moses, and still seemed incredible to men, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ came, to persuade men of this. He, although he is the Son of God, born of the Father invisibly and ineffably, coeternal with the Father, and equal to the Father, and with the Father one God, although he is the Word of the Father, through whom all things were made [^1], and the counsel of the Father, through whom all things are governed, laid down so great a greatness of his, and his incomprehensible majesty and power, which could not be known by men, to the earth, by taking flesh, and appearing to the eyes of men. Since, then, God, that is, the divinity itself, was not seen in Christ, the flesh that was seen was despised; but he proved his inner divinity by miracles; and when he seemed such as could be spurned by human eyes, he did so great things, that in his very works the Son of God might appear. Since, then, he did great things, commanded useful things, corrected vices, chastised, taught virtues, even wrought healings of bodies, that he might heal the minds of the unbelieving, the angry people, where he was born and reared and did these great things, killed him. But he, who had come to be born, had surely come also to die; nor did he want the death of his flesh, which he had taken on for the sake of demonstrating the resurrection by example, to be unfruitful, but rather allowed it by the hands of the impious, so that, when they refused to do what he commanded, he might suffer what he willed. It was done; Christ was killed, buried, rose again, as we know, as the Gospel attests, as it has already preached throughout the whole world; and still you see that the Jews refuse to believe in Christ, even now after he rose from the dead, and glorified in the eyes of his disciples ascended into heaven, while now the proclamations of the Prophets are fulfilled throughout the whole world. For all the Prophets, who foretold that Christ would be born, and would die, and would rise again, and would ascend into heaven, also foretold that his Church would be throughout all nations. But the Jews, if they did not see Christ rising, and going to heaven, would at least see the Church spread throughout the whole world, which surely, when it was being fulfilled, the foretold sayings of the Prophets were being fulfilled.

**2.** In them is fulfilled what we have just now heard from the Gospel; for they therefore do not hear Christ, who rose from the dead, because they did not hear Christ set on earth. For this Abraham said to that rich man, who was tormented in hell, and wanted someone to be sent to those above, to announce to his brothers what is done in hell, and that, before they came to those places of torments, they might live well, doing penance for their sins, that they might deserve to go rather into Abraham's bosom, not to those torments where that rich man arrived. Since, then, that rich man did this, late merciful, who had despised the poor man lying before his gate, and therefore perhaps because he was proud toward him, his very tongue was burning, and there he desired a drop of water. Since, then, he had not done among those above what he ought to have done so as not to come there, late he began to be merciful even for others. But what does Abraham say? *If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be persuaded, if someone rises from the dead* [^2]. It is altogether true, brothers; the Jews today are therefore not persuaded to believe in him who rose from the dead, because they did not hear Moses and the Prophets: for if they wished to hear them, they would find there foretold what has now been fulfilled, and they still refuse to believe. What, then, we have said of the Jews, let us apply to ourselves, lest, while we attend to others, we too fall into that same impiety. The Gospel, dearly beloved, is not read to the Jews; Moses and the Prophets are read, whom they refuse to hear; but if they wished to hear them, they would believe in Christ, because Moses and the Prophets foretold that Christ would come. Let us, then, not be such, when the Gospel is read to us, as they are, when the Prophets are read to them; for among them, as I said, the Gospel is not recited, among us it is recited.

**3.** Behold, you have just now heard from the Gospel two lives, one present, the other to come: the present we have, the one to come we believe; in the present we are, to the one to come we have not yet arrived. While we are in the present, let us acquire the merit of the one to come; for we are not yet dead. Is the Gospel recited in hell? Which surely, if it were recited, that rich man would hear it in vain, because penitence could no longer be fruitful. To us it is read there, and there heard by us, where, while we live, we can be corrected, lest we come to those torments. Do we believe what is read, or do we not believe? Far be it that we suppose this of Your Charity, that you do not believe; for you are Christians, nor would you be Christians in any way, if you did not believe the Gospel of God; because, then, you are Christians, it is manifest that you believe the Gospel. We have heard, it has just now been recited: *There was a rich man*, surely proud, surely lifting himself up in his riches, *who was clothed in purple and fine linen, and feasted splendidly every day* [^3]. But there lay at his gate a certain poor man full of sores, by name Lazarus, whose sores even the dogs licked; and *he desired to be filled with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table* [^4], and could not. Behold the crime of the rich man, that surely he desired to be filled with the crumbs, and could not, with whom humanity ought to have been shared. That rich man, then, if he had compassion on the poor man lying before his gate, and wanted to be merciful from those riches, would come there too, where that poor man came. For it was not poverty that led that Lazarus to rest, and not humility; or indeed it was not riches that drew that rich man back from so great a rest, but pride and unbelief. For that you may know, brothers, that among those above this rich man was an unbeliever, let us prove it from his very words, which he spoke in hell. Attend. He wanted someone to go from the dead, to announce to his brothers what is done in hell; and when this was not granted him, Abraham saying: *They have Moses and the Prophets, let them hear them; not, father Abraham*, he said, *but, if someone goes there from the dead, they will be persuaded* [^5], he shows that he himself too, when he was among those above, did not believe Moses and the Prophets, but desired someone to rise for him from the dead. Attend to such men now, and see where they will be; by the example of this rich man we are admonished, if you have faith. How many there are who now say: Let it be well with us, while we live; let us eat and drink, and enjoy these delights. What is this that is said to us, that there will be something afterward? who has returned here from there? who has risen here from there? These things are said; this that rich man said, and, what alive he did not believe, dead he experienced. Better that alive he be fruitfully corrected, than that dead he be unfruitfully tormented.

**4.** Now then let us change those very words, if perhaps there is anyone among us who is wont to say these things. For God does not now show what he commands us to believe; for this reason he does not show it, that it may be the reward of faith. For if he shows it to you, what merit do you have because you believe? it is now not to believe, but to see; rather God does not show it to you, that you may believe. He commands you what you should believe, he keeps for you what you may see. But if you do not believe, when he commands faith, he does not keep his sight for you; but that is kept for you, from which that rich man was tormented in hell. And when our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ comes, who is now announced to have come in such a way that he is also hoped to be going to come, he will come with the repayments of the faithful and the unbelieving: about to give rewards to the faithful, about to send the unbelieving into eternal fire. And he said this in the Gospel, how he will judge at the end: that he will set some at the right hand, others at the left, and will separate all nations, as a shepherd separates sheep from goats; the just to be at the right hand, the impious at the left; to the just he will say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom, which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world* [^6]; but to the impious and unbelieving: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels* [^7]. What more could the judge profit you, than to tell you his definitive sentence, that you may not be able to run into it? Brothers, everyone who threatens does not want to strike; for, if he wanted to, he would hasten, and strike. He who says: Beware, does not want to find whom he may strike. Men procure blows for themselves, men purchase penalties for themselves, who refuse to believe God so long saying: Beware. And indeed the penalty of one erring which is here, what is it? Perhaps some affliction, and some scourge either for amendment or for proving. For either each one is amended for his sins, lest he fall, unamended, into greater penalties; or the faith of each is proved, with what endurance or with what patience he remains under the scourge of the Father, not murmuring about the Father chastising, and rejoicing toward the one caressing, but so rejoicing toward the one caressing that he gives thanks even to the one chastising: because *he scourges every son whom he receives* [^8]. How much the martyrs suffered, how much they endured! what chains, what squalor, what prisons, what tortures, what flames, what beasts, what kinds of death! They trampled on everything. For they saw something in spirit, so that what they saw in body they did not care about; there was in them the eye of faith, the eye was stretched to things to come, they despised present things. But the one whose eye is extinguished as to things to come, dreads present things, and does not arrive at things to come.

**5.** There is, then, a faith that is built up in us. Now whoever does not want to believe that Christ was born of the Virgin Mary, that he suffered, that he was crucified, let him believe the Jews that he existed and was killed, let him believe the Gospel that he was born of the Virgin and rose again; for there is reason to believe. And the enemy Jews do not dare to say: Christ was not among our nation; or: There was no such man, whoever it is, whom the Christians worship. He existed, they say, and our parents killed him, and he died as a man. If we find the things that followed his death foretold by the Prophets—that the whole world would run to his name, that all nations and all peoples of the nations would adore him [^9], that even all kings were to be put under his yoke—and we see fulfilled after Christ's death what was foretold before Christ's birth, how do we deceive ourselves, if we refuse to believe the rest, when we see many things fulfilled in us? For we ourselves, brothers—not only we who are here Christians, but we the whole world now are. A few years ago we did not exist; and it is a wonder how it came about that what for so many ages did not exist now exists. We read this in the Prophets; lest we think it happened by chance, we find it foretold. For from this our faith is increased, from this it is built up, from this it is strengthened. There is no one who can say: It happened suddenly. Whence? Behold this which never was on earth. Sometimes in the Scriptures God was held a debtor in them, but to repay the debt in his own time. But from what did God owe? had he received a loan from anyone, who bestows all things on all besides, who made those to whom he might give? For not even the men themselves were, to whom anything might be given. Someone can say: Did God grant these goods to my merits? Suppose he granted these goods to your merits. That you might exist, to whose merits did he grant it? When you were not, what did he give you? That you might be, freely; for you had not deserved that before you existed. Believe him, that the rest too he has deigned to give you freely. We have, then, the grace of God, and the whole world held God in a way a debtor; rather, it did not hold him, because it did not know the guarantee he had made. By promising he made himself a debtor, not by receiving a loan. For in two ways one is called a debtor: Repay what you received, or what you promised. Since of what God promised it cannot be said: Repay—for he received nothing from man, who gave the whole to man—it remains that he is not a debtor, except because he deigned to promise.

**6.** This promise was in the Scriptures: these Scriptures among one nation, the Jewish, which he chose to be born of the flesh of his servant, his faithful one, who believed in him. And how was that nation born? From the aged Abraham, and from the barren Sarah: that Isaac might be brought to birth, that he might be born, from whom came the nation of the Jews, a miracle is wrought. The old man hoped for nothing from his members, dared to wish for nothing from the barrenness of his wife; what he did not at all reckon on, God offered him, and he believed God offering, who dared not wish it from God. And when he had believed, and a son had been born to him, of whom he believed innumerable offspring would be born, God asked that very son to be immolated to him. But Abraham was of so great faith, that he did not hesitate to immolate the only one, of whom he had received the promise. Did he hesitate and say to God: Lord, you granted me a son as a great thing in old age; after great vows, after great joy, a son was born to me unhoped for; do you demand that I kill him? was it not better not to give, than to take away the given? He did not say these things, but believed that whatever he saw God to will was profitable. This is faith, brothers. Certainly that poor man was taken up into Abraham's bosom, and that rich man to the torments of hell. That you may know riches are not in fault, Abraham was rich, in whose bosom Lazarus rested. This rich man was rich on earth, as we have Scripture teaching; he had much gold, silver, herds, household; he was rich, but he was not proud. That you may know that in the rich man pride alone was tormented, vices alone were tormented. These had deserved punishment, not the substance of God; for the substance of God is good, to whomever it is given; but for the one using well a reward is acquired, to the one using badly a punishment is repaid. But attend, how Abraham had riches. Did he keep them for his sons? If he offered the son himself at God's command, how did he despise riches?

**7.** So this Scripture, where God by promising had made himself a debtor, lay hidden among the Jews. Our Lord Jesus Christ came, born according to that Scripture, because according to it he was rendered; suffering according to that Scripture, because in it he was foretold as about to suffer; rising according to that Scripture, because in it he was foretold as about to rise; ascending into heaven according to that Scripture, because in it he was foretold as about to ascend. After he had ascended, unknown to the Jews, he began to send his Apostles to the nations, and, in a way, to rouse the sleepers, and to say: Rise, receive the debt that was long ago promised you. Who is it who rouses his creditor, and offers him what he owes? For not because they held God a debtor did the nations rise; they were called, they began to attend to Scripture, and to find there that what they were receiving had long ago been promised them. They received Christ promised, and displayed; they received the grace of God, the Holy Spirit promised, and displayed; they received the Church itself spread through all nations, promised and displayed. The idols which the nations worshiped, God had promised he would overthrow; it is read in the Scriptures, there you find it. You see how God has done in our times what he promised so many thousands of years before. For men had turned themselves, from him by whom they were made, to that which they themselves had made; and since he who makes is always better than what he makes, therefore God is better, not only than the man he made, but than all angels, virtues, powers, seats, thrones, dominions, because he himself founded all things; so that whatever man makes is lower than man himself. To so great madness had men been led, that they adored an idol; who ought to be condemned if they adored the craftsman who made the idol. It is manifest, brothers, that the craftsman is better than the idol he made; and, when men would be detestable if they adored the craftsman, they adore the very idol, which was made by the craftsman. They would be detestable in adoring the craftsman; but they would be better than those who adore the idol. If, then, the better are condemned, how do I lament the worse? But if I said that the one adoring the craftsman is to be condemned, the one who forsakes the craftsman and adores the idol, who surely forsook the better and betook himself to the inferior, how is he to be condemned? But what better one did he first forsake? God, by whom he himself was made. Does he seek the image of God? He has it in himself; for the craftsman could not make the image of God, but God could make an image for himself. But he did not make another thing for you, but made you yourself in his image. By adoring the image of a man, which the craftsman made, you wear away the image of God, which God impressed on you. Therefore, when he calls you to return, he wants to render you that image, which you yourself by earthly desire, by rubbing it, as it were, have lost and obscured.

**8.** Hence it is, brothers, that God seeks his image from us; this he recalls in those Jews, when they showed him a coin [^10]. For first they wanted to tempt him, when they said: Lord, *is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar* [^11]? so that, if he said: It is lawful, they might calumniate him, that he wanted Israel to be under a curse, whom he wanted to be a tributary, to be subjugated to a king, to give tribute. But if he said: It is not lawful to pay tribute, they would calumniate him, that he commanded against Caesar, and was the author of their not paying their tribute which they owed, because they were subjugated. He saw them tempting, as truth sees falsehood, and briefly convicted the lie from the mouth of those lying. For he did not pronounce sentence against them from his own mouth, but made them pronounce sentence against themselves, because it is written: *By your mouth you will be justified, and by your mouth you will be condemned* [^12]. *Why*, he says, *do you tempt me, hypocrites? Show me a coin*. They showed it. *Whose*, he says, *image and inscription does it have* [^13]? They answered: *Caesar's*. And he: *Render therefore to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's* [^14]. Just as Caesar seeks his image in your coin, so God seeks his image in your soul. Render to Caesar, he says, what is Caesar's. What does Caesar seek from you? his image. What does God seek from you? His image. But Caesar's is in the coin, God's image is in you. If at any time you lose a coin, you lament, because you have lost Caesar's image, when you adore an idol, do you not lament, because you do wrong in yourself to the image of God?

**9.** Hold, then, brothers, the promise of the Lord our God, and number now from that number of his promises how much he has rendered. Christ was not yet born, he was promised in Scripture; he rendered him, he was born. He had not yet suffered, had not yet risen; and this he rendered; having suffered, been crucified, he rose. His passion is our reward, his blood is our redemption. He ascended into heaven, as he had promised; and this he rendered. He sent the Gospel through all lands; therefore he willed there to be four Gospels, that the whole world might be signified by the number four, from East and West, from North and South. Therefore he willed to have twelve disciples, so that three, in a way, distributed through four might appear; because the world was called in the Trinity, in the Father and Son and Holy Spirit. He rendered this; he sent the Gospel, as he foretold: *How beautiful are the feet of those evangelizing, of those who announce peace, who evangelize good things!* [^15] as he foretold: *There are no speeches, nor words, whose voices are not heard; their sound went out through all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world* [^16]. As he said, so he sent; the Gospel is written through all lands. The Church too first suffered persecution; he rendered it, because he had promised martyrs as well. Recite the guarantee: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his just ones* [^17]. He rendered the martyrs too, because he had promised them as well. What was to be rendered afterward? *All the kings of the earth will adore in his sight* [^18]. Kings too believed, who first, by persecuting the martyrs, had made them; we see, then, that now too kings have believed. He rendered too what he had promised, that at the command of kings the idols might be broken, at whose first command the Christians were killed. He took away the idols too, because he had promised: *And there will be a regard upon the idols of the nations* [^19]. Since, then, he has rendered so much, brothers, why do we not believe him? Is God a less fit debtor? If he had rendered us nothing at all yet, we held a fit debtor, who made heaven and earth; for he was not going to be poor, so as not to have from where to repay; or to deceive, since he himself is truth. Or is God so great a power, that he can be succeeded, that he has no time to repay?

**10.** It is just, brothers, that God be believed, before he renders anything; because in no way can he lie, he cannot deceive: he is God. So our fathers believed him. So Abraham believed him. Behold, a faith truly to be praised and proclaimed. He had received nothing from him, and believed the one promising; we do not yet believe, who have already received so much. Could Abraham say to him: I will believe, because you promised me this, and rendered it? At the first command he believed, he had received nothing else of the sort. *Go out from your land*, it was said to him, *and from your kindred, and go into the land that I will give you* [^20]. And he believed at once, and he did not give him that land, but kept it for his seed. And he promised to his seed, what? *In your seed all the nations will be blessed* [^21]. His seed is Christ: because of Abraham came Isaac, of Isaac Jacob, of Jacob the twelve, of the twelve the people of the Jews, of the people of the Jews the Virgin Mary, of the Virgin Mary our Lord Jesus Christ. And our Lord Jesus Christ was made the seed of Abraham; and what had been promised to Abraham, we find fulfilled in us. *In your seed*, he says, *all the nations will be blessed*. He believed this, before he had seen anything; he believed, and did not see what was promised. But we see what was promised to him; and whatever was promised to him was to be. For what of the dowry has he not yet rendered? He announced labors to come in this world, and that his holy ones and his faithful would be in labors, and would bring forth fruit with endurance; he foretold it, and we see it; by those very labors we are worn down. What labors foretold are not yet here? For do not think, brothers, that, because you see human affairs now worn down, this is not written in the Scripture of God. The whole is written, and endurance is enjoined on Christians, and good things still more to come, because the evils have come which were foretold to come. For if the things that were said did not come, they would take away our faith even in the good things; but for this reason the evils came first, that we might believe the good things to come.

**11.** Now the world is like a winepress, it is under pressure; but, if you are dregs, you go through the sewers; if oil, you remain in the vat. For there must be pressures. Attend to the dregs, attend to the oil. Pressure sometimes occurs in the world: for example, famine, war, want, dearness, need, mortality, plunder, greed; there are pressures of the poor, labors of cities: these we see. And they were foretold to come, and we see that they are. We find men murmuring amid these pressures, and saying: Behold, in Christian times how many evils there are! Before Christian times how many good things abounded! There were not such evils. From the pressure these come out as dregs, they run through the sewers; their mouth is for that reason black, because it blasphemes: it does not shine. The oil glistens. But you find another man from that same pressure, and from that same threshing that threshed him; is it not the same threshing that threshed him? You have heard the voice of the dregs, hear the voice of the oil: Thanks be to God! Blessed be your name! All these evils with which you wear us down were foretold; we are secure, because the good things too are to come. When we, both we and the wicked together, are amended, your will is done. We know you the Father promising, we know you the Father scourging; instruct us and render the inheritance which you promised at the end. We bless your holy name, because you were never a liar; all things, as you foretold, so you have displayed. In these praises flowing forth from that very pressure, the oil runs into the vats. Yet because this whole world is a winepress, whence too another likeness is given: *As gold and silver are tested in the furnace, so the temptation of tribulation tests the just* [^22], the likeness of the goldsmith's furnace too is set forth. In a narrow pot are three things: fire, gold, and chaff. And there you see an image of the whole world; there is chaff, there is gold, there is fire; the chaff is burned, the fire burns, the gold is tested. So too in this whole world there are the just, there are the impious, there is tribulation; the world is like the goldsmith's furnace, the just like gold, the impious like chaff, tribulation like fire. Would the gold be purified, unless the chaff were burned? It comes about that the impious are reduced to ashes; for when they blaspheme, and murmur against God, they are made ashes. There the purified gold—the just, who tolerably bear all the troubles of this world, and in their tribulations praise God—the purified gold is gathered into the treasuries of God; for God has treasuries, into which to put the purified gold; he has also filthy places, into which to put the ash of the chaff. From this world the whole comes out. You see what you are. For the fire must come; if it finds you gold, it will take away the dross; if it finds you chaff, it will burn you, and reduce you to ash. Choose for yourself what you are. For you cannot say: I will be without fire; you are already in the goldsmith's furnace, where the fire must come. Rather you must be there, because you can in no way be without fire.

**12.** Why, then, do we not believe, brothers, that both the end of the age and the day of judgment will come, that there each of us may receive the things he did in the body, whether good or evil [^23], when we see so many things promised, displayed, and given? Why do we not choose for ourselves, while we live, that place where we may always live? Suppose we have been negligent, let us today be diligent. We ought not to be negligent; what tomorrow is, you do not know. The patience of God admonishes us to do this, that we should correct both ourselves and our life, if it has been evil, and while there is time choose better things. Or do you think that God sleeps, and does not see those doing evil? But perhaps he teaches us patience and first displays patience. He finds a man, perhaps, to have made progress, and not to do what he used to do, that is, evil. This man endures someone malicious, and wants God to take him away; and he murmurs against God, because he keeps that enemy of his, perhaps doing evil, and does not take him away. He has forgotten that God dealt patiently with him too, and, if he had wanted to act severely first, there would be no one to speak. Do you demand God's severity? Because you have crossed over, let another cross over too; for not, because you have already crossed over, have you cut off the bridge of God's mercy; there is still one to cross over. He made you good, when you were evil; he wants another too to be himself good from evil, just as you were made good from evil. So all come in their own order; but some refuse to come, others come. For to such men the Apostle says: But *you, according to the hardness of your heart and your impenitent heart, lay up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works* [^24]. Then if a wicked man wants to persevere in evil, he is not your companion, but he will be your tester; for, if he is wicked, and you good, by tolerating the wicked one you are proved good; you will receive the crown of your proving, but he will have the punishment in the evil of his perseverance. But what God is doing, let us patiently await his good patience, his fatherly discipline. He is a Father, he is kind, he is merciful; rather, if he lets us drift, then he is badly angry at us.

**13.** For attend, brothers, and see those amphitheaters, which now fall. Luxury built them; do you think that devotion built them? Nothing built them but the luxury of impious men. Do you not want what luxury built to fall some day, and what devotion builds to rise? For God allowed, when these were being built, that some day men might recognize the evils they were doing. But because they refused to recognize them, the Lord Jesus Christ came; he began to foretell them their evils, he began to overturn what they held as great; and they say: Christian times are evil. Why? because what you used to die at is overturned for you. But all good things abounded, they say, when these things were done. Yes indeed, so that good things might be made from them. If, then, you know that God once gave you abundance, and you used it badly, and used it unto perdition, see that that abundance made you drift, and lose your soul. Does not a severe father come, and begin to say: This boy is undisciplined; I entrusted to him this or that, how he lost this, and that? If we do not give the seed of the earth, unless it be good, lest the seed perish, how do you want God to give to us, undisciplined and negligent of our life, his abundance for using badly, and you do not want God to cut off the driftings of men? My brothers, he is a physician, and he knows how to cut off a rotten member, lest other places too rot from it. One finger, he says, is cut off there; because it is better that one finger be lacking, than that the whole body rot. If a man, a physician, does this by his art, if the art of medicine takes away some part of the members, lest all rot, why should God not cut whatever he knows to be rotten in men, that they may come to health?

**14.** Do not, then, brothers, be wearied at God scourging, lest he forsake you, and you perish forever; but rather let us ask him to moderate the very blows, and so temper them that we do not fail under them; and let us ask that he amend with health, measure it out, and render afterward what he promised his holy ones. See what Scripture said: *The sinner has provoked the Lord; according to the greatness of his wrath he will not seek it out* [^25]. What is: *according to the greatness of his wrath he will not seek it out?* Because he is greatly angry, *he will not seek it out*, that is, he will let them perish. If, then, he is greatly angry when he does not seek out, he is also greatly merciful when he exercises. But then he exercises, when he scourges, when he fixes our heart to himself. Let us, then, hold his salvation, and not flee his scourge; this teaches us, this admonishes, in this he builds us up. His own Son, who came for this, to console us, what good did he endure here? tell me. Surely he is the Son of God, the Word of God, through whom all things were made [^26]; what good did he endure here? Is he not the one who, when he cast out demons, heard such insults, that it was said to him: *You have a demon* [^27]? To the Son of God, who cast out demons, the Jews said: *You have a demon*. The demons were already better, who confessed the Son of God, than they; for the demons confessed, and they did not confess. But so great was his power, and so great his greatness, and so great his patience, that he tolerated all things. He was scourged, he heard insults, he received slaps, he was spat upon in the face, he was crowned with thorns, he was crucified, at the last he was hung on the wood, he was mocked, he was made sport of, he was killed, he was buried. The Son of God endured so much here; if the Lord, how much more the servant? if the Master, how much more the disciple? if he who created us, how much more we his creation? who, that he might give us an example, left us patience. Why do we fail in that patience, as though we had lost our head, who went before us to heaven? For our head went before to heaven, as though saying: Behold, by which way come: through troubles, through patience; this is the way I handed you. But where does the way lead, by which you see me ascend? Into heaven. He who does not want to go this way, does not want to arrive there; he who wants to come to me, let him come by the way I showed. And you cannot arrive except through the way of troubles, griefs, tribulations, straits. So you will arrive at the rest, which is not taken from you. But do you want that rest which is for a time, and to depart from the way of Christ? Observe the torments of that rich man who was tormented in hell; because he too desired present rest, and found eternal penalties. Dearly beloved brothers, choose rather the harder things, which without end will have rest forever. Turned toward the Lord.
