# Sermon 132

**1.** As we heard, when the holy Gospel was read, the Lord Jesus Christ exhorted us by the promise of eternal life to eat his flesh and drink his blood. You who heard these things, have not all yet understood them. For you who are baptized and faithful, know what he said. But those among you who are still called catechumens, or hearers, could be present hearing when it was read, but understanding? Therefore our sermon is directed to both. Those who already eat the flesh of the Lord, and drink his blood, let them consider what they eat, and what they drink: lest, as the Apostle says, they eat and drink judgment to themselves [^1]. But those who do not yet eat, and do not yet drink, let them hasten, invited to such a banquet. Through these days the teachers feed, Christ feeds daily, his table is that which is set in the midst. What is the reason, O hearers, that you see the table, and do not approach the banquet? And perhaps just now when the Gospel was read, you said in your hearts: We wonder what it is that he says: *My flesh is truly food, and my blood is truly drink* [^2]? How is the flesh of the Lord eaten, and the blood of the Lord drunk? We wonder what he says? Who shut you out against this, that you should not know it? It is veiled: but if you wish, it will be revealed. Approach the profession, and you have resolved the question. For what the Lord Jesus said, the faithful now know. But you are called a catechumen, you are called a hearer, and you are deaf. For you have the ears of the body open, because you hear the words that were said: but you still have the ears of the heart shut, because you do not understand what was said. I argue, I do not merely discourse. Behold it is Easter, give your name for Baptism. If the festivity does not rouse you, let curiosity itself lead you: that you may know what was said: *He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, abides in me, and I in him* [^3]. That you may know with me what was said, knock, and it will be opened to you. And as I say to you: Knock, and it will be opened to you; so I too knock, open to me. Sounding in your ears, I knock at your breast.

**2.** But if the catechumens must be exhorted, my brothers, not to delay to approach so great a grace of regeneration; how great care ought we to have in building up the faithful, that it may profit them that they approach; that they not eat and drink such a banquet to themselves for judgment? But that they may not eat and drink for judgment, let them live well. Be exhorters not by your words, but by your conduct: so that those who are not baptized may so hasten to follow you, that they do not perish by imitating you. You who are married, keep faith of the bed to your wives. Repay what you demand. Husband, you demand chastity of the woman, offer her an example, not a word. You are the head, see where you go. For you ought to go that way which is not perilous for them to follow: rather, where you yourself want her to follow, there you ought to walk. From the weaker sex you demand strength: both of you have the desire of the flesh; let the one who is stronger conquer first. And yet, what must be grieved, many men are conquered by women. Women keep the chastity which men refuse to keep: and in that they do not keep it, they want to seem men; as though for this the sex is stronger, that the enemy may more easily subjugate it. There is a struggle, a battle, a fight. The man is stronger than the woman, the man is the head of the woman [^4]. The woman fights, and conquers: do you yield to the enemy? Does the body stand, and the head lie low? But you who do not yet have wives, and yet already approach the Lord's table, and eat the flesh of Christ, and drink his blood, if you are going to take wives, keep yourselves for your wives. Such as you want them to come to you, such ought they too to find you. What young man is there who does not want to take a chaste wife? And if he is going to receive a virgin, who does not desire her untouched? You seek one untouched, be untouched. You seek a pure one, do not be impure. For it is not that she can, and you cannot. If it could not be, neither could she. But because she can, let it teach you that it can be done. And her, that she may be able, God rules. But you will be more glorious, if you do it. Why more glorious? Her the guardianship of parents presses, the very modesty of the weaker sex restrains: in short, she fears the laws, which you do not fear. Therefore you will be more glorious, if you do it; because if you do it, you fear God. She has many things to fear besides God: you fear God alone. But he whom you fear is greater than all. He is to be feared in public, he in secret. You go out, you are seen; you enter, you are seen; the lamp burns, it sees you; the lamp is put out, it sees you; you enter the bedroom, it sees you; you turn things over in your heart, it sees you. Fear him, him whose care it is to see you; and at least by fearing be chaste. Or if you want to sin, seek where he does not see you, and do what you will.

**3.** But you who have already vowed, chastise the body more strictly, and do not allow yourselves to loose the reins of desire even toward the things that are permitted; so that you may not only turn aside from unlawful intercourse, but also despise the lawful gaze. Remember, in whatever sex you are, whether men or women, that you lead the life of the angels on earth. For the angels do not marry, nor take wives. This we shall be, when we rise again [^5]. How much better are you, who what men will be after the resurrection, this you begin to be before death? Keep your degrees: for God keeps your honors for you. The resurrection of the dead has been compared to the stars set in heaven. *For star differs from star in glory*, as the Apostle says: *So too the resurrection of the dead* [^6]. For virginity will shine there in one way, conjugal chastity will shine there in another way, holy widowhood will shine there in another. They will shine diversely: but all will be there. The splendor unequal, the heaven common.

**4.** Considering, then, your degrees, keeping your professions too, approach the flesh of the Lord, approach the blood of the Lord. He who knows himself to be otherwise, let him not approach. Be rather pricked by my sermon. For those rejoice together who know how to keep for their spouses what they demand of their spouses; those who know how to keep continence in every way, if they have vowed this to God; but those who hear me saying: Whoever do not keep chastity, do not approach that bread, are saddened. And I would not want to say this: but what do I do? Shall I fear man, so as to keep silent about the truth? Therefore if those servants do not fear the Lord, am I too not to fear him? As though I do not know it was said: *Wicked and slothful servant, you should give, and I would demand* [^7]. Behold I have given, O Lord my God; behold in your sight and in the sight of your angels and in the sight of your own people I have doled out your money: for I fear your judgment. I have given, you demand. Even if I do not say it, you will do it. Therefore I rather say this: I have given, you turn them, you spare. Make chaste those who were unchaste, that together in your sight, when the judgment comes, we may rejoice, both the one who doled out and the one to whom it was doled out. Does this please you? Let it please you. Whoever you are who are unchaste, correct yourselves, while you live. For I can speak the word of God, but the unchaste persevering in wickedness, from the judgment and condemnation of God I cannot free.
