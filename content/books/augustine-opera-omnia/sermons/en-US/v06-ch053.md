# Sermon 223/C: On the Holy Night

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, near to his passion, when for our debts he paid what he did not owe, that the bond by which we were held guilty might be blotted out by his blood[^1], said to his disciples: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*[^2]. From this his commendation this solemnity was instituted: that on the yearly return of that day there should be a watch, on which his resurrection was awaited. May he hear us watching in his solemnity, who slept for our salvation. For in the prophecy of that thing and from his own person it had been said: *I slept and took sleep; and I rose, because the Lord will take me up*[^3]. As though he said: Lest the Jews seem to have prevailed against him; I slept, it was of will, not of necessity. He himself surely, he himself slept, as deigning, not as overcome; for he had the power to lay down his soul, and again to take it up[^4]. Yet not for that reason were the Jews without crime, because he slept by power; for that he might show their crime, he said in another Psalm: *I slept, troubled*[^5]. For this he referred to them, who thought him troubled by them, who insulted him as if conquered, and yet, when by nefarious voices they had demanded his death, tried to make themselves strangers to his killing; that it might seem not their deed but Pilate's, who handed him over to the soldiers to be crucified. Therefore that Psalm at once: *The sons of men*, he says, *their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword*[^6]. As though he said: Even if not with your own hands, yet with your own mouth you slew the one whose death you demanded. But what does he say in another Psalm? *Will not he who sleeps add that he rise?*[^7] Behold, he rose who had slept; he watched, and was made *like a solitary sparrow on a roof*[^8], that is, above heaven, where he intercedes for us[^9], where now he dies no more, nor will death any longer have dominion over him[^10]; because *he will not slumber nor sleep who keeps us*[^11]. Behold, those who thought they had harmed him in something, even the kingdom itself, where they were unwilling that he reign over them, being uprooted from there, perished; that visibly too might be fulfilled what in the same Psalm, about to sleep, he says: *Raise me up, and I will requite them*[^12]. Humbling, then, our souls, let us celebrate vigils, that with a watchful heart we may hope for and await the coming of him whose voice will rouse all the sleeping even from the tombs: *Those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^13]. For he it is, of whose sleep, lifted up on the cross, Jacob foretold so long before: *You went up, lying down, you slept like a lion*[^14]. He himself, *the Lion of the tribe of Judah, conquered*[^15]; for Jacob said that too, when he blessed Judah. Since, then, the lion slept, he was counted among the dead; but because the lion conquered, he will come to judge the living and the dead.
