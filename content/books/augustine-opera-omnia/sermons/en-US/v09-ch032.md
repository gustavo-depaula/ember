# Sermon 322: The Tuesday of Easter Week

**1.** Yesterday we promised Your Charity a booklet, where you may also hear, concerning that man healed, the things you could not see. If, then, it please Your Charity—indeed, because what pleases me also ought to please you—let both brothers stand in your sight: that those who had not seen him may see in this man what that man was suffering. Let both, then, stand, the one to whom grace has been given, and the other for whom mercy is to be sought.

**2.** *I ask, most blessed lord papa Augustine, that you order this my booklet, which by your command I have presented, to be recited to the holy people.*  
While we were still dwelling in our homeland, Caesarea of Cappadocia, our brother, the elder by birth, afflicted our common mother with grave and intolerable injuries, to such a degree that he did not hesitate even to lay hands on her. This all of us sons, gathered together alike, bore patiently, so that we did not say even a word to our brother on behalf of our mother, asking why he did this. But she, stirred by the goads of a woman's grief, resolved to punish her injurious son by cursing him. And when, after cockcrow, she hastened to the font of holy Baptism to call down the anger of God upon her said son, then some demon, I know not who, in the likeness of our uncle, as is understood, met her, and asked her first where she was going. She answered him that she was going to curse her son for his intolerable insult. Then that enemy, since he could easily find a place in the heart of the raging woman, persuaded her to curse them all. And she, inflamed by his viperous counsels, prostrated herself at the holy font and seized it, and with hair disheveled and breasts bared she asked this above all from God, that, banished from our homeland and wandering through foreign lands, we should terrify the whole human race by our example. Soon an effective vengeance followed the mother's prayers, and at once that brother of ours, the greater in age and in fault, was seized by such a trembling of the limbs as Your Holiness saw in me until three days ago. And, the order being kept in which we had been born, within one year the same punishment seized us all. But the mother, seeing her curses come to such efficacy, could no longer bear the awareness of her impiety and the reproach of men: but binding her throat with a noose, she ended her grievous life with a more dismal end. So we all, not bearing our reproach, and leaving our common homeland, were scattered abroad in different directions. But of us all, ten brothers, the one who follows me first in the order of birth merited healing at the memorial of the glorious martyr Lawrence, which was recently placed at Ravenna, as we have heard. But I, who am sixth of them in the order of birth, together with this sister of mine, who follows me in age, wherever among the nations, wherever in the lands I had learned there were holy places in which God worked miracles, took the road with great love of the longed-for healing. But to be silent about the other most celebrated places of the saints, I came also to Ancona, a city of Italy, where the Lord works many miracles through the most glorious martyr Stephen, by the same circuit. But I could not be healed elsewhere, because by divine predestination I was kept for this place. Nor did I pass over Uzalis, a city of Africa, where the blessed martyr Stephen is proclaimed often to work great things. Nevertheless, three months ago, that is, on the day of the Kalends of January, both I and my sister, who is here with me, still held by the same affliction, were admonished by an evident vision. For someone of clear aspect, and venerable with white hair, said to me that within the third month the longed-for healing would be at hand for me. But to my sister Your Holiness appeared in a vision, in that form in which we see you present: by which it was signified to us that we ought to come to this place. For I too afterward often saw Your Beatitude through other cities on the journey by which we were coming, exactly such as I now behold. Admonished, then, by evident divine authority, we came to this city about fifteen days ago. Of my affliction either your eyes are witnesses, or my wretched sister, who, for the instruction of all, offers an example of our common evil: that those who see in her what I was like, may recognize in me how much the Lord has worked through his Holy Spirit. I prayed daily with great tears in the place where the memorial of the most glorious martyr Stephen is. But on the Lord's day of Easter, as others who were present saw, while praying with great weeping I held the railing, suddenly I fell. Estranged from my senses, where I was I do not know. After a little I rose up, and did not find that trembling in my body. So, not ungrateful for so great a benefit of God, I have presented this booklet; in which I have set forth both what you did not know of our calamities, and what you have learned of my soundness and salvation: that you may both deign to pray for my sister, and give thanks to God for me.
