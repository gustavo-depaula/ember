# Sermon 184: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1. 1.** The birthday of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, on which *truth has sprung up from the earth*[^1] and a day born for us out of day has shone forth, to be celebrated by us today in its yearly return: *let us rejoice and be glad in it*[^2]. For what so lofty a humility has bestowed on us, the faith of Christians holds, while it is far from the hearts of the ungodly; for *God has hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones*[^3]. Let the humble, then, hold fast to the humility of God, so that by this great help, as upon the beast of their own weakness, they may come to the height of God. But those wise and prudent ones, while they seek the heights of God and do not believe his lowly things, pass these by, and for that reason do not reach those heights either; empty and frivolous, puffed up and lifted high, they have hung, as it were, in the windy middle between heaven and earth. For they are wise and prudent, but of this world, not of him by whom the world was made. For if there were in them the true wisdom that is God's and is God, they would understand that flesh could be taken up by God without his being changed into flesh; they would understand that he took up what he was not and remained what he was; that he came to us in a man and did not depart from the Father; that he persevered as what he is and appeared to us as what we are; that power was placed in an infant body and was not withdrawn from the mass of the world. He whose work, as he abides with the Father, is the whole universe, his work, as he comes to us, is the birth from a Virgin. For the Virgin Mother gave a token of his majesty: as much a virgin before conception as a virgin after the birth; found pregnant by a man, not made so; with child by a male, without a male; the happier and more wonderful for a fruitfulness added, an integrity not lost. So great a miracle these prefer to think feigned rather than done. Thus, since in Christ, who is man and God, they cannot believe, they despise the human; since they cannot despise it, they do not believe the divine. But for us, the more lowly it is to them, the more pleasing let the body of the God-man be in the humility of God; and the more impossible it is to them, the more divine let the virgin birth be in the nativity of the man.

**2. 2.** And so let us celebrate the Lord's birthday with due attendance and festivity. Let men rejoice, let women rejoice: Christ was born a man, born of a woman; and both sexes are honored. Now then let him pass over to the second man, who in the first had been condemned. A woman had persuaded us to death: a woman bore life for us. The likeness of sinful flesh was born, by which the flesh of sin might be cleansed. Let not the flesh, then, be blamed, but rather, that nature may live, let the fault die; for he was born without fault, in whom the one who was in fault may be reborn. Rejoice, holy children, who have chosen above all to follow Christ, who have not sought marriage. He whom you have found to be followed did not come to you through marriage, that he might grant you to despise that through which you came. For you came through fleshly nuptials, without which he came to spiritual nuptials: and he gave you to spurn marriage, you whom he called above all to marriage. Therefore you do not seek out that from which you were born; for you have loved him who was not born in this way more than the rest. Rejoice, holy virgins: a Virgin has borne for you one to whom you may be wed without corruption; whom by neither conceiving nor bearing can you lose what you love. Rejoice, you righteous: it is the birthday of the Justifier. Rejoice, you weak and sick: it is the birthday of the Savior. Rejoice, you captives: it is the birthday of the Redeemer. Rejoice, you slaves: it is the birthday of the Ruler. Rejoice, you free: it is the birthday of the Liberator. Rejoice, all Christians: it is the birthday of Christ.

**2. 3.** He who was born of a mother commended this day to the ages, who, born of the Father, created all the ages. That birth could have no mother, nor did this one seek out a human father. In short, Christ was born both of a father and of a mother; and without a father and without a mother: of the Father God, of the mother man; without a mother God, without a father man. *His generation, then, who shall declare?*[^4] — whether that one without time or this one without seed; that one without a beginning, this one without precedent; that one which never was not, this one which was neither before nor after; that one which has no end, this one which has its beginning there where it has its end.

**3. 3.** Rightly, then, did the prophets announce that he would be born, but the heavens and the angels that he was born. He who contains the world lay in a manger: he was both an infant and the Word. He whom the heavens cannot hold the bosom of one woman bore. She ruled our King; she carried the one in whom we are; she nursed our Bread. O manifest weakness and wondrous humility, in which all divinity so lay hidden! The mother under whom his infancy lay he ruled by his power; and her whose breasts he sucked he fed with truth. May he perfect his gifts in us, who did not shrink from taking up even our first beginnings; and may he himself make us children of God, who for our sake willed to become the son of a man.

[^1]: Ps 85:11 (84:12)
[^2]: Ps 118:24 (117:24)
[^3]: Matt 11:25
[^4]: Isa 53:8
