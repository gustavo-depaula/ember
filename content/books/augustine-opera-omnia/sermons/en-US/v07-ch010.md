# Sermon 238: In the Days of Easter

**1.** Thus the sacred and perennial Gospel reading shows us the true Christ and the true Church, so that we may not err in either of them, or substitute one for the other in place of the holy bridegroom, or bring in to the holy bride not her own husband but another. Therefore, lest we err in either of them, let us hear, as it were, the marriage records of their Gospel.

**2.** Concerning the Lord Christ there have not been lacking, nor are there lacking, those who are so deceived as not to believe that he had true flesh. Let them hear what we have just heard. He is in heaven, but he sounds here; he sits at the right hand of the Father, but he speaks among us. Let him point himself out, let him manifest himself. What need is there for us to seek another witness about him? Let us rather hear him. He appeared to his disciples, and suddenly stood in their midst. You heard it as it was read. But they were troubled: for they supposed they were seeing a spirit. This is what those think who do not believe that he had true flesh: they are Manichees, they are Priscillianists, and any other plagues not even to be named. For they do not think that Christ is nothing—they do not think this—but they think he is a spirit, that he did not have flesh. What about you, Catholic? what about you, the bride, not an adulteress? Well then, what about you, except what you have learned from him? For you could find no better witness about him than himself. So what about you? You have learned that Christ is both the Word, and the spirit of a man, and the flesh of a man. What have you learned about the Word? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God*[^1]. What have you learned about the spirit of a man? *And bowing his head he handed over the spirit*[^2]. What have you learned about the flesh? Hear now. Forgive those who think this, which the disciples first thought in their error. But they did not persevere in error. For the disciples thought what the Manichees think today, what the Priscillianists think today, that there was not true flesh in the Lord Christ, but only a spirit. Let us see whether he left them to err. See how bad is the error that the physician hastened to heal, refused to confirm. They thought, then, that they were seeing a spirit: and he, who knew that these thoughts were bad, rooting them out of their hearts, said: *Why are you troubled? why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet: touch and see that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^3]. Hold fast, against all the insane thoughts of all men, hold what you have received: otherwise you are lost. Christ is the true Word, the only-begotten, equal to the Father, a true human spirit, true flesh without sin. This died, this rose again, this hung on the wood, this lay in the tomb, this sits in heaven. The Lord Christ wished to persuade the disciples that what they saw was bones and flesh: but you contradict him. So he lies, and you speak the truth? you build up, and he deceives? Why did Christ wish to persuade me of this, except because he knew what it profits me to believe, and what it harms me not to believe? So, then, believe. He is the bridegroom.

**3.** Let us hear also about the bride: because there are some, again, who, favoring adulterers, wish to overthrow the true one and substitute a false one. Let us hear also about the bride. When, then, they had touched the feet, the hands, the flesh, the bones, the Lord added and said: *Have you anything here to eat?* that by sharing food too he might be shown to be a true man. He took, he ate, he gave; and while they were still trembling for joy, he said to them: *Did I not say these things to you, while I was still with you?* Why was he now not with them? What does it mean, *while I was still with you?* While I was still mortal, as you too are. What, then, was I saying to you? *That all things must be fulfilled which are written in the Law, and in the Prophets, and in the Psalms about me. Then he opened their minds, that they might understand the Scriptures; and he said to them, that thus it was fitting for Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day*[^4]. Take away true flesh, there will be no true passion, no true resurrection. See, you have the bridegroom: *It was fitting for Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day.* Hold the head: hear about the body. For what must we now show? We who have heard the bridegroom, let us recognize the bride too. *And that repentance and the forgiveness of sins should be preached in his name.* Where? from where? how far? *Among all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^5]. See, you have the bride. Let no one sell you fables, let not the rabid fury of heretics bark from its corner. The Church is spread through the whole world: all nations have the Church. Let no one deceive you: she is the true one, she is the Catholic one. We have not seen Christ; her we do see: about him let us believe. The apostles, on the contrary, saw him, and believed about her. They saw one thing and believed another: and we, on the contrary, see one thing and believe another. They saw Christ, they believed the Church, which they did not see: we too see the Church, let us believe in Christ, whom we do not see; and holding what we see, we will come to him whom we do not yet see. Knowing, therefore, the bridegroom and the bride, let us acknowledge them in their records, lest in so holy a marriage we quarrel.

[^1]: John 1:1-2
[^2]: John 19:30
[^3]: Luke 24:38-39
[^4]: Luke 24:44-46
[^5]: Luke 24:47
