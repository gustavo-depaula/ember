# Sermon 316: On the Solemnity of Stephen the Martyr

**1.** The most blessed and in Christ most glorious martyr Stephen has already fattened us by his own sermon: but after that fattening I set before you, from the sermon of my ministry, a kind of second table. And what sweeter thing do I find to set on it than Christ and the Martyr himself? For that one is the Lord, this one the servant: but Stephen, from a servant, a friend. We, however, without doubt are servants: may he grant that we be friends too. Yet what sort of servants? Such as can with an untroubled conscience sing: *But to me your friends are made very honorable, O God*[^1]. Before holy Stephen was killed in the open, and crowned in secret, you heard what sort of man he was, chosen by the Apostles. Among those deacons he is named first, as among the Apostles Peter. So when he had been ordained by the Apostles, in a short time he preceded his ordainers to the passion: he was ordained by them, but he was crowned first. What, then, did you hear, when his passion was read? *But Stephen, full of grace and the Holy Spirit, did great wonders and signs among the people, through the name of the Lord Jesus Christ*[^2]. Understand who, through whose name. You who know how to love Stephen, love him in Christ. For this he wants, this is pleasing to him: from this he rejoices, this he holds acceptable. For he did not want to boast his own name among those who stoned him. Attend to whom he confessed, when he was being stoned; whom he confessed on earth, whom he saw in heaven; for whom he handed over his flesh, to whom he commended his soul. For do we read, or can we anywhere read in sound doctrine, that Jesus did or does signs through the name of Stephen? Stephen did them, but through the name of Christ. He does this even now: whatever you see is done through the memorial of Stephen, is done in the name of Christ; that Christ may be commended, Christ adored, Christ awaited as judge of the living and the dead, and that by those who love him there may be standing at his right hand. For when he comes, they will stand at the right, they will stand at the left: blessed, those at the right; wretched, those at the left.

**2.** Yet let the most blessed Stephen imitate his Lord. In a marvelous way he suffered among the stones, hard, those throwing—what, but what they were? That you may know that he suffered hard men; he said this to them: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you have always resisted the Holy Spirit*[^3]. You want to die, you hasten to be stoned, you burn to be crowned. *You have always resisted the Holy Spirit.* When he said such things, they raged, and gnashed with their teeth. Add, Stephen, add what they cannot bear, add what they cannot endure: add something by which they may stone you, that we may find what to celebrate. The heavens were opened: the Martyr saw the head of the martyrs; he saw Jesus standing at the right hand of the Father: he saw, that he might not be silent. They did not see, but they envied; and therefore they did not see, because they envied. He was not silent about what he saw, that he might come to him whom he saw. *Behold,* he says, *I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing at the right hand of majesty*[^4]. They held their ears, as though against blasphemy. You recognize them in the Psalm: *Like the asp,* it says, *deaf and stopping its ears, that it may not hear the voice of the charmer, and the medicine compounded by a wise man*[^5]. For just as asps are said, when they are charmed, that they may not break forth and come out of their caverns, to press one ear to the earth, and to stop the other with their tail, and yet the charmer brings them out: so these too were still hissing in their caverns, when they were seething in their hearts. They were not yet breaking forth: they stopped their ears. Now let them break forth, let it appear who they are: let them run to the stones. They ran, they stoned.

**3.** What did Stephen do? what? Attend first to the one whom the good friend was imitating. The Lord Jesus Christ, when he hung on the cross, said: *Father, into your hands I commend my spirit*[^6]. This as man, as crucified, as born of a woman, as clothed in flesh; as about to die for us, as about to be in the tomb, as about to rise on the third day, as about to ascend into the heavens. For all these things are in the man. As man, then: *Father, into your hands I commend my spirit.* He said: *Father:* Stephen: *Lord Jesus.* What does he too say? *Receive my spirit*[^7]. You said to the Father, I to you. I acknowledge the Mediator. You came to lift up one lying down: you did not fall with me. *Receive,* he says, *my spirit.* This he prayed for himself: another thing came into his mind, by which to imitate his Lord. Recall the words of the one hanging on the wood, and attend to the words of the one confessing while being stoned. What did that one say? *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^8]. Perhaps among them then was Stephen, those who did not know what they were doing. For many afterward believed. And it is uncertain to us from where the most blessed Stephen was, whether from those who had first believed in Christ, like Nicodemus who came to him by night, who merited to be buried there where this man too was, because through him this man too was found: whether, then, he was among these, or perhaps among those who after the ascension of the Lord, when the Spirit came, when the disciples were filled, and were speaking in the tongues of all nations, pricked, said to the Apostles: *Men, brothers, what shall we do?*[^9] tell us. For they despaired of salvation, because they had killed the Savior. And Peter said to them: *Repent, and let each of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ; and you will receive the Holy Spirit, and your sins will be forgiven you*[^10]. Do you think all? What of sins remained, when even that sin was forgiven, by which the forgiver of sins was killed? What is worse, than to kill Christ? This was blotted out. What then? Perhaps Stephen was among them. If he was among them; for him too that prayer availed: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.* Yet Saul too was among them. When Stephen the lamb was being stoned, that man was still a wolf, still he thirsted for blood; still he thought too little his own hands by which he might stone, he kept the garments of those stoning. So recalling what was said for him, if he too was among those of whom the Lord said, when he said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do;* imitating, then, in this very thing too his Lord, that he might be his friend, he too said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^11]. But how did he say it? With his knee placed on the earth. He commended himself standing: when he prayed for his enemies, he bent the knee. Why did he commend himself standing? Because he was commending a righteous man. Why for them did he bend the knees? Because he was praying for the wicked. *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge.*

**4.** Do you think Saul heard these words? He heard, but mocked: and yet he belonged to Stephen's prayer. Still he was raging, and already Stephen was being heard for him. For you know now, to say something about Saul, and afterward Paul; surely you know: in the same book it is written how Paul believed. Stephen being killed, the Church at Jerusalem suffered a most grave persecution. The brothers who were there were put to flight: only the Apostles remained; the rest were put to flight. But like burning torches, wherever they came, they kindled. Foolish Jews, when they put them to flight from Jerusalem, were casting coals of fire into the forest. Still Saul, for whom Stephen killed was not enough—which we gladly recall, because we now worship him—what did he do? He received letters from the priests and scribes, that wherever he found men of this way, that is, Christians, he might bring them bound to take the punishments such as Stephen had taken. And angry Saul went, the wolf went to the sheepfolds, to the flocks of the Lord: like a rabid wolf he thirsted for blood, panted for slaughter, went along the way. And he from above: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^12] Wolf, wolf, why do you persecute the lamb? When I was killed, I killed the lion. *Why do you persecute me?* Put off the wolf: from the wolf be a sheep, from the sheep a shepherd.

**5.** This is a most sweet picture, where you see holy Stephen being stoned, you see Saul keeping the garments of those stoning. This is *Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus,* this is *Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus*[^13]. You heard the voice well: *Why do you persecute me?* You were laid low, you were raised up: the persecutor prostrated, the preacher raised up. Say, let us hear: *Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, by the will of God*[^14]. Surely not by your will, O Saul? By your will we know, we have seen your fruits: Stephen was killed by your will. By the will of God, we see your fruits: everywhere you are read, everywhere you are recited, everywhere you turn opposing hearts to Christ, everywhere a good shepherd you gather great flocks. With him whom you stoned, with Christ you reign. You both see yourselves there; you both now hear our sermon; pray both of you for us. He will hear you both, who crowned you, one first, the other afterward: one who suffered persecution, the other who persecuted. That one was then a lamb, but that one was a wolf: but now both are lambs. Let the lambs acknowledge us, and see us in the flock of Christ: let them commend us by their prayers, that they may obtain a quiet and tranquil life for the Church of their Lord.
