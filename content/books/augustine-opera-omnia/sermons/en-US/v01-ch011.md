# Sermon 10: A Treatise of Aurelius Augustine the Bishop

On the Judgment of Solomon

Between the Two Harlot Women

**1.** The Scripture of Kings proclaims the marvelous judgment of Solomon between two women contending over a little son. For it is written: *Then there appeared two harlot women to king Solomon, and they stood in his sight. And one woman said: Take heed, my lord, I and this woman were dwelling in one house, and I gave birth in the house. And it happened on the third day after I gave birth, this woman also gave birth to a son, and we were in one place, and there was no one with us except the two of us in the house. And the son of this woman died in the night, for she lay upon him. And she arose in the middle of the night, and took my son from my side, and laid him in her bosom, and her dead son in my bosom. And I arose in the morning to give milk to my son, and he was dead: I considered him in the morning, and behold, he was not my son whom I bore. And that other woman said, No, but my son is the one who lives, and your son is the one who is dead. And she said, No, your son is the one dead, and my son is the one who lives. And so they spoke in the sight of the king. And the king said to them: You say, This is my son who lives, and her son is dead; and you say: No, but my son lives, and her son is dead. And the king said: Bring me a sword. And they brought a sword in the sight of the king. And the king said: Divide the living child into two parts, and give half of him to this one, and half of him to this one. And the woman whose son was the one living answered, and said to the king, for her womb was stirred over her son; and she said: Take heed, my lord, give her the child and do not put him to death. And this one said: Let it be neither mine nor hers; divide it. And the king answered, and said to the woman who said: Give it to her and do not put it to death, for she is its mother*[^1]. The prudence indeed of the king, granted by a divine gift, stands out marvelous in this judgment. For it was not fitting or proper that any other be judged the mother of the child than she who in a certain way conceived him again when she knew he was taken away, and was again in travail when she defended him from the false mother, and again gave birth when she did not permit him to be killed. Nevertheless, as the divine ancient Books are accustomed to intimate not only faith in the deed done, but also the mystery of the future, this Scripture is to be considered, whether it shows us something signified and figured in these two women.

**2.** And the two women, the Synagogue and the Church, occur at the first glance of consideration. For the Synagogue is convicted of having killed Christ, her son born of the Jews according to the flesh, while sleeping, that is, by following the light of the present life, and not understanding the manifestation of the truth in the words of the Lord; whence it is also written, *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten you*[^2]. But that there were two and only two dwelling in one house signifies, not absurdly, that besides circumcision and uncircumcision no kind of religion has been found in this world, so that in the person of one woman you set up the one kind of circumcised men comprehended under the worship and law of the one God, but in the person of the other woman you understand the whole gentile world of the uncircumcised given over to the worship of idols. But both were harlots. For the Apostle says that the Jews and the Greeks are all under sin. For every soul that, abandoning the eternity of truth, delights in earthly filth, commits fornication away from the Lord. And of the Church coming from gentile fornication it is manifest that she did not kill Christ. But how she too is the mother of Christ is to be considered. Attend to the Gospel, and hear the Lord saying, *Whoever does the will of my Father, he is my mother and brother and sister*[^3]. Where, then, did this one sleep, not so as to smother him with sleep, but yet so that a dead one could be substituted for her and the living one taken away? Or is it perhaps this, that the very sacrament of circumcision, which among the Jews had remained now dead because they understood all of it in a fleshly way—this sacrament of circumcision, then, which did not live among the Jews, who had killed Christ, who is the life of all the sacraments (since in him is understood vitally what among the Jews is celebrated visibly)—this sacrament of circumcision, then, like a lifeless body, certain Jews wished to persuade upon the gentiles who had believed in Christ, as it is written in the Acts of the Apostles, saying that they could not be saved unless they were circumcised? But they persuaded this to those ignorant of the law, as though substituting a dead son in the darkness of night. Nor yet could that persuasion avail anything, except when the sleep of folly crept in some part upon the Church of the gentiles. Whom the Apostle seems to wake as though sleeping, crying out, *O foolish Galatians, who has bewitched you?*[^4] And a little after, *Are you so foolish,* he says, *that, having begun in the spirit, you are now made perfect in the flesh?*[^5] As if he said: Are you so foolish that, whereas you first had a living spiritual work, having lost it, you afterward receive a dead alien one? For the Apostle himself says elsewhere, *The spirit is life because of righteousness*[^6]. And elsewhere, *But to be wise according to the flesh is death*[^7]. But by these and suchlike voices that mother awakes, and morning comes for her, when, with the Word of God, that is, with Christ, who was rising, that is, speaking in Paul, the obscurity of the law is enlightened. For he enlightened her when he said, *Tell me, you who wish to be under the law, have you not heard the law? For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one of the slave woman and one of the free woman: but he who was of the slave woman was born according to the flesh; but he who was of the free woman, by promise; which things are an allegory. For these are the two Testaments, one indeed from Mount Sinai, bearing children for slavery, which is Hagar. For Sinai is a mountain in Arabia, which is joined to that which now is Jerusalem: for she serves with her children. But the Jerusalem which is above is free*[^8]. No wonder, then, if on account of dead works the dead one belongs to her who is below, and on account of spiritual works the living one belongs to that Jerusalem which is above. For the lower regions are below, to which the dead belong; but the higher are above, to which the living belong. By this enlightenment, as though morning being made, the Church understands spiritual grace, repelling from herself the fleshly work of the law as a dead alien thing, and claiming for herself the living faith, since *the just lives by faith*[^9], which she has obtained in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, and therefore, like a three-day-old son, she recognizes him for certain, nor does she suffer him to be snatched from her.

**3.** Now let that one cry out that the Gospel is hers, as though owed to her and generated by her. For this they said to the gentiles in that very contention, who, being of the Jews and thinking in a fleshly way, dared to call themselves Christians. For they said that the Gospel had come as though owed to their justice. But it was not theirs, which they did not know how to hold spiritually. Therefore, in that they were called Christians, glorying in an alien name, like that woman about the son whom she herself had not borne, they dared even to contend, when they themselves, from the works of the law, with spiritual understanding excluded, as though had cast out the soul from the body of their work, and, extinguishing the living spirit of prophecy, had remained at fleshly works without life, that is, without spiritual understanding. Which they desired even to substitute upon the gentiles, and from them to take away, as a living son, the Christian name. Whom the Apostle so refutes that he says that Christian grace belongs to them the less, the more they claim it for themselves as though owed, and as though by the right of works glory that it is their own. *For to him who works,* he says, *the wage is not reckoned according to grace but according to debt. But to him who does not work, but believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned unto righteousness*[^10]. And therefore he excludes those also from their number who, being of the Jews, had rightly believed and retained living spiritual grace. These remnants he says were made safe of the people of the Jews, when the multitude went into perdition. *So then even at this time,* he says, *a remnant according to the election of grace has been made safe. But if by grace, then no longer of works; otherwise grace is no longer grace*[^11], so that those are excluded from grace who claim the reward of the Gospel as though their own, that is, as owed and given to their works, as though the Synagogue cried out: The son is mine. But she lied. For she too had received him, but, sleeping upon him, that is, being proudly wise, had killed him. But this mother was now awake and understood that, not by her merits—for she is a harlot—but by the grace of God, a son was granted to her, namely the work of evangelical faith which she desired to nurse in the bosom of her heart. And so that one sought the glory of men in another's son; this one kept the affection of love in her own.

**4.** But that royal judgment between the two admonishes us of nothing else than that we should contend for the truth, and drive out hypocrisy as a false mother from the spiritual gift of the Church as from an alien living son, and not allow her to lord it over the grace granted to others, who could not keep her own. But let us do this, defending and contending, not even to the danger of division. For that sentence of the judge, when he ordered the little one to be divided, is not the cutting off of unity, but the proof of love. For the name of Solomon, as the Latins interpret it, is "peaceful." The peaceful king, then, does not tear apart the members which by unity and concord contain the vital spirit. But by threatening he finds the true mother, and by judging he separates the false. If, then, it comes to a temptation of this kind, lest the unity of Christian grace be divided, we are taught to say, "Give her the child, only let him live." For the true mother seeks not the honor of the mother but the safety of the son. Wherever he is, the sincere love of the mother will possess him more than the usurpation of the false one.

**5.** Likewise I see that these two women in one house signify two kinds of men in one Church: one of those in whom pretense rules, the other in whom true love reigns. So that we may altogether regard these two as two women, love and pretense. For pretense deceitfully imitates love. And therefore the Apostle guards against this, when he says, *Love without pretense*[^12]. For although they dwell in one house, as long as that Gospel net is in the sea, and encloses good and bad fish at once until it is brought to shore, yet each does her own works. But both were harlots, because all are converted to the grace of God from the desire of the world, nor can anyone truly glory in prior merits of justice. But that a harlot commits fornication is her own; that she has a son is God's. For all men are formed by the one God the Creator. Nor is it to be wondered at that even in the sins of men God works well. For even from the crime of Judas the betrayer our Lord worked the salvation of the human race. But there is this difference, that when God has done something good from the sin of someone, generally the sinner himself would not wish it. Not only because, when he sins, he sins not with that mind by which providence God works justice from his sin—for not with that mind did Judas betray Christ with which Christ suffered himself to be betrayed—but also because, when he has come to know that the outcome of his sin has reached something better which he himself would not wish, he grieves rather than rejoices. As if someone wishing to give poison to a sick enemy is deceived in the appearance of the medicine, and offers one thing for another, something healthful, and the sick man becomes well by the kindness of God, who wished to turn the crime of his enemy into health. Which, however, when that wicked man has come to know, he is tormented over the health of the man which was accomplished through his hands. But if a harlot gladly keeps a conceived son, and, impelled neither by lust nor by avarice of base wages, does not cast out by a draught of abortion from her womb what she had conceived, lest fecundity contradict her sinning, that desire which was flowing out into many, converted to the one gift of God, will now be named not desire but love. The son of the harlot, then, is rightly understood as the grace of the sinful woman. But from the old baseness a new man is born by the pardon of sins.

**6.** The Lord, then, even in the very number of his disciples, although he chose all of them from sinners, nevertheless chose first those who would persevere in love, rather than Judas the pretender. It is not indeed written in what order he was chosen, yet it is known that the good were chosen before him, and not in vain is he numbered last. And after the Lord's ascension, the Holy Spirit, according to the Lord's promise, sent down from above, was poured out upon all who were in one place. By these the Church began; they were good and loved without pretense. Afterward, then, pretense began to work in the Church, and therefore love first gave birth. But on the third day the fruit of love is greater, so that it can now be recognized by continence and justice and the expectation of things to come. But pretense, even if it gives birth, that is, even if for a short time it has rejoiced in the remission of sins, oppressed as though by the worldly sleep of desire, when, cast down from the hope of heavenly rewards, it is dashed back into earthly rest with a heavy heart, as though sleeping it smothers the pardon which by believing it had merited. But such people prefer to rejoice in the name of justice rather than in the truth, and they try, by lying, to transfer to themselves another's good work through dark deceptions, as a living son through the night. And they not only usurp the good works of others, but also lay upon others their own crimes, as though substituting a dead son.

**7.** But when will pretense be permitted so much that it may glory under the false name of justice, with no one forbidding, and apply to itself, for the deceitful boasting of the maternal name, the living spiritual work which it did not itself beget, and which it once had begotten in itself but extinguished by the weight of a most cruel sleep, and substitute its own crimes by laying them upon the good and innocent? When, then, will pretense so reign, except when iniquity shall abound, that is, the darkness of sins shall prevail as though in a blind night, and the love of many shall grow cold, that is, the mother of the spiritual work, like the living child, shall fall asleep? Yet because love will so grow cold as to burn more negligently—for it was not said that it shall be utterly extinguished so as not to be at all—this mother so slept as not to kill her son, but yet gave place to the frauds of pretense. But, awakened, when she sees the impiety which she herself did not do laid upon her by those who do it, and perceives that pretense dares to glory in the spiritual work of grace which she kept, that she is named a worker of iniquity but pretense the mother of the good work, she implores the help of the peaceful Judge. For Solomon is interpreted "peaceful." Whom we see to have pronounced two sentences, the first as though of one ignorant, the last of one judging with manifest knowledge. The first proposes a contest of devotion, the second gives the reward to the victor. In the first the mother is proved, in the last she nurses. In the first, weeping, she casts her seed, in the second, with exultation, she carries back her sheaves. Which pertains to the two times of the Church which the Lord Christ, the peaceful judge, governs: one that now is, the other that is to come. In this we are proved, in that we are crowned.

**8.** But there is no greater proof of love in the Church of Christ than when even the honor itself which seems to be among men is despised, lest the members of the little one be divided and the Christian weakness be torn apart by the rending of unity. For the Apostle says that he showed himself as a mother to the little ones in whom he had done the good evangelical work—not he himself, but the grace of God with him. For that harlot could call nothing her own but her sins, but the gift of fecundity from God. But the grace of the giver is loved the more, the more punishment was owed. And the Lord says well of the harlot, *To whom much is forgiven, she loves much*[^13]. So the apostle Paul says, *I became a little one in your midst, as if a nurse should cherish her children*[^14]. But when it comes to the danger by which the little one is divided, when pretense claims false honor for itself and is prepared to cut unity, let the mother despise her own honor, so long as she sees the son whole and keeps him alive, lest perhaps, while she too obstinately claims the honor owed to her own womb, she give place to pretense to divide the weak members by the sword of schism. Let the mother, love, then, say, *Give her the child. Whether by occasion or by truth, let Christ be proclaimed*[^15]. In Moses love cries out, *Lord, either forgive them, or blot me out of your book*[^16]. But in the Pharisees pretense speaks, *If we let him go, the Romans will come and take away from us our nation and our place*[^17]. For they wished to have not the truth but the name of justice, and they desired to hold the honor owed to the just through deceit. Yet pretense, reigning in them, was permitted to sit in the chair of Moses, so that it could be said by the Lord, *What they say, do, but what they do, do not wish to do*[^18]; so that, having false honor, they might nevertheless by the truth of the Scriptures nurse the little and weak ones. For pretense has its own crime, by which it extinguished, by the weight of its sleep, the new man whom it had received through the grace of the giver, but the milk of faith which it has is not its own. Because, even with the little one killed, who signifies the life being reborn, pretense, now established in evil ways, nevertheless retains in memory, as in its breasts, the words of faith and the Christian teaching which is handed on to all who come to the Church. From this milk even a false mother could pour into the little one sucking the juice of true faith nevertheless. Hence the true mother is secure, when even by pretenders in the Church her little one is nursed with the milk of the divine Scriptures of the Catholic faith, when, division being forbidden, unity is safe, and the last sentence of the judge, by which the last judgment of Christ is figured, love being proved, which for the safety of the little one and the firmness of unity yielded honor even to pretense, so that, holding the love and embrace of life-giving grace, it may enjoy the everlasting reward of the devout mother.
