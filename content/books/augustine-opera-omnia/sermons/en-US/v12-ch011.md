# Sermon 295, Expanded Version

**1.** The suffering of the most blessed apostles Peter and Paul has consecrated this day for us. We are not speaking of obscure martyrs: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth*. These martyrs saw what they preached, following righteousness, confessing the truth, dying for the truth. Blessed Peter, first of the apostles, a fervent lover of Christ, deserved to hear: *And I say to you that you are Peter*. For he himself had said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*. He said to Christ, *You are the Son of the living God;* Christ said to him, *You are Peter. And I say to you that you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church*. Upon this [rock, this] faith you confess, upon what you said, *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*, there I will build my Church. For you are Peter. Peter takes his name from the rock, not the rock from Peter. Peter is named from the rock just as a Christian is named from Christ. Do you want to know from what rock Peter is named? Let Paul speak; listen: *I do not want* *you to be ignorant, brothers*—the Apostle Paul says—*I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, all passed through the sea, all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ*. See where Peter's name comes from. [^1]

**2.** Before his Passion, as you know, the Lord Jesus chose his disciples, whom he called apostles. Among them Peter alone deserved almost everywhere to represent the whole Church. Because of that role, in which he alone represented the whole Church, he deserved to hear: *To you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven*. For it was not one man but the unity of the Church that received these keys. Hence Peter's preeminence is proclaimed, because he bore the figure of the Church's universality and unity when he was told, *To you I entrust* what was entrusted to all. For to know that the Church received the keys of the kingdom of heaven, hear what the Lord says elsewhere to all his apostles: *Receive the Holy Spirit*. And immediately: *If you forgive anyone's sins, they will be forgiven him; if you retain anyone's, they will be retained*. This concerns the keys, of which it was said: *Whatever you loose on earth will also be loosed in heaven, and whatever you bind on earth will also be bound in heaven*. But he said this to Peter. To know that Peter then represented the whole Church, hear what is said to the Church herself, to all the holy faithful: *If your brother sins against you, correct him between you and him alone. If he does not hear you, take one or two with you. For it is written: Every word shall stand on the testimony of two or three witnesses. If he does not hear them either, report it to the Church. If he does not hear her either, let him be to you as a gentile and a tax collector. Amen, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will also be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will also be loosed in heaven*. The dove binds; the dove looses. The building upon the rock binds and looses. Let the bound fear; let the loosed rejoice. Let those who have been loosed fear being bound; let those who are bound pray to be loosed. *Each person is entangled in the cords of his own sins*, and outside this Church nothing is loosed. The man dead for four days is told, *Lazarus, come out*, and he came out of the tomb with hands and feet bound in strips of cloth. The Lord awakens him so that the dead man comes out of the tomb; so the Lord touches the heart, so that the confession of sin comes forth. Yet a person's confession of sin still does little good while he remains bound. Therefore, after Lazarus came out of the tomb, the Lord said to his disciples, to whom he had said, *Whatever you loose on earth will also be loosed in heaven*: *Loose* *him and let him go*. He awakened him himself; he loosed him through the disciples. [^2]

**3.** Thus the Church's strength was especially commended in Peter because he followed the Lord as he went to his Passion, and a certain weakness was marked because, questioned by a servant girl, he denied the Lord. See: the lover suddenly became a denier. He who had presumed on himself discovered himself. For, as you know, he had said: *Lord, with you even to death; and if I must die, I will lay down my life for you*. And the Lord said to the presumptuous man: *Will you lay down your life for me? Amen, I say to you, before the rooster crows, you will deny me three times*. What the physician foretold happened; what the sick man presumed could not happen. But then what? The Lord immediately looked at him. That is what is written there, that is how the Gospel speaks: *The Lord looked at him, and he went outside and wept bitterly*. *He went outside*: this is confession. *He wept bitterly*, he who knew how to love. Sweetness followed in love where bitterness had preceded in grief. [^3]

**4.** Rightly, after the Resurrection, the Lord also entrusted his sheep to Peter himself to feed. For he was not the only disciple who deserved to feed the Lord's sheep; but when Christ speaks to one, unity is commended, and he speaks first to Peter because Peter is first among the apostles. *Simon, son of John*, says the Lord, *do you love me?* He answers: *I love you*. Again he is asked and again answers. He is asked a third time and grows sad, as though he were not believed. But how could the one who saw his heart not believe him? Indeed, after that sadness he answered: *Lord, you know all things; you know that I love you*. For you who know everything cannot be ignorant of this alone. Do not be sad, Apostle; answer once, answer again, answer a third time. Let confession conquer three times in love, because presumption was conquered three times in fear. What you bound three times must be loosed three times. Loose through love what you bound through fear. Yet the Lord entrusted his sheep to Peter once, again, and a third time. [^4]

**5.** Pay attention, my brothers: *Feed*, he says, *my little sheep, feed my lambs*, *feed my sheep*. Did he say, “Yours”? Good servant, feed the Lord's sheep, bearing the Lord's mark. Was Peter or Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in Peter's or Paul's name? Feed, then, his sheep, washed in his baptism, marked with his name, redeemed by his blood. *Feed*, he says, *my sheep*. For heretics, wicked and runaway servants, dividing among themselves what they did not buy and making stolen property into possessions as though their own, imagine that they feed their own sheep. What else, I ask you, does this mean: “Unless I baptize you, you will be unclean. Unless you have my baptism, you will not be washed”? Have you not heard: *Cursed is everyone who places his hope in man*? Hence, beloved, those Peter baptized are Christ's sheep, and those Judas baptized are Christ's sheep. See what the bridegroom says to his beloved in the Song of Songs, when the bride says to him: *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you rest at midday, lest I become like one veiled among your companions' flocks*. *Tell me*, she says, *where you feed, where you rest at midday*, in the brightness of truth, in the heat of charity. “Why are you afraid, beloved? What do you fear?” *Lest I become*, she says, *like one veiled*, that is, hidden, as though not the Church—for the Church is not veiled: *A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden*—and in wandering come not upon your flock but *upon your companions' flocks*. For heretics are called companions. *They went out from us*: before leaving, they approached the same table with us. What, then, is answered her? *Unless you know yourself*—the bridegroom speaks, answering her question—*unless you know yourself, O beautiful one among women*—O true one among heresies—*unless you know yourself*, because so much has been foretold of you: *In your offspring all nations will be blessed*. *The Lord, the God of gods, has spoken and called the earth from sunrise to sunset*. *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance and the ends of the earth as your possession*. *Their sound has gone out into all the earth*. These testimonies were foretold of you. *Unless*, then, *you know yourself, go out yourself*. For I do not cast you out, so that those who remain may say of you: *They went out from us*. *Go out yourself in the tracks of the flocks*: not of the flock of which it was said, *There will be one flock and one shepherd*. *Go out yourself in the tracks of the flocks and feed your goats*, not my sheep as Peter does. For these sheep entrusted to him, Peter deserved to be crowned with martyrdom, which deserved to be celebrated throughout the world in today's solemnity. [^5]

**6.** Let Paul too come from Saul, a lamb from a wolf: first an enemy, afterward an apostle; first a persecutor, afterward a preacher. Let him come, let him receive letters from the chief priests so that wherever he finds Christians he may bring them bound for punishment. Let him receive them, receive them, set out, proceed, pant for slaughter, thirst for blood: *He who dwells in heaven will laugh at him*. For he was going, as it is written, breathing slaughter, and drawing near Damascus. Then the Lord spoke from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* {If Saul could persecute Christ when he hung on the wood, could he do so when he sat in heaven? But what does *Why do you persecute me?* mean?} I am here; I am there: here the head, there the body. {When someone pressed in a crowd feels another's sole stepping on his foot, does the head not cry out for the feet—silence in the foot, a voice in the head for the foot, because there is unity in the body? And does the tongue, which no one has touched, not say, not “You are stepping on my foot,” but “You are stepping on me”?} Let us not marvel, then, brothers: we belong to Christ's body {together with him, our whole head. All that we are together with him is Christ. We are not only Christ's; we are Christ together with him, not without him. See whether I do not prove \*what I said. *He does not say* ‘\**and to offsprings*’ *as of many*—the Apostle is speaking of Abraham's offspring—*not* ‘*to* \**offsprings*’ *as of many, but as of one,* ‘*and to your offspring*,’ *who is Christ*. Therefore Christ is Abraham's offspring. And elsewhere, of us: *If you*, he says, *are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring*. Surely you have understood: if Christ is Abraham's offspring and we are Abraham's offspring, that is, Christ's members, without doubt the whole Christ is he and we, head and body.} *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad*. You hurt yourself, not me. My Church grows through persecutions. But he, terrified and trembling, says: *Lord, who are you? And he said: I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute*. Immediately changed, he awaits the command, puts aside envy, prepares obedience. He is told what to do. And before Paul was baptized, the Lord speaks to Ananias: “Go to that street, to that man named Saul; baptize him, because he is a chosen vessel for me.” A vessel should carry something; a vessel should not be empty; a vessel must be filled—with what but grace? But Ananias answered the Lord Jesus Christ: “*Lord, I have heard that this man has done much to your saints*. And now he carries letters from the chief priests, so that wherever he finds men of this Way, he may bring them bound.” And the Lord said to him: *I will show him what he must suffer for my name*. Ananias trembled on hearing Saul's name; the weak sheep trembled at the wolf's reputation, even under the shepherd's voice. [^6]

**7.** See, the Lord showed him what he must suffer for his name. He himself trained him in toil, in chains, in beatings, in prisons, in shipwrecks. He himself arranged his suffering, himself brought him to this day. One day of suffering for two apostles. But those two too were one; although even if they suffered on different days, they were one. Peter went before; Paul followed, first Saul, afterward Paul, because first proud, afterward humble. Saul was named from Saul, holy David's persecutor. The persecutor was cast down; the preacher was raised up. He changed his \*name from pride to humility. For “Paul” means small. I remind Your Charity of your own words: do we not say every day, “I will see you after a little while,” “A little later I will do this or that”? What, then, is Paul? Ask him himself. *I*, he says, *am the least of the apostles*. [^7]

**8.** Let us celebrate the apostles' feast day, consecrated for us by their blood. Let us love their faith, life, labors, sufferings, confessions, and preaching. For we advance by loving, not by celebrating this festival for fleshly enjoyment. What do the martyrs ask of us? If they ask for anything, they lack something; if they still seek human praises, they have not conquered the world. But if they have conquered it, they ask nothing of us for their own sake, but ask for our own sake. Let our way, then, be made straight before the Lord. It was narrow, thorny, hard; through the passage of so many such people, it has become smooth. The Lord himself passed first, then the fearless apostles, then the martyrs; {old and young passed,} children, women, girls. But who was in them? He who said: *Without me you can do nothing*. [^8]
