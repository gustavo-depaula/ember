# Sermon 139a

**1.** This gospel reading is so famed by so great a miracle, that no one even of the unbelievers has not heard that Lazarus was raised by Christ; how much more is it known among the faithful, what the unbelievers could not be ignorant of? And yet, when it is read, as by a new spectacle the mind is renewed. It is not unfitting, then, that we too, what we are wont to say of it, repeat by learning. Nor will what I am going to say perhaps be wearisome; for this reading is more often repeated in your ears, than our treatment of it. For if it is read when it is not the Sabbath, or when it is not the Lord's day, a sermon on it is not given. This I said, lest you disdain to hear what I will say. Let no one, then, say: He has already said it; because the deacon too has already read it, and yet it was gladly heard. So then hear.

**2.** We have learned that by the Lord Jesus Christ in the holy Gospel three dead persons were raised. One, the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, to whose house he came, and whom, when he had first heard her to be in peril by sickness, he found dead; and he said to her: *Girl, I say to you, arise*, and she rose again. Another, a young man, who was now being carried out dead beyond the gate of the city, and was gravely mourned by his widowed mother; for then he saw these things, but commanded the bearers to stand still, and said: *Young man, I say to you, arise; and that dead man sat up, and began to speak, and he gave him back to his mother* [^1]. The third is this Lazarus, whom just now with the eyes of faith we saw both dying and rising, by a far greater miracle, and a great benefit; for he had been four days dead, and now stank; yet he was raised. What do these three dead persons mean? For they do not signify nothing, and the Lord's doings of miracles are the words of mysteries. We find, then, in the sins of men three kinds of death. Recall those three dead persons; first that girl was dead in the house, had not yet been carried out; but that young man, now carried out beyond the gate; but Lazarus buried, and pressed by the mass of a stone. What, then, are the three kinds in sins? I say. If anyone has consented in heart to an evil desire, and has decided to do what it persuaded by flattering, he is now dead. No one knows it, because he has not been carried out; it is a secret death, it is in the house, it is in the bedroom, yet it is death. Let no one say he has not committed adultery, if he has resolved to do it; if he has consented that it should be done to the tickling delight, he has already done it; he is an adulterer, she is chaste. Question God; let him answer you about this domestic death, about the inner death, about the bedroom death; of which beds we read: *What you say in your hearts, and in your beds be pricked* [^2]. Hear, then, the sentence of the raiser about this death: *He who has looked*, he says, *at a woman to desire her, has already committed adultery with her in his heart* [^3], he who has not yet practiced fornication in his body. But sometimes the Lord looks on, and he repents of having decided, repents of having consented; in the bedroom he is dead, in the bedroom he rises. But if he does what he decided, death has gone forth, now it is outside; but it too is ended by repenting, and the dead man carried out is restored to life. But if to the deed habit too is added, now he stinks, and is pressed as by the mass of a stone of that habit; but not even him does Christ despise, he is able to raise even him; but he weeps. That Christ wept over Lazarus, when the Gospel was read, we heard. Those, then, who are pressed by habit, suffer violence, and Christ groans to raise them. For the divine word much rebukes them, the Scripture much cries out to them; much we cry out to them, that we too may be heard, and as though over Lazarus reviving rejoice. *Take away*, he says, *the stone*. Whence could it come about that he should rise, except with the weight of habit removed? Cry out, bind, chide, accuse, remove the stone; when you see such men, do not spare them; you labor, but you remove the stone. He, he, whose voice reaches the heart, let him cry out: *Lazarus, come forth*; that is: Live, come out of the tomb, change your life, end your death. And that dead man came forth, bound he stood; because, even if he ceased to sin, he is still guilty of the past, and there is need that he pray, and do penance for his deeds; not for those he does, because he no longer does them; he revives, he does not do them; but for those which he did, he is still bound. Therefore to the ministers of his Church, through whom a hand is laid on the penitents, Christ says: *Loose him, and let him go* [^4]. Loose, loose: *what you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven* [^5]. He who had heard these things from me and held them, let him think he has just now read what I wrote; but he who had not heard them, let him write them in his heart, that when he wishes he may read them.

[^1]: See Lk 8:54; 7:14-15
[^2]: See Ps 4:4 (4)
[^3]: See Mt 5:28
[^4]: See Jn 11:39-44
[^5]: See Mt 18:18
