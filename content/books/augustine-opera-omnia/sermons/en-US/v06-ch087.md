# Sermon 229/S: From the Sermons of the Octave of Easter

**1.** We see the earth, we see the sea: they are the works of God; the one lies under our eyes, the other under our understandings; the one is known to our flesh, the other to our mind. What is the dry land in the Church? Every soul thirsting for God is called dry land. For the waters were separated, and the dry land appeared[^1]. This sea is this world, and the evil are the bitter waters; they were separated and gathered together into one gathering, that is, predestined to one end, to which God directs all whom he separates from the saints. But God separates them; for man cannot separate these things, and the dry land appears through the separation. Set two men together: one desires the spectacle, the other the church. Joined in body, they are separated in their desires. The one belongs to the bitter water, the other appears as dry land. How do we prove that this earth, which signifies men desiring good things, is dry land? The psalm says to God: *My soul is like a land without water to you*[^2]. My soul has thirsted for you. It thirsts, it is dry land; it is separated from the waters of the sea. Let it not heed that it is not yet separated in body; already its desire has made the separation. Some desire God, others desire the world. What, then, does the dry land thirst for? Rain from heaven, rain from the clouds, rain from the Scriptures, rain from the firmament. But when it desires the rain, it desires the sweet water separated from the bitter water. But God knows that the dry land desires, for it lies hidden and is in secret. The desires of the sea, that is, worldly desires, appear. If anyone desires money, he is moved toward that money, he wishes to acquire it; because money is a visible thing, his desire appears. But he who desires God, his desire is hidden; because God, whom he desires, is hidden; he is within, he is a hidden thing. It thirsts indeed, and is dry land; but it appears to the eyes of God. And he did not omit the fruit, but at once said: *Let the earth bring forth the herb of fodder*[^3]. Let the earth bring forth on the very day on which it was made dry land. That dry land could not be without fruit for long. Let us too hear the word of God: and let the earth bring forth the herb of fodder, that is, the good works of mercy, of which Isaiah says: *Break your bread for the hungry, and bring the needy without shelter into your house*[^4], and so on. The firmament, then, is the Scripture of God; the luminaries in the firmament, the understanding of the Scriptures; but the lights in heaven, the insights into the Scriptures. Do you wish to come to the heavenly light? First be earth bearing fruit, that is, let the works of mercy go before; for after the works of mercy comes the illumination of that light which you desire.

[^1]: See Gen 1:9
[^2]: Ps 143:6 (142:6)
[^3]: Gen 1:11
[^4]: Isa 58:7
