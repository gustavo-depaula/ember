# Sermon 363A

**1.** Some say that it was impossible for Christ to be born of Mary, asserting that that part of the body was shameful. Yet this is the claim of an insane and blasphemous mind that does not know what it is saying, since even if there had been anything unclean there, the Lord, illuminating it by his presence, would have purified it. Let such a person look at the sun sending its rays everywhere, into sewers and even the filthiest places. See: the sun sends its ray into a sewer; the sewer is dried, the sun's ray is not defiled. Here is another comparison. Your impious soul, the soul that invents such things, where is it? Is it only in your eyes, head, hands, or belly, and not also in your intestines, where your excrement is? Tell me, please. See: your soul is in your intestines, where your excrement is. Fool, your soul is not defiled by your excrement: could God be defiled by his own work?

**2.** There are, then, those who say that after giving birth—after the Lord's birth—holy Mary had children by Joseph, a view foreign to the Catholic Church. They are answered briefly in this way. The organs through which we enter the world are called gates; not only are they called so, but this is also written. For holy Job said of the day of his birth, which he curses: *Because it did not*, he says, *close the gates of my mother's womb*, so that she would not bear me. Thus, as we said above, we have \*proved from the Scriptures that they are gates. Now notice, brothers, how the gate through which the Lord came forth was always closed. The prophet says: *I turned*, he says, *toward the outer gate of the sanctuary facing east, and it was closed. And the Lord said to me: This gate will be closed and will not be opened, and no man will pass through it, because the Lord God of Israel has come forth through it; therefore it will always be closed*. See, brothers, where it is clearly shown that holy Mary was always a virgin and remained a virgin: a virgin before childbirth, a virgin in childbirth, a virgin after childbirth. She conceives and is a virgin; gives birth and is a virgin; nurses and is a virgin. Mary could be a mother; she could not be a woman who had known a man. The virgin's breasts swell, and the mother's genital organs remain untouched. Certainly she who gave birth to wholeness could not lose her own. [^1]

**3.** Great merit, brothers, great gift, great grace! The creature bore the Creator, the servant bore the Lord, the daughter bore her father: daughter of his divinity, mother of his humanity. For our Lord Jesus Christ had two births, both wonderful: one from the Father's equality, the other from the mother's weakness; born of the Father outside time, of the mother without seed, because *the Word became flesh and dwelt among us*. [^2]

**4.** Brothers, the Word, I say, *became flesh*. Why, brothers? Why did our God who made heaven and earth put on flesh? Why, I ask, brothers, why was he born, why did he suffer, why did he die? Seek the reason, and you will find it. In a sense, brothers, through his Incarnation he reproaches us and says: “I was God; you were human beings. I was righteous and immortal; you were unrighteous and mortal. As God, I had nothing by which to die; as human beings, you had nothing by which to live. There was no life with you and no death with me. I brought something to you; I took something from you. I brought you life; I took death from you. Therefore I died from what is yours so that you might live from what is mine. Where did I die from what is yours? In my mother's virginity. Where do you live from what is mine? In my Father's equality.” If he had not done this, none of us would have escaped. Thus he put on flesh for us; for us he was wounded; for us, innocent, he endured punishment. He took your punishment, not your guilt; but by taking punishment without guilt, he both healed guilt and cured punishment. [^3]

[^1]: Job 3:10; Ezek 44:1–2.
[^2]: John 1:14.
[^3]: John 1:14. Cf. Gen 1:1.
