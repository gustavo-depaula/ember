# Sermon 375: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1.** "Epiphany" in Latin means "manifestation." The Lord Christ a few days ago was made manifest by his nativity to the Jews; but today he was declared by a star to the Gentiles. *The ox knew its owner, and the ass its Lord's manger*[^1]. The ox stands for the Jews, the ass for the Gentiles; both came to the one manger and found the food of the Word. The Magi who came to adore Christ, and who signify the firstfruits of the Gentiles, did not receive the Law, did not hear the Prophets: the tongue of heaven was the star. As though it were said to them: What gain have I sought from you? *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^2]. And yet Herod, troubled, asked the Jews where Christ was to be born. And they answered, *In Bethlehem of Judea*[^3]; and they brought forward the prophetic testimony; and while the Magi went on to adore, they themselves remained motionless. On a causeway there are stones; they point out the way, and they themselves do not walk. Yet the Magi went on to Bethlehem; but, the city found, from where can they find the house? Behold, that star which shone from heaven led them down on the earth and stood over the place where the child was. How great the service of the elements, and the wickedness of the Jews! Herod was troubled, as if Christ had come to seek and to find an earthly kingdom. The lion of heaven was born, and the earthly little fox was troubled. The Lord said of Herod: *Go, tell that fox*[^4]. Being troubled, what did he do? He killed infants. What did he do? He killed infants for the sake of the infant Word. They were made martyrs by their blood before they could confess the Lord with their mouth. And these firstfruits Christ sent to the Father. The infant came, and infants went: the infant to us, the infants to God. *Out of the mouth of infants and sucklings you have perfected praise*[^5]. Let us rejoice; the day has shone upon us. The Magi, the firstfruits of the Gentiles, signified us. The Jews recognized him when he was born; the Gentiles recognized him yesterday. The different walls came to the cornerstone, from here the Jews, from there the Gentiles; from different starting points, but not to different ends. You have seen and you know that walls are as far from each other as they are distant from the corner. The nearer they draw to the corner, the nearer they draw to each other: when they have come to the corner, they cling together. This Christ did. Far from each other had been the Jews and the Gentiles, the circumcision and the uncircumcision, those under the Law and those without the Law, the worshipers of the one true God and the worshipers of many false ones. How far apart? But he *is our peace, who made both one*[^6]. But those who came from among the Jews are counted in the good wall; for those who came did not remain in the ruin. We too have been made one with them: but in the One, not in ourselves. Of whom was Christ born? Of the Jews. So you have it written: *Salvation is from the Jews*[^7], but not for the Jews alone. For he did not say, Salvation for the Jews, but, *Salvation is from the Jews*. They laid hold of him, and they themselves lost him; they bound him, and he escaped them; they saw him, and they killed him: we neither laid hold of him, and we hold him; nor saw him, and we believe; we are later, and we go ahead. Those who went before us lost the way: but we have both found the way, and walking in it we shall come to the homeland.

[^1]: Isa 1:3
[^2]: Ps 19:1 (18:1)
[^3]: Matt 2:5
[^4]: Luke 13:32
[^5]: Ps 8:2 (8:3)
[^6]: Eph 2:14
[^7]: John 4:22
