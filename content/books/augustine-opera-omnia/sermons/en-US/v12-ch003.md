# Sermon 61B

**1.** Dearest brothers, we must do what our Lord urged us to do in the Gospel. For he says: *Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, and whoever seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened*. We have a Lord who is ready to give; but notice the three things he says: *Ask, seek, knock*. Let the one who asks bring humility; the one who seeks, diligence; the one who knocks, persistence. For we ask with the mouth, seek with the eyes, and knock with the hands. What is asking but praying? What is seeking but reflecting? What is knocking but doing good with our hands? May God open what we knock at through good works: this is knocking at God's door. Hence the scriptural psalm says elsewhere: *In the day of my trouble I sought God with my hands by night before him, and I was not deceived*. I sought God in the day of my trouble, and in the day of my trouble I did not seek gold, silver, money, or a \*purse, but God alone. Every person wants to be free of trouble, yet trouble is good when it compels a person to seek God. Did he say, “In the day of my pleasures I sought God”? Perhaps when he had pleasures, he did not seek God. God gave him trouble, not in anger but rather in mercy. He did not abandon him but wanted to be sought by him. That is why he said: *In the day of my trouble I sought God*. For we know many who do not seek God when things go well for them, but seek God when they are in trouble. Trouble benefits them, then, by causing them to seek God. For when seeking comes, finding follows, and trouble is removed.[^1]

**2.** But notice how he spoke. After saying, *In the day of my trouble I sought God*, he added: *with my hands*. I sought God with my hands, and he added, *by night*, and further added, *before him*. There are now four things he has said: *In the day of trouble*, *with my hands*, *by night*, and *before him*. What is trouble? What is seeking with one's hands? What is seeking by night? And what is seeking before him? Trouble is when we suffer hardships. Seeking with one's hands means seeking through good works. Seeking by night means seeking in this world, when Christ's truth has not yet shone forth. For this world will pass away and Christ will come. When Christ comes, he will be like the sun shining 〈upon〉 everyone's hearts. Why did he also add *before him*? Because the person who seeks with his hands does good works; but someone who does good works to please people does not do them before him. The one who acts before him does so to please God, not people. Rightly the passage continues: *And I was not deceived*. Someone who sought in this way was not deceived, because he found what he was seeking. That is why he told us: *Ask, seek, knock*.[^2]

**3.** See what follows in the Gospel: *Which of you, if his son asks him for bread, gives him a stone instead of bread? Or if his son asks for a fish, does he offer him a serpent? Or when his son asks for an egg, does he offer him a scorpion? If, then, you who are evil know how to give good things to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give good things to those who ask him*. As long as we live in this world, then, we endure the day of trouble, because *we are away from the Lord*. 〈Whether things go badly〉 or well for us by worldly standards, it is a day of trouble as long as it is a time of pilgrimage. For trouble passes when pilgrimage ends. And pilgrimage will end when, in our heavenly homeland, joined to the holy angels, we live without end, reign without end, and rejoice without end with our king, the Lord Jesus Christ. We shall \*see, love, and praise.[^3]

**4.** There are three things of the Lord that the holy Apostle builds up in us, for these are what we need in this world: faith, hope, and charity. Faith, that we may believe; hope, that we may wait; charity, that we may love. Faith is the fish, hope the egg, charity the bread. Why is faith a fish? Because just as a fish swims in water and lives from it, so faith is received through baptism and lives in this world. Why is hope an egg? Because the chick is not yet seen but is still hoped for. For the Apostle says: *We were saved in hope. But hope that is seen is not hope. For who hopes for what he sees? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with endurance*. Our endurance warms the egg. See how much endurance a hen has when warming an egg. She scarcely rises from it even to eat a little and return; she first has endurance so that afterward she may find joy in her young. Charity, because it conquers all things, is compared to bread, without which no table can sustain life. Hence no one can live without charity—that is, without God—and whoever does not have charity will lose life. Just as bread is placed on the table first, the first course is also set there, but the bread is not removed. Where the course is, other things are set down and taken away; the bread nevertheless remains to the end.[^4]

**5.** Let us, then, ask, seek, and knock, so that our Lord may give us what he knows benefits us in this world and bestow on us in the world to come the eternal life he has promised.

[^1]: Matt 7:7–8; Luke 11:9–10; Matt 7:7; Luke 11:9; Ps 77:2 (76:3 Vg); cf. Matt 10:9; Luke 10:4.
[^2]: Ps 77:2 (76:3 Vg); cf. Matt 6:1–4.
[^3]: Matt 7:9–11; Luke 11:11–13; 2 Cor 5:6.
[^4]: Cf. 1 Cor 13:13; Rom 8:24–25; cf. 1 John 4:8.
