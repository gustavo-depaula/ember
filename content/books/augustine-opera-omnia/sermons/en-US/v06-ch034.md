# Sermon 212: On the Handing Over of the Creed

**1.** It is time that you receive the Creed, in which is contained briefly, for the sake of eternal salvation, all that is believed. The Creed (Symbolum) is named by a word transferred from a certain likeness, because merchants make a "symbol" among themselves by which their partnership is held by a pact of faith. And your partnership is of spiritual merchandise, that you may be like the *traders seeking a good pearl*[^1]. This is the *love which will be poured out in your hearts through the Holy Spirit who will be given to you*[^2]. To this one comes from the faith which is contained in this Creed, that you may believe *in God the Father almighty*, invisible, immortal, the king of the ages, the Creator of things visible and invisible, and whatever else either sincere reason or the authority of the holy Scriptures worthily says of him. And do not separate the Son from this excellence of God. For these things are not so said of the Father as to be foreign to him who said: *I and the Father are one*[^3], and of whom the Apostle says: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^4]. For grasping is the usurpation of what is another's, whereas that equality is the nature of his own; and through this, how will the Son not be almighty, *through whom all things were made*[^5], since he is also *the power and wisdom of God*[^6], of which wisdom it is written that *being one, it can do all things*[^7]? But that nature too is invisible, in that very form in which it is equal to the Father. For by nature the Word of God is invisible, which *in the beginning was with God, and the Word was God*[^8]; in which nature it remains also altogether immortal, that is, in every way unchangeable. For the human soul too is in a certain manner called immortal, but there is no true immortality where there is so great a changeableness, through which it can both fail and advance; whence its death is to be estranged from the life of God through the ignorance that is in it[^9]; but its life is to run together to the fountain of life, that in the light of God it may see light[^10], according to which life you too, through the grace of Christ, come back to life from a certain death to which you renounce. But the Word of God, which is the only-begotten Son, lives always unchangeably with the Father; nor does it fail, because its continuance is not diminished, nor advance, because its perfection is not increased. He himself is also the king of the ages, the Creator of things visible and invisible; because, as the Apostle says: *In him were created all things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominations or principalities or powers: all things were created unto him and through him, and all things consist in him*[^11]. But because *he emptied himself*, not losing the form of God, but *taking the form of a servant*[^12], through this form of a servant the invisible was seen, because *he was born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*. In this form of a servant the almighty was made weak, because *he suffered under Pontius Pilate*; through this form of a servant the immortal died, because *he was crucified and buried*; through this form of a servant the king of the ages *rose on the third day*; through this form the Creator of things visible and invisible *ascended into heaven*, whence he never departed; through this form of a servant *he sits at the right hand of the Father*, who is the arm of the Father, of whom the Prophet says: *And the arm of the Lord, to whom has it been revealed?*[^13] in this form of a servant *he will come to judge the living and the dead*, in which he willed to be a partaker of the dead, since he is the life of the living. Through him the Holy Spirit was sent to us by the Father and by him. The Spirit of the Father and the Son, sent by both, begotten by neither, the unity of both, equal to both. This Trinity is one God, almighty, invisible, immortal, the king of the ages, the Creator of things visible and invisible. For we do not say three gods or three almighty or three creators or whatever else of the excellence of God can likewise be said, because there are not three gods but one God; although in this Trinity the Father is not the Son, and the Son is not the Father, and the Holy Spirit is neither Son nor Father, but he the Father of the Son, he the Son of the Father, he the Spirit of both the Father and the Son. Believe that you may understand, *for unless you believe, you will not understand*[^14]. From this faith hope for the grace in which *all sins will be forgiven you*. For by this *you will be saved — not of yourselves, but it is the gift of God — not of works, lest perhaps anyone be lifted up*[^15]. For you will be his *workmanship, created in Christ Jesus in good works which God prepared, that you may walk in them*[^16]; and, having put off the old man and *put on the new man*[^17], you may be a new creature, singing a new song[^18], about to receive an eternal inheritance through the New Covenant; whence also, after this death which has passed upon all men[^19], which is owed and rendered to the old age of the first man, hope also, at the end, for the *resurrection of your bodies*, not to the sufferings of pains, as the ungodly will rise, nor to the joys of fleshly desires, as fools think, but as the Apostle says: *An animal body is sown, a spiritual body will rise*[^20], where the body will be so subjected to the beatified spirit and so subject, with wondrous felicity, to every ease, that it will no longer *weigh down the soul*[^21] nor seek any refreshment, because it will suffer no failing, but will remain in eternal life, where to our spirit with the body that very eternity will be life.

**2.** This brief sermon, then, on the whole Creed, I have rendered to you as your due. When you have heard the whole Creed, you will recognize this sermon of ours briefly gathered together. Nor ought you in any way to write it, that you may hold the same words, but to learn it thoroughly by hearing, nor, when you have held it, to write it, but to hold it always in memory and recall it. For whatever you are going to hear in the Creed is already contained in the divine letters of the holy Scriptures, and you are wont to hear it all, piecemeal, where there was need. But that it is so collected and reduced into a certain form is not permitted to be written, that there may be a commemoration of the promise of God, where, foretelling the New Covenant through the Prophet, he said: *This is the Covenant which I will ordain for them after those days, says the Lord, giving my laws into their mind, and in their hearts I will write them*[^22]. For the sake of signifying this thing, the Creed, by being heard, is written not on tablets or in any other material, but in the hearts. He will grant it, who called you to his kingdom and glory, that, you being regenerated by his grace, it may be written by the Holy Spirit too in your hearts; that you may love what you believe, and faith may work in you through love; and so you may please the Lord God, the bestower of all goods, not by slavishly fearing punishment, but by freely loving righteousness. This, then, is the Creed which has already been intimated to you as catechumens through the Scriptures and the ecclesiastical sermons, but under this brief form it is to be confessed and advanced in by the faithful.
