# Sermon 126

**1.** The hidden and secret things of the kingdom of God first seek believers, whom they may make understanders. For faith is the step of understanding; but understanding is the reward of faith. The Prophet says this openly to all who seek understanding too hastily and out of order, and neglect faith. For he says: *Unless you believe, you will not understand* [^1]. So faith itself too has a certain light of its own in the Scriptures, in the Prophecy, in the Gospel, in the apostolic readings. For all these things which are recited to us for a time are lamps in a dark place, that we may be nourished toward the day. The apostle Peter says: *We have a more certain prophetic word, to which you do well to attend as to a lamp in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts* [^2].  
You see, then, brothers, how perverse and vicious by haste are those who, like immature conceptions, before birth seek an abortion; who say to us: Why do you bid me believe what I do not see. Let me see something, that I may believe. You bid me believe, while I do not see. I want to see, and by seeing to believe, not by hearing. Let the Prophet say: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*. You want to ascend, and you forget the step. Surely perversely. O man, if I could already show you what you might see, I would not exhort you to believe.

**2.** So: *Faith is*, as it is defined elsewhere, *the substance of those who hope, the conviction of things that are not seen* [^3]. If they are not seen, how are they convicted that they are? For whence are these things that you see, except from that which you do not see? Surely you see something, that you may believe something, and from what you see, you may believe what you do not see. Do not be ungrateful to him who made you see, from which you may be able to believe what you cannot yet see. God gave you eyes in the body, reason in the heart. Rouse the reason of the heart, raise up the inner dweller of your inner eyes, let it take up its windows, let it inspect the creation of God. For there is someone within who sees through the eyes. For when you think intently of something elsewhere, the inner dweller being turned away, you do not see the things that are before your eyes. For the windows open in vain, when he who attends through them is absent. So the eyes do not see, but someone sees through the eyes. Raise him up, rouse him. For he was not denied you. God made you a rational animal, set you over the beasts, formed you to his image [^4]. Should you so use your eyes as a beast, only to see what you may add to the belly, not to the mind? Raise up, then, the rational gaze, use your eyes as a man, attend to heaven and earth, the ornaments of heaven, the fruitfulness of earth, the flights of birds, the swimmings of fishes, the force of seeds, the order of the seasons. Attend to the things made, and seek the maker. Look at what you see, and seek what you do not see. Believe in him whom you do not see, on account of these things which you see. And lest you think yourself exhorted by my speech, hear the Apostle saying: *For the invisible things of God, from the creation of the world, through the things that are made, are understood and beheld* [^5].

**3.** You set these things aside, nor attended as a man, but as an irrational animal. The Prophet cried to you, and cried in vain: *Do not be like the horse and the mule, who have no understanding* [^6]. You saw these things, then, and set them aside. The daily miracles of God had grown cheap not by their ease, but by their constancy. For what is more difficult to know, than that a man is born who was not, that by dying he departs into hidden things who was, that by being born he comes forth into the open who was not? What so wonderful, what so difficult to know, but for God easy to do? Marvel at these things, awake. You know how to marvel at unusual things. Are they greater than what you are accustomed to see? Men marveled that our Lord God Jesus Christ fattened so many thousands from five loaves, and do not marvel that through few grains the lands are filled with crops [^7]. Water made wine, men saw, and were amazed [^8]. What else comes from rain through the root of the vine? He made those things, he made these. Those that you may be fed, these that you may marvel. But both are to be marveled at, because they are works of God. A man sees unusual things and marvels. Whence is the very man who marvels? Where was he? Whence did he come forth? Whence the form of the body? Whence the distinction of members? Whence this comely bearing? From what beginnings? From how contemptible ones? And he marvels at other things, when the marveler himself is a great miracle. Whence, then, are these things that you see, except from that which you do not see? But, as I had begun to say, because these things had grown cheap to you, he himself came to do unusual things, that even in the usual ones you might recognize your craftsman. He came to whom it was said: *Renew the signs* [^9], to whom it was said: *Make wonderful your mercies* [^10]. For he bestowed them. He bestowed them, and no one marveled. So he came small to the small, the physician came to the sick, who could come when he willed, return when he willed, do whatever he willed, judge as he willed. And what he wills, that very thing is justice. And what he wills, I say, that very thing is justice. For not unjust is what he wills, nor can what he does not will be just. He came to raise up a dead man, to give back, while men marveled, to the light a man who was in the light, who daily brings to the light those who were not.

**4.** He did these things, and was despised by many, attending more not to how great things he did, but to how small a one did them, as though saying within themselves: These things are divine, but this one is a man. Two things, then, you see, divine things and a man. If divine things cannot be done except by God, see lest in the man God lie hidden. Attend, I say, to what you see, believe what you do not see. He did not forsake you, who called you to believe. Although he bade you believe that which you cannot see, yet he did not leave you seeing nothing, from which you might be able to believe what you do not see. Are the very creatures small signs, small indications of the Creator? He came too, and did miracles. You could not see God, you could see a man. God was made man, that in one there might be for you both what you would see, and what you would believe. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^11]. You hear, and do not yet see. Behold, he comes, behold, he is born, behold, he takes on a body, behold, he comes forth from a woman, who made male and female. He who made male and female, was not made through male and female. For you were perhaps going to despise the one who was born, you do not despise how he was born, because he always was before he was born. Behold, I say, he took on a body; he was clothed with flesh, he came forth from the womb. Now do you see? Now, I say, do you see flesh? I ask, but I show the flesh. You see something, and do not see something. Behold, in the very birth, behold there are now two, both what you may see, and what you may not see. But that through that very thing which you see, you may believe what you do not see. You had begun to despise, because you see who was born. Believe what you do not see, because he was born of a virgin. How little, he says, is he who was born. But how great is he, who was born of a virgin! And he who was born of a virgin brought you a temporal miracle. He was not born of a father, namely of a human father, and was born of a mother. But let it not seem impossible to you, because he was born through a mother alone, who made man before father and mother.

**5.** So he brought you a temporal miracle, that you might seek and marvel at him, the eternal. For he himself who *like a bridegroom came forth from his chamber* [^12], namely from the virginal womb, where holy nuptials were made, the Word and the flesh, brought, I say, a temporal miracle, but he himself is eternal, he himself is coeternal with the Father, he himself is he who *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^13]. But this you could not see. He made for you that by which you might be healed, that you might be able to see what you did not see. What you despise in Christ is not yet the contemplation of the healed, but the medicine of the sick. Do not hasten to the vision of the healthy. The angels see, the angels rejoice, the angels are fed and live, nor does that from which they are fed fail, nor is their food diminished. In the lofty thrones, in the parts of the heavens, in those things which are above the heavens, the Word is seen by the angels, and is rejoiced in, and is eaten, and abides. But that man might eat the bread of angels [^14], the Lord of angels was made man. This is our salvation; the medicine of the weak, the food of the healthy.  
And he spoke to men, and said what you have heard: *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing* [^15]. Now, do we think, is there anyone who understands? Now, do we think, is there anyone in whom the eye-salve of the flesh makes progress toward gazing somehow on the splendor of the divinity? He spoke, let us too speak. He, because he is the Word; we, because we are from the Word. But why do we, somehow, speak of the Word? Because we are made to the likeness of the Word through the Word. As much, then, as we grasp, as much as we can be partakers of his ineffability, let us too speak, and let no one contradict us. For our faith has preceded, that we may say: *I believed, therefore I have spoken* [^16]. I speak, then, what I believe. Whether even what I somehow see, he sees more; this you cannot see. But when I have said it, he who sees what I say, whether he believes both that I see what I said, or does not believe it, what is that to me? Let him see it sincerely, and of me let him believe what he wills.

**6.** *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing* [^17]. Here the error of the Arians rises up, but rises up to fall, because it is not humbled to rise [^18]. What is it that moved you? You want to call the Son less. For you heard: *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. From this you want the Son to be called less. I know this, I know it. This moved you. Believe that he is not less. You cannot yet see, believe. This is what I was saying a little before. But how, you will say, shall I believe against his very words? He himself says: *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. Attend to what follows too: *For whatever the Father does, these the Son too does* [^19]. He did not say: "such things." Let Your Charity attend a little, lest you make a din for yourselves. There is need of a tranquil heart, of devout and dedicated faith, of religious intent, not on me the little vessel, but on him who sets the bread in the little vessel. Attend, then, a little. For in those things which we said above, exhorting to faith, that the mind imbued with faith might be capable of understanding, the things that were said sounded festive, glad, easy, gladdened your minds, you followed, you understood what I said. But what I am going to say, I do not indeed despair that some will understand, yet I do not hope that all will understand. And because God set before us through the gospel reading something from which to speak, nor can we avoid what the master set forth, perhaps I fear lest those who have not understood, who perhaps will be the more numerous, think that I spoke to them in vain. But yet on account of those who have understood I do not speak in vain. Let him who understands rejoice; let him bear patiently what he does not understand who does not understand; let him bear what he does not understand, and defer it until he understands.

**7.** He did not, then, say: Whatever the Father does, such things the Son does, as though the Father did some things, and the Son did other things. For it seemed as though he had said this, since above he said: *The Son does nothing of himself, except what he sees the Father doing* [^20]. Attend. Nor there did he say, except what he hears the Father commanding, but: *He sees*, he says, *the Father doing*. If, then, we question the carnal understanding, or rather sense, he set before himself, as it were, two craftsmen, the Father and the Son, the Father doing with nothing seen, the Son doing with the Father seen. It is still a carnal gaze. Yet that we may understand the things that are higher, let us not turn away from these lower and abject things. First let us set before our eyes something thus, let us suppose there are two carpenters, father and son. The father made a chest, which the son could not make, except he saw the father making it. He attended to the chest the father made, and made another chest like it, not the same one. I defer a little the following words, and now I question the Arian: Do you so understand it, as I set it forth? The Father made something, which when the Son saw the Father doing, he himself too made some such thing. For this the words seem to sound, by which you were moved. For he did not say: The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he hears the Father commanding; but he said: *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. Behold, if you so understand it, the Father made, and the Son attended, that he too might see what he should do, but should do some other such thing, such as the Father had made. This which the Father made, through whom did he make it? If not through the Son, if not through the Word, you have run into the blasphemy of the Gospel. For *all things were made through him* [^21]. So now what the Father had made, he had made through the Word. If he had made it through the Word, he had made it through the Son. Who is the other who attends, to make some other thing which he saw the Father doing? You are not wont to say that the Father has two sons. There is one only-begotten begotten of him. But through his mercy he is alone unto divinity, and not alone unto inheritance. The Father made coheirs to his only-begotten, not those whom he begot of his substance as he begot him, but those whom from his household he adopted through him. For we were called unto the adoption of sons, as holy Scripture testifies [^22].

**8.** What, then, do you say? The Only-begotten himself speaks, the only-begotten Son himself speaks in the Gospel, the Word itself made words for us, we heard him saying: *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. Now the Father has made it so that the Son sees what he should do, and yet the Father does nothing except through the Son. Surely you are troubled, heretic, surely you are troubled; but you are troubled as though hellebore has been taken, that you may be healed. Now you do not find yourself, your opinion and your carnal gaze, as I judge, you yourself condemn. Set the eyes of the flesh behind you, raise up, if you have anything in the heart, gaze on divine things. You hear indeed human words through a man, through the Evangelist. Through the Gospel you hear human words, as a man. But you hear of the Word of God, that you may hear human things, and know divine things. The master stirred up, that he might instruct; he sowed a question, that he might move attention. *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. It was consequent that he should say: For whatever the Father does, such things the Son does. He did not say this, but: *Whatever the Father does, these same things the Son too does* [^23]. The Father does not do some things, the Son other things, because all that the Father does, he does through the Son. The Son raised Lazarus [^24], did the Father not raise him? The Son enlightened the blind man [^25], did the Father not enlighten him? The Father enlightened through the Son in the Holy Spirit. It is a Trinity, but one working, one majesty, one eternity, one coeternity, and the same works of the Trinity. The Father does not create some men, the Son others, the Holy Spirit others. The Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit create one and the same man. And the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God, creates.

**9.** You attend to the plurality of persons, but acknowledge the unity of divinity. For on account of the plurality of persons it was said: *Let us make man to our image and likeness*. He did not say: I will make man, and attend when I make him, that you too may be able to make another. *Let us make*, he says. I hear plurality. *To our image*. Again I hear plurality. Where, then, is the singularity of divinity? Read what follows: *And God made man* [^26]. It is said: *Let us make man*, and it is not said: The gods made man. The unity is understood in that it was said: *God made man*.  
Where, then, is that carnal intent? Let it be confounded, hidden, destroyed. Let the Word of God speak to us. Now, devout, now believing, now imbued with faith and having acquired some merit of understanding, let us turn ourselves to the Word itself, to the fountain of light, and say together: Lord, the Father made the same things as you; because whatever the Father does, he does through you. We heard you the Word in the beginning: We did not see, but we believe. There consequently we heard, that all things were made through you [^27]. All things, then, that the Father does, he does through you. The same things, then, you do as the Father. What is it? Why did you want to say: *The Son cannot do anything of himself?* For I see a certain equality of you with the Father, in that I hear: *Whatever the Father does, these same things the Son does* [^28]. I acknowledge the equality, here I understand, here I grasp as I can: *I and the Father are one* [^29]. What is it: That you can do nothing, except what you see the Father doing? What is this?

**10.** Perhaps he says to me, rather let him say to all of us: This which I said: *The Son cannot do anything, except what he sees the Father doing*, how do you understand my seeing? What is my seeing? Set aside a little the form of servant, which he took on for your sake. For in that form of servant our Lord had eyes and ears in the flesh, and in that human form such as we too bear there was the same figure of body, the same lineaments of members. That flesh had come from Adam, but he was not Adam. So the Lord, walking either on earth or on sea, as it pleased him, as he willed, because whatever he willed he could, looked at what he willed. He cast his eyes, he saw. He turned away his eyes, and did not see. Behind was one who followed, before was one who would be seen. With the eyes of the body he saw what was before. But nothing lay hidden from his divinity. Set aside, set aside, I say, a little the form of servant. See the form of God, in which he was before the world was made, in which he was equal to the Father. Through him receive this and understand what he says to you: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God* [^30]. There see him, if you can, that you may be able to see what his seeing is. *In the beginning was the Word*. How does the Word see? Has the Word eyes, or do our eyes find themselves in him, and not carnal eyes, but the eyes of devout hearts? *For blessed are the pure of heart, for they will see God* [^31].  
You look at Christ, man and God. He shows you the man, he keeps for you God. And see that he keeps God for you, who showed himself man to you. *He who loves me*, he says, *keeps my commandments. He who loves me, will be loved by my Father, and I will love him*. And as though it were said: What will you give him whom you love? *And I will show*, he says, *myself to him* [^32]. What is this, brothers? The one whom they already saw, he himself promised that he would show himself to them. To whom? To those by whom he was seen, or to those by whom he was also not seen? So speaking he says to a certain Apostle, asking to see the Father so that it would suffice him, and saying: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*. And he, standing before the eyes of the servant, in the form of servant, keeping for deified eyes the form of God, said to him: *So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me? He who sees me, sees the Father too* [^33]. You seek to see the Father, see me. You see me, and do not see me. You see what I took on for you, you do not see what I kept for you. Hear the commandments, purge the eyes. Because: *He who loves me, keeps my commandments, and I will love him*. As though to one keeping my commandments, and as though to one healed by my commandments, *I will show myself to him*.

**11.** If, then, brothers, we cannot see what the seeing of the Word is, where do we go? What vision do we perhaps too hastily demand? What do we want shown to us that we cannot see? Therefore these things have been said which we desire to see, not that we can yet grasp. For the seeing of the Word, if you see it, perhaps in that which you see, the seeing of the Word, you will see the Word itself, so that the Word is not one thing, the seeing of the Word another, lest there be there something compounded and coupled and double and joined together. For it is something simple with ineffable simplicity. Not as a man, where the man is one thing, the seeing of the man another. For sometimes the seeing of the man is extinguished, and the man remains. This is what I was saying, that I would say something which not all could understand, though may the Lord too bring it about that some understand. My brothers, to this I exhort, that we may see the seeing of the Word. Is this beyond your strength, because they are small? Let them be nourished, let them be perfected. From what? From the commandments. What commandments? *He who loves me, keeps my commandments*. What commandments? For now we want to grow, now to be strengthened, now to be perfected, that we may see the seeing of the Word. Say now, Lord, what commandments? *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another* [^34]. This love, then, brothers, let us draw from the richness of the fountain, this let us grasp, in it let us be nourished. Grasp that by which you may be capable. Let love beget you, love nourish you, love perfect you, love strengthen you, that you may see the seeing of the Word, that the Word is not one thing, and his seeing another; but that the very thing which is the seeing of the Word, this is the Word. And quickly perhaps you will understand, that this which was said: *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*, is such, as if he said: He would not be the Son, unless he were born of the Father. Let it suffice, brothers. I know that I have said something which perhaps when thought is laid open, when said in many words is perhaps obscured.
