# Sermon 22/A

**1** Every soul that intently sings to God in the words of the psalm must then think of its own perils, and be touched by a kind of intimate affection, if perhaps it then suffers any tribulation from without or any difficulty within, and refer what it sings to what it suffers. We have responded: *O Lord, deliver me from the hand of the sinner, and from the hand of the criminal and the wrongdoer*[^1]. Perhaps each one of you, when he hears these words from the reader or speaks them himself, thinks of nothing but some enemy of his, or one who slanders him, or one who is preparing to throw him into prison, who perhaps draws up a false bond against him: this man he calls the sinner, this man the criminal, from this man he longs to be delivered. You see him singing, and singing with affection, even fitting his face to the words of the psalm, sometimes even wetting his cheeks with tears, sighing between the words of the song. One who does not know how to discern affections praises that man and says: "He listens to the reading with great affection. How he groans, how he sighs!" But that man is thinking of that enemy of his, from whom he dreads some slander, some violence, some fraud and circumvention; and with all his strength, with all his marrow, with voice, face, sighs he says: *O Lord, deliver me from the hand of the sinner, and from the hand of the criminal and the wrongdoer*[^2]. One who so sings sings as the old man, and, understanding carnally, tries, though he is old, to sing a new song. Let him sing as a new man, if he sings a new song. What is it to sing as a new man? Let him be renewed by the desire of the new life, let him crave something else, let him sigh to God for another thing's sake, let him be a lover of the kingdom of heaven. I will say it more briefly: let him be a lover of God, let him love God, let him love freely. For this too the devil wished to charge, and in this he wished to convict the holy man Job, as though he were the old man, that he might not be allowed to reach what is promised to the new man, when he said: *Does Job worship God for nothing?*[^3] If, then, he worships freely, he has overcome the devil; if he does not worship freely, he has been overcome by the devil. For the slanderer's charge would hold, if Job did not worship the Lord freely. "He worships you," he says, "because you have given him such great things." Therefore God permitted Job to be tempted, that he might both show him to be one by whom he knew he was worshipped freely, and set him forth for us to imitate. For he was known to God, but hidden from us. See, then, what befell the perversity of the devil: while he wishes to show one prevaricator, he made many imitators of the holy man. For he did not subject Job to himself, but showed him to us. What then? Whence was it understood that Job worshipped freely? Because he did not give thanks when he had things and blaspheme when he did not have them, but set the good pleasure of God before all his riches, as if to say: "I have the one who gave; why do I ask what he gave?"

**2** I will say, then, brothers: whoever worships the Lord for this reason, that he may have riches, may have the honors of the world, and asks these things of him, it is plain that he does not worship freely. He worships for a reward. If God does not give him these things, he is to be deserted. Whoever gives them is thought worthy of greater worship. And no one gives them but he. Yet if another had given them, the lovers of these things would have deserted God and fled to that other. Strive toward God; let God be for you the whole good, let God be for you the true good. When, then, all his things had been taken away, the devil, because he did not carry off the man who loved God freely, was laid low even by that man's own voice: *The Lord gave and the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done*[^4]. He did not say, "And what shall I do against the Lord? I will lament, because I cannot overcome him." *Blessed be*, he said, *the name of the Lord*[^5]. Only for his sons, whose sin he feared, did he tear his garments, praying surely with a father's affection for them. But what God took away he received willingly, because he embraced God himself more willingly.

**3** Someone stands up and says: "I do not worship God for the sake of riches, and he himself knows it, nor for the sake of fleeting and temporal honors." And for the sake of what, then? Let us refine out the man who worships freely, who loves freely. And for the sake of what? "I do not want riches," he says, "I want a sufficiency of things. Let nothing be lacking in my house, let me be safe with my wife, with my children. This is enough for me." Still you do not yet worship God freely. In Job himself he showed us this. For the devil, by God's permission indeed—it was God who had taken away the riches, not he—had cast them down, because Job so trod them underfoot that he depended on God. He took away his health too; God permitted this. And Job was shown to worship God so freely that he worshipped and loved him not even for the sake of his own body's health. And the devil did not strike him in one member. From head to foot he made him rotten. And this for his fuller subjection, that he might be triumphed over the more, that the one who had seduced the immortal Adam might be overcome by a rotting man. He struck him, then, with a grievous wound from head to foot, and, rotting with worms, he endures a tempting wife, the devil's helper left to him. She was that very Eve, but he was not that Adam. He insinuated the persuasion of blaspheming, that Job, having lost his patience, might lose the one he worshipped freely. But when would he lose the one to whom he so clung? For what did he answer to the woman saying such things? *You have spoken like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not endure the bad?*[^6] See one who worships freely, who worships not even for the sake of his own body's health, which we have in common with the beasts.

**4** There is, then, something else that God keeps for us. For its sake let him be worshipped, for its sake let him be loved. He keeps himself for those who love. He wishes to show his face to the cleansed, not to the eye of the flesh, but to the eye of the heart: for *blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^7]. Love, that you may see, for what you will see is not cheap, is not slight. You will see him who made whatever you love. And if these things are beautiful, what is he himself who made them? God does not want you to love the earth, God does not want you to love heaven—that is, the things you see—but himself whom you do not see. But you will not always not see him, if you do not always not love him. Love him absent, that you may enjoy him present. Desire him whom you may hold, whom you may embrace. Cling first by faith, afterward you will cling by sight. For now, as a pilgrim, you walk by faith and by hope. When you arrive, you will fully enjoy him whom as a pilgrim you loved. He himself founded the homeland to which you hasten to come. From there he sent letters to you, that you may not delay to return from your pilgrimage. If, then, you are making for that homeland where you may enjoy the founder of the homeland, you are now in the desert amid many temptations, and the enemy must be guarded against. Learn against whom you sing: *O God, deliver me from the hand of the sinner, and from the hand of the criminal and the wrongdoer*[^8]. The sinner is the devil, brothers, the devil is the criminal, the wrongdoer. From his hand choose to be delivered, that, the journey accomplished, beside which he dares to lie in wait, you may come to the homeland into which he cannot be admitted.

**5** Hear that the devil is a sinner. It is written: *From the beginning the devil sins*[^9]. And who does not know that he is a criminal and a wrongdoer? What is so criminal as a murderer? Who first killed a man, except the one who deceived Adam? And he himself is a wrongdoer, who acts against justice, because he never stood in the truth. Against this criminal and wrongdoer it is sung: *O Lord, deliver me from the hand of the criminal and the wrongdoer*[^10]. Do not sing it against your neighbor who slanders you, do not sing it against the powerful man who moved your boundary stone, do not sing it against the very plotter who is preparing to kill you. For all these are men who plot against you. They are flesh, they are blood, they pass away. Hear the Apostle saying: *Your struggle is not against flesh and blood*[^11]. Against whom, then, is the struggle? Against whom must it be said: *Deliver me, O Lord, from the hand of the sinner, and from the hand of the criminal and the wrongdoer*[^12]? *Your struggle is not*, it says, *against flesh and blood, but against the principalities and powers and rulers of this world of darkness, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places*[^13]. By avoiding a man you guard yourself; by praying you guard against the devil. For a visible enemy is avoided, so that where he is present, you are not there; for you avoid the one you see. How will you avoid the one you do not see? By praying. Pray against him: your weapons are those of prayer. You keep silent, and so he lies in wait; you pray, and he burns. But pray out of the affection by which you love freely.
