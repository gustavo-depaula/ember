# Sermon 113

**1.** What we are admonished, we ought to admonish. The recent gospel reading admonished us to make friends from the mammon of iniquity, that those who make them may themselves be received by them into eternal dwellings. Who are they who will have eternal dwellings, except the holy ones of God? And who are they who are to be received by them into eternal dwellings, except those who serve their need, and cheerfully supply what they require! Let us recall, then, that at the last judgment the Lord will say to those who will stand at his right hand: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat* [^1]: and the rest that you know. And when they asked when they had shown him these services, he answered: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me* [^2]. These least are those who receive into eternal dwellings. This he said to those on the right, because they did it: this he said to those on the left, because they refused to do it. But those on the right who did it, what did they receive, or rather what are they to receive? *Come*, he says, *blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat. When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me* [^3]. Who, then, are the least of Christ? They are those who left all their own, and followed him, and whatever they had they distributed to the poor; that they might serve God unencumbered, without the worldly fetter, and lift up their shoulders, freed from the burdens of the world, on high, as though winged. These are the least. Why least? Because humble, because not lifted up, not proud. Weigh these least, and you will find a heavy weight.

**2.** But what is it that he says, that they are to be made friends from the mammon of iniquity? What is the mammon of iniquity? First, what is mammon? For it is a word that is not Latin. It is a Hebrew word, akin to the Punic tongue. For these tongues are associated with each other by a certain nearness of meaning. What the Punic speakers call "mammon," in Latin is called "gain." What the Hebrews call mammon, in Latin is called riches. So that we may say the whole in Latin, our Lord Jesus Christ says this: *Make friends for yourselves from the riches of iniquity* [^4]. Some, by ill understanding this, seize the goods of others, and bestow something of them on the poor, and think they do what is commanded. For they say: To seize the goods of others is the mammon of iniquity; to dole out something of it, especially to needy saints, this is to make friends from the mammon of iniquity. This understanding must be corrected, rather utterly blotted out from the tablets of your heart. I do not want you to understand it so. From just labors make alms: from what you rightly have, give. For you are not going to bribe the judge Christ, that he may not hear you along with the poor, from whom you take. For if you despoiled someone weak, you the stronger and more powerful, and he came with you to any man in this land as judge, having some power to judge, and wished to plead his cause with you, if you gave the judge something from that plunder and spoliation of the needy man, that he might judge for you, would that judge even please you? To be sure, he judged for you, and yet so great is the force of justice, that he displeases even you. Do not paint yourself such a God, do not set in the temple of your heart such an idol. Your God is not such as you yourself ought not to be. If you do not judge thus, but judge justly; even so your God is better than you: he is not inferior; he is more just, he is the fountain of justice. Whatever good you have done, from there you took it; and whatever good you belched forth, from there you drank. Do you praise the vessel, because it has something from it, and find fault with the fountain? Do not want to make alms from usury and interest. To the faithful I speak, to those to whom we dole out the Body of Christ I speak. Fear, correct yourselves: lest I say afterward, You do it and you do it. And I think, that if I do, you ought not to be angry at me, but at yourselves, that you may be corrected. For to this avails what was said in the Psalm: *Be angry, and do not sin* [^5]. I want you to be angry, but so as not to sin. But that you may not sin, at whom have you to be angry, except yourselves? For what is a penitent man, except a man angry at himself? That he may receive pardon, he exacts a penalty from himself; and he rightly says to God: *Turn your eyes from my sins, for I acknowledge my sin* [^6]. If you acknowledge, he too pardons. You who used to do it, do not do it: it is not lawful.

**3.** But now if you have done it, and have such monies, and have filled your purses from it, laid up treasure from it: from evil is what you have, now do not add evil, and make friends for yourselves from the mammon of iniquity. Did Zacchaeus have from good? Read and see. He was a chief of the publicans, that is, one to whom the public taxes were brought: from there he had his riches. He had oppressed many, taken from many, heaped up much. Christ entered his house, and salvation came upon his house: for so the Lord himself says: *Today salvation has been made to this house* [^7]. But see that very salvation. First he desired to see the Lord, because he was small in stature: but the crowd hindering, he climbed into a sycamore tree, and saw him passing. But he looked at him, and said: *Zacchaeus, come down; I must stay with you* [^8]. You hang, but I do not hang you up, that is, I do not put you off. You wanted to see me passing, today you will find me dwelling with you. The Lord entered to him: filled with joy he said: *Half my goods I give to the poor* [^9]. Behold how he runs, who runs to make friends from the mammon of iniquity. And lest he be held guilty from elsewhere: *If I have taken anything from anyone*, he said, *I will restore fourfold* [^10]. He inflicted a condemnation on himself, lest he run into damnation. So you who have from evil, make good from it. You who do not have from evil, do not acquire from evil. Be good, you who make good from evil: and when you have begun to do some good from evil, do not yourself remain evil. Your coins are turned into good, and do you remain evil?

**4.** There is indeed another understanding too; and I will not keep silent about it. The mammon of iniquity is all the riches of the world, from wherever they are. For from wherever they are gathered, it is the mammon of iniquity, that is, they are the riches of iniquity. What is, they are the riches of iniquity? It is money, which iniquity calls by the name of riches. For if you seek true riches, there are others. With such riches naked Job abounded, when he had a heart full toward God, and brought forth praises to God, all his things lost, like most precious gems [^11]. From what treasure, if he had nothing? These are the true riches. But these are called riches by iniquity. You have them, I do not blame it: an inheritance came, your father was rich, and left them to you. You acquired them honestly: from just labors you have a full house, I do not blame it. Yet even so do not call them riches. For if you call them riches, you will love them: and if you love them, you will perish with them. Lose them, lest you perish: give, that you may acquire: sow, that you may reap. Do not call these riches; because they are not true. They are full of poverty, and always subject to mishaps. What kind of riches are they, for which you fear a thief, for which you fear your servant, lest, having killed you, he carry them off and flee? If they were true riches, they would give you security.

**5.** Therefore those are the true riches, which, when we have them, we cannot lose. And lest perhaps on account of them you fear a thief, they will be there where none can carry them off. Hear your Lord: *Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where the thief does not approach* [^12]. Then they will be riches, when you have made them migrate. As long as they are on earth, they are not riches. But the world calls them riches, iniquity calls them so. God therefore the mammon of iniquity, because iniquity calls those things riches. Hear the Psalm: *Lord, free me from the hand of the sons of strangers, whose mouth has spoken vanity; and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity. Whose sons are like new plants established from their youth. Their daughters arrayed, adorned round about like the likeness of a temple. Their storerooms full, overflowing from this into that. Their oxen fat, their sheep fruitful, multiplied in their goings. There is no breaking down of the wall, nor passage, nor outcry in their streets* [^13]. You have seen the Psalm, what kind of happiness it described: but hear what it is, those it set forth as sons of iniquity. *Whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity*. These it set forth, and said their happiness only upon earth. And what did it add? *They called blessed the people that has these things* [^14]. But who said it? The sons of strangers, foreigners, and not belonging to the seed of Abraham: they *called blessed the people that has these things*. Who said it? *Whose mouth has spoken vanity*. Therefore it is vain to say that those are blessed who have these things. And yet it is said by those whose mouth has spoken vanity. By them are called those riches, which are called the mammon of iniquity.  
But you, what do you say? Because those sons of strangers, because those whose mouth has spoken vanity, called blessed the people that has these things; you, what do you say? These riches are false, give me true ones. You blame these, show what you praise. You want me to despise this, show what I should prefer. Let the Psalm itself say. For he who said: *They called blessed the people that has these things*; gives us such an answer, as though we said to him, that is, to the Psalm itself, Behold, you have taken these from us, and given us nothing: behold, we despise these, these; from what shall we live? from what shall we be blessed? Because those who spoke, they themselves will receive from themselves. For they said that men having riches are blessed. You, what do you say?

**6.** He answers as though so questioned, and says: They call the rich blessed: I say: *Blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself* [^15]. You have heard the true riches, make friends from the mammon of iniquity, and you will be the blessed people, whose God is the Lord himself. Sometimes we pass along the way, and see most pleasant and rich estates, and say: Whose is that estate? It is asserted to be so-and-so's: and we say, Blessed man: we speak vanity. Blessed he whose is that house, blessed he whose is that estate, blessed he whose is that herd, blessed he whose is that servant, blessed he whose is that household. Take away vanity, if you want to hear the truth. *Blessed is he whose God is the Lord himself*. For not he whose is this estate is blessed: but he whose is that God. But that you may announce most plainly the blessedness of things, you say that your estate has made you blessed. Why? Because from it you live. For when you praise as a great thing your estate, you say this: It feeds me, from it I live. Attend to that from which you live. He is the one from whom you live, to whom you say: *With you is the fountain of life* [^16]. *Blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself*. O Lord my God, O Lord our God, that we may come to you, make us blessed in you. We do not want it from gold, nor from silver, nor from estates: we do not want it from these earthly and most vain things, the passing things of a perishable life. Let our mouth not speak vanity. Make us blessed in you, because we shall not lose you. When we have held you, we shall neither lose you, nor shall we perish. Make us blessed in you, because *blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself*. Nor is he angry if we say of God, Our estate. For we read that *the Lord is the portion of my inheritance* [^17]. A great thing, brothers, both we are his inheritance, and he is our inheritance: because both we cultivate him, and he cultivates us. There is no injury to him that he cultivates us. For if we cultivate him as our God, he cultivates us as his field. And that you may know that he cultivates us, hear the one whom he sent us: *I am*, he says, *the vine, you are the branches, my Father is the husbandman* [^18]. Therefore he cultivates us. But if we render fruit, he prepares the granary. But if under so great a cultivator we want to be barren, and bring forth thorns instead of wheat: I do not want to say what follows, let us end with joy. Turned toward the Lord, and so on.
