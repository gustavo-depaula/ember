# Sermon 350/C

**1.** O man, fragile in body, changeable in time, dissolved by death, pressed by burdens, wearied by anxieties, hear my counsel: Relieve yourself by giving to the poor what you have acquired by your labors; give something to him who has not, because you too do not have something, for you do not have eternal life; give, then, from what you have, that you may receive what you do not have. The beggar knocks at your door; you knock at the door of your Lord. God does with his beggar what you do with yours; give, then, and it will be given to you. If you are unwilling, you must see to it. For the poor man cries out and says to you: "I ask for bread and you do not give; you ask for life and do not receive; let us see which of us labors at the greater loss, I who am cheated of a morsel, or you who are deprived of eternal life; I who am chastised in the belly, or you who are chastised in the mind; finally, I who burn with hunger, or you who are to be burned and consigned to fire and living flames?" To these words of the poor man, I know not whether the pride of the rich man will be able to answer. *To everyone*, says the Lord, *who asks you, give*[^1]. If to everyone, how much more to the needy and the wretched, whose leanness and pallor beg, whose tongue is silent, whose squalor and groaning ask for alms.

**2.** Hear me, then, O rich man, and may my counsel please you: redeem your sins with almsgiving, do not brood over gold; naked you came forth from your mother's womb, naked you will return to the earth[^2]; and if naked you will return to the earth, for whom do you gather upon the earth? I believe that, if you could carry something with you, you would have devoured living men. Behold, you will go forth naked; why do you not bestow money, whether well or ill gathered? Send ahead to where you are going, make transfers of perishing things, that you may come to the kingdom of heaven. For if you gave some man ten coins, for which he would afterward repay you a hundred, how you would rejoice, with what gladness of mind you would exult! If, then, you rejoice in interest, lend to your Lord, bestow on your God from his own; for with multiplied interest he will repay you. Do you wish to know how manifoldly he will repay you? For a morsel, for a coin, for a tunic, you receive eternal life, the kingdom of heaven, blessedness without end. Weigh against your morsel eternal life, everlasting riches; there is no comparison; for you give the earth and receive him who made heaven and earth. For he himself is our reward, without whom the rich man begs and with whom the poor man is most abundantly rich. For what does the rich man have, if he does not have God? What does the poor man not have, if he has God?

[^1]: Luke 6:30
[^2]: See Job 1:21
