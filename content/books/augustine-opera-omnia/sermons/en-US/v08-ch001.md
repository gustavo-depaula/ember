# Sermon 273: On the Feast Day of the Martyrs Fructuosus the Bishop and the Deacons Augurius and Eulogius

**1.** The Lord Jesus not only instructed his martyrs by precept but also strengthened them by example. For so that those who were to suffer might have something to follow, he himself suffered first on their behalf: he showed the way and made the road. Death belongs either to the soul or to the body. But the soul both cannot die and can die: it cannot die, because its capacity for awareness never perishes; and yet it can die, if it loses God. For just as the soul is itself the life of its own body, so God is the life of the soul itself. Therefore, just as the body dies when the soul, which is its life, leaves it, so the soul dies when God leaves it. But so that God may not leave the soul, let it always remain in faith, so that it does not fear to die for God; and then it does not die, abandoned by God. It remains, then, that the death which is feared is to be feared for the body. But even on that score the Lord Christ made his martyrs secure. For how could they be uncertain about the wholeness of their limbs, when they had received assurance about the number of their hairs? *The very hairs of your head*, he says, *are numbered*[^1]. And in another place he says still more plainly: *For I tell you that not a hair of your head will perish*[^2]. The Truth speaks—and does weakness tremble?

**2.** Blessed are the saints, in whose memorials we celebrate the day of their suffering: they received, in exchange for a temporal welfare, an eternal crown and immortality without end; to us they left, in these solemnities, an exhortation. When we hear how the martyrs suffered, we rejoice and glorify God in them; nor do we grieve that they died. For if they had not died for Christ, would they be alive even today? Why should their confession not accomplish what sickness was going to accomplish anyway? You heard the questions of the persecutors, you heard the answers of those who confessed, when the saints' passion was read. Among the rest, what was that saying of the blessed bishop Fructuosus? When someone said to him, asking that he keep him in mind and pray for him, he answered: I must pray for the catholic Church, spread abroad from the east to the west. For who prays for individuals? Yet no individual is passed over by one who prays for all. No member is overlooked by him whose prayer is poured out for the whole body. What, then, do you think he was advising the man who asked him to pray for him? What do you suppose? Surely you understand. You are reminded by us. The man was asking him to pray for him. And I, he says, pray for the catholic Church, spread abroad from the east to the west. If you want me to pray for you, do not depart from her for whom I pray.

**3.** And what too of that saying of the holy deacon who suffered and was crowned with his bishop? The judge said to him: Do you also worship Fructuosus? And he replied: I do not worship Fructuosus, but I worship God, whom Fructuosus also worships. By this he warned us to honor the martyrs and, together with the martyrs, to worship God. For we ought not to be such as we grieve to see the pagans are. They indeed worship dead men. For all those whose names you hear, to whom temples were built, were men; and in human affairs most of them, indeed almost all, held royal power. You hear of Jupiter, you hear of Hercules, you hear of Neptune, you hear of Pluto, Mercury, Liber, and the rest: they were men. This is declared not only in the fables of the poets but also in the history of the nations. Those who have read know it; those who have not read should believe those who have. Those men, then, by certain temporal benefits won human affairs over to themselves, and they came to be worshiped by empty men chasing after empty things, so that they were called gods and held to be gods; so that temples were built to them as gods, supplication was made to them as gods, altars were raised to them as gods, priests were ordained to them as gods, and victims were sacrificed to them as gods.

**4.** But the true God alone ought to have a temple, and sacrifice ought to be offered to the true God alone. These things, then, which are owed rightly and wholly to the one true God, the wretched and deceived were rendering to many false gods. From this a perverse error besieged human wretchedness; from this the devil lay brooding over the prostrate minds of all. But when the grace of the Savior and the mercy of God at last looked upon the unworthy, there was fulfilled what was foretold by the prophet in the *Song of Songs*: *Arise, O north wind; and come, O south wind; and blow through my garden, and the spices shall flow forth*[^3]. It is as if it were said: *Arise, O north wind.* For the northern region of the world is cold. Under the devil, as under the north wind, souls grew cold, and, having lost the warmth of love, they froze. But what is said to him? *Arise, O north wind.* It is enough that you have brooded, enough that you have possessed, enough that you have lain over the prostrate: *Arise. Come, O south wind,* the wind from the region of light and warmth: *and blow through my garden, and the spices shall flow forth.* These spices were read of just a little while ago.

**5.** What are these spices? Of them the Lord's own bride says: *We will run after the fragrance of your ointments*[^4]. Mindful of that fragrance, the apostle Paul says: *We are the good fragrance of Christ in every place, both among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing.* A great mystery: *We are the good fragrance of Christ in every place, both among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing. To some indeed a fragrance of life unto life, to others a fragrance of death unto death. And who is sufficient* to understand *these things*?[^5] How does a good fragrance quicken some and kill others? A good fragrance, not a bad one. For he does not say: A good fragrance quickens the good, and a bad fragrance kills the bad. He does not say: To some we are a good fragrance unto life, to others a bad fragrance unto death. He does not say this; but: *We are the good fragrance of Christ in every place.* Woe to the wretched whom a good fragrance kills. If, then, you are a good fragrance, O Paul, why does this fragrance kill some and quicken others? That it quickens some, I hear, I understand; that it kills others, I grasp with difficulty—especially because you said: *And who is sufficient for these things?* It is no wonder that we are not sufficient. May he make us sufficient, whose fragrance it was of which we speak. For the Apostle answers me at once: Understand: *We are the good fragrance of Christ in every place, both among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing.* We are nonetheless a good fragrance, *to some a fragrance of life unto life, to others a fragrance of death unto death.* This fragrance quickens those who love, it kills those who envy. For if there were no splendor of the saints, the envy of the wicked would not arise. The fragrance of the saints began to suffer persecution: but, like flasks of ointment, the more they were broken, the more widely the fragrance was spread abroad.

**6.** Blessed are those whose passion has been recited. Blessed is holy Agnes, whose day of suffering is today. A virgin who was what she was called. In Latin Agnes means "lamb"; in Greek, "chaste." She was what she was called: deservedly was she crowned. What then, my brothers, what shall I say to you about those men whom the pagans worshiped as gods, to whom they rendered temples, priesthoods, altars, and sacrifices? What shall I say to you? That they are not to be compared with our martyrs? Even this very thing is an insult, that I so much as say it. To whatever feeble believers there may be, of whatever sort, even though they are still carnal and to be nourished with milk and not solid food, God forbid that those sacrilegious men be compared with them. Against one little faithful Christian old woman, what is Juno worth? Against one weak old Christian man trembling in all his limbs, what is Hercules worth? Hercules conquered Cacus, Hercules conquered the lion, Hercules conquered the dog Cerberus: Fructuosus conquered the whole world. Compare man with man. Agnes, a girl of thirteen, conquered the devil. That girl conquered him who, through Hercules, deceived many.

**7.** And yet, dearly beloved, we do not regard our martyrs, with whom those men are in no respect to be compared, as gods, nor do we worship them as gods. We do not render them temples, nor altars, nor sacrifices. Priests do not make offerings to them—God forbid. They are offered to God. Indeed, these things are offered to God, by whom all things are bestowed on us. Even when we make an offering at the memorials of the holy martyrs, do we not offer to God? The holy martyrs have an honorable place. Take note: in the recitation at the altar of Christ they are recited in a better place; yet they are not adored in Christ's stead. When have you heard it said at the memorial of Saint Theogenes, by me, or by any brother and colleague of mine, or by any presbyter: I offer to you, Saint Theogenes? Or: I offer to you, Peter? Or: I offer to you, Paul? You have never heard it. It is not done: it is not permitted. And if it be said to you, Do you then worship Peter? answer what Eulogius answered about Fructuosus: I do not worship Peter, but I worship God, whom Peter also worships. Then Peter loves you. For if you wish to regard Peter as God, you strike against the rock, and take care lest you break your foot by striking against the rock.

**8.** That you may know that what I say is true, listen—I admonish you. In the *Acts of the Apostles*, when the apostle Paul had worked a great miracle in Lycaonia, the citizens of that region or province thought that gods had come down to men, and they believed Barnabas to be Jupiter and Paul to be Mercury, because he was the readier in speech. Believing this, they brought garlands and victims and wished to offer sacrifice to them. They at once, not mocking but horrified, immediately tore their garments and said to them: *Brothers, what are you doing? We also are mortal men like you: but we proclaim to you the true God. Turn away from these empty things*[^6]. You see how the saints shuddered at being worshiped as gods. Likewise the blessed evangelist John, who wrote the Apocalypse, when he was astonished at the marvelous things shown to him, in terror fell at a certain place at the feet of the angel by whom all things were being shown to him. And the angel, to whom no man is to be compared, said to him: *Rise, what are you doing? Adore God. For I am your fellow servant, and of your brothers*[^7]. The martyrs hate your wine jars, the martyrs hate your frying pans, the martyrs hate your drunken revels. I say this without offense to those who are not such: let those take it to themselves who do such things. The martyrs hate these things, they do not love those who do them. But much more do they hate it if they are worshiped.

**9.** Therefore, dearly beloved, exult on the days of the holy martyrs: pray that you may follow the footsteps of the martyrs. For it is not that you are men and they were not men; it is not that you were born and they were born elsewhere; it is not that they bore flesh of a different kind than the flesh you bear. We are all from Adam, and we all strive to be in Christ. Our Lord himself, the very head of the Church, the only-begotten Son of God, the Word of the Father through whom all things were made, had flesh of no other kind than ours. Therefore he willed to take up manhood from a virgin, to be born in flesh from one of the human race. For if he had made himself a body from some other source, who would believe that he bore the flesh that we also bear? Yet he bore flesh in the likeness of sinful flesh, while we bear sinful flesh. For he was born not from the seed of a man, nor from the desire of male and female: but from what? From the message of the Father. And yet, though he was wondrously born, he deigned to be born mortal, and to die for us, and to redeem us by his blood, insofar as he is man. See what I am saying, brothers: Christ himself, though he is God, though he is with the Father one God, though he is the Word of the Father, the only-begotten, equal to the Father and coeternal—yet insofar as he deigned to be man, he chose rather to be called priest than to demand a priest for himself; he chose rather to be a sacrifice than to require one; insofar as he is man. For insofar as he is God, all that is owed to the Father is owed also to the only-begotten Son. Therefore, dearly beloved, venerate the martyrs, praise them, love them, proclaim them, honor them: worship the God of the martyrs. Turning to the Lord, and so on.
