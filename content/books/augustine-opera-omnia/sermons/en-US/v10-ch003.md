# Sermon 341/A: Here Begins on the Humility of Our Lord Jesus Christ

**1.** We commend to you, most beloved brothers, the humility of our Lord Jesus Christ—rather, he himself commends it to us all. See how great a humility. The prophet Isaiah cries out: *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of the flesh is as the flower of the grass; the grass has withered, the flower has fallen, but the Word of the Lord abides forever*[^1]. How he despised and cast down the flesh! How he set above it and praised the Word of God! Again I say, again attend: behold the casting-down of the flesh: *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of the flesh is as the flower of the grass*. What is *grass*? What is *the flower of the grass*? It follows and says. Do you wish to hear what grass is? *The grass has withered, the flower has fallen*. What of the Word of God? *It abides forever*. Let us recognize the Word, which *abides forever*; let us hear the Evangelist praising the Word. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; the same was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made. What was made, in him is life; and the life was the light of men*[^2]. A great praise, as of the eternal Word; a lofty praise, as of the Word of God abiding forever. And what does the Evangelist say afterward? *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^3]. If the Word, God, did only this, that he became flesh, the humility would be incredible; and blessed are they who believe this incredible thing: for our faith consists of incredible things. The Word of God was made grass, the dead one rose again, God was crucified—they are incredible; because your sickness had become great, so that you might be healed by means of incredible things. For that humble physician came, found the sick man lying down, shared his weakness with him, calling him to his divinity; he was made, amid sufferings, the slayer of sufferings, and dying he was hung upon the wood, that he might slay death. He made for us a food which we might receive and be healed. From where is this food, and whom does it fatten? Those who have imitated the humility of the Lord. Will you not imitate even his humility? How much more his divinity? Imitate his humility, if you can. When? From where? He, God, was made man; you, a man, recognize that you are a man. Would that you recognized what he himself was made for your sake! Recognize yourself for his sake; may you see that you are a man, and yet that you are worth so much that for your sake God was made man. Do not refer this to your pride, but to his mercy; for the Lord our God has redeemed us with his blood, and he willed the price of our souls to be his blood, innocent blood.

**2.** And, as I had begun to say, brothers, if God humbled himself so much only as to become a man, who would demand more of him? For you are not humbled to this, that from a man you should be made a beast; and yet what likeness is there? If you were humbled to this, that from a man you should become a beast, you would not be humbled by so great an interval as the interval by which God was humbled. For a man made a beast would have become irrational, having been something rational, yet mortal and mortal; for a man is mortal, and a beast is mortal; a man is born as a beast is born; a man is conceived as a beast is conceived; a man is fed with bodily nourishment and grows as a beast does. How much he has in common with a beast! He has one disparate thing alone, the reason of the mind, where the image of the Creator is set. But God, who was made man, the eternal was made mortal, clothed himself with flesh from the stock of our race without sin, was made man, was born, taking up that in which he might suffer for us. But behold, he has not yet suffered: now look at what he has been made for your sake, before he suffers. Is that a small humility? God was made man. O man, see that you are a man. For your sake God is man: and you do not wish to recognize that you are a man? Let us attend, brothers, to those who do not wish to recognize that they are men. Who are those who do not wish to recognize that they are men? Those who justify themselves and blame God. Let a man suffer something hard or harsh in this life; he has nothing so ready on his tongue as to reproach God and praise himself; and crying out in the indignation of his tribulation, he does not confess his sins, but boasts of his merits, and says: God, what have I done to you? Why do I suffer these things? God, what have I done to you? says man to God. Let God answer him: You say well, "What have I done to you?" because to me you have done nothing, but everything to yourself. For if you did anything to God, you would do what would delight God: that is to do something to him. But now, whatever you have done, you have done to yourself, because, following your own will, you have despised his command. Plainly, if you understand it so, you say rightly. For what can you do to God, that you should cry out, "What have I done to you?" He who throws a stone into the sky, does he throw it at the sky, or at himself? What you have sent up, and it did not stick there, has returned upon you; so too, if you throw all your blasphemies at God, so all your insults, so whatever things move your sacrilegious and impious and proud mind, the higher you throw them, the heavier the weight with which they fall back upon you.

**3.** What, then, were you going to do to God? But you would be doing something to him if you did his word; if you did what he had commanded, you would rightly cry out: What have I done to you? And yet examine your justice, search your conscience, enter your heart, do not cry out outside, look within, return to the inner chambers of your heart. See whether you have truly done nothing evil; see whether you are suffering anything worthy of what you have done, set in whatever tribulation; for nothing is owed to the sinner but the scourge of burning and eternal fire. You abandoned your God, you followed your desires. What do you suffer, when you are scourged? It is correction, not condemnation. If God scourges you in this life, he is not angry with you. Do not offend him while he scourges, do not provoke him to spare you. You provoke him by murmuring, and he lets you go. Flee under the scourge of the one correcting you; flee not from the scourge, but under the scourge; where he strikes, run there. He indeed knows where he may strike and where he may find you; and in vain do you wish to hide yourself from the eyes of him who is everywhere. Do you wish to flee from an angry God? Flee to a God appeased; from him nowhere except to him. You thought you were fleeing from him, when you raised your proud neck; humble it, and flee to him. *He scourges every son whom he receives*[^4]. But do you scorn to be scourged? Then scorn to be made an heir. To the inheritance the good Father trains you: good when he spares, good when he strikes, truly everywhere merciful.

[^1]: Isa 40:6-8
[^2]: John 1:1-4
[^3]: John 1:14
[^4]: Heb 12:6
