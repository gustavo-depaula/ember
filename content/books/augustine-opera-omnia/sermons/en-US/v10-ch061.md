# Sermon 375/C: A Tractate for the Fifth Day of Holy Pasch

**1.** Today's reading of the holy Gospel has again manifested the Lord to his servants, Christ to the Apostles, and convicts an unbelieving disciple. For one of the twelve disciples, the apostle Thomas, gave his assent to the resurrection of the Lord Christ neither when the women nor even when the men announced it. And surely he was to be sent with the preaching of the Gospel. When, then, he afterward began to preach Christ, how could he want to be believed about what he himself had not believed? I think he was ashamed of himself when he rebuked unbelievers. His fellow disciples, indeed his fellow apostles, say to him: *We have seen the Lord*. And he himself answered: *Unless I put my hands into his side, and touch the place of the nails, I will not believe*[^1]. He wanted assurance to be given him by touching. For if the Lord had come in order to be touched, how then in that reading which is read above does he say to Mary: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^2]? To the believing woman he says: *Do not touch me*; to the unbelieving man he says: Touch. For Mary had already come to the tomb, and at first thought the Lord, standing, to be the gardener, and had said: *Lord, if you have taken him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him*[^3]. The Lord, however, answered her with her own name: *Mary*. At once she recognized the Lord in the voice of her own name: he called, and she recognized; he made her happy by calling, by giving recognition. At once, then, when she heard her name from that authority and in the tongue with which he was accustomed, she too answers what she was accustomed: *Rabboni*[^4]. Now, then, Mary had believed; and the Lord says to her: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*.

**2.** But now, in that reading which sounded most recently in your ears, what did you hear Thomas saying? I do not believe, unless I touch. And the Lord to Thomas himself: Come, touch, *put your hands into my side, and do not be unbelieving but believing*[^5]. If you think it too little, he says, what I offer to your eyes, I offer myself to your hands as well. For perhaps you are of those who sing in the Psalm: *In the day of my tribulation I sought the Lord with my hands by night before him*[^6]. Why did he seek with his hands? Because he sought by night. What is, he sought by night? He carried the darkness of unbelief in his heart. And this came about not only for his own sake, but also for the sake of those who were going to deny the true flesh of the Lord. For Christ could have healed the wounds of his flesh in such a way that not even the traces of the scars would appear; he could, then, not have borne the traces of the nails in his limbs, not borne the trace of the wound in his side; but those scars were permitted to be in his flesh, so that in the hearts of men the wound of faithlessness might be taken away, and the marks of the wounds might bring about true wounds. For he who permitted the marks of the nails and of the lance to be in his body knew that there would one day be heretics so impious and perverse as to say that our Lord Jesus Christ both feigned his flesh and spoke lying words to his disciples and to our evangelists, when he said: Touch and see. Behold, Thomas doubts. How does he doubt? Unless I touch, I do not believe: he keeps faith beyond what is for touch. *Unless I touch*, he says, *I will not believe*. What, do we suppose, does the Manichaean say? Both Thomas saw, and Thomas touched, and Thomas felt the place of the nails, and the flesh was false. So then, if he had been there, he would not have believed even by touching.

**3.** Let your love, then, attend to the horrendous ruin, the detestable falsehood, the incredible impiety. See how great a distance there is between holy Mary Magdalene, who, when she had heard, *Mary*, at the single voice of Christ believed that he had risen. Between her faith, then, and the faith of those who, as soon as they saw Christ in the breaking of bread, at once believed—what do we suppose is the difference? What? She saw as it were in semi-darkness, they saw most openly; yet both they and she believed by seeing. Afterward he appeared to all: *They thought they saw a spirit*[^7]. He took from them their empty opinion, and implanted the most certain truth. *They thought they saw a spirit*. This too the Manichaeans suppose, that Christ was a spirit, that he was not flesh. Remain you too in such faith, if Christ wished his disciples to remain there. You who suppose that Christ was a spirit and appeared in a phantasm, that is, as though the flesh in Christ were not true—the disciples too supposed this: you have been wounded with the disciples, be healed with them. When the disciples supposed this, that Christ was a spirit, and that what they saw seemed rather than was, what did the Lord do? *Why*, he says, *do thoughts arise in your heart?*[^8] No enemy came to you from without, and an inner thought cuts the throat of your souls. You think about me: *Why do thoughts arise in your heart?* What thoughts? *Because they thought they saw a spirit*. The Lord fears such thoughts, lest they kill the faith of the disciples; therefore fear to have such thoughts: what the physician fears, the sick man is not safe from. *Why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet; handle and see, for a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see me to have*[^9]. They saw, and touched, and believed, and announced: and the Manichaeans still contradict concerning Thomas: I do not believe unless I touch. Therefore, *do not be unbelieving*[^10]. Were the Lord's scars false, and are the words of the Manichaean true? Far be it from us. Rather, the words of the Lord are true, the Lord showed true bones; the scars true: he raised true limbs into heaven; but corruption he did not raise into heaven. Flesh, he says: death has died.

**4.** Let us return to that question about holy Mary. Behold, he is touched by the disciples: *Handle and see, for a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see me to have*[^11]. He is touched by the doubting Thomas; Thomas cries out: *My Lord and my God!*[^12] And the Lord to him: *Because you have seen, you have believed; blessed are those who do not see, and believe*[^13]. Those to whom you announce me surpass your faith. You announce what you have seen, you announce what you have touched, you announce what, by seeing and touching, you scarcely believed; and yet he is going to believe you who has neither seen nor touched. You see me, and you do not believe me: you touch me, and you scarcely believe me; another hears you, and believes in me. But you ask: Why is it that Thomas is permitted to touch, and to Mary it is said, *Do not touch me*? In this place he himself gave the reason: *For I have not yet*, he says, *ascended to the Father*[^14]. What is this? Here you are on earth, and you forbid yourself to be touched: when you have ascended, who touches you? Set on earth, you push away the hand near you. In this place we can most elegantly surmise, and say: The Lord did well to keep his touch for unbelievers; this woman he forbids to touch, because she had already believed. For what need was there to touch and seek the one whom by hearing she recognized? But yet he was not silent; afterward he gave the reason. *Do not touch me*. Why? *For I have not yet ascended to my Father*: touch me as I ascend to the Father. What is, touch me as I ascend to the Father? Touch me as equal to the Father. What is, touch me as equal to the Father? Touch God, that is, believe in God. Easy is what you see: the form of a servant was assumed for your sake, it is the garment of God; it is no great thing to see flesh. The Jews too saw, who killed him; the Gentiles did not see, and believed. Therefore, he says, as you see me, in the flesh the limbs, the figure you know, as you see me, *do not touch me*: that is, do not remain there, do not direct your gaze only that far, let not the end of your faith be here. I wish indeed that you believe me to be man, but do not remain there; stretch out the hand of faith, do not remain there.

**5.** And do not touch in the way the heretic Photinus touched: he said that Christ is a man, nothing more; therefore he did not grasp, he did not understand, he did not touch. *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^15]. So, lest she think Christ to be only a man: Do not touch me thus: my garment is before you; keep what has been made in heaven, send forth the hand of the heart; and then you touch me, when I ascend to the Father. So touched those who confessed: *He ascended into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father*[^16]. Behold, thus the Church touches, whose figure Mary bore. Let us touch Christ, let us touch. To believe is to touch. Do not stretch out your hand only as far as the man; say what Peter said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^17]. Let not Christ, then, seem to you a man only; for if you touch as the heretic said, you will be like Photinus. But again, do not shun the man Christ, do not remain there. I do not say that you should turn aside; what do I say? Do not remain there: he does not reach the dwelling who wishes to remain on the way. Rise, walk: the man Christ is your way, God Christ is your homeland. Our homeland is *truth and life*[^18]; our way, *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^19]. We were slow to walk, the way came to us; because the way came to us, let us walk. The man Christ is our way: let us not desert the way, that we may attain to the only-begotten Son of God, equal to the Father, transcending the whole creation, coeternal with the Father, the day without a day, the maker of faith. For this reason let us walk, that we may touch this.

**6.** So, finally, touched the woman who suffered an issue of blood. What faith there was in her, that the Lord said to her: Now come forward and show yourself in the crowd; obtain praise, from where you have obtained also health! *Go, daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace*[^20]. If you ask what her faith was, hear. *She said in her heart: If I touch the hem of his garment, I shall be made well*[^21]. She touched in order to obtain what she believed; she did not touch in order to prove what she had not believed. Then the Lord asks, saying: *Who touched me?*[^22] Do you then not know, Lord, who touched you? You see the thought, and you ask about the act? What is, *Who touched me?* I will show you who touched me: faith touched me; it by its touch made power go out from me. Where I have not been, where I have not walked, where I was not born, there it was believed in me: *A people I did not know served me*[^23]. O touching! O believing! O laying claim! And this a woman, wearied by sufferings of blood—like the Church, afflicted and wounded in the martyrs by the shedding of blood, but full of the powers of faith; the Church which formerly spent its substance on physicians, that is, on the gods of the Gentiles, who could never cure it: to which Church the Lord showed his presence not bodily, but spiritually. Now, then, both the touching woman and the touched Lord knew each other. But so that those who needed to know salvation might be taught to touch, he says: *Who touched me?* And the disciples answered: *The crowds press upon you, and do you say, Who touched me?*[^24] As though you were in some lofty place, where no one touches you, do you ask who touched you, whom uncounted crowds press upon? The Lord says: *Someone touched me*[^25]: I felt one woman touching more than the crowd pressing. The crowd easily knows how to press: would that it might learn to touch!

**7.** Let the conclusion of our sermon, brothers, be that we believe our Lord Jesus Christ—before Abraham, before Adam, before heaven and earth, before angels and archangels, and thrones and dominions, and principalities and powers, before all things founded and made, whether the things that are seen or the things that are not seen, without any span of time, without any number of years—to be coeternal with the Father, equal to the Father, the true only-begotten of the Father, the power and the wisdom of the Father: equally everlasting, equally immortal; and truly immortal, because altogether unchangeable; equally invisible, because incorporeal; equally powerful, and himself almighty. Let us believe the only-begotten Son of God to be such. But remember, when we have said, *And in Jesus Christ his only Son our Lord*, remember what follows: *who was born of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary*[^26]. He was born of a mother without a father, below on earth—he who already above, of the Father, before all times, without a mother, was born to make the times. In true flesh now, near the end of times, he was begotten of true flesh; but his flesh had the likeness of the flesh of sin, it was not the flesh of sin. And whence had it the likeness? Because it was mortal. And whence was it not the flesh of sin? Because it had come through the faith of the Virgin. But that very flesh of Christ—it itself grew, it itself came to a young man's age. In it Christ hungered, thirsted, ate, drank, grew weary, rested, slept: in it all these things, sin nowhere. In it he suffered; in it was manifested the man, the hidden God, seeking man in man, seeking the lost through the one taken up. In it, therefore, for the sake of man himself, he suffered unworthy things from men. It was true flesh that was held fast by the Jews; it was true flesh that ate the Pasch with the disciples. The Jews buffeted true flesh; the Jews set thorns upon true flesh; the unbelieving hung true flesh upon the wood; the faithless and wretched pierced with a lance true flesh, the soul now departing; the disciples took down true flesh from the wood, and laid it in the tomb; the truth raised true flesh; the truth showed true flesh to the disciples after the resurrection; the truth displayed the scars of true flesh to the hands of those who handled him. Let falsehood, then, blush, because truth has conquered.
