# Sermon 335/B: On the Feast of the Holy Martyrs

**1.** We call the holy martyrs blessed above other men, who contended against sin even to blood; and we call them blessed, because for the truth they underwent death, and by dying found life. For they would not die, unless man had sinned; for man, if he kept the commandment, would live. God threatened him with death, if he sinned: so by fear of death he ought to have abstained from the offense; but he rather believed the deceiving serpent, than the Creator counseling him. He did not believe God, and found what he did not believe. So nature by its own force runs into death: and, as far as is in it, struggles not to die; but it dies unwilling, because it sinned willing. For the first man, by sinning, did not despise death, but, even if he sinned, did not believe he would die. It was said to him: Do not touch: if you touch, by death you will die. Surely he did not say: I will touch, and I will die? Surely he did not say to himself: God has threatened me with death, that I should not taste of that tree; I will rather taste, and find out what is in that food; and if death follows, what is it to me? He did not say this: because by pride and curiosity he wanted to sin, but by nature he did not want to die. Finally even the serpent itself, indeed the devil in the serpent, the persuader to sin, the overthrower of faith, did not take from him the fear of death by contempt of death. For he did not say to him in persuading him to evil: You fear to die; and what is it to die? Death is no evil, without cause you fear what is no evil. He did not say this to him: for he knew how much the will recoils from death by nature. But what did he say to him? *You will not die by death*[^1]. He did not persuade contempt of death, but took away faith in death. So he did what he was persuaded to do, not because he despised death, but because he did not believe he would die. Death followed the sinner: it begot us, not such as he was created, but such as by sinning he had become. We were born, drawing fault and punishment from the transgressor. One came against one: because *through one man death, and through one man the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive*[^2]. So one came against one: nor did he come in the way that the one had come whom he came to help; but he came from a virgin, he came without lust, he came conceived not by desire but by faith. So one came against one. That he might help man, he took up something of the kind; but he did not take up the whole that the one had whom he came to help. So he came, and found us lying in fault and punishment; he took up the punishment alone, and loosed both the fault and the punishment.

**2.** So the Lord Christ took up the martyrs to be exhorted, not only by his word, but also by his example, that they should not fear death. Christ's exhortation was not such as the serpent's seduction. For the serpent said: *If you sin, you will not die by death.* Christ said: If you sin, by death you will die. What is: If you sin, by death you will die? If you deny me, indeed if you do not deny me, you will die. But *do not fear those who kill the body*[^3]. O man, do not now fear those who kill the body. Then you ought to have feared death, lest you come into death; then if you feared death, you would not come into death: now if you fear death, you will be freed from death. But dismiss the devil's seductive persuasion. The Lord our God himself said one thing then to man, says another now. Then he said to him: Do not sin, lest you die; now he says to him: Die, lest you sin. Then he said to him: By sinning you come to death; now he says to him: By dying you arrive at life. So the punishment of the sinner has become the instrument of virtue: then by hearing the devil they died, now we by dying for the truth overcome the devil. Why do you exalt yourself, my captor? By your own I conquer you. You persuaded sin, and took away faith in death; you said: You will not die; I believed, and I died. You persuaded, you deceived, you tricked, you held, by evil persuasion you sent me into death; but by dying for Christ you lost those whom you possessed. You have nothing by which to terrify, when death itself is taken up, lest one sin, which ought to have been feared, lest we sin. Against your poisonous whispers the martyr sings: *I will sing to my God, as long as I live*[^4]. So when you are dead, you do not sing to God? Indeed then you sing more, than while you live. For now it is not "long," which has an end. I would not say that even Adam himself lived long, if he died today; for what would the long times past profit, when they were past? He lived long, yet he lived: but if he lives, tell me, and if what lives will have no end, then I say it is long. We Christians, to whom eternal life is promised, ought not to understand what some author of the pagans understood, who said: *For what is this very "long," in which there is something last?* Take away the end, and I acknowledge length; but if you add an end, I prove brevity. So I will sing to my God, as long as I live. So we ought to take that "long," that it may truly be long. As long as I live, therefore always, because I live, refer it to that: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house, forever and ever they will praise you*[^5]. This reward the holy martyrs loved, and by loving life they were willing to die. Because, then, they died patiently, do you think they loved death? Far be it, they loved life, they desired life: they wanted to live without death, who chose to die for life; they despised what is not long, that they might come to what is long.

**3.** But what kind of persecutor is he, who comes and says: Deny Christ, if you do not want me to kill you? For the sake of life shall I deny life? for the sake of a short life shall I deny eternal life? What if you spare me today, and tomorrow a fever kills me? Why, by threatening me with death, do you want to take away my faith? If a fever kills me, it does not take away my faith. This life, which you say you give, is not in your power: so for its sake I will not deny Christ, who has both death and life in his power. So shall I deny him, who as long as he wishes I live here, not as long as you wish here? And when I cease to live here, I come to him, where there is nothing but to live. Behold, if you do not deny him, I kill you. I know who speaks, through you he now says to me: Deny, and you will not die. Almost this he said to me then too: Touch, and you will not die. I refused then, now I beware of you. So however sweet this life is, and no one wants to end it, miserable as it is. If this life, such as it is, is so sweet, what is that life, my brothers? Attend to the glory of the martyrs: unless death were bitter, the glory of the martyrs would be nothing. If death is nothing, what great thing did the martyrs despise? Attend to the Lord himself: *Greater love than this no one has, than that he lay down his soul for his friends*[^6]. And he who had power to lay down his soul, and had power to take it up again, yet, that he might transfigure us into himself, *My soul,* he says, *is sorrowful even to death*[^7]. To blessed Peter too: *When you have grown old,* he says, *another will gird you, and carry you where you do not want*[^8], even when you have grown old. What kind of life, then, is the happy life, if it must be loved even when miserable? The action of this life, which is so loved, what is it? To desire, to fear, to hope, to be deceived, to labor, to fall sick: true sadness, false gladness: to pour out prayers, to fear trials. What kind of life is this? who can describe its misery with any eloquence? Yet it is loved. But the action of that life, what is it? I will not go on longer, I will not describe it long: *I will sing to my God, as long as I live.*

**4.** The martyrs were lovers of life, therefore they were endurers of death. Nevertheless, my brothers, so sweet is this life, foul, miserable, so sweet that the martyrs could not despise it for the truth and for eternal life, except with the help of him who commanded that they despise it. Often even desire despises death, but where there is no soundness: one vice is pressed by another, for desire is of this world. *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world: if anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him; for all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age*[^9]. Often men despise death through the desire of the flesh; they despise death through the desire of the eyes, they despise death through the ambition of the age: but all these are of the world. He who despises death for the love of God, can in no way fulfill it without the help of God. Finally, even the martyr himself, when he said: *I will sing to my God, as long as I live,* and was meditating on eternal life, roused, turned himself, and said: *Do not trust in princes*[^10]. Lest, when the prince spares you, you trust in him: let the Prince of princes, God himself, help you, that you may have faith. Be faithful from faith: and do not trust in princes, *and in the sons of men, in whom there is no salvation*[^11]. Therefore not even in yourself, because *salvation is the Lord's*[^12]. For what you are by yourself, and as far as concerns yourself, do you want to know? *His spirit will go out, and he will return to his earth; on that day all their thoughts will perish*[^13]. Behold what you are, if in you there is nothing but you. Since, then, you ought not to trust in the sons of men, in whom there is no salvation—for their salvation is not in them from themselves; whence it was said: *Salvation is the Lord's, and your blessing is upon your people*[^14]—if you do not trust in men, in whom there is no salvation, neither ought you to trust in yourself, because you are a man, in whom there is no salvation.

**5.** Answer now, and tell me: If I do not trust in myself, then I do not despise death; I do not fulfill the commandment, that I should not deny Christ. *Blessed is he whose helper is the God of Jacob*[^15]. You indeed despise death; you believe, and fulfill the commandment; you trample the threats of the persecutors; you most ardently love and desire eternal life. It is true that you do; but: *Blessed is he whose helper is the God of Jacob.* Take away the helper, I find nothing but the deserter. The deserter Adam, the helper Christ. What is that deserter, but what was said to him: *You are earth, and into earth you will go*[^16]? His spirit will go out, and he will return to his earth. But the helper underwent death, and taught us to despise what he took up. And the helper says to me: You fear sin, but therefore you want to sin, lest you die. Behold I suffer what you fear. What you fear I suffer; fear that which I do not do. What do you fear? Death. Behold I suffer it. Fear that which I do not do, sin. *Who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth*[^17]. So do not do what he did not do, and do not fear what he suffered. For you are not going to do death, but to suffer it; fear your deed, not your suffering. Fear what you do willing, not what you suffer unwilling; death does not kill you, if you are not death to yourself. Nevertheless not even death itself would be, if sin had not been. It is to be despised, it is true, it is to be trampled, it is passing: why even it at all? Would that it alone! What is: Would that it alone? Would that the first death alone, loosing the spirit from the flesh. Fear the second death, where the soul is not parted from the flesh, but the soul is tortured with the flesh. Do not fear the passing death, fear the abiding one: none is worse than where death does not die. By fearing death you wanted to sin: sin killed the inner man in you; sin killed the very life of your flesh. Finally, that death of the body would not follow, unless the death of the soul had preceded. The soul deserted God willing, and deserts the flesh unwilling. Yet the Lord did not desert the flesh unwilling: when he willed he died, because when he willed he was born. But why this? Lest you should fear, behold all was done. And do we fear death: we fear death, as though we were going to escape death. Fear what you can avoid, that is, sin. You can avoid sin, you cannot avoid death; and yet what cannot be escaped is feared more than what can be avoided. We have recognized ourselves, we have seen ourselves, we have examined ourselves; let us groan in ourselves, let us pour out prayers, lest we enter into trials. Let us not presume on our own strength to conquer these things: *for blessed is he whose helper is the God of Jacob, whose hope is in the Lord his God*[^18]; not in himself, because he is a man. But *cursed is everyone who puts his hope in man*[^19].
