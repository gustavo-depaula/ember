# Sermon 72: (Augmented) - (on the Words of the Lord in the Gospel According to Matthew: Either Make the Tree Good and Its Fruit Good)

**1.** (Our Lord Jesus Christ admonished us that we be good trees and be able to have good fruits. For he says: *Either make the tree good, and its fruit good; or make the tree bad, and its fruit bad. For the tree is known by its fruit*[^1]. Where he says: *Make the tree good, and its fruit good*, this surely is not an admonition, but a wholesome precept, obedience to which is necessary. But what he says: *Make the tree bad, and its fruit bad*, is not a precept, that you should do it, but a warning, that you should beware. For he said it against those who thought that they, when they were bad, could speak good things or have good works. This the Lord Jesus says cannot be. For first the man must be changed, that the works may be changed. For if the man remains in that which is bad, he cannot have good works; if he remains in that which is good, he cannot have bad works.)

**2.** (But who was found good by the Lord, since Christ died for the impious? So he found all bad trees. But he gave to those believing in his name the power to become sons of God[^2]. Whoever, therefore, is a good man today, that is, a good tree, was found bad, and made good. And if, when he found the bad trees, he had wished to root them out, what would have remained that was not worthy to be rooted out? But he came first to dispense mercy, that he might afterward exercise judgment, to whom it is said: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^3]. So he gave to believers the remission of sins, he was unwilling to keep account with them even of the past records. He gave the remission of sins: he made the trees good. He deferred the axe: he gave security.)

**3.** (Of this axe John speaks, saying): *Now (the axe is laid to the root of the trees. Every tree that does not make good fruit will be cut down, and cast into the fire*[^4]. With this axe the householder threatens in the Gospel, saying: *Behold, it is three years that I come to this tree, and I find no fruit on it. Now I ought to clear the place: therefore let it be cut down*[^5]. And the vinedresser intercedes: *Lord, leave it this year too; I will dig)*) *around it and apply a basket of dung; if it makes fruit, well; if not, you will come and cut it down*[^6]. As through three years the Lord visited the human race, that is, in three certain times. The first time is before the Law; the second, in the Law; the third is now, which is the time of grace. For if he did not visit the human race before the Law, whence Abel? whence Enoch? whence Noah? Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob? whose God he wished to be called, whose all nations were, as though he were the God of three men: *I am*, he says, *the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob*[^7]. But in the Law, if he did not visit, he would not give the Law. After the Law the householder himself too came: he suffered, died, and rose again, gave the Spirit, made the Gospel to be preached through the whole world. And still a certain tree remained unfruitful. There is still a certain part of the human race: still it does not correct itself. The vinedresser intercedes: the Apostle prays for the people: *I bend*, he says, *my knees for you to the Father, that, rooted and grounded in love, you may be able to grasp with all the saints what is the breadth and length, height and depth, to know also the love of Christ surpassing knowledge, that you may be filled unto all the fullness of God*[^8]. By bending his knees, he intercedes for us before the householder, that we may not be rooted out. So it is necessary that he come: let him find us fruitful. The digging-around of the tree is the humility of the penitent: for every ditch is humble. And the basket of dung is the filth of the penitent: for what is filthier than dung? and yet, if you use it well, what is more fruitful?

**4.** Let each one, then, be a good tree. Let him not think he can have good fruits, if he remains a bad tree. There will be no good fruit, except of a good tree. Change the heart, and the work will be changed. Root out craving, plant love. For just as craving is the root of all evils[^9], so love is the root of all good things. Why, then, do men murmur among themselves or contend: "What is good?" Oh, if you knew what it is! The good which you wish to have is not greatly good. The good which you do not wish to be, this is a great good! For you wish to have health of body? So be it, let it be good. Yet it is not a great good, which even the bad man can have. You wish to have gold and silver? Behold, this too I say: it is good, but if you have used it well; but you do not use it well, if you will be bad. And through this gold and silver is bad to the bad, good to the good: not because gold and silver make them good, but because, since it finds them good, it is turned to a good use. You wish to have honor? It is good, but this too if you use it well. To how many has honor been an occasion of ruin! to how many has honor been the ministry of a good work!

**5.** Let us distinguish, then, these goods, if we can, because we are speaking of good trees. And there is nothing that anyone ought to think of, except to strive, as far as he can, to turn back from the other things by which the eyes are spoiled, to turn his eyes upon himself, to descend into himself, to examine himself, to inspect himself, to seek himself and to find himself; and what displeases, let him kill, and what pleases, let him adopt, and what he adopts, let him plant. For when a man has found himself empty of better goods, why is he greedy for outward goods? Behold, what does a chest full of goods profit, with an empty conscience? You wish to have good things, and do not wish to be good! Do you not see that you ought to blush at your goods, if your house is full of goods and has you bad? For what is there that you wish to have bad? Tell me. Nothing at all: not a wife, not a daughter, not a son, not a manservant, not an estate, not a tunic, finally not a boot; and yet you wish to have a bad life! I beg you, prefer your life to your boot. All the things that lie around your eyes, sumptuous and beautiful, are dear to you; and to yourself you are cheap and foul! If the goods, of which your house is full, which you longed to have, which you trembled to lose, could answer you, would they not cry out to you: "As you wish to have us good, so we too wish to have you as a good lord"? With a silent voice they appeal against you to your lord: "Behold, you gave so great goods to this man, and he himself is bad! What does it profit him what he has, when he does not have the one who gave all things?"

**6.** So one admonished by these my words, and perhaps pricked, asks what is good, what kind of good, whence one is good. You have understood well that you ought to ask this. I will answer the one asking, and say: This is the good which you cannot lose unwillingly. For you can lose gold, even unwilling; you can lose a house, can lose honors, can lose the very health of the flesh. But the good by which you are good, you neither receive unwilling, nor lose unwilling. I ask, (then, what kind of good this is. The Psalm indeed admonishes us of a great thing, perhaps the one we seek. For it says: *Sons of men, how long heavy of heart?*[^10] How long that tree in the three years? *Sons of men, how long heavy of heart?* What is *heavy of heart*? *Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?* And afterward it says what is to be sought: *Know that the Lord has magnified his Holy One*[^11]. Now Christ too has come, now he has been magnified, now he has risen and ascended into heaven, now through the whole world his name is preached: *How long heavy of heart?* Let) the past times suffice: now that that Holy One has been mag)nified, *how*) *long* (*heavy of heart?* After the three) years (what remains, except the axe?) *How*( *long heavy of heart?*) *Why d*(*o you love*) *vanit*(*y and seek a li*)*e*? (Still vain things, still) useless things, (still pompous things), still fl(eeting things, so, when Christ the Holy One has been) magnif(ied, still these things are sou)ght! (Now the truth cries out,) and stil(l vanity is sought!) *How*( *long heavy of heart?*)

**7.** (Deservedly is this world strongly scourged: for the world has now come to know the words of the Lord. *And the servant*, he says, *not knowing the will of his lord and doing things worthy of stripes, will be beaten with few*[^12]. Why? That he may seek the will of his lord. So the servant not knowing the will: this was the world, before the Lord magnified his Holy One; it was a servant not knowing the will of his lord, and therefore it was beaten with few. But the servant now knowing the will of his lord[^13]: this is now, from when the Deity magnified his Holy One, and not doing his will, will be beaten with many. What wonder, then, if the world is much beaten? It is a servant knowing the will of the lord and doing things worthy of stripes. So let it not refuse to be beaten with many, since, if it is unwilling to hear the instructor unjustly, it will justly suffer the avenger; or let it not murmur against the chastiser, when it sees itself worthy of stripes, that it may merit mercy.)

**9.** But because he did not hear unto giving full peace, except him who turns away from evil and does good—but to turn away from evil is to be angry with oneself, to be pricked on one's bed; but to do good is to sacrifice the sacrifice of justice; to seek peace is to hope in the Lord—when he had said: *Sacrifice the sacrifice of justice*, he added: *And hope in the Lord*[^14]. Now you are angry with yourself, now you are pricked, now you have turned away from evil, now you have offered the sacrifice of justice, still in hope, in good works: one thing you do not yet hold, lest perhaps you say: "Now I have done all these things, and still I have received nothing," there remains for you the work of endurance. For *tribulation produces patience, patience proof, proof produces hope. But hope does not confound*[^15]. Be secure: you will not be confounded. Hope in him. Now it is still as it were night: you do not yet see God, nor hold what he promised, but in this night do what the psalm says: *In the day of my tribulation I sought God with my hands by night before you, and I was not deceived*[^16]. *In the day of my tribulation:* reckon this whole life the day of tribulation, and say: *I sought God with my hands*, not with my eyes. For it is still night. Seek with the hands, merit by works, do good things and say that in this night you sought the Lord with your hands. There follows, what follows for you too: *And I was not deceived.* By night I sought, by groping I sought, and yet I found. This is *I sought with my hands:* diligently and by works I inquired. But how *with my hands did I seek?* *Before him*, on account of that: *Do not do your justice before men*[^17], not that you shun the face, but lest you seek your own praise. For if you shun the face of those looking on, when you do good things, where is that: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds?* But see what follows: *And glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^18]—that he may be glorified in you who made you good. For you were fit to make yourself bad, you are not fit to make yourself good. You were fit to wound yourself, you are not fit to heal yourself. So when you do good works, wish this, that God be glorified in you, because, when you have sacrificed the sacrifice of justice—that is, good works—it follows that you hope in the Lord[^19].

**10.** "And what am I going to hope for? how long am I going to hope?" Here do not; it is not of this earth what you seek. You wish to be blessed, I know. For who does not wish it? You seek a good thing, but you do not seek it in its own region. As if you heard that gold is good and cannot be found except under the earth, you snatched up a hoe and dug in your house. Questioned what you seek, you would answer: "Gold." But he who knows where gold is found would answer you: "You seek a good thing, but not where you ought." You seek to be blessed, you wish to be full of joys, you wish to be satisfied with goods so that you are altogether in no want and nothing is lacking to you: a great thing, but it is not here. How you may arrive, I tell you. What shall I say, except what I said? Be angry with yourself, be pricked on your bed, change yourself. Sacrifice the sacrifice of justice and hope in the Lord[^20]. In him you will have what you seek; when you have come to him, there you will have it, because you will have him himself of whom it is said: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^21].

**11.** Finally, here too what follows? When he had said *be pricked*—that is, let it repent you that you were bad—*sacrifice the sacrifice of justice*—be fervent in good works—he added: *Hope in the Lord*, and says: *Many say: "Who shows us good things?"*[^22] Behold, they were pricked. "On my bed I was bad: I was angry with myself[^23]. I was a thief, I was a plunderer, I was this and that: I was angry with myself, I was pricked on my bed, I changed myself. Now I do good works: when I see one hungry, I hold out bread; when I see one naked, I clothe him; when I see a stranger, I take him in; when I see one quarreling, I make him agree; when I see one oppressed, I redeem him; when I see one sick, I visit him; when I see one dead, I bury him: where is the good that was promised me? Now I have amended myself, now I have changed, now, corrected from bad works, I do good things: where are the things that were promised me?" *Many say*—would that few said—*many say*. What do many say? "*Who shows us good things?*"—where is what was promised me? "I dispense my gold and see nothing. What I dispense, I see; what is promised me, I do not see." It is night. Seek: the hands will not fail, and you will not be deceived[^24]. *Many say: "Who shows us good things?"*

**12.** What was he going to say to those who said to him: "*Who shows us good things?*" And what is the good itself? *The light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord*[^25]. Behold the good. *The light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord.* We were made to the image of God[^26], and we are not re-engraved except by him who made us, nor reformed except by him who created us. So *the light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord*, that we may be your coin and be stored in your treasure. *The light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord.* This is the coinage of Christ: the face of our Commander is sealed in us. This coin Christ, as it were a tax collector, sought, when, to those slandering about the tribute, showing the coin and answering that it had the image of Caesar, he said: *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's*[^27]. Caesar seeks his image in your gold, will God not seek it in your soul? Behold what the good things are. Why do *many* still *say: "Who shows us good things?" How long heavy of heart?* You seek goods in which you may fear more things. You seek transitory goods, you seek temporal goods, you seek goods from which, living, you are dismissed, or, dying, you dismiss them. *How long*, then, *heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?* Do you wish me to show you the good? Have it and you will know. It can be had in the heart, it cannot be shown to the eyes.

**13.** Finally, see what follows *the light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord: You have given*, he says, *gladness in my heart*[^28]. *In my heart*, not in my storeroom, not in my chest, but *in my heart.* See that *the light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord.* For one acquires gold too and puts it in a chest: his heart is glad, but over vanity, not over truth. Do you wish to see how vainly he rejoices who so rejoices and who suspects the false to be true? This man has a chest full: he goes out of his house; wherever he turns, he rejoices, because it is full. Another had a chest full and lost by a thief what he had: he does not know it, still he rejoices; he does not know it has perished, still he rejoices. How was that yours which was not in you? Behold, now it perishes, and you still rejoice. You feed on empty hopes. Whence, except because you seek vanity? To what end? For what good, with what fruit? Behold, you have lost it, even if you loved what you lost. And how have you perished? Return, then, to the highest good, that you may be able to say: "*The light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord.* We have been made your coins: gather us to your treasures, and we render the tribute of Caesar, ourselves to you, to Caesar what you gave, to you what you made, to you who made us. *You have given gladness in my heart:* there is whence I rejoice, the good is within, it is stored in the inner chest." This you cannot lose unwillingly; there is he himself who made it, delighting in his temple, because you have been pricked on your bed[^29].

**14.** Finally, after he said: *The light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord; you have given gladness in my heart*[^30], he looked back at those many who said: "*Who shows us good things?*", and said: *From the time of their grain, wine, and oil they have been multiplied*[^31]—their grain, their wine, their oil. These things they attended to and craved earthly things. And when it was well with them, they were filled with these earthly things—*they have been multiplied*—*they have called blessed the people that has these things.* But what do you say? *Blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^32]. Because *the light of your face is sealed upon us, O Lord.* But those, *multiplied from the time of their grain, wine, and oil*, let them withdraw from me; let those who love such things not say to me: "*Who shows us good things?*" There is what I may show, but there are not those to whom I may show it. So *from the time of their grain, wine, and oil they have been multiplied.*

**15.** You, what? *In peace, in the selfsame.* What is *in the selfsame?* That which is not changed, which is not various, which is not temporal, which is not now thus, now otherwise. *In the selfsame I will sleep*[^33]. It is hard to explain what I feel, what he said: *I will sleep in the selfsame.* *I will sleep in peace: in the selfsame I will sleep.* He who sleeps has, as it were, a holiday from the senses of the flesh. Why, then, for your blessed life do you seek outside, forms and colors for the eyes, songs and sweet chants for the ears, various flavors for the taste, various pleasures for the touch? It is not such a good by which you are good, not such a good of which you said: "*Who will show us good things?*" Within, in the image of God, is the good, whence is formed this good *which neither eye saw nor ear heard*[^34]. Therefore sleep, take yourself away from the desire of the eyes, do not converse with the senses of the flesh, that your Lord may say to you: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^35]. So *in peace, in the selfsame I will sleep and rest*[^36].

**16.** Whence does one come there? *Because you, O Lord, singularly have made me dwell in hope*[^37]. This which was said: *Hope in the Lord*, was said when iniquity had now been cleared away, when the sacrifice of justice had now been offered. *Hope in the Lord*[^38], but *you singularly have made me dwell in hope.* What is *singularly?* Not like the *many* who *say: "Who will show us good things?"*, craving many things and scattered among diverse pleasures—one seeks this, another that, they do not seek one thing. From the evil of that one this one wishes his own good. That one cannot enrich himself, unless this one has come to want. This larger fish cannot be satisfied, unless the smaller fish has been devoured. Those who seek these goods do not seek goods, because they seek non-goods. *Singularly*, then, you will receive this, which the impious do not receive. O you singular man—the whole body of Christ—O you singular man—the body whose head is Christ—*dwell singularly in hope*, do not seek the goods of the nations. *For all these things the nations seek. Seek first the kingdom and justice of God*[^39]—this is the sign of the face of God[^40]. Seek this in yourself and, if there is need, *all these things will be added to you*[^41]. Does he feed a robber and will he forsake you? *The earth is the Lord's and its fullness*[^42].

**17.** Do not you withdraw from your God, love your God. To whom you say: "Give me this and give me that," say to him at some time: "Give me yourself." If you love, love freely, do not be an unchaste soul. Your wife would not please you, if she loved your gold, if she loved you for this reason, that you gave gold, that you gave a good tunic, an excellent estate, a choice slave, a comely eunuch; because, if she loved these things in you, she would not love you. Do not rejoice in such love: an adulterer can often give more. You wish your wife to love you freely, and do you wish to sell your faith to God? "Because I believe in you," you say to your God, "give me gold." Are you not ashamed? "Because I believe," you say, "give me gold." You have set your faith up for sale: see its price. It is not worth so much, it is not worth gold or silver, your faith is not worth so much; it has a great price: God himself is its price. Love him, and love him freely. For if you love him for the sake of something else, you do not love him. You ought not to wish him for the sake of something else, but whatever else you wish you ought to wish for his sake, that all things be referred to the love of him, not that he be referred to other loves, but that he be preferred to other loves. Love him, love him freely. To this that great athlete was provoked by the great adversary saying: "*Does Job worship God for nothing?*"[^43]

**18.** So if you wish the good, when you say: "*Who shows us good things?*", God himself is it, who made you good and who makes you better; he is good by no other good, but good by himself. For for you to be good, another good comes to you; for him to be good, no good comes to him. For he himself is good to himself by himself. Commending this great good, the Lord says: "*Why do you question me about the good? No one is good, except the one God*"[^44]. Are there not other good things? is it not written: *God made all things very good*[^45]? But all these things are good through God. But God is not good through these things, nor is he good through other things that have good, but he is the good of goods. Behold what kind of good: seek it. *Why do you still love vanity and seek a lie?*[^46] Behold what is the good: seek the good of goods. You seek riches: it is not singular. Attend: *Singularly you have made me dwell in hope*[^47]. Why is it not singular? Both the good and the bad have it. You seek health of body: both the good and the bad have it, and men and beasts, it is not singular. You seek honors: the bad too attain them, it is not singular. That is singular, which only the good will have. To the good here God gives goods, and to the bad; he himself gives evils both to the good and to the bad: to the latter for torment, to the former for exercise. Evils are common to the good and the bad. The singular evil, the bad eternal fire; the singular good, the good eternal God. For whatever other things there are of creatures, God gives them even to his enemies: earth and heaven; they use the lights of heaven, the fruits of the earth, health of body, the honors of the world—his enemies use them. It is a creature: what he created he gives them. Him who created, he keeps for you, but if you love freely. For the time will come when he gives himself to you, and it will be said to the impious: *Let the impious be taken away, that he may not see the brightness of God*[^48]. So, brothers, let us love God freely, let us do good things, let us endure evils, let us hope in him, that at some time, he being rendered, we may be satisfied by him alone, who lives and reigns through the immortal ages. Amen.
