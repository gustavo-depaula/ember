# Sermon 299/B: Discourse for the Heavenly Birthday of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul

**1.** The suffering of the blessed apostles has consecrated this day for us; by despising the world, they earned this glory throughout the world. Peter was the first of the apostles; Paul was the last of the apostles. Christ, the first and the last, brought the first and the last to a single day of suffering. To recall what I said, consider Α and Ω. The Lord himself says plainly in *Revelation: I am the alpha and the omega; the first*, before whom there is no one; *the last,* after whom there is no one; preceding all things, bringing all things to their end. Do you want to contemplate the first? *All things were made through him*. Are you looking for the last? *For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.* So that you might begin to live, you had him as your creator; so that you may live forever, you have him as your redeemer. Let us consider, dearly beloved, that first of the apostles, the most blessed Peter, who says in his letter: *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, so that we might follow in his footsteps.* Indeed, you heard when the Gospel was read: *Follow me.* He questioned him—Christ questioned Peter, the teacher his disciple, the Lord his servant, the physician the man he had healed—and said to him: *Peter, do you love me?* And, as you know, he did not merely say, *Do you love me?* He added, *more than these*. Do you love me more than these, more than they do? Peter did not answer, “I love you more than they do,” for it was not for a human being to judge the hearts of others. Instead he answered: *Lord, you know that I love you.* Why do you ask me about what you have done in me? You know what you have given. Why do you question me about a love I have only from you? You know that I love you. The Lord asked the same question again, and Peter gave the same answer again. The Lord questioned him a third time, *and Peter was saddened because he said to him a third time, Do you love me?* The Lord was questioning Peter's love; let us question Peter's sadness. Why do we think Peter was saddened because he said to him a third time, “Do you love me?” However often the Lord wished to ask, why should the servant be saddened? But at the Lord's third question, he recalled his own third denial. You understand, blessed Peter, you understand your failure; you remember and are saddened. But rejoice after your sadness. Let love confess the one whom fear denied. Look, then, at this man who was formerly a denier and is now a lover—or rather, who was a lover before, but still weak. We say that Peter denied Christ, but we do not say that he followed Christ into the danger of suffering. The physician kept to the proper order of healing: first he showed Peter to Peter, and afterward he showed himself in Peter. It was as though he said to him: “You presumed that you would die for me, but you did not rely on me, because you presumed on yourself. Questioned by a servant girl, you discovered yourself; you wept and returned to me.”

**2.** So now, entrusting his sheep to Peter, the Lord foretold the suffering we celebrate today. *When you were*, he said, *younger, you fastened your own belt and walked where you wished; but when you grow old, another will fasten your belt and carry you where you do not wish. He said this to indicate by what death he would glorify God.* It happened: Peter denied him, Peter wept, Peter washed away his denial with tears. Christ rose again, and Peter was questioned about his love. He received the sheep entrusted to him, not his own sheep, but Christ's. For he did not say to him, “Feed your sheep,” but *Feed my sheep*; feed those I bought, because I redeemed you too. Then Christ the Lord spent forty days with his disciples. As they watched, a cloud received him, and he ascended into heaven. They followed his ascent with their eyes. Afterward they stayed in the city; on the fiftieth day they received the Holy Spirit and were filled. At that very moment they learned the languages of all nations and began speaking them, to the amazement and wonder of those who had killed Christ. Then this former denier, this present lover, sprang forward alone from among them all, because he was first among them all, and began preaching Christ to the Jews who had killed Christ. He sowed the faith of Christ in them and persuaded many of those by whom he had feared being killed for Christ to die for Christ themselves.

**3.** Where was it said, where was it foretold, that Christ's apostles would speak in the languages of all nations? *The heavens declare the glory of God*: understand as the heavens those who bear God; *and the works of his hands*—that is, the glory of God—*the firmament proclaims*. These are the heavens. Day pours out a word to day, and night proclaims knowledge to night. Let Christ never go unspoken by day or night. Yet consider how day pours out a word to day: Christ to his disciples, light to lights. And night proclaimed knowledge to night: Judas told the Jews where Christ was. Christ was seized, Christ was killed, death was killed in Christ, because Christ rose again and ascended into heaven and sent the Holy Spirit he had promised. They were filled with him like new wineskins with new wine. For the Lord had said: *No one puts new wine into old wineskins*. And know that this was fulfilled. The Jews were amazed, and some spoke as though mocking, without knowing what they were saying: *These men* *are full of new wine.* Thus, with the Holy Spirit giving, dictating, and teaching, they spoke languages they had not learned. Among their own people they had learned one, perhaps two. But now they spoke—what shall we say?—three languages, four, five, six? Why ask for a number? *There are no languages or forms of speech in which their voices are not heard*; you just heard the psalm when it was sung. They themselves were killed, but their words were written down. What did their killers accomplish? *Their sound has gone out into all the earth.* We here in Africa were far from there, where there were no languages or forms of speech in which their voices were not heard. We were far away, we lay far away, we slept far away; but to awaken us from sleep, *their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. Rise, you who sleep, and rise from the dead, and he will enlighten you* who said to Peter: *Do you love me?* Who can say anything worthy of Peter? Who can say enough when speaking of Peter? Without offense, blessed Peter, allow me to be silent for a little while about you, whose sound awakened me. My sermon cannot devote itself to you alone: you did not suffer alone today. You are indeed the first of the apostles, but the last was found worthy to be your companion.

**4.** Let blessed Paul also come forward for us, and let us say a little something about Paul; for he wished to be named Paul, since he was earlier called Saul. Saul first, Paul afterward—because proud first, humble afterward. Recall his first name, and in it you recognize the crime of a persecutor. Saulus was named after Saul. Saul, from whom he was called Saulus, persecuted the holy David, in which holy David there was a figure of Christ, who was to come from the seed of David through the Virgin Mary. Saulus filled the role of Saul when he persecuted the Christians. He had been a most savage persecutor: when blessed Stephen was stoned, he himself guarded the garments of those who stoned him, so that he might stone him by the hands of all. After the suffering of the most blessed Stephen, the brethren who were in Jerusalem were scattered; and because they were lights, they burned with the Spirit of God; wherever they came, they set things ablaze. Then Saul, seeing the Gospel of Christ grow, was filled with the most bitter zeal: he received letters from the chief priests, and set out to bring back bound for punishment whomever he found confessing the name of Christ; and he went breathing slaughter, thirsting for blood. So, even so, as he went, as he thirsted for blood, as he sought whom he might bring back and kill, just as he was, a persecutor, he heard a voice from heaven. My brethren, what good had he deserved? what evil had he not deserved? And yet by a single voice from heaven the persecutor was struck down, the preacher was raised up.

**5.** Behold Paul for you after Saul: behold, now he preaches, behold, now he shows us what he had been and what he is. *I am*, he says, *the least of the apostles*[^1]. If the least, then rightly Paul. Recall the Latin word: *paulum* means a little. Surely we speak this way: I will see you after a little. So that Paul confesses himself the least, like the fringe on the Lord's garment, which the ailing woman touched. Indeed she who suffered from a flow of blood prefigured the Church of the gentiles; and to those gentiles Paul was sent, both least and last: since the fringe is the smallest part of a garment, and the last. Paul confessed both of himself: he called himself both least and last. *I am the least of the apostles*—he said it; *I am the last of the apostles*—he said it. We do him no wrong, he said it himself. And what else did he say? Let him say it, lest we seem to do him wrong; though it is in no way a wrong to Paul where the grace of Christ is commended, yet, brethren, let us hear him. *I am*, he says, *the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle*[^2]. Behold what he was: who am not worthy to be called an apostle. Why? *Because I persecuted the Church of God*[^3]. And whence are you an apostle? *But by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace in me was not in vain, but I labored more than all of them*[^4]. I ask you, holy Paul—men who do not understand suppose Saul is still speaking: I labored more than all of them seems spoken proudly. Yet it was truly spoken: but what follows? When he saw that he had said something that could lift him up to a certain loftiness, *I labored*, he says, *more than all of them*, and immediately, *yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^5]. Humility recognized itself, weakness trembled, perfect love confessed the gift of God. Speak now then as one full of grace, as a vessel of election, as one made what you were not worthy to be; speak, write to Timothy, and announce this day. *For I*, he says, *am now being offered up*[^6]. A passage was just read from Paul's letter; here was read what I now say: *for I*, he says, *am now being offered up*. My offering is at hand: for the slaying of the saints is a sacrifice to God. *For I am now being offered up, and the time of my dissolution is at hand. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; for the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me on that day*[^7]. He will render to my merits, who gives the merits. He was made an apostle who was not worthy: and will not one who is worthy be crowned? For there, when he received grace, not owed but freely given: *I am not*, he says, *worthy to be called an apostle, but by the grace of God I am what I am*. But now he demands a debt: *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith*; for the rest there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, the crown of righteousness is owed to me. And that you may know it is owed: which he will render to me. He did not say, he gives to me, or, he grants to me, but, the Lord, the just judge, will render to me on that day. In mercy he granted to me, in justice he will render to me. I see, blessed Paul, to whose merits a crown is owed; but looking back to what you were, I recognize that your very merits are the gifts of God. You said: *I have fought the good fight*; but you yourself said: *Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*[^8]. So you fought the good fight, but you conquered by Christ's gift. You said: *I have finished the course*; but you yourself said, *it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy*[^9]. You said: *I have kept the faith*; but you yourself said: *I obtained mercy, that I might be faithful*[^10]. We see therefore that your merits are the gifts of God, and so we rejoice in your crown. And if I have been unequal to the praises of the blessed apostles whose feast we celebrate, yet I have not fallen short of your love's expectation, so far as he who crowns them has deigned to grant.
