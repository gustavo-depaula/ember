# Sermon 15: A Sermon Delivered in the Third District

In the Basilica of Peter at Carthage

On the Verse of the Psalm:

"Lord, I Have Loved the Beauty of Your House"

**1.** We love the beauty of the house of the Lord, and the place of the dwelling of his glory, if we ourselves too are that. What, then, is the beauty of the house of the Lord and the place of the dwelling of his glory, except his temple, of which the Apostle says, *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^1]? Just as, then, in handmade structures, when they are elegantly and magnificently built, our corporeal gaze is soothed, so when living stones, the hearts of the faithful, are held together by the bond of love, it is the beauty of the house of God and the place of the dwelling of his glory. Learn, then, what you ought to love, that you may be able to love. For whoever loves the beauty of the house of God, there is no doubt that he loves the Church, not in the constructed walls and roofs, not in the gleam of marbles and gilded ceilings, but in the faithful, holy people, loving God with their whole heart and with their whole soul and with their whole mind, and their neighbor as themselves.

**2.** But in the Christian assembly, as far as concerns participation and communion in the sacraments, *they are multiplied beyond number*[^2]. One thing, then, is the number, another those beyond the number. The number is those of whom the Apostle says, *The Lord knows who are his*[^3]. But beyond the number, since *in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and silver, but also of wood and clay; some indeed for honor, others for dishonor*[^4]. The number, then, of vessels for honor; beyond the number, the vessels for dishonor. Since, then, there are these two kinds of vessels, do we doubt where the beauty of the house of God is? If, then, you wish to love, doing what you have sung, the beauty of the house of God and the place of the dwelling of his glory, seek the vessels for honor. And I do not want you to say, "I sought, and did not find." For therefore you sought and did not find, because you yourself were not what you sought. Like cleaves to like, the unlike flees the unlike. If you are a vessel for dishonor, without doubt a vessel for honor will be burdensome to you even to look at. Do you not hear how some people say of someone, *He is burdensome to us even to look at*[^5]? What is burdensome to you to look at, when will it be open to you to find? For these vessels are of the inner man. Surely, when a just man has been seen, the just man is not yet recognized. The just and the unjust have the same appearance. Both are men, but not both the house of God, even if both are called Christians. Both are a vessel, but not both for honor, but one for honor, the other for dishonor.

**3.** Is the great house to be abandoned on account of the evil vessels? God knows how to use, that is, the Lord of the great house, both the vessels for honor and the vessels for dishonor. As it is the mark of the wicked to use even good things wickedly, so, on the contrary, it is the mark of God to use even the wicked well. How many good things the wicked use! For every creature of God is good. How do the wicked use them wickedly? As Scripture rebukes them, saying, *You ask, and do not receive, because you ask wickedly, that you may consume it in your lusts*[^6]. What name did these who use the good things of God wickedly receive? It follows and says, *Adulterers*. Whence adulterers? *Do you not know that the friend of this world is constituted an enemy of God? Adulterers,* it says[^7]. There are adulterous souls, there are fornicating souls: let us examine them. There are fornicating souls, in a certain way prostituted to many false gods; but adulterous souls, as though already joined in lawful wedlock, and yet not keeping the chastity of the soul for that lawful wedlock. But that I may say this more expressly, the soul of a pagan is fornicating, that of a wicked Christian is adulterous. The fornicating soul of a pagan does not have a lawful husband; prostituted through diverse demons, it is corrupted. But why is the soul of a wicked Christian adulterous? Because it neither loves chastity nor forsakes its husband. Do not, then, say, "Why are these in the house of God?" It is answered you: They are vessels for dishonor. God knows how to use them. He who created knew, since he who could create knew how to order: they have their own place in the great house. But if you ask me how God uses them well, I confess, I cannot, as a man, unfold the counsel of God. For I know how to be awestruck with the apostle Paul, who too, when he considered it, was awestruck, and, awestruck, cried out, *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments, and inscrutable his ways! For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, that it should be repaid to him? For from him and through him and in him are all things: to him be glory forever and ever*[^8]. For us consideration, admiration, trembling, exclamation, because no penetration. But for him what? *Glory forever and ever*. Whether of the vessels for honor or of the vessels for dishonor, to him *glory forever and ever*. Some he crowns, others he condemns, nowhere does he err. Some he proves, by others he proves, all he orders.

**4.** "What," someone says, "do evil men do in this world?" Answer me. In the goldsmith's furnace, what does the chaff do? I think the chaff is not there without cause, where the gold is purged. Let us see what things are all there: there is the furnace, there is the chaff, there is the gold, there is the fire, there is the craftsman. But those three, gold, chaff, fire, are in the furnace; the craftsman, at the furnace. Attend also to this world. The world is a furnace; the chaff, evil men; the gold, good men; the fire, tribulation; the craftsman, God. Attend and see: the gold is not purged, if the chaff is not burned. But see in this psalm, where we love the beauty of the house of God and the place of the dwelling of his glory, see there the gold, see the voice of the gold. It desires to be purged: *Prove me, Lord, and test me, burn my reins*[^9]. *Prove me, Lord, and test me. Prove me,* he says, *Lord, and test me.* He who ought to have feared the test asks for the test. *Prove,* he says, *me, Lord, and test me.* And see whether he does not seek the fire: *Prove me and test me, burn my reins and my heart*. Do you not fear lest you fail in the fire? No, he says. Why? *Because your mercy is before my eyes*[^10]. Behold, he says, why I securely say, *Prove me, Lord, and test me, burn my reins and my heart*—not because I am fit by my own strength to bear the fire of testing, but *because your mercy is before my eyes*. You, he says, who granted to me that I should be proved gold, will you permit me to perish in the furnace? Surely you send me to be purged, you draw me out purged. May the Lord guard your going in and your going out. See that very going out, see the entering into the furnace. *Count it all joy, my brothers, when you fall into various temptations*[^11]. Behold, you have heard the entering, seek the going out. For it is easy to enter; to go out is a great thing. But do not fear: *God is faithful*—surely, since you had entered, you were thinking about the going out—*God is faithful, who does not permit you to be tempted beyond what you can, but will also make with the temptation a way out*. What is, *a way out? That you may be able to endure*[^12]. You entered, you fell in, you endured, you went out.

**5.** A great material for the purging of the good is the abundance of the wicked. For in the multitude of the wicked, although the good lie hidden, mixed in, *the Lord knows who are his*[^13]. Under the hand of so great a craftsman, a speck of gold cannot perish in a great heap of chaff. How much chaff there, how little gold? But do not fear; so great is the craftsman that he can purge, he cannot destroy. See the gold, the blessed Apostle, in this furnace of this world, how he is proved by dangers—that we may come to the unhonored vessels that are within, which the Lord of the great house knows how to use well. The Apostle, then, when he was being proved by dangers, what did he say? *In dangers on the sea, in dangers in the desert, in dangers from my race, in dangers from the gentiles*[^14]. These are all without. Attend within: *In dangers among false brothers*[^15]. I address, then, the gold of God, I address the vessels made for honor, I address the grains laboring in the threshing of the floor among the chaff. To you I say, whoever hears—not me, but through me: Be good, bear with the evil. I do not want you to say, "Who is good?" Rather, I want you to say even this: that however good you are, you will not be without some evil. Whence it is most rightly said, *No one is good but God alone*[^16]. But that good one who does good things is God. If, then, God is good who does good things, and he alone is the good maker of good things, how is he the maker of good things, if no man is good? According to his small measure, then, a man too is good in proportion. And if he were not, the Lord himself would not say, *A good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good things*[^17].

**6.** Be good, then, and bear with the evil. Be good simply, and bear with the evil doubly. Good, only within: for if not within, nowhere good. Be good, then, within, bear with the evil both without and within. Without, bear with the heretic, bear with the pagan. Bear also within with the evil Christian, because *a man's enemies are those of his own household*[^18]. Patient with many troublesome evil people within, you fret, you are indignant, as though the time of winnowing had already come. You are placed in the threshing, you are still in the threshing, the floor is still being threshed. Still the lapfuls and the sheaves, as the nations believe, are being gathered to the floor. Do you think you can be wheat alone on the floor? You err. Groan on the floor, that you may rejoice in the barn. Many evils are done by evil Christians. Those who are outside and are unwilling to be Christians find occasions for excuses. To one who exhorts him to believe, he answers these things: "Do you want me to be what this one and that one is?" And he names this one and that one. Sometimes he even says the truth. But when the truth cannot be found, what great thing is it to slander? When that one does not fear to slander, he makes another suspect what he does not see. When you hear a man saying these things, perhaps because you know your brothers to be evil, you say within yourself, "He speaks the truth." *In dangers among false brothers*[^19]. But do not fail. What that man seeks, be it. Be a good Christian, that you may convict the slanderous pagan.

**7.** But that one slanders even the good. He says false things, and generally he is believed. What does the gold do? On every side is chaff, fire. Put down the filth, not the faith. Be cleaner, by that very exercise be cleaner. Let him avail you who takes away what makes you filthier, not who oppresses what is gold. For if you fail, you perish in the chaff, and if you perish in the chaff, you were not gold, but pretended to be gold. *The Lord knows who are his*[^20]. But those who are evil, of whom you blush when you are among the evil who are outside, remember that they are not, in the great house where you are, a vessel for honor, but for dishonor. The Apostle instructed you, let God govern you. If there were no evil men for whom we might pray, when would it be said to us, *Pray for your enemies*[^21]? Or would we perhaps wish to have good enemies? How can that be? You will not have a good enemy, unless you are evil. But if you are good, your enemy will be none but an evil one. Pray for your enemies. Therefore, you good, pray for the evil. Return to your heart, O you who are being purged in this furnace, if your voice could be, *Prove me, Lord, and test me; burn my reins and my heart, because your mercy is before my eyes*[^22]: behold, return to your heart. You are under God, you are about to pour out a prayer. There comes to your mind the one who harmed you, there comes the one who oppressed you, there comes the one who despoiled you, there comes the one who put you in prison. Come now, attend to your heart, look to your Lord. Behold your evil enemy, behold your good Lord. Your evil enemy harms you. Pray for your enemy, your good Lord says to you. Between your evil enemy and your good Lord, what will you do? Will you pray against the one, or obey the other?

**8.** You undertake, by the command of your Lord, to pray for that malignant enemy of yours. What will you do? The Lord has commanded, has commanded hard things, but has promised great things. What hard things has he commanded? *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute you*[^23]. They are hard, but *on account of the words of your lips I have kept hard ways*[^24]. Whence, by my own strength, to keep hard ways, except *because your mercy is before my eyes*[^25]? Behold, he has commanded hard things, has commanded bitter things, see what he has promised: *Pray for those who persecute you, that you may be sons of your Father who is in heaven*[^26]. If he said to you, Pray for your enemy that you may be a son of your father, lest your fleshly father disinherit you—who is going to leave you that which he will not carry away from here—you would fear, and would do it. To you it is promised, for these hard things, that you may be a son of the Most High. Think of the Father and recognize the inheritance. Speak, then, begin to pray for that great enemy of yours, who has done you many evils, who has heaped up many hard things upon you, begin to pray for him, and see your heart quarreling with you. In that, then, which you wish, which pleases you, in which you delight according to the inner man, you obey your Lord, and pray for your enemy, you are gold. But in that, when you have begun to pray, fleshly weakness begins to quarrel with you, that is the filth, from which God wishes to purge you in the furnace.

**9.** Be exercised, then, in the midst of the evil, O good man, if you are good—good not from your own, because you were evil, but from his who is never evil—be exercised in the midst of the evil. And I do not want you to say to me, "At least, if it were necessary for our exercise that there be evil men, let them be few, let the evil be few, and the good many." Do you not attend that, if they were few, they would not harm the many? Surely consider, prudent man, that if there were many good and few evil, the few evil would not dare to harm the many good. But if they did not dare, they would not exercise them. But now, because there are many evil, the few good labor among the many evil; and when there is labor there is sweat; and when there is sweat the gold is purged. Be, then, for the beauty of the house of God. Now weakness has quarreled with you in your heart. Call upon him, that you may conquer. May God be present to you, may he help who commands. Now you have become victor over your weakness, now you have taken up the spirit and the fruit of praying for your enemy. See what good there is. Compare him to yourself. He meditates temptations, you pour out prayers. He, if he harms, harms openly; what you pray for him, God knows. He does not believe, because he does not examine your heart. When, then, he openly harms, you secretly pray. In this winepress—because the Church too is compared to a winepress—see whether he who openly harms is not the dregs running through the public street. The dregs run through the public street, but the oil has hidden passages to its seat. And though it passes hiddenly, in its greatness it appears. For how many, my brothers, how many in this conflict of affairs, in the malice of this world, in this abundance of evils, have withdrawn themselves and turned to God, and bidden farewell to the world, and begun suddenly to give their goods to the poor, who a little before were seizing the goods of others! But many robbers, invaders, despoilers appear publicly: those are the dregs running through the streets. But those, one here, one there, joined in heart, the evil one being ashamed to remain doing evil, thinking on the warnings of God, mocking the hope of the world, awaiting the heavenly hope, changing loves and ways, are oil in the winepress of holiness, are a vessel for honor in the great house, are gold in the furnace, are grain in the barn. There is the beauty of the house of God.
