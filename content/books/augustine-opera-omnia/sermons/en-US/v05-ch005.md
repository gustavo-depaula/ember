# Sermon 152

**1.** Your Charity ought to remember that I discoursed to you on a most difficult question from the Epistle of the apostle Paul, where he says, *For I do not do the good that I will, but the evil that I hate, that I do*[^1]. Those of you, then, who were present, remember; be present now in mind, that you may build upon what you heard. For there follows the reading that has been recited today, which the reader began here: *God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and from sin condemned sin in the flesh; that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit*[^2]. But the words that were read then, and not treated, are these that follow: *So then I myself serve the law of God with the mind, but the law of sin with the flesh. There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. For the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and death. For what was impossible for the law, in which it was weakened through the flesh*[^3]. And there follows what was read today: *God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh*. The difficulty is not in obscure meanings, when the spirit helps. May he therefore help us, as you pray; for the very desire by which you wish to understand is a prayer to God. From him, then, you must look for help. For we, like peasants in a field, work on the outside. But if there were none who worked on the inside, neither would the seed be fixed in the earth, nor would the top spring up in the field, nor would the shoot be strengthened and reach the trunk; nor would branches, nor fruit, nor leaves be born. So the Apostle himself said, distinguishing the work of the workers and of the Creator, *I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase*. And he added, *So neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^4]. If God does not give the increase within, this sound is empty to your ears. But if he does give it, what we plant and water has some value, and our labor is not empty.

**2.** I have already told you that what the Apostle says is to be taken thus: *I serve the law of God with the mind, but the law of sin with the flesh*; so that you permit the flesh nothing more than the longings without which it cannot be. But if you consent to the evil longings, and do not wrestle against them, conquered you will lament; and it is to be wished that you lament, lest you also lose the very sense of grief. As much, then, as lies in our prayers, in our will, in our supplication, when we say, *Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil*[^5], this surely we desire: that not even those evil longings themselves should arise from our flesh. But as long as we live here, we are not able to bring it about. On account of this he says, *But to accomplish the good I do not find*[^6]. What do I find to do? Not to consent to the evil longing. *To accomplish I do not find*: that is, not to have an evil longing. It remains, then, in this warfare, that with your mind not consenting to the evil desires you serve the law of God, but with your flesh coveting, yet you not consenting, you serve the law of sin. The flesh does its longings; do you also do yours. Its longings are not crushed, not extinguished by you; let it not extinguish yours; that in the struggle you may labor, not, conquered, be dragged along.

**3.** There follows, then, and the Apostle says, *There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus*[^7]. Even if they have the desires of the flesh, to which they do not consent; even if the law in their members fights against the law of their mind, and wishes to take the mind captive; yet because through the grace of Baptism and the washing of regeneration the very guilt with which you were born has been loosed, and whatever you previously consented to of evil desire—whether by any shameful deed, or any crime, or any evil thought, or any evil speech—all has been blotted out in that fountain, into which you entered a slave, from which you came out free; because, then, these things are so: *There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus*. There is now none; before there was. By one, all into condemnation[^8]. This evil generation had wrought, but this good regeneration wrought. *For the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^9]. It is present in your members, but does not make you guilty. You have been freed from it; free, fight; but see that you are not conquered, and become a slave again. You labor by fighting, but you will rejoice by triumphing.

**4.** But I told you—and you ought especially to remember this—lest, perchance, on account of this struggle, without which a human being cannot exist (even one who lives righteously; rather, it is the one who lives righteously who is in it, for the one who does not live righteously does not fight, but is dragged along)—lest, then, you suppose on this account that there are, as it were, two natures from diverse principles, as the Manicheans rave, as though the flesh were not from God. That is false; both are from God. But human nature deserved this within itself as a result of sin. It is therefore a sickness; it is healed, and it ceases to be. The discord that now exists between spirit and flesh labors for the sake of concord; the spirit labors in order that the flesh may agree with it. Just as if in one house a man and his wife have a quarrel between them, the husband must labor to tame the wife. Let the wife, once tamed, be subject to the husband; the wife being made subject to the husband, let there be peace in the house.

**5.** But when he had said, *The law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^10], he set before us these laws themselves to be understood. Look at them, and distinguish them; this distinction is necessary enough for you. *The law*, he says, *of the spirit of life*—behold, one law: *has freed you from the law of sin and death*—behold, another law. And there follows, *For what was impossible for the law, in which it was weakened through the flesh*[^11]—behold, a third law. Or perhaps this is one of the two? Let us inquire and, with the Lord's help, see. What did he say about that good law? *The law of the spirit of life has freed you from the law of sin and death*. He did not say that this law was powerless to accomplish anything: *The law of the spirit of life has freed you*, he says, *from the law of sin and death*. That good law has freed you from this evil law. For what is the evil law? *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and taking me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^12]. Why is this too called a law? With perfect rightness. For it was done most justly that the man who was unwilling to obey his Lord should have his own flesh disobey him. Above you is your Lord, below you is your flesh. Serve the higher, that the lower may serve you. You despised the higher; you are tormented by the lower. This, then, is the law of sin, which is also the law of death. For through sin came death. *On the day you eat, you shall die the death*[^13]. This law of sin, then, drags the spirit and strives to subjugate it. *But I delight in the law of God according to the inner man*[^14]. And through this comes that struggle, and in that very contest it is said, *I serve the law of God with the mind, but the law of sin with the flesh. The law of the spirit of life has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^15]. For how did the law of the spirit of life itself free you? First of all, it gave the forgiveness of sins. For this is the law of which it is said to God in the Psalm, *And in your law have mercy on me*[^16]. The law of mercy, the law of faith, not of works. What, then, is the law of works? You have already heard the good law of faith: *The law of the spirit of life has freed you from the law of sin and death*. You have heard too the other law, of sin and death. *For what was impossible for the law, in which it was weakened through the flesh*. This law, then, named in the third place, somehow does not fulfill anything, as it were; but that law of the spirit of life fulfilled it, because it freed you from the law of sin and death. So this law, named in the third place, the very Law that was given to the people through Moses on Mount Sinai, is called the law of works. It knows how to threaten, not to help; to command, not to aid. It is the one that said, *You shall not covet*. Hence the Apostle says, *I would not have known coveting, unless the law said, You shall not covet*. And what did it profit me that the law said, *You shall not covet? Sin, taking occasion through the commandment, deceived me, and through it killed me*[^17]. I was forbidden to covet, and I did not fulfill the commands, but was conquered. Before the law I was a sinner; the law being received, I became a transgressor. *For sin, taking occasion through the commandment, deceived me, and through it killed me*.

**6.** *And so*, he says, *the law indeed is holy*. This law too, then, is good (for the Manicheans reproach it as well, just as they do the flesh). Of it the Apostle says, *And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good. Did that which is good, then, become death to me? Far from it! But sin, that it might appear as sin, worked death in me through what is good*. These are the Apostle's words; see and attend. *And so the law indeed is holy*. What is so holy as *You shall not covet*? The transgression of the law would not be evil unless the law itself were good. For if it were not good, it would not be evil to transgress an evil thing. But because it is evil to transgress it, therefore it is good. What is so good as *You shall not covet? The law*, then, *is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*. How he satisfies, how he drives it home! He cries out, as it were, against the slanderers. What do you say, Manichean? Is the law that was given through Moses evil? "It is evil," they say. O monsters! O effrontery! You said once: It is evil. Hear the Apostle saying, *The law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*. Will you be silent at last? *Did that which is good*, he says, *then become death to me? Far from it! But sin, that it might appear as sin, worked death in me through what is good*. And here too, *through what is good*; thus he accuses the guilty party in such a way that he does not depart from the praise of the law. *Through what is good*, he says, *it worked death in me*. Through what good? The commandment. Through what good? The law. How did it work death? *That sin might appear; that sin, sinning through the commandment, might become beyond measure*. Hence: *beyond measure*. When he sinned without the commandment, it was less; when he sins through the commandment, he exceeds the measure. For when someone is not forbidden, he thinks he does well. Forbidden, he begins to be unwilling to do it; he is conquered, dragged, subjugated; it now remains for him to call upon grace, because he could not keep the law.

**7.** And through this, that law of which it was said, *For the law of the spirit of life has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^18], is the law of faith, the law of the Spirit, the law of grace, the law of mercy. But that law of sin and death is not the law of God, but of sin and death. But that other law, of which the Apostle says, *The law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*[^19], is the law of God, but of works, the law of works; the law of works, which commands but does not help; the law that shows you sin, but does not take it away. By one law sin is shown to you, by another it is taken away. There are two Testaments, the Old and the New. Hear the Apostle saying, *Tell me, you who wish to be under the law, have you not read the law? For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the slave woman and one by the free woman. But he who was by the slave woman was born according to the flesh, while he who was by the free woman was born through the promise; which things are an allegory. For these are the two Testaments: one indeed on Mount Sinai, bearing children for slavery, which is Hagar*[^20]—the slave of Sarah, who was given to Abraham, and bore Ishmael the slave. The Old Testament, then, pertaining to Hagar, *bears children for slavery. But the Jerusalem that is above is free, which is our mother*[^21]. Therefore the children of grace are children of the free woman; the children of the letter are children of the slave woman. Seek the children of the slave woman: *The letter kills*. Seek the children of the free woman: *But the Spirit gives life*[^22]. *The law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and death*, from which the law of the letter could not free you. *For what was impossible for the law, in which it was weakened through the flesh*[^23]. For your flesh was rebelling, your flesh was subjugating you; it heard the law, and only stirred up your coveting the more. The law of the letter, then, was weakened through the flesh; and through this it was impossible for the law of the letter to free you from the law of sin and death.

**8.** *God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh*[^24]—not in the flesh of sin. In flesh indeed, but not in the flesh of sin. All the rest of the flesh of all human beings, then, is the flesh of sin; his alone is not the flesh of sin, because concupiscence did not conceive him, but grace; yet it has the likeness of sinful flesh, whence it could also be nourished, and hunger, and thirst, and sleep, and grow weary, and die. *In the likeness of sinful flesh God sent his Son*.

**9.** *And from sin he condemned sin in the flesh*. From what sin? What sin? *From sin he condemned sin in the flesh; that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us*. Now let that righteousness of the law be fulfilled in us; now let that righteousness which is commanded be fulfilled in us through the Spirit who helps; that is, let the law of the letter be fulfilled in us through the spirit of life: *who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit*[^25]. From what sin, then, did the Lord condemn what sin? I see, I do indeed see what sin he condemned, I see it plainly: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world*[^26]. What sin? Every sin; he condemned all our sin. But from what sin? He himself had no sin; of him it is said, *He committed no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth*[^27]. None at all, neither by inheriting nor by adding; he had no sin, neither of origin nor of his own iniquity. The virgin demonstrates his origin; and his holy manner of life sufficiently shows that he did nothing for which he was worthy of death. So he says, *Behold, the prince of this world comes* (signifying the devil), *and in me he will find nothing*. The prince of death will find no reason to kill me. And why, then, do you die? *But that all may know that I do the will of my Father, let us go from here*[^28]. And he went on to the suffering of death, a voluntary death, not of necessity but of choice. *I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again. No one takes it from me, but I lay it down and take it up again*[^29]. If you marvel at the power, understand the majesty. As God speaks, so Christ speaks.

**10.** From what sin, then, did he condemn sin? Some have understood it and reached a sense not to be condemned. Yet what the Apostle meant, so far as it seems to me, they could not at all track down. They said something not bad, however; this I tell you first, and then what seems to me, and what divine Scripture itself shows to be most true. When they were troubled: From what sin did he condemn sin? Did he have sin? this they said: *From sin he condemned sin*, from a sin not his own; yet *from sin he condemned sin*. If, then, not from his own, from whose? From the sin of Judas, from the sin of the Jews. For whence did he shed his blood for the forgiveness of sins? Because he was crucified by the Jews. By whose betrayal? Judas's. When the Jews killed him, Judas betrayed him. Did they do well, or did they sin? They sinned. Behold from what sin he condemned sin. It was well said, and truly said, that even from the sin of the Jews Christ condemned all sin, because, as they persecuted him, he shed the blood by which he blotted out all sin. Yet see what the Apostle says in another place: *For Christ*, he says, *we perform an embassy, as though God were exhorting through us; we beseech you for Christ*—that is, as though Christ besought you, on his behalf we beseech you—*be reconciled to God*. And there follows, *Him who knew no sin*. God to whom we beseech you to be reconciled, him who knew no sin—that is, Christ God, that Christ who knew no sin—*he made sin for us, that we might be the righteousness of God in him*[^30]. Can the sin of Judas here be understood, the sin of the Jews, the sin of any other person, when you hear, *Him who knew no sin he made sin for us*? Who? Whom? God made Christ, God made Christ sin for us. He did not say: He made him a sinner for us; but, *he made him sin*. If it is wicked to say that Christ sinned, who could bear that Christ is sin? And yet we cannot contradict the Apostle. We cannot say to him: What is it you are saying? For if we say this to the Apostle, we say it to Christ himself. For he says in another place, *Or do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ*[^31]?

**11.** What, then, is it? Let your Charity attend to a great and deep mystery. You will be blessed if you have loved the understanding of it and have reached the beloved object. Truly, truly our Lord Christ, Jesus our Savior, our Redeemer, was made sin, that we might be the righteousness of God in him. How? Hear the law. Those who know it know what I am saying; and those who do not know, let them read it, or hear it. In the law even the sacrifices that were offered for sins were called sins. You have it that, when a victim was brought for sin, the law says, *Let the priests place their hands upon the sin*—that is, upon the victim for sin. And what else is Christ but a sacrifice for sin? *Just as Christ also*, he says, *loved you and handed himself over for you, an offering and sacrifice to God for an odor of sweetness*[^32]. Behold from what sin he condemned sin: from the sacrifice that he became for sins, from that he condemned sin. This is the *law of the spirit of life* that *has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^33]. For that other law, the law of the letter, the law that commands, is indeed good—*the commandment holy and just and good*—but *it was weakened through the flesh*, and what it commanded could not be fulfilled in us. Let another law, then, as I had begun to say, show you sin, and another take it away; let the law of the letter show sin, let the law of grace take sin away.
