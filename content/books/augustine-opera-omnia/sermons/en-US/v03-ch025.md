# Sermon 67: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 11:25: "I Confess to You, Father, Lord of Heaven and Earth, Because You Have Hidden These Things from the Wise," and What Follows

**1. 1.** When the holy Gospel was read, we heard that the Lord Jesus exulted in the Spirit, and said: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones*[^1]. So far the words of the Lord, if worthily, if diligently, if—what is first—piously we consider them; we find first that not always, when we read in the Scriptures "confession," ought we to understand the voice of a sinner. But especially this had to be said, and your love admonished of it: because, as soon as this word sounded in the mouth of the Reader, there followed also the sound of the beating of your breast, namely when you heard what the Lord says: *I confess to you, Father.* At this very thing that sounded: *I confess*, you beat your breasts. But to beat the breast, what is it, except to accuse what lies hidden in the breast, and by an evident blow to chastise the hidden sin? Why did you do this, except because you heard: *I confess to you, Father? I confess*, you heard: who confesses, you did not attend to. Now, then, notice. If *I confess* was said by Christ, from whom all sin is far; it is not only of a sinner, but sometimes also of one praising. We confess, then, either by praising God, or by accusing ourselves. Both confessions are pious, whether when you reprove yourself, who are not without sin; or when you praise him who cannot have sin.

**1. 2.** But if we think well, your reproof is his praise. For why do you now confess in the accusation of your sin? in the accusation of yourself why do you confess, except because from being dead you have become alive? For Scripture says: *From a dead man, as from one who is not, confession perishes*[^2]. If confession perishes from a dead man, he who confesses lives: and if he confesses sin, surely he has come back to life from death. If the confessor of sin has come back to life from death, who raised him up? No dead man is the raiser of himself. He could raise himself who, with the flesh dead, was not dead. For he raised what had been dead. He raised himself who lived in himself, but in his flesh to be raised was dead. For not the Father alone raised the Son, of whom it was said by the Apostle: *Wherefore God also exalted him*[^3]: but the Lord also raised himself, that is, his body; whence he says: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^4]. But the sinner is dead, especially he whom the mass of habit presses, as Lazarus was, as it were, buried[^5]. For it was too little that he was dead; he was also buried. Whoever, then, is pressed by the mass of bad habit, of a bad life, that is, of earthly cravings, so that there is now done in him what is said miserably in a certain psalm: *The fool said in his heart, There is no God*[^6]; he becomes such a one as that of whom it was said: *From a dead man, as from one who is not, confession perishes.* Who will raise him, except he who, the stone removed, cried out, saying: *Lazarus, come forth?*[^7] But what is it to come forth, except to bring forth what was hidden? He who confesses comes forth. He could not come forth, unless he lived: he could not live, unless he were raised up. So in confession the accusation of oneself is the praise of God.

**2. 3.** So someone says, What does the Church profit, if the confessor, now raised up by the Lord's voice, comes forth? What does the Church profit the one confessing, to whom the Lord says: *What you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven too?*[^8] Attend to Lazarus himself: he comes forth with bonds. He already lived by confessing; but he did not yet walk free, entangled with bonds. What, then, does the Church do, to which it was said: *What you loose will be loosed*; except what the Lord at once says to the disciples: *Loose him, and let him go?*[^9]

**2. 4.** So whether we accuse ourselves, or praise God, we praise God twice. If we piously accuse ourselves, we surely praise God. When we praise God, we proclaim him as one who is without sin: but when we accuse ourselves, we give glory to him through whom we have risen. If you do this, the enemy finds no occasion by which to circumvent you before the judge. For when you yourself are the accuser, and the Lord the deliverer; what will he be, except a slanderer? Deservedly that man provided himself a defense here against enemies, not conspicuous ones, flesh and blood, rather to be pitied than to be guarded against; but against those enemies against whom the Apostle exhorts us to be armed: *Our struggle is not against flesh and blood*[^10]: that is, against the men whom you see raging against you. They are vessels, another uses them: they are instruments, another plays them. *The devil entered*, it says, *into the heart of Judas, that he might betray the Lord*[^11]. Someone says: What then have I done? Hear the Apostle: *Neither give place to the devil*[^12]. You by your evil will gave place: he entered, possessed, uses it. If you gave no place, he would not possess.

**3. 5.** So admonishing us he says: *Our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers*[^13]. Someone might think against the kings of the earth, against the powers of the age. Why? Are they not flesh and blood? Once it was said, *not against flesh and blood.* Turn away from every man. What enemies, then, remain? *Against principalities and powers of spiritual wickedness, the rulers of the world*[^14]. He gave, as it were, more to the devil and his angels. He gave more, he called them rulers of the world. But lest you understand it wrongly, he explains what the world is, of which they are the rulers. *The rulers of the world, of these darknesses*[^15]. What is, *of the world of these darknesses?* The world is full of those of whom it is the ruler, his lovers and unbelievers. These the Apostle calls darknesses. The rulers of these, the devil and his angels. These darknesses are not natural, are not unchangeable: they are changed, and made light; they believe, and by believing are enlightened. When this has been done in them, they will hear: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^16]. For when darkness, not in the Lord: again when light, not in you, but in the Lord. For what do you have that you have not received? Because, then, there are invisible enemies, they are to be fought invisibly. Indeed you conquer a visible enemy by striking: you conquer an invisible one by believing. The enemy man is visible; to strike too is visible: the enemy devil is invisible; to believe too is invisible. So there is an invisible fight against invisible enemies.

**4. 6.** From these enemies how does a certain one say himself safe? For this I had begun to say, and had need to treat at some length about these enemies. Now, then, the enemies being known, let us see the defense. *Praising I will call upon the Lord, and I shall be safe from my enemies*[^17]. You have what to do. Praising, call upon him: but praising the Lord, call upon him. For if you praise yourself, you will not be safe from your enemies. Praising the Lord, call upon him, and you will be safe from your enemies. For what does the Lord himself say? *The sacrifice of praise will glorify me; and there is the way, where I will show him the salvation of God*[^18]. Where is the way? In the sacrifice of praise. Do not set your foot outside this way. Be in the way: do not depart from the way; from the praise of the Lord not even a nail's breadth, much less a foot. For if you wish to deviate from this way, and to praise yourself instead of the Lord, you will not be safe from those enemies: because of them it was said: *Beside the path they set scandals for me*[^19]. So whatever good you think you have from yourself, you have deviated from the praise of God. Why do you now wonder if the enemy seduces you, when you are the seducer of yourself? Hear the Apostle: *For he who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, deceives himself*[^20].

**4. 7.** So attend to the Lord confessing: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^21]. *I confess to you*, I praise you. I praise you, I do not accuse myself. But as far as concerns the assumption of the man himself, it is wholly grace, singular grace, perfect grace. What did that man who is Christ merit, if you take away grace, and so great a grace, by which there had to be one Christ, and that one whom we know? Take away this grace, what is Christ but a man? what but what you are? He assumed a soul, assumed a body, assumed a complete man: he fits it to himself, the Lord makes one person with the servant. How great this grace? Christ in heaven, Christ on earth: Christ at once both in heaven and on earth: nor two Christs, but the same Christ both in heaven and on earth. Christ with the Father, Christ in the womb of the virgin: Christ on the cross, Christ among those below helping certain ones: but that same day Christ in paradise with the confessing robber. And there what did the robber merit, except that he held that way where he showed his salvation? From which let not your foot depart. For in that he accused himself, he praised God, and made his life blessed. He presumed indeed upon the Lord, and said to him: *Lord, remember me, when you come into your kingdom*[^22]. For he considered his crimes, and held it a great thing if he were spared even at the end. But the Lord at once, when he said: *Remember me;* but when? *When you come into your kingdom: Amen*, he said, *I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise*[^23]. Mercy offered what misery deferred.

**5. 8.** So hear the Lord confessing: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth.* What do I confess? in what do I praise you? For this confession, as I said, has praise. *Because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones*[^24]. What is this, brothers? Understand it from the contrary sense. *You have hidden these things*, he says, *from the wise and prudent:* and he did not say: You have revealed them to the foolish and imprudent: but he said: *You have hidden* them indeed *from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones.* To the wise and prudent who are to be mocked, the arrogant who are falsely great, but truly swollen, he opposed not the unwise, not the imprudent, but the little ones. Who are the little ones? The humble. So: *You have hidden these things from the wise and prudent.* By the name of wise and prudent, he himself explained that the proud are understood, when he said: *You have revealed them to little ones.* So: You have hidden them not from little ones. What is, not from little ones? Not from the humble. What is, not from the humble, except from the proud? O way of the Lord! either it did not exist, or it lay hidden, to be revealed to us. Whence did the Lord exult? Because it was revealed to little ones. We ought to be little ones; for if we wish to be great, as it were wise and prudent, that is not revealed to us. Who are the great? The wise and prudent. *Saying they were wise, they became fools*[^25]. You have a remedy from the contrary. If by saying you were wise you became a fool; say you are a fool, and you will be wise. But say it: say it, and within say it; because so it is as you say. If you say it, do not say it before men, and not say it before God. Utterly, as concerns yourself, as concerns your things, you are dark. For what else is it to be a fool, except to be dark in the heart? Finally, of those he says thus: *Saying they were wise, they became fools.* Before they said this, what else? *And their foolish heart was darkened*[^26]. Say that you are not light to yourself. At most, you are an eye; you are not light. What does an eye, open and sound, profit, if light is lacking? So say that you are not light from yourself to yourself; and cry out what is written: *You will light my lamp, Lord:* by your light: *Lord, you will lighten my darkness*[^27]. For mine is nothing but darkness: but you are the light driving away darkness, enlightening me: not being light from myself to myself; but light not by participation, except in you.

**5. 9.** So John too, the friend of the bridegroom, was thought to be Christ, was thought to be light. *He was not the light; but that he might bear testimony to the light*[^28]. But what was the light? *It was the true light.* What is true? *Which enlightens every man.* If the true light which enlightens every man; then John too, speaking rightly, confessing rightly: *We have received of his fullness*[^29]. See whether he said anything other than: *You will light my lamp, Lord*[^30]. Finally, now enlightened, he bore testimony. On account of the blind, the lamp of the day bore testimony. See that he is a lamp: *You*, he says, *sent to John, and you wished to exult for an hour in his light: he was a lamp burning and shining*[^31]. *He a lamp*, that is, a thing enlightened, kindled that it might shine. What can be kindled can also be extinguished. But that it may not be extinguished, let it not suffer the wind of pride. So: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent*, those thinking themselves light, and they were darkness; and by the very fact that they were darkness, and thought themselves light, they could not even be enlightened. But those who were darkness, and confessed themselves to be darkness, were little ones, not great; were humble, not proud. So they rightly said: *You will light my lamp, Lord.* They acknowledged themselves, they praised the Lord, they did not depart from the way of salvation. Praising the Lord they called upon him, and were safe from their enemies[^32].

**5. 10.** Turned to the Lord God, the Father almighty, with a pure heart let us give him, as far as our littleness can, the greatest and most abundant thanks; praying with our whole soul his singular gentleness, that he may deign to hear our prayers in his good pleasure, that by his power he may drive the enemy from our acts and thoughts, that he may multiply for us faith, govern the mind, grant spiritual thoughts, and lead us to his blessedness, through Jesus Christ his Son, Amen.
