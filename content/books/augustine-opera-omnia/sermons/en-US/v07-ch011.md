# Sermon 239: In the Days of Easter, on the Resurrection of Christ According to Mark and Luke

**1. 1.** Today, see, we have already heard for the third time from the Gospel the resurrection of our Lord: just as you remember I told you, since this is the custom, that the resurrection of the Lord should be recited according to all the Evangelists. It is the Gospel of Mark that we have just now heard read. Now Mark merited this office, although he was not among the number of the twelve, just as Luke too. For although there are four Evangelists, Matthew, John, Mark, and Luke, two of them are from those twelve apostles, that is, Matthew and John. But the priority of those was not unfruitful, so as not to have companions following. Mark and Luke were not equals of the apostles, but next to equals. For the Holy Spirit willed to choose, even from those who were not among the twelve, two to write the Gospel, lest it should be thought that the grace of evangelizing reached only as far as the apostles, and that the fount of grace failed in them. For since the Lord says of his spirit and of his word, that if anyone receives it and holds it worthily, *there will become in him a fountain of water springing up to eternal life*[^1]: a fountain surely shows itself by flowing out, not by staying still: through the apostles the grace reached others, and they were sent to evangelize. For he who called the first ones, he himself called the second; he himself has called even down to the last age the body of his Only-begotten, that is, the Church spread through the whole world.

**2.** What, then, have we heard Mark saying? That the Lord appeared on the road to two, as Luke also said, whose Gospel we heard yesterday: *He appeared*, he says, *to two on the road in another form*[^2]. But Luke said this same thing in other words, yet did not deviate from the same meaning. For what did Luke say? *Their eyes were held, so that they should not recognize him*[^3]. And what did Mark say? *He appeared to them in another form.* What the one said, *their eyes were held so that they should not recognize him*; this the other said, *in another form.* For another form was seen, their eyes being held, not opened. What then, brothers, since Luke said—what I believe you most recently remember from yesterday's reading—that when he broke the blessed bread, their eyes were opened; what do we think? If their eyes were then opened, then with eyes closed were they accompanying him on the road, and could they know where they were setting their steps, if they had their eyes closed? Their eyes were opened, then, to recognition, not to sight. And so our Lord Jesus Christ, before the breaking of the bread, speaks unknown with the men; in the breaking of the bread he is recognized: because there he is received, where eternal life is received. He is received as a guest, who prepares a house in heaven. For he says, according to the Evangelist John: *There are many dwellings with my Father: otherwise I would have told you, I go to prepare a place for you. But if I go and prepare it, coming again I will take you to myself*[^4]. The Lord of heaven willed to be a guest on earth, a stranger in the world, through whom the world was made: he condescended to be a guest, that you might have a blessing by receiving him; not because he was in need, when he entered as a guest.

**3. 3.** The Lord fed holy Elijah by means of a raven in time of famine: and the birds served him whom men were persecuting. The raven brought the servant of God bread in the morning, and meat in the evening. He was not in need, then, whom God fed by the ministry of birds: and yet, though Elijah was not in need, he is sent to a widow in Zarephath, and it is said to him: *Go to that widow, she will feed you.* Had God failed, that Elijah should be sent to a widow? But if God always provided bread for his servant without human ministry, from where would the widow have her reward? He is sent, then, not in need, to one in need; not hungry, to one hungry; and he says to her: *Go, and bring me a little, that I may eat.* She had a little, which she was about to consume, and to die. She answered how much she had, she made it known to the prophet: and the prophet said to her: *Go, first bring it to me.* She did not doubt, but brought it. She offered refreshment, and merited a blessing. Holy Elijah blessed the jar of meal and the cruse of oil[^5]. That had been stored in the house to be consumed; and that oil hung on a peg to come to an end: a blessing came, and those vessels became treasuries. The little cruse of oil became a fount of oil, the little meal surpassed the most abundant harvests.

**4. 4.** If Elijah was not in need, was Christ in need? Therefore, my brothers, holy Scripture admonishes us that very often God makes those servants of his needy whom he could feed, in order to find people who do works of mercy. Let no one be proud because he gives to the poor: Christ was poor. Let no one be proud because he receives a guest: Christ was a guest. Better is the one received than the one receiving; richer the one accepting than the one handing over. He who was accepting possessed all things: he who was giving had received from the one to whom he gave what he gave. Let no one, then, be proud, my brothers, when he gives to the poor: let him not say in his soul, I give, he receives; I provide shelter, he lacks a roof. Perhaps what you lack is more. Perhaps the one you receive is righteous: he lacks bread, you truth; he lacks a roof, you heaven; he lacks money, you righteousness.

**4. 5.** Be a lender, pay out what you may receive back. Do not fear that God will judge you a usurer. By all means, by all means be a lender. But God says to you: What do you want? Do you want to lend at interest? What is it to lend at interest? To give less, and to receive more. See, give to me, God says to you: I receive less, and give more. What? A hundredfold, and eternal life. The man you seek to whom to give, so that your money may grow—the man you seek, when he receives, rejoices; when he repays, he weeps: to receive, he prays; not to repay, he makes false charges. Give indeed also to a man, and do not turn away from him who asks a loan. But receive only as much as you gave. Let not the one to whom you gave weep: for then you have lost the kindness. And if this very thing that was given, or what he received, is demanded, and perhaps he does not yet have it to hand: you have borne with him when he asked, wait for him when he does not have it: when he has it, he will repay you. Do not bring straits upon the one whose straits you relieved. See, you gave, and you demand: but he does not have the means to repay; when he has it, he will repay you. Do not cry out and say: Surely I am not seeking interest? I ask only as much as I gave: what I gave, this I will receive. You do well, but he does not yet have it. You are not a usurer, and yet you want the one you helped to seek out a usurer, so that he may repay you. If you do not demand interest, lest he have to endure you as a usurer, why do you want him to have to endure another usurer for your sake? You press, you choke: even if you demand only as much as you gave; yet by choking and bringing him into straits, you have not given a kindness, but rather brought greater straits upon him. But perhaps you say: He has the means to repay: he has a house, let him sell it; he has property, let him sell it. When he asked of you, he asked precisely so as not to sell. Let him not do for your sake what you helped him not to do. Let it be so done among men, this God commands, this God wills.

**5. 6.** But are you greedy? God says to you: Be greedy, be as greedy as you can; but apply to me for your greed. God says to you: Apply to me, I made my rich Son poor for you. For our sake *Christ became poor, though he was rich*[^6]. Do you seek gold? he made it. Do you seek silver? he made it. Do you seek servants? he made them. Do you seek cattle? he made them. Do you seek possessions? he made them. Why do you seek only the things he made? Take him who made them. Consider how he loved you. *All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^7]. All things through him, and he himself among all things. He who made all things was made among all things. He who made man was made man: he was made what he had made, lest the one he had made should perish. He who made all things was made among all things. Consider his riches: what is richer than he, through whom all things were made? And yet he, though he was rich, took mortal flesh in the womb of a virgin. He was born an infant, wrapped in infant's swaddling clothes, laid in a manger; patiently he awaited the ages, patiently he endured the times, he through whom the times were made. He nursed, he wailed, he appeared an infant. But he lay there, and he reigned: he was in the manger, and he held the world: he was nursed by his mother, and adored by the gentiles: he was nursed by his mother, and announced by the angels: he was nursed by his mother, and declared by a shining star. Such riches, such poverty: riches, that you might be created; poverty, that you might be restored. That he, then, being poor, was received as a guest like a poor man, was the condescension of the one received, not the misery of one in need.

**6. 7.** Perhaps you say to yourself: O blessed those who merited to receive Christ! O if I had then been alive! O if I had been one of those two whom he found on the road! Be you on the road, and Christ the guest will not be lacking. For do you think it is no longer permitted you to receive Christ? From where, you say, is it permitted? Now, rising, he was manifested to his disciples, he ascended into heaven, there he is at the right hand of the Father; he is not going to come except at the last age, to judge the living and the dead: and he will come in glory, not in weakness; to give the kingdom, not to seek hospitality. When he gives the kingdom, has it slipped your mind what he is going to say: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^8]? He who is rich is needy until the end of the age. He is needy indeed, not in the head, but in his members. Where is he needy? In those over whom he grieved, when he said: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^9] Let us, then, serve Christ. He is with us in his own, he is with us in us: nor did he say in vain: *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the age*[^10]. By doing these things we recognize Christ in good works, not with the body, but with the heart; not with the eyes of the flesh, but with the eyes of faith. *Because you have seen, you have believed*, he said to a certain unbelieving disciple of his, who had said: *I will not believe, unless I touch.* And the Lord: *Come, touch, and do not be unbelieving.* He touched, and cried out: *My Lord and my God!* And the Lord: *Because you have seen me, you have believed*[^11]. This is all your faith, that you believe what you see: I praise those who do not see, and believe; because when they see, they will rejoice.
