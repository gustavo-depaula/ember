# Sermon 211: In Lent

**1.** These holy days which we keep in the observance of Lent admonish us to speak to you of brotherly concord, that whoever has a complaint against another may end it, lest he be ended. Do not despise these things, my brothers. For since this mortal and fragile life, which amid so many earthly temptations is in peril and prays not to be submerged, cannot be in any righteous man without sins of some kind, there is one remedy by which we can live, because God our master taught us to say in prayer: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^1]. We made a pact and agreement with God, and subscribed in a bond to the condition of paying the debt. *Forgive us*, we ask with full confidence, if we too forgive; but if we do not forgive, let us not think our sins forgiven us. Let us not deceive ourselves, let no man deceive himself, God deceives no one. It is human to be angry — and would that we could not even do this — it is human to be angry, but your anger, a short shoot just born, ought not to be watered by suspicions and to grow into the beam of hatred[^2]. For anger is one thing, hatred another. For often even a father is angry with his son, but does not hate his son, he is angry to correct. If he is angry for this, that he may correct, he is angry by loving. Therefore it is said: *You see the speck in your brother's eye; but the beam in your own eye you do not see*[^3]. You blame anger in another, and hatred is held in yourself. In comparison with hatred, anger is a speck; but if you nourish the speck, it will be a beam; if you pluck it out and throw it away, it will be nothing.

**2.** If you noticed... what did you notice? When the letter of blessed John was read, his sentence ought to have terrified you. For he says: *The darkness has passed, the true light now shines*. Then, continuing, he added: *He who says he is in the light and hates his brother is in the darkness even until now*[^4]. But perhaps a man thinks that such darkness is as those shut up in prisons suffer. Would that it were such! And yet in such no one wants to be. But in this darkness of prisons the innocent can be shut up. For in such darkness the martyrs were shut up. Darkness was poured around on every side, and the light flourished in their hearts. In that darkness of prison they did not see with their eyes, but by love of the brotherhood they saw God. Do you wish to know what that darkness is of which it is said: *He who hates his brother is in the darkness even until now*? In another place he says: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^5]. He who hates his brother walks, goes out, comes in, proceeds, laden with no chains, shut up in no prison, yet bound by guilt. Do not think him to be without a prison: his prison is his heart. When you hear: *He who hates his brother is in the darkness even until now*, lest perhaps you despise the darkness, he adds and says: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*. You hate your brother and walk securely and are unwilling to be reconciled, although God gives space for this very reason? Behold, you are already a murderer, and still you live; if you had God angry, with the hatred of your brother you would suddenly be snatched away. God spares you, spare yourself, be reconciled with your brother. But perhaps you are willing and he is not? Let it be enough for you. You have whereof to grieve for him; you have freed yourself. Say, if you are willing to be reconciled and he is not, say securely: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^6].

**3.** Perhaps you have sinned against him, you wish to be reconciled with him, you wish to say to him: Brother, forgive me, I have sinned against you. He is unwilling to forgive, unwilling to release the debt; what you owe him, he is unwilling to release to you. Let him watch out for himself, when he has to pray. When he is to come — he who was unwilling to forgive you what you perhaps sinned against him — when he is to come to prayer, what will he do? Let him say: *Our Father who are in heaven*. Let him say, let him draw near: *Hallowed be your name*. Still say: *Your kingdom come*. Follow on: *Your will be done, as in heaven so on earth*. Still walk on: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^7]. You have said it. What follows, see lest perhaps you wish to pass beyond and say something else. There is no way you can pass by, there you are held. Say, then — or, if you have no reason to say it: *Forgive us our debts*, do not say it. And where is that which the same Apostle said: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^8]? But if the conscience of frailty bites, and in this age there is everywhere an abundance of iniquity, then say: *Forgive us our debts*. But see what follows. For you were unwilling to forgive your brother's sin, and are you going to say: *As we too forgive our debtors*[^9]? Or are you not going to say it? If you are not going to say it, you are going to receive nothing; but if you are going to say it, you are going to say something false. So say it, and say what is true. How will you say what is true, you who were unwilling to release your brother's sin?

**4.** Him I have admonished; now I console you, O whoever you are — if indeed you are one — who have said to your brother: Forgive me what I have sinned against you. If you have said it with the whole heart, with true humility, with unfeigned love, as God sees in the heart whence you said it, but he was unwilling to forgive you, do not be anxious. You are both servants, you have a Lord; you owe to your fellow servant, he was unwilling to forgive you, appeal to the Lord of both. What the Lord has forgiven you, let the servant, if he can, exact[^10]. I say something else. I have admonished him who was unwilling to forgive his brother, when the other asks to be forgiven, to do what he was unwilling to do, lest, when he prays, he not receive what he desires; I have admonished also him who asked pardon for his sin from his brother and did not receive it, that in that which he did not obtain from his brother he should be secure about his Lord. There is also something else that I would admonish. Your brother has sinned against you and was unwilling to say to you: Forgive me what I have sinned against you. This weed abounds; would that God might root it out of his field[^11], that is, out of your hearts! For how many there are who know they have sinned against their brothers and are unwilling to say: Forgive me. They did not blush to sin and they blush to ask; they did not blush at iniquity and they blush at humility. So them first of all I admonish, whoever of you have a discord with your brothers and recall yourselves to yourselves and consider yourselves and bear a just judgment upon yourselves, within in your hearts, and find that you ought not to have done what you did, ought not to have said what you said: ask pardon, brothers, from your brothers, do what the Apostle says: *Forgiving one another, as God too in Christ forgave you*[^12]; do it, do not blush to ask pardon. Accordingly I say to all, men and women, lesser and greater, laymen and clergy, I say also to myself: let us all hear, let us all fear, if we have sinned against our brothers. We have still received a respite of living, we do not die for that reason; for we still live, we are not yet condemned; while we live let us do what the Father commands who will be the judge, and let us ask pardon from the brothers whom perhaps by sinning against them we have somewhat offended, somewhat injured. There are persons humble in the order of this age, from whom, if you ask pardon, they are lifted up in pride. This is what I say: sometimes a master, a man, sins against his servant; for although the one is master, the other servant, yet both are servants of another, because both are redeemed by the blood of Christ — yet it seems hard for me even to command this, to enjoin this, that, if perhaps a master sins against his servant, by unjustly quarreling, by unjustly striking, he should say: Forgive me, give me pardon. Not because he ought not to do it, but lest the other begin to be proud. What then? Before the eyes of God let him repent, before the eyes of God let him punish his own heart, and if he cannot say to the servant, because it is not fitting: Give me pardon, let him address him gently. For a gentle address is a request for pardon.

**5.** It remains that I address those against whom others have sinned, and those who sinned against them have been unwilling to ask pardon. For I have already addressed those who have been unwilling to grant it to brothers asking pardon. Now therefore, since I address you all, because the days are holy, lest your discords remain, I believe that some of you have thought in your hearts, you who know that you have some discords with your brothers and have found that it is not you yourselves who sinned against them, but they against you. Even if you do not speak to me now — for it is mine to speak in this place, but yours to be silent and hear — yet perhaps by thinking you speak and say to yourselves: I want to be reconciled, but he injured me, he sinned against me and is unwilling to ask pardon. What then? Shall I say: Go to him and you too ask pardon? Far be it. I do not want you to lie, I do not want you to say: Give me pardon, you who know yourself not to have sinned against your brother. For what does it profit you, when you would be your own accuser? Why do you seek to be forgiven by one whom you did not injure or against whom you did not sin? It profits you nothing, I do not want you to do it. Do you know, have you well examined, do you know that he sinned against you, not you against him? I know, he says. Let this your knowledge be your sentence. Do not come to your brother who sinned against you and further ask pardon from him. There ought to be among you other peacemakers who rebuke him, that he should first ask pardon from you. Only be you ready to forgive, be wholly ready to forgive from the heart. If you are ready to forgive, you have already forgiven. You still have something to pray: pray for him, that he may ask pardon from you, because you know it harms him if he does not ask, pray for him that he may ask. Say to the Lord in your prayer: Lord, you know that I have not sinned against that brother of mine, but rather that he sinned against me, and that it harms him that he sinned against me if he does not ask pardon from me; I, with a good will, ask that you forgive him.

**6.** Behold, I have told you what... especially through these days of your fasts, of your observances, of your continence, you ought to do, that you may be reconciled with your brothers. May I too rejoice over your peace, I who am saddened by your quarrels, that, *all forgiving one another, if anyone has a complaint against another*[^13], we may securely keep Easter, securely celebrate the passion of him who owed nothing to anyone and paid the price for those who owed — I mean the Lord Jesus Christ, who sinned against no one and against whom almost the whole world sinned, and yet he did not exact punishments but promised rewards. We have him, then, as witness in our hearts, that if we have sinned against anyone, we should ask pardon with a true heart; if anyone has sinned against us, we should be ready to give pardon and should pray for our enemies[^14]. Let us not wait to be avenged, brothers. What is it to be avenged, except to be fed on another's evil? I know that men come daily, bend their knees, knock their foreheads on the ground, sometimes water their face, washed with tears, and in such great humility and disturbance say: Lord avenge me, kill my enemy. Plainly, pray that he may kill your enemy and save your brother; that he may kill the enmity, save the nature. So pray that God may avenge you: let him perish who was persecuting you, but let him remain who may be restored to you.
