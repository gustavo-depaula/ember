# Sermon 170

**1.** All the divine readings are so connected with one another, as though they were one reading; because they all proceed from one mouth. There are many mouths of those carrying out the ministry of the word; but there is one mouth of him who fills the ministers. We heard the apostolic reading, and perhaps something troubles someone in what is written there: *According to the righteousness that is from the law, having been without complaint. The things that were gains to me, these I reckoned losses for the sake of Christ*. Then he goes on and says, *Not only losses, but I even considered them to be dung, that I might gain Christ, and be found in him not having my own righteousness which is from the law, but the righteousness which is from the faith of Jesus Christ*[^1]. For how did he consider it dung and losses, to conduct himself without complaint according to the righteousness that is from the law? For who gave the law? Did not he himself send the law before, who afterward came with forgiveness to those guilty of the law? But we believe that he came with forgiveness to those whom the law held guilty. But did the law hold guilty those who conducted themselves without complaint according to the righteousness that is from the law? If, then, the Lord brought forgiveness and pardon of sins to those guilty of the law, did he not bring it to the apostle Paul, who says he conducted himself in the law without complaint? But let us hear him in another place: *Not from the works*, he says, *which we did, but according to his mercy he saved us, through the washing of regeneration*[^2]. And again: *Who before was a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious; but I obtained mercy*[^3], and the rest. By the one he establishes that he conducted himself in the law without complaint; by the other he confesses that he was such a sinner, that every sinner may for this reason not despair of himself, because Paul deserved forgiveness.

**2.** See, brothers, and consider the force of this saying, how the apostle Paul considers it losses and dung, where he says he conducted himself without complaint. By this a fulfiller of the law, by this guilty of the law, at one and the same time, before Baptism, before grace. But not without cause does he say it is losses; lest harmful thoughts creep in, that the apostle Paul said this because one gave the law, another the Gospel; as the Manichean with perverse mind thinks, and the other heretics, who said that the giver of the law which was given through Moses was one, but the bestower of gospel grace another; that one indeed an evil God, this one a good God. What wonder, brothers? In the obscurity of the law, as it were at closed doors, they suffered darkness; because they did not knock with devotion. We find the same Paul at one time saying most openly that the law is good[^4]; which yet he says was given for this, that sin might abound; and that sin abounded for this, that grace might superabound[^5]. For men presumed on their own strength, and by doing whatever they reckoned was lawful for them, they sinned against the hidden law of God. Hence this manifest law was promulgated to those who did not at all seem guilty to themselves. The law was given to them, not to heal, but to prove them sick. The law ran before the physician, that the sick man, who thought himself healthy, might find himself sick; and it said, *You shall not covet*[^6]. And because before the law was given there was not yet transgression: *For where there is no law*, he says, *neither is there transgression*[^7]; before, one sinned without the law; but the law being given, after one sinned, one sinned more, because one sinned with transgression. Man found himself conquered by his own desires, which by an evil habit he was nourishing against himself; who also had been propagated from Adam with the bond and obligation of sin. Hence the Apostle says, *We too were once by nature children of wrath*[^8]. Hence it is that he says not even a one-day-old infant is clean from sin[^9]; not from what he committed, but from what he contracted.

**3.** Hear the Psalm speaking inner things, and singing the more secret things of our sins. For in the person of the human race it is said to Christ, *Against you only have I sinned, and done what is evil before you*. He says this not in the person of David alone, but in the person of Adam, from whom is the human race. For hear what follows: *Against you*, he says, *only have I sinned, and done what is evil before you; that you may be justified in your words*. It is said to Christ; whence do we understand this? Hear what follows: *And conquer when you are judged*[^10]. God the Father was not judged, God the Holy Spirit was not judged; we find none but the Son alone judged, in that flesh which he deigned to take from our lump; not from the manner of the concupiscence of man and woman; a virgin believed, a virgin conceived, a virgin gave birth, a virgin remained. And therefore it is said, *And conquer when you are judged*. For he was judged, and conquered; because he was judged without sin. It was a matter of patience to undergo the judgment, not of guilt. Many innocent persons are judged, but in the very cases they plead. For as to the rest, they are not without sin; because, just as before men one is made a doer, so before God a thought is a sin. Your deed before the eyes of God is your thought. The witness of the deed is the judge himself; the accuser of the deed is conscience itself. Therefore that one was judged truly innocent, and therefore conquered. For he alone conquered, not over the judge Pontius Pilate, nor over the raging Jews; but over the devil himself, who scrutinizes all our sins with the diligence of envy.

**4.** And what does the Lord Jesus say of the devil himself? *Behold, the prince of this world comes*. It has often been said to your Charity that this world is called the sinners. And why are sinners called by the name of the world? Because by love of the world they dwell in the world. For those who do not love the world do not dwell in what they do not love. *Our*, he says, *way of life is in heaven*[^11]. If, then, he who loves God dwells in heaven with God; he who loves the world dwells in the world with the prince of the world. And so all the lovers of the world, they themselves are the world; the dwellers in the world, not in the flesh, which all the just are, but in mind, which only sinners are, whose prince is the devil. Just as a house is called the dwellers in the house; according to which manner of speaking we say a marbled house is bad, and a smoky house is good. You find a smoky house, which good people dwell in, and you say: A good house. You find a marbled and paneled house, which the wicked possess, and you say: A bad house, calling "house" not the walls and the receptacles of bodies, but the dwellers themselves. So Scripture called the dwellers in the world "the world," by the concupiscence of love, not by the conduct of the body. Therefore: *Behold*, he says, *the prince of the world comes, and in me finds nothing*. In him alone the devil found nothing. And as though it were said to him: Why, then, do you die? There follows: *But that all may know that I do the will of my Father, rise, let us go from here*[^12]. He rises, and goes to the passion. Why? *Because I do the will of my Father*. On account of this unique innocence, then, the Psalm says, *Against you only have I sinned, and done what is evil before you; that you may be justified in your words, and conquer when you are judged*[^13]; because he found nothing evil in you. But why did he find it in you, O human race? Because it follows and says, *For I was conceived in iniquity, and in sins did my mother conceive me*[^14]. David says this. Seek whence David was born; you will find from a lawful wife, from no adultery. According to what propagation, then, does he say, *I was conceived in iniquity*; except because there is there something of the propagation of death, which everyone draws with himself who is born of the union of man and woman?

**5.** Each one, then, having concupiscence, let him attend to the law saying, *You shall not covet*; he finds in himself what the law forbids, and becomes guilty of the law. But finding in himself that to which he is subjected, let him now begin to say, *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man; but I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and taking me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^15]. He has come to know himself sick, let him implore the physician: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death*? Let the physician answer: *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^16]. *The grace of God*, not your merits. Why, then, did you say that you conducted yourself in the law with righteousness without complaint? Attend: he said without complaint of men. For there is a certain righteousness which a man can fulfill, so that no man complains of a man. For it says, *You shall not covet another's property*. If you do not seize another's, there will be no complaint of men. So at some time you covet, and do not seize. But the sentence of God is over you, because you covet; you are guilty of the law, but in the eyes of the lawgiver. You live without complaint: why, then, these losses? why this dung? This knot is somewhat tighter; but he who is wont will untie it. But this, not I alone by devout submission, but all of us, let us deserve by devout intention. Whatever the Jews did, by which men would not complain, and their conduct in the law would be without complaint, they ascribed to themselves, and assigned that very righteousness according to the law to their own strength; they could not fulfill it, but they did it insofar as they could; by ascribing it to themselves, they did not even fulfill this devoutly.

**6.** Therefore he says that this is to fulfill the law: that is, "not to covet." Who that lives can do this? Let the Psalm help us, which was just now sung: *Hear me in your righteousness*[^17]; that is, not in mine. If he said: Hear me in my righteousness; he would as it were call it a merit. He does indeed in some places call it his own righteousness too; but here he distinguishes better, because even when he calls it his own, he says it is given; as we say, *Give us this day our daily bread*[^18]. How, *ours*; how, *give*? Therefore here, speaking more distinctly, he says, *Hear me in your righteousness*. And it follows, *And do not enter into judgment with your servant*. What is, *Do not enter into judgment with your servant*? Do not stand with me in judgment, exacting from me all that you commanded, exacting all that you ordered. For you will find me guilty, if you enter into judgment with me. There is need, then, he says, for me of your mercy rather than of your most clear judgment. Why, then, *Do not enter into judgment with your servant*? It follows, and he says, *Because no living thing will be justified in your sight*[^19]. For I am a servant: why do you stand with me in judgment? I will use the mercy of the Lord. Why? *Because no living thing will be justified in your sight*. What did he say? As long as one lives in this life, no one has been justified—but in the sight of God. Not without cause did he add, *in your sight*; except because someone can be justified in the sight of men, so that this too is fulfilled, *According to the righteousness that is from the law, having been without complaint*[^20], in the sight of men. Refer it to the sight of God: *No living thing will be justified in your sight*.

**7.** What, then, shall we do? Let us cry out: *Do not enter into judgment with your servant*[^21]. Let us cry out: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^22]. This Psalm, then, we have heard, this Apostle we have heard; because, when that righteousness comes according to which the angels live, when that righteousness comes where there will be no concupiscence, from there let each one measure himself, what he is now, and what he will be then; and he will find, in comparison with that righteousness, that this is losses and dung. But whoever thinks that he can now fulfill righteousness, when he has lived well and innocently according to the probability of human estimation, has remained on the way; he does not desire better, because he thinks he has fulfilled it; and ascribing it most of all to himself, he will be proud. And the humble sinner is better than the proud righteous man. Therefore he says, *And that I may be found in him not having my own righteousness which is from the law*, as the Jews thought; *but the righteousness which is from the faith of Christ Jesus*. Then he goes on and says, *If by any means I may attain to the resurrection of the dead*[^23]. There he believed he would fulfill righteousness—that is, that he would have full righteousness. In comparison with that resurrection, the whole life we lead is dung. Hear the Apostle still more openly saying, *If by any means I may attain to the resurrection of the just*; *not that I have already received it, or am already perfect*. And he goes on then: *Brothers, I do not reckon that I have laid hold of it*[^24]. How does he compare righteousness to righteousness, salvation to salvation, faith to sight, pilgrimage to the city?

**8.** See how he fulfills this: *Brothers, I do not reckon that I have laid hold of it. But one thing*. What is *one thing*, but to live by faith, in hope of eternal salvation, where there will be full and perfect righteousness, in comparison with which the things that are to pass away are losses, and the things that are to be rejected are dung? What, then? *But one thing: forgetting what is behind, stretching out to what is ahead, according to my intention I follow toward the palm of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^25]. And to those who could presume on their own perfection: *As many of us as are perfect, let us be of this mind*. A while ago he had said he was imperfect, but now perfect. Why, except because this is the perfection of man, to have found that he is not perfect? *As many of us as are perfect, let us be of this mind. And if perhaps you are of any other mind, that too God will reveal to you*[^26]; that is, so that, if you prove yourselves justified in some progress of the soul, by reading the Scriptures, and by finding what the true and perfect righteousness is, you may find yourselves guilty, and by the longing for things to come condemn the present, and live by faith and hope and love; and understand that what you still believe, you do not yet see; what you still hope for, you do not yet hold; what you still desire, you do not yet fulfill. But if the love of pilgrims is such, what will the love of those who see be? Therefore the one who taught the righteousness of God, and did not establish his own, cried out from the Psalm, *Hear me in your righteousness. And do not enter into judgment with your servant; because no living thing will be justified in your sight*[^27].

**9.** Concerning this life it is said to Moses, *No one has seen the face of God, and lived*[^28]. For one must not live in this life so as to see that face. One must die to the world, that we may live to God forever. Then we shall not sin, not only by deeds, but not even by concupiscences, when we shall see that face, which conquers all concupiscences. For it is so sweet, my brothers, so beautiful, that, it being seen, nothing else can delight. There will be an insatiable fullness, no loathing; we shall always hunger, we shall always be filled. Hear those two sentences from Scripture: *Those who drink me*, says Wisdom, *will still thirst; and those who eat me will still hunger*[^29]. But lest you suppose that there will be want and hunger there, hear the Lord: *He who drinks of this water will not thirst forever*[^30]. But you say: When will it be? Whenever it will be, yet wait for the Lord, await the Lord, act manfully, and let your heart be strengthened[^31]. Surely there does not remain as much as has been accomplished? Look from Adam to the present day, how many ages have rolled by, and behold, now they are no more. A few days, in a way, remain; for so must one speak of what remains in comparison with the ages gone by. Let us exhort one another, let him exhort us who came to us, who ran the way, and said: Follow; who ascended first into heaven, that the head from the loftier places might come to the aid of the other members laboring on earth; who said from heaven, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^32]? Therefore let no one despair; what was promised will be rendered to us at the end; there that righteousness will be fulfilled.

**10.** You have heard the Gospel agree with these sayings. *It is the will*, he says, *of the Father, that all that he has given me should not perish, but have eternal life; and I will raise them up on the last day*[^33]. Himself on the first day, us on the last day. The first day belongs to the head of the Church. For our day, Christ the Lord, has no setting. The last day will be the end of the age. I do not wish you to say: When will this be? For the human race it will be far off, for each man it will be near; because the last day is each one's day of death. For when you have gone out from here, you will be received according to your merits, and you will rise to receive what you did. Then God will crown, not so much your merits as his own gifts. Whatever he gave you, if you have kept it, he will recognize it. Now, then, brothers, let our desire be only in heaven; let it be only for eternal life. Let no one please himself, as one who has lived justly here, and compare himself to those who live badly, like the Pharisee who justified himself[^34], who had not heard the Apostle: *Not that I have already received it, or am already perfect*[^35]. Therefore he had not yet received what he desired. He had received the pledge, so he said: *Who gave us the pledge, the Spirit*[^36]. The thing whose pledge it was, to this he desired to come; a certain participation, but it differs. Now we participate in one way, then we will participate in another. Now through faith, through hope, in the same Spirit; but then it will be sight, it will be the reality; but the same Spirit, the same God, the same fullness. He who cries to the absent will display himself to the present; he who calls the pilgrims will nourish and feed them in the homeland.

**11.** Christ has been made the way for us, and do we despair of arriving? This way cannot be ended, cannot be cut off, cannot be corrupted, nor besieged by rain, nor by floods, nor by robbers. Walk secure in Christ, walk; do not stumble, do not fall, do not look back, do not remain on the way, do not withdraw from the way. Only guard against all these things, and you have arrived. When you have arrived, then now glory in this; not in yourself. For he who praises himself does not praise God, but turns himself away from God; just as one who wishes to withdraw from the fire—the fire remains hot, but he grows cold; just as one who wishes to withdraw from the light—if he withdraws, the light remains bright in itself, but he is darkened. Let us not withdraw from the warmth of the Spirit, from the light of the truth. We have now heard the voice, but then we shall see face to face. Let no one please himself, let no one insult another. Let us all so wish to make progress, that we do not envy those who make progress, do not insult those who fail; and there will be fulfilled in us with joy what was promised in the Gospel: *And I will raise them up on the last day*[^37].
