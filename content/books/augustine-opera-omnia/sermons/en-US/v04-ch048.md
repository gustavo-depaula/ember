# Sermon 130a

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, who called himself bread, seeks the hungry [^1]. But nothing hungers for this bread except health of mind, that is, of the inner belly. From this visible bread take a likeness. Since sick men, who through disease have contracted a loss of appetite, can praise the best bread but cannot eat it, so too the inner languor of the inner man is not moved to eat this heavenly bread, since it is pressed by loss of appetite, and, although it praises, is not delighted to take it. But the Lord himself says, as we have heard: *Work for the food that does not perish, but that abides unto eternal life* [^2], distinguishing it from this visible and bodily food, of which in another place: *Everything that goes into the mouth, goes into the belly and is sent out into the privy* [^3]—therefore it perishes. *Work*, then, *for the food that does not perish*, he says, *but that abides unto eternal life*. This food he called bread and showed himself to be bread. But what is it to work for this food, except to eat this food? For if this food is bread, likewise it is Christ. Who of us works Christ, who of us makes Christ, except by doing what Christ commanded? *You are*, says the Apostle, *the body of Christ and members* [^4]. Let us work Christ, that is, let us work this food.

**2.** Well did those who questioned and heard, hear for us and question for us. *What*, they say, *shall we do, that we may work the work of God* [^5]? And with that wonderful and great brevity: *This is*, he says, *the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent* [^6]. This is brief: if you weigh it, it is great. It is quickly said, not easily fulfilled. Someone of so great a multitude of bystanders says to me, he says: "Who of us is there who has not believed in Christ?" If, then, we have all believed in Christ, we now find nothing to be admonished. Why? Because *this is the work of God*. Do you seek anything else than to do the work of God and to await the reward of God? Why, then, do you trouble yourself, why are you occupied with the great pages of Scripture and labor by running through them to seek and find how you may do the work of God? Behold you have, with your Lord speaking to you, truth and brevity: do not stretch yourself, do not sweat, do not labor, do not boil. *This is the work of God, that you believe*, he says, *in him whom he sent*. Where, then, is what James admonishes, saying: *You believe that God is one? You do well. The demons too believe and tremble* [^7]? Do not think, he says, that you do something great by believing that God is one: *The demons too believe and tremble*. But perhaps the demons have no belief about the Son of God? And what is it that they say to him: *We know who you are* [^8]? Or perhaps they speak lying? do they say what they do not know? Behold in another place more plainly: *You are the holy one of God*, they say, also *You are the Son of God*, they say. When Peter said this, he heard from the Lord: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven. And I say to you, Simon Bar-Jona, that you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church* [^9]. On account of what all this? Because he said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God* [^10]. This the demons too said, to whom it was said: *Be silent* [^11]. Who easily discerns these things, whence the like voice is not praised, except because the heart is unlike? Understand, then, dearly beloved, at our admonishing, what you daily read and say. The demons too believed Christ to be, and to be the Son of God. They believed Christ, but did not believe in Christ. Now the whole intent of our sermon leans on this, as far as we can, if with the Lord helping we can, to explain what it is to believe in Christ.

**3.** For *this is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent* [^12]. He did not say "believe him" or "believe him to be," but *believe in him*. We have heard the words of the prophets: we believe them, but we do not believe in them. We have heard the Apostles preaching: we believe their preaching, but we do not believe in them. We do not believe in Paul, but we believe Paul. For he repels from himself certain men wanting to set their hope in him and, as it were, to believe in him, that they should not be in him, but he with them in Christ. *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in his name* [^13]? In short, not only the Apostles and holy teachers, but even we ourselves, not to be compared even to their least footsteps, daily say: "Believe me," but we never dare to say: "Believe in me." "Believe me": who does not say it? "Believe in me": who says it, or who that says it is not mad? In whom, then, must one believe? Of whom the same Paul says: *To the one believing in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned unto righteousness* [^14]. Not, then, does Paul justify the ungodly, for then you would believe in him and your faith would be reckoned unto righteousness: for you would believe *in him who justifies the ungodly*. For when you do not believe in him—for Paul does not justify the ungodly, nor Elijah, nor any angel, but the just one of the just, the holy one of the holy, of whom it was said: *That he himself may be just and justifying the one who is of faith* [^15]. Of you it can be said that you are just, but it is never heard that you are also justifying. For what is justifying, except making just? As enlivening is making living, as saving is making safe, so justifying is making just. Who is it who makes just? He who came just without sin. Who is it who makes just? He who was not made just here, but came just. Behold in whom we should believe, that we may do the work of God, because *this* itself *is the work of God*, to believe *in him who justifies the ungodly*.

**4.** "It suffices me, then," someone says; "I have believed in Christ: why do you admonish me further? *This is the work of God*: let him who promised the reward demand nothing else of me. For he who promises the reward commands me what to do, and pledges what I shall receive. He circumscribes the boundary of the work, when I have burned with love of the reward. He himself says to me: 'Do you want to live forever?', on account of the one who asked: *What shall I do, that I may have eternal life*?, 'Do you want to live forever? *Do this, and you will live*.' When I have asked: *What shall I do*?, he answers most briefly: '*This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*.'" Why, then, to that rich man: *If you want to come to life, keep the commandments*, and when he said: "*What commandments*?", the precepts of the law were numbered: *You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not steal, you shall not give false witness, honor father and mother and you shall love your neighbor as yourself* [^16]? Could he not have answered also him, with that briefest summary, saying *What shall I do, that I may have eternal life?*: "Believe in me"? For *this is the work of God*, since above he defined it, to which thing he added nothing. *This is the work of God*—what more do you seek?—*that you believe in him whom he sent*. To that man precepts are given, and many, to us it is said: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*. Or did the Lord perhaps deal graciously with us; did he want to burden that man, not relieve him? Far be it! Let us understand, then, if we can—which we, as we can, promised we would explain—what it is to believe in Christ, and let us do this, work this, in this make progress daily, to this come from day to day, until by coming we arrive. For at the beginning of our faith we promised this too, that we have begun in some part, and, when we have completed it, nothing further will be demanded of us. For *this is the work of God*, nor is there any other work of God, except *that we believe in him whom he sent*.

**5.** And since a little before we distinguished that it is one thing to believe him, to believe him to be, and to believe in him—to believe him is to believe that the things he speaks are true, to believe him to be is to believe that he himself is Christ, to believe in him is to love him—now examine these of yours: to believe that the things he speaks are true, to believe that he himself is Christ, to love Christ. To believe that the things he speaks are true, many even of the wicked can do. For they believe them to be true and do not want to do them: they are sluggish to work. But to believe that he himself is Christ, this even the demons could. So Peter: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*, said it loving; the demons said it fearing. But not everyone who fears loves, everyone who loves fears. But perhaps—rather not perhaps, but certainly—everyone who loves fears, but with a chaste fear—*for the fear of the Lord is chaste, abiding forever and ever* [^17]—not with a servile fear. For *perfect love casts out fear* [^18]. It is one thing to fear lest he come, another to fear lest he forsake. For to fear lest he come was in those who said: *We know who you are. Why have you come before the time to destroy us* [^19]? But the fear lest he forsake was in the one who had said: *I will be with you even unto death* [^20]. When, then, you hear: "Believe in Christ," let it be too little for you to believe Christ, that is, that the things Christ speaks are true; let it be too little for you to believe Christ to be, that is, that he himself is the one whom God foretold through the prophets; but believe in Christ, that is, love Christ. When you have fulfilled this, nothing further will be demanded of you, since *the fullness of the law is love* [^21]. When you have so believed in Christ, as to love Christ with that ardor, see whether these will not be your words: *Who will separate us from the love of God* [^22]? Do not, then, long seek how to do what Christ commands: you cannot but do it, if you love Christ. Love, and you do it. As much as you love, so much you do; as much less you do, the less you love. Fulfill love, and you perfect the work. See how truly it was said: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*, that is, that by loving you go into him, that is, be incorporated into him.

**6.** For someone can answer me and admonish me to speak more cautiously: "You said this is to believe in Christ, to love Christ, and you said we ought to believe in Christ, but not to believe in Paul; ought we then not to love Paul?" Therefore, as we distinguished between believing and believing, so the loving itself must be distinguished. I love Paul, and do not believe in Paul. I do not love Christ, unless I have believed in Christ. For I love Paul, but not—by loving—do I go into Paul: I will be with Paul, I will not be in Paul. *For what is Apollos, what is Paul? Ministers through whom you believed* [^23], not in whom you believed. Therefore, brothers, to believe in God is the work, by believing and loving to go into Christ. *Believe in God*, says Christ himself, *and believe in me* [^24]. What prophet dared to say this, what patriarch, what apostle, what martyr, what angel? Therefore let us believe in him, let us love him, but by loving be incorporated into him. Let us understand our God both made man for our sake, and from this, that God was made man, justifying us. For if he were man only, he would have to be justified with us, he would not be the justifier. But now because he is our God and on account of nature was made man what he was not, on account of possession remained what he was, that in him at once we might have both that by which we go and that to which we come. Let us believe in him, that is, let us love him as God, that by loving we may go into him, from whom by neglecting we had departed.

**7.** But if this is in us, the bread delights; now there is no loss of appetite; we do not praise and shrink, but even the praised bread, silent, we eat, because we love, the loss of appetite being turned away, because the languor being healed. For what will our soul repay him, which says: *What shall I repay the Lord?*, which says: *Bless the Lord, my soul, and do not forget all his repayments, who is merciful to all your iniquities* [^25]? This was done, when we were washed. But what follows comes to be: *Who heals all your languors* [^26]. As much as the languors are healed, so much the heavenly food delights; so much the bread of life that came down from heaven delights [^27], as much as our languors are healed. But by whom are they healed, except him to whom we said: *Turn us, God of our health* [^28]? *Turn us*: you turn us, because, before you heal us, we are averse and refuse to touch the praised bread out of loss of appetite. We are averse when languid, we are turned when healed. *Turn us*, then, *God of our health, and turn away your anger from us*. For the languor itself is the anger of God. *He who believes in the Son, has eternal life* [^29]. Why? Because he has worked the work of God. *For this is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*. *But he who is unbelieving toward the Son, will not have life, but the anger of God abides upon him* [^30]. It will not come upon him, but *abides upon him*. He is forsaken, not healed. Attending, then, the one who said: *Turn us, God*, because we cannot be turned, except insofar as we are healed, added: *of our health*. For this bread does not delight the unhealthy. As though he set forth the cause, then, from which we may be turned. What is: *Turn us*? Heal us. For in that we are not healthy, we are turned less to the bread of salvation. We praise, but are not delighted. Who would not approve, who would not say: "It is true," when I have said: *What you do not want done to you, do not do to another* [^31]? "It is true": you cry out, and you cry out nothing truer. Eat what you praise. You want to deceive a man, you want to be deceived by no one. You want to be enriched from another's evil, you do not want anyone to be enriched from your evil. Do you not set up a law against yourself, and is your heart not prescribed by which you are punished? Why do you not want it done to you, if it is good? Why do you do it, if it is evil? What will you answer, what will you say? "It delights me to do it and it delights me to do it," "this delights and this delights": they are the words of one languishing. Cry out: *Turn us, God of our health, and turn away your anger from us* [^32].

**8.** For in that same psalm this too was said: *The Lord will give sweetness, and our earth will give its fruit* [^33]. *But the fruit of the Spirit is love* [^34]. Whence this fruit, unless the Lord gives sweetness, by which *the love of God has been poured out in our hearts?* Not from ourselves, but *through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us* [^35]. You see that the Lord will give sweetness, that our earth may give its fruit. You see that our earth, that is, our heart, our soul, our earth does not give its fruit, unless God rains. *The earth was moved*. It was moved to give birth and to bear. But why was it moved? *For the heavens dripped* not of themselves, but *from the face of God* [^36]. *For what is Apollos, what is Paul? Ministers of God through whom you believed* [^37]. They are the clouds of God: unless they are filled, they do not pour out. *The earth*, then, *was moved*—with God of our health turning us—*the earth was moved, for the heavens dripped from the face of God*. By God it was moved, for it would not be moved, except for the *voluntary rain*, as there follows. After it was said: *The earth was moved, for the heavens dripped from the face of God*, lest you think that very rain of the heavens to be not God's, *a rain*, he says, *voluntary*, not owed—for what did he owe us, except punishment? *A rain*, then, *voluntary*—because *with the shield*, he says, *of your good will you have crowned us* [^38], not by the brightness of our merits, but *with the shield of your good will*—*a rain*, then, *voluntary, God setting apart for his inheritance, and it was weakened* [^39]. She is weakened too who gives birth. *For the earth was moved* to give birth, nor would it bear, unless weakness preceded. *But you perfected it* [^40].

**9.** What is *it was weakened?* It did not presume upon itself. What is *it was weakened?* It hoped the whole from you. What is *it was weakened?* *When I am weak, then I am strong* [^41]. Therefore *it was weakened*, it understood it was of the grace of God, not of its own merits, of its own strength; it understood, *it was weakened*, and lost presumption, that it might receive blessing. *It was weakened*. Let it not, then, presume upon itself, let it cry to the Lord, weak: *Turn us, God of our health*. There follows, then: *And it was weakened, but you perfected it*. Why *did you perfect it?* Because it *was weakened*, because it understood that it could not be perfected by itself. Therefore because *it was weakened, you perfected it*. That same earth cries out in Paul: *On account of which I asked the Lord, that he would take it from me* [^42], namely the goad of my flesh for correction. For why did he say this was applied to him, except to restrain his own presumption and strength? *For lest by the greatness*, he says, *of revelations I be lifted up, there was given me a goad of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me* [^43]. Therefore be weakened. The voluntary rain has been set apart for you, not owed. Cry out from weakness, because *I am not worthy to be called an apostle* [^44]. Therefore be weakened, that he may perfect you. For he said to you, praying: *My grace suffices you, power is perfected in weakness* [^45].

**10.** So that same Apostle, in the reading we heard, concluded thus: *That I may be found in him*. How *in him?* *Not having my own righteousness*. For if your own, not *in him*. What is *not having my own?* As though gained by me, because fulfilled by my own strength. *Not having my own righteousness which is from the law, but that which is through faith* [^46]. How *my own*, if it is *from the law?* A troublesome question, but we are now wearied. Accordingly if I say something less, let him fill it up whose we are. *And I may be found in him not having my own righteousness*. If he did not add: *which is from the law*, who would not think he meant this: *Not having my own righteousness*, as though that which comes from human teaching? Whence the Lord Christ said in another place: *My teaching is not mine* [^47], that is, human, it is not of this conspicuous substance which you see. For how is it not his, when the teaching of the Father is his Word? And who, except Christ, is his Word? Would men, then, think the Apostle had said this, when he says: *I may be found in him not having my own righteousness*, unless he added: *which is from the law, but the righteousness which is from the faith of Jesus Christ* [^48]? Why? Did not Christ too give the law? If you had *the righteousness which is from the law*, how would you have your own, when you did not give yourself the law, but received it from Christ, from the Son of God? For Christ did not begin to be from the Virgin, but from the Father, before the mother whom he created from whom he might be created, when he made that from which he might come to be. *For the mother Zion, said the man, and the man was made in her. And*—who *was made man in her*—*he himself, the Most High, founded her* [^49]. He himself, then, made her in whom he might come to be. He himself, then, was in the time of Moses, who gave the law, because surely Moses, with many intervening steps, was from Abraham; but Christ says: *Before Abraham, I am* [^50]. It is too little to say before Abraham, before all the angels, before heaven and earth, before all creation: *For all things were made through him* [^51].

**11.** What, then, is *that I may be found in him not having my own righteousness*, and he added: *which is from the law?* I will say, if I can briefly; the giver of grace will perfect it in your hearts. What is it, except that the Jews so had the law itself, that they did the works of the law out of fear of damnation, not out of the fire of love? Therefore because they acted out of servile fear, they were servants. But if you begin out of love to do what is written in the law: *You shall not covet* [^52], whence is this to you, except that *the love of God has been poured out* in your heart, not from yourself, but *through the Holy Spirit who has been given* to you [^53]? Since *a voluntary rain, God setting apart for his inheritance, and it was weakened* [^54], that it might hope nothing of itself. For it was said to it: "Do," and it did; "Do not kill," and it did not kill; "Do not commit adultery with another's wife," and it did not commit adultery. It was said: "Do," and it did. It was said: "Love what you do," *and it was weakened*. You do not kill your enemy out of fear: if impunity were given, would you not spare him? Would you do what David did, to whom the Lord so gave his enemy into his hands, that he might do to him what he willed? He chose to spare, he chose to spare the one whom he was permitted with impunity to kill [^55]. If, then, impunity were granted you about killing your enemy, would you spare him? How would you spare him lest he die, of whom you say: "O that he would die"? Therefore the law imposed forbade the evil deed to the one fearing in servile fashion, it did not root out the evil desire. You restrain yourself from the deed, but you have the desire to do it. Why do you have the desire to do it? Because you do not have the love of righteousness. Therefore there is nothing further to be forbidden you. Love. What do I do, to love? . . . Therefore out of love, because, if you do it out of fear, you do not love. Your righteousness it will be, though from the law, because you do not do what the law forbids, yet your own: for you do not do it out of fear, not out of love. When will it not be your own righteousness? If it is from the faith of Christ. For by believing in him you obtained what he commands.

**12.** This the Scripture of the present psalm attends to. Does not God say: *Turn to me* [^56]? The Scriptures are full of it. *Turn to me, turn to me*. For the languor began to be moved. For what is: *Turn to me?* Not—which is easily done—that you who attended to the West attend to the East. Would that you do this within, because this is not easy! You turn your body from hinge to hinge: turn your heart from love to love. *Turn to me*: God cries out. The earth feared, lest, not turned, it be condemned; it moved itself, that it might be turned: the languor did not follow, the bread did not delight the sick. *Turn to me*. The languor saw it could not do what was commanded, and cried out from the prophet: *Turn us, God*; faith entered the heart; it asked that what was commanded be done, it said: *Turn us, God*. Because *it was weakened, but you perfected it, that I may be found in him not having my own righteousness which is from the law, but* the righteousness of Christ *which is from faith*. What is *from faith?* Which it obtained by asking, as much as it could not under the one commanding. *For everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved* [^57]. This is the righteousness of faith which we preach; *this is the living bread that came down from heaven* [^58], healing that it may be eaten, strengthening because it is eaten, fattening because it is desired, since of it it was said to our soul, that *it satisfies your desire with good things* [^59]. And so on.
