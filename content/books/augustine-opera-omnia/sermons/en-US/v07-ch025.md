# Sermon 252: In the Days of Easter

**1.** In many and various ways our Lord Jesus Christ shows, in the holy Scriptures, both the height of his divinity and the mercy of his humanity, as he is wont, in mysteries and sacred signs; so that those who ask may receive, and those who seek may find, and to those who knock it may be opened. For this too which was read today from the holy Gospel seeks one who understands, and produces spiritual joy when it has been understood. Let your Holiness attend to what it means, that the Lord showed himself to the disciples in the way that the Evangelist narrates. For the disciples went out to fish, and the whole night caught nothing. But the Lord appeared to them in the morning on the shore, and asked whether they had any food: they said they had none. He said to them: *Cast the nets on the right side, and you will find*[^1]. He who had come as if to buy gave so much freely; and gave from the sea, as from his own creation. A great miracle indeed. They cast at once, and caught so much that they could not draw out the nets for the multitude of fish. But if you attend to who did this great miracle, it is not marvelous: for he had already done many greater things. For since he had raised the dead before the resurrection, it was no great thing after the resurrection that he caused fish to be caught. We ought, then, to question the miracle itself, what it speaks to us inwardly. For not without cause did he not say merely, Cast the nets; but: *Cast on the right side.* Then it belonged to the Evangelist to give also the number of the fish. It belonged also to say: *And though they were so many*, that is, so great, *the nets were not torn*[^2]. In this place he made us remember that once, at the Lord's command, the nets were cast, when he chose the disciples, before he himself suffered. There were Peter, John, and James. They cast the nets, they caught fish without number, and when one boat was filled, they asked to be helped by the neighboring boat: and the two boats were filled with fish (this before the resurrection), so many that the nets were broken. Why is no number given there? Why were the nets broken there, and here they were not broken? Why was it not said there that the nets should be cast on the right side, but here he said: *Cast the nets on the right side?* It is surely not without cause. For the Lord did not do these things as it were in vain and to no purpose. Christ is the Word of God, who speaks to men not only with sounds, but also with deeds.

**2.** This, then, is set before us to discuss with your Charity, what this difference means. For those nets which were cast before, and caught an innumerable number of fish, and loaded the two boats, and were broken, were not cast on the right side; but neither was it said, on the left. Now the mystery of that catch is fulfilled in this time. But that other mystery, which not without cause he made after the resurrection, when he was no longer going to die, but to live always; not only in his divinity, by which he never died, but also in his body, by which he condescended to die for us. Therefore not in vain that one before the passion, this one after the resurrection. There neither on the right nor on the left, but only: *Cast the nets*: but here: *Cast on the right.* There no number, but only a copious multitude, so that it almost sank the two boats; for this too was said there: but here there is both number and greatness of the fish. Then there the nets were broken, here it belonged to the Evangelist to say: *And though they were so great, the nets were not torn*[^3]. Do we not see, brothers, that the word of God is the nets, and this age the sea, and that all who believe are enclosed within those nets? If perhaps anyone doubts that this is signified, let him attend to the fact that the Lord himself said in a parable what he showed in the miracle. For he says: *The kingdom of heaven is like a net cast into the sea, which gathers fish of every kind. When it was full, drawing it out they dragged it to the shore; and sitting beside the shore they chose the good into vessels, but the bad they cast outside. So it will be at the end of the age: the angels will go out, and separate the bad from the midst of the just, and cast them into the furnace of fire: there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^4]. It appears, then, that the nets cast into the sea have a sign of faith. Is not the sea this age, where men devour one another like fish? Do not small storms and waves of temptation trouble this sea? Are the dangers of those sailing—that is, of those seeking the heavenly homeland on the wood of the cross—small? The likeness, then, is most manifest.

**3.** Let us only see this, brothers (because the Lord's resurrection signifies the new life which we will have when this age has passed), how the word of God was first cast into this sea, that is, into this world. Into this age, troubled with waves, and dangerous with storms, and burdensome with shipwrecks, the word of God was cast, and it caught many, so that two boats were filled. What are the two boats? Two peoples. To which two peoples, as to two walls, the Lord was made the cornerstone, to join them in himself coming from different directions. For the people of the Jews came from one custom far different: but the people of the gentiles came from idols. The people of the Jews came from the circumcision: the people of the gentiles came from the uncircumcision. They came from different directions: but in the cornerstone they are joined. For walls never make a corner, except coming from different directions. In Christ, then, the two peoples agreed, called from the Jews who were near, and from the gentiles who were far. For because the Jews were near (for they already worshiped one God), when they believed in Christ, attend to what they did. Whatever they had, they sold, and laid the prices of their estates before the feet of the apostles: and it was distributed to each, as each had need. They were freed from the burdens of worldly business, and with light shoulders followed Christ: they put their neck under the gentle yoke, and embracing the cornerstone from nearby, were at peace. But the people of the gentiles also came from afar, and they too reached that stone, and met in peace. These two peoples those two boats signify. But they were filled with so great a multitude of fish that they almost sank. For we read that even among those who had believed from the Jews there arose carnal men, who caused pressure to the Church, and forbade the apostles to speak the Gospel to the gentiles, saying, Christ was sent only to the circumcised: so that if the gentiles wished to receive the Gospel, they should be circumcised. Hence the apostle Paul, sent to the gentiles, had become hateful, preaching the truth, to those who had believed from Judea. For the Apostle wished that the people of the gentiles, coming from a different direction, should yet touch the corner, where there was firm peace. Those carnal men, then, demanding circumcision, did not belong to the number of the spiritual: nor did they yet see that, the carnal sacred signs being past, he had come by whose present light the shadows would be put to flight. Yet because they made seditions, they as it were sank the boat by their multitude.

**4.** Let us attend also to the boat of the gentiles. Let us see whether so great a multitude has not been gathered into the Church that the grains of wheat scarcely appear there among so great a number of chaff. How many robbers, how many drunkards, how many slanderers, how many spectators of the theaters! Do not the very ones who fill the theaters fill the churches? And often they seek in the churches by seditions such things as they are wont to seek in the theaters. And often, if something is said or commanded spiritually, they resist, they struggle against it, following the flesh, fighting against the Holy Spirit. For which thing Stephen too accused the Jews. In this city, my brothers, have we not experienced—as your Holiness recalls with us—with how great danger to ourselves God expelled drunkenness from this basilica? Was not the boat almost sunk with us by the sedition of the carnal? From what, except from that innumerable number of fish? Then this too was said there, that the nets were broken. When the nets were broken, heresies and schisms were made. The nets indeed enclose all: but the impatient fish, unwilling to come to the food of the Lord, dash themselves where they can, and break out, and go out. And those nets indeed are spread throughout: but those who break out, break out in places. The Donatists broke out in Africa, the Arians broke out in Egypt, the Photinians broke out in Pannonia, the Cataphrygians broke out in Phrygia, the Manichees broke out in Persia. In how many places was that seine torn? And yet those whom it encloses, it brings to the shore. It brings them indeed, but surely not those who broke the nets? All the bad go out. They do not indeed go out, except the bad: but both good and bad remain. For how else is the seine brought to the shore with fish both good and bad, of which the Lord spoke in the parable?

**5.** For the threshing floor too has this likeness, when it is threshed. There is chaff, there are grains: but yet those who look at the threshing floor scarcely see anything but chaff. Diligence is needed for the grains to be found in the chaff. But the winds blow over the threshing floor from every side. And when it is threshed, before it is lifted up so that it can be winnowed, does it not suffer the winds? The wind blows from this side, for example, it carries off the chaff: again it blows from there, it carries it to the other side. From whatever side it carries off the chaff, it casts it into the hedge, and into the thorns, and anywhere. It cannot carry off the wheat from there: the wind carries off nothing but the chaff. But yet when the winds blowing from every side have carried off the chaff, will only the wheat remain on the floor? Nothing goes from there but the chaff: but both chaff and grain remain. When does all the chaff go? When the Lord comes bearing the winnowing fan in his hand, and will cleanse his threshing floor, will store the grains in the barn, but will burn the chaff with unquenchable fire. Let your Holiness attend better to what I say. For sometimes the winds that carry off the chaff from the floor blow again from the hedge where the chaff had clung, and recall it to the floor. As, for example, some man or other, set in the Catholic Church, suffered some temptation of tribulation. He saw that he could be helped carnally in his business among the Donatists: it was said to him, You will not be helped, unless you take communion here. The wind blew, it cast him into the thorns. If it happens that he again has some worldly business, which cannot be settled except in the Catholic Church, not attending to where he is, but where he may more conveniently settle his affairs, as if the wind blowing from the other side of the hedge, he is recalled to the Lord's floor.

**6.** These, then, brothers, who seek carnal things in the Church, and do not set before themselves what God promises—because here are temptations, dangers, difficulties; but after the temporal labors he promises everlasting rest, and the society of the holy angels: not setting these things before themselves, then, but desiring carnal things in the Church, whether they are on the floor, or are outside, they are chaff. We neither greatly rejoice over them, nor do we caress them with empty flatteries. It is good for them that they become wheat. For this is the difference between that real chaff and these carnal men, that that chaff does not have free choice, but to man God gave free choice. And if a man wishes, yesterday he was chaff, today he becomes wheat: if he turns himself away from the word of God, today he becomes chaff. And there is nothing to seek, except what the last winnowing finds them.

**7.** Attend now, brothers, also to that blessed, mystical, great Church, which the one hundred fifty-three fish signify. For what this present Church is like, we have heard, and known, and seen: but what that one will be like is in prophecy for us; but it has not yet arrived in experience. But yet it is permitted to rejoice over the future one, although we do not yet see the present one. The nets were cast then neither on the right nor on the left: for they were going to catch both good and bad. For if it were said, On the right; the bad would not be understood there: if it were said, On the left; the good would not be understood there. But since they were going to have good and bad, the nets were cast at random, and they caught, as we have discussed, the wicked and the just. But now, the Church to come in that holy Jerusalem, where the hearts of all mortals will lie open, it is not to be feared that anyone who is bad will enter that Church. For he will not cover the cunning of a most wicked heart under the skin of mortality. For now the Lord has come: and therefore after the resurrection he commands that these nets be cast on the right, when he is no longer going to die. And that is fulfilled which the Apostle says: *Until the Lord comes, and illumines the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart: and then there will be praise for each one from God*[^5]: when the consciences which are now covered will be made public. Then, then, the good alone will be there, the bad will be expelled. For the nets were cast on the right, they cannot have the wicked.

**8.** Why, then, one hundred fifty-three? Will the saints be so many? For if there be counted, not only all the faithful, who departed from the body in a good life, but the martyrs alone; if one day of the passion of martyrs be counted, thousands of men are found crowned. Therefore what the one hundred fifty-three fish mean is without doubt to be sought out. What does fifty mean? For in this number, that is, in fifty, there is a mystery: because fifty multiplied by three makes one hundred fifty. For the number three seems to be added for this reason, that we may be admonished from what multiplication one hundred fifty-three were made: as if it were said: Divide one hundred fifty into three. For if it were said: One hundred fifty-two, admonished by that number which exceeded, we would divide into seventy-five: since seventy-five taken twice make one hundred fifty. For the binary number added would indicate a division by two. If it were said: One hundred fifty-six, we should divide into twenty-fives, so that there would be six parts of them. But now because it was said, One hundred fifty-three, we ought to divide that whole number, that is, one hundred fifty, into three parts. The third part of this number, then, is fifty. And so our whole consideration is to be fixed on the number fifty.

**9.** Are these perhaps the fifty days that we now celebrate? For not without cause, my brothers, does the Church hold the custom of ancient tradition, that during these fifty days *Alleluia* should be said. For *Alleluia* is the praise of God. Therefore the activity of our rest is signified to us who labor. For when after this labor we will come to that rest, our only business will be the praise of God, our activity there is *Alleluia*. What is *Alleluia?* "Praise God." Who praises God without failing, except the angels? They do not hunger, do not thirst, do not grow sick, do not die. For we too said *Alleluia*, and it was sung here in the morning, and just now, when we were already present, we said *Alleluia*. A certain fragrance of divine praise and of that rest touches us, but for the greater part mortality presses us down. For we grow weary with saying it, and want to refresh our members: and if *Alleluia* is said for long, the praise of God is burdensome to us, because of the mass of our body. For fullness without ceasing in *Alleluia* will be after this age and after labor. What then, brothers? Let us say it as much as we can, that we may merit to say it always. There our food is *Alleluia*, our drink *Alleluia*, the activity of our rest *Alleluia*, all our joy will be *Alleluia*, that is, "the Praise of God." For who praises anything without failing, except one who enjoys it without distaste? How great, then, will be the strength in the mind, how great the immortality and firmness in the body, that neither the mind's attention fail in the contemplation of God, nor the members succumb in the continuation of the praise of God?

**10.** Why, then, are fifty days celebrated in this mystery? The Lord spent forty days with the disciples after the resurrection, as the Acts of the Apostles testify: after forty days he ascended into heaven, and on the tenth day after he ascended, he sent the Holy Spirit. Filled with him, the apostles, and all who had gathered together, spoke in tongues, and did those mighty works which we, reading and believing, embrace, speaking the word of God with great confidence. He spent forty days on earth with the disciples, but before the passion he fasted forty days: you do not find anyone else having fasted forty days, except the Lord, and Moses, and Elijah. The Lord as the Gospel, Moses as the Law, Elijah as Prophecy: because the Gospel has testimony from the Law and the Prophets. Therefore on the mountain too, when our Lord Jesus wished to show his glory, he stood between Moses and Elijah. In the middle, in honor, he himself shone: the Law and the Prophets attested from the sides. The number forty, then, signifies this time, in which we labor in the world: because wisdom is here dispensed to us temporally. For the vision of immortal wisdom is one thing, without time; but it is another thing for it to be dispensed temporally. For there were patriarchs, and they passed from here: their dispensation was temporal. I do not say, they live temporally; for they always live, and live with God. But the temporal dispensation of the word was made through them. For they do not now speak here; but what they spoke is written, and is read in time. The prophets came in their own time, and departed. The Lord came in his own time: for never did the presence of his majesty withdraw, never does he, established everywhere by his divinity, withdraw; but, as it was said in the Gospel: *He was in this world, and the world was made through him, and the world did not know him: he came to his own, and his own did not receive him*[^6]. How was he here, and how did he come, except that he was here by majesty, he came by humanity? For in that he came in the flesh, he himself ministered wisdom to us temporally: temporally through the Law, temporally through the Prophets, temporally through the Scriptures of the Gospel. For when the times have passed, we will see that very wisdom as it is, which renders the number ten. For the number seven indicates the creation: because in six days God worked, and on the seventh rested from his works. But the number three suggests the Creator, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. It is perfect wisdom devoutly to subject the creation to the Creator; to distinguish the maker from what is made, the craftsman from the works. He who confuses the works with the craftsman understands neither the art nor the craftsman: but he who distinguishes is filled with wisdom. This, then, is the ten, the fullness of wisdom. But when it is distributed temporally; because in the number four is the mark of temporal things, ten taken four times makes the number forty. And the year is varied in four ways, by spring, summer, autumn, and winter: and there especially appears in time a certain fourfold alternation. Scripture too mentions four winds. For through the four cardinal points the Gospel has gone, which is dispensed in time: and that is the Catholic Church, which has taken hold of the four parts of the world. Therefore ten in this way makes the number forty.

**11.** Therefore they fasted forty days, signifying that in this time abstinence from the love of temporal things is necessary. For this those perpetual fasts through so many days signify, in the forty days. Hence too that people of Israel was led forty years through the desert, before they entered the land of promise to reign. So we too in this life, where there is the greatest anxiety, where there is fear, where there are dangers of temptations, are led, as it were through the desert, by the temporal dispensation. But when we have carried out well the number forty, that is, lived well in this temporal dispensation, walking according to the precepts of God, we will receive as a reward that ten of the faithful. Because the Lord too, when he hired laborers for the vineyard, gave them a denarius as a reward. To all a denarius, both those whom he hired in the morning, and those at midday, and those at the end of the day, to all he gave a denarius. Because if anyone is faithful from his early age, he is going to receive the denarius; now not through the distribution of times, but in the eternal contemplation, wisdom distinguishing the Creator from the creation; that he may fully enjoy the Creator, and from the creation praise the Creator. But suppose some young man believed, and in his earlier time was not faithful; he is going to receive the denarius. But suppose an old man believed, now at the setting of the day, as it were hired to the vineyard at the eleventh hour; he too is going to receive the denarius. And so to that forty well carried out, add the reward of the denarius, and it becomes the number fifty, which signifies the Church to come, where God will always be praised. But because all were called in the name of the Trinity, that they may live well in the number forty, and receive the denarius, multiply that fifty by three, and it becomes one hundred fifty. Add the very mystery of the Trinity, it becomes one hundred fifty-three, which number of fish was found on the right: in which number, nevertheless, are innumerable thousands of saints. Hence no bad ones will be cast away, because they will not be there: nor by any schism will the nets be torn, which are the bonds of unity and peace.

**12.** I think the great mystery has been sufficiently expounded. But you know that it pertains to us to work well in the time of forty, that we may be able to praise the Lord in the fifty. Therefore we celebrate those forty days, before we keep vigil, in labor and fasting and abstinence: for they signify the present of this time. But after the Lord's resurrection, because these days signify everlasting gladness (they are not yet this, but they signify this: the thing is in a mystery, brothers, not yet in fulfillment: for when Easter is celebrated, the Lord is not crucified: but as by an anniversary celebration we signify past things, so also future things that are not yet): in this time, then, the fasts are relaxed; for the number of these days signifies the rest to come. But see, brothers, lest by much drunkenness, as if you were permitted great extravagance, wishing to celebrate these days carnally, you fail to merit to celebrate forever what they signify, with the angels. For perhaps any drunkard whom I rebuke is going to say: You told us that these days signify everlasting gladness; you suggested to us that this time foretells angelic and heavenly joy: ought I not, then, to do well by myself? Would that it were well, and not badly. For it signifies joy to you, if you are the temple of God. But if you fill the temple of God with the uncleanness of drunkenness, the Apostle sounds to you: *Whoever corrupts the temple of God, God will corrupt him*[^7]. Let this be written in the hearts of your Holiness, that a man who understands less and lives better is better than one who understands much and does not live well. It is indeed fullness and perfect blessedness for one to understand quickly and to live well: but if perhaps one cannot do both, it is better to live well than to understand quickly. For he who lives well merits to understand more: he who lives badly will lose even what he understands. So it was said: *To him who has, it will be given; but from him who has not, even what he seems to have will be taken away*[^8].
