# Sermon 362/A: On the Resurrection of the Dead [on the Birthday of the Martyr Marcellinus]

**1.** If faith is present, the consolations of Christians are not empty. But that faith may be present, the examples of the saints have gone before and the documents of the books cry out. For why should we not believe the things which are narrated to us as done in past times, when we see done the things which were foretold as going to be? For those who foretold the future things which we behold narrated to us the past things. These things which we now see on earth, that all men leave the demons whom they were serving, that the images which they adored are broken, that everywhere the temples are overthrown and the whole human race is converted to one name, were foretold in the holy books, and when they were foretold they seemed greatly incredible. But because we see them fulfilled, they have become cheap, because they are ready to hand. Accordingly let us set before our eyes the lands and all the nations which were before, where there was no Christian at all, where sacrifice was offered to demons, stone altars were set up, sacrifices were offered to stones, priests were appointed and whatever is owed to the true God was rendered to false gods, that is, to demons. Let us set before our eyes such lands, and that there had arisen suddenly someone or other who said that all those things could quickly be changed and that, with those vain things abandoned, men would be converted to one God: who would not mock him? Who would not think him raving? Who would deign to hear him? Who would hesitate to drive him away? And yet these things have been done. But where these things which we see are written, which when they were said were not seen, there too are written the things which are not yet seen. What are the things which are not yet seen? That the Lord himself will come in the body in which he deigned here to appear and to die and rise again, but now immortal and incorruptible, as he abides in the heavens and sits at the right hand of the Father; that there will be a judgment; that all the dead will rise from the tombs; that all bodies will return from every consumption, and not only the monuments, but the world itself will give back from the elements what it received. When these things have been done, they will likewise not be wonders, as these things which have now been done are not wonders. But now they are not believed because they are not yet shown. Here faith is required. Believe what you do not yet see: what great thing will you do if you are going to believe then when you have seen? A reward is given to belief because it believed what it did not yet see, that it may rejoice from it when it sees; and again punishment is rendered to unbelief which was unwilling to believe what it did not yet see, that it may lament when it sees.

**2.** But this alone is wont as it were to move men, how consumed bodies will rise which are buried for this reason, because they offend the eyes when they are consumed; because they were dear to us with the souls which they had, but with the souls departing from there those things lie which we loved, and we do not wish them to be corrupted before our sight, therefore we bury them. When, then, it is said that they are going to rise, you attend, O human heart, to what now happens, and do you not believe what is to come? For if you weigh and judge rightly, you consider too little the secret and daily miracles of nature. For from where come forth in the cornfields the things which are not buried in the seed? From where do leaves grow green again on the trees? From where are the forests, bare in winter, clothed in summer? For this reason all these things are repaired, that we may believe that bodies too can be repaired in their time. It is more incredible that what was not should be established than that what was should be restored. Man who was not was established: and do you not believe that what was can be restored? These things which are buried are both flesh and bones: you, before you were born, what were you? You were led out of the hiding-places of nature, that in this appearance you might appear visible to the eyes: and do you not believe that when you have departed into the hiding-places of nature, he can lead you forth from there, who could create you before you were?

**3.** Finally let us believe this because he said it who cannot deceive. He who promised us the resurrection of bodies, when he was here in the body, raised the dead. He who promised us the resurrection of bodies, when he was here in the body, himself rose on the third day. Let us not believe even these things to have been done, if there did not follow from them the things which were foretold as going to follow. When the Lord appeared after the resurrection to his disciples, because they too did not believe that he rose, *they thought they saw a spirit. He said to them: Why are you troubled and thoughts ascend into your heart? See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself. Touch and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^1]. He offered himself not only to be seen by the eyes but also to be handled by the hands. *He spent with them forty days, going in and coming out, eating and drinking*[^2], but by power not by necessity. He persuaded them of the grace of the resurrection by the evidence of a true body, and before their eyes ascended into heaven. But when they saw him after the resurrection, *he said to them: These are the words which I spoke to you, while I was still with you, that all things must be fulfilled which are written in the law and the prophets and the psalms about me. Then he opened to them the understanding*—this the Gospel says—*that they might understand the Scriptures, and he said to them that so it is written and so it behooved Christ to suffer and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that penance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^3]. This the Gospel holds. See when it was written. For it was written then, when that was not which was there foretold. For Christ was passing and his disciples beheld him in the body in which they knew him. Was there then, when they beheld him in that body in which they knew him, that which follows? And what? *And that penance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem.* That was, this was not. That the disciples saw which was, they believed that which was not yet. We now see fulfilled: penance and the remission of sins are preached in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ through all the nations. Is it not done? Was it now already, when those things were being shown and when they were being written? As, then, the Apostles saw Christ and believed the future Church, so we see the Church, let us believe about Christ. For of these two they saw one, they believed the other. But what they saw we do not see. If we see what they believed, let us believe what they saw.

**4.** This our Lord Jesus Christ, then, the Word of the Father, the only-begotten Son, through whom all things were made, who deigned at an opportune time to assume flesh from the virgin Mary and to appear to human eyes, to do worthy things, to suffer unworthy things, to work divine things, to bear human things, to die, to rise again, to ascend into the heavens, this Lord our God, then, hear what he said about the future resurrection, and, with disputations set aside, believe, because he who said it cannot deceive: *The hour will come*, he says, *when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and will come forth, those who did good into the resurrection of life, but those who did evil into the resurrection of judgment*[^4]. Thanks be to God, then, on account of which those who did evil will rise &lt;...&gt; to judgment. All things, brothers, have been forgiven us in baptism. Believe, dearest brothers, that he whom he wished in a point of time to free from all sins, he grants to reign with him without end.

[^1]: Luke 24:37-39
[^2]: See Acts 1:3-4, 10:41
[^3]: See Luke 24:44-47
[^4]: John 5:28-29
