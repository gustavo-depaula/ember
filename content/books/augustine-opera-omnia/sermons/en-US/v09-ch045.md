# Sermon 335: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1.** Since it is the day of the holy martyrs, from what should it rather delight us to say something, than of their glory? May the Lord of the martyrs help us, because he himself is their crown. With the voice of the martyrs we heard a little before the blessed apostle Paul proclaiming: *Who will separate us from the love of Christ?* This is the voice of the martyrs: *Tribulation? or distress? or persecution? or famine? or nakedness? or peril? or sword? As it is written, For your sake we are put to death all the day long, we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter. But in all these things we more than conquer through him who loved us*[^1]. This is the voice of the martyrs, to endure all things, and to presume nothing of themselves, to love him who is glorified in his own: *that he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^2]. They knew too that which we sang a little before: *Rejoice in the Lord, and exult, you righteous*[^3]. If the righteous rejoice in the Lord, the unrighteous know not how to rejoice except in the world. But that is the first battle-line to be conquered: first the delights are to be conquered, and afterward the pains. How can he overcome the world raging, who cannot overcome it flattering? This world flatters, by promising honors, riches, pleasures: this world threatens, by holding out pains, want, humiliations. He who does not despise what it promises, how can he overcome what it threatens? Riches have their delight: who does not know it? But righteousness has more delight. Be delighted by riches with righteousness: but where such a moment of trial falls, that these two come into trial, riches and righteousness, and you cannot have both, but if you put your hand to riches, you must lose righteousness; if you put your hand to righteousness, riches perish: now choose, now fight; now let us see whether you sang not without cause: *Rejoice in the Lord, and exult, you righteous:* now let us see whether you heard not without cause: *Who will separate us from the love of Christ?* And indeed he passed over all the things with which the world flatters; and he wanted to recall to you, whom the world flatters. Why? Because he was foretelling the contests of the martyrs, those contests, namely, where they conquered persecution, hunger, thirst, want, ignominy, at the last the fear of death and the most savage enemy.

**2.** But see, brothers, that it is all the art of Christ that does it. The Apostle admonishes us, that we prefer the love of Christ to the world. How great distresses they suffer, who want to seize the goods of others! *Or persecution,* he says? Even that does not break them. Avarice is terrified, the greedy man seizes and fears the penalty, he boils for plunder. Many also suffer hunger, while they acquire and make gains, whom we order to fast, and they excuse themselves on account of the stomach. They have leisure all day to count their coins, and they sleep fasting. *Or nakedness,* he says? what shall I say of nakedness? Daily merchants escape naked from shipwreck, and again sail toward danger. From what do men daily run risk, except for the sake of acquiring riches? Nor does the sword forbid them. Forgery is a capital crime, and yet an inheritance is diminished. If, then, temporal desire merits this, why should not the inheritance of Christ merit it? The greedy man says in his heart, who perhaps does not dare with his tongue: Who will separate us from the desire of gold? Tribulation? or distress? or persecution? The greedy too can say to gold: For your sake we are killed all the day long. Excellently, then, do the holy martyrs say in the Psalm: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^4]. Distinguish, he says, my tribulation: the greedy too are troubled. Distinguish my distress: the greedy too suffer distress. Distinguish my persecutions: the greedy too suffer them. Distinguish my hunger: for acquiring gold the greedy too go hungry. Distinguish my nakedness: for gold the greedy too are stripped. Distinguish my death: for gold the greedy too die. What is: *Distinguish my cause? For your sake we are put to death all the day long.* They for the sake of gold, we for your sake. A like punishment, but a distinguished cause. Where the cause is distinguished, there the victory is certain. So if we look to the cause, we love the solemnities of the martyrs. Let us love in them, not the sufferings, but the causes of the sufferings. For if we loved the sufferings only, we will find many who suffer worse things in bad causes. But let us attend to the cause, attend to the cross of Christ: there was Christ, there were also thieves. A like punishment, but an unlike cause. One thief believed, the other blasphemed. The Lord, as from a tribunal, judged between both: him who blasphemed he condemned to Tartarus; the other he led with himself into paradise. Why this? Because, though the punishment was equal, the cause was unequal. So choose the causes of the martyrs, if you want to come to the palms of the martyrs.

[^1]: Rom 8:35-37
[^2]: 1 Cor 1:31
[^3]: Ps 32:11 (31:11)
[^4]: Ps 43:1 (42:1)
