# Sermon 159/A: (Dolbeau 13)

**1.** The solemnities of the holy martyrs are exhortations to martyrdoms, that it may delight us to imitate what it delights us to celebrate. To this both the holy Scripture, fresh in our ears, exhorts us and in a manner kindles us, the apostle saying: *Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? As it is written: For your sake we are put to death all the day long, we are counted as sheep for the slaughter*[^1]. Then follow other things that seem violent toward separation, in which, however, he gives the victory, that we not be separated from him who is not separated from us. For on account of these straits and impending pressures, the faithful promiser and kindly giver deigned to promise us that he would be with us *unto the consummation of the world*[^2]. *For I am sure,* he says, *that neither death nor life, neither angels nor principalities, neither things present nor things to come, neither power, nor height nor depth, nor any other creature*—for he could not name them all—*will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord*[^3]. And that he might make us most strong, lest we be separated from Christ, by hope and confidence against all the temptations of this world, because our strength in no way suffices if we are forsaken by divine aid, he said above: *If God is for us, who is against us?*[^4] The apostle, having briefly recalled certain things that seem to assail the Christian faith by raging, lest such things separate us from the love of Christ, armed us to endure all things for Christ.

**2.** But because not only the things that rage try to separate us, but also the things that flatter, just as we are armed with the apostolic words against the things that try to separate us from Christ by raging, so we are armed by the Lord Christ himself against the things that strive to seduce us by flattering. For it is to be feared lest perhaps one whom the cruel sword does not separate, carnal affection may separate. Therefore, against those things too which assail our faith by flattering, lest they storm what they assail, let us hear the Lord saying: *If anyone comes to me and does not hate father, mother, wife, children, brothers, and moreover his own soul, he cannot be my disciple. And he who does not bear his cross and follow me cannot be my disciple*[^5]. The Lord recalled both: both what is sweet by enticing, that it may deceive, and what is grievous by threatening. For against the flatteries of deception from carnal affection he says: *If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife, children* and the rest; but against those things which by their roaring and terrifying turn one away from the faith, he fortifies us, built up and armed with the one name of the cross. *He who does not,* he says, *bear his cross and follow me,* that is, he who does not tolerate—for this is to *bear*—all the things that are bitter and hard in this world as a cross, *and follow me, cannot be my disciple.*

**3.** Now, then, let us set the martyr of Christ in the midst between those who threaten and those who flatter; both doors are knocked at, by which one enters to the heart: desire and fear. The door of desire is knocked at by father, mother, wife, children, brothers: all are alluring, agreeable, sweet. But are they sweeter than God? Are they sweeter than Christ? If you do not believe, *taste and see how sweet the Lord is*[^6]. The door of fear is knocked at by threats, fury, reproaches, and finally the very pains of the body, which no one can fail to feel by despising them. For if he despises money, he suffers losses, not pains. All this is in the mind of the one who despises: he does not torture you who takes from you what you do not love. But you love money: you fear the one who threatens losses, nor does he torture you, but you have tortured yourself, because you have entangled yourself in pains. *For the love of money is the root of evils, which certain men coveting have strayed from the faith and entangled themselves in many pains*[^7]. He threatens losses: he who does not find you covetous, finds you free; you laughed at him as empty-handed, because you did not entangle yourself. So too the other things that we learn by healthful teaching to despise, we are admonished not to love, that we may be found free in temptations. To which freedom that apostolic reading exhorts us, where it is: *And let those who use this world be as though not using it*[^8], that they may have the use of liberty, not the affection of desire. *For the figure of this world is passing away. I wish you to be without solicitude*[^9].

**4.** But there remains the weakness of the flesh, which each of us, as long as he lives in this world, cannot put away from himself. What do you do? When the flesh is pricked, does the heart not grieve? It is a wound of weakness, until it takes on the garment of immortality. There is a great struggle there. The will turned away from money, the martyr of God stands secure against those who terrify with losses. "Let him take," he says, "what I do not love, where do I feel it?" He threatens exile, but threatens one by whom the heavenly fatherland alone is desired. Here the Christian, wherever he goes, is a pilgrim. He threatens reproaches. He has what to answer: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^10]. He threatens dishonor. How great is a transitory honor! A man threatens dishonor, but the Lord of men promises eternal honor. All these things despised, liberty is safe. But what of the flesh? There remains the soul with its garment, and that garment which only death takes off. Now the man, that is, the soul, is pressed from close by, from the weakness of his own flesh. Therefore those words of the Lord in the magnificent psalm, where almost the Gospel is recited, are seen, indeed understood, as words of one hanging on the cross, where it is said: *They have dug my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones. But they considered me, they divided my garments among themselves and cast lots over my clothing*[^11]. They are words of one already suffering, already hanging on the cross, and yet there it is said: *Do not depart from me, for tribulation is near*[^12]. If you understand *near* as "of approaching time," they will not be the words of one hanging on the cross: tribulation was already present, if he was hanging. But why was it said: *it is near?* It is already in the flesh. Nothing is nearer to the soul than the flesh which it carries. Other things are outside, they are called external, because they are outside. When the flesh is tortured, the soul is knocked at from close by.

**5.** These things are grave, *but in all these we more than conquer through him who loved us*[^13]. But why do we conquer in these too? Because *if God is for us, who is against us?* Therefore the Lord deigned by the name of the cross to signify all things terrible, hard, bitter, intolerable, and savage. For even among the very kinds of death, nothing is more intolerable than the cross. For it was added with great weight by the same apostle, when he commended to us the lowliness of the Lord; for he says: *He did not count it robbery to be equal to God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and found in form as a man*[^14]. How great the humbling of the Lord, to become a man! But hear yet more. See, he was humbled, because he became a man. What further? *He humbled,* he says, *himself, becoming obedient unto death*[^15], not only unto human birth, but even *unto death.* Do you have anything to add to this? "I do," he says: *even the death of the cross*[^16]. Looking to this end of the Lord, the bitterest of all, he called a cross all the things that the Christian flesh suffers rightly for his name, if the faith has endured them. For faith suffers nothing, indeed it even makes the sufferings tolerable. Equipped, then, on both sides, and having fortified both doors of desire and fear, desiring nothing more than what God promises, fearing nothing more than what God threatens, we drive off both flatterers and threateners.

**6.** But someone arises and makes us a question and says: "Whatever is bitter, whatever is hard, whatever is savage and harsh to endure must indeed be borne for the faith of Christ, must wholly be borne, but it troubles me that he who among his precepts set the first precept for loving the neighbor: *Honor your father and mother, which is the first commandment in a promise, that it may be well with you*[^17]; and when the apostle recalled this as a great thing, the Lord says to me: *If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother.* Where shall I hear? Which of these shall I take? How shall I comply with the one commanding that I honor father and mother, and likewise commanding that I hate father and mother? Is it not one and the same who commands both, or—as certain vain men think—is one he who gave the law and another he who propagated the Gospel, since in the law it is written: *Honor father and mother,* in the Gospel: *Whoever does not hate father and mother?*" Far from it! He himself is both the giver of the law and the propagator of the Gospel, he himself entirely. You, acknowledge the Lord: lest he command contrary things, he distributed the precepts among times. For if you think that the precept by which we are bidden to honor father and mother, because it is read earlier in the Old Testament, is contrary to the evangelical precept, in the same Old Testament there is written something such as what we just now heard in the Gospel when it was read. *He who says to father and mother: I do not know you, and who does not acknowledge his own sons, he has kept my covenant*[^18]. How alike it is, how twin, how equal it is recognized. Therefore the one Lord commands both, but let us distinguish the times.

**7.** The martyr is armed for the crown, and there comes a flattering father and mother, to hinder what God promises, promising a vain inheritance and taking away the eternity of light. Here clearly do not acknowledge those who hold you back; rather hate the father here, out of piety hate such a one, lest he remain such. So hate father, so mother, so children, so brothers, so the wife, in whom there is a greater question, when it has come to this point, hate even her, if she too tries to hinder. Beware of Eve: then she is not your member, but the serpent's instrument. You who once foolishly heard her first voice, and did not profit by experience, but that very wife, to hinder a martyr husband from his crown, with womanish wiles prescribes to the man, as it were, from the law and from the Gospel itself. "Hear," she says, "the precept." What precept? "*What God has joined, let not man separate*[^19]. Clearly hear it, do not spurn it. *What God has joined, let not man separate.*" But see, lest the wife separate you from God. "But what he has joined, man ought not to separate." Ought human affection separate a man from God himself? And where will be what you just now heard: *Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?* Here, then, do not fear: man does not separate what God has joined, but God separates the one who tries to separate. Do not, then, listen to your wife saying to you: "I am your member, see, you are a member." Answer: "If my member were rotting and by rotting were trying to make the whole body waste away, would it not be cut off by the physician? Do I not hear the Lord himself and true physician saying: *For it is expedient for you that one of your members perish, rather than that your whole body go into hell*[^20]?" Therefore answer, the law well understood, against one misusing the law. For the serpent does no great thing, attempts no great thing in wishing through Eve to deceive you from the law, who deceived the first Adam without the law. For he saw you now feeding on the law, and he sets a snare in your food. But say: *My eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he will pluck my feet out of the snare*[^21]. Therefore in this peril too, where you are tempted from the law, let your eyes be toward the Lord, because he too was tempted by the same serpent from the law.

**8.** For when the Lord, to set us an example of combat and victory, had deigned to be tempted by the devil, just as he deigned to be crucified by the impious, in the first temptation—for we read that it was threefold—he answered from the law the devil who said: *If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become bread*[^22], he from the law: *Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*[^23]. Again in another temptation: *Fall down and adore me, and I will give you these things*[^24], and he from the law: *It is written: You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*[^25]. But when that cunning serpent saw himself twice over repelled from the law, he stretched a third snare from the law: "*Cast yourself down,*" he says, from the pinnacle of the temple, "*if you are the Son of God*." And at once to the instrument of the law, that he might fight from where he had been overcome: "*For it is written,*" he says, "*He has given his angels charge over you, in their hands they will lift you up, lest perhaps you strike your foot against a stone*[^26]. Because, then," he says, "*if you are the Son of God,* the angels take you up lest you be hurt, *cast yourself* down and prove to us what you are." Did the Lord here, because the devil dared to lay his hand on the law, take his hand from the law? Here too from the same law he wounded the enemy, laid him low, made him go off confounded. For he says: *It is written: You shall not tempt the Lord your God*[^27]. Here too, then, when you see your wife trying to hinder you from martyrdom, reciting the law to you and saying: "*What God has joined, let not man separate,*" you too recite there the law of the Gospel: *Whoever comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children . . .*"

**9.** Do not, then, be angry, wife. The one with whom you here are angry in this cause, because he hates you, hates also the father in this cause, hates also the mother. But it is too little for the wife, because it is written: *A man shall leave father and mother and cleave to his wife*[^28]. For if the parents, that is, father and mother, say to the son: "If your wife does not wish to remain with us, you remain with us, and dismiss her," let the wife take up this precept and fight, prescribe to the man and say from the law of God: "Rather dismiss them and do not dismiss me. For I recite: *A man shall leave father and mother and cleave to his wife.*" You have recited, you have understood, you have conquered; clearly no one doubts that the father and mother trying to separate the son from his wife are to be dismissed, that the wife not be deserted. But attend, woman, if you deign to be faithful, and do not disdain to hear the word of faith, attend: whence you have conquered, thence allow yourself to be conquered. See, we restrain your father-in-law and your mother-in-law, the father and mother of your husband, who begot, who reared, who brought him to this age at which he became your husband; we wholly restrain them and we have reproved them by divine authority and said: "Do not wish to separate the son from his wife on your account, for he is better separated from you than from her. If it can be, be all together. But if this cannot be, it is better that he be with her than with you without her." We have pleaded your cause. You have conquered in this judgment: allow yourself to be conquered in another, because there too we plead your cause. Then, having faithfully pleaded the cause of your flesh, ought we to forsake the cause of your everlasting salvation? Learn. The father and mother of your husband did not separate you from your husband: you shall not separate your husband from your God.

**10.** There a fornication more to be feared is that against the chastity which the soul owes to God; there a fornication more to be feared, which the apostle feared, saying: *I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so your minds too be corrupted from the chastity of God which is in Christ Jesus*[^29]. You see that there too chastity is required. Why do you not wish to be separated from your husband? Lest you have a necessity of fornication? But in your husband's martyrdom you ought to fear nothing. For when he is crowned and translated from human affairs to divine ones, you will be a widow; if you marry, you will not be a fornicator. But perhaps, as the wife of a martyr, you blush to marry further. See, with your husband crowned, let your faith too be in safety. Do not draw your husband away from the embrace of the spiritual marriage. There too chastity is required, and more there because greater there, more there because eternal there. *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*[^30]. For what else do you do, that your husband be overcome by your flatteries, but that there be no virtue in him? In whom there will be no virtue, how badly off will your husband be! He is overcome, beaten down, made effeminate by your flatteries: how will he be your husband, who will no longer be a man? For what do you do, but that he yield to your flatteries, despise the precepts of God, deny Christ, sacrifice to idols? See, the one whose flesh you seek, his soul fornicates; see, he hears: *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you.* But you wish him to cleave to you and be separated from God: hear what follows: *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you; but for me it is good to cleave to God*[^31]. Hear your husband armed with faith, hurling against your iniquity the darts of justice: *I am sure,* he says, *that neither death nor life, neither angel nor principality, neither height nor depth, nor any other creature will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus*[^32]. Perhaps here you say: "But in all these he did not name the wife." In all these, you say, he did not name the wife. Deceitful one, the apostolic virtue kept watch even against you by adding: *nor any other creature.* Or will you perhaps dare to say that you are not a creature? If you are a creature, indeed because you are a creature, neither will you separate him whom no creature will separate.

**11.** We have spoken as though men hasten to martyrdoms and are hindered by their wives: let us also console the weaker sex. How many women have been crowned and were not overcome by husbands flattering unmanfully! Whence was Perpetua made perpetual, unless . . . perpetual? Whence was Felicity capable of such great felicity, unless because temporal unhappiness did not terrify her? Therefore, lest women too in this cause be seduced by the flatteries of husbands, let Perpetua be attended to by them, let Felicity be attended to, and let perpetual felicity be held fast.

**12.** Let not, then, father and mother be angry in such a cause, nor wife, nor children, nor brothers; and if they are angry, it was added: hate moreover your own soul. In this cause, then, you love your wife badly, you who are bidden also to hate your own soul. *For what does it profit a man,* he says, *if he gains the whole world but suffers the loss of his own soul?*[^33] And yet you are bidden to hate it too, lest it too seduce you, wishing to be here, wishing always to cleave to this flesh, unwilling to migrate to better things; hate such a soul, lest you have such a one. For what else does it whisper within in men whom the peril of persecution tempts, but "Deny, live: you will do penance afterward"? It is the soul: it wishes to perish. Hate it as it wishes to perish. Lest you permit it to perish, chastise, rebuke, fashion it, call God to your aid. For it withers from you and fails in the perils of this life, it contemplates eternal life with eyes of faith that are not straight: lift it up by exhortation and say: *Why are you sad, my soul, and why do you trouble me?*[^34] Perhaps it will answer you: "Because I am weak." Then you have what follows: *Hope in the Lord,* he will be your strength, he will be your firmness. *Hope in the Lord, for I will confess to him,* I will not deny him, for I will confess him. But you, soul, were murmuring: "Deny him." *I will confess to him: the salvation of my countenance and my God*[^35]. Therefore he who loves his soul, let him lose it here, that he may find it in eternal life. Therefore do not listen to those whispering contrary things, neither father nor mother, nor wife, nor children, nor your own soul.

**13.** Let the wife be heard with the fear of conjugal separation only so long as she does not urge something whereby God is offended. Thus all ranks will keep their order: virginity excelling, widowhood following, marriage third, none without reward. Let virgins think of Mary, widows of Anna, the married of Susanna. All have their rewards, and from all these ranks martyrs have by no means been lacking. The husband fears in the crisis of martyrdom lest his wife be Eve to him; the wife too fears in the crisis of martyrdom lest her husband be a serpent to her. But let neither hear anyone urging something against God. Thus far let carnal affection avail; let not the weakness of the flesh transcend the spiritual wall: let it be below, because it is below. Let the soul, subject to God, lead the flesh; let not the soul be seduced from God into the rule of the flesh: that is perverse. You wish to have rule in your own house, much more does God in his. Hold and meditate on these things, imitate these things in all the solemnities of the martyrs, *and the God of peace will be with you*[^36]. Turned to the Lord . . .
