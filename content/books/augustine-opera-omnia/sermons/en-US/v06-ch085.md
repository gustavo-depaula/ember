# Sermon 229/P: On the Love of Peter

**1.** Those things are read according to the evangelist John which were done after the resurrection of the Lord. Among them, this was read today, how the Lord questioned the apostle Peter whether he loved him more than the rest[^1]. In this Peter, call to mind the rock. For he it is who, when the Lord asked him whom his disciples said he was, answered: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.* On hearing this, Jesus said to him: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven. And I say to you*—as though to say: You said to me, I say to you. What?—*You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell will not conquer it. To you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven. Whatever you bind on earth will be bound also in heaven; whatever you loose on earth will be loosed also in heaven*[^2]. We see the rock commended in Peter. And the apostle Paul says of the former people: *They drank of the spiritual rock that followed them; and the rock was Christ*[^3]. Therefore this disciple is "Peter" from the rock, as "Christian" is from Christ. Why did I wish to say these things first? That I might commend to you the Church, to be recognized in Peter. For Christ built the Church not upon a man but upon Peter's confession. What is Peter's confession? *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^4]. Behold the rock, behold the foundation, behold where the Church is built which the gates of hell do not conquer[^5]. What are the gates of hell, but the pride of the heretics?

**2.** This Peter, then, bearing so great a person as I have commended to you, is questioned by the Lord after the resurrection, as was read, and it is said to him: *Simon, son of John*—for he was called Simon from his birth: he was the son of John—*Simon, son of John, do you love me more than these?*[^6] Who questions? He who knew all things[^7]. Like one who did not know? *He who gave knowledge.* The Lord himself did not wish to be instructed, but he wished the other to confess. He asks once, and the other says to him: *You know, Lord, that I love you.* And he: *Feed my lambs.* And again he asks, as though once were not enough: *Simon, son of John, do you love me?* I love you, Lord, I love you, Lord: again this. And again he that: *Feed my lambs.* And a third time—For whom is once not enough? For one who does not know it is enough. How much more for one who foreknows!—he asks a third time: *Do you love me? Peter was made sorrowful,* as though for this reason he were continually questioned, because he was doubted of, and he says: *Lord, you know all things: you know that I love you*[^8]. If anything were hidden from you, you would do well to question me. If you know all things, you know in all things this that you ask. And he: *Feed my sheep*[^9]. Three times love confessed, which fear had three times denied. Therefore the Lord questioned three times, that a threefold confession might blot out the threefold denial.

**3.** This is that Peter, the denier and the lover; the denier through human weakness, the lover through divine grace. For when Peter denied, he was shown to himself. For he had presumed and had in a way fanned the air with his own strength in proud boasting, when he said: *Lord, I am with you, even unto death*[^10], presuming on his own strength. And then indeed he heard who he was. For the sick man was presuming about himself, and the physician was touching the pulse of his heart. In his inmost parts he found the sick man, and like a true and truthful physician he foretold what was to come, as though saying: Even though you seem well to yourself. You do not know that there is still a fever, but now there is a revelation to you. You will be troubled when the onset comes. So when he was questioned by the maidservant, and there was fulfilled in him what the physician foretold, not what the sick man presumed—that before the cock crowed, such a servant should three times deny the Lord—what is written in the Gospel after he had three times denied? *The Lord looked back at him, and he wept bitterly*[^11]. He would never have wept, if the Lord had not looked back. Finally, that you may understand how it was said: *He looked back at him,* inspect the Gospel and see that the Lord was in such a place when Peter denied, that he could not look back at him with bodily eyes; for he himself was being heard by the chief men of the Jews in the inner part of the house. But Peter denied in the courtyard. But where does he not look back, who is everywhere?

**4.** On account of this he was questioned about love, and the sheep of Christ were entrusted to him. It was said to him: *Feed my lambs. Feed my sheep*[^12]. That Lamb was entrusting lambs. He was entrusting his lambs, who made lambs for himself. How did the Lamb make lambs for himself? *Behold the Lamb of God.* Of him it was said: *Behold the Lamb of God.* And how did he make lambs for himself? *Behold him who takes away the sin of the world*[^13]. Thus he made these too whom we now see in white. Would that the wolves may not devour them! Do you, Lord, guard them, who entrusted them to Peter. Do you guard them, who guard Peter too. He himself is also the sheep of whom it is written: *He was led like a sheep to the slaughter*[^14]. He is always a lamb, because always without sin. And do not wonder that he is without sin: *In the beginning was the Word, the Word was with God, the Word was God*[^15]. Whence could there be sin there? Look to the human birth. He willed to be born without sin, whom a virgin conceived, whom no carnal concupiscence desired. The conception of the Son: the faith of the mother. *He was born of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary*, not drawing original sin from Adam, not drawing it, not adding it. Born innocently, living innocently, dying innocently. *Behold the Lamb of God; behold him who takes away the sin of the world.* When he entrusted his sheep to Peter, he was entrusting us. When he was entrusting us to Peter, he was entrusting his members to the Church. Entrust, then, O Lord, your Church to your Church, let your Church entrust itself to you. For we say: *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory*[^16]. For what are we without you, but Peter when he denied you three times? The Lord, to show Peter to himself, that is, to show Peter to Peter, turned his face away from him a little, and he denied. He turned his face to him when he looked back, and he wept. With tears he washed away his fault, he poured water from his eyes and baptized his conscience. Let us look upon him in a certain psalm where it is written: *I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever*[^17]. Who, if not Peter, is saying: *I am with you even unto death*[^18], and *if I must die for you, I will not depart from you*[^19]? In his abundance he says it. What does he say? *I shall not be moved forever.* But after he was questioned, denied three times, and wept, see what he adds—for they are the words of one now repenting—: *I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever. Lord, in your will you furnished strength to my beauty. You turned away your face from me, and I became troubled*[^20]. You turned away your face, and you showed me my own face: *I do not know what you say; I do not know the man*[^21]. Behold Peter himself in himself; behold what it is: *You turned away your face, and I became troubled.* Therefore the Lord, by looking back, gave back his face, and he was made firm.
