# Sermon 249: In the Days of Easter

**1.** We have heard the Gospel, how the Lord Jesus, after the resurrection, appeared to the disciples fishing at the sea of Tiberias. When he first called them, he had said to them: *Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men*[^1]. And then indeed, when they were called, casting the nets at his word, they caught a great multitude of fish; but the number was not given. Then in that first fishing he had not said to them: *Cast the net on the right side*: but had only said: *Cast*; nor did he say, On the right; nor, On the left. And so great a number of fish was caught that it was without number, and their boats were loaded. And how were they loaded? Thus the Gospel speaks: *So that they were almost sinking*[^2]. Then he said to them what I recalled: *Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men.* We belong to those very nets, we are indeed caught by those nets; but we do not remain captives. Let a man not fear to be caught: if he can be caught, he cannot be deceived. But what does this last fishing mean, about which the Gospel was read today? The Lord appeared to the fishermen, standing on the shore, and asked them whether they had any food. They said they had none; for they had caught nothing the whole night. And he said to them: *Cast on the right side*: which he had not said then. And they did; and they could not draw the nets for the multitude of fish. And there were found one hundred fifty-three fish. And since in that first fishing it was said that the nets were broken by the multitude of fish, it belonged to the care of the Evangelist in this fishing to say: *And though they were so great, the net was not torn*[^3].

**2.** Let us distinguish the two fishings; one before the resurrection, the other after the resurrection. In that one the nets are cast at random: the right is not named, lest only the good be understood; the left is not named, lest only the bad be understood: therefore good and bad are mixed. And the nets were broken by the multitude. The broken nets signify schisms. We see it, so it is, so it happens. The two boats are filled because of the two peoples of the circumcision and the uncircumcision: and they are so filled that they are pressed, and almost sink. What this signifies is to be groaned over. The crowd troubled the Church. How great a number have those living badly made, pressing and groaning? But because of the good fish the boats were not sunk. Let us discuss that latest fishing after the resurrection. No bad one is there, great security, but only if you will be good. Among the bad be good, and you will be good without the bad. In this fishing there is something for you to be moved about: you are among the bad. O you who hear me faithfully, O you to whom what I say is not lost, O you in whom the word does not pass through the ears, but descends into the heart, O you who fear more to live badly than to die badly: because if you have lived well, you cannot die badly: you, then, who hear me, so that you not only believe, but also live well; live well, and among the bad live well, do not break the nets. Those who pleased themselves too much, and as it were refused to bear with the bad, broke the nets, perished in the sea. Live well among the bad, let not bad Christians persuade you to live badly. Let not your heart say: I alone am good. If you have begun to be good; believe that there are others too, if you yourself could be so. Do not commit adultery, do not fornicate, do not commit fraud, do not steal, do not bear false witness, do not swear falsely, do not get drunk, do not deny a thing lent, do not fail to return what you have found of another's in the street. Do these and such things, secure among the bad fish. You swim within the same nets; but you will come to the shore, after the resurrection you will be found on the right. There no one is bad. For what does it profit you that you know the law, that you know the commandments of God, that you know what is good and bad, if you do not do them? Is not the conscience itself punished by that very knowledge? Learn so as to do.

**3.** The commandments of God, because of the great mystery of perfection, are contained in the Decalogue. The ten precepts of the Law are written on tablets of stone by the finger of God, that is, the Holy Spirit: on one tablet, the precepts that pertain to God; on the other, those that pertain to man. Why this? Because on the love of God and of neighbor hang the whole Law and the Prophets. But what do these ten avail? The law was given, but *if a law had been given that could give life, righteousness would be entirely from the law*[^4]. You know the law, and you do not do the law; *The letter kills*: but that you may do what you know: *but the Spirit gives life*[^5]. Let the seven be added to the ten. For as the law is signified by the Decalogue, so the Holy Spirit is shown to be sevenfold. He himself is invoked over the baptized, that God may give them, according to the prophet, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding: see, two. The Spirit of counsel and fortitude: see, four. The Spirit of knowledge and piety: see, six. The Spirit of the fear of the Lord: see, seven[^6]. When these seven have been added, they become ten. What did I say? It is as it were absurd: When seven have been added to ten, they become ten: as if I have forgotten how to count. So I should have said: When seven have been added to ten, they become seventeen. This all know: for when I said, When seven have been added to ten, they become ten; were not these boys laughing at me? And yet I say it, and I repeat it, I do not blush. When you have understood, you will not blame the one counting, but love the one discoursing. Those ten precepts of the law are: but I have also enumerated the seven cooperations of the Holy Spirit. When these seven have been added, they become ten: when the Holy Spirit has been added, the law is fulfilled. But if these seven have not been added, those ten do not come to be: they will be in the letter, but the letter kills; knowledge makes a transgressor. Let the Spirit be added, and the law is fulfilled, by the help of God, not by your strength. So see: let us not love too much to belong to those ten. *For if righteousness is from the law, then Christ died for nothing*[^7]. But to what should we belong? to those seven? It is as if we could do, but did not know what we are doing. Therefore let us belong to seventeen. The law commands, the Spirit helps: the law deals with you, that you may know what to do; the Spirit, that you may do it. Therefore let us belong to seventeen, and let us count seventeen, and we will find ourselves in one hundred fifty-three. You already know it, I have often said it, often shown it. From one to four are ten: but only if you add them all. Let two follow one: add the two also, now they are three: after two let three follow, now they are six: after three let four follow, now they are ten. Why do I wear myself out? I speak what you know. Add the rest of the numbers, and you will arrive. When you have arrived at seventeen, by growing you will arrive at one hundred fifty-three. What is by growing? By making progress, as it were step by step, you will arrive at the right hand. Obey us, count for yourselves.
