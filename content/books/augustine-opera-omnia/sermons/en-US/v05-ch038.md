# Sermon 175

**1. 1.** What was just now read from the holy Gospel, this the apostle Paul too says, whose words are these: *A faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the first*[^1]. There was no cause for Christ the Lord to come, except to save sinners. Take away diseases, take away wounds, and there is no cause for medicine. If the great physician came from heaven, a great sick man was lying throughout the whole world. That sick man is the human race. But not all have faith[^2]. The Lord knows who are his[^3]. The Jews were proud, they exalted themselves, they were high-minded, they thought themselves righteous, and they moreover accused the Lord for gathering sinners. Those, then, who were proud and high-minded were left on the mountains, they belong to the ninety-nine. What is, they were left on the mountains? They were left in earthly fear. What is, they belong to the ninety-nine? They are on the left, not on the right. For ninety-nine are counted on the left; add one, and one passes to the right. *For he came*, as he himself says in another place, *The Son of man came to seek and to save what had perished*[^4]. For all had perished: from the time the one sinned, in whom was the whole, the whole perished. But one came without sin, who would save from sin. But those, by being proud—which is worse—were both sick and believed themselves healthy.

**2. 2.** Those are more dangerously sick who have lost their mind to fevers. They laugh, and the healthy weep. For the frenzied man laughs; but he is not healthy. Moreover, one who is of sound mind weeps over the frenzied man laughing. First, if you set these two before someone: Which is better, to laugh or to weep? who would not choose for himself to laugh? Finally, on account of the wholesome grief of penitence, the Lord placed in weeping the duty, in laughter the benefit. How? When he says in the Gospel, *Blessed are those who weep, for they will laugh*. Therefore in weeping is the duty, in laughter is the reward of wisdom. For he placed laughter for joy, not cackling, but exultation. So if you set these two before someone, and ask which of them is better, to laugh or to weep, every man does not wish to weep, and wishes to laugh. Moreover, if you add persons to these affections, and so set it forth with persons: Which is better, for a frenzied man to laugh, or a healthy man to weep? a man chooses for himself weeping with health rather than laughter with madness. So much does the health of the mind avail, that it is chosen even with mourning. Those, then, who thought themselves healthy were much more dangerously and desperately sick; and by that very sickness by which they had lost their minds, they even struck the physician. It is too little to say they struck him: I will say it all; they not only struck him, but even killed him. But he, even while he was being killed, was a physician, he was being beaten, and he was healing; he bore with the frenzied man, nor did he forsake the sick man: he was held, he was bound, he was struck with blows, he received the blows of a reed, he was mocked, he was insulted, finally he was heard, condemned, hung on the wood, on every side raged at; and he was a physician.

**3. 3.** You recognize the frenzied; recognize the physician too. *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^5]. They raged with lost minds, and by raging poured out the physician's blood; but he made from his very blood a medicine for the sick. For not truly in vain did he say, *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*. A Christian prays, and is heard; does Christ pray, and is not heard? For he who hears with the Father, because he is God, how is he not heard as man, which he was made for us? Plainly he was heard. There they were, there they raged; of them were those who reproached him and said, *Behold, he eats with tax collectors and sinners*[^6]. They were in that very people, by whom the physician himself was being killed, and in whose blood an antidote was being prepared for them too. For since the Lord poured out not only blood, but even spent his very death for the confection of the medicine; he rose again to demonstrate the example of resurrection. By his patience he suffered, to teach our patience; and in his resurrection he demonstrated the reward of patience. Likewise, as you know, and we all confess, he ascended into heaven, then from him the Holy Spirit was sent, having been promised before. For he had said to his disciples, *Sit in the city, until you are clothed with power from on high*[^7]. So his promise came too, the Holy Spirit came, filled the disciples, they began to speak in the tongues of all nations: a sign of unity went forth in them. For then one man was speaking in all tongues; because the unity of the Church was going to speak in all tongues. Those who heard were astonished. For they knew that the men had been unlearned, of one tongue only; and they marveled and were amazed that men of one tongue, or at most two, were speaking in the tongues of all nations: they were suspended in amazement, they lost their elation, they were made from a mountain into valleys. Now if they are humble, they are valleys; what you pour into them they take in, they do not let go. If water comes upon a height, it runs down and flows off: if water comes to a hollow and low place, it is both taken in and stands. Such were those now; they were amazed, they marveled, they had lost their savagery.

**4. 4.** Finally, as Peter spoke to them, they were pricked, and there was fulfilled in them what the Psalm had foretold: *I was turned to misery, while a thorn was driven in*[^8]. What is the *thorn*? The pricking of penitence. So you have the very words of Scripture in *the Acts of the Apostles*: *They were pricked in heart, and said to the Apostles: What shall we do*? Why did they say, *What shall we do*? We know what we have done: *what shall we do*? As far as concerns our deed, it is despair of salvation: let there be, then, in your counsel, if it can be, some hope of health. We know what we have done, tell us what we should do. What is it that we have done? For we did not kill just any man; and we would have done much evil if we had killed any innocent man. We chose a robber, we killed the innocent; we chose a dead man, we killed the physician; tell us: *what shall we do*? And Peter: *Do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^9]; that you may pass over from the ninety-nine to the hundred: because, when you were ninety-nine, you did not think penance necessary for yourselves, and you moreover insulted the Lord gathering sinners and wishing to make them penitent. Now, then, pricked, because you have come to know your sin, *do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*; let him be baptized in the name of him whom you killed without crime: and your sins are forgiven you. They were led back into hope: they grieved, they groaned, they were converted, they were healed. They were the very ones: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^10].

**5. 5.** Let each one, then, dearly beloved, when he hears that the Lord Jesus Christ came not for the sake of the righteous, but for the sake of sinners, not love to be a sinner; lest perhaps he say in his heart: If I am righteous, Christ does not love me; if I am a sinner, he loves me: because he came down for the sake of sinners, not for the sake of the righteous. For he answers you: If you have recognized the physician, why have you not feared the fever? Surely the physician comes to the sick man, it is agreed: but for this reason the physician comes to the sick man, that he may not be always sick. What, then, do we say? what do we pronounce? what do we define? Does the physician love the sick man, or the healthy? He loves what he wishes to make; not what he finds. To the sick man indeed he comes, to the healthy he does not come: do not attend to the fact that he comes to the one, and does not come to the other; for he loves the healthy more than the sick. For, that you may know that he loves the healthy more than the sick; would he do what he hated?

**5. 6.** Therefore attend to the apostle Paul: *A faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the first*[^11]. He said: *of whom I am the first*. How was he the first? Were there not so many Jewish sinners before him? Were there not sinners in the human race before him? Was no one before him, among all men, held by sin? Was Adam not before him, who first sinned, and cast us all headlong into death? What is, *of whom I am the first*? For that for which he came, I am the first? But this is not true either. Peter was chosen first, Andrew first, the other apostles first; you are the last apostle; how do you say, *of whom I am the first*? Therefore, the last apostle, the first sinner. And how the first sinner? Peter sinned before you, when he denied the Lord himself three times[^12]. I do not wish to say that he too, unless he had been found a sinner, would not have passed from the left to the right.

**6. 7.** What, then, is, *of whom I am the first*? Because I am worse than all. Therefore he wished "the first" to be understood as the worse. Just as among craftsmen, whoever wishes to build, what does he say? Who is the first builder here? who is the first workman? Or if he wishes to be cured: Who is the first physician here? He surely does not ask who is first in age, or who is first in profession; but who is first in skill. Just as those are first in skill, so this one is first in iniquity. Why is Paul first in iniquity? Recall Saul, and you will find out. You attend to Paul, you have forgotten Saul: you attend to the shepherd, you have forgotten the wolf. Is he not the one for whom, to stone Stephen, one hand was not enough, and he kept the garments of others? Is he not the one who persecuted the Church everywhere? Is he not the one who received letters from the chief priests? Because it was too little for him to persecute the Christians who were in Jerusalem; but he wished to come to other places, where he might find them, and bind them, and bring them to be punished. Was he not, while making the journey, breathing and panting for slaughter, struck from heaven, and, thunderstruck, did he not hear the voice of the Lord unto salvation? While he walks, he is laid low; that he may see, he is blinded. He himself, then, is the one who was first a persecutor, none was worse than he.

**7. 8.** Hear something whence you may understand more. The Lord Christ himself was speaking to Ananias, when that one was already laid low, already raised up; and he said to him, *Go to that street, there you will find Saul of Tarsus in Cilicia, speak to him*. For he saw the man Ananias coming in to him, and baptizing him. He heard the name of Saul, and trembled between the hands of the physician himself. But what is sweeter—whence Saul was named, I believe you recall, and on account of those who do not recall, let me mention it. Saul was that persecutor of David. In David Christ was, in David Christ was prefigured, in Saul Saul was prefigured: as it were David to Saul from heaven, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^13]? "Ananias" is interpreted "Sheep": the shepherd was speaking to the sheep, and the sheep feared the wolf. So great a fame of this wolf had gone before, that the sheep did not think itself safe, not even between the hands of the shepherd. And the Lord to him, as to a trembling sheep. For when he had heard this, he said, *Lord, I have heard about this man how great evils he did to your holy ones in Jerusalem, and now it is said that he received letters from the chief priests, that whomever he found he might lead bound*[^14]. Where do you send me? a sheep to a wolf? But he did not hear this excuse. For he had already said to his few little sheep, *Behold, I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves*[^15]. If the sheep have been sent into the midst of wolves, why do you tremble to go, Ananias, to one who is no longer a wolf? You feared the wolf; but the Lord your God answers you: From a wolf I have made a sheep; I make from a sheep a shepherd.

**8. 9.** Therefore, just as Saul himself, afterward Paul, gives thanks that he came to the mercy of God, because he was found first—that is, eminent—in sins: *And yet I obtained mercy, that in me Christ Jesus might show all his long-suffering, for the sake of those who would believe in him unto eternal life*[^16]; so that all may say to themselves: If Paul was healed, why do I despair? If by so great a physician so desperate a sick man was healed, why will I not fit those hands to my wounds? why will I not hasten to those hands? That men might say this, for this reason Saul was made from a persecutor an apostle. For wherever the physician comes, he seeks someone there in despair, and heals him: and even if he finds a most poor man, yet let him find a desperate one; there he seeks not a fee, but commends his art. Let me say, then, what I had begun. Just as Saul gives thanks to Christ that he, a sinner, was taken up and healed, nor said: Let me remain in sin, because for my sake Christ came, not for the sake of the righteous; so too you, who had heard that Christ came for the sake of sinners[^17], do not sleep for yourself in the sweet bed; but hear Paul himself saying, *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you*[^18]. Do not love the bed of sin. *You have turned all his bed in his infirmity*[^19], it was said before. Rise, be healthy, love health, and do not again through pride go from the right to the left, from the valley to the mountain, from humility to swelling. When you have been made healthy—that is, when you have begun to live justly—ascribe it to God, not to yourself. For not by praising yourself were you made safe, but by pronouncing against yourself. For if you praise yourself through pride, you will be more gravely sick. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled; and he who humbles himself will be exalted[^20]. Let us turn to the Lord, etc.
