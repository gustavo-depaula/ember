# Sermon 194: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1. 1.** Hear, children of light, adopted into the kingdom of God; dearest brothers, hear; hear, and exult, you righteous, in the Lord, that *praise may befit the upright*[^1]. Hear what you know, recall what you have heard, love what you believe, preach what you love. As we celebrate this day yearly, so await the sermon due to this day. Christ was born, God of the Father, man of the mother. Of the Father's immortality, of the mother's virginity. Of the Father without a mother, of the mother without a father. Of the Father without time, of the mother without seed. Of the Father the beginning of life, of the mother the end of death. Of the Father ordering every day, of the mother consecrating this day.

**2. 1.** For he sent ahead as a forerunner the man John, who was to be born then, when the days began to shorten: and he himself was born when the days began to lengthen, that by this might be prefigured what the same John says: *He must increase, but I must decrease*[^2]. For human life ought to fail in itself, to advance in Christ; so that *those who live may live no longer for themselves, but for him who died and rose again for all*[^3]: and let each one of us say what the Apostle says: *It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me*[^4]. For *he must increase, but I must decrease*.

**2. 2.** All his angels praise him worthily, whose food he is, eternal, giving them life with incorruptible nourishment; for he is the Word of God, by whose life they live, by whose eternity they live forever, by whose goodness they live forever blessed. They praise him worthily, God with God, and *give glory to God in the highest*[^5]. *But we are his people and the sheep of his hands*[^6]; according to the measure of our weakness, let us, reconciled, merit peace through good will. For the voice of those very angels is truly today's voice, which, exulting, they poured forth when the Savior was born for us: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^7]. They, then, praise fittingly; let us too praise obediently. They are his messengers, we too are his flock. He filled their table in heaven, he filled our manger on earth. For the fullness of their table is that *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^8]. The fullness of our manger is that *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^9]. For that man might eat the bread of the angels, the creator of the angels was made man. They praise by living, we by believing: they by enjoying, we by asking: they by receiving, we by seeking: they by entering, we by knocking.

**3. 3.** For who among men could know all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden in Christ[^10], and concealed in the poverty of his flesh? For *he became poor for our sake, though he was rich, that by his poverty we might be enriched*[^11]. For when he took up mortality and consumed death, he showed himself in poverty: but he promised riches deferred, he did not lose them taken away. How great is the abundance of his sweetness, *which he has hidden for those who fear him, but accomplishes for those who hope in him*[^12]! For *we know in part, until that which is perfect comes*[^13]. That we might be made fit to grasp him, he who is equal to the Father in the form of God, made like us in the form of a servant, refashions us to the likeness of God: and, made the son of a man, the only Son of God makes many sons of men sons of God; and, having nourished servants through the visible form of a servant, he perfects them as free to see the form of God. For *we are children of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be. And we know that when he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^14]. For what are those treasures of wisdom and knowledge, what are those divine riches, except that they suffice for us? And what is that abundance of sweetness, except that it satisfies us? *Show us*, then, *the Father, and it is enough for us*[^15]. And in a certain psalm, one of us, or in us, or for us, says to him: *I shall be satisfied when your glory is made manifest*[^16]. But he and the Father are one: and he who sees him sees also the Father[^17]. Therefore *the Lord of hosts, he is the King of glory*[^18]. Turning us, he will show us his face; *and we shall be saved*[^19], and we shall be satisfied, and it will suffice for us.

**4. 4.** Conclusion. So let our heart say to him: *I have sought your face; your face, Lord, will I seek; turn not your face from me*[^20]. And let him himself answer our heart: *He who loves me keeps my commandments: and he who loves me will be loved by my Father; and I will love him, and will show myself to him*[^21]. Those to whom he said this surely saw him with their eyes, and heard the sound of his voice with their ears, and thought of a man with a human heart: but he was promising to show that very one whom *eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^22], to those who love him. Until that comes to pass, until he shows us what suffices us, until we drink of him, the fountain of life, and are satisfied[^23]; meanwhile, while, walking by faith, we are pilgrims away from him[^24], while we *hunger and thirst for righteousness*[^25] and long for the beauty of the form of God with ineffable ardor, let us with devout homage celebrate the birthday of the form of a servant. We cannot yet contemplate that he was begotten of the Father *before the morning star*[^26]; let us frequent the fact that in the night hours he was born of a virgin. We do not yet grasp that *his name remains before the sun*[^27]; let us recognize his tabernacle set in the sun[^28]. We do not yet behold the Only-begotten remaining in his Father; let us remember the bridegroom coming forth from his chamber. We are not yet fit for the banquet of our Father; let us recognize the manger of our Lord Jesus Christ.
