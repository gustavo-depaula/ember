# Sermon 205: In Lent

**1.** We enter on this present day the observance of Lent, presented in its solemn return: on which our exhortation too is solemnly owed to you; that the word of God, ministered through our office, may feed in the heart those who are about to fast in body; and so that the inner man, refreshed with his own food, may be able to carry out the chastisement of the outer, and to sustain it more robustly. For it befits our devotion that we, who are about to celebrate the passion of the crucified Lord, now drawing near, should make for ourselves too a cross of repressing fleshly pleasures, as the Apostle says: *But those who are Christ's have crucified their flesh with its passions and desires*[^1]. On this cross, indeed, throughout this whole life, which is led amid temptations, the Christian ought perpetually to hang. For in this life there is no time for plucking out the nails, of which it is said in the Psalm: *Pierce my flesh with nails by your fear*[^2]. The flesh is the fleshly desires; the nails are the precepts of righteousness: with these the fear of the Lord pierces them, which crucifies us as an acceptable victim to him. Whence the Apostle likewise says: *I beseech you therefore, brothers, by the mercy of God, to present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God*[^3]. This cross, then, on which the servant of God is not only not confounded, but even glories, saying: *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^4]: this cross, I say, is not of forty days, but of this whole life, which is signified by the mystic number of these forty days; whether because man, who is to lead this life, is formed, as some assert, in forty days in the womb; or because the four Gospels agree with the law of ten, and four tens mark this number, and show us that both Scriptures are necessary for us in this life; or for some other more probable cause, which a better and clearer understanding can find. Whence both Moses and Elijah and the Lord himself fasted forty days: that it might be intimated to us, in Moses and in Elijah and in Christ himself, that is, in the Law and the Prophets and in the Gospel itself, that this is dealt with among us, that we should not be conformed and cling to this age, but should crucify the old man, conducting ourselves not in revelings and drunkennesses, not in beds and shameless acts, not in strife and rivalry; but should put on the Lord Jesus, and make no provision for the flesh in its desires[^5]. So always live here, Christian: if you do not want to plunge your steps into the earthly mire, do not come down from this cross. But if this must be done throughout this whole life, how much more through these days of Lent, in which this life is not only enacted, but also signified?

**2.** On the other days, then, let not your hearts be weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness[^6]; but on these days also fast. On the other days, do not touch adulteries, fornications, and all unlawful corruptions: but on these days abstain even from your spouses. What you take from yourself by fasting, add by giving in alms. The time that was occupied in rendering the marriage debt, let it be spent on supplications. The body that was loosened by fleshly affections, let it be prostrated in pure prayers. The hands that were entangled in embraces, let them be stretched out in prayers. But you who fast also on the other days, on these increase what you do. You who on the other days crucify the body with perpetual continence, on these cleave to your God with more frequent and more intent prayers. All of one mind, all faithful faithfully, all sighing in this pilgrimage with longing for the one homeland, and fervent with love. Let no one envy in another, let no one mock, a gift of God which he himself does not have. In spiritual goods, count as your own what you love in a brother: let him count as his own what he loves in you. Let no one, under the appearance of abstinence, strive rather to change than to cut off delights; so as to seek precious foods, because he does not eat meat, and unwonted liquors, because he does not drink wine: lest, under the pretext of as it were taming the flesh, he rather carry on the business of pleasure. To the clean, indeed, all foods are clean[^7]: but luxury is clean in nothing.

**3.** Above all, brothers, fast from quarrels and discords. Remember the prophet reproaching certain ones and crying out: *In the days of your fasting your own wills are found, because you goad all who are under your yoke, and strike with fists; your voice is heard in shouting*[^8], and other such things. To these, having recalled them, he added: *This is not the fast I have chosen, says the Lord*[^9]. If you wish to shout, frequent that shout of which it is written: *With my voice I cried to the Lord*[^10]. For that is not of strife, but of love; not of the flesh, but of the heart. It is not such as that of which it is said: *I waited for it to do judgment, but it did iniquity; and not righteousness, but a shout*[^11]. *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given you*[^12]. These are the two wings of prayer, by which it flies to God: if one forgives the one who has offended, and gives to the needy.
