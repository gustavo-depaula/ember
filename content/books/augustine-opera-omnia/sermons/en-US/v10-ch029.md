# Sermon 354/A: From a Sermon on the Good of Marriage

**1.** *Let him who can grasp it, grasp it*[^1]. "But I cannot," he says. You cannot? "I cannot." A certain nurturing authority of the Apostle takes you up, that *if they do not contain themselves, let them marry*. Let something be done, whence one may come to pardon. Let it belong to pardon, lest one rush into eternal punishment. Let what is lawful be done, whence what is not lawful may be forgiven. This is what indicates that which follows: *I would rather they marry than burn*[^2]. He conceded, I say, something to incontinence, because he feared something greater: he feared eternal punishments, he feared what awaits and remains for adulterers. Even this, that married people, conquered by concupiscence, use one another beyond the necessity of procreating children, I will place among those things for which it is daily said: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^3].

[^1]: Matt 19:12
[^2]: 1 Cor 7:9
[^3]: Matt 6:12
