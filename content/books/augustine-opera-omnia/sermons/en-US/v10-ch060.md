# Sermon 375/B

**1.** We have heard the Gospel: the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ was read. Christ rose, therefore Christ died. The resurrection is the witness of his death, and the death of Christ is the slaying of fear. Let us not fear to die; Christ died for us. Let us die with the hope of eternal life; Christ rose, that we might rise. We have in his death and resurrection a work appointed and a reward promised: the work appointed is the passion, the reward promised is the resurrection. This work the martyrs fulfilled: let us fulfill it by piety, if we cannot by suffering. For it does not fall to all to suffer for Christ and to die for Christ; yet dying itself falls to all. Happy are those for whom there came about, for Christ's sake, what had to come about anyway: for there was a necessity of dying, but there was no necessity of dying for Christ. To all death is coming, but not to all a death for Christ. To those for whom it fell to die for Christ, in a way they gave back what had been paid out to them. The Lord had paid out to them, that he might die for them; they gave back by dying for him. But whence would the wretched, needy man give back, if the happy Lord had not given? And so what Christ had paid out to the martyrs, he gave them the means to repay to Christ. For the voice of the martyrs is: *Unless the Lord had been among us, perhaps they would have swallowed us up alive*[^1]: the persecutors, it says, would perhaps have swallowed us up alive. What is "alive"? Knowing that we would be doing evil if we denied Christ, yet we would do so great an evil alive, that is, knowingly; and so they would have swallowed us up alive, not dead. What is "alive"? Knowing, not unknowing. And by what power did they not do what they were compelled by the persecutors to do? Let them themselves be asked, let them themselves say. Behold, they answer: Unless the Lord had been among us. Therefore he himself gave what would be repaid to him. Thanks be to him: he is rich, and, as is written of him, *he became poor, that he might make us rich*[^2]; by his poverty enriched, by his wounds healed, by his lowliness exalted, by his death made alive.

**2.** *What shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me?*[^3] the martyr was saying. Hear what follows. For he attended and sought what he might render to the Lord: and what does he say? *I will take the cup of salvation*[^4]. This I will render to the Lord: the cup of salvation, the cup of martyrdom, the cup of suffering, the cup of Christ. This is the cup of salvation, because our salvation is Christ. So I will take, he says, his cup, and render it to him. Of that very cup he himself too said to the Father before his passion: *Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me*[^5]. He had come to suffer, he had come to die; he had death in his power. Or, if I lie, hear him yourself: *I have power*, he says, *to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again. No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself, and again I take it up*[^6]. Have you heard the power? No one takes it. Without cause the Jews boast: from this they have guilt, not power. Christ, because he willed, died; he himself says in the psalm: *I slept, and took my rest*[^7]. They cried out, *Crucify, crucify*[^8]: they laid hold of him, they hung him up. So let them live, because they could do something. I slept. And what afterward? And I took my rest. Truly it was a sleep of three days. What then afterward? *And I rose, because the Lord took me up*[^9]. He speaks according to the form of a servant, the Lord took me up: as also in another place: *Shall not the one who sleeps add that he rise again?*[^10] The Jews boast, as though they had conquered me. Shall not the one who sleeps add that he rise again? They, in order to kill, hung him up: but I slept, because when I willed I laid down my soul, and when I willed I rose.

**3.** Therefore he himself is the cup which he wished to pass, to drink which he had come. What then is it, Lord, that you said: Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me? You said to your disciples, about to suffer and to die: *My soul is sorrowful even to death*[^11]. I ask, then, in these words those words of yours: I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again. Whence do I hear: My soul is sorrowful even to death? No one takes it: why are you sorrowful? You have power to lay down your soul: why do you say, Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me? He answers the questioner and says to you: O man, in my flesh I have taken you up: surely, then, if I have taken you up in my flesh, have I not taken you up in my voice too? When I say, I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again, I speak as the maker; when I say, My soul is sorrowful even to death, I speak as the thing made. Rejoice over me in you, recognize yourself in me. When I say, I have power to lay down my soul, I am your help; when I say, My soul is sorrowful even to death, I am your mirror.

**4.** Have you not read that he died? Do we deny it? If we deny the death, we deny the resurrection too. He died from that wherein he deigned to be man; he rose from that wherein he deigned to be man; for we too are men and are going to die, and are going to rise. Did the Word in him die? Could that which *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^12] suffer anything? What can such a Word suffer? And yet it was fitting that the Word should die for us: and he could not die, and it was fitting that he should die. In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. Where is blood? Where is death? Is there death in the Word? Is there blood in the Word? If there is neither death in the Word nor blood in the Word, where is our price? Is not our price his blood? Whence, then, would he give this price, if the Word remained alone, unless the Word took on flesh? Flesh, moreover, living by a human soul: so that, since the Word could not be slain, the flesh alone might be slain, which lived by its own soul. For not even the soul could be slain: which soul, by cleaving to the divinity, was one spirit—the Lord himself only clothing himself with it, not it believing in him, as is written of us: *He who joins himself to the Lord is one spirit*[^13]. For we, when we were unbelievers, were unworthy and estranged from God; but by believing we cleaved to God. That soul, however, was created worthy of cleaving to God, when it was taken up, new and untried, into the unity of the divine person. By that unity and singularity of two unequal spirits, when it departed, the flesh died, which, living in a new manner and a new kind from that very unity of the two spirits—that is, having a twofold and admirable life—was abandoned only for a little time. For God is spirit, and the human spirit is his image, and both are immortal.

**5.** So, then, the Lord our God, our Savior, in a way addresses us, saying: O men, I made man upright, and he made himself perverse. You departed from me, you perished in yourselves; but I am going to seek what had perished. You departed from me, he says, you lost life: *And the life was the light of men*[^14]. Behold what you abandoned, when in Adam you all perished. The life was the light of men. What life? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^15]. It was life: you were lying in your death. I, the Word, had nothing whereby I might die; you, man, had nothing whereby you might live. Because the Lord Christ deigns to, I took up his words: if he took up mine, how much more do I take up his! In a way, in silence, speaking by the very deeds, our Lord Christ says: I had nothing whereby I might die; you, man, had nothing whereby you might live. I took from you whereby I might die for you: take from me whereby you may live with me. Let us celebrate an exchange: I give to you, give to me. I receive from you death: receive from me life. Awake: see what I give, what I receive. Lofty in heaven, I received from you upon earth lowliness; your Lord, I received from you the form of a servant; your health, I received from you wounds; your life, I received from you death. The Word, I was made flesh, that I might be able to die. I did not have flesh with the Father: I took it from your mass, whereby I might pay it out for you—the Virgin Mary was of our mass; there Christ took on flesh from us, that is, from the human race. I received from you flesh, whereby I might die for you: receive from me the life-giving Spirit, whereby you may live with me. Finally, I died of what is yours: live of what is mine.

**6.** Therefore, brothers, when you hear: He was born of the Spirit from the Virgin Mary, he suffered, he was beaten, he received blows; when you hear: Christ suffered these things, do not suppose that that Word, in the beginning with God, in its nature and in its substance could suffer anything of the kind. But can we say that the Word of God, the only-begotten God, did not suffer for us? He suffered, but according to his soul and his flesh capable of suffering. For he took on the form of a servant, that he might suffer as a man. For he had both soul and flesh, because he had come to free the whole man, not by losing life, but by giving life. But, to give a comparison, so that you may more quickly grasp what we say: just as, for example, when the martyr Stephen, or Phocas, or some other, suffered and was killed and buried, their flesh alone was killed and buried, but their souls could neither be killed nor buried, and yet we say most rightly: Stephen, or Phocas, or whoever else died for the name of Christ—so, when the only-begotten of God suffered and was killed and buried, his flesh alone, of course, was killed and buried; but his soul, and much more his divinity, could not be killed; and therefore we say without anxiety that the only Son of God, that is, the only-begotten God of God, died for us and was buried. Whence indeed truthfully, not falsely, Christ the Lord himself said—he who is truth without lie: *For God so loved the world, that he gave his only-begotten, that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life*[^16]. And the Apostle likewise says of God the Father: *He who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all*[^17]. Do you wish, then, to know what Christ is? Do not attend to the flesh alone, which lay in the tomb; do not attend to the soul alone, of which he said: My soul is sorrowful even to death; do not attend to the Word alone, because the Word was God; but attend to the fact that Christ as a whole is Word and soul and flesh.

**7.** But take nothing away from the soul of Christ. For the heretics, the Apollinarians, said that that soul did not have a mind, that is, that it did not have understanding, but that the Word was to it in place of a mind and understanding. This is what Apollinaris says. The Arians, however, say: He did not have any soul at all. You, then, hold faithfully that Christ as a whole is altogether Word and soul and flesh. And when you hear, My soul is sorrowful, understand a human soul, not a brute one: for a soul without intellect is the soul of a beast, not a human soul. If he did not come to free the mind, he did not have a mind. Therefore Christ is one, Word and soul and flesh. What is man? Soul and flesh. What is Christ? Word and man, and through this Word and soul and flesh, Christ one. When you beat a man with your fists, what of him do you strike? The soul or the flesh? You confess that it is the flesh. And yet the soul cries out: Why do you strike me? Why do you beat me? If you were to say to the soul, Who touched you? I strike the flesh, not you—would not whoever heard you saying these things laugh, and judge you foolish or mad? So, then, those who scourged the flesh of the Word of God, or struck it with blows, cannot say: We scourged the flesh, or struck it, not the Word or the soul of Christ; for they scourged the whole Christ, that is, the Word and soul and flesh, or struck him with blows: for they did not scourge his dead body or strike it with blows. And although they could not kill on the cross either his soul or his very divinity, which is the true life, yet they took delight in slaying the whole Christ in their heart and in their evil will. For whoever persecutes someone in order to kill him wishes to extinguish him wholly, just as the whole light of a lamp dashed to the ground is extinguished, so that it does not shine at all, when any wrongdoer has seen it to be an obstacle to him. Which in a man can in no way come about, that is, that he be wholly extinguished, since he has one substance mortal, the other immortal: for nothing in him is mortal except the flesh. But the only-begotten of God, Christ, much more could not be wholly extinguished, when the Jews thought to extinguish him—he who, in his three substances, that is, one eternal and divine, and in two temporal, that is, human, had only one mortal, that is, his flesh; but his soul, and especially his divinity, he had without doubt immortal. And therefore by his death of a short time he alone could redeem us from our eternal death—he who was not only flesh and a human soul, but God and soul and flesh, one only-begotten of God. *For he who descended into the lower parts of the earth is the very one who also ascended above all the heavens*[^18]: which a man alone could not do.

**8.** Without anxiety, then, let us exult and rejoice, dearest brothers, because he redeemed us by his death—he who, even when slain, triumphed over his enemies. For, slain, he slew death, and forever freed us from its hand, *and ascending on high he led captivity captive, and*, the Holy Spirit having been sent, *gave* his *gifts to men*[^19]: he who even, lying in the tomb, could bring the believing thief into paradise.
