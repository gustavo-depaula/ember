# Sermon 90/A: (Dolbeau 11)

**1.** In this Gospel reading which has just been read, let us too, as it were seeing the Lord, not with the eyes of the flesh but—which is more healthful—with the eyes of faith, take up the disposition of the one asking what is the great commandment in the law. For he who was asking, because he saw not with the eyes of faith but of the flesh, was testing rather than seeking. But let us, believing, seek, that by seeking we may find; let us too say: Lord, *what is the great commandment in the law?*[^1] But let us say it not with the cunning of one testing, but with the disposition of one learning. For he now answers us what he answered that man, who, if he did not believe, sought for us, not for himself; but if he believed, now let the hearer be the more instructed, if then the tester could be corrected.

**2.** Let us first observe this, how that man asked about one commandment, desiring to know what was the commandment in the law—not what was the only one, but what was the great one; but the Lord answered not one, but two commandments. For perhaps the man, when he had heard what was the great one, would ask the several following ones; but the Lord, lest after the great one many be asked, joined a second one, that there might be fulfilled in it what had been prophesied so long before: *For the Lord will make a word shortened and finished upon the earth*[^2]. Now it is done, in this reading it is fulfilled. For many are the precepts of the law, and like an incomprehensible forest a growth of commandments springs up through all the pages. And who could fulfill what no one is able to retain in the mind? But the Lord Christ, full of mercy, just as he showed himself great in a small body, so he enclosed the wide law in a brief precept. In that smallness of body we have the whole Son of God; in the brevity of these commandments we hold the whole law of God. Mercy ended sloth. Do not think how long you may learn, but rather how you may do what you have quickly learned.

**3.** *You shall love,* he says, *the Lord your God with your whole heart and with your whole soul and with your whole mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second,* he says, *is like it. You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments depend the whole law and the prophets*[^3]. Why were you stretching yourself out through the spreading of the branches? Hold these roots, and the whole tree is in your hand. The Lord, as we see, briefly intimated this; we are compelled to say more about these two commandments. Or are we perhaps not compelled, and does what we have heard from the Lord suffice? Clearly it suffices, but not for all. For the greater anyone is, the more what is said briefly suffices for him. The great seek brief things; the small, because they understand less, desire more. We fear to offend the disdain of the former, we do not wish to burden the weakness of the latter. Yet if we are silent, those who understand less complain; and let those who already understand bear with us, that those who do not yet understand may grasp us.

**4.** What better, what briefer could be said to you, O man, than that you love *the Lord God with your whole heart and with your whole soul and with your whole mind?* Do it, and be secure about eternal life and about the blessed life. For if you love the Lord your God with your whole heart, your whole soul, your whole mind, you leave yourself nothing from which you might love yourself. Love your God with your whole heart and your whole soul and your whole mind. And what will remain from which you may love yourself? But if nothing will remain from which you may love yourself, in the second precept how will you *love your neighbor as yourself?* See, here is one question, hear it. The Lord says, as we noticed in the reading itself when it was read: *On these two commandments depend the whole law and the prophets.* But further, the apostle Paul, if you noticed when his letter too was read, says that the man fulfills the law who loves his neighbor, not adding the first and great commandment, that one love God with the whole heart, soul, and mind. For he says thus: *For "You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not covet," and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself." Love of the neighbor works no evil. The fullness of the law, then, is love*[^4]. Here he says: *He who loves his neighbor has fulfilled the law.* And if he named only three precepts, *You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not covet,* we would think that in love of the neighbor only these three precepts are contained. But when he added and said: *If there is any other commandment,* he enclosed the whole in love of the neighbor. What here remains for the love of God? When you hear: *God with the whole heart, the whole soul, the whole mind,* nothing seems to remain for love of the neighbor. Again, when you hear: This and this and this, *and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying: You shall love your neighbor as yourself,* nothing seems to remain for love of God. How, then, did the Lord say not "in one," but *on these two commandments depend the whole law and the prophets?*

**5.** Therefore, to expound this which I have set forth, briefly, as much as we can with the Lord's help, let us begin rather from love of the neighbor. We too are men, mortal, ignorant, not yet made equal to the angels, far unlike the fellowship of incorruption, and by this very unlikeness God is far from us, although by mercy he is near. Who, then, are we, and by what powers of our thinking should we dare to conjecture the Lord? We have a neighbor; in the neighbor now strive to love God with the whole heart, the whole soul, the whole mind. O man, *if you do not love the brother whom you see, how will you be able to love the God whom you do not see?*[^5] You recognize the words taken from the discourse of the apostle John. It is prescribed for us, then: let us begin from the neighbor, that we may arrive at God.

**6.** But someone will say to me: "I love God and my neighbor, not in words but in deeds." Prove it. "I love," he says, "my neighbor." What great thing do you do? Do you not see in irrational animals how mutual love prevails, how birds desire to be with birds of their own kind and are wearied by solitude? Do you not see, when animals have shared a stall together, how on a journey they desire to follow one another, and one is separated from the other with the greatest difficulty? What great thing, then, do you do, if a man wishes to be with a man? It still belongs to beasts of burden. I do not know whether such love is required of us by God. You say perhaps: "I love my neighbor; for I love my son, and as myself." This too is easy. Tigers love their young. For none of these would be propagated, unless one were loved by another. Transcend the things given into your power: none of these was made to the image of God. God made man to his image, that he might have *power over the fish of the sea and the birds of the air and all the creeping things that creep upon the earth*[^6]. See what you have beneath you, and by what love of his image you are far otherwise fashioned for the creator. Whence, finally, do you prove that you love your son? Your son himself, I say, whence do you prove that you love him? Because you were keeping for him an inheritance which he will not be able to possess together with you? For he does not hold it with you, but at your decease he will succeed. Do you not remember that in that inheritance your father too made his passage before you? And if it remains the same from of old, your grandfather too passed through it: all passing, none remaining. You leave, then, mortal things to one about to die, or, what is truer, you do not know for whom you gather them, and you boast yourself a lover of your son.

**7.** *They shall announce,* it says, *to their sons, that they may put their hope in God*[^7]. If you love thus, you love. If you love otherwise, you do not even love, because you do not even love yourself. For surely, what have you heard? *You shall love your neighbor as yourself.* This rule I lay down—rather, I do not lay it down, but recognize it. For I both behold it and recall it as laid down for us all. This is the rule: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself.* I do not now say: You shall love son, spouse, near friend, near acquaintance *as yourself.* Perhaps you answer: "I love them." First I ask whether you love yourself. Here is the whole force of the precept, here the whole question turns: for you will not be able to love your neighbor as yourself, if you do not yet love yourself. "For who is there," you say, "who does not love himself?" I would wish to find one who loves himself. For I do not attend to how the creature errs, but to what the creator teaches. He who made us knows us better. Let us, then, hear him. Since you were saying: "I love myself," even if I required you to prove it, you would answer me: "When I am hungry, I restore my body, because I love myself; I do not wish to be worn out by labors, because I love myself; I do not wish to be straitened by want, because I love myself; I do not wish to have a fever, because I love myself; I do not wish to be in pain, because I love myself." Do you wish to hear what he says who made you? See only how very well you love yourself: whether you do not love iniquity. *For he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^8]. I do not ask you, ask yourself. If you wish to grow by another's harm, if you wish it to go ill with another that it may go well with you, surely, if you so wish, if you wish this, you love iniquity, you hate your own soul. If, then, you have hated your own soul, I do not entrust to you your neighbor, that you may love him as yourself. Shall I entrust to you another man, so that I seek two? You who have destroyed yourself, are you to preserve me? First, then, love yourself, that you may know how to love your neighbor as yourself.

**8.** How you should love yourself, do you wait to hear from me? Let us rather hear him who made both you and me. See how you should love yourself: attend to the great precept, that you may love yourself. For toward that which you love, you must needs wish to bring the one you love. And you love iniquity: thither you will lead the one you love as yourself. The many pursuits of men, whether evil or good, are set before the eyes of all. You love a charioteer: you urge all you love to watch with you, to love with you, to shout with you, to go mad with you. If they do not love him, you insult them, you call them dolts, as you would yourself. And if you do not wish to give him half, you wish him whom you love as yourself to have only as much. For you do not wish him to grow rich at your loss, you do not wish his good at the detriment of your own good. Why? Because you think gold a good, therefore you reckon yourself great because you have gold. You wish him to increase, not yourself to decrease. Why do you love this thing which you would diminish by your loss? In all these things you love iniquity, you hate your own soul. When you wish securely to draw your neighbor, whom you love as yourself, draw him to that good which no multitude of fellow sharers straitens; which good, however many possess it, is whole to all and to each. Unless you love such a good, how will you love your neighbor as yourself?

**9.** What is that? In the first and great commandment you have: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, with your whole soul, and with your whole mind.* For when you begin to love God, then you love yourself. Do not fear: however much you love God, you will not be excessive. The measure of loving God is to love him without measure. Love, then, *with your whole heart, with your whole soul, with your whole mind,* because you have nothing more. For what more do you have from which to love your God, than your whole self? Do not, then, fear lest, by leaving yourself nothing from which to love yourself, you perish. You do not perish, because by loving God with your whole self you will be there where you do not perish. Rather, if from there you slacken your love toward yourself, you will not be in him, but in yourself. Then, therefore, you perish, because you will be in one who perishes. If you wish not to perish, be in him who cannot perish. This the force of love has, this the ardor of love has. In those most foul and base pursuits let us see these things. The lovers of a charioteer in the show hang wholly on him, they are nowhere but in the one they watch; the lover does not at all regard himself, he does not know where he is. Accordingly the one more lukewarm in that pursuit, who stands beside him and sees him so fervent, says: "He is not with himself." And you, if it can be, when you are in God, do not be with yourself. If you are with yourself, you entrust yourself to yourself, you are about to lose yourself, you are not fit to preserve yourself.

**10.** Remember how he went and perished, who said to his father, his preserver: *Give me my portion of the substance that falls to me*[^9]. See, he went away, see, he consumed all, see, he fed swine, see, he was worn down in want: made far from his father, when he wished to be with himself. For, wishing to be with himself, he did not even remain with himself. If you slip away from your God, you more quickly slip away also from yourself, and you go out from yourself, and you forsake even yourself. Therefore to such men it is said: *Return, transgressors, to the heart*[^10], return to yourselves, that you may be able to return also to him who made you. Whence he too, when he was in want, his father forsaken, forsaken by himself, what is said of him? *And returning to himself, he said*[^11]. *Returning to himself:* you see that he had forsaken even himself. Well, son, you have corrected yourself, you have returned to yourself. Do not remain in yourself, lest you lose yourself again. He too, partly amended, remembered this. For as soon as he returned to himself, he was unwilling to remain in himself, because, *returning to himself, he said: I will arise, and I will go to my father*[^12]. Well returned, he understood that he ought to be there whence he had fallen . . . to be that very thing, indeed he did not deserve to be.

**11.** If you love this, if you cherish it, I entrust to you your neighbor. For I see whither you tend and whither you have willed yourself. Thither lead him, nor can you lead elsewhere the one you love as yourself: for now you love yourself too. Lead your neighbor, draw him, seize him, be urgent in season. If the day of the show were dawning, you, a lover of the hunter, would not be lulled by due sleep, nor would the hour of going to the amphitheater pass you by. And when the hour of going had come, you would most troublesomely rouse your neighbor too, perhaps lulled in sleep and preferring to sleep rather than to go; you would press the sluggard; you would wish, if it could be, to snatch him from his bed and set him in the amphitheater, nor would you be troublesome to him, except until sleep was shaken off. For, sleep shaken off, he would proceed and give thanks for your trouble. And perhaps, having now snatched him with you to the amphitheater, whither you had both hurried, the one you loved being defeated, you would return confounded. Love God wholly, *with your whole heart and with your whole soul and with your whole mind:* so alone, so will you love yourself. In this way alone do you love your neighbor as yourself. For you draw him to one over whom you cannot blush.

**12.** Therefore it was necessary that those two precepts be commended, because he does not love who loves his neighbor, unless he first loves God. For when he loves God, he does not love iniquity, lest by loving iniquity he hate his own soul. If, then, he does not love iniquity, let him love equity, and there he loves God. Let him not seek him as it were with the eyes: let him seek him with the mind, let him love him rather with the heart and with affection. Let him not set before himself what is not God, lest he love and not God, lest he love an empty thought. For lest perhaps in this thought we should err and compose God for ourselves according to a fleshly affection and fashion him as we wished, Scripture, taking us away from thoughts of this kind, says: *God is love*[^13]. If, then, you love, love that from which you love, and you love God. Have you not heard? *For he who loves iniquity hates his own soul.* If you love, love this very thing from which you love, and you love God. For that from which you love *is love.* You love by love; love love, and you have loved God, since *God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God*[^14]. Therefore the two precepts had to be commended. For one would suffice: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself,* but lest man err in loving his neighbor, because he was erring in loving himself, the Lord willed in the love of God to fashion your love, by which you may love yourself; and then he entrusted to you your neighbor, that you might love him as yourself.

**13.** Now, then, if you please, let even one precept of the apostle suffice you. You have now understood two; even one suffices. For before you understand two, one does not suffice. For you begin to love yourself badly, and you love badly the one you love as yourself. Nor is it to be said: You love badly, but: You do not love. Therefore *you shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not covet,* where the whole is, there he recalls you to yourself. For you can refrain from adultery from fear of punishment, not from love of justice. *You shall not kill:* you can both wish to kill and fear the punishment. The murder will not be in your hand, but in your heart you will be held guilty. For you wish to kill a man, but you fear; you wish to kill: you do not yet love not to kill. Let what you do be within, let it be there where he sees who crowns; there fight, there conquer: for there you have one who watches you. Therefore *you shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not covet, and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying: You shall love your neighbor as yourself.* You love God too. You cannot have this without that; this second follows the first. Let the first be, and it draws the second. The second cannot be without the first. Fulfill, then, the one, you who were thinking about two. You cannot fulfill one, except in two. For the second too is called second from following. Therefore it follows. *Love your neighbor as yourself:* this suffices me. Can you not also think about God? How, then, do you begin to love yourself? *Love of the neighbor works no evil. The fullness of the law, then, is love.* But where is love? In love of God and in love of the neighbor. Choose which love you will. You choose love of the neighbor: it will not be true, unless God be loved. You choose love of God: it will not be true, unless the neighbor be understood along with it.

**14.** But you do not yet have love: groan, believe, ask, obtain. What is commanded, what the law enjoins, faith obtains. For if you already have what faith obtains, *for what do you have that you did not receive?*[^15] But if you do not yet have it, ask that you may receive. It is *love* that we ask for; if we do not yet have it, let us ask, lest we remain empty. For whence shall we have it from ourselves, who have deserved nothing good, but evil? We shall have it from him to whom our soul says: *Bless the Lord, O my soul, and do not forget all his recompenses, who is gracious to all your iniquities*[^16]. This happens in baptism. But if this alone happened, what would we remain? But there follows: *Who heals all your weaknesses*[^17]. For our weaknesses healed, we shall not loathe our bread. See, then, what follows, our weaknesses healed: *Who will redeem your life from corruption*[^18]. This happens now in the resurrection of the dead. And what follows the redemption of our life from corruption? *Who crowns you.* Perhaps by your merits? Attend to what follows: *Who crowns you in pity and mercy*[^19]. *For judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy*[^20]. Therefore, our sins forgiven, our weaknesses healed, our life redeemed from corruption, our crown rendered to us in his mercy, what shall we do, what shall we have? *Who satisfies you with good things*[^21], not evil. You were greedy: you were not satisfied with gold, because the greedy man is not satisfied with gold. Be just and be satisfied with God. Nothing at all satisfies you but God; nothing suffices you but God. *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^22]. Let us, then, love the works of mercy, while our weaknesses are being healed, so that, our weaknesses healed, our desires may flourish; and being healed, may flourish; and flourishing, be satisfied; so that there may be for us a judgment, but with mercy. For it is grievous if it be without mercy. It is difficult that he find nothing in you to punish. You have already pleased yourself well: for he knows something that is hidden from you, he finds in you what you were hiding, or perhaps even what you did not know. Therefore let the works of mercy be fervent, and in this want of temporal things let us love our neighbors, that we may merit to feel judgment with mercy.
