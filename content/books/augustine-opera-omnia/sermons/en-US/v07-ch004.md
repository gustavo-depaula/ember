# Sermon 233: In the Days of Easter

**1. 1.** *The faith in the resurrection is proper to Christians.* You have heard the reading of the holy Gospel about the resurrection of Christ. In the resurrection of Christ our faith is established. The passion of Christ both pagans and the impious and the Jews have believed: the resurrection none but Christians. The passion of Christ signifies the miseries of this life; the resurrection of Christ shows the blessedness of the life to come. In the present let us labor; in the future let us hope. Now is the time of work; then, of reward. He who is lazy in performing the work is shameless in demanding the reward. You have heard what he said to his disciples after the resurrection. He sent them to preach the Gospel, and it was done: the Gospel was preached, it came to us. And see: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^1]. By walking on and on, the Gospel came to us, and to the ends of the earth. He briefly settled things for us, telling his disciples what we are to do, what we are to hope for. For he said, as you heard when he was speaking: *He who believes and is baptized will be saved*[^2]. Faith is required of us, salvation is offered: *He who believes and is baptized will be saved*. Precious is what is promised to us; freely is done what is commanded.

**1. 2.** *He who believes and is baptized will be saved.* What then? Were those who heard these things not saved? Do not many who are saved believe, and are they not saved even before they believe? Saved indeed: but vain is the salvation of men. What sort of salvation is this, which you have in common with your beast? And yet even this, from where does it come, except from him of whom it is written: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord?* And he added: *According to the multitude of your mercy, O God*[^3]. For so great is the multitude of your mercy that salvation comes from you into this mortal flesh of men, comes also to the flesh of cattle. This is the multitude of your mercy. And to your sons, what? See, *men and beasts you will save, O Lord*. Nothing more for us? What is granted to men of any sort, what to cattle, is this granted to us also? Not so, surely.

**2. 2.** But what then? Hear: *But the sons of men will hope under the shelter of your wings: they will be inebriated with the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight. For with you is the fountain of life*[^4]. Christ is the fountain of life. We had salvation in common with the cattle, until the fountain of life came to us: the fountain of life came to us, and the fountain of life died for us. Will he deny his life, who paid out his death for us? This is the salvation that is not vain. Why? Because it does not pass away.

**2. 3.** *How men and sons of men differ.* This distinction is to be well noted: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord*. *Men*, those belonging to the man. *But the sons of men*, those belonging to the Son of man, *will hope under the shelter of your wings*. Set before your eyes two men. Lift up your faith, let your heart awake: remember the man by whom we were deceived; remember the man by whom we were redeemed. Was that man a son of man? Adam was a man, he was not a son of man. Therefore the Lord Christ constantly calls himself the Son of man, to make us remember the man who was not a son of man: that we may remember in the one death, in the other life; in the one sin, in the other the forgiveness of sins; in the one bondage, in the other freedom; in the one condemnation, in the other absolution. These two men, then, are signified in these words: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord. Men*, those belonging to the man: *and beasts*, together with the beasts you will save them. For man, set in honor, did not understand, as it is written: *Man, set in honor, did not understand; he is compared to the senseless beasts, and is made like them*[^5]. Therefore: *Men and beasts*, to whom men have been made like, because they did not understand and have been compared to those over whom they were created to have dominion, *you will save*.

**3. 4.** *True salvation was not found here by Christ. He was born mortal, that he might take it away.* Is this the salvation of which it is said: *He who believes and is baptized will be saved*[^6]? That salvation is another, far other. The angels have it: do not seek it on earth. It is great, but it is not here. That thing is not from this region, there is no such salvation here. Lift up your heart. Why do you seek here on earth this salvation? Salvation itself came here, and found our death here. When our Lord Jesus Christ came to us in the flesh, did he find this salvation here in our region? Something great this merchant brought here, coming from his own region: this merchant found in our region what abounds here. What abounds here? To be born and to die. The earth is full of these wares, to be born and to die. He was born, and he died. But by what way was he born? He came to this region, but not by the way we too came. For he came from heaven, from the Father. And yet he was born mortal. He was born of the Holy Spirit, of the Virgin Mary. Surely we were not so born of Adam and Eve? We through the desire of the flesh, he, however, not through it. For Mary was a virgin, without a man's embrace, without the heat of desire; and that she might not suffer this heat, it was said to her: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you*[^7]. The Virgin Mary, then, did not lie with a man and conceive: but she believed and conceived. For he was born mortal, for mortals. Why mortal? Because in the likeness of sinful flesh: not in the flesh of sin, but in the likeness of sinful flesh. What does the flesh of sin have? Death and sin. What did the likeness of sinful flesh have? Death without sin. If it had sin, it would be the flesh of sin: if it did not have death, it would not be the likeness of sinful flesh. Such did he come, the Savior came: he died, but he killed death; he ended in himself what we feared; he took it up, and killed it; as a great huntsman takes a lion, and slays it.

**4. 5.** Where is death? Seek it in Christ, now it is not there: but it was, and it died there. O life, the death of death! Be of good cheer, it will die in us too. What went before in the head, will be repaid in the members: death will die in us too. But when? At the end of the world, in the resurrection of the dead, which we believe and of which we do not doubt. *For he who believes and is baptized will himself be saved.* Follow on to what you should fear: *But he who does not believe will be condemned*[^8]. Therefore death will die in us, it will live on in the damned. Where death will not know death, there will be everlasting death: because the torments will be eternal. In us it will die, and will not be. Do you want to know? I tell you a few words of those who triumph, that you may have something to meditate on, something to sing in your heart, something to hope for with your whole soul, something to seek by faith and good work. Hear the words of those who triumph, when there will be no death; when in us too, as in our head, death will die. The apostle Paul says: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality. Then will come to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory*[^9]. I told you that death will die in us: *Death is swallowed up in victory*. This is the death of death. It will be swallowed up, so as not to appear. What does it mean, so as not to appear? So as not to be, neither within nor without. *Death is swallowed up in victory*. Let those who triumph rejoice; let them rejoice, let them say what follows: *Where, O death, is your victory? where, O death, is your sting?*[^10] Where is it? You seized, you possessed, you conquered, and you said within yourself; you struck, and you killed; *Where, O death, is your victory? where, O death, is your sting?* Has not my Lord broken it? O death, when you clung to my Lord, then you also perished for me. By this salvation *he who believes and is baptized will be saved. But he who does not believe will be condemned*[^11]. Flee condemnation, love and hope for eternal salvation.
