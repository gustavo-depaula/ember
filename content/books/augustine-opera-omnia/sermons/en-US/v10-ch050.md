# Sermon 368

**1.** Just now, brothers, when the divine reading was read, we heard the Lord saying: *He who loves his soul will lose it*[^1]. As it were contrary to this sentence seems what the Apostle says: *No one ever hated his own flesh*[^2]. If, then, there is no one who hates his own flesh, how much more is there no one who hates his own soul? For the soul is much preferred to the flesh: because it is the dweller, the flesh the dwelling; and the soul has dominion, but the flesh serves; the soul is superior, the flesh subject. If, then, no one ever hated his own flesh, who is there who would hate his own soul? Through this the present gospel reading brought us no small question, where we heard: *He who loves his soul will lose it.* It is dangerous to love the soul, lest it perish. But if for this reason it is dangerous that you love your soul, lest your soul perish, therefore you ought not to love it, because you do not wish it to perish. But if you do not wish it to perish, you love it. What is this? If I love, I lose: therefore let me not love, lest I lose. But because I fear to lose, therefore I do not love; and surely, what I fear lest I lose, I love. The Lord says elsewhere too: *What does it profit a man, if he gains the whole world, but suffers the loss of his own soul?*[^3] Behold, that the soul is so to be loved, that it is preferred to the gain of the whole world; and yet let him who loves his soul take heed, because if he loves it he will lose it. Do you not wish to lose it? do not love it; but if you do not wish to lose it, you cannot not love it.

**2.** There are, then, those who perversely love their soul: and this the word of God wishes to correct, not that they should hate their soul, but that they should rightly love it. For by loving badly they lose it, and a certain great thing happens as it were preposterous and contrary: but yet it so happens, that, if you love it perversely, you lose it; if you hate it rightly, you preserve it. There is, then, a certain perverse love of it, and a certain right hatred of it; but the perverse love is from hatred, the right hatred is from love. What is the perverse love of the soul? When you love your soul in iniquities. Hear that this perverse love comes from hatred: *But he who loves iniquity, hates his own soul*[^4]. But see that right hatred comes from love; there the Lord, following, says: *But he who hates his soul in this world, will find it unto eternal life*[^5]. Surely what you wish to find unto eternal life, you love much. For what you love for a time, what does it profit? Either you are withdrawn from it, or it is withdrawn from you: when you have been taken away, the lover himself perishes; when that has been taken away, what you loved perishes. Where, then, either the lover perishes, or what is loved, it is not to be loved. But what is to be loved? That which can be with us forever. If you wish to have your soul safe forever, hate it for a time. Therefore right hatred comes from love: perverse love comes from hatred.

**3.** What, then, is the measure of loving the soul? Do you think the martyrs did not love their souls? You see surely now, if anyone's life of the present age is in peril, how his friends run for it: how one runs to the church, the bishop is asked to interrupt, if he has any business in hand, to run, to hasten. Why? For the soul. And all tremble, all decree that one must hasten, with all other things interrupted: every haste is praised, every delay is accused. Why? For the soul. What is, for the soul? That the man may not die. Did the martyrs not know how to love their souls? and yet this is for the soul, that the man may not die. The death of man is iniquity. If you run a hundred thousand for this life, how many thousands ought you to run for eternal life? If you hasten to gain a few days, and those uncertain—for today a man freed from death does not know whether he will die tomorrow—yet if one so runs for the gain of a few days, because even unto old age they are few days, how must one run for eternal life? And yet for this men are slothful: you scarcely find one who would move even a slow step for eternal life. Perverse love of the soul, then, abounds; but right love is of very few. For as there is no one who does not love his soul, so there is no one who does not love his flesh: whence it can come about that both what the Apostle said is true, *No one ever hated his own flesh*[^6], and the soul is not loved. Let us learn, then, brothers, to love our souls. Every pleasure of the world is going to pass away. There is a useful love, there is a harmful love: let love be hindered by love; let the harmful love withdraw, and the useful love succeed. But because men are unwilling to withdraw from there, therefore nothing else can enter into them: that they may not contain it, they are full; let them pour out, and they contain. For they are full of the love of carnal pleasures, full of the love of the present life, full of the love of gold and silver, of the possessions of this world. Those who are full, then, are like vessels. Do you wish honey to enter, from where you have not yet poured out vinegar? Pour out what you have, that you may contain what you do not have. Therefore first there is renunciation of this world, and then conversion to God. He who renounces pours out, he who is converted is filled: but only if it is done not with the body alone, but also with the heart.

**4.** But it is asked, brothers, how this love grows: for it has its beginnings, has its increases, has its perfection. And we ought to know who has begun, that we may exhort him to the increases: who has not even begun, that we may admonish him where he may begin: who has both begun, and grown, that we may incite him to perfection. First, let your love attend to this: all loves and affections are first in men toward themselves, and so toward another thing which they love. If you love gold, first you love yourself, and so the gold: because if you are dead, there will be no one to possess the gold. Therefore love begins for each one from himself, and cannot but begin from himself. And no one is admonished to love himself: for this is present not only in men, but even in the beasts. For you see, brothers, how not only the huge beasts and great animals, as are oxen or camels or elephants, but even flies, even the smallest little worms, how they are unwilling to die, and love themselves. All animals flee death. Therefore they love themselves, they wish to guard themselves: some by speed, some by hiding-places, some by resisting and fighting back; yet all animals fight for their life, are unwilling to die, wish to guard themselves. They love themselves, then. Another thing too begins to be loved. But what is that other thing? Whatever you love is either what you are, or below you, or above you. If what you love is below you, love it for consolation, love it for handling, love it for use, not for being bound. For example, you love gold, do not bind yourself to gold: how much better are you than gold? For gold is shining earth: but you, that you might be enlightened by the Lord, were made to the image of God. Although gold is a creature of God, yet God did not make gold to his image, but you: therefore he placed gold below you. This love, then, is to be despised: these things are to be taken up for use, not to be clung to as it were by the glue of love by the chain of love. Do not make for yourself members which, when they begin to be cut off, you grieve and are tormented. What then? Rise from this love, by which you love things lower than you are: begin to love equals, that is, what you are. But what need is there of many words? If you will, you can do it briefly.

**5.** But in what order we may have true love and true charity, the Lord himself told us in the Gospel, and evidently showed. For he says thus: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with all your strength; and your neighbor as yourself*[^7]. First, then, love God, then yourself; after this love your neighbor as yourself: yet first learn to love yourself, then so love your neighbor as yourself: for if you do not know how to love yourself, how will you be able truly to love your neighbor? For some men think they love themselves in a lawful order, when they seize the goods of others, when they make themselves drunk, when they serve lust, when through various slanders they acquire unjust gains. Let such men hear the Scripture saying: *He who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^8]. If, then, by loving iniquity you not only do not love yourself, but even hate yourself, how will you be able to love either God or your neighbor? If, then, you wish to keep the order of true charity, do justice, love mercy, flee luxury; begin, according to the precept of the Lord, to love not only friends, but even enemies. And when you have striven faithfully to keep these things with your whole heart, by these virtues as by certain steps you will be able to ascend, so that you may merit to love God with your whole soul and all your strength. And when you have come to this happy perfection, you will count all the concupiscences of this world as dung, and with the prophet you will be able to say: *But for me to cling to God is good*[^9].
