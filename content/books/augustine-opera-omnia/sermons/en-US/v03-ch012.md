# Sermon 60/A: Here Begins a Sermon on the Gospel Where the Lord Says: "Do Not Give What Is Holy to the Dogs, nor Cast Your Pearls Before Swine," and What Follows

**1.** Your love knows, since you are sons of the Church of Christ, rooted and grounded in the catholic faith, that the mysteries of God are hidden not for this reason, that they are begrudged to learners, but that they may not be opened except to those who seek. But for this reason closed things are read from the holy Scriptures, that they may rouse the mind to seek. Just now the Gospel reading was read to us, where the Lord commands that pearls not be cast before swine. For when the Lord admonished his servants and disciples of this, saying: *Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine*[^1], he indeed forewarned them of this; but because they themselves could not easily recognize who were the dogs and swine, who were to be avoided, lest the pearls be cast to them, lest what is holy be given to such, and lest it be closed even to the worthy, he at once added and said: *Ask, and you will receive: seek, and you will find: knock, and it will be opened to you; for everyone who asks receives, and the seeker, finding, and to the one who knocks it will be opened*[^2]. So, *Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine*, he commands the stewards, his disciples, whom he was making preachers of the Gospel. But what he added: *Ask and you will receive, seek and you will find, knock and it will be opened to you*, he commanded the people; so that, when one asks and seeks and knocks, then it may be understood that he is no longer a dog and a pig, to whom the pearls ought not to be cast.

**2.** By this most evident Gospel testimony it appears clearly enough in another place, when the Lord withdrew into the regions of Tyre and Sidon[^3]. A Canaanite woman, coming out from those borders, began to ask for the healing of her daughter. The Lord did not heed her: he seemed like one despising her, that her faith might appear. See how he delays: the gift, which nevertheless he wished to give, he dissembled from her, that he might draw from her heart the voice by which she would be worthy to receive it. For when even the disciples said to the Lord: *Send her away, for she cries after us*, the Lord said: *It is not good to take the bread of the children and cast it to the dogs*[^4]. See, it is like that precept, *Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine. I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^5]. For she was of the Gentiles. It was to come about that the Gospel would be preached to the Gentiles too: the apostle Paul was sent to the Gentiles, especially he was sent; but after the passion and resurrection of the Lord, one had to go to the preaching of the Gospel among the Gentiles. But the Lord had come in bodily presence to those sheep that were lost of the house of Israel, because from there too many believed. For from that number were the Apostles: from that number were those hundred and twenty upon whom the Holy Spirit came on the day of Pentecost, whom the Lord had promised in the Gospel, saying: *I send you the Spirit of truth*[^6]; and whatever he promised about that Spirit, he furnished after his passion and ascension on the day of Pentecost. And there were there a hundred and twenty upon whom the Holy Spirit came, and they were filled, surely of the number of the Jews[^7]. How the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel are chosen, this I convey to your Holiness. The apostle Paul too says that the risen Lord was seen by more than five hundred brothers[^8]: they were of that number. When the Lord too was preached after his ascension, many thousands of Jewish men believed. Likewise to those who crucified the Lord, the blood of the Lord was given: raging, they shed their own price: for by that very blood which they shed they were redeemed. And because that voice of the one hanging on the cross was not empty, by which he said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^9]; they, then, first shed the blood as their price, afterward also drank it. So he had said he was sent to these sheep. But yet he had foretold that the Gentiles too would believe. For nothing happened as if new, which was not foretold beforehand. For the Prophets too announced the faith of the Gentiles; and he himself, while he was still here before the passion, said: *I have other sheep, which are not of this fold: I must bring them too, that there may be one flock and one shepherd*[^10]. Therefore he was also called the cornerstone: for in a corner two walls are joined, and two walls make a corner only when they come from different directions. For if both come from one side, they do not make a corner. So the people coming from the Jews, that is, from the circumcision; those coming from a different direction, that is, from idols and from the uncircumcision, the Gentiles—they came indeed from diversity, but were joined in one stone. *The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner*[^11]. So the Gentiles had not yet come, and already one was from the Gentiles, that Canaanite woman prefiguring the Church of the Gentiles.

**3.** So she asks, and it is said: *It is not good to take the bread of the children and cast it to the dogs*[^12]. When she asked vehemently, she was called a dog. If she, taking it as a word of insult, as it were, which had come forth from the mouth of truth, had grown vexed, and departed having received the reproach, saying in her heart: I came to ask a benefit: if it is given, let it be given; if it is not given, why am I a dog? For what evil have I done, because I asked, because I came, to receive a benefit?—she knew of whom she asked the benefit; what came forth from his mouth she received, did not reject, and pressed more sharply in asking, confessing that she was what she had heard. For she says: *Yes, Lord*; that is, you have spoken truly, that I am a dog. And because he had said "the bread of the children," it is too little that she confessed herself a dog; she confessed also her masters, whom he had called children. *For the bread of the children*, he says, *it is not good to take and cast to the dogs.* And she: *Yes, Lord*, she says; *for the dogs too eat of the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters*[^13]. What do you see, brothers? She asked vehemently, sought vehemently, knocked greatly. Now, then, because she asked, sought, knocked, she is not a dog. The Lord, then, does not now give what is holy to a dog. For he showed that she was not a dog, because with affection she sought, knocked: so that he might confirm what he had said. For when he had commanded the stewards: *Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine*, reproving those who wished to receive, that they might not be dogs, if before they were dogs: Ask, he said, seek, knock. This, then, he showed in the Canaanite woman, whom he himself first called a dog; who, when, having heard the reproach, she did not grow indignant, but, having taken up the reproach, confessed her humility, then the Lord himself took the reproach from her. He himself had said "dog"; he himself had commanded: *Do not give what is holy to the dogs.* Why did he take away the reproach, which he himself had spoken, except because she, having received the reproach, was changed through humility, and the more, because she confessed what she had heard, ceased to be what she had heard?

**4.** What is it that I said, that by confessing what she had heard, she ceased to be what she had heard? Like that publican who was in the temple; but the Pharisee, who boasted of his merits, and insulted, as it were, the sinner standing far off. He, with eyes cast down to the earth, did not even dare to lift his face to heaven, because he did not dare to lift his conscience to God; but he struck his breast, saying: *Be merciful to me a sinner*[^14]. And what does the Lord say? *Amen I say to you, this publican went down justified rather than that Pharisee: for everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted*[^15]. If by saying he is a sinner he is justified, by saying he is a sinner he ceased to be now a sinner. Why? because the publican went down justified rather than the Pharisee. As, then, this one, by confessing himself a sinner, lost being what he was, so she too, by confessing herself a dog, lost being what she was. What does she hear from the Lord? No longer, dog; but what? *O woman, great is your faith: be it done to you as you wish*[^16]. He cast the bread; rather, he gave it, he did not cast it; because now he was giving not to a dog, but to a human being. For he gave to the faith of the one asking, to the faith of the one seeking, to the faith of the one knocking; and therefore he praised the faith, because she did not reject humility. Let your Holiness attend to the words of the Lord saying: *Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine.* And whom does he wish to be understood as dogs? Dogs are the slanderous, barking; but swine are those defiled with the mire of carnal pleasures. So let us not be dogs and swine, that we may merit to be called sons by the Lord; just as that Canaanite woman merited, from a dog, to be called a woman, the Lord saying: *O woman, great is your faith: be it done to you as you wish.*
