# Sermon 48

**1** We have heard the readings of the divine utterances as they were recited. They are set before us as the matter of our speaking. From them we ought to savor, from them to sow what we savor, with the help of him *in whose hand* are, as it is written, *both we and our discourses*[^1]. Nor in vain is it written in another place: *In the Lord I will praise the discourse, in the Lord I will praise the word*[^2]. This is praised in the Lord, which the Lord gives. Although, then, we are weak, yet we are his vessels. We grasp as much as we can, we share without envy what we grasp. May he supply in your hearts what we have done too little, because even what we work in your ears, what is it unless he does the whole in your hearts?

**2** What the first prophetic reading commended to us, recall as I recall it. *What worthy thing*, it says, *shall I offer the Lord?*[^3] A man was seeking a sacrifice by which to appease God, or by which to please God. *Shall I bend the knee*, he says, *to the most high God, shall I appease him with thousands of bulls or with tens of thousands of fat goats, or shall I offer to God the fruit of my womb for the sin of my soul?*[^4] Shall I offer my firstborn, he says, to my God for the sin of my soul? *It is answered you, O man.* O man, by whom is it answered, except by the one by whom man was made? *It is answered*, then, *you, O man*, seeking what you may offer God, and whence you may appease God or whence you may please God. *It is answered you what is good, or what else does the Lord require of you, but that you do judgment and justice and love mercy and be ready to walk with the Lord your God*[^5]. You were seeking what you might offer for yourself. Offer yourself. For what does the Lord seek of you, except you? Because in all earthly creation he made nothing better than you. He seeks you from yourself, because you had lost yourself. But if you do what he commanded, he finds in you judgment and justice: judgment first in yourself, justice toward your neighbor. How judgment in yourself? That what you were should displease you, and you may be able to be what you were not. Judgment, I say, about yourself in yourself without favoritism toward your own person, that you not spare your sins, nor let them please you because you do them, nor praise yourself in your goods, and accuse God in your evils. For this is a perverse judgment, and therefore not a judgment. For to show that a perverse judgment is no judgment, he did not say: What does the Lord require of you, except to make right judgment, but: to make, he says, judgment. For if right, then judgment; but if perverse, not judgment, but a fault. What, then, were you doing, when you had lost yourself and went after your perdition, you went and did not return? What were you doing? I know what you were doing. In your goods you praised yourself, in your evils you blasphemed God. This is a perverse judgment, and therefore, as I said, not even a judgment. Do you want, then, to make right judgment, that is, judgment? Correct what you were doing; reverse it, and it will be right. What is "correct it"? Praise God in your goods, accuse yourself in your evils. When, then, you have displeased yourself as perverse, and corrected yourself by the help of him who created you, upright you will keep justice. For God will please you, if you are upright. You will not differ from the upright one, unless you are crooked and perverse. But upright, you will agree with the upright, and without doubt God will please you. For when he displeased you, he displeased your perversity.

**3** Hear the holy psalm: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart!*[^6] Did this one who says this in the psalm have God displease him? Far be it from me to accuse him and not rather believe one confessing. See, hear with me, and consider what he said: *How good*, he says, *is the God of Israel!* To whom? *To the upright in heart. But my*, he says—when I was not upright in heart—*but my feet were almost moved, my steps were a little less than poured out*[^7]. What is *my feet were moved*, this is *my steps were poured out*. What is *almost*, this is *a little less*. Why, then, does he say: *almost, a little less my feet were moved, my steps were poured out?* Almost, he says, I slipped, almost I fell. Whence did you come into such great peril? *Because I was zealous over sinners*, he says, *beholding the peace of sinners. I was zealous*, he says, *over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^8]: that is, by seeing evil men happy, I tottered under God, almost I fell from God. See whence God had displeased him: why do the evil have good things?

**4** Finally see the words of one tottering, what he said within himself: *Behold, these sinners*—in the psalm itself are the words of one tottering—*behold, these sinners have obtained riches in the world*[^9]. *And I said: How does God know?*[^10] He himself says it in the psalm, he himself speaks, to whom, not yet upright, God had displeased because the evil abounded in goods. *How*, he says, *does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High?* See still what he adds, see how, by tottering, he approaches the fall, and is near to perdition. See, I say, what he adds: *Have I then in vain justified my heart, and washed my hands among the innocent?*[^11] I have lost, he says, all that I have lived well. *I justified my heart, I washed my hands among the innocent*, to this end, that the evil are happy, and I am afflicted. *And I was*, he says, *scourged all day long*[^12]. They rejoice, and I am scourged. They rejoice who blaspheme God, I am scourged who worship God. *How does God know?* Hence he tottered, hence he almost fell, hence he thought that human affairs did not pertain to God.

**5** Since, then, he thought this, not with an upright but with a perverse heart, and was as it were led by a verisimilar reasoning, on account of this incongruity, to believe that the governance of human affairs did not pertain to God, it had pleased him so to preach, to assert this, to teach this. He was called back by the authority and preaching of the saints. For see his words: *If I said*, he says, *if I said I will tell it so*—I will preach so, I will teach so, I will say to men that the care of human affairs does not pertain to God—*If I said I will tell it so: behold, I have rejected the generation of your children*[^13]. How, then, shall I tell it so? Moses did not tell it so, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob did not tell it so, Jeremiah did not tell it so, Isaiah did not tell it so, the other Prophets did not tell it so. But all these are your children. If, then, I tell it so, I will reject the generation of your children.

**6** What, then, shall I do? *I undertook to know*[^14]. I undertook, he says, to know. But it is a great thing to know, it is difficult to know. This, he says, after he said: *I undertook to know, it is a labor before me*[^15]—how I may know both that God is just, and knows human affairs, and that it is well with the perverse, that it is sometimes ill with the upright. How it is just, I undertook to know, and it is a labor before me.

**7** How long the labor? *Until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand in the last things*[^16]. Enter, then, into the sanctuary of God, faithful soul, enter into the sanctuary of God, devout soul, you to whom God does not displease in your evils, and to whom God does not displease in the goods of the evil. And if you do not know by what reason it happens, believe that what God permits or does is not done unjustly. You were led by human reason, be called back by divine authority, and believe that there is something there that lies hidden from you. For it is to be believed by a most certain faith that God can be neither perverse nor unjust. So, entering by faith into the sanctuary of God, entering by believing, you learn by understanding. For so he says: *Until I enter into the sanctuary* of God, where faith enters. And after faith, what? *And understand in the last things*. The last things will come, when it will be ill for no good man, well for no evil man. The last things will come, I say, when the devout will be distinguished from the impious, the just from the unjust, the praisers of God from the blasphemers of God. They will come, when they will be distinguished, so that, as it was said, it will be ill for no good man, well for no evil man. Why, then, is it not so now? Perhaps even now it is so. But what is now in hiddenness, afterward will be in the open.

**8** Enter with me, if you can, into the sanctuary of God. Perhaps there, if I can, I will teach you. Rather, learn with me from the one who teaches me, that even now it is not well for the evil, and that it is better for the good than for the evil; although the full happiness of the good has not yet come, and the last punishment of the evil has not yet come. Perhaps you understand with me that it is not well for the evil. For I ask you and inquire of you, why is it ill for you? You will answer: "Want oppresses, difficulty presses, perhaps pain of the limbs, fear from an enemy." Is it therefore ill for you, because you suffer evils; and well for him, who is the very evil? There is much difference between suffering evil and being evil. What you suffer, you are not. For you suffer evil, but you are not evil. Evil, I say, you suffer, but you are not evil. But he does not suffer evil, and he is evil. Do not, then, be deceived, do not be deceived. It cannot be that it is ill for you who suffer evil, and well for him who is evil. For when he is evil, do you think he does not suffer evil, when he suffers himself? It is ill for you, because you suffer another's evil in your body. And is it well for him, who suffers himself as an evil in his heart? It is ill for you, because you have a bad estate. And is it well for him, who has a bad soul? Be good, you who have good things. Riches are good, gold is good, silver is good, households are good, possessions are good, all these things are good, but as means whence you may do good, not things that make you good. Have the goods that make you good. What are those, you say? Do judgment, do justice. Are the things you have good? Do judgment, do justice. Be you too good among your good things. Blush before your goods: be a good man, about to abide, among perishable goods. Blush before your goods: do not be evil in them, lest you perish with them. How the rest stands, my brothers, how justice is to be kept, and how mercy is to be loved, and how each one ought to be ready to walk with the Lord his God, we will discourse to you at another time, the Lord granting. Hold me as a debtor, lest you have me long as a wearier.
