# Sermon 344: On the Love of God and the Love of the World

**1.** Two loves wrestle with each other in this life, in every temptation: the love of the world, and the love of God; and whichever of these two prevails draws the lover there as if by a weight. For it is not by wings or feet, but by affections that we come to God. And again, it is not by bodily knots and chains, but by contrary affections that we cling to the earth. Christ came to change love, and from one who was earthly to make a lover of the heavenly life; made man for our sake, he who made us men; and God taking up man, that he might make men gods. This is the contest set before us, this the wrestling with the flesh, this the wrestling with the devil, this the wrestling with the world. But let us trust, since he who appointed this contest does not watch without his help, nor exhort us to presume upon our own strength. For he who presumes upon his own strength, surely because he is a man, presumes upon the strength of a man; and *cursed is everyone who places his hope in man*[^1]. Burning with the flame of this pious and holy love, the martyrs burned the grass of the flesh by the strength of the mind; but they themselves, whole in spirit, arrived at him by whom they were kindled. Yet the due honor will be granted, in the resurrection of the dead, to the very flesh that despised itself. For it was sown in dishonor, so that it might rise in glory[^2].

**2.** To those kindled with this love, or rather that they may be kindled, he says this: *Whoever loves father or mother above me is not worthy of me; and whoever does not take up his cross and follow me is not worthy of me*[^3]. He did not take away the love of parents, of wife, of children, but ordered it; he did not say, Whoever loves; but, *Whoever loves above me*. This is what the Church says in the Song of Songs: *He has set love in order in me*[^4]. Love your father, but not above the Lord; love your begetter, but not above the Creator. Your father begot you, but he himself did not form you; for who, or what sort of being, was to be born to him, he did not know when he sowed the seed. Your father nourished you, but the father did not establish out of his own bread for you when you were hungry. Finally, whatever your father keeps for you on earth, he departs that you may succeed him, he makes room for your life by his death. But what God the Father keeps for you, he keeps with himself; so that you may possess the inheritance with the Father himself, and not await him as a predecessor as though you were a successor, but cling to one who will always remain, yourself always to remain in him. Love your father, then, but not above your God. Love your mother, but not above the Church, who begot you to eternal life. Finally, from the love of your very parents weigh how much you ought to love God and the Church. For if those are to be so loved who begot one doomed to die, with how great a love are those to be loved who begot one who will come to eternity, who will remain in eternity? Love your wife, love your children according to God, so that you may take counsel for them to worship God with you; and when you are joined to him, you will fear no separation. Therefore you ought not to love above God those whom you love altogether badly if you neglect to lead them to God with you. Perhaps the hour of martyrdom will come. You wish to confess Christ. Having confessed, perhaps you will receive a temporal penalty, you will receive a temporal death. Your father, or wife, or son coaxes you not to die, and by coaxing brings it about that you die. If they do not bring it about, there will come into your mind: *Whoever loves father, or mother, or wife, or children above me is not worthy of me*.

**3.** But carnal affection is bent by the coaxing of one's own people, and in a certain way human softness slips. Draw in the folds of your flowing garment, gird yourself with virtue. Does the love of the flesh torment you? Take up your cross, and follow the Lord. And your Savior himself, although God in the flesh, although God with the flesh, yet showed human affection where he said: *Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me*[^5]. He knew that this cup could not pass; he had come to drink it. That cup was to be drunk by will, not by necessity. He was almighty: if he willed, it would surely pass, because he is God with the Father, and both he and God the Father are one God. But from the form of a servant, from that which he took up from you for you, he uttered the voice of a man, the voice of the flesh. He deigned to transfigure you into himself, so that you might speak weak things of him, so that in him you might lay hold of strong things. He showed the will by which you could be tempted; and at once he taught which will, to which will you ought to prefer it. *Father*, he said, *if it can be done, let this cup pass from me*. This is the human will; I bear a man, I speak from the form of a servant. *Father, if it can be done, let this cup pass*. It is the voice of the flesh, not of the spirit; the voice of weakness, not of divinity. *If it can be done, let this cup pass from me*. That is the will of which it is also said to Peter: *But when you have grown old, another will gird you, and take you, and carry you where you do not wish*[^6]. From where, then, did the martyrs too conquer? Because they preferred the will of the spirit to the will of the flesh. They loved this life, and weighed it. From there they considered how greatly eternal life is to be loved, if this perishing one is so loved. The one doomed to die does not wish to die; and yet he will necessarily be dying, although continually unwilling to die. You accomplish nothing by not wishing to die, you effect nothing, you wring nothing out; you have no power to take away the necessity of death. What you fear will come, though you are unwilling; what you defer will be present, though you refuse it. For you are busy to defer death; surely not to take it away? If, then, among the lovers of this life so much is busied about deferring death, how much must be labored about taking it away? You surely do not wish to die. Change your love, and a death is shown to you, not one that will be present though you are unwilling, but one that, if you are willing, will not be.

**4.** See, then, if a little love has awakened in your heart, if a spark has flashed from the ash of the flesh, if it has caught hold of some strength in your heart, which by the wind of temptation is not only not extinguished but even more vehemently kindled; if you do not burn like tow, which you would be extinguished by one light breath; but burn like oak, burn like coal, so that you are rather roused by the breath; see two deaths, one temporal, and that the first; the other everlasting, and that the second. The first death is prepared for all; the second only for the evil, the impious, the unbelieving, blasphemers, and whatever else is opposed to sound doctrine. Attend, set these two deaths before yourself. If it can be done, you do not wish to suffer both. I know, you love to live, you do not wish to die; and you would wish so to pass from this life into another life, that you should not rise again having died, but be changed for the better while alive. This you would wish, this human affection has; this the soul itself, in some way, has in its will and desire. Since by loving life, it hates death; and since it does not hate its own flesh, it does not wish to happen to it what it hates. *For no one ever hated his own flesh*[^7]. The Apostle shows this affection where he says: *We have a dwelling from God, a house not made by hands, eternal in the heavens. For in this we groan, desiring to be clothed over with our habitation which is from heaven. In which we do not wish*, he says, *to be unclothed, but to be clothed over, that the mortal may be swallowed up by life*[^8]. You do not wish to be unclothed: but you must be unclothed. Yet you ought to act so that, unclothed through death of the tunic of flesh, you may be found clothed with the breastplate of faith. For he goes on, following this: *If indeed, being clothed, we be not found naked*[^9]. For the first death will unclothe you of the flesh, to be laid aside for a time, and received back in its own time. This you will wish or not wish. For not because you wish it will you rise; or if you do not wish it, you will not rise; or if you do not believe in the resurrection, you will therefore not rise. You must rather act so that you who are going to rise, willing or not willing, may so rise that you have what you will wish. For the Lord Jesus himself said: *The hour comes when all who are in the tombs shall hear his voice, and shall come forth*, whether they be good or be evil; *all who are in the tombs shall hear his voice, and shall come forth*, and shall be brought out of the hidden places. No creature will hold a dead person under the voice of the living Creator. *All*, he says, *who are in the tombs shall hear his voice, and shall come forth*. In saying *All*, he made as it were a confusion and a commingling. But hear the distinction, hear also the separation: *those who have done good*, he says, *to the resurrection of life; those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^10]. This judgment, to undergo which the impious shall rise, is called the second death. Why, then, O Christian, do you dread this first one? It will come though you are unwilling, it will be present though you refuse it. Perhaps you ransom yourself from barbarians, lest you be killed; you ransom yourself at a great price, you do not at all spare your possessions, and you strip your children; and ransomed, tomorrow you will die. You must be ransomed from the devil, who drags you with himself to the second death, where the impious set at the left hand shall hear: *Go, you cursed, into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^11]. From this second death you must be ransomed. You will answer: With what? Do not seek goats and bulls; do not, finally, rummage through your strongbox, and say in your mind: To ransom myself from barbarians, I had money; to ransom yourself from the second death, have justice. The barbarian himself could take your money from you beforehand, and afterward lead you off captive, so that you would have nothing with which to ransom yourself, he possessing all your goods who possessed you too; but you do not lose justice unwillingly; it remains in the inmost treasury of the heart; hold it, possess it, from it you are ransomed from the second death. And if you do not wish it, it will therefore not be, because that by which you ransom yourself from this death, if you are willing, will be. The will obtains justice from the Lord, and drinks it as from its own fountain. To which fountain no one is forbidden to approach, if he approaches worthily. Finally, see your help. Your silver ransomed you from barbarians, your money ransomed you from the first death; the blood of your Lord ransomed you from the second death. He had blood with which to ransom us; and for this he received blood, that there might be what he could pour out for ransoming us. The blood of your Lord, if you will, was given for you; if you are unwilling to be, it was not given for you. For perhaps you say: My God had blood with which to ransom me; but now, since he has suffered, he has given it all; what has remained to him that he may give also for me? This is the great thing, that he gave it once, and gave it for all. The blood of Christ is salvation to the one willing, punishment to the one unwilling. Why, then, do you doubt, you who do not wish to die, to be freed rather from the second death? From which you are freed, if you will take up your cross and follow the Lord; because he took up his own, and sought the servant.

**5.** Altogether, my brothers, do not those who so love temporal life most exhort you to love eternal life? How much do men do, in order to live a few days? Who will be able to count the efforts and exertions of all who wish to live, who die after a little while? How much do they do for those few days? Why do we do so much for eternal life? What shall I say, for ransoming a few days, and these on earth? For I call them few days, even if, having been freed, one grows old; I call them few days, even if, having been freed, the boy becomes decrepit. I do not say that, ransomed today, he will perhaps die tomorrow. Behold, for an uncertain outcome, for those very few uncertain days, how much do they do? What do they devise? If through bodily disease they come into the hands of a physician, and all hope of health is despaired of by those who pronounce and examine; if some physician is promised who is fit to free even the despaired-of one, how much is promised! How much, indeed, is given for an uncertain outcome! That one may live a little, that is let go from which one lives. And further, if one falls into the hands of an enemy or a robber, that he may not be killed, that he may be ransomed, even, if a father is detained, the children run, and spend what he was going to leave them, to ransom one whom they can carry out to burial. What entreaties? What prayers? What efforts? Who could unfold this? And yet I wish to say something more serious, and unless it happened, more incredible. For what do I say, that men give money in order to live, that they leave nothing for themselves? To live a little, those few and uncertain days, with fear, with labor, how much do they spend? How much do they give? Woe to the human race! I said that to live they spend that from which they live: hear what is worse, what is more serious, what is more wicked, what is, as I said, incredible unless it happens. That they may be allowed to live a little, they give even that from which they could live forever. Hear what I have said, and understand. For it is still closed, and yet it moves many, to whom the Lord has already opened it while it was closed. Let those go who give and lose that from which they can live, so that they may be granted to live a little. Attend to those who lose that from which they can live forever, so that they may be granted to live a little. What is this? It is called faith, it is called piety; all this is as it were the money by which eternal life is acquired. The enemy will come from across, terrifying, and will not say to you: Give me your money, that you may live; but will say to you: Deny Christ, that you may live. And if you do this, that you may be allowed to live a little, you will lose that from which you could live forever. Is this to love life, you who feared death? Good man, why did you fear death, except by loving life? Christ is life. Why do you crave a small life, so as to lose the secure one? Or perhaps you did not lose faith, but you did not have anything to lose? Hold, then, that from which you may live forever. Attend to your neighbor, how much he does that he may live a little. Attend also to the one who denied Christ, how great an evil he did for a few days of life. And do you not wish to despise those few days of life, that you may die on no day, and live in an everlasting day, be protected by your Redeemer, be made equal to the angels in the eternal kingdom? What did you love? What did you lose? In order to follow the Lord, you did not take up your cross.

**6.** See how prudent he wishes you to be, who said to you: *Take up your cross, and follow me. Whoever finds*, he says, *his soul will lose it; and whoever loses it for my sake will find it*[^12]. Whoever finds it will lose it; whoever loses it will find it. In order to lose it, it is first necessary to find it; and when you have lost it, the last thing is that you find it again. There are two findings: in the middle is the one losing through which they pass. No one can lose his soul for Christ unless he has first found it; and no one can find his soul in Christ unless he has first lost it. Find, that you may lose; lose, that you may find. How are you first going to find it, so that you have what you may lose? When you consider yourself partly mortal, when you consider him who made you, and who by breathing in created your soul for you, and you see that you owe it to him who gave it—it must be rendered to him who lent it; it must be guarded by him who established it—you have found your soul, finding it in faith. For you have believed this, and you have found your soul. For you were lost before you believed. You have found your soul: for you had been dead in unbelief, you have come back to life in faith. You are such a one of whom it can be said: *He was dead, and has come back to life; he was lost, and is found*[^13]. Therefore you have found your soul in the faith of the truth, if you have come back to life from the death of unbelief. This is to have found your soul. Lose it, and let your soul be seed for you. For the farmer too, by threshing and winnowing, finds the wheat, and again by sowing loses the wheat. There is found on the threshing-floor what had been lost in the sowing. There is lost in the sowing what may be found in the harvest. Therefore, *whoever finds his soul will lose it*. He who labors to gather, why is he slothful to sow?

**7.** See, however, where you may find it, and why you should lose it. For from where would you find it, unless he kindled a light for you to whom it is said: *You will light my lamp, O Lord*[^14]? Now, then, you have found it, with him kindling the lamp for you. See why you should lose it. For what was so diligently found is not to be lost just anywhere. He did not say: Whoever loses it will find it; but: *Whoever loses it for my sake*. When perhaps you look upon the body of a shipwrecked merchant on the shore, you give tears in pity, and say: Woe to this man! For the sake of gold he lost his soul. You weep well, you pity well. Give weeping to him to whom you do not also offer help. For he was able to lose his soul for the sake of gold; for the sake of gold he will not be able to find it. He was fit for the loss of his soul; he proved less fit for gaining it. For we should not consider what he lost, but why he lost it. If for the sake of greed, behold where the flesh lies, where is what was dear? And yet greed commanded, and for gold the soul was lost; and for Christ the soul is not lost, nor let it be lost. Fool, do not doubt: hear the counsel of the Creator. He himself instructs you to be wise, who made you before you were one who is wise. Hear, do not doubt to lose your soul for Christ. You commend to the faithful Creator what you are said to lose. You indeed will lose it: but he receives it, to whom nothing is lost. If you love life, lose it that you may find it; because when you have found it, there will no longer be what you may lose, there will be no reason why you should lose it. For that life is found which is found so that it can in no way be lost. Because Christ too, who by being born, dying, and rising gave you an example, *rising from the dead now dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^15].
