# Sermon 392: To the Married

**1.** We have heard the Apostle saying to us: *We perform an embassy for Christ, exhorting you to be reconciled to God*[^1]. He would not exhort us to be reconciled, unless we had been enemies. So the whole world was an enemy to the Savior, a friend to the one who took it captive: that is, an enemy to God, a friend to the devil. And the whole human race, like that woman, was bent to the earth. Now a certain man, understanding these enemies, cries out against them and says to God: *They bent down my soul*[^2]. The devil and his angels bent the souls of men to the earth, that is, that, prone toward the things that are temporal and earthly, they should not seek things above. For surely the Lord says this of that woman whom Satan had bound, behold, eighteen years; and now she had to be loosed from her bond, and to be loosed on the sabbath day. But they brought a charge against the one who raised her up—who, but the bent? Since they too, not understanding the things which God had commanded, gazed on them with an earthly heart. For they celebrated the mystery of the sabbath carnally, and did not see it spiritually.

**2.** Hear, dearest ones, members of Christ and sons of mother Church. What I say to the candidates for baptism, let the faithful hear; what I say to the faithful, let the candidates hear; what I say to the candidates and the faithful, let the penitents hear; what I say to the faithful and the candidates and the penitents, let the catechumens hear, let all hear: let all fear, let no one despise. Let your listening be my consolation, lest my grief be a testimony against you. To the candidates I say: It is not lawful for you to fornicate. Let either wives or no wives suffice for you: it is not lawful for you to have concubines. Let God hear, if you are deaf; let his angels hear, if you despise. It is not lawful for you to have concubines. And if you do not have wives, it is not lawful for you to have concubines, whom you afterward dismiss in order to take wives; how much more will it be condemnation for you, if you wish to have both concubines and wives? It is not lawful for you to have wives whose former husbands are alive; nor for you, women, is it lawful to have husbands whose former wives are alive. These are adulterous marriages, not by the law of the forum, but by the law of heaven. Nor is it lawful for you to marry a woman who departed from her husband by divorce, while her husband is alive. Only for the cause of fornication is it lawful to dismiss an adulterous wife: but while she is alive it is not lawful to marry another. And to you, women, neither is it granted to have as husbands those men from whom their wives departed by divorce; it is not lawful: they are adulteries, not marriages. Augustine is despised; let at least Christ be feared. Do not imitate the crowd of the wicked, of unbelievers, my sons: do not follow the broad ways, whose end leads to destruction. Whoever has been baptized, let him either vow continence to God, or remain with his wife, or, if he has none, marry a wife.

**3.** Hear me, you faithful, that is, the baptized. Why will you die, now reborn? When, baptized, you go by crooked and slippery and unclean ways, do you not know that you perish? You perish, my sons, believe it. Do you not want to believe? What shall I do for you? You who are faithful, and hear me, if perhaps you have committed such things, do not add to them; and that God may forgive you, pray. If you could not have, or would not have, conjugal chastity or continence, and have strayed from your purpose either of the conjugal bond or of devoted continence, let there be in you the grief and humility of repentance. I say it more openly: let no one say, I did not understand. You who after taking your wives have defiled yourselves by unlawful intercourse, if besides your wives you have lain with any woman, do penance, such as is done in the Church, that the Church may pray for you. Let no one say to himself: I do it secretly, I do it before God: God knows, who will forgive me, because I do it in my heart. Was it, then, said without reason: *What you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven*[^3]? Were the keys, then, given to the Church of God without reason? Do we make the Gospel void, do we make the words of Christ void? Do we promise you what he denies? Do we not deceive you? Job says: *If I was ashamed in the sight of the people to confess my sins*[^4]. So great a just man, the pure gold of the divine treasury, tested in such a furnace, says this; and a son of pestilence resists me, and a proud neck, a crooked mind, is ashamed to bend the knee under the blessing of God? Perhaps—indeed it is not doubted—for this reason God willed that the emperor Theodosius do public penance in the sight of the people, especially because his sin could not be hidden; and is a senator ashamed of what an emperor was not ashamed of? Is one ashamed—not even a senator, but only a town councillor—of what an emperor was not ashamed of? Is a plebeian or a tradesman ashamed of what an emperor was not ashamed of? What is this pride? Would it not alone suffice for hell, even if there were no adultery?

**4.** Finally, my brothers—the men hear me, the women hear me—why are you angry at me? Would that you would do what is written: *Be angry, and do not sin*[^5]. I ought to fear lest there befall me what befell the apostle Paul, which just now, when it was read, if you were attentive, you heard: *Have I, then, become your enemy by preaching the truth?*[^6] And if it is so, let it be. If it is necessary that I be your enemy, I would rather wish you to be an enemy than righteousness. I commend you to be guarded even by your wives. They are my daughters, just as you too are my sons. Let them hear me: let them be jealous for their husbands; let them not keep for themselves that empty glory by which matrons are wont to be praised by unchaste husbands, because they bear the unchastity of their husbands with an even mind. I do not want Christian women to have such patience: let them altogether be jealous for their husbands; not for the sake of their own flesh, but for the sake of their husbands' souls. Altogether I admonish, I command, I order: the bishop orders, Christ in me orders. He knows in whose sight my heart burns. I, I say, order: do not permit your husbands to fornicate. Call the Church to account against them. I do not say the public judges, not the proconsul, not the vicar, not the count, not the emperor; but Christ. In all other things be the handmaids of your husbands, subject in obedience. Let there be in you no insolence, no pride, no contemptuous neck, no disobedience: serve altogether as handmaids. But when it comes to that business in which the blessed Apostle made you equal, saying: *Let the husband render the debt to the wife, and likewise the wife to the husband*; he added: *The wife does not have power over her own body, but the husband*. Why do you exalt yourself? Hear what follows: *Likewise the husband does not have power over his own body, but the wife*[^7]. When it comes to this, cry out for your right. Your husband sells your gold for his need: bear it, woman; bear it, handmaid; do not quarrel, do not contradict. The contempt of your gold is the love of your husband. If he sells your farm for his need, which is also yours (for it cannot be his own, which is not yours, if there is love in you, which ought to be in a wife), bear it patiently; and if he hesitates, you offer it: despise all things for the love of your husband. But desire him to be chaste, quarrel for chastity. Let your farm perish patiently, let not his soul perish while you bear with it.

**5.** I do not say to the men, that in this matter they should be jealous for their wives. I know that they do it, I know it well. Who would bear an adulterous wife? And a woman is commanded to bear an adulterous husband! O justice! Why, I ask you? why? Because I am a man. You are a man? In your strength let us test whether you are a man. You are a man? Conquer lust. How a man, when the wife is stronger? You are the head of the woman, man, it is true. If he is the head, let him lead, let the wife follow. But where the house is rightly ordered, the man is the head of the woman. If you are the head, lead: let her follow her head. But see where you go. Do not go where you do not want her to follow: do not go where you fear your follower, lest you both rush together into the pit of adultery: lest, when you do it, you teach her what you do. It grieves your soul, if you both rush together into the pit of adultery: let it grieve you, if you alone rush. You are jealous, you do not want her to fall: be afraid, do not you fall. But do not, most chaste women, imitate your unchaste husbands. Far be it from you. Let them either live with you, or perish alone. To an unchaste husband a woman does not owe chastity, but she owes it to God, she owes it to Christ. Let her not do it for the sake of him who does not deserve it; but let her do it for the sake of Christ. Let her attend to her own price, let her read her own contract. Finally, let him think what he pleases who perhaps is indignant because I discuss such things: for I know that those who are wise love me for it; because not without reason it is written: *Rebuke a wise man, and he will love you: rebuke a fool, and he will go on to hate you*[^8]. He did not say: He will begin; but, *he will go on*: because he already hated. So I know that the wise love me in this. Let them keep themselves from communion, who know that I know their sins; lest they be cast out from the rails. But those whom I do not know, these I deal with before God. Let them too do penance, and thereafter abstain from the uncleanness of their fornications.

**6.** To the penitents I say: what is it that you are doing? Know that you are doing nothing. What does it profit that you are humbled, if you are not changed? To the catechumens I say: burn with the will to receive grace. But choose for yourselves in the Church of God those whom you may imitate. If you do not find any: alas for me, my God! What is it I say: If you do not find any? Is there, then, in the people of the faithful no one whom you may find? For so many years, so many men we have baptized in vain, if there are not there those who keep what they received, who guard what they heard. Far be it from me to believe this. Better that I were not your bishop, if it is so. But I hope there are, I believe there are. From this, however, comes my wretched condition, that for the most part I am compelled to know adulterers, the chaste I cannot know. In secret is that wherein I may rejoice, in the open is that wherein I am tormented. So desire the grace of God, choose those whom you may imitate, with whom you may live, and with whom you may have the sweet converse of love. Do not admit evil whisperings. *Evil conversations corrupt good morals*[^9]. Live like ears of grain among the weeds: bear the tribulations of this age, like grains on the threshing floor. The winnower will come: let no one be a separator here and there at this time.
