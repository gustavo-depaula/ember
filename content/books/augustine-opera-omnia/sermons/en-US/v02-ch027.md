# Sermon 39

**1** We have heard, brothers, God saying through the prophet: *Do not delay to turn to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day. For his wrath will come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^1]. He promised you that, on whatever day you turn, he forgets your past malice. But did he promise you the life of tomorrow? Or did God perhaps not promise it to you, and an astrologer promised it to you, that God might condemn both you and him? God has, for our salvation, set the day of death as uncertain. Let each one, for his salvation, think of his own last day. It is the mercy of God that a man does not know when he will die. The last day lies hidden, that all days may be observed.

**2** But the world holds us, enticements flatter on every side. The greatness of money delights, the splendor of honor delights, the terror of power delights. These things delight, but let the Apostle be heard: *We brought nothing into this world, but neither can we carry anything out*[^2]. Honor ought to seek you, not you it. For you ought to recline in a lower place, that the one who invited you may make you go up to a more honored place. But if he is unwilling, eat where you recline, because you brought nothing into this world. Is it too little for you that you eat of another's? Recline wherever, and eat. Will you say, "Of my own"? Hear the Apostle: *We brought nothing into this world*. You came to the world, you found a full table. But *the earth is the Lord's, and its fullness*[^3].

**3** For those who wish, he says, to become rich. He did not say, Those who are rich, but: *Those who wish to become rich*; he accused desires, not means: *Those who wish to become rich fall into temptation and many harmful desires, which plunge men into ruin and perdition*[^4]. Money delights. Do you not fear this? Money is a good thing, great money is a good thing. *They fall into temptation*. Do you not fear? *They fall into many foolish and harmful desires*. Do you not fear? Where do desires lead? *Which plunge men into ruin and perdition*. And are you still deaf? Do you not fear ruin and perdition? Thus God thunders, and do you snore?

**4** But to those who are already rich, the Apostle still gave counsel: *Charge*, he says, *the rich of this world not to be proud in mind*[^5]. The worm of riches is pride. It is difficult for one who is rich not to be proud. Take away pride, and riches will not harm. But attend to what you ought to do with them, lest what God bestowed lie idle with you. *Not to be proud in mind*. Take away this vice. *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*. Take away this vice too. When you have taken away these things, exercise the good works you hear of. *Let them be*, he says, *rich in good works. Let them not hope in the uncertainty of riches*. But where should they hope? *In the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy*[^6]. God furnishes the world to the poor man, furnishes it to the rich man. Surely not, because he is rich, will he fill two bellies? Attend and see that from the gifts of God the poor sleep satisfied. He who feeds you feeds them through you.

**5** Therefore let money not be loved. But if it is now had, let this be done with it. Be rich, you who have it. But where rich? *In good works. Let them give easily*, he says, *let them share*[^7]. Here now avarice contracts. Hear what follows: *Let them give easily, let them share*. As though it pours cold water over, it stiffens, it pulls tight the purse, and says: "I do not lose my labors." Unhappy man, do you not want to lose your labors? See, you will die, and you who brought nothing here can carry nothing away from here. When you carry nothing away, have you not lost all your labors? Hear, then, the counsel of God. Do not be terrified because he said, *Let them give easily, let them share*. Hear what follows. Wait, do not shut the door against me, nor the hearing of your heart; wait. Do you want to know: *Let them give easily, let them share*, because you will not lose, and this alone you will not lose? *Let them treasure up*, he says, *for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^8]. Therefore this life that delights you is a false life. You live here as if in dreams. If you live here as if in dreams, you will wake up when you die, and so you have nothing to find in your hands. As if a beggar should sleep and in dreams an inheritance came to him, nothing is happier than he before he rises. He sees himself in dreams handling fine garments with his hands, precious vessels of gold and silver, entering the most pleasant and ample estates, great households waiting on him. He wakes, and weeps. And just as, awake, he accuses the man who stripped him, so he accuses the one who roused him. Most openly the psalm spoke of this: *They slept their sleep*, it says, *and all the men of riches found nothing in their hands*[^9], after they finished their sleep.

**6** Because, then, you brought nothing here, you will carry nothing away from here. Send up what you have found, and you will not lose it. Give to Christ: for here Christ wished to receive. You give to Christ, and you lose it? You do not lose it if you entrust it to your servant, and you lose it if you entrust it to your Lord? You do not lose it if you entrust to your servant what you have acquired, and you will lose it if you entrust to your Lord what you received from your Lord himself? Christ wished to be in need here, but for our sake. All the poor whom you see, Christ could have fed, as he fed Elijah by a raven. Yet he took away the raven even from Elijah. That he might be fed by a widow, he provided not for Elijah but for the widow. When, then, God makes poor men—because he himself does not want them to have—when he makes poor men, he tests the rich. For so it is written: *The poor man and the rich man met one another*[^10]. Where did they meet? In this life. The one was born, the other was born too: they found one another, they met one another. And who *made them? The Lord*. The rich man, that he might help the poor; the poor man, that he might test the rich. Let each one act according to his strength. Let him not act so as to suffer straits himself. We do not say this. Your superfluous things are necessary to another. You heard just now, when the Gospel was read: *Whoever gives a cup of cold water to one of the least of mine for my sake will not lose his reward*[^11]. He set the kingdom of heaven up for sale, and willed its price to be a cup of cold water. But when the one who gives alms is poor, then his alms ought to be a cup of cold water. He who has more, let him do more. That widow gave from two mites. Zacchaeus gave half his goods, and reserved the other half to repay his frauds. Alms profit those who have changed their life. For you give to Christ in need to redeem your past sins. For if you give for this reason, that you may always be allowed to sin with impunity, you do not feed Christ, but you try to corrupt the judge. Therefore give alms for this, that your prayers may be heard and that God may help you to change your life for the better. And you who change it, change that same life for the better. And through alms and prayers let past evils be blotted out, and future good things, everlasting, come.
