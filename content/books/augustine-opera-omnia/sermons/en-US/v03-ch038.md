# Sermon 76: Again on Matthew 14:24-33: on the Lord Walking upon the Waters of the Sea, and on Peter Faltering

**1. 1.** The Gospel which was most recently read, about the Lord Christ, who walked upon the waters of the sea; and about the apostle Peter, who, walking, faltered by fearing, and, sunk by mistrusting, emerged again by confessing[^1]; admonishes us to understand that the sea is the present world, but the apostle Peter the type of the one Church. For Peter himself, first in the order of the Apostles, most ready in the love of Christ, often alone answers for all. And finally, when the Lord Jesus Christ asked whom men said that he was, and the disciples answered with the various opinions of men, and the Lord again asked, saying: *But who do you say that I am?* Peter answered: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^2]. One gave the answer for many, unity in the many. Then the Lord said to him: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* Then he added: *And I say to you*[^3]. As if he said: Because you said to me: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God: I too say to you: You are Peter*[^4]. For before he was called Simon. But this name, that he should be called Peter, was imposed on him by the Lord: and this in that figure, that he might signify the Church. For because Christ is the rock (petra), Peter is the Christian people. For "rock" is the principal name. Therefore Peter is from "rock," not "rock" from Peter: just as "Christ" is not named from "Christian," but "Christian" from Christ. *You are*, then, he says, *Peter; and upon this rock* which you confessed, *upon this rock* which you have known, saying: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God, I will build my Church*[^5]: that is: Upon myself, *the Son of the living God, I will build my Church*. Upon myself I will build you, not myself upon you.

**2. 2.** For men, wishing to be built upon men, said: *I indeed am of Paul, but I of Apollos, but I of Cephas*, that is, *Peter.* And others who were unwilling to be built upon Peter, but upon the rock: *But I am of Christ*[^6]. But the apostle Paul, when he knew that he was chosen, and Christ despised: *Is Christ*, he says, *divided? Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^7] As not in the name of Paul, so not in the name of Peter; but in the name of Christ: that Peter might be built upon the rock, not the rock upon Peter.

**2. 3.** So this same Peter, surnamed from the rock, blessed, bearing the figure of the Church, holding the primacy of the apostolate, immediately after a little, when he had now heard that he was blessed, had now heard that he was Peter, had now heard that he was to be built upon the rock, after he heard of the Lord's future passion, because he had foretold to his disciples that it would soon be, it displeased him. He feared lest he lose, dying, him whom he had confessed the fountain of life. He was disturbed, saying: *Far be it from you*, he says, *Lord; this shall not be*[^8]. Be merciful to yourself, God; I do not wish you to die. Peter said to Christ, I do not wish you to die; but Christ said better, I wish to die for you. Finally, he at once rebukes the one whom a little before he had praised; and the one whom he had called blessed, he calls Satan. *Go*, he says, *behind me, Satan; you are a scandal to me: for you do not savor the things that are of God, but the things that are of men*[^9]. What does he wish us to make of what we are, who so blames that we are men? Do you wish to know what he wishes us to make of ourselves? Hear the Psalm: *I said: You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High*, but by savoring human things: *But you will die like men*[^10]. The same Peter, a little before blessed, afterward Satan, in one moment, within a few words. You wonder at the difference of names, attend to the differences of causes. Why do you wonder, that a little before blessed, afterward Satan? Attend to the cause why blessed. *Because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^11]. Therefore blessed, *because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you.* For if flesh and blood revealed this to you; from your own: but because *flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven;* from mine, not from your own. Why from mine? Because *all things that the Father has are mine*[^12]. Behold you have heard the cause why blessed, and why Peter. But why that which we shudder at, and are unwilling to repeat? Why, except because from your own? *For you do not savor the things that are of God, but the things that are of men.*

**3. 4.** Gazing at this, let us, a member of the Church, distinguish what is of God, what of our own. For then we will not falter, then we will be founded on the rock, we will be fixed and stable against winds, rains, rivers, namely the temptations of the present world. Yet see that Peter, who then was our figure: now he has confidence, now he falters; now he confesses the Immortal one, now he fears lest he die. Accordingly, because the Church of Christ has the firm, has also the weak; and can be neither without the firm, nor without the weak: whence the apostle Paul says: *But we who are firm ought to bear the burdens of the weak*[^13]; in that Peter said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^14], he signifies the firm; but in that he trembles and falters, and is unwilling for Christ to suffer, by fearing death, by not acknowledging life, he signifies the weak of the Church. In that one apostle, then, that is, Peter, first and chief in the order of the Apostles, in whom the Church was figured, both kinds had to be signified, that is, the firm and the weak: because without both the Church does not exist.

**3. 5.** Hence, then, is also what was just now read: *Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you upon the waters*[^15]. *If it is you, command me:* for I cannot do this in myself, but in you. He recognized what was to him from himself, what from him, by whose will he believed he could do what no human weakness can. So: *If it is you, command:* because when you have commanded, it will be done. What I cannot by presuming, you can by commanding. And the Lord: *Come*[^16], he says. And without any doubt Peter, at the word of the one commanding, at the presence of the one sustaining, at the presence of the one ruling, without any hesitation leaped down into the waters, and began to walk. He could do what the Lord could, not in himself, but in the Lord. *For you were once darkness, but now light;* but *in the Lord*[^17]. What no one can do in Paul, no one in Peter, no one in any other of the Apostles, this he can do in the Lord. Therefore Paul, well and usefully despising himself, commending him: *Was Paul*, he says, *crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^18] Not, then, in me, but with me; not under me, but under him.

**4. 6.** So Peter walked upon the waters at the command of the Lord, knowing that he could not have this from himself. By faith he prevailed in what human weakness could not. These are the firm of the Church. Attend, hear, understand, act. For we must not deal with the firm elsewhere, that they become weak: but we must deal with the weak, that they become firm. But the presumption of firmness hinders many from firmness. No one will be firm from God, except one who feels himself weak from himself. *A voluntary rain setting apart, O God, for his inheritance*[^19]. Why do you run ahead, you who know what I am going to say? Let the swiftness be tempered, that slowness may follow. This I said, and this I say: hear, grasp, do. No one is made firm by God, except one who feels himself weak from himself. *A voluntary rain*, then, as the Psalm says, *voluntary;* not of our merits, but *voluntary. A voluntary rain*, then, *setting apart, O God, for his inheritance: for it was weakened; but you perfected it*[^20]. Because you set apart a voluntary rain, not attending to the merits of men, but to your grace and mercy. The inheritance itself, then, was weakened, and recognized itself weak in itself, that it might be firm in you. It would not be made firm, if it were not weakened, that from you it might be perfected in you.

**5. 7.** See a little portion of this inheritance, Paul; see him weakened, who said: *I am not fit to be called an Apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God*[^21]. Why, then, are you an Apostle? *By the grace of God I am what I am. I am not fit*, but *by the grace of God I am what I am*[^22]. Paul was weakened, but you perfected him. But now, because it is by the grace of God that he is what he is, see what follows: *And his grace in me was not empty, but I labored more than all of them*[^23]. See lest by presumption you lose what you merited by weakness. Well this, well: *I am not fit to be called an Apostle; by his grace I am what I am; and his grace in me was not empty:* all most excellently. But: *I labored more than all of them:* as though you had begun to attribute to yourself what a little before you had given to God. Acknowledge it, and follow: *But not I, but the grace of God with me*[^24]. Well, weak one, you will be exalted most firmly, because you are not ungrateful. You are the same Paul, in yourself small, in the Lord great. You are the one who asked the Lord three times that the thorn of the flesh, the angel of Satan, by whom you were buffeted[^25], be taken from you. What was said to you? what did you hear when you asked this? *My grace is sufficient for you; for power is perfected in weakness*[^26]. For he was weakened, but you perfected him.

**5. 8.** So too Peter: *Command me*, he says, *to come to you upon the waters*[^27]. I, a man, dare it, but I do not ask a man. Let God command the man, that he may be able to do what a man cannot. *Come*, he says. And he went down, and began to walk upon the waters: and Peter could do it, because the rock had commanded. Behold what Peter is in the Lord; what in himself? *Seeing the strong wind, he feared; and when he had begun to sink, he cried out: Lord, I perish, free me*[^28]. He presumed of the Lord, he could do it of the Lord: he faltered as a man, he returned to the Lord. *If I said, My foot is moved*[^29]. The Psalm speaks, it is the voice of the holy song; and if we acknowledge it, it is ours too; rather, if we wish, it is ours too. *If I said, My foot is moved.* Why moved, except because *mine?* And what follows? *Your mercy, O Lord, helped me*[^30]. Not my strength; but *your mercy.* For did the Lord desert the one faltering, whom he heard calling upon him? Where is that: *Who has called upon God, and been deserted by him?* Where too that: *And everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^31]? At once holding out the help of his right hand, he lifted the one sinking, he rebuked the one mistrusting: *O you of little faith, why did you doubt?*[^32] Of me you presumed, of me you doubted.

**6. 9.** Come, brothers, the sermon must be concluded. Attend to the world as if a sea, the wind strong, and a great tempest. For each one his own craving is the tempest. You love God; you walk upon the sea, under your feet is the swelling of the world. You love the world; it will swallow you up. It knows how to devour its lovers, not to carry them. But when your heart fluctuates with craving, that you may conquer your craving, call upon the divinity of Christ. Do you think a contrary wind is present only when there is adversity of this world? For when there are wars, when tumults, when famine, when pestilence, when to any of men, even to one, a private calamity happens, then a contrary wind is thought, then God is thought to be called upon. But when the world smiles with temporal happiness, it is as if there is no contrary wind. Do not from this ask about the tranquillity of the time: ask, but your own craving. See whether there is tranquillity in you; see whether the inner wind does not subvert you: this see. It is of great virtue to wrestle with happiness, lest it entice, lest it corrupt, lest happiness itself subvert. It is, I say, of great virtue to wrestle with happiness; it is of great happiness not to be conquered by happiness. Learn to tread the world underfoot: remember to trust in Christ. And if your foot is moved, if you falter, if you do not overcome certain things, if you begin to sink, say: *Lord, I perish, free me*[^33]. Say: *I perish;* lest you perish. For he alone frees you from the death of the flesh, who died in the flesh for you. Turned to the Lord, etc.
