# Sermon 207: In Lent

**1.** With the help of the mercy of the Lord our God, the temptations of the age, the snares of the devil, the toil of the world, the enticement of the flesh, the waves of turbulent times, and every corporeal and spiritual adversity, must be overcome by alms, fasts, and prayers. Although these things ought to be fervent for the Christian throughout his whole life, yet especially with the Paschal solemnity drawing near, which by its yearly return rouses our minds; renewing in them, by a salutary memory, that our Lord, the only Son of God, showed mercy to us, fasted and prayed for us. For "alms" in Greek is "mercy." But what greater mercy upon the wretched could there be than that which brought down the Creator of heaven from heaven, and clothed the founder of earth with an earthly body; which made him who in the eternity of the Father remains equal coequal with us in mortality, which laid the form of a servant upon the Lord of the world; that the Bread himself might hunger, that Fullness might thirst, that Strength might be weakened, that Health might be wounded, that Life might die? But this, that our hunger might be fed, our dryness watered, our weakness consoled, our iniquity extinguished, our love kindled. What greater mercy, than that the Creator be created, the Ruler serve, the Redeemer be sold, the Exalter be humbled, the Raiser be slain? To us, concerning the giving of alms, it is commanded that we give bread to the hungry[^1]; he, that he might give himself to us hungering, first handed himself over for us to the raging. It is commanded us to receive the stranger: he came for our sake into his own, and his own did not receive him[^2]. Finally, let our soul bless him *who is propitious to all its iniquities, who heals all its diseases, who redeems its life from corruption, who crowns it in pity and mercy: who satisfies its desire with good things*[^3]. So let us perform our alms so much the more lavishly, and so much the more frequently, the nearer the day becomes on which the alms bestowed on us is celebrated. Because a fast without mercy is nothing to him who fasts.

**2.** Let us also fast, humbling our souls, as the day draws near on which the master of humility humbled himself, being made subject even to the death of the cross[^4]. Let us imitate his cross, fixing our desires, tamed by the nails of abstinence. Let us chastise our body and subject it to servitude[^5]: and lest through untamed flesh we slip into unlawful things, in taming it let us withdraw somewhat even of the lawful. Surfeiting and drunkenness are to be avoided even on the other days: but on these days even permitted lunches are to be removed. Adulteries and fornications are always to be execrated and fled: but on these days we must abstain even from our spouses. Easily will the flesh obey you, that it cling not to what is another's, which has grown used to being reined in even from its own. But surely care must be taken lest you change, not lessen, your pleasures. For you may see some seeking, in place of their usual wine, unwonted liquors, and compensating much more sweetly, by the pressing of other fruits, for what they deny themselves from the grape; seeking out, beyond meat, foods of manifold variety and pleasantness; and gathering, as it were opportunely for this time, sweetnesses which at another time they would be ashamed to pursue: so that, namely, the observance of Lent is not the repression of old desires, but the occasion for new delights. Provide against these things, brothers, with as much vigilance as you can, lest, persuaded of them, they creep upon you. Let frugality be joined to fasts. As the fullness of the belly is to be chastised, so the incitements of the gullet are to be guarded against. The kinds of human foods are not to be detested, but the fleshly delight is to be reined in. Esau was reprobated not by a fat calf or by fattened fowls, but by an immoderately desired lentil dish[^6]. Holy David repented of having desired water more than was right[^7]. Not, then, with elaborate or precious foods, but with whatever cheaper ones are at hand and set out, is the body to be refreshed by the fast, or rather propped up.

**3.** On these days, by the supports of pious alms and frugal fasts, our prayer is lifted up to the heights: because mercy is not shamelessly asked of God, when by a man it is not denied to a man, nor is the serene intent of the asking heart hindered, clouded by the phantasms of fleshly pleasures. But let prayer be chaste, lest perhaps we wish for not what love, but what cupidity seeks; lest we imprecate some evil on our enemies; lest, against those whom we cannot harm by injuring or avenging, we rage by praying. Surely, as we are made fit for praying by alms and fasts, so our prayer itself does alms, when it is directed and poured forth not only for friends, but even for enemies, and it fasts from anger and hatred and from the most pernicious vices. For if we fast from foods, how much more should it fast from poisons? Finally, we are refreshed at the due and opportune times by the taking of food: let us never delight that prayer with such viands. Let it take up these perpetual fasts: because it has its own proper food, which it is commanded to take without interruption. Let it always, then, fast from hatred, always be fed with love.

[^2]: See John 1:11
