# Sermon 56: On the Lord's Prayer, in the Gospel of Matthew 6, to the Candidates for Baptism

**1. 1.** The blessed Apostle, showing that these times—when it was to come about that all nations would believe in God—had been foretold by the Prophets, set down this testimony which is written: *And it shall be, every man who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved*[^1]. For previously the name of the Lord, who made heaven and earth[^2], was called upon among the Israelites alone: the other nations called upon mute and deaf idols, by whom they were not heard; or demons, by whom they were heard to their own harm. But when the fullness of time came[^3], what was foretold is fulfilled: *And it shall be, everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.* Then, because the Jews themselves envied the nations the Gospel—even those who believed in Christ, and said that the Gospel of Christ ought not to be announced to those who were not circumcised; because against these the apostle Paul set this testimony: *And it shall be, whoever calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved*; he at once added, to convict those who were unwilling that the nations be evangelized[^4], and said: *But how will they call upon him in whom they have not believed? Or how will they believe him whom they have not heard? But how will they hear without a preacher? Or how will they preach, unless they are sent?*[^5] Because, then, he said: *How will they call upon him in whom they have not believed?*, therefore you did not receive the prayer first, and afterward the creed; but first the creed, where you might know what you believe, and afterward the prayer, where you might know whom you call upon. The creed, then, pertains to faith, the prayer to petition: because he who believes is himself heard in calling upon him.

**2. 2.** But many ask for what they ought not to ask, not knowing what is good for them. Two things, then, the one who calls upon God ought to beware of: that he not ask what he ought not, and that he not ask of one of whom he ought not. From the devil, from idols, from demons nothing ought to be asked that ought to be asked: from the Lord our God, the Lord Jesus Christ, God the Father of the Prophets, the Apostles, and the martyrs, from the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from God who made heaven and earth and sea and all that is in them, from him must it be asked, if anything must be asked. But one must beware lest even of him be asked what we ought not to ask. Because we ought to ask for human life, if you ask it of deaf and mute idols, what does it profit you? Likewise of God the Father, who is in heaven, if you wish for the death of your enemies, what does it profit you? Have you not heard or read in the psalm, in which the damnable traitor Judas was foretold, how the prophecy said of him: *Let his prayer be turned to sin?*[^6] If, then, you rise and pray evils for your enemies, your prayer will be turned to sin.

**3. 3.** In the holy psalms you have read, as it were, the one who speaks in the psalms imprecating many evils on his enemies. "And surely," someone says, "the one who speaks in the psalms is just: why does he wish such evils on his enemies?" He does not wish them, but foresees them: it is the prophecy of one foretelling, not the wish of one cursing. For in spirit they knew to whom evil would come, to whom good: and through prophecy they spoke, as though wishing what they foresaw. But you, whence do you know whether one for whom you ask evil today may not be better than you in the future? "But I know he is malicious." And you know you are malicious. Although perhaps you even dare to judge of the heart of another, which you do not know: but you know yourself malicious too. Do you not hear the Apostle saying: *Who was formerly a blasphemer and a persecutor and insolent; but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief?*[^7] When the apostle Paul persecuted the Christians, binding them where he found them, dragging them off to the priests to be heard and punished[^8]; what do you think, brothers, the Church prayed against him, or for him? Surely the Church of God, which had learned from her Lord, who hanging on the cross said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^9]; prayed such things for Paul, indeed still for Saul, that this might be done in him which was in fact done. For because he says: *But I was unknown by face to the churches of Judea that are in Christ; only they heard that he who once persecuted us now evangelizes the faith which he once ravaged, and they glorified God in me*[^10]: why did they glorify God, except because, before it was done, they asked God?

**3. 4.** Our Lord, then, first cut away much speaking[^11], lest you bring many words to God, as if you wished by many words to instruct God. When, then, you ask, godliness is needed, not wordiness. *For your Father knows what is necessary for you, before you ask him*[^12]. Do not, then, speak much: because he knows what is necessary for you. But lest perhaps someone here say: "If he knows what is necessary for us, why do we say even a few words? Why do we pray? He himself knows: let him give what he knows is necessary for us." But for this reason he willed that you pray, that he might give to one who desires, lest what he gave grow cheap: because he himself inspired the very desire. The words, then, which our Lord Jesus Christ taught in the prayer, are the form of our desires. It is not lawful for you to ask anything other than what is written there.

**4. 5.** *You, therefore*, he says, *say: Our Father, who are in heaven*[^13]. In the "our," you see, you have begun to have God as Father. But you will have him, when you have been born. Although even now, before you are born, you have been conceived of his seed, in the womb, as it were, of the Church, in the font of bringing to birth. *Our Father, who are in heaven.* Remember that you have a Father in heaven. Remember that you were born of father Adam unto death, and are to be regenerated of God the Father unto life. What you say, say in your hearts. Let there be the affection of one praying, that there may be the effect of one being heard. *Hallowed be your name*[^14]. What you ask to be hallowed, the name of God, is holy. Why do you ask what is already holy? Then, when you ask that his name be hallowed, do you not, as it were, ask it for him, and not for yourself? Understand, you ask it for yourself too. For you ask this, that what is always holy in itself may be hallowed in you. What is *hallowed?* That it be held holy, not despised. So you see that when you wish it, you wish good for yourself. For it is an evil for you, if you despise the name of God, not for God.

**5. 6.** *Your kingdom come*[^15]. To whom do we say it? And if we do not ask, is the kingdom of God not going to come? For it is of that kingdom that it is said which will be after the end of the age. For God always has a kingdom; and he is never without a kingdom, whom all creation serves. But what kingdom do you wish for? That of which it is written in the Gospel: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^16]. Behold of what we say: *Your kingdom come.* We wish that it come in us; we wish that we be found in it. For behold, it will come: but what does it profit you, if it finds you on the left hand? So here too you wish well for yourself, you pray for yourself. This you desire, this you crave by praying, that you may so live that you belong to the kingdom of God, which is to be given to all the saints. So, that you may live well, you pray for yourself, when you say: *Your kingdom come.* Let us belong to your kingdom; let there come to us too what is to come to your saints and just ones.

**5. 7.** *Your will be done*[^17]. If you do not say it, will God not do his will? Remember what you professed in the creed: *I believe in God the Father almighty*. If he is almighty, do you pray that his will be done? What, then, is *Your will be done?* May it be done in me, that I not resist your will. So here too you pray for yourself, and not for God. For the will of God will be done in you, even if it is not done by you. For even those to whom he is going to say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^18], the will of God will be done in them, that the just and holy may receive the kingdom; and those to whom he is going to say: *Go into the eternal fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels*[^19], the will of God will be done in them, that the evil may be condemned to the everlasting fire. It is another thing that it be done by you. So that it may be done in you, you do not pray without cause, except that it may be well with you. Whether, then, it be well with you, or ill with you, it will be done in you: but let it also be done by you. Why, then, do I say: *Your will be done in heaven and on earth*, and do not say: "Your will be done by heaven and by earth"? Because what is done in you, he himself does by you. Never is anything done by you, that he does not himself do in you. But sometimes he does in you what is not done by you: but never is anything done by you, if he does not do it in you.

**5. 8.** But what is *in heaven and on earth*, or: *as in heaven, so also on earth*? The angels do your will, let us do it too. *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth.* The mind is heaven, the flesh is earth. When you say, if indeed you say, what the Apostle says: *With the mind I serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin*[^20]; the will of God is done in heaven, but not yet on earth. But when the flesh has consented to the mind, and death has been swallowed up in victory[^21], so that no carnal desires remain with which the mind contends; when the quarrel on earth has passed, when the war of the heart has passed, when what was said has passed: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; for these are opposed to each other, so that you do not do the things you wish*[^22]: when, then, this war has passed, and all desire has been changed into love, nothing will remain in the body that resists the spirit, nothing to be tamed, nothing to be bridled, nothing to be trodden down; but the whole, through concord, will press on to justice; your *will is done in heaven and on earth.* We wish for perfection when we pray this. Again, *Your will be done in heaven and on earth.* In the Church, the spiritual are heaven, the carnal are earth. *Your will be done*, then, *in heaven and on earth*: that as the spiritual serve you, so the carnal too, changed for the better, may serve you. *Your will be done in heaven and on earth.* There is another sense, very devout. For we are admonished to pray for our enemies. The Church is heaven; the enemies of the Church are earth. What, then, is *Your will be done in heaven and on earth?* Let our enemies believe, as we too believe in you: let them become friends, let them end their enmities. They are earth, therefore they oppose us; let them become heaven and they will be with us.

**6. 9.** *Give us this day our daily bread*[^23]. And here it is now manifest that we pray for ourselves. When you say: *Hallowed be your name*, it must be explained to you that you pray for yourself, not for God. When you say: *Your will be done*, this too must be explained to you, lest you think you wish well for God, that his will be done, and not rather pray for yourself. When you say: *Your kingdom come*, this too must be explained, lest you think you wish well for God, that he may reign. But from this place onward, even to the end of the prayer, it appears that we ask God for ourselves. When you say: *Give us this day our daily bread*, you profess yourself a beggar of God. But do not blush: however rich anyone is on earth, he is a beggar of God. A beggar stands before the house of a rich man: but the rich man himself stands before the house of a great rich man. He is asked, and he asks. If he were not in need, he would not knock at the ears of God with prayer. And of what is the rich man in need? I dare to say, the rich man is in need of his very daily bread. For why do all things abound to him? Whence, except because God gave them? What will he have, if God withdraws his hand? Have not many gone to sleep rich, and risen poor? And that he lacks nothing is of the mercy of God, not of his own power.

**6. 10.** But this bread, dearly beloved, by which the belly is filled, by which the flesh is daily refreshed; this bread, then, you see God gives not only to his praisers but also to his blasphemers, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust[^24]. You praise, he feeds you: you blaspheme, he feeds you. That you may do penance, he waits for you: but if you do not change yourself, he condemns you. Because, then, both the good and the evil receive this bread from God, do you think there is not some bread which the sons ask, of which the Lord spoke in the Gospel: *It is not good to take the bread of the children and cast it to the dogs?*[^25] There is plainly. What is this bread? And why is this too called daily? For it is necessary: without it we cannot live, without bread we cannot. It is impudence to ask God for riches: it is not impudence to ask for daily bread. It is one thing whence you grow proud, another thing whence you live. Yet, because this visible and tangible bread is given to both the good and the evil; there is a daily bread which the sons ask: the very word of God, which is dispensed to us daily. It is our daily bread: from it not bellies but minds live. It is necessary for us even now as laborers in the vineyard; it is food, not wages. For he who hires a laborer for the vineyard owes him two things: both food, lest he fail, and wages, in which he may rejoice. Our daily food on this earth is the word of God, which is always dispensed to the churches: our wage after the labor is named eternal life. Again, in this our daily bread, if you understand what the faithful receive, what you, baptized, are to receive; we ask well and say: *Give us this day our daily bread*: that we may so live as not to be separated from that altar.

**7. 11.** *And forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^26]. And this petition need not be explained, because we ask for ourselves. For we beg that our debts be forgiven us. For we are debtors, not of money, but of sins. Perhaps you say now: You too? We answer: We too. And you, holy bishops, are you debtors? We too are debtors. You too? Far be it, lord, do not do yourself an injury. I do not do myself an injury, but speak the truth: we are debtors. *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^27]. Both we have been baptized, and we are debtors. Not because anything remained that was not forgiven us in baptism: but because by living we have contracted what is daily forgiven. Those who are baptized and depart, ascend without debt, go on without debt; but those who are baptized and are kept in this life contract something from mortal frailty, whence, even if there is no shipwreck, yet the bilge must be drained: because if it is not drained, little by little there enters in what may sink the whole ship. And to pray this is to drain the bilge. But we ought not only to pray, but also to give alms: because when the bilge is drained lest the ship sink, it is done both with voices and with hands. We act with voices, when we say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*; but we act with hands, when we do: *Break your bread for the hungry, and bring the needy without shelter into your house*[^28]. *Shut up alms in the heart of the poor man, and it will entreat for you to the Lord*[^29].

**8. 12.** When all our sins, then, have been forgiven through the washing of regeneration, we would be thrust into great straits if the daily cleansing of holy prayer were not given us. Alms and prayers cleanse sins; only let not such things be committed whence we must necessarily be separated from the daily bread; avoiding the debts to which a certain and severe damnation is owed. Do not call yourselves just, as though you had nothing whence to say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* Abstaining from idolatry, from the consultations of astrologers, from the remedies of enchanters; abstaining from the deceptions of heretics, from the schisms of schismatics; abstaining from murders, from adulteries and fornications, from thefts and robberies, from false testimonies: and if perhaps any other things—I do not say those which have deadly outcomes, whence one must necessarily be cut off from the altar, and bound on earth that he be bound in heaven[^30]: very dangerously and mortally, unless what is loosed in heaven is loosed on earth—these excepted, then, a man does not lack whence he may sin. By seeing willingly what he ought not, he sins. And who could restrain the swiftness of the eye? Since the eye (oculus) is said to have taken its name from swiftness (velocitas). Who could restrain the ear or the eye? The eyes, when you wish, can be closed, and are quickly closed: the ears you close with effort; you lift your hand, you reach them: and if someone holds your hands, they are open; nor can you close them against cursing, impure, flattering, and deceiving words. When you have heard something you ought not, even if you have not done it, do you not sin with the ear? You hear something evil willingly. How many sins the deadly tongue commits! Sometimes such as separate a man from the altar. To it belongs the matter of blasphemies. And many empty things are said, which do not pertain to the matter. Let the hand do nothing evil; let the foot not run to anything evil; let the eye not be directed to wantonness; let the ear not lie open willingly to filth; let the tongue not be moved to what is unfitting. You say: Who restrains thoughts?

**9. 12.** My brothers, very often we pray, and think of something else, as though forgetful before whom we stand, or before whom we lie prostrate. If all these things are gathered against us, do they then not press because they are small? What does it matter whether lead presses you, or sand? Lead is one mass, sand is small grains, but they press by their abundance. Sins are small: do you not see that rivers are filled from tiny drops, and lands are washed away? They are small, but they are many.

**9. 13.** Let us say daily, then, and say it with a true heart, and do what we say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^31]. We make a pledge with God, a pact and an agreement. This your Lord God says to you: Forgive, and I forgive. You have not forgiven? You hold it against yourself, not I. Surely, my dearest children, since I know what is good for you in the Lord's Prayer, and most of all in that whole prayer this sentence: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*, hear me. You are to be baptized, forgive all things: let whoever has anything against another in his heart forgive it from the heart. So enter, and be certain that absolutely everything is forgiven you which you have contracted, both by being born of parents according to Adam with original sin—on account of which sin you run with the little one to the grace of the Savior—and whatever you have added by living, in words, deeds, thoughts: all is forgiven; and you will go out from there, as from the sight of your Lord, with security from all debts.

**10. 14.** Now, on account of those daily sins of which I have spoken, because it is necessary for you to say, as a kind of daily cleansing, this: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*; what will you do? You have enemies: for who lives on this earth without having an enemy? Attend to yourselves: love them. In no way can a raging enemy harm you as much as you harm yourself, if you do not love your enemy. For he can harm either your estate, or your cattle, or your house, or your manservant, or your maidservant, or your son, or your spouse, or, at most, if power is given him, your flesh: but can he, as you can, harm your soul? Extend yourselves to this perfection, dearly beloved, I exhort you. But did I grant you that? He granted it to you, to whom you say: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* Yet let it not seem impossible to you: I know, I have come to know, I have proved that there are Christian men who love their enemies. If it has seemed impossible to you, you will not do it. First believe that it can be done: and pray that the will of God be done in you. For what does the evil of your enemy profit you? If he had no evil, he would not even be your enemy. Wish him good, let his evils end, and he will not be an enemy. For it is not human nature in him that is your enemy, but his fault. Is he your enemy because he has a soul and flesh? This is what you are: you have a soul, he has a soul; you have flesh, he has flesh. He is of one substance with you: together you were made of earth by the Lord, you were given life. He is what you are too: look upon your brother. First, our two parents were Adam and Eve; he the father, she the mother: therefore we are brothers. Let us pass over the first origin; God is the father, the Church the mother: therefore we are brothers. But my enemy is a pagan, is a Jew, is a heretic; and whence I just now said: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* O Church! your enemy is a pagan, a Jew, a heretic: he is earth. If you are heaven, call upon the Father who is in heaven, and pray for your enemies: for Saul too was an enemy of the Church; so it was prayed for him, and he became a friend. He not only ceased to be a persecutor, but labored to be a helper. And if you seek the truth, it was prayed against him: but against his malice, not his nature. Pray you too against the malice of your enemy: let it die, and let him live. For if your enemy dies, you have, as it were, been rid of an enemy, but you have found no friend; but if his malice dies, you have found a friend too.

**11. 15.** Still you say: Who can? Who does this? Let God do it in your hearts. I too know: few do it, those who do it are great, the spiritual do it. Are all the faithful in the Church who approach the altar, receiving the body and blood of Christ, such? Are all such? And yet all say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* If God answers them: Why do you ask me to do what I promised, when you do not do what I commanded? What did I promise? To forgive your debts. What did I command? That you too forgive your debtors. How can you do these things, if you do not love your enemies? What, then, shall we do, brothers? Is the flock of Christ reduced to so small a number? If only those ought to say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*, who love their enemies: I do not know what to do, I do not know what to say. For am I going to say to you: If you do not love your enemies, do not pray? I do not dare; rather, that you may love them, pray. But what shall I say to you? If you do not love your enemies, do not say in the Lord's Prayer: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors?* Suppose I say: Do not say it. If you do not say it, they are not forgiven: if you say it and do not do it, they are not forgiven. Therefore it must both be said and done, that they may be forgiven.

**12. 16.** I see something whence I can console not the Christian fewness, but the multitude: and I know that you desire to hear this. *Forgive, that it may be forgiven you*[^32], Christ said. And what do you say in the prayer? whence we now treat: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* So forgive, Lord, as we forgive. You say this: "So forgive, Father who are in heaven, our debts, as we too forgive our debtors." For this you ought to do: and if you do not do it, you will perish. But what? When you hear: An enemy asks pardon, forgive at once. And is this much for you? It was much for you to love a raging enemy: is it much for you to love a man entreating you? What do you say? He raged, and you hated him. I would prefer that you had not hated him even then: I would prefer that then, when you were suffering him raging, you would remember the Lord saying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^33]. This, then, I would greatly wish, that even at that time, when the enemy raged against you, you would look to the Lord your God saying these things. But perhaps you are about to say: "He did it, but as the Lord, because he is Christ, because he is the Son of God, because he is the Only-begotten, because he is the Word made flesh: but I, an evil and powerless man?" If your Lord is too much for you, let your fellow servant be thought of by you. Holy Stephen was being stoned: and amid the stones, on bended knee, he prayed for his enemies, and said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^34]. They threw stones, they did not beg pardon: and he prayed for them. Such I wish you to be: extend yourself. Why do you always drag your heart on the earth? Hear, extend your heart upward, love your enemies. If you cannot love one raging, love at least one entreating. Love the man who says to you: Brother, I have sinned, forgive me. Then, if you do not forgive him, I do not say: You blot out the prayer from your heart; but: You will be blotted out from the book of God.

**13. 17.** But if at least then you forgive, or at least then dismiss the hatred from your heart—hatred, I say, dismiss from your heart, not discipline. What if the one who asks pardon must be chastised by me? Do what you wish: for I think that you love your son even when you beat him. You do not heed the tears of the one being whipped: because you are keeping his inheritance. This I say, that you dismiss hatred from your heart, when your enemy asks pardon of you. But perhaps you say: "He lies, he pretends." O judge of the heart, tell me the evil thoughts of your father, tell me your own thoughts of yesterday. He asks, he begs pardon: forgive, utterly forgive. If you do not forgive, you harm not him, but yourself. For he knows what he is going to do. Will you, a fellow servant, not forgive your fellow servant? He will go to your Lord, and say to him: "Lord, I asked my fellow servant to forgive me, and he was unwilling to forgive: do you forgive me." Is it not lawful for the Lord to release the debts of his servant? He, having received pardon, departs from the Lord absolved: you remain bound. How bound? The time of prayer will come, it will come that you say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* The Lord will answer you: By no means; when you owed me so much, you asked me, and I forgave you: ought you not also to have had mercy on your fellow servant, as I had mercy on you? These words are from the Gospel, not from my heart[^35]. But if, asked, you have forgiven the one begging pardon, now you can say this prayer. And if you are not yet fit to love one raging, yet you can say this prayer: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* Let us pass on to the rest.

**13. 18.** *Lead us not into temptation*[^36]. *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*, we say on account of past sins, which we cannot make not to have been. You can act so as not to do what you have done: but how do you act so that what you have done was not done? On account of those things which are already done, this sentence of healing comes to your aid: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* On account of those things into which you may fall, what will you do? *Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil*: that is, from temptation itself.

**14. 19.** And those three petitions will be: *Hallowed be your name, Your kingdom come, Your will be done in heaven and on earth*; these three petitions are on account of human life. For the name of God ought always to be hallowed in us, we ought always to be in his kingdom, we ought always to do his will: this will be unto eternity. Daily bread is necessary only now; from this point on, the rest of what we pray pertains to the necessity of the present life. Daily bread is necessary in this life, the forgiveness of our debts is necessary in this life: for when we come to that other life, we shall be done with debts; on this earth there is temptation, on this earth one sails dangerously, on this earth through the cracks of frailty something enters that must be bailed out. But when we have been made equal to the angels of God, far be it that we should say, far be it that we should ask God to forgive our debts, which will be none. Here, then, daily bread; here, that debts be forgiven; here, that we enter not into temptation: because in that life temptation does not enter; here, that we be delivered from evil; because in that life there will be no evil, but the everlasting good will remain.
