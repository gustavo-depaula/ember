# Sermon 37

**1** He who commended this day in his saints will grant us that the weakness of our voice may suffice for your attention. This I have commended for this reason, that you may deign to help me by your silence. For the spirit is eager toward you, but the flesh is weak. And the spirit itself, whatever joys it has perhaps conceived from the Scripture of God, is in labor and seeks to give birth to them in your ears and minds. Provide a nest within you for the sermon. For in Scripture too the turtledove is commended, seeking a nest for itself where it may set its young. And this which we carry in our hands—the Scripture, namely, which you see—commends to us a certain woman to be inquired after and praised, of whom you heard a little before when it was read, having a great husband, a great one, the husband who found her lost and adorned her when found. Of her, according to the tenor of the reading which you see me carrying, I will say a few things, as time allows, which the Lord suggests. For it is the day of the martyrs, and therefore the mother of the martyrs is the more to be praised. Now who this woman is you have learned from my preface. See also whether you recognize her as I read. Every hearer now, as far as appears well enough from your affection, says in his heart: "It must be the Church." I confirm this thought. For what other could be the mother of the martyrs? So it is. What you have understood, this it is. The woman of whom we wish to say something is the Church. For it would not befit us to speak of any other woman. Although in the recitation of the passion of the martyrs we heard of women too, of whom we may fittingly speak, yet we do not pass them over when we praise their mother.

**2** Attend to whose members you are; inspect whose sons you are. *Who will find a valiant woman?*[^1] It is fitting to call the strength of the martyrs that of a woman. For unless she herself were strong, those members of hers would have failed in the passion. *Who will find a valiant woman?* It is difficult to find her—rather, it is difficult not to know her. Is she not herself the city on the mountain, which cannot be hidden? Why, then, was it said, *Who will find?* when it ought to have been said, Who will not find? But you see the city set on the mountain. But that it might be set on the mountain, the one who had been lost was found. When she was made bright, who does not see her? When she lay hidden, who would find her? For she herself is the city, and that one sheep which the shepherd sought when lost, and which, found, he joyfully carried back on his shoulders. The shepherd himself is the mountain; the sheep on his shoulders is the city on the mountain. It is easy for you to see her set on the mountain. When would you have found her, when she lay hidden in the brambles, in the thorns of her own offenses? For there to have sought her is a great thing; there to have found her is a wonderful thing. This difficult finding of her is commended when it is said: *Who will find a valiant woman?* "Who"—because one, not because not even one. As it was said of her husband himself, the lion of the tribe of Judah, of whom the prophecy foretold: *You went up, lying down*[^2]—namely, onto the cross. *You went up*, that is the cross; *lying down*, that is death. What is *You went up*, except what is written: *And they crucified him*[^3]? Whence he too says: *As Moses lifted up the serpent in the desert, so must the Son of man be lifted up, that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life*[^4]. What is *lying down*? *And bowing his head, he gave up the spirit*[^5]. When, then, it was said there too, *You went up, lying down*, there followed: *You slept like a lion*[^6]. *You slept like a lion*, you did not flee like a fox. What is *You slept like a lion*? By power, not by necessity. But when it was said, *You slept like a lion*, it followed and said: *Who roused him?*[^7] Who roused him? For not no one, but who among men? Because none but *God*, who *exalted him* from the dead, *and granted him a name which is above every name*[^8]. And he himself raised himself. Whence he says: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^9]. And now, when you heard, *Who will find a valiant woman?* do not think it is said of the Church that lies hidden, but of that Church which was found by one, so that she might lie hidden from no one. Let her, then, be described, praised, commended, to be loved by us all as a mother, for she is the wife of one. Who will find a valiant woman? This woman so valiant, who does not see her? But already found, already eminent, already conspicuous, already glorious, already adorned, already shining, already—to put it briefly—spread through the whole world of lands.

**3** *But she who is such is more precious than precious stones*[^10]. What great thing is it, that this woman is more precious than precious stones? If you think only of human greed, if precious stones are taken literally, what great thing is it that the Church is found more precious than any stones whatever? There is no such comparison. But there are precious stones in her. These stones are so precious that they are called living. There are, then, precious stones adorning her, but she herself is more precious. I want to commend something to your Charity—as much as I grasp, as much as you grasp, as much as I fear, as much as you ought to fear—about these precious stones. There are in the Church precious stones, and there always have been, learned men, abounding both in knowledge and in eloquence and in every instruction of the law. Plainly these stones are precious. But of their number some have strayed from the adornment of this woman. For as far as pertains to the doctrine and eloquence by which he shines—for he shines with the Lord's teaching—Cyprian was a precious stone, but he remained in her adornment. Donatus was a precious stone, but he sprang back from the joining of the adornment. The one who remained wished to be loved in her. The one who was shaken out from there sought a name for himself apart from her. The one abiding with her gathered to her. The one springing back longed not to gather, but to scatter. Evil sons, why do you follow a precious stone shaken out from the adornment of this woman? You answer me: "What then? Do you understand as he does? Or do you speak as he does? Or are you as learned as he?" Granted that he is intelligent—*good understanding to those who do it*[^11]—granted that he is learned, granted that he is instructed in the liberal disciplines and the mysteries of the law, he is a precious stone. Turn back from him to her: she is more precious than precious stones. A precious stone, if it is not in the adornment of this woman, lies in darkness. A precious stone, wherever it lies, if it is not in the adornment of this woman, lies in darkness. It needed to remain in the adornment of this woman, to be in the joining of her adornment. But I would say confidently: precious stones are so called because they are worth dearly. Now that one is cheap, he has lost his price, who does not have love. Let him boast of his doctrine, let him boast of his tongue, let him hear the appraiser of this matron's true stones. Let him hear, I say, a certain craftsman, inspector of the adornment. Why does he boast his tongues, now no longer a precious but a cheap stone? *If I speak with the tongues of men*, he says, *and of angels, but have not love, I have become like sounding brass, or a clanging cymbal*[^12]. Where is that stone? It no longer shines, but clangs. Therefore learn to appraise stones, merchants of the kingdom of heaven. Let no stone please you except the adornment of this woman. She who is more precious than precious stones is herself the price of her own adornment.

**4** *The heart of her husband trusts in her*[^13]. Plainly he trusts, and he taught us to trust. For he commended the Church to the ends of the earth, through all the nations, from sea to sea. If she did not persevere to the end, the heart of her husband would not trust in her. The heart of her husband trusts in her. Foreknowing, he trusts; he who trusts cannot be deceived. It was not said: The heart of her sons trusts in her. For her sons, being little ones, could be deceived. The heart of the one trusts in her whose heart no lie deceives. It is true, then, what he trusts. *She who is such will not lack spoils*[^14]. Not because she does not seek spoils does she therefore not lack them, but because she will abound with many. *She who is such will not lack spoils*. Whence she despoils the world, spread everywhere; she snatches trophies on every side from the devil. For this her husband too promised, to whom she says in another psalm: *I exult in your words, like one who has found many spoils*[^15]. How does she lack spoils, who snatches on every side, drags on every side, acquires on every side?

**5** *For she works for her husband good things and not evil, in all time*[^16]. Hence it is that this woman despoils the nations, working for her husband good things and not evil. In all time she works good and not evil. Not for herself, but for her husband, *that the one who lives may now live not for himself, but for the one who died for all and rose again*[^17]. For her husband, then, she works good; before God she works good. Him she serves, to him she is devoted, him she loves, to please him she always strives. She does not adorn herself, neither for her own eyes nor for others' eyes. She is not of those who please themselves, not of those who seek their own: *For she works for her husband. But those who work for themselves all seek their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^18].

**6** *Finding wool and flax, she made what is useful with her hands*[^19]. The holy discourse describes this matron as a worker in wool and a worker in flax. But it is asked by us what wool is, what flax is. Wool I think to be something carnal, flax something spiritual. This I dare to conjecture from the order of our garments. For linen garments are inner, woolen garments outer. Whatever we work in the flesh is in the open; whatever in the spirit, in secret. But to work in the flesh and not work in the spirit, although it may seem good, is not useful. But to work in the spirit and not work in the flesh is the way of the slothful. You find a man stretching out alms to a poor man with his hand, yet not thinking of God there, but wishing to please men. He can seem a woolen garment, he does not have the inner linen. You find another saying to you: "It is enough for me to worship God in conscience, to adore God. What need is there for me either to go to church or to mingle visibly with Christians?" He wants to have the linen without the tunic. This woman does not know, nor commend, such works. Spiritual things, indeed, must be spoken and taught without carnal things, but those who receive them ought both to hold to spiritual things and not to do carnal things carnally. *This woman found wool and flax, and made what is useful with her hands*. This wool and this flax are in the holy Scriptures. Many find them, but do not want to make anything useful with their hands. She found and made. When you hear, you find; when you live well, you make. *Finding wool and flax, she made what is useful with her hands*. See her to whom it is said: *Stretch out to the right and to the left; for your seed will inherit the nations; there is no reason for you to spare, stretch your cords farther*[^20]. See her here: *She has become like a ship that trades; from afar she gathers riches for herself*[^21]. The riches of this woman are the praises of her husband. See how from afar she gathers riches for herself: *From the rising of the sun to its setting, praise the name of the Lord*[^22].

**7** *She rises from the nights, and gave food to her household, and works to her maidservants*[^23]. *She rises from the nights*. What are the nights worth? They do not press her down, they do not force her to lie in darkness. And *she rises from the nights*. The nights are tribulations. But who is this who both rises from the nights and advances in tribulations? And *she gave food* to her household: in the nights she offered herself to be imitated. By doing she taught what she said must be done, and then she gave food to her household in the nights. Who eats by night? Surely even then she gave food. For those to whom she gave it always hunger. *For blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they will be satisfied*[^24]. *From the night my spirit watches toward you, O God*[^25]. *At midnight I rose to confess to you*[^26]. These nocturnal nourishments abound in the house of this woman. No one there suffers hunger. Nor does one grope to find what may be eaten: the lamp of prophecy burns. But must one only eat and be idle? For she who *gave food to her household, gave also works to her maidservants*. Are these maidservants her own, or her husband's? Or, because they are her husband's, are they hers too? Or is she herself the many maidservants? For she, though mistress of the household, yet does not disdain to be a maidservant. Let her attend to her price, let her love her Lord. Let her acknowledge, I say, that she is a maidservant, and not fear her condition. For he does not disdain to make a wife of the one he bought at so great a price. And every good wife calls her husband her lord. Indeed she not only calls him so, but savors this, sounds this, bears this in her heart, professes this with her mouth, reckons the marriage tablets the deeds of her purchase. She is, then, a maidservant, giving works to maidservants. She is a maidservant: for she is the mother of the one who says: *I am your servant and the son of your maidservant*[^27].

**8** You were going to ask what she does in those works, even the nocturnal ones. Hear what she does: *Looking ahead, she bought a field*[^28]. *Looking ahead*, not in the present but in the future, *she bought* this *field*, looking ahead by faith and hope. Hence *she also rises from the nights*. *For if we hope for what we do not see, we await it with patience*[^29]. Suffering tribulation in all things, she looks ahead to the field she buys. Hence she is called a valiant woman. What are those nights compared to that field? *For our present temporal slight tribulation*—when we rise from the nights—*works for us in an incredible measure an eternal weight of glory*—the heart toward that field—*while we look not to the things that are seen, but to the things that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporal; but the things that are not seen are eternal*[^30]. What is that field? What is its beauty? Let us burn to possess it. Do we not suppose it is the very one of which God said: *And the beauty of the field is with me*[^31]?

**9** *Looking ahead, she bought a field*[^32]. Where she bought it, there she has the field. Where the field? Whence did she buy it? Where she also placed her treasure, that it might be for her: *Where your treasure is, there is your heart also*[^33]. *Looking ahead, she bought a field*. Whence did she buy it? Lest perhaps you hope and sigh and do nothing, that field does not love a slothful lover. There, surely, when you have come to it, perhaps you will rest, and there will be no need to labor. For that field is not such as this one where Adam ate his bread in the sweat of his brow. Yet now, that you may come to the beauty of that field, procure for yourself the means with which to procure it. Work there. How? Gather the price. For this is what this woman does. See whether it is left unsaid. When it had been said, *Looking ahead, she bought a field*, as though you said, "Whence did she buy it?"—*and from the fruits of her hands she planted a possession*[^34]. These were the very works she gave to her maidservants, that from the fruits of her hands she might plant a possession forever. For he called it that possession which is to come. He insinuates this by the word *looking ahead*.

**10** *Girded strongly about her loins, she strengthened her arms*[^35]. Truly valiant. See whether she is not a maidservant. How devotedly she serves, how readily. Lest the flowing folds of carnal desires hinder her at work, she girds her loins, that she may tread on nothing superfluous while she hastens in the work. For there is the chastity of this woman, bound by the belt of the precept, and always ready for every good work. *Girded strongly about her loins, she strengthened her arms*, not to fail. Whence this? *She tasted that it is good to work*[^36]. Where is the palate by which this is tasted? Men flee labor as if bitter. By fearing to taste, they do not know what is bitter. A good conscience does a good work. And what is sweeter, brothers, than a good conscience? If it is not present, and an evil one pricks, all things are bitter. Taste, then, taste, and you will see how it savors, how it will delight you, how afterward you will not cease until you consume it all. *She tasted that it is good to work*.

**11** *Her lamp is not extinguished all night*[^37]. *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel*[^38]. *You will light my lamp, O Lord*[^39]. Her lamp is her hope. Toward it every man works, whatever good he does for the sake of hope. And in the night this lamp burns. For if we hope for what we do not see, therefore it is night. But if we both do not see and do not hope, it is both night and the lamp does not burn. What is more unhappy than such darkness? But that we may not fail in the darkness, and may await with patience what unseen we hope for, let our lamp burn all night. For he who daily speaks the word to us pours in as it were oil, lest the lamp be extinguished.

**12** *She stretches out her hands to useful things*[^40]. How far does she stretch these hands? *From sea to sea, and from the river*, where she began, *to the ends of the earth*, where she arrived. Whence it was not in vain said: *Stretch out to the right and to the left*[^41]. *She stretches out her hands*, but *to useful things*.

**13** *Her arms too she strengthened upon the spindle*[^42]. "Upon the spindle [in fusum]"—not from "pouring in" [infundendo], but upon that instrument of wool-working which is called a spindle. About this spindle let me say what the Lord grants. For these wool-workings are not foreign to men. Hear what it is: *She strengthened her arms upon the spindle*. She could have said: upon the distaff. She said spindle, perhaps not in vain. Although it can seem, and not absurdly be understood, that by the spindle wool-working is signified, by wool-working a good work, as of a chaste woman and a matron diligent and unwearied. Yet I, dearly beloved, will not be silent about what I understand in this spindle. Everyone who lives in good works in the holy Church, not a neglecter but a doer of the precepts of God, does not know what he will do tomorrow; he knows what he has done today. He fears about future work, he rejoices about past work. And that he may persevere in good works he keeps watch, lest perhaps, neglectful of future things, he lose the past. Yet in praying to the Lord, in all his supplication, he has a firm conscience not about future work but about past, from what he has done, not from what he is going to do. Now, then, if you see with me that this is true, attend in wool-working to these two instruments: the distaff and the spindle. On the distaff the wool is wound, which, to be drawn out and spun into thread, passes over to the spindle. What is wound on the distaff is future; what is gathered on the spindle is now past. Your work, then, is on the spindle, not on the distaff. For on the distaff is what you are going to do; on the spindle, what you have done. See, then, whether you have something on the spindle, let your arms be strengthened there. There will your conscience be strong, there, secure, you will say to God: "Give, because I have given; forgive, because I have forgiven; do, because I have done." For you do not ask the reward except for the work done, not for the work to be done. Whatever, then, you work, let your whole spirit be toward the spindle. Because what hangs on the distaff too must be passed over to the spindle, but what is gathered on the spindle must not be called back to the distaff. Therefore see what you do, that you may have it on the spindle, that you may strengthen your arms upon the spindle, that the whole may strive toward the spindle, that the spindle may have something to console you, to confirm you, to give you confidence in begging and in hoping for the promises.

**14** "And what shall I do?" perhaps you say, "what do you bid me have on the spindle?" Hear what follows: *But her hands she opened to the poor*[^43]. Come now, we are not ashamed to teach you holy wool-working. See, if anyone has a full purse, a full barn, a full storehouse—all these are on the distaff; let them pass over to the spindle. See how she spins—rather, see how she may spin [neiat]—provided all be instructed, let the grammarians not be feared. *But her hands she opened to the poor, and stretched out her fruit to the needy*[^44]. Hands to the poor, fruit to the needy. There is a certain poor man who seeks your hands. There is a certain needy man who seeks your fruit. He who truly wants from you only what will profit his need is a poor man seeking your hands. But there is another needy one who says: *As having nothing, and possessing all things*[^45]. Not as wishing his need to be satisfied from your gift, but, as in the Lord's tree which he planted and watered, he seeks fruit. Hear him himself saying to certain people, when he was speaking of such things: Not because I seek a gift, but I require fruit[^46].

**15** *Her husband is not anxious about the things that are in the house, when he tarries somewhere else*[^47]. *Her husband is not anxious about the things that are in the house, because the Lord knows who are his*[^48]. How should he be anxious, when *those whom he predestined, them he also called; whom he called, them he also justified; whom he justified, them he also glorified? If God is for us, who is against us?*[^49] *Her husband is not anxious*: he knows his own, his own know him. *When he tarries somewhere else*. Where does he tarry, except whence he is to come? He tarries there, as though he delays there. For many now long for his coming, and their longing is deferred, until the number of the members of this matron is fulfilled. But many abuse his delay for their own impiety. And the evil servant says: *My Lord tarries*. And he begins to beat his fellow servants, and to be drunk with the evil. *His Lord will come on the day he does not know, and at the hour he is unaware of, and will divide him*[^50]. For it is the body of ministers and those set over others that gives food to the fellow servants in time. *He will divide him*, it says. It has good men, it has evil men; he separates the good from the evil. *And he will set his portion with the hypocrites*[^51]. Not the whole ministry, because there are there also those who long for the coming of the Lord. There are there also some of that number of whom it is said: *Blessed is that servant whom, when his Lord comes, he will find so doing*[^52]. Therefore he will come, and will divide him.

**16** Now meanwhile he tarries somewhere else, but he is not anxious what is done in the house. *For all with her are clothed*[^53]. Will he be anxious about the nakedness of his servants, when he tarries somewhere else, having such a wife? They are clothed, and most excellently. Do you want to know how excellently? *As many of you as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ*[^54]. All, absolutely, with her are clothed. Both the evil servants and the good servants are clothed. But the good servants are clothed who have put on Christ, not only in the form of the sacrament, but also in the work of example, following the footsteps of their Lord. But others only as far as the sacrament, about to render an account for their garment. Yet that woman does not cease, that woman does not cease to clothe all, that no one may complain, no one say: "I did not work well, because I am not clothed." See, then, how you ought to be clothed. For our garments let us also do work. *For all with her are clothed*.

**17** What for her husband? She who clothes the servants, does she work nothing for her husband? *She made double cloaks for her husband*[^55]. She works. *She made double cloaks for her husband*. Now you acclaim. I believe you recognize what the double cloaks are which the Church makes for her husband Christ. The cloaks she makes for him are praises: praises of faith, praises of confession, praises of preaching. Why double cloaks for Christ? You praise God, you praise man. Praise doubly, and praise singly: doubly, because he is both man and God; singly, that you not be feigned. Some woman or other in the school of a certain Photinus—a stone as it were precious, shaken out from the adornment of this woman, now cheap and abject, whence the Photinian heretics are named—chose to make a single cloak for her husband. He does not accept it, who surely takes double cloaks from his wife, truly his, as we read written. For that man said Christ was man only. Again there appeared some other detestable woman, as if she too were weaving a cloak for the husband, but weaving ragged little fables. For she says: "Christ is only God, having nothing at all of man." This the Manicheans say. The Photinians, man only; the Manicheans, God only. The former confess nothing divine in the Lord. The latter, as it were, the whole divine, and yet so false that not even the human. For if he was not man, then he did not die, then he was not crucified, then neither did he rise. For he could rise who died. Therefore even to the doubting disciple he showed false scars. For without doubt those scars were false, if true wounds did not precede. But if true wounds preceded, true flesh. If true flesh, true death, true cross, true man, and the whole truth: praise abounding from the web of this woman. But those who in praiseworthy fashion feared these double cloaks, remained double by a lie. *She made double cloaks for her husband*. Plainly she made double cloaks. We confess God, we confess man. Praise God in the man, the man in God. She wove that most precious cloak of praise: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God*[^56]. She wove also another cloak, on account of his daily converse among men: *The Word became flesh and dwelt among us*[^57]. *She made double cloaks for her husband*.

**18** *Of fine linen and purple she made garments for herself*[^58]. She made garments for herself too. For it did not befit the matron of so great a husband either to go naked or in rags. *She made garments for herself of fine linen and purple*. Of fine linen, by white confession; of purple, by glorious passion. Her fine linen we recognize when we pray; her purple we praised this morning in the martyrs.

**19** *Her husband becomes conspicuous in the gates*[^59]. He who tarries somewhere else, he who on account of such a wife is not anxious about his house, he whom now no one sees as he tarries somewhere else, *becomes conspicuous in the gates*. Attend to when. See what follows: *When he sits in council with the elders of the earth*[^60]. Nothing more evident. Read another prophecy: *He will come to judgment with the elders of his people*[^61]. In that council—that is, in that judgment, the saints judging with him, to whom it was said, *You will sit on twelve seats judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^62]—he will be conspicuous. For the Son of man will come, as he said, *in his majesty, and all the angels with him*[^63]. There all the angels: both the angels of the heavens, and the angels who announce the word of God. For a prophet too was called an angel. For an angel is a messenger. And: *Behold, I send my angel before your face*[^64], was said of John. And: *You received me as an angel of God*[^65], the Apostle says. He, then, he who now tarries somewhere else, of whom many say, "When is he going to come, or who is going to come?" *will be conspicuous in the gates*—that is, in the open, in the manifest. *In the gates he will be conspicuous*. But he admits some, against others he shuts them. *Her husband will be conspicuous in the gates, when he sits in council with the elders of the earth*. Until this happens, let her meanwhile do what she was doing. Let her work, let her not cease. Let her await him, who is to be conspicuous in the gates; let her not tremble at the holy council of God's judgment. Let her come with a good conscience, let her come glorious, because they too are his members and his sons who are to judge with her husband.

**20** *She made fine sheets and sold them*[^66]. It is good that she made fine sheets. Why did she *sell* them? Except that she does not seek a gift, but requires fruit. Understand first, brothers, this selling as free. And does anyone buy for free? If he takes for free, he does not buy. If he buys, he gives a price. If he gives a price, he does not take for free. And where is it: *You who thirst, go to the water, buy for yourselves without silver*[^67]? When you buy, you do not give silver, and yet you buy. If you buy, you give something, yet you do not give silver, you give yourself. For refer the fine sheets to those linen things—they are spiritual—which this woman makes and preaches through all lands. And perhaps it is to be called selling, because the Apostle said: *If we have sown for you spiritual things, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal things?*[^68] For there is that reckoning of giving and receiving. For in every selling there is the reckoning of giving and receiving. But the Apostle is saddened against certain market-places, where he did not sell fine sheets. *No church*, he says, *shared with me in the reckoning of giving and receiving*[^69]. But he who sells thus does not seek a gift, but requires fruit, lest you think him as it were a seller of the Gospel. He is indeed a merchant of his Lord, but he seeks a greater price. For he plainly sells, he gives spiritual things. And what does he seek? Perhaps carnal things? These too are indeed owed, but the Apostle did not seek them, saying: *For I do not seek your things, but you yourselves*[^70]. Give, then, the price, give yourselves. For did not Joseph in Egypt too sell grain, and yet make the buyers themselves the king's servants? Wishing to live in that famine, they received grain and wept, made servants. Do we fear to be made servants? Woe to us, if we are not his servants. What does it profit us to refuse such a Lord? We will both be under the devil, and suffer famine, and not escape the power of the true Lord. Give yourself, and buy for yourself a sheet—that is, a spiritual covering. Just as you yourself are also the price of a certain bread. For what? Because you give yourself to pleasure, do you not, for the concupiscence of the flesh, as if to buy a harlot, give yourself as the price? How great a thing it is to give yourself to God, to buy for yourself the living bread that came down from heaven, with that same price which is yourself? For the price of a harlot is as much as one loaf of bread. *She made fine sheets and sold them*.

**21** *And girdles to the Canaanites*[^71]. She made girdles for the Canaanites. Let them gird themselves, work, come, be servants of this house, that all may be clothed, all be fed. For she made girdles, surely for work, for she too, doing work, girded her loins strongly. Who are the Canaanites? Neighboring nations, foreigners to the people of Israel. *You who once were far off have been made near in the blood of Christ. You who were once strangers to the Testaments, and not having the hope of the promise, and without God in this world*[^72], *but now citizens of the saints and members of the household of God*[^73], having received girdles, work in the Lord's house, now made members of God's household from Canaanites—whence too was that woman just now recited in the Gospel. She was a Canaanite, she did not dare approach the table of the children, but, like a dog, sought the crumbs. See how she girded herself for work. Her girdle was faith. This he praises: *O woman, great is your faith!*[^74]

**22** Let us see the rest. *She is clothed with strength and comeliness*[^75]. With comeliness, as with fine linen; with strength, as with purple. For because she is strong, she is therefore bloody in passion. *And she rejoiced in the last days*[^76]. *In the last days she rejoiced*; therefore here she was long in tribulation. For whence would she have purple garments without tribulation?

**23** *She opened her mouth attentively*[^77]. May the Lord grant us, set in her, praising her, cleaving to her, awaiting her husband with her and in her, that we too may open our mouth attentively. Not rashly, but attentively, cautiously, carefully. *I was among you in much fear and trembling*[^78], the Apostle said. As if to say, "I opened my mouth attentively": *Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians*[^79]. *She opened her mouth attentively. And she set order to her tongue*[^80], praising the creature as creature, the Creator as Creator, the angels as angels, the heavenly things as heavenly, the earthly things as earthly, men as men, beasts as beasts. Nothing disturbed, nothing disordered. Not taking in vain the name of the Lord her God, not thinking the substance of the creature to be of the Creator, so speaking the whole in order that she does not set lower things before better, nor subject the better to the lower. *She arranged the order of her tongue*. Nothing is more beautiful than this order. Whence she herself says: "*Set love in order within me*[^81]. Do not act in reverse, do not disturb and confound what God has ordered. *Set love in order within me*. Love me as me, love God as God, lest you offend God on my account, lest you offend me on account of any other besides me. *Set love in order within me*." Blessed her daughter, set in this order, whose passion we celebrate today among the rest and whose confession we heard a little before, ordering her tongue: *Honor*, she said, *to Caesar as to Caesar, but fear to God*[^82]. *She*, then, *opened her mouth attentively, and set order to her tongue*.

**24** *Strict are the ways of life of her houses*[^83]. Strict, strong, severe. There is no place there for dissipation, she does not love dissoluteness. *And she does not eat idle bread*[^84]. Deservedly she acquired so much.

**25** Here, then, this woman, laborious, watchful, anxious, severely chastising her household, rising from the nights, gazing at the lamp lest it be extinguished, strong in tribulation, patient though the promises are not yet received, strengthening her arms upon the spindle, not eating idle bread—but after these labors, as it were of poverty and worldly need, what will it be, that in the last days she rejoiced? What will it be, do you want to hear? Hear for what hope our lamp burns all night. Now hear. *Her sons rose up and were enriched*[^85]. Now we live in poverty, in poverty we watch. And when we die, in poverty we sleep. But we shall rise and be enriched. Then her sons will be enriched. *Her sons rose up and were enriched*. Compare now whatever riches of this earth, liable to thieves, to moths. Why do you boast? Because you are weak, therefore many things are necessary for you. You need to be much clothed, because you cannot endure cold; to use beasts of burden, because you cannot walk on foot. These are the props of weakness, not the ornaments of power. What are those riches of the angels? They have one garment of light. It is never worn out, never soiled. Those are the true riches, where there will be no want, no need. Why now do you seek this, before you rise? If you are a son of this woman, attend to when riches are promised you. *Her sons rose up and were enriched*. Prepare yourself to receive the riches of the resurrection. Do not love these, that you may deserve to come to those. *Her sons rose up and were enriched*.

**26** *And her husband praised her*[^86]. We praise her, but not from our own. Her husband himself praised her. When her sons rose up and were enriched, he attended to her, and inspected her, and praised her. Who would not wish to hear how he praised her? If you heard so joyfully when she was praised by us, how would we hear, if we could hear as her husband praised her? He praised her in the resurrection. When we have risen, we shall hear it. Or did he not even now leave that praise unsaid? Here is the praise itself, it follows. Let us hear—so may you live in her, and with her may we hear how her husband praised her, seeing her now with such blessedness of sons, of the rich in the resurrection of the dead.

**27** *Many*, he says, *daughters have wrought power*[^87]. These are the praises by which her husband praises her. *Many daughters have wrought power*. What daughters, to whom she is compared? And she is not compared. *Many daughters have wrought power. But you have surpassed them*. Attend, I beg you: we are now at the end of the reading. For I fear lest I have you wearied there, where I most demand you attentive. Let us hear her praises. *Many daughters have wrought power. But you have surpassed them, and you have set yourself above them all*. *You*, he says, *have surpassed them all, you have set yourself above all*. What, then, are those other daughters who have wrought power, whom she has surpassed, and above whom she has set herself? Or what power did they work, or whence did she surpass them? For there are evil daughters, which are the heresies. Why daughters? Because they too were born of her. But evil daughters, daughters not by likeness of morals, but by likeness of sacraments. They too have our sacraments, they have our Scriptures, they have our Amen and our Alleluia, most of them have our Creed, many have our baptism. Therefore daughters. But of this woman do you want to know what was said somewhere in the Song of Songs? *As a lily among thorns, so is my beloved among the daughters*[^88]. A wondrous saying: he called the same ones both thorns and daughters. And do those thorns work power? They plainly do. Do you not see how the heresies too pray, fast, give alms, praise Christ? I can say there are there false prophets, of whom it was said: *They work many signs and wonders, to deceive, if it can be, even the elect. See, I have foretold you*[^89]. Thorns too work power. Of which power it is said: *Did we not eat and drink in your name, and in your name work many mighty deeds?*[^90] *We ate and drank*—not of any food whatever would he say it. You know of what food or drink he could say it. *And we worked many mighty deeds*. Many daughters work power, we do not deny it. The thorns too have a flower, but they do not have fruit. But this woman, to whom it is said, *But you have surpassed them and set yourself above all*—whence did she surpass them, except that she has not only a flower, but also fruit?

**28** What fruit does she have? Whence did she surpass them? Let it be told me. *I show you*, he says, *a still more excellent way*[^91]. What more excellent way? For thence she surpassed them, thence she set herself above all. *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding brass, or a clanging cymbal*[^92]. He speaks with tongues: that pertains to the power of the flower. *If I know all sacraments and all knowledge, and have all prophecy and all faith, so that I move mountains*—how great power!—*but have not love, I am nothing*[^93]. Hear still other powers, pertaining to the flower, not to the fruit: *If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and hand over my body to burn, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^94]. This more excellent way she has, whence it is said to her: *Many daughters have wrought powers*. Many spoke with tongues, knew all sacraments, worked many mighty deeds, cast out demons, distributed their goods to the poor, handed over their bodies to the fires. They are beneath you because they did not have love. *But you have surpassed them and set yourself above all*, heavy not only with flower, but with fruit, abounding in fruit. See that very cluster, where it begins. When he had enumerated the works of the flesh: *Fornications*, he says, *uncleannesses, luxury, idol-worship, sorceries, enmities, contentions, jealousies, animosities, dissensions, heresies, envyings, carousings, drunkennesses, and the like, which I foretell you, as I have foretold, that those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^95]. When all the thorns to be cast into the fire had been enumerated: *But the fruit of the Spirit is*, he says, *love*[^96]. And from this head, from this as it were root, the rest are woven on: *joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, self-restraint*[^97]. Whence this beautiful cluster? Because it hangs from love. *Many daughters have wrought power. But you have surpassed them and set yourself above all*.

**29** In those others, what remained? *False are favors, and vain the beauty of a woman*[^98]. Why false favors and vain beauty? Because *if I have not love, I have become sounding brass and a clanging cymbal, I am nothing, it profits me nothing*[^99]. *False*, then, *are favors, and vain the beauty of a woman*. For a wise woman will be blessed. She who sought what she might understand, who guarded understanding—she is wise, she will be blessed, not those false beauties, not that vain favor. *A wise woman will be blessed*. *But let her praise the fear of the Lord*[^100]. Let her who will be blessed praise that whence she will be blessed, because she is wise. What should she praise? The fear of the Lord, by which she was led to wisdom. *For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^101]: *But let her praise the fear of the Lord*. Laborious through so many nights, patient amid so many scandals, provident to look ahead, strong to endure, constant to persevere, her labors finished: *Give her of the fruits of her hands*[^102]. She did, she did. She is worthy to receive. *Give her of the fruits of her hands*. Give what? *Come, blessed of my Father. Give her of the fruits of her hands*. Give what? *Receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^103]. See what to give. Of what fruits of her hands? *For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat. Give her of the fruits of her hands*[^104].

**30** And what business will she then have, when her labors are finished? *And let her husband be praised in the gates*[^105]. He himself will be the harbor of our labors—to see God, and to praise God. There it will not be said: "Rise, labor, clothe the servants, clothe and adorn yourself in purple, give food to the household, attend lest the lamp be extinguished, be anxious, rise from the nights, open your hand to the poor, pass over from distaff to spindle." There will be no works of necessity where there is no necessity. There will be no works of mercy where there is no misery. You do not break bread for the poor where no one begs. You do not take in a stranger where all live in their own homeland. You do not visit the sick where all are perpetually sound. You do not clothe the naked where all are clothed with eternal light. You do not bury the dead where all live without end. Yet, not doing these things, you will not be doing nothing. For you will see the one you longed for, and you will praise him without failing. This is the fruit you will receive. Then that one thing you asked for will be: *One thing I asked of the Lord, this I will seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life*[^106]. And what will you do there? *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord. And let her husband be praised in the gates. Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^107].
