# Sermon 374: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1.** The yearly celebration of this day requires of us a yearly sermon, owed to your ears and to your hearts. Today our Savior drew the Magi to himself from so distant a nation. They came to adore the infant, the Word of God. Why did they come? Because they saw an unusual star. And how did they recognize that it was Christ's star? They could indeed see the star; but could it speak to them and say, I am the star of Christ? Without doubt it was made known in another way, by some revelation. Still, a king had been born in an unusual manner, one who was to be adored even by foreigners. Had not kings been born before in Judea, or throughout the whole earth among the various nations? Why was this one to be adored—and adored by foreigners—not with a terrifying army, but in the poverty of the flesh, with the majesty of his power hidden? When he was born, he was adored by the Israelite shepherds, to whom the angels announced him; but the Magi were not of the nation of Israel. They worshiped the gods of the Gentiles, that is, demons, by whose deceptive power they were deceived. They saw, then, a certain unusual star, and they marveled: without doubt they asked whose sign it was, this thing so new and unaccustomed that they saw. And surely they heard—surely from angels, by some admonition of revelation. Perhaps you ask: From good angels, or from evil ones? Even the evil angels, that is, the demons, confessed that Christ is the Son of God. But why should they not have heard it from good angels too, since in the adoring of Christ their salvation was now being sought, and iniquity was not holding sway? The angels, then, could have said to them: The star you have seen is Christ's; go and adore him where he has been born, and at the same time make known of what sort and how great is the one who has been born. And they, having heard this, came and adored. They offered gold, frankincense, and myrrh, according to their custom; for such things they were accustomed to offer to their gods.

**2.** But before they did this, before they found him in the city where he had been born, they came asking, *Where is he who has been born king of the Jews?*[^1] Could they not have learned this too by revelation, just as they had learned that the star belonged to the king of the Jews? Could not the same star have led them to that city, just as it later led them to the place where Christ was, an infant with his mother? It surely could have; yet it was not done—so that they might inquire this of the Jews. Why did God will this to be inquired of the Jews? So that, while they point out the one in whom they do not believe, they might be condemned by their own testimony. Notice that even now the Magi become the firstfruits of the Gentiles: the greater the impiety from which they are freed, the greater the glory they give to the one who frees them. They ask, *Where is he who has been born king of the Jews?*[^2] Herod, hearing the name of king, trembles like a rival. He summons those learned in the Law and asks them, according to the Scriptures, to point out where Christ was to be born. They answer, *In Bethlehem of Judea*[^3]. The Magi went on and adored; the Jews remained behind, the very ones who had pointed the way. O great mystery! Today, by the books of the Jews, we win our argument; they become believers through their own books. We show the pagans what they refuse to believe. For sometimes the pagans put this sort of question to us, when they see the things written down being so fulfilled that they cannot deny at all that the things foretold and read aloud in the sacred books are presented through the name of Christ among all the nations—in the faith of kings, in the overthrow of idols, in the changing of human affairs; and sometimes they dare to say: You saw these things happen, and you wrote them down as if they had been foretold. A certain poet of theirs did this: those who have read it recognize it. He related that someone went down to the lower world and came into the region of the blessed, and that the future princes of the Romans were shown to him—princes whom the very man who wrote this already knew to have been born. For he narrated things past, but wrote them down as though they had been foretold as things to come. So you too, the pagans say to us, saw all these things happen, and wrote yourselves books in which they are read as though foretold. O glory of our King! Rightly were the Jews conquered by the Romans, yet not destroyed. All the nations subdued by the Romans passed over into Roman law; this nation was both conquered and remained in its own law, and, as regards the worship of God, kept its ancestral customs and rite. Even with its temple overthrown and its former priesthood extinguished, as was foretold by the prophets, they still keep circumcision and a certain custom by which they are distinguished from the other nations. For the sake of what, if not for the sake of testimony to the truth? The Jews are scattered everywhere, carrying the books in which Christ is foretold, and is presented just as he was foretold, so that he may now be shown to the pagans. I bring forth the book, I read the prophet, I show that the prophecy has been fulfilled. The pagan doubts, lest perhaps I have invented this myself. My enemy holds this book, handed down to him of old by his forefathers. From it I convict them both: the Jew, because I have come to know what was prophesied there and fulfilled; the pagan, because I did not invent it.

**3.** Therefore let the demons not seduce, with a show of divination, the unwary and those who are wrongly curious about temporal things; and let them not, deceiving the impious with proud arrogance, demand for themselves the honor of sacrifices. The disciples of the one truly divine man truly foretold divine things. True sacrifice is owed to the one true God. The figures of this, before grace, were foreshadowed by victims. What was to come about in one way, divine providence foretold in many ways, so as to show how great it was. He is himself the physician, he himself the medicine: the physician, because he is the Word; the medicine, because *the Word was made flesh*[^4]. He is himself the priest, he himself the sacrifice. He it is who changed the way of the Magi; he even now changes the life of the wicked. His manifestation in the flesh—which in Greek is called Epiphany—the Gentiles, justified in the spirit, celebrate together on today's festival, so that the festival may renew our memory, that devotion may keep piety vigorous, that gathering together may make love fervent, and that truth may shine out upon the envious.

[^1]: Matt 2:2
[^2]: Matt 2:2
[^3]: Matt 2:5
[^4]: John 1:14
