# Sermon 378: On the Day of Pentecost

**1.** A festival is pleasing to God where piety flourishes and love is fervent. For this is itself the effect of the presence of the Holy Spirit, which the Apostle teaches, saying: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^1]. So the coming of the Holy Spirit filled a hundred and twenty men set in one place. When the *Acts of the Apostles* was read, we heard: A hundred and twenty had been gathered into one, holding the promise of Christ. For he had told them to be in the city until they were clothed with power from on high. *For I*, he says, *will send my promise upon you*[^2]. A faithful promiser, a kind giver. What he promised while he was on earth, he ascended into heaven and sent. We have the pledge of the future eternal life and of the kingdom of heaven. Did he not cheat us in his recent promise, and will he cheat us in our future expectation? When all men contract some business among themselves, and are released by the pledge of a money matter, they generally receive or give a deposit; and a deposit given gives assurance that that very thing too will follow, of which the deposit went ahead. Christ gave us a deposit, the Holy Spirit; and he who could not deceive us nonetheless made us secure when he gave the deposit—a thing which, even if he did not give it, he would without doubt furnish what he had promised. What did he promise? Eternal life, of which he gave the deposit, the Holy Spirit. Eternal life is the possession of those who dwell at home; the deposit is the consolation of those on pilgrimage. For it is better called a deposit than a pledge. For these two seem like each other, but yet they have a certain difference not to be neglected. Both when a pledge is given and when a deposit is given, it is done so that what is promised may be fulfilled; but when a pledge is given, a man gives back what he received, the matter being completed for which he received the pledge; whereas when a deposit is given, it is not taken back, but added to, so that the sum may be completed. We have, then, the deposit: let us thirst for the very fountain from which the deposit comes. We have as a deposit a certain sprinkling of the Holy Spirit in our hearts: if anyone feels this dew, let him desire the fountain. For why do we have a deposit, except that we may not faint with hunger and thirst on this pilgrimage? For we hunger and thirst, if indeed we recognize ourselves to be on pilgrimage. He who is on pilgrimage, and knows that he is on pilgrimage, desires his homeland; and while he desires it, the pilgrimage is irksome. If he loves the pilgrimage, he forgets the homeland, and does not want to return. Such is not our homeland, that we should set anything before it. For sometimes men, while on pilgrimage, become rich. Those who were in want in their own homeland grow rich on pilgrimage, and refuse to return. We, from our Lord—from the time when he breathed the breath of life into the first man—have all been born as pilgrims. Our homeland is in the heavens, the angels our fellow citizens. From our homeland, to exhort us to return, letters have been sent to us, which are read aloud daily among the peoples. Let the world grow cheap; let him be loved by whom the world was made.

[^1]: Rom 5:5
[^2]: Luke 24:49
