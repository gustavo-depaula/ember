# Sermon 142: Augm.

**1.** &lt;On the burial of catechumens&gt; My lord, father, and brother, because he deigns to, commands me to bring this to the notice of your Holiness. And indeed it is his concern especially, but in that love of which we were speaking we share all things with you, that we may be sharers of Christ. Grief is wont to be somewhat invidious even when pardonable. For who would not forgive one who is grieving and disturbed, if perhaps he speaks invidiously? Yet all of you ought to know, dearly beloved—what many of you, indeed almost all, do know—that, according to the custom and discipline of the Church, the bodies of deceased catechumens ought not to be buried among the bodies of the faithful, where also the sacraments of the faithful are celebrated, nor can it be granted to anyone. Otherwise it will be nothing other than a culpable showing of partiality. For why should it be granted to the richer and not granted to the poor, if there is some solace for the dead there? For the merits of the dead are regarded not in the places of their bodies but in the dispositions of their souls. My brothers, learn to think of these things too, as faithful: for the sake of the sacraments, bodies cannot be placed where they ought not.

**2.** Yet we lament that the catechumen we were speaking of has departed from here, and we grieve over him. And from this we admonish you, brothers, that no one be sure of living tomorrow. Run to grace, change your conduct: let this serve you as an admonition. What was healthier than he, what more vigorous than his body? Suddenly he died. He was in good health, he passed away—and would that he had passed away and not truly died. For what shall I say, my brothers? Am I to soothe a man and say that catechumens too go where the faithful go? Are we to flatter men's griefs to the point of disputing against the Gospel? We cannot, my brothers. The living must run, lest the dead be truly lamented and be truly dead. If people ran for the sacraments of the living as they run for the tombs of the dead, perhaps no one would be reasonably lamented—for even if he were lamented, he would be lamented out of fleshly feeling. For one who has obtained better things is not to be lamented, having left behind the temptations of this world, and nowhere fearful, secure in Christ, not fearing the adversary the devil, not shuddering at any slanderous man.

**3.** For perhaps that Lazarus, whose wounds the dogs licked, was not buried: for God was silent about his burial. Nothing was said of him except that, when he had died, he was carried into the bosom of Abraham. It was not even said that he was buried. For he who, while living and hungering, was despised, perhaps even when dead was cast away unburied. And yet he was carried by the angels into the bosom of Abraham. *But the rich man also died*, he says, *and was buried*[^1]. What profit, perhaps, was even the marble tomb to a soul among the dead, thirsting and not receiving a drop from the tip of a finger?[^2] I do not wish to say more, my brothers; it is enough to have alarmed you this far, lest we increase the grief of certain of our brothers who have been struck by this misfortune. For I ought not to have said even these things, were we not compelled to exhort and admonish you all.

**4.** Consider human frailty, my brothers; run while you live, that you may live; run while you live, lest you truly die. The discipline of Christ is not to be feared. He cries out, *My yoke is easy, and my burden is light*[^3], in this very chapter that we were treating a little before: *Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.* And you dispute against this and say, "I do not yet wish to be one of the faithful"? "I cannot." What is "I cannot," except that the yoke of Christ is harsh and the burden heavy? So your flesh suggests the truth to you, and Christ lies? He says, *It is easy*, and your vanity says, "It is harsh." He says, *It is light*, and your vanity says, "It is heavy." Believe Christ rather, for his yoke is easy and his burden light. Do not be afraid, put your neck under it without fear. The yoke will be the gentler upon your neck, the more faithful your neck is. And so, brothers, let us have said these things and admonished your Charity for two reasons: lest anyone ask for this and be saddened if he does not obtain it, and that each of you, O catechumens, while you live, may take care lest you perish dead, and lest neither your own people nor Mother Church herself find a way in which help can be brought to you.

[^1]: Luke 16:22
[^2]: See Luke 16:24
[^3]: Matt 11:30
