# Sermon 47

**1** The words we have sung contain our profession that we are the sheep of God. Nor do we unfittingly beg with tears the mercy of him whose sheep we are. For we said: *Let us weep before the Lord who made us, for he himself is the Lord our God*[^1]. Lest anyone weeping despair that he can be heard, a certain bond was recalled of God's hearing us: *For he himself is the Lord our God, who made us. He is our God; we are the people of his pasture and the sheep of his hands*[^2]. Human shepherds, or even heads of households, masters of cattle, did not themselves make the sheep they have, did not themselves create the sheep they feed. But the Lord our God, because he is God and creator, made for himself sheep that he might have and feed. Neither did another establish those he himself feeds, nor does another feed those he himself established. Let us, then, weep before him. For we are not in good while we are in this world. For when we please the Lord in the region of the living, then our tears will be wiped away, and we will speak praises to him who has rescued us from the bonds of death, our feet from slipping, our eyes from tears, that we may please the Lord in the region of the living, because it is difficult to please him in the region of the dead. But there is here too whence we may please him, by begging his mercy upon us, by abstaining from sins as much as we can, but as much as we cannot, by confessing and weeping. So we are in this life hoping for another life, weeping in hope—rather, weeping in reality, rejoicing in hope.

**2** Having professed in this song, then, that we are his sheep, the people of his pasture, the sheep of his hands, let us hear what he speaks to us as to his sheep. Before, in the earlier reading, he was speaking to the shepherds. But in the present and today's reading he speaks to the sheep. In those words of his, then, we were hearing with trembling, you with security. What, then, in these today's words? Shall we, in turn, hear with security, you with trembling? Not at all. First, because, even if we are shepherds, the shepherd hears with trembling not only what is said to the shepherds, but also what is said to the sheep. For if he hears with security what is said to the sheep, he has no care for the sheep. Then, we have already said to your Charity that two things are to be considered in us: one, that we are Christians; the other, that we are set over others. In that we are set over others, we are reckoned among the shepherds, if we are good. But in that we are Christians, we too with you are sheep. Whether, then, the Lord speaks to the shepherds, or to the sheep, we ought to hear all things with trembling, nor let anxiety depart from our hearts, that *we may weep before the Lord who made us*.

**3** Let us hear, then, brothers, whence the Lord reproaches the wicked sheep, and what he promises his sheep. *And you*, he says, *my sheep, thus says the Lord God*[^3]. First, how great a happiness it is to be the flock of God, if anyone considers it, brothers, even in these tears and these tribulations he conceives a great joy. For he is not in the flock of one whom wolves can scourge, or whose sleep robbers can catch. For to him it was said: *You who feed Israel*[^4], of whom it was said: *He who keeps Israel will neither slumber nor sleep*[^5]. He, then, watches over us while we watch, watches also while we sleep. If, then, from a human shepherd a man's cattle are secure, how great ought our security to be when God feeds us, not only because he feeds us, but also because he made us?

**4** One anxiety is laid upon us: to hear the voice of the shepherd. And now is the time of hearing, because he has not yet taken up the time of judging. He who speaks now keeps silent. For he speaks in the precept, he keeps silent in the judgment. Therefore he says in a certain place: *I have kept silent; shall I always keep silent?*[^6] How did he keep silent, since he said this very thing by speaking? He who says, *I have kept silent*, does not keep silent, because to say this very thing, *I have kept silent*, is not to keep silent. I hear you, then, speaking in so many precepts, in so many sacraments, in so many pages, in so many books. I hear you finally in this very thing you say: *I have kept silent; shall I always keep silent?* How, then, did you keep silent? Because I do not yet say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^7]. And I do not yet say to others: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^8]. And these very things I do not yet say, in such a way that I already foretell them. The last sentence which the judge is about to pronounce, about to write with his own hand on the tablet, beyond which sentence he is about to judge nothing more, the parties do not hear: while they go outside, it is written. Both parties are anxious and in suspense, against or for which his sentence will come forth. Great is the secret of the judge, whence it is called the "secretarium." Great the fear of those who are in the case; what he is thinking, and what he writes, is unknown. And he is a man, and those of whom he judges are surely men. But he is our God, and we are the people of his pasture and the sheep of his hands. And though he is the Creator, we the creature; he immortal, we mortal; he invisible, we visible; he did not want it to lie hidden from us in this life what last sentence he is about to pronounce at the end. No one who wants to condemn says beforehand, "I condemn"; no one who wants to strike says beforehand, "I strike."

**5** Great, then, is his gentleness, great his mercy, great his meekness. But if only we do not abuse his patience for our own wickedness, and, while he bears our sins, we should heap sin upon sin as if to add to his burden; as if that he might bear more, who does not labor when he bears. Our sins, which he still spares, because he still endures them, show his patience but heap up our burden. *Do you not know*, he says, *that the patience of God leads you to repentance?*[^9] That patience is what he calls keeping-silent, of which he says: *I have kept silent; shall I always keep silent?*[^10] Therefore, when he reproached certain men and said: *You who preach that one must not steal, you steal; you who say one must not commit adultery, you commit adultery*[^11], and the rest, he said: *Or do you despise the riches of his goodness and longsuffering?*[^12] Because he is good, because he is longsuffering, because he sees and keeps silent, because he sees and endures, do you think him unjust? *Do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance?* And see whether he will always keep silent, who now keeps silent. *But you*, he says, *according to your hardness of heart and your impenitent heart, treasure up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works*[^13]. Therefore he keeps silent; will he always keep silent? Likewise he says, after certain sins enumerated: *These things you did, and I kept silent*—that is: these things you did and I did not avenge; *you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you*[^14]. And in truth many think this, when they have done many evils, and have seen no evil befall them; not only do their evil deeds please them, but they even think they please God: impiety goes so far that the impious despiser even thinks God like himself. And when God leads him by warning, teaching, exhorting, reproaching, to his own likeness, he not only does not follow the likeness of God, but wants to lead God to his own likeness. This is a greater iniquity than the very sins from which he does not correct himself. *You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you*. And what follows? *I will convict* you. Why this? *I have kept silent; shall I always keep silent?* And so, brothers, since this discourse, which proceeds from the mouth of God, both terrifies me and you—for we all have one good hope in him, and we all ought equally to fear, lest, having offended him, we not find what we hoped for, but experience what we despised—let us all hear as the sheep of God, while he who keeps silent speaks, while he warns us, and does not yet judge us who made us, while there is leisure to hear, while it is permitted even to read.

**6** *And you*, he says, *my sheep, thus says the Lord God: Behold, I judge between sheep and sheep, and rams and goats*[^15]. What do goats do here in the flock of God? In the same pastures, at the same fountains, the goats too, destined for the left, are mixed with those at the right, and those who will be separated are first tolerated. And here the patience of the sheep is exercised to the likeness of the patience of God. For the separation will be his—of some to the left, of others to the right. But now he himself keeps silent, you want to speak. But whence do I say: You want to speak? Whence he keeps silent. From the vengeance of judgment, not from the word of reproof. He himself does not yet separate, you want to separate. He himself tolerates them mixed, who sowed. If, before the winnowing, you want the wheat to be cleansed, by your own wind, worst of men, you will be winnowed. Was it permitted the servants to say: *Do you want, we will go and gather them up?*[^16] For they were vexed, seeing the weeds, and grieved that weeds were mixed with the good crop. And they said: *Did you not sow good seed? Whence, then, did the weeds appear?*[^17] He rendered the account whence they appeared; yet he did not permit them to be plucked up before the time. Although the servants themselves were vexed against the weeds, yet they sought counsel and command from the master. Those weeds displeased them amid the crop, but the servants saw that if, even in plucking up those very weeds, they did anything of their own accord, they themselves would be reckoned among the weeds. From the Lord they awaited the command, they sought the order of their king: *Do you want, we will go and gather them up?* And he: *No*. And he rendered the reason: *Lest perhaps, when you want to gather the weeds, you root up the wheat together*[^18]. He calmed them from indignation, but did not leave them in grief. For it seemed grievous to the servants that there were weeds amid the wheat, and truly it was grievous. But the condition of the field is one thing, the rest of the barn another. Endure, for to this you were born. Endure, because perhaps you have been endured. If you were always good, have mercy; if you were ever evil, do not lose the memory. And who is always good? More easily, if God examines you diligently, will he find you even now evil, than you will find yourself always good. Therefore these weeds must be endured amid the wheat, the goats among the rams, the kids among the sheep. But what of the wheat? *In the time*, he says, *of harvest, I will say to the reapers: Gather first the weeds, and bind them in bundles to burn them, but my wheat store in the barn*[^19]. The mixing of the field, then, will pass; the separation of the harvest will come. Now the Lord requires of us the patience which he sets forth in himself, saying to you: "I surely, if I now wished to judge, would I judge unjustly? I, if I now wished to judge, could I be deceived? If I, who always judge rightly and who cannot be deceived, defer my judgment; do you, not knowing how you are to be judged, dare to judge so prematurely?" See, brothers, how to those servants wishing to root up the weeds before the time, he did not concede this work even at the harvest. For he said: *In the time of harvest I will say to the reapers*. He did not say: I will say to you. But what if the servants themselves will be the reapers? No. For, expounding all things one by one: *The reapers*, he says, *are angels*[^20]. A man, then, fenced in by flesh, bearing flesh, or perhaps wholly flesh—that is, flesh in body, carnal in mind—do you dare to usurp beforehand another's office, which at the harvest will not even be yours? This about separating the weeds. About the goats, what? *When the Son of man comes, and all the angels with him, he will sit on the throne of his glory, and all the nations will be gathered before him, and he will separate them, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats*[^21]. And he will come, and will separate. The harvest will come, and they will be separated. Now, then, is not the time of separation, but of toleration. Nor do we say these things, brothers, that the diligence of correction may sleep. Rather, lest we come unwary into that judgment, and we negligent blind ones, blind to our own blindness, suddenly find ourselves on the left, let discipline be exercised, let judgment not be rushed.

**7** What, then, does the Lord say? *Behold, I judge between sheep and sheep, and rams and goats*[^22]. *I judge.* Great security: he himself judges, let the good be secure. Their judge no adversary corrupts, no advocate circumvents, no witness deceives. But as much as the good are secure, so much let the evil fear. He is not a judge from whom anything is hidden. For will God, judging, seek witnesses, through whom he may learn who you are? Whence can he be deceived as to who you are, who knew what you were going to be? He questions you, not another about you. *The Lord*, it says, *questions the just and the impious*[^23]. But he questions you, not that he may learn from you, but that he may confound you. Having, then, such a judge, whom no one deceives against us, no one for us, let us so act that we do not fear his coming judgment, but await and desire it. For does the wheat fear to be put into the barn? Rather it longs vehemently and desires it. Do the sheep fear to be set at the right? Rather, nothing is so slow to them as the time until it happens. These indeed, from the soul and with all sincerity, say, when they pray: *Your kingdom come*[^24]. But the heart of the evil man both trembles in these words and his tongue falters. For how do you say: *Your kingdom come?* See, it will come. What sort will it find you? Act so, then, that you pray securely. And if perhaps something is in your conscience of error and sin, you have the medicine in that very prayer: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^25]. For God willed you so to be a debtor that you might have a debtor. By sinning, indeed, you make yourself an enemy to God, but see lest perhaps you have an enemy. Forgive, and it will be forgiven you. What you, who can be found in sin, do, this will he do for you who can be judged for no sin. But if, as a man set in sin, you do not spare your debtor, and do not regard your own condition in him, and do not dread for the rest the lapse of your frailty; what will he do to you, who judges so securely as one who never sins?

**8** Effort, then, must be given to a pure conscience. And if perhaps some scruple is in it, *let us anticipate his face in confession*[^26]. In the psalm now, when it was sung, we heard: *Let us anticipate his face in confession*. Let us anticipate him, lest he himself anticipate us. After confession he will not bring vengeance, if you too after confession do not repeat the iniquity. Anticipate before you are anticipated. For that he will come is certain. You will lose, if you do not desire what is to come. For even with you unwilling he will come. Will you defer him by refusing him to come? Just as he knew the hour at which he had to be judged, so he knows also the hour at which he ought to judge. He will come; do you see what you will be. Today there is a scruple, today let there be confession, today let the scruple be renounced. Today it is forgiven, today it is loosed. There is no reason for you to say: "God defers pardon." Do not you defer your medicine. For you have something in your soul that distresses you. And if it distresses, it also worries you. Surely, if in your house there were some stone hurting your eyes, you would order it taken from the midst, especially if you were about to receive some somewhat greater guest in your house. When, then, you call upon God, you call him into yourself. How will he come to you, for whom you have not cleansed a place to receive him? But are you less fit to take from your heart what you yourself made for yourself? Call upon him to cleanse it; invite him to enter. Provided that you do now what you are going to do, while he speaks by warning and keeps silent by judging.

**9** Here he said goats, he said rams, and he judges between them. And what does he say to them? *Is it not enough for you that you fed on good pastures? And the remnants of your pasture you trampled with your feet, and you drank the water that had settled*—that is, that was pure and tranquil—*and the rest you troubled with your feet: and my sheep fed on the tramplings of your feet, and drank the water troubled by your feet*[^27]. What is this? The pastures of God are good, and the fountains of God are pure. We have these in the holy Scriptures. Who, then, are they who drink from there what is tranquil, and feed from there on what is clean, and trample the remnants and trouble the water so that the other sheep receive trampled grass and drink troubled water? And this surely you see displeases the shepherd, who says, while these things are done: *I judge between sheep and sheep*, surely to this end, that they not be done. There are many who learn tranquilly, but teach in disturbance, and, though they have a patient teacher, rage against the learner. For how tranquilly Scripture itself teaches us, who does not know? Someone comes, then, and reads the precepts of God, reads and grasps them, grasps them tranquilly, drinking from the tranquil, feeding on the green and clean. Someone comes to hear something from him. He is angered, he disturbs, sometimes accusing the slowness of one who understands somewhat late, he makes the one disturbed understand less what he could have heard tranquilly.

**10** Nor would I say this, brothers, because hardness is never to be reproved—the hardness which the very great tranquillity of truth reproves, saying: *O senseless and slow of heart to believe!*[^28]—if these things are done with that love by which we wish to instill care in men, to instill diligence of attention, and perhaps to clear the cloud of his mind, which he has contracted from the cares of the world, and perhaps, by thinking of other useless things, he cannot hear what is useful. Then, even if anyone sees slowness in himself, not without cause is he accused, that he may ask God and loose the slowness, may grant the truth. For either, if by our negligence we understand less what we heard, surely the negligence is to be reproved. Or if it is slowness, when it has been accused, there will be whence God may be asked. Such teachers, then, are not to be reproved, but those who do this with a bitter mind, an envious mind, who themselves trample the pastures and trouble the fountains. Whatever they perhaps know, they want to know in such a way that others do not know it. Men of a malicious mind, full of hellish zeal, livid not in body but in heart, they have read and understood. When they are questioned: "It is too much for you, shall I believe these things to you? And are you worthy to read or hear these things?" Why do you trouble the water? The fountain flows for both. Why do you trample the common grass? You did not rain that it might grow.

**11** There is another thing in these words, which can not absurdly be understood. There are men who think it suffices them, in living well, to have a conscience, and do not much care what another thinks of them, not knowing that when a man sees a man of good conscience living rather negligently, joining himself anywhere to anyone and in any place, having the knowledge that an idol is nothing, and yet reclining in the idol-temple—his conscience, being weak, is built up not to the things he searches out, but to the things he suspects. For neither can a man your equal, your brother, enter into your conscience, which God knows. Your conscience is before God, your conduct before your brother. If, suspecting something evil of you, he, disturbed, is built up to do something which he thinks you do, when you live so, what does it profit that the belly of your conscience drew pure water, and he, from your negligence, has his conduct troubled?

**12** And you hear such men, when they are reproved that they not do these things, answer us and say: "The Apostle said: *If I wished to please men, I would not be the servant of Christ*[^29]." And here you trouble the water, you trample the pastures. Attend more carefully, lest you trouble the water for yourself too, to what the Apostle said: *If I wished to please men, I would not be the servant of Christ*. I receive it most excellently, I gladly acknowledge the apostolic sentence. But have you not read another thing in the Apostle? *Please all in all things, as I too please all in all things, not seeking what is profitable to me, but to many, that they may be saved*[^30]. Again, have you not heard the same Apostle? *Be without offense to Jews and Greeks, and to the Church of God*[^31]. A third time, have you not heard the same Apostle? *For we provide good things, not only before God, but also before men*[^32]. He says, then: "Expound to me, then, how I am to understand things diverse and contrary, the Apostle saying here: *If I wished to please men, I would not be the servant of Christ*; saying here: *Please all in all things, as I too please all in all things*; saying here: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^33]; saying here: *We provide good things not only before God, but also before men*." If you hear tranquilly, if you do not trouble for yourself the water of your own mind, as far as I am able, I will perhaps expound it. There are men who are rash judges, detractors, whisperers, murmurers, seeking to suspect what they do not see, seeking even to boast of what they do not even suspect: against such, what remains, except the testimony of our conscience? For neither, brothers, even in those whom we want to please, do we seek our own glory, or ought we to seek our own glory, but their salvation, that, if we walk well, by following us they may not stray. Let them be imitators of us, if we are of Christ; but if we are not of Christ, let them be imitators of Christ. For he himself feeds his flock, and with all who feed well he alone is, because all are in him. We do not, then, seek our own benefit when we want to please men, but we rejoice to please them in what is good, for the sake of their benefit, not for the sake of our own dignity. But against whom the Apostle said: *If I wished to please men, I would not be the servant of Christ*, is manifest. And for whose sake he said: *Please all in all things, as I too please all in all things*, is manifest. Both are clear, both tranquil, both pure, both not troubled. Do you only feed and drink, do not trample and trouble.

**13** For you have surely heard the Lord Jesus Christ himself, the master of the Apostles: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^34]—that is, who made you such. *For we are the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hands*[^35]. Let him, then, be praised who made you good if you are good, not you who through yourself could be nothing but evil. Why do you want to lead the truth into the contrary, so that, when you do something good, you want yourself to be praised; when you do something evil, you want the Lord to be blamed? For surely he who said, *Let your works shine before men*, himself said in the same discourse: *Do not do your justice before men*[^36]. But as these things seemed contrary to you in the Apostle, so also in the Gospel. But if you do not trouble the water of your heart, here too you will recognize the peace of the Scriptures, and you too will have peace with them. But if you refuse to have peace with them, you commit your own quarrel within yourself; they will not lose their peace. For on account of those who commend themselves by boasting to men, and so air their good works that they set the end of their good works in the praise of men, and reckon that same praise of men as, so to speak, the reward of their good works, of these it is said: *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward*[^37]. Against them it is said: *Beware of doing your justice before men*[^38]. Accordingly it follows: *to be seen by them*. He did not extend his meaning further, here he made the end. Do not so do whatever good you do before men, that you may be seen by them, that this very thing be the end of your good work, to be seen by them. Do not, then, do it so, that you may be seen by them. But here he does not set the end, that we may be seen by men, before whom he wants our good deeds to be, but he says: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good works*. He did not rest, nor remain here, but led you up from there and bore you away from yourself—for you would fall, if you were in yourself—and set you where you would be safe. *Let them see*, he says, *your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^39]. Do not be angry that he is glorified. Be with him, and in him you will be glorified. *That no flesh*, the Apostle says, *may glory before him*[^40]. Shall we, then, remain without glory? No. For he himself says: *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^41]. For even the testimony of our conscience is glory to us so, because it is in him. For if our glory is such that we please ourselves and become pleasing to ourselves, very foolish is the man who pleases himself.

**14** Let us take care, then, brothers, not only to live well, but also to conduct ourselves well before men, nor only to care to have a good conscience, but, as far as our weakness can, as far as the vigilance of human frailty allows, let us take care to do nothing even that may come into an evil suspicion in a weak brother, lest perhaps, by chewing pure grass and drinking pure waters, we trample the pastures of God, and the weak sheep eat what is trampled, and drink what is troubled. And woe, on account of him who says: *I judge between sheep and sheep*[^42].

**15** *For these reasons thus says the Lord God to them: Behold, I judge between the strong sheep and the weak sheep*[^43]. Let him say something else. We have already heard of those who trample the grass and trouble the waters. Let us hear another kind of evil, and a great kind of evil. Afterward he makes no mention of goats. Once he named them, that we might know they exist. For he himself knows them well. Afterward he speaks as though all were sheep. First he spoke as he himself sees, but afterward as we see. For that the goats are within, and that at the end they will be separated, let it be known to the sheep: now, as it were, I distinguish between sheep and sheep. He alone knows sheep and goats by predestination and foreknowledge, who was able to predestine, who to foreknow. Now, because all are under the sign of Christ and all approach the grace of God, you think yourself a sheep, while God perhaps knows you a goat. But as a sheep hear what you hear: *Behold, I judge between the strong sheep and the weak sheep*.

**16** *Because with your sides and shoulders you pushed, and with your horns you struck, and everything that was failing you pressed down, until you scattered them outside*[^44]. Who would not understand this? Who would not shudder? If there are no sheep outside, it has not happened. But if we lament that many sheep stray outside, woe to those by whose shoulders and sides and horns it was done. For they would not do these things, except strong sheep. Who are the strong? Those presuming on their own strength. Who are the strong? Those glorying in their own justice. They did not divide the sheep, did not send them outside, except those who said they were just. Bold with their shoulders to push, because they do not bear the burden of God; evil sides, friends conspiring, the fellowship of obstinacy; horns raised, pride lifted up. Push with your sides and shoulders, winnow with your horns, send outside what you did not buy. Surely the whole cause is this, that you are just and others unjust, and it was unworthy that the just should be with the unjust—unworthy, that is to say, that there should be wheat among weeds, unworthy that the sheep should feed among goats, until the shepherd came who in separating would not err. So are you the angel rooting up the weeds? I would not acknowledge you as the angel rooting up the weeds, not even if the harvest had already come. Before the harvest, you—rather, whoever it be—is not the true one. He who designated the reapers designated also the time. Men could call themselves angels too. We perhaps find in the Scriptures even men called angels, but yet I attend to the time of harvest. You can impose on yourself the name of angel; you cannot shorten the time of harvest. And so you speak falsely that you are it, because the time when you may be it has not yet come. Accordingly, when it comes, and the true reapers have been sent, I do not know where they will find you—whether to be cleansed, you who would be stored in the barn, or to be bound, you who would be cast into the fire. For I say "perhaps" for this reason, because I do not dare to judge. Now I grieve over you outside. Whether you are to be inside, I do not know.

**17** Meanwhile hear, from another testimony of Scripture, what was written of you while you live, and do not wish to root up the weeds when it is not the time, but do you yourself return within while it is time. Another Scripture of God says: *The evil son calls himself just*[^45]. These are your shoulders and sides and horns. Badly strong, how much better you would be weak. Badly strong, but not healthy. Badly strong, the frenzied man strikes even the physician. You say you are perfect, that you may bring about a defect. How much rather, how much more usefully you would be weak, that he might perfect you who knows the imperfect. The apostle Paul, the vessel of election, lest he be lifted up in the revelations—which we would not dare to say, unless we believed him saying it—*In the greatness*, he says, *of the revelations, lest I be lifted up, there was given me a thorn of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me*[^46]. Lest he raise his horns, he said he was buffeted. *For which*, he says, *I asked the Lord three times, that he would take it from me. And he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness*[^47]. How much more useful, then, is the weakness that is perfected, than that firmness which pushes the sheep, which winnows to shut them out. *You, then, are the evil son, you call yourself just. The evil son calls himself just, but he does not wash his outgoing*[^48]. Attend, my brothers, to a sentence brief in number of words, but huge in weight of truth. He calls himself just, that he may go out and shut out. He calls himself just, but he is evil: therefore he does not wash his outgoing. What is "he does not wash"? He does not cleanse it, does not defend it, does not excuse it. For why did you separate yourself? Why did you go out? Why does your heart tremble when you hear from the divine books: *They went out from us, but they were not of us*[^49]—if indeed that evil strength, by which you push and dash and winnow the sheep of God, permits a trembling to your heart? For surely when you hear, *They went out from us, but they were not of us*, he was speaking who was in the Church. The Church is spread throughout the world; what do you do outside? For I do not announce the Church spread throughout the world. The Prophets announced it, the Apostles announced it, the Lord himself announced the Church spread throughout the world. Now, when the psalm was read, we heard: *The Lord does not reject his people*—as if it were asked why—*because in his hand*, it says, *are the ends of the earth*[^50]. He does not reject, and you push. You push, you winnow, you shut out. You cast at men the name of betrayers, you do not prove it. These are the horns of one winnowing, not the gentleness of one feeding. See, the people of God in the ends of the earth. See, the people of God, groaning and weeping before God who made them, says in the psalm to the very Lord before whom it weeps: *From the ends of the earth I cried to you, when my heart was in anguish*[^51]. See how it humbles itself in the anguish of heart. And what does it say was granted it? On the rock you exalted me. On the rock, Christ, you exalted me, you did not cast me down from the mountain, Donatus. Go now, and raise your horns, stretch out your sides, puff up your shoulders and push the sheep, and say: "I am just." The Scripture will answer you: Evil, not just. *The evil son calls himself just.* If you are just, why do you go outside? Why do you cast outside? What do you do outside with those you cast out? Did you flee the goats as a sheep? Better you be separated by the shepherd from them to the right, than be confounded with them to the left. They were goats, you a sheep, you would feed with the goats. How had the pastures, how had the fountains offended you? Last of all, how had the shepherd himself offended you, who for a time mixed both flocks, who, even though it is right for him to do so whenever he pleases, yet reserved the separation to the end? For he, even if he now separated, would not err. He defers to the end, you separate before the time. You do not await the end, not knowing when your end may be. Whence this, except that even those whom you accused as goats, you falsely accused? For if you had accused truly, you would not have separated yourself. Your separation is their purification. They were weeds—why did you want to separate them before the time? Mixed with them, you would be wheat, and you would be rooted in the same field, watered by the same rain. Why, then, did you go out? Do you find a cause? Those you accuse you do not convict. But by going out before the time and separating yourself, you yourself are convicted. See that you are an evil son. You call yourself just, but you do not wash your outgoing. I do not say to you: You are rather a betrayer. But if I should say it, I easily prove it. But I refuse to say it for this reason, because your people did it, not you. I do not impute to you the deeds of others, even of your own people. I attend to your deed. That you are outside, I reprove; your outgoing I reprove. I altogether remove all the things that can be said against you. I pass over your drunkennesses, your interest and usury upon usury. I pass over the bands and frenzies of the Circumcellions. I pass over all these, and whatever else I cannot enumerate. Not all among you perhaps do these things. Let him who does not do these things there, he to whom these things displease which are done there, let him approach, let him speak. I do not charge him with another's crime; let him wash his outgoing. You see that it is rightly said to him: *The evil son calls himself just*. For the Lord says it, who speaks the truth: *The evil son calls himself just*. Not I, but he. But does he want me to call him just? Let him come, let him bear good fruits in catholic peace, let him keep them in catholic peace, because there is no fruit where there is not toleration. *And they will bear fruit*, he says, *with toleration*[^52]. Do you want to see how you have been hailed upon? Hear from another place: *Woe to those who have lost toleration*[^53].

**18** Now imagine someone thinking—for it often happens—where the Christian is. He was moved to be a Christian, he attends to the human race running together to the name of Christ. With no temporal advantage set before him he wants to be a Christian, not to gain a greater friend, not to attain a desired wife, not to escape some distress of this world—although many even entering thus are corrected once entered. But let us make someone thinking of his soul, and wanting to be a Christian. He attends to where he sees two parties; he seeks the causes why these separated themselves from those. They answer him: "We separated ourselves as just men from sinners," as though speaking to a blind man who hears what they say, but does not see what they do. If, then, observing their custom, and the things I recalled a little before, he says to them: "I beg you, you say you are just, and therefore you contend that you rightly separated. Why are there among you such and such men?" And they perhaps, because they do not dare to deny it, because the things said are cast before the eyes: "There are indeed among us such men, but are we all such?" "Excellent. I see you, then, outside with sinners. Why not inside? For the reward of your separation ought to have been not to live with the sinner. If you were so outside that you did not have those whom you pretend to have fled, I would somehow bear your separation." Let this man, then, desiring to be a Christian, attend to where the Christian is. He sees those separated as it were from sinners, full of sinners. Again let him attend to the Church of Christ according to the probable life of the morals of the human race, according to which morals even he himself, coming from the world, can somehow judge. Let him see here too some sober, some drunken, some patient poor, some seeking to plunder others' goods, and other such things. He sees here, he sees there too. Let him now attend to God, what he says of his Church. He finds the Lord saying his Church is among all the nations. He finds God saying, even in that parable of the weeds: *The field is this world*[^54]. The field is not Africa, but this world. Through the whole world wheat, through the whole world weeds—yet *the field is the world, the sower is the Son of man, the reapers are the angels*[^55], not the leaders of the Circumcellions—both grow until the harvest, the weeds do not grow and the wheat decrease, but both grow until the harvest. What harvest? Hear him: *The harvest is the end of the world*[^56]. He hears these things plainly, and judging rightly what does he say? "I will not be in that cutting. I will be here, and I will be good in the name of him whose I will be. And I will be good, not making myself good, but awaiting to be made good by him, not calling myself good and just, but desiring to be called so by him." He enters, he becomes a catholic. See, he himself washes his entrance; do you too wash your outgoing. You cannot: *For the evil son calls himself just, but he does not wash his outgoing*.

**19** *With your sides and shoulders you pushed, and with your horns you struck, and everything that was failing you pressed down, until you scattered them outside. And I will save my sheep*[^57]. As their iniquity and cruelty is to be detested, so the mercy of our shepherd, truly our God, is to be praised: he will save his sheep. Perhaps, my brothers, though through his least servants, perhaps through the unworthy he does this, when we say this: May he save his sheep, let them hear the voice of their shepherd and follow him. Let them not seek the Church from the mouth of men. From the mouth of God let them seek it, from the mouth of Christ let them seek it. Whom he calls impious is impious, whom he calls just is just, whom he calls a sheep is a sheep, whom he calls a goat is a goat. He himself is the truth, let him speak, from him let the Church be sought. Tell us, Lord, where is your Church? And he to all: "Do you know where I am?" Let all answer: "In heaven at the right hand of the Father." "Whole is the faith: this I taught, this I sowed, but I sowed it through the world. When you confess me in heaven, surely that psalm comes to your mind: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God*[^58]. Do you seek the Church? Read what follows: *And over all the earth your glory*." There, brothers, where it was said: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God*, of Christ rising and ascending, there at once it follows: *And over all the earth your glory*[^59]. The bridegroom is in heaven, the bride is on earth. He above all the heavens, she over all the earth. O heretic, you believe what in heaven you do not see, you deny what on earth you see! Let him say these things, then, let him say them and be heard: May he save his sheep. *And I will save*, he says, *my sheep, and they will no longer be a prey, and I will judge between sheep and sheep*[^60].

**20** *And I will raise up over them one shepherd*[^61]. Did he not himself in the earlier reading say: *I will feed them*? Now he who feeds raises up one shepherd. Or did he perhaps, within so small an interval of the reading, grow weary of feeding, and raise up a shepherd to whom to entrust the care of the sheep, that he himself might be secure? Let us hear what shepherd he means; and there we understand why he himself, even with this shepherd raised up, feeds, and feeds alone. *I will raise up over them one shepherd, and my servant David will feed them, he himself will feed them*[^62]. That it is a prophecy of Christ coming to men from the seed of David, you quickly understand, brothers, if you know the times. This prophet Ezekiel was of the time of the captivity, which came about by the migration of the people into Babylon. From the time of David to the time of this migration there are fourteen generations. See how long afterward he says: *And David will feed them*. If this were said in the time of Noah, or in the time of Abraham, or in the time of Moses, or at least in the time of Saul himself, whom David the son of Jesse succeeded in the kingdom, we would rightly understand it said of that David the son of Jesse, that he would be the shepherd of the flock of God, to whom, reigning, that people was committed. But now David had already reigned, had already departed from this life, was already laid with his fathers, already rested according to his merit. What is it that he says: I will raise up David and make him for them one shepherd, except that that David is the one who comes from the seed of David? How, then, does God raise up a shepherd for us? Which *one shepherd? And my servant David will feed them*. Long ago he himself fed us. Now his servant David feeds us. Why as another? For surely when he fed, God fed. And when God fed, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit fed. Now he is raised up and becomes as it were another shepherd. But not another. Not another according to the form of God, because in the form of God he himself and the Father are one God. But in the form of a servant he is raised up as another to feed, because the Father is greater. Hear the one feeding, and Christ feeding: *I and the Father are one*[^63]. Hear Christ raised up to feed: *The Father is greater than I*[^64]. One, then, feeds; because *though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^65]. But he is raised up to feed, *because he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^66]. This too the very words here testify: *My servant David will feed them*. A servant, in the form of a servant. A servant, because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man. He humbled himself, made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross*[^67]. Let him, then, be raised up to feed. *For which God*, he says, *exalted him from the dead, and granted him a name which is above every name*[^68]. Now, his servant David raised up, now the form of a servant raised up which he set at his right hand, *he granted him a name which is above every name*. See how he feeds, how widely he feeds: *That in the name of Jesus every knee may bend, of things heavenly, earthly, and infernal*[^69]. Into what narrow party do you, heretical vanity, push the broad possessor? Or do you so trust your proud shoulders and horns that you not only do not gather to the shepherd, but try to shut out the shepherd himself from the flock? *My servant David will feed them.* Hear, sheep, David feeding you. Hear the voice of David your shepherd, not the voice of robbers, not the howling of wolves. *My servant David will feed them. He himself will feed them.* O commendation! *He himself will feed them.* Let no one say he feeds except him: *He himself will feed them.* Whoever wants to feed, let him feed in him, because he himself will feed them. God a little before said: I will feed. Now he says: He himself will feed them. Let the Son answer, and say to us: "Both are truly said. *I and the Father are one*. He who says: *I will feed*, does not lie by saying: He himself will feed; and when he says: He himself will feed, he does not lie by saying: I will feed." *Do you not believe*, he says, *that I am in the Father and the Father in me? Philip, he who sees me sees the Father too*[^70]. Rightly it is said: *I will feed*; rightly it is said: He himself will feed. It is a distinction, not a separation. *He himself will feed them.* Do not, sheep, be dismayed. He who said: He himself will feed them, does not leave you. God feeds, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, God himself feeds. But the form of a servant was to be distinguished, not separated and made foreign and set up in another person. The Creator took up the creature into himself, the Creator was not changed into the creature. He assumed what he was not, he did not lose what he was.

**21** *My servant David will feed them. He himself will feed them, and will be their shepherd, and I the Lord will be their God*[^71]. Attend, brothers. See the unity of the godhead and yet the distribution of the persons, lest we say that he who is the Father is the Son, or that he who is the Son is the Father. See, he said: *He himself will feed them*, who a little before had said: *I will feed them*. *And he will be*, he says, *their shepherd, and I the Lord will be their God*. Expound to us, Lord. Let no one trouble the water; let us drink what flows pure from the pure fountain. For why did you say, as it were separately, *He himself will be their shepherd, I will be their God*, as though he were our shepherd, you our God? Why, Lord, are you not our shepherd, and he not our God? Hear tranquilly, be gentle to hear the word, that you may understand. For perhaps now some ear hears me, holding a divergent view, and rotted by heretical poison, and mocks me for saying the Father and the Son are one God, though he does not mock so many thousands of brothers having one soul. And he says to me: "See, openly God says: *My servant David will be their shepherd*, whom you yourself understood as Christ, nor can it be understood otherwise. For you gave the reason that these things were said when David had already slept. *Christ, then, will be their shepherd; I the Lord*, he says, *will be their God*. He the shepherd, he the God. Do you, then, expound to me, what is: *I will feed*." Who was saying: *I will feed?* Surely God, speaking, said: *I will feed*. As he did not separate Christ by feeding, when he said: *I will feed*, so he did not separate Christ in godhead when he said: I am God. See, Christ is shepherd, the Father too is shepherd. So the Father is God, Christ too is God. As from the shepherd Christ you do not separate the Father, so from God the Father you do not separate Christ. The Father has with the Son the compassion of feeding, the Son has with the Father the equality of godhead. But unless he said it so, you would think that he who is the Son is the Father. Therefore he admonished you both to the unity of the godhead and to the distribution of the persons, that what he says—*He will feed, and I will be their God*—not separating himself from the Son who feeds, nor separating the Son from himself who rules—you may understand in the Father God the Son, and in the Son understand the shepherd the Father. *I*, he says, *the Lord will be their God, and my servant David a prince in their midst*[^72]. Why *in their midst?* Because *the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us*[^73]. *A prince in their midst.* Hence too the *mediator of God and men*[^74], because God with the Father, because man with men. Not a mediator, the man apart from the godhead; not a mediator, God apart from the humanity. See the mediator. Divinity without humanity is not a mediatrix, humanity without divinity is not a mediatrix. But between divinity alone and humanity alone, the mediatrix is the human divinity and divine humanity of Christ. *And my servant David a prince in their midst. I the Lord have spoken*[^75]: not some heretic or other. *I the Lord have spoken.*

**22** *And I will set with them a covenant of peace*[^76]: through him surely who says: *My peace I give you, my peace I leave you*[^77]. This *is the testament of our Father, it is a testament of peace*. Any inheritance whatever may be divided among co-heirs; the inheritance of peace cannot be divided. Our peace is Christ. Peace makes both one, not two of one. *For he himself is our peace*, he said, *who made both one*[^78]. It is the testament of God, the inheritance is peace. Let it be possessed by concordant co-heirs, not divided by quarreling ones. *And I will set with them a covenant of peace.* Watch, heretics, hear from the shepherd the testament of peace, come to peace. You are angry at the Christian Emperors, because they would not let your testaments be valid in your houses. See how worthy is the punishment. And what is it that your testament is not valid in your house? What is it? How much is it? This grief is a warning, not yet a condemnation. For God wished you to have fellow-feeling with the testament of his peace. You grieve over your testament, if your testament does not stand in your house. Surely you are to die, and you will not know what is done in that house afterward. *For on that day*, it says, *all his thoughts will perish, and he will no longer know his place*[^79]. Therefore, though not to know what is done afterward in your house when you are dead, yet you grieve that your testament does not stand in your house. Christ, dead, rose again, looked down from heaven that his testament might stand. Wake up from your grief, be corrected by your torment. To wood badly bent you know heat is usually applied. Let this grief correct you. It is not yet the flame of eternal fire. It is as if the heat of the hearth is brought to your bent heart, that it may be warned and corrected by it. Grieve—you grieve rightly—that your testament does not stand in your house. The house of God is your heart. If you want your testament to be valid in your house, why do you not want the testament of God to be valid in his house? You leave your sons walls, and if you knew your sons would divide them otherwise than you arranged, you grieve. Over one most worthless house, over a roof about to fall, how great care you have, how great anxiety you conceive. How, with burning fevers, with disease pressing, with death itself urging, you resist as much as you can, panting out your last words, to fulfill your testament. How many lawyers you consult, how many frauds, that your testament may stand against the very law of the Emperor, you seek! From close by God answers you: Do not seek frauds, do not hunt for slanderous formulas. Do you want your testament to stand? Let mine stand in you. You grieve that another takes your acquisition, one you did not want. What of my inheritance, so broad, so devout? *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^80]: I said it to my servant, God says to you, and he believed it when he did not see it. You see it, and deny it. See, he kept the testament once made, you tear up the opened one. For then the testament was kept, when it was heard. Then it was opened, when it was fulfilled. Even to your hands the testament has been kept. Surely you want to be an heir. Surely your co-heir does not so contend with you as to say to you: "Take this part, I that one," or: "Take you the lesser, and I the greater"? He does not say: "Let us divide together," but: "Let us have it together." For this is what he willed who made the testament. Open it, and read. And you cry: "That it might not be burned, I did it; that it might not be burned, I kept it." That it might not be burned, did you keep it? Open it, and see that you kept what you would be burned by—although that you kept it, far be it that I should believe, since I see you do not keep what it commands. *And I will set with them a covenant of peace.*

**23** *And I will exterminate the evil beasts from the land*[^81]. The beasts, the enemies of the testament of peace. Of these beasts it is said in another psalm: *Rebuke the beasts of the reed*[^82]. What is *the beasts of the reed?* The beasts hostile to the holy Scripture, because it is written with a reed. *I will exterminate the evil beasts from the land: and they will dwell in the desert in hope*[^83]. What is *in the desert?* In solitude. What is "in solitude"? Within, in the conscience. A great solitude, through which not only no man passes, but no one even sees. There let us dwell in hope, because not yet in reality. For all that is ours outside fluctuates with the storms and temptations of the world. There is an inner desert; there let us question our faith. Let us question whether love is there within. Let us see whether not the lips sound, but also the breast, when we say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^84]. If it sounds true, if we say true where no man sees, there is the desert where we rest in hope. Because all this tribulation passes, and what was hope will become reality, and the whole of ours will be in reality. Because then we will be conspicuous to ourselves, and the thought will not be a sheep as it were hidden. And conscience will not be a desert, because all will be known to themselves and will not have their thoughts unknown, when *the Lord comes, and enlightens the hidden things of darkness; and makes manifest the thoughts of the heart, and then there will be praise to each one from God*[^85]. But now you see two men in tribulation, you cannot see their heart. Perhaps one is bitten by conscience, the other rests in conscience, as in a desert. *And they will dwell in the desert in hope: and they will have sleep*—that is, rest, as it were, with the senses withdrawn from all the noise of the world; within they will rest with themselves—*in the streams*[^86]. In that inner desert itself there are certain streams of memory flowing, divine waters welling up from the mind of one holding and recalling the Scripture. For what you read, what you heard, if you have committed to memory pure and clear and holy, when you begin to rest in that inner desert—that is, in a good conscience—it is filtered from the inner parts of your mind, and there flows in a way the recollection of the word of God, and with the rest you rest in hope and say: "It is true, it is well with me, this is my hope, this God promised me, he does not lie, I am secure." And this security is sleep in the streams. *And they will have sleep in the streams.*

**24** *And I will give them, around my hill, a blessing*[^87]. Though it be a mountain, though it be a hill, around it let it be well with us. The hill itself is Christ. For so he is in our midst, and we are around him. For long ago he had said: *David a prince in their midst*. And because a prince, therefore a hill: gentle, not steep and difficult to one ascending, but only if he set his feet not from on high. *And I will give them, around my hill, a blessing: and I will bring down rain in its season*[^88], the rain of the word of God. For there is an evil rain too, which throws down the house set on sand, against which it is a great thing for the house founded on the rock to resist. For it is the rain of temptation, seeking ruin, not watering the earth. This rain will not be such, which the Lord says he brings down. For what does he say? *They will be rains of blessing*[^89]. The named rain had made you suspicious: they will be rains of blessing, not of temptation. *They will be rains of blessing.*

**25** And see to what that rain advances. *And the trees that are in the field will give their fruit*[^90]. In the field, in a certain plain not steep, in a certain easiness of life. A certain easiness of this life, having in itself nothing steep, laborious, difficult, he called a field. Such is the life of many of the faithful in the Church of God, having wives, children, their own houses. They are, as it were, trees in the field; they were able to ascend nothing steep. But let them receive the rain, these trees too will give their fruit. The fruit of these trees is this: *Break your bread to the hungry, and bring the homeless needy into your house*[^91]. To such trees the Apostle said: *Not that I seek a gift, but I require fruit*[^92]. *And the trees that are in the field will give their fruit*: and if they do not have a greater one, yet they have a certain one of their own. *And the earth will give its produce*[^93]: the whole earth. *And they will dwell in their land.* Now the fields, hills, mountains will give their produce. What can the field do? What can the hill do? What can the mountain do? Let the husbandman alone be acknowledged. *And they will dwell in their land in hope.* You see that he promises the things which at this time he grants us. As long as he says *in hope*, I understand it still of this time. For when we have come to the promised things, there will no longer be hope, but the reality itself will be.

**26** *And they will know that I am the Lord, when I break the forks of their yoke: the forks by which their neck is pressed*[^94]. Lord, break the forks by which the heretics press the necks of the weak. For what is so narrow and pressed by a fork as: "Do not hear Christ, hear me"? Remove the fork, allow me to breathe. I do not know what you say. I hear the voice of my shepherd: *Among all the nations, beginning at Jerusalem*[^95]. Let me follow the shepherd. Why do you press? Take the fork from my neck, that I may take up the gentle yoke of my Lord. He hears this, and presses. Lord, the heretic does not want to lift the fork, do you break the fork. The cross of the Lord lifts up on high, the fork of the heretic presses down. But they will be broken: *when I break the forks of their yoke*. For they want to impose their domination on men, wanting them to be under themselves, not under God. *When I break the forks of their yoke: and I will rescue them from the hand of those who reduced them to slavery*[^96]. What is *reduced them to slavery?* They forced them to sin. *For everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^97]. See what they have persuaded them, brothers, that they say: "They will render an account for us. We are sheep. Where they lead, we follow." Are you a sheep? Hear the shepherd, not the wolf.

**27** *And they will no longer be a prey to the nations*[^98]. For in all the nations they are not lacking, some here, some there. Not those there who are here, but yet they are not lacking, pressing the necks of the faithful with forks, neither here nor there. They disagree among themselves; against unity they all agree. But unity does not disagree with itself, but against all who disagree with it everywhere fights, everywhere labors. But there is rest in the desert. *And they will no longer be a prey to the nations, and the beasts of the earth will no longer eat them*. Hearing the voice of the shepherd, they will be rescued from the teeth of the wolves. Those beasts of the reed will not eat them, who want to turn the Scriptures to their own meaning, turning their ears from the open Scriptures, wanting themselves to be heard, and the Scriptures not to be heard. *And the beasts of the earth will not eat them, and they will dwell in hope*[^99]. See how often he shows that what he now promises, he promises here: he speaks of things which he still displays here. *And there will be no one to terrify them.* How will there be no one to terrify them? There will plainly be no one. *In the Lord I trust*[^100]. Now when a man begins to say: *In God I will praise the discourse, in the Lord I will praise the word*[^101], not in myself—they praise the word in themselves, saying: "Believe what we say to you." We praise the word in the Lord, saying: "Let us believe what is said to us by the Lord." There will be no one to terrify us, because: *In God I will praise the discourse, in the Lord I will praise the word: in God I have hoped, I will not fear what man may do to me*[^102]: *There will be no one to terrify them.*

**28** *And I will raise up for them a plant of peace*[^103]. The testament of peace, the plant of peace. Let what God plants sprout, and let what the heretic sowed be uprooted. What God planted of himself, of his Church; of himself in heaven, of the Church on earth; of himself above all the heavens, of the Church through all lands; this God planted. "But come here, be in the party of Donatus, in Africa alone is the Church": God did not plant it, I do not recognize the plant of God. What you say is to be uprooted, not watered. *And I will raise up for them a plant of peace: and they will no longer be those who are exterminated by famine in the land*[^104]. Truly, brothers, because there is a famine, seek and see how great a famine they suffer. And what is worse, they have food around their mouth but do not eat it; just as the sick often die from disgust, not because what they may eat is lacking, but because they refuse to eat and turn away. For surely the Scriptures speak these things, and here surely and there the psalm sounds: *All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will adore in his sight*[^105]. See, food is set in the little dish. If you were healthy and ate, would you remain there? *And they will no longer be those who are exterminated by famine in the land, and they will no longer bear the reproach of the nations*[^106]. Truly, brothers, to such a height has the Church been raised up in the name of Christ, that now all evil-speakers are confounded, nor dare to speak evil. This alone remained to them, that against us they say: "Why do you not agree among yourselves?" The gentile pagans who remained, having nothing to say against the name of Christ, cast the dissension of Christians at Christians. Therefore whoever pass over from the heretics to the Catholic will not have this reproach of the nations. Nor will they bear the reproach of dissension, because they remain in the root of unity, in the planting of love: *They will not bear the reproach.*

**29** *And they will know that I am the Lord their God, and they my people, the house of Israel, says the Lord God*[^107]. See, there are sheep, see, there is also a vineyard. Just as, when Isaiah spoke of a vineyard, reproaching a certain evil vineyard, lest the vine should say, "I did not understand," he expounded at the end, saying: *But the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel*[^108], lest they say: "It was not said of us, but of some vineyard or other." So here too, when he had spoken of the sheep, at the end, lest anyone say: "Perhaps there are some sheep of God somewhere or other, whom God cares for, and I do not know them"—although it is too absurd for human sense for one to think this, yet that shepherd, having compassion on the weak, descends even to such thoughts, and expounded most openly what his sheep were. *And you my sheep, and the sheep of my flock, are men*[^109]. But what men? All men? No. *Blessed* is the one *whose hope is the Lord God*[^110]. And: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart! Blessed is the man whose God is the Lord himself*[^111].

**30** God is over all. Yet somehow no one easily dares to say "my God," except one who believes in him and who loves him. He himself says: "My God." You made yours to yourself the one whose you are. This he himself loves. Plainly, affected by sweetness toward him, with a secure and confident love, say: "My God." You say it securely, you say it truly, because he is yours, and you have not brought it about that he is not another's. For you do not say "my God" as you say "my horse." For your horse is yours, not another's horse. But God is both yours, and his who says "my God" as you say it. Individuals say "my God," and "my God." He is the God of all, offering himself in common to all to be enjoyed, whole in all, whole in each. For those who say "my God" do not divide him among themselves. If this discourse which I cast out with my tongue, and with a sound consisting of letters and syllables, reaches whole to each, nor do those who hear divide it among themselves; if a discourse sounding bodily to the ears of the body, clearer near, fainter far off, is yet received whole by all who hear, not dividing it among themselves syllable by syllable, but all receiving the whole—how much more is that God, everywhere present, filling all things, not clearer near and fainter far off, but reaching from end to end mightily and ordering all things sweetly, equally possessed by all! This light, my brothers, surely bodily, shines from heaven, rises, sets, goes round, is moved from place to place. Yet the eyes of all go forth in it and are directed. And the eyes of all equally possess it, do not divide it. No rich man has fixed a boundary in it, nor by forestalling to see has shut out or narrowed the eyes of the poor man. Let the poor man say: "My God"; let the rich man say: "My God." That one has less, this one has more, but of silver, not of God. That he might come to God, the rich Zacchaeus gave half his patrimony; that Peter might come, he left his nets and boat; that the widow might come, she gave two mites; that the poorer might come, he held out a cup of cold water; that one utterly poor and needy might come, he bestowed only a good will. They gave diverse things, but came to one, because they did not love diverse things. So you too, men, sheep of God, sheep of the flock of God, do not be disturbed by your temporal diversities, that some are in honor, some without honor, some with money, some without money, some beautiful in body, some less beautiful, some weary with age, some young, some boys, some men, some women. God is equally present to all. He has more place with him who brings more, not of silver, but of faith. *And you*, he says, *my sheep, and the sheep of my flock, are men; and I your God, says the Lord God*[^112]. O blessed are we with such a possession, and such a possessor! For both he possesses us, and we possess him. He possesses us that he may cultivate us, and we possess him that we may worship him. But we worship as God, he cultivates as a field. He cultivates us that we may bear fruit, we cultivate him that we may give fruit. The whole returns to us, he does not need us. *I will give you*, he says, *your inheritance and your possession, the ends of the earth*[^113]: see, we are his possession. *The Lord*, he says, *is the portion of my inheritance, and of my cup*[^114]: see, he is our possession. But yet, by the distinction: *You are men, I the Lord your God, says* the Lord *our God*.
