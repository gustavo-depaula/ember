# Sermon 171

**1.** The Apostle commands us to rejoice, but in the Lord, not in the world. *For whoever wishes to be a friend of this world*, as Scripture says, *will be reckoned an enemy of God*[^1]. But just as a man cannot serve two masters[^2], so no one can rejoice both in the world and in the Lord. These two joys differ much from one another, and are wholly contrary. When one rejoices in the world, one does not rejoice in the Lord; when one rejoices in the Lord, one does not rejoice in the world. Let joy in the Lord conquer, until joy in the world is ended. Let joy in the Lord always increase: let joy in the world always be lessened, until it is ended. These things are not said for this reason, that while we are in this world we ought not to rejoice; but that, even set in this world, we may now rejoice in the Lord. But someone says: I am in the world; surely if I rejoice, I rejoice there where I am. What then? because you are in the world, are you not in the Lord? Hear the same Apostle speaking to the Athenians, and in *the Acts of the Apostles* saying of God and of the Lord our Creator, *In him we live, and move, and are*[^3]. For he who is everywhere, where is he not? Did he not exhort us to this? *The Lord is near, be anxious about nothing*[^4]. It is a great thing that he who ascended above all the heavens is near to those who dwell on earth. Who is this one far off and near, but the one who by mercy was made near to us?

**2.** For the whole human race is that man who lay on the road half-alive, left by robbers, whom the priest and the Levite passing by despised, and to whom the Samaritan, passing by, came near to cure and help. But to relate this, whence did the occasion arise? A certain man, asking what are the best and highest precepts in the law, he advised that there are two: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind; and you shall love your neighbor as yourself*. But the man: *And who is my neighbor*? And the Lord related: A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho. He showed him in a way to be an Israelite. And he fell among robbers. When they had stripped him and inflicted grievous blows on him, they left him on the road half-alive. The priest passed by, surely a neighbor by race, he passed by the one lying there. The Levite passed by, this one too a neighbor by race, he too despised the one lying there. The Samaritan passed by, far off by race, near by mercy, and did what you know[^5]. In which Samaritan the Lord Jesus Christ wished himself to be understood. For "Samaritan" is interpreted "Guardian." Therefore *rising from the dead, he dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^6]; because *he who guards Israel does not sleep, nor slumber*[^7]. Finally, when the Jews blasphemed with such great reproaches, they said to him, *Do we not say truly that you are a Samaritan, and have a demon*[^8]? When, then, two reproachful words were objected to the Lord, and it was said to him, *Do we not say truly that you are a Samaritan, and have a demon*? he could answer: I am neither a Samaritan, nor do I have a demon; but he answered, *I do not have a demon*[^9]. What he answered, he refuted; what he kept silent about, he confirmed. He denied that he had a demon, who knew himself to be the expeller of demons; he did not deny himself to be the guardian of the weak. Therefore *the Lord is near*; because the Lord was made near to us.

**3.** What is so far off, what so remote, as God from men, the immortal from mortals, the just from sinners? Not far in place, but in unlikeness. For we are accustomed to speak thus too, when we say of two men, when their morals are diverse: That one is far from that one. Even if they stand side by side, even if they dwell more closely, even if they are bound by one chain; the devout is far from the ungodly, the innocent far from the guilty, the just far from the unjust. If this is said of two men, what of God and men? Since, then, the immortal and just one was far from us, as from mortals and sinners, he came down to us, that that far-off one might become near to us. And what did he do? Since he himself had two goods, and we two evils; he two goods: righteousness and immortality; we two evils, iniquity and mortality: if he had taken on both our evils, he would have been made our equal, and would need a liberator along with us. What, then, did he do, that he might be near to us? Near—not what we are, but near us. Attend to two things: he is just, he is immortal. In your two evils, one is fault, the other is punishment: the fault is that you are unjust, the punishment is that you are mortal. That he might be near, he took on your punishment, he did not take on your fault: and if he took it on, he took it on to blot it out, not to commit it. Just and immortal, far from the unjust and mortal. A mortal sinner, you were far from the just immortal one. He was not made a sinner, as you are; but he was made mortal, as you are. Remaining just, he was made mortal. By taking on the punishment and not taking on the fault, he blotted out both the fault and the punishment. Therefore *the Lord is near, be anxious about nothing*[^10]. Even though he ascended in body above all the heavens, he did not withdraw in majesty. He is everywhere present, who made all things.

**4.** What is the joy in the world? To rejoice over iniquity, to rejoice over baseness, to rejoice over disgrace, over deformity. Over all these the world rejoices. All of which would not exist, unless men had willed them. Some are things that men do, others things they suffer, and which, even unwilling, they bear. What, then, is this world, and what is the joy of the world? I say, brothers, briefly as I can, as far as God helps; hastily, briefly I say it. The gladness of the world is unpunished wickedness. Let men live luxuriously, fornicate, fool around at the spectacles, glut themselves with drunkenness, befoul themselves with baseness, suffer no evil: and behold the joy of the world. Let those evils which I have mentioned not be chastised by famine, nor by the fear of war, nor by any fear, nor by any disease, nor by any adversities; but let all be in abundance of goods, in peace of the flesh, in the security of an evil mind: behold, see the joy of the world. But God does not think as a man does: God's thought is one thing, man's another. It is of great mercy not to leave wickedness unpunished; and lest he be forced in the end to condemn to hell, now he deigns to chastise with the scourge.

**5.** For do you wish to know how great a punishment no punishment is—yet not to the just man, but to the sinner, for whom there is temporal punishment, lest eternal succeed? Do you wish, then, to know how great a punishment no punishment is? Question the Psalm: *The sinner has provoked the Lord*. He cried out vehemently, he attended, he considered, he cried out: *The sinner has provoked the Lord*. Why, I beseech? what did you see? But the one who cried out this saw the sinner living luxuriously with impunity, doing evil, abounding in goods, and cried out: *The sinner has provoked the Lord*. Why did you say this? For what did you see? *On account of the greatness of his wrath he will not seek it out*[^11]. Understand, Christian brothers, the mercy of God. When he chastises the world, he does not wish to condemn the world. *On account of the greatness of his wrath he does not seek it out*. For this reason he does not seek it out, because he is greatly angered. His wrath is great. By sparing he rages, but justly he rages. For there is severity, as it were a "savage truth." If, then, he sometimes rages by sparing, it is good for us that he come to our aid by chastising. And yet, if we consider the deeds of the human race, what do we suffer? *He has not dealt with us according to our sins*[^12]. For we are sons. Whence do we prove this? The Only-begotten died for us, lest he remain one. He who died one was unwilling to be one. For the only Son of God made many sons of God. He bought himself brothers by his blood, he proved himself by being rejected, redeemed by being sold, honored by being injured, gave life by being killed. Do you doubt that he will give you his goods, who did not disdain to take on your evils? Therefore, brothers, *rejoice in the Lord*, not in the world: that is, rejoice in truth, not in iniquity; rejoice in the hope of eternity, not in the flower of vanity. So rejoice: and wherever, and however long you are here: *The Lord is near, be anxious about nothing*[^13].
