# Sermon 65/A

**1.** Exhorting us to his love, the Lord began from the mention of those persons whom we justly love. *He who loves*, he says, *father or mother more than me is not worthy of me*[^1]. If, then, he is not worthy of Christ who prefers father to Christ, how is he worthy of any trace of Christ who prefers gold to Christ? For there are things that are loved badly in the world, and when they are loved badly in the world they make the lover unclean. Unlawful love is a great defilement of the soul, and a weight pressing down one who desires to fly. For as much as just and holy love snatches the soul to things above, so much does unjust and unclean love sink it to the lowest. To each one his own love is, that he may be carried where he ought, his own weight. For he is not carried where he ought not, but where he ought. But he who loves well will be borne up to what he loves, and where will he be except where is the good that he loves? For with what reward does the Lord Christ exhort us to love him, except that there be fulfilled what he asks of the Father: *I wish that where I am, they too may be with me?*[^2] Do you wish to be where Christ is? Love Christ, and by this weight you are snatched to the place of Christ. Nor does the thing that draws or snatches you upward let you to the lowest. Seek no other machines for going upward; by loving you strive, by loving you are snatched, by loving you arrive. For you strive when you wrestle with unclean love; you are snatched when you conquer; you arrive when you are crowned. *Who will give me wings*, says a certain lover, *like a dove, and I will fly and rest?*[^3] He was still seeking wings, he did not yet have them, and therefore he groaned; he did not yet rejoice, he was still wrestling, he was not yet snatched up.

**2.** The murmurs of unjust loves, then, stand around. On every side they solicit and hold back the one who desires to fly, on every side visible things, as it were, compel themselves to be loved. But let them not compel; let them be understood, and they will be conquered. The world is beautiful, it allures with the variety of its manifold beauty. It cannot be counted how much unlawful love suggests daily. By how simple a love the manifold is overcome! That many loves may be overcome, one love is necessary; one good against all the evil ones. For unity conquers variety, and love conquers cupidity. That man said: *Who will give me wings?*, wishing to have whereby he might fly to rest, because he had no rest even in these goods that are called good in this world, who loved another thing. The lover of his fatherland has even a delightful exile as bitter, among the many things that persuade themselves to be loved. It is a great punishment not to have what you love. But you do not have what you love; you have what you can love, but you do not have what you have now begun to love. What is present that can be loved, if that which is loved is absent? It is a torture of the heart to love and not to have. For example: someone loves his fatherland, has money. For love of his fatherland let him not love money. If he loved money in his pilgrimage, which would abound to the pilgrim, that very money perhaps would hold him back, would hinder his return. Say what you will, it hinders the return. But if that one thing is present, it suffices; the rest are reckoned superfluous, which do not help toward reaching what is loved. Moreover, if it were said to him: Money helps you to be able to return to your fatherland, he would take it up, would attend to it, would seek it, but not for its own sake. The ship helps you, he would seek it, but not for the ship's sake. The sailors help, the helmsman helps, he who loads the provisions helps: these are taken up, these are sought, but not for their own sake. The rest are taken up, the one thing is loved. The rest are taken up for this, that one may arrive at that which is loved.

**3.** Do we think we can say: *One thing I have asked of the Lord?*[^4] Let us now say it, let us say it if we can, let us say it as we can, as much as we can. See how blessed is the heart now using this voice within, where he alone hears to whom it is said—for many say outwardly what they do not have inwardly, because they glory in the face and not in the heart. Let each one see, then, how blessed is the heart that says within, where it knows what it says: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek.* What is it? For he names either one thing or one petition. What is it? *That I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*[^5]. This is the one thing, but how good! Weigh this against many. If you now savor something, if you now seek something from there, if you know how to burn from there with holy desire, weigh this against many, bring forth the balance of justice, put on one side gold, silver, precious stones, honors, dignities, powers, nobilities, the praises of men (when shall I number all?), put on the whole world; see whether there is any comparison, see whether you can put these two things at least to be weighed, the whole world with the Creator of the world.

**4.** What does gold say to me? Love me. But God, what to me? I will use you, and so use you that you do not hold me and separate me from yourself. Whatever else says to me: Love me, is a creature. I love the Creator. Good is what he made, but how much better is he who made it! I do not yet see the beauty of the Creator, but the utmost beauty of creatures. What I do not see I believe, by believing I love, and by loving I see. Let the blandishments of dead things be silent, then, let the voice of gold and silver be silent, let the gleam of gems be silent, let, finally, the enticements of this light be silent, let all be silent. I have a clearer voice which I follow, which moves me more, which rouses me more, which kindles me more closely. I do not hear the din of earthly things. What shall I say? Let gold be silent, let silver be silent, let the rest of this world be silent.

**5.** Let the father say: Love me. Let the mother say: Love me. To these voices I am going to say: Be silent. Is what they demand not just? Do I not repay what I received? The father says: I begot. The mother says: I bore. The father says: I raised. The mother says: I nourished. Perhaps the voices of those saying are just: You wish to be borne on his wings, do not fly as a debtor, repay what we advanced. Let us answer the father and mother saying justly: Love us; let us answer: I love you in Christ, I do not love you instead of Christ. Be with me in him, not I with you without him. But we, they say, do not wish Christ. But I wish Christ more than you do. Do I attend to the begetter and lose the Creator? I answer the father: Led by pleasure you begot me, by goodness he created both me and you. Is he despised because we exist, he who loved us before we existed? Let us say to the mother too: You could conceive, but could you also form? You could be heavy in the womb with my flesh, but could you breathe a soul into the flesh? When you carried me enclosed, you did not know whether I was female or male. Were you ignorant of your burden, as God is not ignorant of his work? You dare to say: Let us not go to him, you who do not hear me saying: Let us go together. I hear more, and I fear and love. Nor did you furnish more than he who created me in you... except that he was created for my sake. For he through whom all things were created, was created among all things for our sake. Do I despise Christ by loving my mother, he who for my sake, being God, willed to have a mother? Perhaps for this reason, wishing to have a mother, that he might have one in whom to teach me to despise, for the sake of the kingdom of heaven, both father and mother.

**6.** Addressing the disciples he says: *And do not call yourselves a father on earth, your one Father is God*[^6]. Then, teaching the prayer, he instructed us to say: *Our Father who are in heaven*[^7]. The father whom I had on earth I have placed in the tomb. I have a Father always in heaven. *Do not*, he says, *call yourselves a father on earth, for your one Father is God.* It would seem hard if he imposed a precept and did not furnish an example. As he was saying certain things about the kingdom of heaven with his disciples, his mother stood outside, was announced as standing outside[^8]. The mother, I say, with his brothers, that is, with his kinsmen, was announced as standing outside. What mother? That mother who conceived by faith, that mother who, remaining a virgin, bore him, that faithful and holy mother was announced as standing outside. So if he, leaving aside what he was saying, had gone forth to her, he would have built up in his heart not a divine but a human affection. Lest you hear your mother calling you back from the kingdom of heaven, he, for the sake of the word of the kingdom of heaven, despised even the good Mary. Is holy Mary, wishing to see Christ, despised, who, hindering him from seeing Christ, is heard as a mother? Let us recall what he then answered when his mother with his brothers, that is, with the kinsmen of his line, was announced as standing outside. What did he answer? *Who is my mother and who are my brothers? And stretching out his hand to his disciples: These*, he said, *are my brothers. Whoever does the will of my Father who sent me, he is to me brother and sister and mother*[^9]. He spurned the synagogue from which he was begotten, he found those whom he regenerated. If those are mother, brother, sister, who do the will of him who sent him, there Mary is his mother.

**7.** *Whoever does the will of him who sent me, he is to me brother and sister and mother.* You have whereby you may be a brother of Christ, love with him the paternal inheritance. You have whereby you may be a sister of Christ, love with him the same inheritance. You have whereby you may be a mother of Christ, if what she conceived in the womb, you conceive in the heart. In names of relationship of this kind, human affection is straitened. In carnal propagation, no one can be the brother and mother of one man. Who is ignorant that he cannot? But love is not straitened. Surely the Church is the spouse of Christ, the Church is the bride of Christ. We know by what mystery the first prophecy of the first man was spoken: *The two shall be in one flesh*[^10]. We know how the Apostle expounds this, saying: *But I say in Christ and in the Church*[^11]. If, then, without doubt the Church is the spouse of Christ, yet in another way, in another sound understanding, the Church can be the mother of Christ. For if of any one woman it could be said: *He who does the will of him who sent me is brother, sister, mother*, how much more can this be said of the whole Church, which daily conceives the members of Christ in the catechumens and brings forth the members of Christ from the unbelievers? *But you*, says the Apostle, *are the body and members of Christ*[^12]. I ask you, O members of Christ: Who bore you? You answer: Mother Church. How is the Church not the mother of Christ, who brings forth the members of Christ? This is the house in which he chose to dwell who asked for one thing[^13]. How would he not despise a spouse, wishing to dwell in the spouse of Christ? How would he not despise a mother, wishing to dwell in the mother of Christ? How would he not despise a father, wishing to have as a father the Father of Christ? Let not the parents be angry. For much is yielded to those before whom God alone is preferred. Or if they do not wish even God to be preferred to them, what do they wish, what do they urge? Let us hear them. I think they do not dare to say to us: Prefer us to God. They do not say it. No one says this, not even a madman. Not even he who says *in his heart: There is no God*[^14] says this to his son. In no way, then, will either father or mother dare to say this, that he be preferred to God. I do not say preferred, but not even compared. What, then, do they say? God told you. What did God tell me? *Honor your father and your mother*[^15]. I acknowledge it, God told me. Do you not be angry when I prefer to you only him who said this. I love, I love altogether, I love you too. But he who taught me to love you is better than you. Only do not draw me against him, and love with me him who taught me to love you, but not more than him.

**8.** *He who loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me*[^16]. But he added: *more than me.* Love, he says, your parents, but not more than me. His bride cries out to you: *Order love in me*[^17]. Love in order, that you may be ordered. Distribute to things their own weights and moments. Love your father and mother, but you have what to love more than father and mother. If you love them more, you will be condemned, and if you do not love them, you will be condemned. Let us yield honor to our parents, but let us prefer to them our Creator, whom we love more, both by fearing and loving and obeying and honoring and believing and desiring. *He who loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me.* So let the parents, not spurned but honored in order, withdraw a little from the midst.

**9.** Someone has a wife; someone has children. They cry out: Love us. Answer: I love you. Say to your wife: If I did not love you, I would not have married you. Say to your children: If I did not love you, I would not have begotten you, would not have raised you. But what is it you wish? Do you wish to call back the martyr proceeding to Christ—not that he may forsake you—and do you begrudge a crown to the one you love? Repay the turn. He loves you, you too love him. Why does he love and you hate? Behold, he denies Christ, he is condemned. See what you have done. Would you wish the one you love to be condemned by an earthly judge? If you would wish it, surely you do not love him. But because you love him, you all the more do not wish him to be condemned by an earthly judge. For if he denies Christ, he is not condemned on earth, but is condemned by him who made heaven and earth. Why do you not dread the one you love being condemned by the greater judge? The earthly judge rages as far as death. This one rages as far as death; that one even beyond death. What, then, do you do, who bring evil upon the one you love? From what do you call back the one you love? That he may have hell and not have a crown. Is this to love! But that you do not wish him to suffer these things for Christ, you do not believe. For if you believed, you would not call him back from suffering, but would wish to suffer with him.

**10.** About children it is easy. The wife presses more: Do not, she says, send me away. You will depart and I will remain a widow. God joined us, let not man separate us. Answer such voices. Let them not break you, let them not corrupt you. They are unjust, they must be examined. Not because he said to you from the Gospel: *What God has joined, let not man separate*[^18], ought you to dread these things, that you be separated from God while you wish to be joined to a wife. If separation is to be feared from a wife, how much more from the Creator? *God joined, let not man separate.* But neither will you be separated from your wife when, for the name of Christ, you go ahead to the crown. The widow whom you forsake has you as an advocate for herself, because you have not forsaken her whom for those things' sake you left. But if, apart from suffering for Christ, you had died as human things go, would it be called a separation? Does the husband die first and no separation come about? Evil woman, this is not to provide for him, but to envy your husband. But you will remain a widow? More blessed if you will remain a widow. But are you anxious lest you be enticed into a second marriage? A second marriage is indeed lawful, but perhaps even from this you will be safe. You blush to marry, the wife of a martyr.

**11.** So let no one love more than Christ father, mother, children, wife. Those very things which are rightly loved, which are piously loved, in which there is sin if they are not loved, let no one love more than Christ, let no one love as Christ. If he loves so, let it be said according to the mode of loving, not according to the measure. What is, that you love so according to the mode of loving, not according to the measure? This is, not carnally, but spiritually. But that you not love so, this is, as much and equally. For it is not only a sin to love someone more than Christ, but it is a sin to love Christ not more than someone. I do not, he says, love them more. Behold, you do not sin. But let me hear another thing. How much do you love? You answer: I love Christ as much as my parents, as much as my spouse, as much as my children. Still you sin. You who would sin by preferring, sin by comparing. Does it seem right to you that Christ be loved by you as much as father, mother, wife? Is it right for you that you equate the creature to the Creator? Is it right? Where is that which cries out: *Order love in me?*[^19] Your father did not die for you, nor your mother, nor your children. If danger befalls you, they wish you to live, but rather to outlive you. If it happen that it be said to a father: Either you will die, or your son, do you think there is found anyone who says: I rather than my son? Do we think such a father, such an old man, who would not rather choose a life even soon to be ended than to be spent for his son? Few days remain to that man, old, decrepit, weary, bent over, and those few days he does not easily wish to spend for the more days of his son. Now, by the weight of old age, he is near burial, and he chooses rather to be bereaved of his desire for the light than to die. What kind of light will it be after his son's funeral? How unpleasant, how mournful, how bitter! Yet the light is loved, the son is buried.

**12.** Christ loved you before you existed; he created you; before the foundation of the world he predestined you; once created, through father and mother he nourished you. For the parents who hand you over did not make you from their own. He loved you, created you, nourished you, handed himself over for you, heard reproaches for you, received wounds for you, redeemed you with his blood. Do you not shudder, and say: *What shall I repay the Lord for all that he has repaid me?*[^20] What will you repay the Lord for all that he has repaid you? Hear him saying: *He who loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me*[^21]. Hear him speaking, fear him threatening, love him promising. What did you repay the Lord for all that he repaid you? Behold, you have now repaid him. What did you repay? Did you save him as he saved you? Did you bring him into eternal life as he brought you? Did you create him as he created you? Did you make him Lord as he made you a man? What did you repay except that you returned to him? If you think truly, you did not give to him, but furnished it to yourself. Nor yet did you have even this from yourself. *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^22] Why do you not find what to repay the Lord? Repay yourself there, render to him what he made. Render yourself, not your things. His creature, not your iniquity.

**13.** So imbued, so instructed, so trained from the law of God, ask one thing of the Lord, seek this[^23]. Nothing will be lacking, *he will not deprive of good things those who walk in innocence*[^24]. But he is not innocent who is harmful to himself. Why do you expect to have mercy on another, who perhaps have not yet learned to have mercy on yourself? *Have mercy on your soul, pleasing God*[^25]. You wish God to please you, you do not wish to please God. But you are such a one whom God cannot please. For then he will please you, if he favors your iniquities! *You suspected*, he says, *iniquity, that I would be like you*[^26]. *Have mercy on your soul, pleasing God.* It is not good that, when you are perverse, God please you. Correct yourself. Do not wish to make God crooked. Please God, and God will please you. Be upright, lest you seek not only yourself but God too perverse. For you sit idle and compose God according to your desires. If God did this! Oh, if he did that! He will do nothing but good. But God displeases the evil. *How good is God to Israel, to the upright in heart*[^27]. Let us ask, then, one thing of the Lord, brothers. When I say it to you, I say it to myself too. Let us all ask this one thing. Let us all hear this from the one. Let us ask one thing of the Lord, let us seek this, to dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of our life. All those days are one eternal day. When you hear: *all the days of my life*, do not fear lest those days come to an end. Those days truly do not come to an end, because not even when they exist do we crave a human day. Not one day stands for us, nor does one day stand for us, they all flee. Before it comes, it departs. Of this day, then, of which we speak, how much has already fled? We do not hold even the hour in which we are. It too flees, another comes, and it too will not stand but will flee. What do you love? Grasp what you love, hold what you love, have what you love. It neither stands nor permits to stand. *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of man like the flower of grass. The grass withered, the flower fell*[^28]. All these flee. Do you wish to stand? *But the word of the Lord abides forever.* So in his Word, which abides forever, stand and hear him, and with him you will abide forever.
