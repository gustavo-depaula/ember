# Sermon 223/D: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** It is known to you, dearest brothers — for you are not ignorant of what you do — that this vigil is dedicated to the Lord, and preferred to all the vigils which are offered for the worship of God, for this reason, that in it the memory of the Savior is renewed by a yearly solemnity, who *was handed over for our trespasses, and rose for our justification*[^1]; so that his whole Church, which is his body, may be able to sing: *But I will exult in the Lord, and rejoice in God my Savior*[^2].

**2.** For for us too, the night of this age being passed, there will be a resurrection of the flesh unto the kingdom, of which in our Head the example went before. For on this account the Lord willed to rise by night, because, according to the Apostle: *God, who said that light should shine out of darkness, has shone in our hearts*[^3]. That light should shine out of darkness, then, the Lord signified by being born at night, and also by rising at night. For light out of darkness is Christ out of the Jews, to whom it was said: *I have likened your mother to the night*[^4]. But in that nation, as in that night, the virgin Mary was not the night, but in a certain way the star of the night; whence too a star marked his birth, which led the distant night, that is, the Magi of the East, to adore the light; so that in them too might come to pass what was said: *Light shines out of darkness.* So that the resurrection and the nativity of Christ might agree too, as in that new tomb no dead one was laid before, none after; so in that virginal womb no mortal was either before or after sown.

**3.** Although, then, in this vigil of ours the Lord is not awaited as though still about to rise, but the memory of his resurrection is renewed by the yearly solemnity, yet in celebrating this we so recall things past that we also figure something, by this same vigil, which we do by living from faith. For all this time, in which this age runs on, in the manner of night, the Church watches with the eyes of faith intent on the holy Scriptures as on nocturnal lights, until the Lord comes. Hence it is that the apostle Peter says: *We have the more sure prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts*[^5]. On account of which the Savior himself too commands us a spiritual vigil, where, having spoken of his sudden coming, *watch*, he says, *because you do not know the day or the hour*[^6]. As, then, now, who in the name of the Lord have myself come to you, I have found you watching in his name, so the Lord himself, in whose honor this solemnity is celebrated, will find his Church watching with the light of the mind, when he comes, that he may also rouse it, sleeping in the tombs in body.

[^1]: Rom 4:25
[^2]: Hab 3:18
[^3]: 2 Cor 4:6
[^4]: See Hos 4:5
[^5]: 2 Pet 1:19
[^6]: Matt 25:13
