# Sermon 340: On the Day of His Ordination

**1.** Always indeed, from the time this burden, of which a difficult account is rendered, was laid on my shoulders, the care of my honor disquiets me: but I am much more moved by the consideration of this weight, when its anniversary day, renewing the former memory, so sets it before my eyes, that, what I already received before, I hold as though I were drawing near to receive it today. But what is dreaded in this office, except that what is perilous in our honor should delight us more than what is fruitful in your salvation? Let me, then, be helped by your prayers, that he may deign to bear my burden with me, who does not disdain to bear me myself. When you pray this, you pray for us too. For this my burden, of which I now speak, what else is it but you? Pray for me strength, as I pray that you be not heavy. For the Lord Jesus would not call his burden his own, unless he carried it with the one carrying it. But you too uphold me, that according to the apostolic precept we may carry one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ. If he does not carry it with us, we sink; if he does not carry us, we sink. Where it terrifies me, that I am for you, there it consoles me, that I am with you. For to you I am a bishop, with you I am a Christian. The former is the name of an office undertaken, the latter of grace; the former is of danger, the latter of salvation. Finally, as on a great sea we are tossed by the tempest of that activity; but recalling by whose blood we have been redeemed, we enter as it were the harbor of security by the tranquility of this thought; and laboring in this our own office, we rest in the common benefit. If, then, it delights me more that I have been bought with you, than that I have been set over you, then, as the Lord commanded, I will be the more abundantly your servant, lest I be ungrateful for the price by which I merited to be your fellow servant.

**2.** For I ought to love the Redeemer, and I know what he said to Peter: *Peter, do you love me? Feed my sheep*[^1]. This once, this again, this a third time. Love was questioned, and labor was commanded: because where love is greater, labor is less. *What shall I render to the Lord for all the things he has rendered to me?*[^2] If I say that I render this, that I feed his sheep, even this I do *not I, but the grace of God with me*[^3]. Where, then, shall I be found a renderer, when everywhere I am gone before? And yet, because we love freely, because we feed his sheep, we seek a reward. How will this be? how does it agree: I love freely so as to feed; and: I demand a reward because I feed? In no way would this be, in no way would a reward be sought from him who is loved freely, unless the reward were he himself who is loved. For if we render this, in return for his redeeming us, because we feed his sheep, in return for the very thing, that he made us to be shepherds, what do we render? For bad shepherds, which far be it from us, we are by our own malice; but good ones, which is present to us from him, we cannot be except by his grace.

**3.** Whence we too, my brothers, command, asking, that you not receive the grace of God in vain. Make our ministry fruitful. *You are God's tillage*[^4]: receive from without the planter and the waterer, but from within the giver of the increase. The restless are to be rebuked, the faint-hearted consoled, the weak upheld, the gainsayers refuted, the plotters guarded against, the unskilled taught, the slothful roused, the contentious checked, the proud restrained, the despairing lifted up, the quarreling pacified, the needy helped, the oppressed freed, the good approved, the bad tolerated, all loved. In this so great and so manifold and varied action of diverse matters help us both by praying and by obeying; that it may delight us not so much to be over you as to profit you.

**4.** For just as it is expedient for us to strive to beseech the mercy of God for your salvation, so it behooves you too to pour out prayers to the Lord for us. Nor let us judge this incongruous, which we know the Apostle did; for he so desired to be commended to God by prayers, that he himself supplicated the whole people, saying: *Pray for us*[^5]. And therefore we ought to say this, which may both exhort ourselves and instruct you. For just as we must think with great fear and anxiety how we may fulfill the office of the priesthood without reproach; so you too must observe to be eager to have humble obedience to all that is commanded you. Let us pray, then, together, most beloved, that my episcopate may profit both me and you; for it will profit me, if I say the things to be done; you, if you do the things heard. For if both we for you and you for us pray without ceasing with the perfect love of charity, we will come happily to eternal blessedness, with the Lord's help. Which may he himself deign to grant, who lives and reigns forever and ever. Amen.

[^1]: John 21:17
[^2]: Ps 116:12 (115:12)
[^3]: 1 Cor 15:10
[^4]: 1 Cor 3:9
[^5]: 1 Thess 5:25
