# Sermon 6: On the Fact That the Lord Appeared to Moses

In the Burning Bush

**1.** When the holy readings were being read, we turned our attention to those things written in the first reading that was recited to us, and we take care to share briefly with your Holiness the things the Lord suggests, lest, receiving the divine mysteries in a fleshly way, you should not only fail to profit but even fail. For there first occurs to our gaze, from that divine reading, that God appeared to Moses. But God deigns to appear through his substance as he is only to pure hearts. For so it is written in the Gospel, *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^1]. But to the bodily eyes of the saints, whenever God wished to appear, he appeared not through himself but through a visible and sensible creature, as far as it can be perceived by this flesh, either through a sounding voice, that is, to the ears, or through fire to the eyes, or through an angel appearing in some visible form, but bearing the person of God. So let us understand, brothers, that God appeared to Moses. For that majesty which made heaven and earth, which rules the whole world, to which the angels always cling, contemplating its beauty with pure minds, could not appear to the mortal eyes of a man unless a visible and sensible creature was assumed, which pertains to these visible eyes of the body, since even the Wisdom of God himself, through whom all things were made, would not appear to human eyes unless he assumed mortal flesh.

**2.** Just as, then, the Word of God, that is, the Son of God, assumed flesh that he might appear to the eyes, so God always, that he might appear to the eyes of people, deigned to appear in some visible creature. For most plainly you have in the Acts of the Apostles that an angel appeared to Moses in the bush. Is this Scripture true, and that one false? Or that one false and this one true? But what? If we are Christians, if we believe well, both are true. If, then, both are true, how did God appear here, how is an angel said to have appeared there, except that that Spirit, who, acting in the Acts of the Apostles, said that an angel appeared, expounded this reading of how God appeared? That declaration is the exposition of this obscurity. Lest you understand that God appeared through himself, there it was expounded to you how God appeared through an angelic creature. But why do you wonder that it is said, when an angel appears, *God said*, and, *God called Moses*, and he approached the place, and, *The Lord said to Moses*? Because it is not the temple, the angel, that is regarded, but the indweller of the angel. For the angel himself was the temple of God. For if he deigns to dwell and speak in a man, so that when a prophet speaks it is said, "God said," how much more through an angel? And when it is said, "God said through Isaiah," what was Isaiah? Was he not a man bearing flesh, born of father and mother as all of us? And yet he speaks, and what do we say of his utterances? "Thus says God." How, then, God, if Isaiah, except that it is God through Isaiah? So here too, the angel speaking, God is said to speak. Why, except that it is God through the angel?

**3.** Attend therefore, the question resolved, also to that as a sign of which it seems to have been done, that he appeared in a bush, and the bush was not consumed, was not set on fire, and appeared as fire, and did not burn the bush. Do we think the bush signifies something good, since they are thorns? Thorns signify sinners. Therefore the bush signified the people of the Jews full of thorns, full of sins. And although the great majesty of God appeared there in the people, the sins were not consumed, just as by this fire the thorns were not burned up. For if the fire had consumed the thorns, it would signify that the word of the Lord that was spoken to the Jews had consumed their sins, and that that Law put an end to their iniquities. The fire is in the bush as the Law is in the Jews; the thorns of the bush are as the sins of the Jews; the fire here did not burn the thorns, as the Law there did not consume the sins.

**4.** But God speaks to Moses (you already know these things, and we ought not to hold you longer, on account of the narrowness of the time): *I am who am; He who is has sent me*[^2]. For when he asked the name of God, this was said: *I am who am. And you shall say to the sons of Israel, He who is has sent me to you*[^3]. What is this? O God, O our Lord, what are you called? "I am called Is," he said. What is, "I am called Is"? Because I abide forever, because I cannot be changed. For those things that are changed are not, because they do not abide. For what is, abides. But what is changed, was something and will be something; yet it is not, because it is changeable. Therefore the unchangeableness of God deigned to intimate itself by this name, *I am who am*.

**5.** What, then, is it that afterward he again told himself another name, when it was said, *And the Lord said to Moses, I am the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob: this is my name forever*[^4]? How is it there, I am called this because I am, and behold here another name, *I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*? Because, just as God is unchangeable, he made all things through mercy and the Son of God himself deigned, by taking up changeable flesh, while remaining what the Word of God is, to come and to come to the aid of man. He clothed himself, then, in mortal flesh, he who is, that it might be said, *I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*.

**6.** Then attend now to the signs, the signs that God gave to Moses. When he said, *If the people say to me, God did not send you, by what signs shall I show that you have sent me?* it was said, *Cast down the rod that you have in your hand. He cast down the rod and it became a serpent, and Moses shuddered. The Lord said again: Take hold of its tail. And it became a rod as it was*[^5]. He gave another sign also: *Put your hand into your bosom. And he put his hand in. Bring it out. He brought it out, and it became* white as snow, that is, *leprous*[^6]. For a white color in human skin is diseased. *Put it again into your bosom*[^7]. He put it in, and it received its color. He gave him a third sign: Take *water from the river, and pour it on level ground*[^8]. He took it, poured it out, and it was turned into blood. *By these signs the people will hear you. If they do not hear you at the first, they will hear at the second or the third*[^9].

**7.** Let us strive, as far as the Lord helps, to declare the things they signify. The rod signifies the kingdom, the serpent mortality. For from the serpent death was administered to man. That very death the Lord deigned to assume. The rod, then, coming to the earth, had the appearance of a serpent, because the kingdom of God, which is Christ Jesus, came to the earth. He was clothed with mortality, which he also fastened to the cross. Now your Holiness knows that, when that people in the wilderness, stiff-necked and proud, murmured against God, it began to be bitten by serpents and to fall by those very bites. In mercy God gave a remedy, which remedy indeed brought present health, but proclaimed future wisdom. He said to Moses, *Hang up a bronze serpent in the middle of the wilderness on wood, and say to the people: Whoever has been struck, let him gaze upon this serpent. And the people who were struck gazed upon the serpent and were healed*[^10]. And the Lord attests in the Gospel by such a sign. For when he was speaking with Nicodemus, he said, *As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of man be lifted up, that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life*[^11]. That is, whoever has been struck by the serpents of sins, let him gaze upon Christ, and he will have health unto the remission of sins. Therefore, brothers, it is mortality itself which was assumed by the Lord, which his body the Church must necessarily have, whose head is the man in heaven. So the Church has mortality, which was inflicted through the persuasion of the serpent. For we owe death to the sin of the first man, but afterward we shall come to eternal life, through Jesus Christ our Lord. But when does he come to life and return to the kingdom? At the end of the world. For he therefore held the tail, where the end is, that he might recall it to its former state.

**8.** What of that hand? It is certain that that hand too signifies the people, the bosom the people. What is the bosom of Moses? The secret of God. When the man was in the secret of God, he was sound and of good color. He went out from the secret of God, Adam departed from paradise, God being offended, and he became diseased. That hand, then, became white; but it returned to the bosom, through the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and received its color. But what of that water? That water signifies wisdom. For often water is set as a likeness of wisdom, and it was said, *It shall become in him a fountain of water springing up unto eternal life*[^12]. But that water, wisdom, which on the earth was made blood, does it not show us the Word made flesh and dwelling in us? Certainly it shows it. All things, then, are signs and mysteries of the future people, concerning our Lord Jesus Christ. And if there are any other sacraments in the old Books, whether we understand them or do not understand them, they want one who seeks, not one who reproves. Let us ask, then, let us seek, and let us knock, that it may be opened to us. To them future sacraments were foretold; we see the present ones in the Church.
