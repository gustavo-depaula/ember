# Sermon 138

**1.** We have heard the Lord Jesus commending to us the office of the good shepherd. In this commendation he surely admonished us, as it is given to understand, to be good shepherds. And yet, lest the multitude of shepherds be understood in a perverse way: *I am*, he says, *the good shepherd*. And whence one is a good shepherd, he consequently shows: *The good shepherd*, he says, *lays down his soul for the sheep. But the hireling, and he who is not a shepherd, sees the wolf coming, and flees: because he has no care for the sheep; for he is a hireling* [^1]. The good shepherd, then, is Christ. What of Peter? Is he not a good shepherd? Did not he too lay down his soul for the sheep? What of Paul? What of the other Apostles? What of the blessed bishop martyrs who followed their times? What too of this holy Cyprian? Are not all good shepherds; not hirelings, of whom it is said: *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward* [^2]? All these, then, are good shepherds, not only because they shed their blood, but because they shed it for the sheep. For they shed it not out of pride, but out of love.

**2.** For even among the heretics, those who on account of their iniquities and errors have endured some troubles, boast in the name of martyrdom, that, whitewashed with this cloak, they may more easily steal, because they are wolves. But if you want to know in what number they are to be held, hear the good shepherd, the apostle Paul; for not all who hand over their bodies even to the fires in suffering are to be reckoned to have shed their blood for the sheep, but rather against the sheep. *If with the tongues*, he says, *of men and of angels I speak, but do not have love, I have become as sounding bronze, or a tinkling cymbal. If I know all sacraments, and have all prophecy, and all faith, so as to move mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing*. A great thing, then, in the end is faith moving mountains. Those things indeed are great; but if I have these without love, he says, not they, but I am nothing. But this has not yet touched those who falsely boast in sufferings with the name of martyrdom. Hear how he touches them, rather how he transfixes them. *If I distribute*, he says, *all my goods to the poor, and hand over my body to be burned*. Now it is they themselves. But see what follows: *But do not have love, it profits me nothing* [^3]. Behold one comes to suffering, behold one comes even to the shedding of blood, comes even to the burning of the body; and yet it profits nothing, because love is lacking. Add love, all things profit; take away love, the rest profit nothing.

**3.** What kind of good is this love, brothers? What more precious? what more luminous? what firmer? what more useful? what more secure? Many are the gifts of God, which yet even the evil have, who will say: *Lord, in your name we prophesied, in your name we cast out demons, in your name we did many mighty works*. Nor will he answer: You did not do them. For in the sight of so great a judge they will not dare to lie, or boast of things they did not do. But because they did not have love, he will answer all of them: *I do not know you* [^4]. But how does he have even a little love, who, even when convicted, does not love unity? Commending this unity to good shepherds, the Lord did not want to call many shepherds. For, as I already said, was Peter not a good shepherd, Paul, the other Apostles, and the later holy bishops, and blessed Cyprian? All these are good shepherds: and yet he did not commend to the good shepherds good shepherds, but the good shepherd. *I am*, he says, *the good shepherd* [^5].

**4.** Let us question the Lord with whatever understanding, and let us converse with so great a householder in most humble debate. What do you say, Lord, good shepherd? For you are the good shepherd, who are the good lamb; the same the shepherd and the pasture, the same the lamb and the lion. What do you say? Let us hear, and help us to understand. *I am*, he says, *the good shepherd*. What of Peter? Either not a shepherd, or an evil one? Let us see whether not a shepherd. *Do you love me?* You said to him, Lord: *Do you love me?* and he answered: *I love you*. And you to him: *Feed my sheep*. You, you, Lord, by your question, by the support of your mouth, made the lover a shepherd. He is a shepherd, then, to whom you entrusted sheep to be fed. You yourself commended them, he is a shepherd. Let us now see whether not a good one. By that very question and his answer we find it. You asked whether he loved you: he answered: *I love you*. You saw the heart, that he answered truly. Is he not good, then, who loves so great a good? Whence that answer brought forth from his inmost being? Whence that Peter, having your eyes as witnesses in his heart, saddened that you questioned him not only once, but again and a third time, that by the threefold confession of love he might blot out the threefold sin of denial; whence then, saddened that he was often questioned by one who knew what he asked, and had granted what he heard; whence, saddened, did he speak such words: *Lord, you know all things, you yourself know that I love you* [^6]? Would he, confessing, rather professing such things, lie? Truly, then, he answered your love, and from his inmost heart brought forth the voice of one loving. But you said: *A good man from the good treasure of his heart brings forth good things* [^7]. Therefore both a shepherd, and a good shepherd; nothing indeed to the power and goodness of the shepherd of shepherds; but yet he too both a shepherd, and good; and the other such men good shepherds.

**5.** What is it, then, that you commend to good shepherds one shepherd, except that in one shepherd you teach unity? And the Lord himself expounds it more openly through our ministry, recalling Your Charity from that very Gospel, and saying: Hear what I commended: *I am the good shepherd*, I said; because all the rest, all the good shepherds, are my members. One head, one body, one Christ. Therefore both the shepherd of shepherds, and the shepherds of the shepherd, and the sheep with the shepherds under the shepherd. What are these, except what the Apostle says: *For as the body is one, and has many members, but all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body; so too Christ* [^8]? Therefore if so too Christ, rightly Christ, having in himself all good shepherds, commends one, saying: *I am the good shepherd*. I am, I am one, with me all in unity are one. He who feeds outside me, feeds against me. He who does not gather with me, scatters. Therefore hear that very unity more vehemently commended: *I have*, he says, *other sheep, which are not of this fold*. For he was speaking first to the fold of the race of the flesh of Israel. But there were others of the race of the faith of that same Israel, and they were still outside, were among the Gentiles, predestined, not yet gathered. These he knew who had predestined them; he knew who had come to redeem them by his blood poured out. He saw them, who did not yet see him: he knew them, who did not yet believe in him. *I have*, he says, *other sheep, which are not of this fold*; because they are not of the race of the flesh of Israel. But yet they will not be outside this fold, because *I must bring them, that there may be one flock and one shepherd* [^9].

**6.** Rightly to this shepherd of shepherds, his beloved, his bride, his beautiful one—but made beautiful by him, before foul with sins, afterward fair by indulgence and grace—she speaks, loving and burning for him, and says to him: *Where do you feed?* And see how, with what affection this spiritual love is here raised up. Far better do those delight in this affection, who have tasted something of the sweetness of this love. They hear this well, who love Christ. For in them this, and of them the Church sings in the Song of Songs: those who love Christ, as foul, and alone fair. *For we saw him*, she says, *and he had no form or comeliness* [^10]. Such he appeared on the cross, such he displayed himself crowned with thorns, foul and without comeliness, as though his power lost; as though not the Son of God. Such he was seen by the blind. For in the person of the Jews Isaiah said this: *We saw him, and he had no form or comeliness*. When it was said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross. He saved others, himself he cannot save* [^11]. And striking with a reed on the head: *Prophesy to us, Christ, who struck you?* Because he had no form or comeliness. Such, Jews, you saw him. Because blindness in part came to pass on Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles entered [^12], until the other sheep came. Because, then, blindness came to pass, therefore you saw the comely one without comeliness. For if you had known, you would never have crucified the Lord of glory [^13]. But you did it, because you did not know. And yet he who bore with you as though foul, for you, the fair one, prayed: *Father*, he says, *forgive them, for they do not know what they do* [^14]. For if he is without comeliness, what is it that she loves who says: *Announce to me, you whom my soul has loved* [^15]? What is it that she loves? What is it that she burns for, what is it that she so fears lest she stray from him? What is it that she so delights in him, to whom it is the only punishment to be without him? What would there be for which he would be loved, unless he were fair? But how would she so love, if he so appeared to her, as to the blind persecutors, and those who do not know what they do? What kind, then, did she love? Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men. *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, grace is poured out on your lips* [^16]. Therefore from your very lips *announce to me, you whom my soul has loved. Announce*, she says, *to me, O you whom*, not my flesh, but *my soul has loved. Announce to me where you feed, where you lie at midday; lest perhaps I become like one covered over the flocks of your companions* [^17].

**7.** It seems obscure, it is obscure: because it is the mystery of the sacred bridal chamber. For she herself says: *The king brought me into his chamber*. This is the secret of such a chamber. But you who are not profane from this chamber, hear what you are, and say with her, if you love with her: but you love with her, if you are in her. Say it all, and yet let one say it, because unity says it: *Announce to me, you whom my soul has loved*. For in them was toward God one soul and one heart [^18]. *Announce to me where you feed, where you lie at midday*. What does midday signify? Great fervor and great splendor. Therefore make known to me who are your wise ones, fervent in spirit, and shining in doctrine. *Make your right hand known to me, and those learned in heart in wisdom* [^19]. To these let me cling in your body, with these let me be joined, with these let me enjoy you. Tell me, then: *Announce to me where you feed, where you lie at midday*; lest I run into those who say one thing of you, feel another of you; believe one thing of you, preach another; and have their own flocks, and are your companions; because they live from your table, and handle the sacraments of your table. For they are called companions, because they eat together, as it were eating together. Such men are reproached in the Psalm: *For if my enemy had spoken great things against me, I would surely have hidden myself from him, and if he who hated me had spoken great things against me, I would surely have hidden myself from him. But you, my likeminded one, my leader, and my acquaintance, who together with me took sweet food, in the house of God we walked with consent* [^20]. Why now against the house of the Lord with dissent, except because they went out from us, but were not of us [^21]? Therefore, O you, *whom my soul has loved*, lest I fall in among such, your companions, but such as were the companions of Samson, not keeping faith with their friend, but wanting to corrupt his wife [^22]. Therefore lest I run into such, *lest I become over them*, that is, fall in among them, *as one covered*, as one lying hidden and obscure, not as one set on a mountain. *Announce*, then, *to me, O you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie at midday*; who are the wise and faithful, in whom you most of all rest: lest perhaps as one lying hidden I run into the flocks, not yours, but of your companions. For you did not say to Peter: Feed your sheep; but: *Feed my sheep* [^23].

**8.** Let the good shepherd, then, beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, answer this his beloved; let him answer her whom he made fair from the sons of men. Hear what he answers, understand; beware what frightens, love what admonishes. What, then, does he answer? How, not blandly, but with blandishments he renders severity. He rebukes, that he may bind, that he may keep. *Unless you know yourself*, he says, *O fair among women*; for however much others be fair with the gifts of your husband, they are heresies; with adornment not with inner parts they are fair, outside and externally they shine, with the name of righteousness they whitewash themselves: *But all the beauty of the king's daughter is within* [^24]. *Unless*, then, *you know yourself*, that you are one, that you are throughout all nations, that you are chaste, that by the perverse conversation of evil companions you ought not to be corrupted. *Unless you know yourself*, that he rightly betrothed you to me to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ; and rightly present yourself to me, lest by evil conversations, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so too your senses be corrupted from my chastity [^25]. *Unless*, then, you so *know yourself, go out, you; go out*. For to others I will say: *Enter into the joy of your Lord* [^26]. To you I will not say: Enter; but: *Go out*; that you may be among those who went out from us. *Go out, you*. But *unless you know yourself*, then *go out*. But if you know yourself, enter. *But if*, however, *you do not know yourself, go out, you, in the footsteps of the flocks, and feed your kids in the tents of the shepherds* [^27]. *Go out in the footsteps*, not of the flock, but *of the flocks; and feed*, not as Peter my sheep, but *your kids; in the tents*, not of the shepherd, but *of the shepherds*; not of unity, but of dissent; not set there, where there is one flock and one shepherd. She was confirmed, was built up, the beloved was made stronger, ready to die for the husband, and to live with the husband.

**9.** These words which we have recalled from the holy Song of Songs, from a certain bridal song of the bridegroom and bride; for they are spiritual nuptials, in which we must live in great chastity; because Christ granted to the Church in spirit, what his mother had in body, that she be both mother and virgin; these words, then, the Donatists take to their perverse sense in a very different way. And as I will not keep silent, so what you should answer them, with the Lord helping, as far as I can, I will briefly admonish. When we have begun to press them with the light of the unity of the Church spread throughout the whole world, and have demanded that they show from the Scriptures some testimony where God foretold that the Church would be in Africa, as though the other nations being lost; they are wont to have this testimony in their mouth, and to say: Africa is in the south; the Church, then, they say, asking the Lord where he feeds, where he lies down; he answers: *At midday*, so that as it were the voice of the one asking is: *Announce to me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down*; and the voice as of the one answering: *At midday*, that is, in Africa. If, then, she who asks is the Church, and the Lord answers where he feeds, in Africa, because in Africa was the Church: she who asks was not in Africa. *Announce*, she says, *to me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down*; and he answers a certain Church apart from Africa: *At midday*, in Africa I lie down, in Africa I feed, as though I do not feed in you. Further, if she who asks is the Church, which no one doubts, which not even they contradict; and they hear something or other about Africa: she, then, who asks is apart from Africa; and because she is the Church, there is a Church apart from Africa.

**10.** Behold I accept that Africa is in the south; although Egypt is more in the south under the sun of midday than Africa. But there in Egypt, how he is a shepherd, those who know it acknowledge; let those who do not know it seek out how great a flock he gathers there, how great a number of holy men and women he has utterly despising the world. That flock has so grown, that even thence it has expelled superstitions. So, to omit how thence it has, by growing, put to flight every superstition of idols, which there was strong; I accept what you say, O evil companions; I utterly accept it, I assent that Africa is in the south, and that Africa is signified in that which was said: *Where do you feed, where do you lie at midday?* But you too likewise attend that these words are still of the bride, not yet of the bridegroom. Still the bride says: *Announce to me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie at midday, lest perhaps I become like one covered* [^28]. Deaf one, blind one, if at midday you see Africa, why in "one covered" do you not understand a woman? *Announce to me*, she says, *you whom my soul has loved*. Surely she addresses a man, when she says: *whom she has loved*. As if she said: Announce to me, you whom my soul has loved (in the feminine); we would understand the bridegroom to speak these things to the bride; so when you hear: *Announce to me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down*; add there too, to her words pertain also what follows, *at midday*. I ask *where you feed at midday, lest perhaps I become like one covered over the flocks of your companions*. I hear utterly, I accept about Africa what you understand; *at midday*, she herself is signified. But the Church of Christ, as you understand, beyond the sea addresses her bridegroom, fearing to fall into the African error. *O you whom my soul has loved, announce to me*, teach me. For I hear at midday, that is, in Africa, there are two parties, rather many divisions. *Announce*, then, *to me where you feed*, which sheep belong to you, which fold you bid me love there, to which I ought to be joined. *Lest perhaps I become like one covered*. For they mock as it were one hidden, they insult as it were one lost, as though existing nowhere else. *Lest*, then, *as one covered*, as one hidden, *I become over the flock*, that is, over the congregations of heretics, *of your companions*, the Donatists, the Maximianists, the Rogatists, and the rest of the plagues gathering outside; and therefore scattering; I ask you, announce to me, if there I should seek my shepherd, that I may not run into the whirlpool of rebaptization. I exhort you, I beseech you by the holiness of such nuptials, love this Church, be in such a Church, be such a Church; love the good shepherd, the husband so beautiful, deceiving no one, desiring no one to perish. Pray also for the scattered sheep; let them too come, let them too acknowledge, let them too love; that there may be one flock and one shepherd. Turned toward the Lord . . .
