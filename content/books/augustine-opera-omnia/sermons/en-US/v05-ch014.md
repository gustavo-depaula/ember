# Sermon 159/A

**1.** The solemnities of the holy martyrs are exhortations to martyrdoms, that it may be a delight to imitate what it is a delight to celebrate. To this the holy Scripture, fresh in our ears, exhorts and in a way kindles us, the Apostle saying, *Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or the sword? As it is written: For on account of you we are put to death all the day, we are reckoned as sheep for the victim*[^1]. Then follow other things that seem violent unto separation, in which, nevertheless, he gives victory, lest we be separated from the one who is not separated from us. For on account of these straits and impending pressures, the faithful promiser and benign giver deigned to promise us that he would be with us *even to the consummation of the age*[^2]. *For I am certain*, he says, *that neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities, nor things present nor things to come, nor power, nor height nor depth, nor any other creature*—for he could not name everything—*will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord*[^3]. And so that he might render us most strong in hope and confidence against all the temptations of this age, lest we be separated from Christ—because our strength in no way suffices if we are forsaken by divine help—he said above, *If God is for us, who is against us*[^4]? The Apostle, having briefly mentioned certain things that seem to assail the Christian faith by raging, lest such things separate us from the love of Christ, armed us to endure all things for Christ.

**2.** But because not only the things that rage try to separate, but also the things that flatter, just as we have been armed by the apostolic words against the things that try to separate us from Christ by raging, so we have been armed by the Lord Christ himself against the things that strive to seduce us by flattering. For it is to be feared lest perchance one whom the cruel sword does not separate, carnal affection may separate. Therefore, against those things too that assail our faith by flattering, lest they take by storm what they assail, let us hear the Lord saying, *If anyone comes to me and does not hate father, mother, wife, children, brothers, and moreover his own soul, he cannot be my disciple. And he who does not carry his cross and follow me cannot be my disciple*[^5]. The Lord mentioned both: what is sweet by enticing, so as to deceive, and what is hard by threatening. For against the flatteries of deception, from carnal affection, he says, *If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife, children*, and the rest; but against those things that, by roaring and terrifying, turn one away from faith, by the one name of the cross he fortifies us, built up and armed. *He who does not*, he says, *carry his cross and follow me*—that is, who does not bear (for this is what "carry" is) all the bitter and hard things in this age, as it were a cross, *and follow me, cannot be my disciple*.

**3.** Now then, let us set the martyr of Christ in the midst, between those who threaten and those who flatter; both doors are knocked at, by which one enters to the heart: desire and fear. At the door of desire knock father, mother, wife, children, brothers: all are alluring, sweet, pleasant. But are they sweeter than God? are they more pleasant than Christ? If you do not believe, *taste and see how sweet the Lord is*[^6]. At the door of fear knock threats, fury, reproaches, and finally the very pains of the body, which no one can fail to feel by despising them. For if he despises money, he suffers losses, not pains. All this lies in the soul of the one despising: he does not torment you who takes from you what you do not love. But you love money: you fear the one who threatens losses, and he does not torment you, but you have tormented yourself, because you have engrafted yourself onto pains. *For the root of evils is greed, which some craving have strayed from the faith and engrafted themselves onto many pains*[^7]. He threatens losses: the one who finds you not greedy finds you free; you have laughed at him as empty-handed, because you did not engraft yourself. So too the other things which by wholesome teaching we learn to despise, we are admonished not to love, that in temptations we may be found free. To which liberty that apostolic reading exhorts us, where it says, *And let those who use this world be as those who do not use it*[^8], that they may have the use of liberty, not the affection of desire. *For the figure of this world passes away. I wish you to be without anxiety*[^9].

**4.** But there remains the infirmity of the flesh, which each of us, as long as he lives in this age, cannot estrange from himself. What do you do? When the flesh is pricked, will the heart not grieve? It is a wound of infirmity, until it takes on the garment of immortality. There the great struggle is. With the will turned away from money, the martyr of God stands secure against those who terrify with losses. "Let him take," he says, "what I do not love—where do I feel it?" He threatens exile, but threatens one by whom only the heavenly homeland is longed for. This Christian, wherever he goes, is a pilgrim. He threatens reproaches. He has something to answer: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^10]. He threatens dishonor. How great is honor that passes away! A man threatens dishonor, but the Lord of men promises eternal honor. All these things being despised, liberty is safe. But what of the flesh? The soul remains with its garment, and with that garment which nothing but death strips off. Now the man—that is, the soul—is pressed from close by, from the infirmity of his own flesh. Therefore the words of the Lord in the magnificent Psalm seem—rather, are understood—to be those of one hanging on the cross, where almost the gospel is recited, where it was said, *They have pierced my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones. But they themselves looked upon me, they divided my garments among themselves, and over my garment they cast lots*[^11]. They are the words of one already suffering, already hanging on the cross, and yet it is said there, *Do not depart from me, for tribulation is near*[^12]. If you understand *near* as "of an approaching time," they will not be the words of one hanging on the cross: tribulation was already present, if he was hanging. But why was it said, *it is near*? It is already in the flesh. Nothing is nearer to the soul than the flesh which it carries. Other things are outside, are called external, because they are outside. When the flesh is tormented, the soul is knocked at from close by.

**5.** These things are grave, *but in all things we more than conquer through him who loved us*[^13]. But why do we conquer even in these? Because *if God is for us, who is against us*[^14]? Therefore the Lord deigned by the name of the cross to signify all things terrible, hard, bitter, intolerable, and savage. For even among the kinds of death there is nothing more intolerable than the cross. Indeed, it was added with great weight by the same Apostle, when he commended to us the humility of the Lord; for he says, *He did not consider it robbery to be equal to God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^15]. How great the humiliation of the Lord, to become a man! But hear further. Behold, he was humbled, because he was made a man. What more? *He humbled*, he says, *himself, made obedient unto death*—not only unto a human birth, but even *unto death*. Have you anything to add to this? "I have," he says: *but the death of the cross*[^16]. Looking on this end of the Lord, the most bitter of all, he called by the name "cross" all the things that the Christian flesh suffers when rightfully, for his name, the faith has endured them. For faith suffers nothing; rather, it makes even the sufferings bearable. Instructed, then, on both sides, and having both doors of desire and fear fortified, desiring nothing more than what God promises, fearing nothing more than what God threatens, we repel both those who flatter and those who threaten.

**6.** But someone stands up and raises a question for us and says: "All the bitter things, all the hard things, all the savage and harsh-to-endure things are indeed to be borne for the faith of Christ, to be borne entirely; but it troubles me that he who among his precepts placed the first precept for loving one's neighbor: *Honor your father and mother, which is the first commandment with a promise, that it may be well with you*[^17]; and when the Apostle mentioned this as a great thing, the Lord says to me, *If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother*[^18]. To which shall I listen? Which of these shall I accept? How shall I obey the one commanding me to honor father and mother, and likewise the one commanding me to hate father and mother? Is he not one and the same who commands both, or—as certain vain people suppose—is it one who gave the law and another who propagated the Gospel, since in the law it is written, *Honor father and mother*, in the Gospel, *Whoever does not hate father and mother*?" Far from it! He himself is both the giver of the law and the propagator of the Gospel; he himself, entirely. Recognize the Lord: lest he command contrary things, he distributed the precepts according to the times. For if you think that the precept commanding us to honor father and mother, because it is read earlier in the Old Testament, is contrary to the gospel precept, in that same Old Testament something is written like what we just now heard read in the gospel. *He who says to father and mother, "I do not know you," and who does not acknowledge his own children, he has kept my testament*[^19]. How similar, how twinned, how equal it is recognized to be. Therefore one Lord commands both, but let us distinguish the times.

**7.** The martyr is armed for the crown, and father and mother come flattering, to hinder what God promises, promising a vain inheritance and taking away the eternity of light. Here plainly do not acknowledge those who hold you back; rather, hate the father here, out of devotion hate such a one, that he may not remain such. So father, so mother, so children, so brothers, so the wife—in whom is the greater question, when it has come to this point—hate even her, if she too tries to hinder you. Beware of Eve: then she is not your member, but the ministry of the serpent. You who unwisely heard her first voice, and did not profit by experience, but the very wife, to hinder the martyr husband from the crown, by feminine wiles prescribes to the husband as it were from the law and from the very Gospel. "Hear," she says, "the precept." What precept? "*What God has joined, let not man separate*[^20]. Hear plainly, do not scorn it. *What God has joined, let not man separate*." But see that the wife does not separate you from God. "But what he has joined, man ought not to separate." Ought human affection to separate man from God himself? And where will be what you just now heard, *Who will separate us from the love of Christ*[^21]? Here, then, do not fear: man does not separate what God has joined, but God separates the one trying to separate. Do not, then, listen to the wife saying to you, "I am your member, behold, you are a member." Answer: "If my member were rotting, and by rotting were trying to make the whole body waste away, would it not be cut off by the physician? Do I not hear the Lord and true physician himself saying, *For it is profitable for you that one of your members perish, rather than that your whole body go into hell*[^22]?" Answer, then, with the law well understood, against the one who uses the law badly. For the serpent does nothing great, attempts nothing great in wishing to deceive you about the law through Eve—the serpent who deceived the first Adam without law. For he saw you now feeding on the law, and sets a snare in your food. But say, *My eyes are always toward the Lord, for he will pluck my feet out of the snare*[^23]. So in this peril too, where you are tempted about the law, let your eyes be toward the Lord, because he too was tempted by the same serpent about the law.

**8.** For when the Lord, to set us an example of contest and victory, deigned to be tempted by the devil, just as he deigned to be crucified by the ungodly, in the first temptation—for we read that there were three—he answered from the law the devil who said, *If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become loaves*[^24]; he, from the law: *Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*[^25]. Again, in another temptation: *Worship me prostrate, and I will give you these things*; and he, from the law: *It is written: You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve*[^26]. But when that cunning serpent saw himself twice repulsed from the law, he stretched a third snare from the law: "*Throw yourself*," he said, "*down* from the pinnacle of the temple, *if you are the Son of God*." And at once, to the instrument of the law, that he might fight from where he had been overcome: "*For it is written*," he said, "*He has commanded his angels concerning you, that they bear you up in their hands, lest you dash your foot against a stone*[^27]. Because, then," he said, "*if you are the Son of God*, the angels receive you lest you be harmed, *throw yourself* down and prove to us what you are." Surely here, because the devil dared to put his hand to the law, did the Lord take his hand from the law? Here too, from the same law, he wounded the enemy, laid him low, made him go off confounded. For he said, *It is written: You shall not tempt the Lord your God*[^28]. And here too, then, when you see the wife trying to hinder you from martyrdom, reciting the law to you and saying, "*What God has joined, let not man separate*," recite there too the law of the Gospel: *Whoever comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children*[^29].

**9.** Do not, then, be angry, wife. The one at whom you are angry here, in this cause, because he hates you, also hates the father in this cause, also hates the mother. But it is too little for the wife, because it is written, *A man will leave father and mother and will cleave to his wife*[^30]. For if the parents—that is, father and mother—say to the son, "If your wife does not wish to stay with us, you stay with us, and dismiss her," let the wife take up this precept and fight, prescribe to the husband and say from the law of God, "Rather, dismiss them and do not dismiss me. For I recite, *A man will leave father and mother and will cleave to his wife*." You have recited, you have understood, you have conquered; plainly no one doubts that father and mother trying to separate the son from his wife are to be dismissed, that the wife not be deserted. But attend, woman, if you deign to be faithful, and do not disdain to hear a word of faith, attend: from where you have conquered, allow yourself there to be conquered. Behold, we restrain your father-in-law and your mother-in-law, the father and mother of your husband, who begot him, who nourished him, who brought him to this age at which he might become your husband; we wholly restrain and rebuke them by divine authority, and we have said: "Do not wish to separate the son from his wife on your account, for he is better separated from you than from her. If it can be done, all be together. But if this cannot be done, it is better that he be with her than with you without her." We have pleaded your cause. You have conquered in this judgment: allow yourself to be conquered in another, because there too we plead your cause. We have faithfully pleaded the cause of your flesh: ought we to abandon the cause of your everlasting salvation? Learn. The father and mother of your husband did not separate you from your husband: do not separate your husband from your God.

**10.** There fornication is more to be feared, in that chastity which the soul owes to God; there fornication is more to be feared, which the Apostle feared when he said, *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve in his craftiness, so too your minds be corrupted from the chastity of God which is in Christ Jesus*[^31]. You see that there too chastity is demanded. Why do you not wish to be separated from your husband? Lest you have the necessity of fornication? But in your husband's martyrdom you ought to fear nothing. For he being crowned and translated from human affairs to divine, you will be a widow; if you marry, you will not be a fornicatress. But perhaps as the wife of a martyr you blush to marry further. Behold, your husband being crowned, let your faith also be safe. Do not drag your husband from the embrace of the spiritual marriage. There too chastity is demanded, and more there because greater there, more there because eternal there. *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*[^32]. For what else do you do, that your husband may be overcome by your flatteries, but that there be no virtue in him? In whom there will be no virtue, how badly off will your husband be! He is overcome, crushed, made effeminate by your flatteries: how will he be your husband, who will no longer be a man? For what do you do, but that he yield to your flatteries, despise the precepts of God, deny Christ, sacrifice to idols? Behold, the one whose flesh you seek—his soul fornicates; behold, he hears, *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*. But you wish him to cleave to you and be separated from God: hear what follows: *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you; but for me to cleave to God is good*[^33]. Hear your husband armed with faith, hurling against your iniquity the darts of righteousness: *I am certain*, he says, *that neither death nor life, nor angel nor principality, nor height nor depth, nor any other creature will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus*[^34]. Perhaps here you say: "But in all these he did not name the wife." In all these, you say, he did not name the wife. Deceitful one, the apostolic power kept watch even against you by adding, *nor any other creature*. Or will you perhaps dare to say that you are not a creature? If you are a creature—rather, because you are a creature—neither will you separate the one whom no creature will separate.

**11.** Thus we have spoken as though men were hastening to martyrdoms and being hindered by their wives: let us console the weaker sex too. How many women have been crowned and were not overcome by men flattering unmanfully! Whence was Perpetua made perpetual, but because . . . perpetual? Whence was Felicity capable of so great felicity, but because temporal infelicity did not terrify her? Therefore, lest women too in this cause be seduced by the flatteries of men, let Perpetua be regarded by them, let Felicity be regarded, and let perpetual felicity be held.

**12.** Therefore let father and mother not be angry in such a cause, nor wife, nor children, nor brothers; and if they will be angry, it is added: hate moreover your own soul[^35]. In this cause, then, you love your wife badly, you who are commanded even to hate your own soul. *For what does it profit*, he says, *a man, if he gain the whole world, but suffer the loss of his soul*[^36]? And yet you are commanded to hate it too, lest it too seduce you, wishing to be here, wishing always to cleave to this flesh, unwilling to migrate to better things; hate such a soul, that you may not have such a one. For what else does it whisper within, in the people whom the peril of persecution tempts, but "Deny, live: you will do penance afterward"? It is the soul: it wishes to perish. Hate it as it wishes to perish. Do not permit it to perish; chastise, rebuke, form it, call God to your aid. For it withers from you and fails amid the perils of this life, with the eyes of faith not straight it contemplates eternal life: lift it up by exhortation and say, *Why are you sad, my soul, and why do you disturb me*? Perhaps it will answer you: "Because I am weak." Then you have what follows: *Hope in the Lord*, he himself will be your virtue, he himself will be your firmness. *Hope in the Lord, for I will confess to him*—I will not deny him, for I will confess him. But you, soul, were murmuring, "Deny him." *I will confess to him: the salvation of my countenance and my God*[^37]. Therefore he who loves his soul, let him lose it here, that he may find it unto eternal life[^38]. Therefore do not listen to those whispering contrary things, neither father nor mother, nor wife, nor children, nor your own soul.

**13.** Let the wife be heard, with fear of conjugal separation, only as long as she does not advise anything by which God is offended. Thus all the degrees will keep their order: excellent virginity, widowhood following, marriage third, none without reward. Let virgins think on Mary, widows on Anna, the married on Susanna. All have their rewards, and from all these degrees the martyrs have by no means been lacking. The husband fears, at the moment of martyrdom, lest his wife be an Eve to him; the wife too fears, at the moment of martyrdom, lest her husband be a serpent to her. But let no one listen to anyone advising anything against God. Let carnal affection be valid up to this point; let the infirmity of the flesh not transcend the spiritual wall: let it be below, because it is below. Let the soul, subject to God, lead the flesh; let the soul not be seduced away from God into the dominion of the flesh: that is perverse. You wish to have the chief place in your house, much more does God in his. Hold and meditate on these things, imitate them in all the solemnities of the martyrs, *and the God of peace will be with you*[^39]. Let us turn to the Lord . . .
