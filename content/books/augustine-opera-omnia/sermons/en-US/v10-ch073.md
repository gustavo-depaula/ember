# Sermon 386: On the Love of Enemies

**1.** Attend, my brothers, to love, which the divine Scripture so praises that it makes nothing equal to it. When God admonishes us to love one another, does he admonish only this, that you love the one who loves you? This is mutual love; this does not suffice for God: for he wished it to reach even to loving enemies, saying: *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute you; that you may be sons of your Father who is in heaven, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, who rains upon the just and the unjust*[^1]. What do you say? Do you love your enemy? You will perhaps answer: Through weakness I cannot. But make progress, and bring it about that you can: especially because you are going to pray to a judge whom no one can deceive, who will plead your case. Entreat, then, this judge, where no clerk disturbs, no official keeps you off, no advocate is hired who can pour out prayers for you, or say words you have not learned: but the only Son of God himself, equal to the Father, sitting at the right hand of the Father, his assessor, your judge, has taught you a few words which any unlettered man can hold and say, and in them has set up your case for you; he has taught you the heavenly law, how you are to pray. But perhaps you will answer: Through whom do I ask? Through myself, or through another? He who taught you to pray, he himself pleads the prayer for you, because you were the guilty one. Rejoice, because he will then be your judge who is now your advocate. So then, because you are going to pray, you are going to plead your case in a few words, you will come to those words: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^2]. For God says to you: What do you give me, that I should forgive you your debts? What gift do you offer, what sacrifice of your conscience do you place upon my altars? At once he taught you what you should ask and what you should offer. You ask: *Forgive us our debts*: and you offer—what? *As we also forgive our debtors*. You are a debtor to one who cannot be deceived: you too have a debtor. God says to you: You are my debtor, he your debtor; this I do to you, my debtor, what you do to your debtor. From this you offer me a gift, in that you spare your debtor. You ask me for mercy; do not you be slow in mercy. Attend to what Scripture says: *I desire mercy rather than sacrifice*[^3]. Do not offer sacrifice without mercy; because your sins are not forgiven you unless you offer with mercy. But perhaps you say: I have no sins. However cautious you are, brother, yet living in the flesh in the world, you act amid pressures and straits, and are engaged amid innumerable temptations; you cannot be without sin. Certainly God says to you: Be secure about sin, do not forgive if you have nothing for me to forgive you, but rather exact it if you owe nothing: but if you are a debtor, rather be glad that you have a debtor, in whom you may do what may be done in you. Hear me, and examine yourself, since even of the few upright ones who can truthfully pray the Lord's Prayer, truthfully say: Lord, forgive me, as I too forgive. Not deceitfully, not feignedly, but from a true heart do it, that in you too it may be done truly. For if he who harmed you, who sinned against you, asks pardon of you, and you forgive him; now you can securely say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*. For if you resist the one who asks, you too will be despised when you ask. You shut your door against the one who knocks, you will find it shut when you knock. For if you open the bowels of indulgence to the one who asks you, God too will open to you when you ask him. For now I address those who ask pardon of their Christian brothers, and do not receive it. Behold, if you grant it, you will pray securely. But that other—if he asks you, and you do not grant it, how will you be secure? For whoever you are who have sinned, and have not deserved pardon, do not fear, entreat God, his and yours: for they are debts: can a servant exact debts which the master has forgiven? If perhaps he who sinned against you does not ask you, if he does not beg pardon; if he sinned, and on top of that is still angry, what will you do? Do you forgive, or do you not forgive? Behold, you have not forgiven. Why? Because he did not ask. If you have not forgiven for this reason, that he did not ask, do not waver in the Lord's Prayer, say it securely, do not beat your breast, because you did not forgive one who did not ask. So that one who did not ask remained: it is exacted, utterly exacted, of him: yet let perfect love be in you, pray for the one who does not ask: because you pray for one in great peril.

**2.** Here now attend to your Master and Lord, not sitting on a chair, but hanging on the wood, the crowds of enemies looked at on every side, and saying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^4]. See the master, hear the imitator. Did the Lord Christ then pray for those who asked, and not rather for those who insulted and killed him? Did the physician abandon his office because the frenzied man raged? For he says: *Forgive them, for they know not what they do*. They kill the Savior, because they do not seek salvation. On the contrary, you too will perhaps say: And when can I do what the Lord could? Why do you say this? Attend to where he does this; attend that he did this on the cross, not in heaven. For God is always in heaven with the Father: but on the cross, for your sake, he is man, where he offered himself to all to be imitated. For your sake he sent forth that very voice, that it might be heard by all. For he could have prayed for them in silence, but then you would not have an example. But if the Lord is too much for you, let the servant not be too much for you. Can you not imitate your Lord, when he hung on the cross? Attend to Stephen, his servant, when he was stoned. First he says, as a servant to the Lord: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit*; and after this, kneeling, he says: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^5]: and when he had said this, he fell asleep in the rest of love. He found the richest peace, because he wished peace for his enemies. Did he too then pray for those who asked, and not for those who raged, who stoned and killed him? You have an example, learn, see how, standing for himself, he prayed, and for them he knelt. Do we suppose, brothers, that he loves them more than himself? Standing for himself, as a righteous man, he was easily heard. But for the unjust he had to kneel. He showed, then, love even toward enemies asking pardon. Therefore, brothers, for the security of the Lord's Prayer, forgive from the heart those who ask, that the Lord may forgive you your sins, in this mortal body and in the future, forever, etc.

[^1]: Matt 5:44-45
[^2]: Matt 6:12
[^3]: Hos 6:6; Matt 9:13
[^4]: Luke 23:34
[^5]: Acts 7:59-60
