# Sermon 223/A: On the Beginning of Genesis, on the Paschal Vigil

**1.** We have heard many divine readings, to whose length neither can we render an equal sermon, nor would you grasp it if we could. As much, then, as the Lord grants, we wish to speak to your Charity of the very head of the Scriptures, where, when it was read, we heard that *in the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^1]. Attend and consider who made it. But I know that you cannot consider who made it; consider what he made, and praise him who made it. *In the beginning God made heaven and earth.* Behold, the things that were made are at hand, are beheld, delight. The work lies open, the maker lies hidden; for both that from which he is seen lies open, and that from which he is loved lies hidden. When, then, we see the world, and love God, that is surely better, from which we love, than that from which we see. With the eyes we see, with the mind we love. Therefore let us prefer the mind to the eyes, because he too is better whom we love out of the hidden, than his work which we see out of the open. Let us ask, then, if it pleases, when God made so great a mass, by what engines he made it. The engine of the maker is the word of the one commanding. Why do you wonder? It is the work of the Almighty. If, then, you ask who made it, God made it; if you ask what he made, he made heaven and earth; if you ask through what he made it, he made it through the Word, which he did not make. The Word, through which heaven and earth were made, that Word was not made. For if it was made, through what was it made? *All things were made through it*[^2]. If whatever was made was made through the Word, without doubt the Word was not made, through which all things were made. Finally, the narrator of the works, the servant of God, Moses: *In the beginning*, he says, *God made heaven and earth.* He made heaven and earth in the beginning. Through what did he make it? Through the Word. Did he make the Word too? No: but what? *In the beginning was the Word*[^3]. It already was, through which he made; therefore that which was not, he made. We can understand, and we rightly understand, that in the only-begotten Word itself heaven and earth were made. For through which they were made, in it they were made. This can be and be understood as the beginning, in which God made heaven and earth. For the Word itself is also the Wisdom of God, to whom it is said: *You made all things in wisdom*[^4]. If in wisdom God made all things, and his only-begotten Son is without doubt the Wisdom of God[^5], let us not doubt that in the Son were made the things which we have learned were made through the Son. For the Son himself is surely the beginning. For when the Jews asked and said: *Who are you?* he answered: *The Beginning*[^6]. Behold, *in the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^7].

**2.** But now the rest, whether when they are arranged and disposed, or when they are adorned, or when the things which had not been made in heaven and on earth are created, God says, and they come to be. *And God said: let it be, and it was made*[^8]. And so through each work: he said, and it was made. *He himself spoke, and they were made*[^9]. With what tongue did he speak? so that who might hear, did he speak? Let us not always be nourished with milk: raise up your minds with us to solid food. Let no one think of God as a body, let no one think of God as a man, let no one think of God as an angel; although he deigned so to appear to the Fathers, not through his very substance, but through his creature subject to him; for otherwise he would not, invisible, appear to human sight. Let us seek what is better in us, and from there let us try to attain what is better than all. What is better in us is the mind; what is better than all is God. Why do you seek a better thing with a worse thing? In you the body is lower than the mind; in things nothing is better than God. Raise up what is better in you, that you may attain, if you can, to him who is better than all. For I too, when I speak, speak to minds. Visible faces indeed I behold, I myself too visible in body; but through that which I see, I address that which I do not see. I bear within a word conceived in the heart, and I wish to bring forth in your ears what I conceived in the heart; I wish to tell you what is within, to bring forth to you what is hidden: I seek how it may be able to reach your mind. I approach first, as it were the door of your mind, your ears; and since the word, which I conceived in the heart, is invisible, and I cannot draw it to you, I provide it, as it were, a vehicle, sound. Behold, the word lies hidden, the sound lies open; I place the hidden upon the open, and I reach the hearer; and so the word goes forth from me, comes to you, and has not departed from me. If, then, it is permitted to compare small things to great, the lowest to the highest, the human to the divine, God too did this. The Word lay hidden with the Father: that it might come to us, it took up, as it were, a vehicle, it took up flesh; it proceeded to us, and did not depart from the Father; but before his incarnation, before Adam himself the father of the human race, before heaven and earth and all that is in them, *in the beginning was the Word*[^10], and: *in the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^11].

**3.** But God made the earth, still before it was adorned, before its form was laid bare. *It was invisible and unformed, and darkness was upon the deep.* There was darkness, where there was no light; for not yet was light made. *The Spirit of God was borne above over the waters*[^12], and he too the maker, nor separated from the Father and the only-begotten Word. For behold, if we attend diligently, the Trinity is intimated to us. For where it is said: *In the beginning he made*, it is understood of the Father and the Son: in the beginning, that is, in the Son, God the Father. The Spirit remains, that the Trinity may be completed: *The Spirit of God was borne above over the waters. And God said.* To whom did God say? Before the creature was made, was there one who heard? There was, he says. I ask, who? The Son himself. To the Son, then, God said. By what word did he speak to the Word? For if the Son already was, as no Christian doubts, surely this too was. The Son was the Word, and to the Word the Father was speaking. Did, then, words run between God and the Word? Far be it: take away, brothers, the hindrances of fleshly thought; think the invisible things invisibly, let not likenesses of the body turn before the eyes of your mind. Pass over whatever is beheld in you: transcend whatever in you is even not beheld; for the body is beheld, the soul is not beheld, but yet is changed. Now it wills, now it does not will; now it knows, now it does not know; now it remembers, now it forgets; now it advances, now it fails. This is not God, God is not this nature, the soul is not a portion of the substance of God. For whatever God is, is an unchangeable good, an incorruptible good. Even if God is invisible, the soul is invisible, yet the soul is changeable, God unchangeable. Transcend, then, not only what is beheld in you, but also that which is changed in you. Transcend the whole, transcend yourself.

**4.** A certain lover of invisible goodness, a certain lover of invisible eternity, says in his sighs and groanings of his love: *My tears have become my bread day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God?*[^13] How would his tears not truly become for the one groaning and loving his bread, that he might feed on them in some manner as from the pleasure of food, and willingly weep, as long as he does not see what he loves, when it is said to him daily: *Where is your God?* If I say to a pagan: *Where is your God?* he will show me idols. If I break the idol, he will show a mountain, will show a tree, will show a cheap stone from the river; for what he took from many stones, and placed in a more honored place, and, bowed down, adored, this is his god. Behold, he says, pointing his finger, behold is my god. When I have mocked the stone, when I have taken it away, when I have broken it, when I have thrown it away, when I have despised it, he points his finger to the sun, to the moon, points to any star you please; that one he calls Saturn, that one Mercury, that one Jupiter, that one Venus. Whatever he wishes, wherever he points his finger, he answers me: Behold is my god. And because I see the sun, and cannot break it, the stars I cannot cast down, heaven I cannot overturn, he seems to himself as it were superior, by showing visible things, and stretching out his finger to whatever he wishes, and saying: Behold is my god. And he turns to me, saying: *Where is your God?* When I hear: *Where is your God?* I have nothing to show to the eyes, I find minds barking, blind; for the eyes by which he has whence to see I have not what to show. Whom I truly have to show, he has not eyes whence to see. It pleases me to weep, as it were to feed on bread in tears. For my God is invisible; he who speaks to me requires visible things, when he says: *Where is your God?* But I, that I might come to my God, as he says in that very Psalm: *These things I meditated, and I poured out my soul above me*[^14]. My God is not below my soul, but is above my soul. Whence do I attain to that which is above my soul, unless I pour out my soul above me? And yet to this insolent one, requiring visible things, showing visible things, exulting over visible things, with my God helping, I will try in some way to answer. This certainly you say to me: *Where is your God?* I answer you: Where are you yourself? I answer, I say, I think not unfittingly. You asked where God is, but I where my questioner himself is. He will say: Behold where I am; you see me, I speak to you. And I to him: I seek my questioner: his face I see, his body I see, his voice I hear, his tongue I observe; I seek the very one who fixes his eyes on me, who moves his tongue, who sends forth his voice, who by questioning desires to know. All this, of which I speak, is the soul. So I will not deal with you longer. You say: Show me your God; I say: Show me your soul. You labor, you are wearied, you are fixed, when I say: Show me your soul; I know that you cannot. Why can you not? Because your soul is invisible. And yet it is better in you than your body; but my God is better than your soul. How, then, shall I show my God, when you do not show me your soul, than which I show my God better? Behold, if you say to me: From the work recognize my soul; because I fix my eyes to see, my ears to hear, I move my tongue to speak, I bring forth my voice to sound, from this understand and know my soul; you see that you cannot show it, but you bid me know it from its works. Behold, I too from the works will show you my God. Nor do I go further, nor do I perhaps send your unbelief to the things you do not grasp. I do not so recall the works of my God: He made invisible things, he made visible things, that is, *heaven and earth, the sea and all that is in them*[^15]. I do not send you through many things: I return to you yourself. You surely live: you have a body, you have a soul; the body is visible, the soul is invisible; the body the dwelling, the soul the dweller; the body the vehicle, the soul using the vehicle; the body, as it were, a vehicle to be governed, the soul the charioteer of your body. Behold, your senses are manifest, as it were doors in your body, through which something may be announced to your soul dwelling within: eyes, ears, smell, taste, touch, the arranged members. What is that within, whence you think, whence you give life to these? All this, which you wonder at in yourself, he who made it, he himself is my God.

**5.** Therefore, my brothers, if I have approached your minds, your inner men, by some fitting address, as I could, if by speaking I have reached those who dwell within in houses of clay[^16], that is, your souls dwelling in your bodies, do not conjecture about the divine things from those you know. God surpasses all things, heaven and earth. Do not set before your eyes, as it were, some craftsman composing, disposing, contriving, turning on a lathe, twisting some great thing; or surely, as it were, an emperor on a royal seat, bright, adorned, sitting, and by commanding creating. Break the idols in your hearts; attend to what was said to Moses, when he inquired the name of God: *I am who am*[^17]. Whatever else is, in comparison with him is not. That which truly is does not know how to be changed in any part. Everything whatever that is changed and flows, and at a certain time does not cease to be changed, was and will be; in it you do not grasp *is*. But God has not "was" and "will be." But what was, now is not; what will be, is not yet; and what so comes that it may pass away, therefore will be that it may not be. Consider, if you can, *I am who am*. Do not be rolled by wills, do not be agitated by voluntary and temporal thoughts. Stand at *is*, stand at the very *is*. Where do you go? Stand, that you too may be able to be. But when do we hold a flighty thought, and fix it to that which remains? when can we? Therefore God had mercy; and he who is, and he who said: *This you shall say to the children of Israel: He who is sent me to you*[^18], having spoken the name of his substance, afterward speaks the name of his mercy. What is the name of his substance? *I am who am. You shall say to the children of Israel: He who is sent me to you.* But Moses was a man, and was among those things which in comparison with him are not; he was on the earth, was in the flesh; and in that very flesh was a soul, was a changeable nature, was under the burden of human frailty. For that which was said: *I am who am*, when did he grasp it? For through that which was seen by his eyes, he was speaking to him who was not seen, and God, hidden, used the one who was seen as, it were, an instrument. For what Moses saw was not the whole of God; because neither is what sounds from me, in that I am a man, the whole of my word. I have in my heart a word which does not sound; the sound passes, the word remains. Therefore when God spoke to a man, the invisible, through that by which he deigned to appear visible, was speaking, the eternal temporal things, the unchangeable fragile things, when he said: *I am who am*, and: *You shall say to the children of Israel: He who is sent me to you*, as though that one were unable to grasp what is, *I am who am*, and: *he who is sent me to you*, or perhaps, if he himself grasped it, it was to be read by us, who cannot grasp it, at once after the name of substance he spoke the name of mercy. As though he said to Moses: What I said: *I am who am*, you do not grasp; your heart does not stand, you are not unchangeable with me, nor is your mind unchangeable. You heard what I am; hear what you may grasp, hear what you may hope. *God said again to Moses: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^19]. You cannot grasp the name of my substance: grasp the name of my mercy. *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.* But what I am is eternal: Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob indeed eternal, rather I do not say eternal, but made eternal by him. Finally, the Lord himself thus also convicted the calumniating Sadducees, who, when they denied the resurrection, gave them from this the testimony of holy Scripture: *Read what the Lord said in the bush to Moses: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. He is not the God of the dead, but of the living; for all of them live*[^20]. Accordingly here too God did not add, when he had said: *I am who am, this is my name forever.* For of this no one doubts that what is, is therefore, because it is eternal; but where he says: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*, there he added: *This is my name forever*[^21]. As though he said: Why do you fear about the mortality of the human race? why, being strong, do you tremble, lest, when you have died, you be no more? *This is my name forever.* An eternal name could not be *the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*, unless Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob lived forever.  
Turning to the Lord, and a prayer.  
May the power of his mercy confirm our heart in his truth, may it confirm and tranquilize our souls; may his grace abound upon us, and may he have mercy on us, and take away scandals from us, and from his Church, and from all our dearest ones, and make us please him by his power and the abundance of his mercy upon us forever. Through Jesus Christ his Son, our Lord, who with him lives and reigns, and with the Holy Spirit, forever and ever. Amen.
