# Sermon 363B

**1.** Dearest brothers, the divine words are read to us wisely and for our salvation, to feed our souls. *For* the Christian *does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*. Just as we need earthly food daily for this temporal life, so we need spiritual food for eternal life. For if this temporal life, full of many dangers and hardships, is loved by many, how much more should eternal life be loved, which is being prepared for us with the angels? The Lord says: *In the resurrection they will neither marry nor take wives, but will be equal to God's angels*. For this eternal life, then, the divine law was brought forth, the patriarchs chosen, priests and Levites received the anointing of chrism, prophets came, angels were sent, and finally the Lord himself, God's Son, descended from heaven to earth and restored us to his image. From this we must infer how great and what kind of need was ours, that the majesty on high should come in our flesh. What greater need than our eternal death? What harsher punishment than the hard slavery of sins? What greater cruelty than our eternal captivity? For we were bound in death, in slavery, in captivity. How do we prove our eternal death? Hear the Apostle saying: *Death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those who did not sin*. How do we prove slavery? Christ the Lord says: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*. How do we prove our captivity? The voice of the martyrs cries out in the psalms too: *Turn back our captivity, Lord, like a torrent in the south*. And if this temporal captivity under barbarian enemies is so cruel and bitter, though it is escaped by flight, ransomed with money, or finally ended by death, what will that eternal captivity be like, which is not ended even by death, but is tormented forever in the underworld?[^1]

**2.** This manifold need, then, brothers, brought Christ the Lord to earth at this time. For by coming in our flesh he removed death and restored life, removed slavery and restored freedom, removed captivity to demons and restored to us adoption as sons, as the prophet says in the psalms: *He ascended on high, led captivity captive, and gave gifts to people*. Rightly, then, Christ the Lord came to us to save us, as we read: *not a prince or an envoy, not an angel, but the Lord himself* came and saved us. A wonderful thing, brothers: Christ came into this world, who was always in the world, is in the world, and will come again into this world. How is his coming into the world proved? The Apostle says: *The saying is humane and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into this world to save sinners, of whom I am the first*. That he was in this world, the Evangelist testifies: *He was in the world, and the world was made through him, and the world did not know him*. That he is also in the world with us now, the Lord himself told the apostles: *Go, teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit; and see, I am with you every day until the end of the age*. That he will come again, an angel tells the apostles: *As you see Christ going into heaven, so you will see him coming*, as the prophet had foretold long before: *God will come openly, our God, and he will not be silent*. Since, then, Christ the Lord *was in the world and the world did not know him*—that is, the human race did not believe in him when invisible—what a great mystery and wonderful sacrament! For the world's Creator willed to become a creature of the world and took away the world's sins, as the Evangelist says: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*—[^2]

**3.** since, then, brothers, we believe that Christ the Lord's first coming has already occurred and praise him in believing, we are met from the opposing side by the question of unbelievers, Jews, gentiles, pagans, and Manichees, who say: “What is this that Christians assert, saying that the God of majesty came into this world to save the human race? Why do they say this? Did he have no one in heaven to send? Had he no angel, no other power, as when he chose Moses and Aaron to free the people of Israel from captivity? Certainly, if he decided to come himself, why did he come through a woman? Why did he pass through shameful organs?” We answer them: he could have freed us in another way, because God is almighty; but it was not enough for God to have power alone without also the justice of that power. For power lies in strength, justice in reason. Reason therefore required that the human being be saved through him through whom every human being was made, as we read in the divine Scriptures that God the Father said to his Son: *Let us make man in our image and likeness*. As for their saying, “He passed through shameful organs,” they are easily defeated. There is no shamefulness where virginity is untouched, nor can any stain be alleged where nature is undefiled. For if this bodily sun, composed of light and heat, passes through muddy and filthy places and cannot be polluted, how much more could Christ's incorporeal divinity not be defiled by being born through the Virgin? A virgin conceived, a virgin gave birth, a virgin remained. What Eve lost, the Virgin Mary restored. The virgin Eve had brought us death; the Virgin Mary bore us the Savior. Hence equitable and just reason determined that man should be saved by retracing the very steps through which he had first perished.[^3]
