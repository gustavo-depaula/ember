# Sermon 223/E: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** So great a brightness and solemnity of this vigil admonishes us, brothers, illuminating the renewed memory of the Lord's resurrection by its yearly return, to recall and to do what he himself said to the disciples about his already impending passion: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*[^1]. Let us watch, then, and pray, lest we enter into temptation, not on this night only, but throughout the whole time of this life, which on earth is a temptation. For so it is written: *Is not human life a temptation upon earth?*[^2] If, then, to enter into temptation is nothing but to be led or brought into temptation, that is, to be deceived and caught, or in any way, to put it all more briefly, to be conquered by temptation, what else must be done throughout the whole night of this life, in which through the light of faith we ought to be day, than what the Lord admonished the disciples: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation?* The watch of the mind is, lest faith sleep, hope wither, and love grow cold. But with faith watching, hope flourishing, love fervent, throughout this whole time, in which we are engaged in the night of this age, let us with assiduous prayer say: *Bring us not into temptation*[^3]. So, with his help, we do what the Lord says: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*. If truly it is not to be dreaded to enter into temptation, when we can also come out of it, according to the apostle James saying: *Count it all joy, my brothers, when you fall into various temptations*[^4], it remains that the hope be in what the apostle Paul says: *God is faithful, who will not permit you to be tempted beyond what you can bear, but will with the temptation also make a way of escape, that you may be able to endure*[^5]. Of this way of escape it can not absurdly be understood that we are admonished even by the holy Canticle, where it is read: *May the Lord keep your going in and your going out*[^6]; that, as into a furnace, he may suffer us, vessels of clay, not only well formed to enter, but also to be able to come out intact, as it is written: *The furnace proves the potter's vessels, and the temptation of tribulation just men*[^7]. But if the matter is so, surely when the Lord said to the disciples: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*, he saw so great a weight of his passion impending, that he did not want them to enter into that temptation, from which he saw them not yet fit to be allowed to come out. On account of which he had already said to the most blessed apostle Peter: *You cannot follow me now, but you will follow afterward*[^8]. Who, when he believed himself able not only to follow, but even to go before, saying: *I will lay down my soul for you*[^9], with the strong winds of the Lord's passion bearing down, and the sea on which they sailed vehemently and deeply troubling it, he would have been submerged at the question of one servant girl, had not, mercy at once obtained by tears, he been freed by the right hand of the Lord. Let us watch, then, brothers, and pray, lest we enter into temptation which we cannot bear, and, into whatever we enter, we may be able, either by a way of escape given to bear it, or by endurance given to come out; that we may not be brought into temptation without a way out, like feet into fetters, like a wild beast into nets, like birds into snares.

**2.** That we may not suffer this, he will grant it to us to whom we sang to the Lord: *For he is gloriously magnified*; who in the laver of regeneration has already granted us what we sang: *horse and rider he has cast into the sea*[^10]. For all our past sins, which were pursuing us as if from behind, he submerged and blotted out in baptism. Our darkness, which the unclean spirits governed as their beasts of burden, that is, their aids, and, as riders, drove where they wished; and therefore the Apostle calls them *the rulers of this darkness*[^11]. Of whom, since we have been freed through baptism, as through the Red Sea, that is, made bloody by the sanctification of the crucified Lord, let us not turn back in heart to Egypt, but, through the other temptations of the desert, with him as our protector and ruler, let us press on toward the kingdom.

[^2]: Job 7:1
