# Sermon 377: On the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** Most beloved brothers, who can worthily utter a temporal word about the eternal Word? How can the lowest suffice for the greatest? The heavens praise him, the powers praise him, the ethereal powers praise him, the lights of heaven praise him, the stars praise him; the earth too praises him as it can: not so as to praise him worthily, but lest, ungrateful, it condemn itself. Who unfolds, who tells, who even comprehends him who *reaches mightily from end to end, and orders all things sweetly*[^1]—how he *exulted to run his course, so that his going forth was from the top of heaven, and his return unto the top of heaven*[^2]? If he reaches everywhere, whence does he go out? If he reaches everywhere, where does he go? He is not stretched out over places, nor varied over times, nor does he have comings and goings: abiding in himself, he goes around everywhere, whole. What are the spaces that do not have the Almighty, that do not hold the Immense, that do not receive the one who comes? If you think of the Word, we have said nothing. But to teach the lowly to say something of him, he humbled himself, *taking the form of a servant*[^3]. In this form he descended; in this form, according to the Gospel, *he advanced in the pursuit of wisdom*[^4]; in this form he was a sufferer; in this form he fought bravely; in this form he died; in this form he conquered death and rose; in this form he returned to heaven—he who never departed from heaven. Blessed, therefore, is he in the firmament of heaven, who, according to the Apostle, *was made a curse for us, that the blessing of Abraham might come upon the Gentiles*[^5]. *He exulted like a giant*[^6]. What sort of giant? By dying he overcame death. What sort of giant? He broke the gates of hell: he went out, and ascended. Who is this king of glory, on whose account it was said to certain princes: *Lift up your gates, O princes; and be lifted up, O eternal gates*[^7]? Be lifted up, he is great; you are narrow, you do not hold him; be lifted up. To what end? *That the king of glory may enter*. They are terrified: *Who is this king of glory?* He is not recognized. He is not only God, but also man; not only man, but also God. He suffers—surely he is God? He rises—surely he is man? Or is he both God and man? For he truly suffers, and truly rises. But this is said twice in one and the same psalm: *Lift up your gates, O princes; and be lifted up, O eternal gates, and the king of glory shall enter in*. And this very thing is repeated after the same words, as though it were thought superfluous and unnecessary. But in the repetition of the same words attend to the endings, and notice why it was said twice. For as to him who rises once and ascends once, twice the gates of the lower world and of heaven are opened. For it is a new thing, God present to the lower world; a new thing, man taken up to the heavens. At both times, in both places, the princes are terrified. *Who is this king of glory?* Whence do we distinguish this? Hear what is answered in both cases. To those asking it is said: *The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle*[^8]. What battle? To undergo death for mortals, to suffer alone for all, the Almighty not resisting, and yet conquering as he dies. Great, then, is this king of glory, even among the inhabitants of the lower world. This too is repeated to the heavenly powers: *Lift up your gates, O princes; and be lifted up, O eternal gates*. Are there not eternal gates, the keys of which Peter received? But because he raises man with himself, there, as if unrecognized, it is said: *Who is this king of glory?* But there, because he is no longer a contender but a victor, because he does not fight but triumphs, there it is not answered, *The Lord mighty in battle*, but, *The Lord of hosts, he himself is the king of glory*[^9].
