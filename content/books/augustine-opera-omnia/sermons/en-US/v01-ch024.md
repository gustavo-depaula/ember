# Sermon 19: A Sermon of Saint Augustine

Delivered in the Restored Basilica

On the Day of the Games

**1.** Singing, we asked the Lord to turn his face away from our sins, and to blot out all our misdeeds. But you can observe, brothers, what we have heard in the same psalm: *For I acknowledge my iniquity, and my sin is ever before me*[^1]. But it is said to God elsewhere, *Do not turn your face away from me*[^2], to whom we have said, *Turn your face away from my sins*[^3]. Since, then, one person is both man and sinner, the man says, *Do not turn your face away from me*, the sinner says, *Turn your face away from my sins*. This, then, is said: Do not turn your face away from him whom you made, turn your face away from that which I made. Let your eye, he says, distinguish both, lest on account of the fault the nature perish. You made something, I too made something. What you made is called nature; what I made is called fault. Let the fault be healed, that the nature may be preserved.

**2.** *My misdeed,* he says, *I acknowledge*[^4]. If I acknowledge, then do you pardon. Let us live well, and, living well, let us not in the least presume that we are without sin. Let life be praised in such a way that pardon is asked. But desperate men, the less intent they are on their own sins, the more curious they are about others'. For they seek not what they may correct, but what they may bite. And since they cannot excuse themselves, they are ready to accuse others. Not so did this man show us the example of praying and making satisfaction to God, saying, *For my misdeed I acknowledge, and my sin is ever before me*[^5]. This man was not intent on others' sins. He summoned himself to himself, nor did he flatter himself but penetrated himself, and descended more deeply into himself. He did not spare himself, and therefore not shamelessly asked that he might be spared. For sin, brothers, cannot be unpunished. If sin remains unpunished, it is unjust; therefore without doubt it must be punished. This your God says to you: "Sin must be punished either by you or by me." Sin, then, is punished either by the man repenting or by God judging. It is punished, then, either by you without you, or by God with you. For what is repentance, except one's own anger against oneself? He who repents is angry with himself. For if it is not done feignedly, whence comes the beating of the breast? Why do you strike, if you are not angry? When, therefore, you beat your breast, you are angry at your heart, that you may make satisfaction to your Lord. For thus too can be understood what is written, *Be angry and do not sin*[^6]. Be angry because you sinned, and, punishing yourself, do not sin. Rouse up your heart by repenting, and this will be a sacrifice to God.

**3.** Do you wish God to be appeased? Know what you do with yourself, that God may be appeased toward you. Attend to the same psalm; for there it is read, *For if you had wished sacrifice, I would surely have given it; in holocausts you will not delight*[^7]. Will you, then, be without sacrifice? About to offer nothing, about to appease God by no offering? What did you say? *If you had wished sacrifice, I would surely have given it; in holocausts you will not delight.* Go on, and hear, and say, *A sacrifice to God is a contrite spirit; a contrite and humbled heart God does not despise*[^8]. The things you were offering cast away, you have found what you may offer. For you used to offer among the fathers the victims of cattle, and they were called sacrifices. *If you had wished sacrifice, I would surely have given it.* Those, then, you do not seek, and yet you seek a sacrifice. Your people say to you, What shall I offer, who do not offer what I used to offer? For the people themselves, as some depart and others are born, are themselves the people. The sacraments are changed, not the faith. The signs by which something was signified are changed, not the thing that was signified. For Christ a ram, for Christ a lamb, for Christ a calf, for Christ a goat—Christ is all. A ram, because he leads the flock: he was found in the thicket, when father Abraham was ordered to spare his son, yet not to depart thence without offering a sacrifice. And Isaac was Christ, and the ram was Christ. Isaac carried the wood for himself: Christ bore his own cross. For Isaac a ram, yet not for Christ a Christ. But in Isaac and in the ram, Christ. The ram was held by the horns in the thicket; ask the Jews whence then they crowned the Lord. He is a lamb: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^9]. He is a bull: attend to the horns of the cross. He is a goat, on account of the likeness of the flesh of sin. These things were veiled, *until the day should breathe and the shadows be removed*[^10]. In the same Lord Christ, then, not only as he is the Word, but also as he is the *Mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^11], both the ancient fathers believed, and to us they transmitted the same faith by preaching and prophesying. Whence the Apostle says, *Having the same spirit* of faith, because it is written, *I believed, therefore I spoke*[^12]. Having the same which they too had who wrote, *I believed, therefore I spoke. Having,* then, *the same,* he says, *spirit of faith, because it is written* by the ancients, *I believed, therefore I spoke, we too believe, therefore we too speak.* When, therefore, holy David said thus, *For if you had wished sacrifice, I would surely have given it, in holocausts you will not delight*[^13], then those sacrifices were offered to God which now are not offered. Therefore when he sang, he prophesied, he spurned present things and foresaw future things. *In holocausts,* he says, *you will not delight.* When, therefore, you will not delight in holocausts, will you remain without sacrifice? Far be it. *A sacrifice to God is a contrite spirit; a contrite and humbled heart God does not despise.* You have what you may offer. Do not look around for a flock, do not prepare ships and cross over to the farthest provinces whence to bring spices. Seek in your heart what is pleasing to God. The heart must be made contrite. Why do you fear lest, contrite, it perish? There you have, *Create in me a clean heart, O God*[^14]. That, then, a clean heart may be created, let the unclean be made contrite.

**4.** Let us be displeasing to ourselves when we sin, because sins displease God. And since we are not without sin, let us at least be like God in this, that what displeases him displeases us. In some part you are joined to the will of God, because that displeases you in yourself which he too hates who made you. He himself is your craftsman, but see yourself and blot out in yourself what is not from his workshop. For God, as it is written, made man upright. *How good is the God of Israel to those who are upright in heart!*[^15] If, then, you are upright in heart, God will not displease you, God will be good to you, you will praise God. Altogether, both in that which he bestows and in that which he chastises, you will praise God. For he who said, *How good is the God of Israel to those who are upright in heart*, had looked at himself, who at one time was not upright in heart, and God displeased him. But afterward he came to his senses, and saw that God was not perverse, but that he himself had not been upright. And, recalling the times of his depravity, and present in his correction, he said, *How good is the God of Israel!* But to whom? *To those who are upright in heart.* For what of you? *But my feet,* he says, *were almost moved, a little more and my steps had been poured out*[^16], that is, I almost slipped. Whence this? *Because I was envious of the sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^17]. Whence, then, this man's feet were moved and his steps almost slipped, since he did not keep silence, he admonished as a thing to be guarded against. He was expecting from God, according to the Old Testament, not knowing that there were signs of future things, he was expecting, then, from God the happiness of the present life, and on this earth he was seeking what God was keeping for his own in heaven. He wished to be happy here, when happiness is not here. For happiness is a good and great thing, but it has its own region. From the region of happiness Christ came, and here even he himself did not find it. He was mocked, he was reproached, he was seized, he was scourged, he was bound, he was struck with the palms, afflicted with the insult of spittings, crowned with thorns, hung on wood. At the last, *the issues of the Lord are from death*. In the psalm it is written [those who knew it acclaimed this]: *And the issues of the Lord are from death*[^18]. Why, then, here, O servant, do you seek happiness, where *even the issues of the Lord are from death?* When, therefore, that man, of whom I had begun to speak, was seeking happiness in a region not its own, and for the sake of obtaining it in this life clung to God and served him and did his commandments as he could, he saw this great thing, or as a great thing, that what he was seeking from God, and for the sake of which he was serving God, was present with those who did not serve God, but worshiped demons and blasphemed the true God. He saw and was moved, as though he had lost the fruit of his labor. This is that he was envious of the sinners, beholding the peace of sinners. Finally you have there, *Behold these sinners and those abounding in the world have obtained riches. Have I then without cause justified my heart, or washed my hands among the innocent, and been scourged all the day?*[^19] I worship God, they blaspheme God. To them happiness, to me calamity. Where is the equity? Hence his feet were moved, hence his steps almost poured out, hence ruin near. For see to what danger he came. There he says, *And I said, How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High?*[^20] See to what danger he came by seeking from God earthly happiness as a great reward. Learn, then, dearly beloved, if you have it, to despise it, and not to say in your hearts, "Because I worship God, therefore it is well with me." For you will see how you think it well with you, and that it is well also with those who do not worship God, and your steps will be moved. For either you have it while worshiping God, and you will see that one who does not worship God has such things, and therefore you will think you worship God in vain, because he too who does not worship God has happiness. Or you do not have it, and you will much more accuse God who gives it to his blasphemers and denies it to his worshipers. Learn, then, to despise earthly things, if you wish to serve God with a faithful heart. Do you have it? Do not think that you are good from it, but make yourself good from it. Do you not have it? Do not think that you are evil from it, but beware of the evil to which the good man does not come.

**5.** For this man, coming to his senses and reproving himself because he had begun to think wrongly of God, panting as a sinner and beholding the peace of sinners, reproving himself, then, said, *For what is there for me in heaven, and what have I wished from you upon earth?*[^21] Now coming to his senses, now with his heart corrected, he acknowledged how much the worship of God is worth, the worship of God which he had sold off at the cheapest price, when for it he was seeking earthly happiness. He acknowledged what is owed on high to the worshipers of God, where we are commanded to have our heart, and we answer that we have our heart there. And would that we did not lie, at least at that hour, at least at that moment, at least at that point of time, when we answer. Looking back, then, and correcting his heart, he reproved himself for having once sought on earth earthly happiness as though a wage for the worship of God. But, reproving himself, he said, *For what is there for me in heaven?* What is there for me there? Eternal life, incorruption, the kingdom with Christ, the fellowship of angels, where there is no disturbance, no ignorance, no danger, no temptation; true, certain, fixed security. Behold, *what is there for me in heaven. And what have I wished from you upon earth?* What have I wished from you upon earth? What have I wished? Fleeting, perishable, flighty riches. What have I wished? Gold, the pallor of the earth; silver, the wanness of the earth; honor, the smoke of time. Behold what I wished from you upon earth. And because I saw this in sinners, my feet were moved, and a little more my steps were poured out. O how good he is to those who are upright in heart! What, then, do you seek, faithful Prophet? Gold and silver and earthly riches? Is, then, the faith of a faithful matron worth as much as that which even a harlot has? Is, then, the faith of a faithful man worth as much as that which even an actor, a charioteer, a hunter, a robber has? Far be it, my brothers, far be it that your faith be worth so much. May God turn this away from your hearts. It is not worth so much. Do you wish to know? For it Christ died. Why, then, do you seek an earthly wage, sold off to gold and coins? You do injury to the faith for which Christ died. And what is it, he says, what is it worth? Attend to him who says, *For what is there for me in heaven?* For he did not express what that will be. So he said, *And what have I wished from you upon earth?* Praising the one, casting away the other, yet he said both. What is the one? *What the eye has not seen*[^22]. What is the other? What the faithful eye does not seek. What is the one? What Lazarus full of sores found. What is the other? What the puffed-up rich man had. What is the one? What cannot perish. What is the other? What cannot be held. What is the one? Where there will be no labor. What is the other? What fear does not leave. *For what is there for me in heaven?* What? He himself who made heaven. The price of your faith is your God. Him you will have; he himself prepares himself as a reward for his worshipers. Consider, dearly beloved, the whole creation, heaven, earth, sea, the things that are in heaven, that are on earth, that are in the sea, how beautiful, how wonderful, how worthily and orderly arranged. Do these things move you? They move you plainly. Why? Because they are beautiful. What, then, is he who made them? I think you would blush if you saw the beauty of the angels. What, then, is the creator of angels? He himself is the reward of your faith. O you greedy, what suffices you, if God himself does not suffice you?

**6.** Let us, then, live well, and that we may be able to do this, let us call upon him who commanded it. Nor let us require from the Lord an earthly wage for our good life. Let us stretch out our intent toward the things that are promised. Let us put our heart there, where it cannot rot away with worldly cares. These things that occupy people pass away, these things fly away, human life upon the earth is a vapor. There come too so many and such daily dangers of this very fragile life. Great earthquakes are reported from the eastern regions. Some great cities have suddenly collapsed. Terrified, those who were at Jerusalem—Jews, pagans, catechumens—all were baptized. Seven thousand people, perhaps, are said to have been baptized. The sign of Christ appeared on the garments of the baptized Jews. These things are reported by the most steadfast account of faithful brothers. The city of Sitifis too was shaken by a most grievous earthquake, so that all, perhaps for five days, remained in the fields, and there nearly two thousand people are said to have been baptized. On every side God terrifies, because he does not wish to find what he may condemn. Something is being done in this winepress. The world is a winepress; its pressures abound. Be oil, not dregs. Let each one turn to God and change his life. The oil has a hidden way, it tends to its own secret places. Another scoffs, mocks, blasphemes, cries out through the streets: the dregs flow off. Yet the Lord of the winepress, through his workmen, through his holy angels, does not cease to work. He knows his own oil, he knows what he receives, by what weight of pressure it is strained out. *For the Lord knows who are his*[^23]. Flee the dregs, they are public and black. *The Lord knows who are his.* Be oil, flee the dregs. Let all who name the name of the Lord withdraw from iniquity. But do not conceive hatreds, or quickly end them. For those things are not to be feared. Do you fear an earthquake? Do you fear the roaring of the sky? Do you fear wars? Fear the fever too. Suddenly, when those great things are feared, they themselves do not come, and from the side one little fever takes a man away. And if that judge finds such a one as he does not know, to whom he is to say, *I do not know you, depart from me*[^24], what will happen afterward? Whither does one go? Through whom is it sought? Whence is life to be redeemed for restoring? Who is permitted to live again and amend what he did wrongly? It is finished. Few of you indeed have gathered, but if you have heard well, you abound. Let him who deceives not deceive you, for he who does not deceive does not deceive you.
