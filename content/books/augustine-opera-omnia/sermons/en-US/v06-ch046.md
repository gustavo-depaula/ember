# Sermon 219: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** The blessed apostle Paul, exhorting us to imitate him, among many other marks of his virtue, says also: *In watchings often*[^1]. How much more eagerly, then, ought we to watch in this vigil, as it were the mother of all holy vigils, in which the whole world watches? Not that world of which it is written: *If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him: for all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age, which is not from the Father*[^2]. For such a world, that is, the children of unbelief, the devils and his angels rule: against whom the same Apostle says that we have a wrestling, where he says: *We have not a wrestling against flesh and blood, but against the principalities and powers, and rulers of this world, of this darkness*[^3]. Which we too once were, *but now light in the Lord*[^4]. By the light of vigils, then, let us resist the rulers of darkness. Not, then, does that world watch in this solemnity: but that of which it is said: *God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, not imputing to them their trespasses*[^5]. Although the celebrity of this vigil is so clear throughout the whole world, that it compels even those to watch in body who in heart — I will not say sleep, but — are buried in hellish impiety. They too watch this night, of which is visibly rendered what was promised so long before: *And the night will be illuminated like the day*[^6]. This happens in the hearts of the pious, to whom it was said: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^7]. This happens also among the rivals of all, both those who see in the Lord, and those who envy the Lord. There watches this night, then, both the enemy world and the reconciled world. This one watches, to praise the physician, being freed: that one watches, to blaspheme the judge, being condemned. This one watches, fervent and shining in pious minds: that one watches, gnashing with its teeth and wasting away. Finally, love permits not this one, iniquity not that one; Christian vigor not this one, devilish envy not that one, to sleep at all in this celebration. Whence by our very enemies, unknowing, we are admonished how we ought to watch for ourselves, if even those who envy us watch on our account. For of those who are in no way marked with the name of Christ, yet this night many for grief, many for shame; some too who draw near to faith, now for the fear of God, do not sleep. This solemnity rouses them for diverse causes. How, then, ought the friend of Christ to watch rejoicing, when even the enemy watches grieving? How may the Christian be kindled to watch in so great a glory of Christ, when the pagan blushes to sleep? How does it befit him who has entered this great house to watch in so great a festival of his, when he already watches who arranges to enter? Let us watch, then, and pray; that both outwardly and inwardly we may celebrate this vigil. Let God speak to us in his readings; let us speak to God in our prayers. If we obediently hear his oracles, he dwells in us whom we entreat.

[^1]: 2 Cor 11:27
[^2]: 1 John 2:15-16
[^3]: Eph 6:12
[^4]: Eph 5:8
[^5]: 2 Cor 5:19
[^6]: Ps 139:12 (138:12)
[^7]: Eph 5:8
