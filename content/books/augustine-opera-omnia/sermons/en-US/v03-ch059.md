# Sermon 92: On the Same Words of the Gospel, Matthew 22:42-46

**1.** The question put to the Jews, Christians must resolve. For the Lord Jesus Christ, who put it to the Jews, did not himself resolve it—not for the Jews, but for us he resolved it. And I will remind your Charity, and you will find that he resolved it. First, see the knot of the question. He asked the Jews what seemed to them about the Christ, whose son he was: because they too hope for the Christ. In the Prophets they read of him, they awaited him to come, they killed him present: because where they read that the Christ would come, there they read that they would kill the Christ. But they hoped for his future coming in the Prophets: for they did not see their own future crime. So, then, he questioned them about the Christ, not as about one unknown, or whose name they had never heard, or whose coming they had never hoped for. For because they still hope for him, therefore they err. We too indeed hope for him; but to come as judge, not to come to be judged. But the holy Prophets prophesied both: that he would come first to be unjustly judged, and would come afterward to judge justly. *What, then,* he says, *seems to you about the Christ? whose son is he? They answered him: David's*[^1]. Right out of the Scriptures. But he: *How does David in the spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I put your enemies as a footstool for your feet? If, then, David in the spirit calls him Lord, how is he his son*[^2]?

**2.** Here we must beware lest Christ be thought to have denied that he is the son of David. He did not deny himself the son of David, but asked the manner. You said he is the son of David, I do not deny it: but he calls him Lord; tell me how he who is also Lord is the son: tell me how. They did not say, but were silent. Let us say it, with Christ himself expounding. Where? Through his Apostle. First, how do we prove that Christ himself expounded it? The Apostle says: *Do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ*[^3]? Therefore in the Apostle he deigned to resolve this question. First, Christ speaking through the Apostle, what did he say to Timothy? *Remember that Jesus Christ rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel*[^4]. Behold, Christ is the son of David. How is he also the Lord of David? Say, Apostle: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*[^5]. Recognize the Lord of David. If you recognize the Lord of David, our Lord, the Lord of heaven and earth, the Lord of the angels, equal to God in the form of God if you recognize: whence the son of David? Attend to what follows. The Apostle shows you the Lord of David by saying: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal to God.* The son of David, whence? *But he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant; made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man: he humbled himself, made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. Wherefore God also exalted him*[^6]. Christ rose of the seed of David, the son of David, because he emptied himself. How did he empty himself? By taking what he was not, not by losing what he was. He emptied himself, he humbled himself. Though he was God, he appeared as man. He was despised, walking on earth, who made heaven. He was despised as a man, as of no power. Not only despised, but moreover even killed. He was a stone lying low, the Jews struck against him, and were shattered. But what does he himself say? *Whoever strikes against this stone will be shattered: but on whomever this stone comes, it will crush him*[^7]. First he lay low, and they struck against him: he will come from above, and will crush the shattered.

**3.** You have received both the son of David, and the Lord of David: the Lord of David always, the son of David in time: the Lord of David born of the substance of the Father, the son of David born of the virgin Mary, conceived by the Holy Spirit. Let us hold both. One of these will be for us an eternal habitation; the other of these is for us liberation from pilgrimage. For unless our Lord Jesus Christ had deigned to become man, man would have perished. He became what he made, that what he made might not perish. True man, true God: God and man, the whole Christ. This is the Catholic faith. He who denies Christ to be God is a Photinian: he who denies Christ to be man is a Manichee. He who confesses Christ to be God equal to the Father, and true man, who truly suffered, truly shed blood: for the truth would not free us, if he gave a false price for us: he who confesses both is a Catholic. He has a homeland, he has a way. He has a homeland: *In the beginning was the Word*[^8]; he has a homeland: *Though he was in the form of God, he did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*[^9]. He has a way: *The Word was made flesh*[^10]; he has a way: *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^11]. He himself is the homeland to which we go, he the way by which we go. Through him let us go to him, and we will not go astray.
