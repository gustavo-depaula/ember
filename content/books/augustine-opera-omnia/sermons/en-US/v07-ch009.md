# Sermon 237: A Discourse Given on Wednesday

**1.** What followed in the Gospel according to Luke concerning the resurrection of the Lord was finished today, where we heard that the Lord appeared in the midst of his disciples as they were debating about his resurrection and not believing. So unexpected and incredible was it to them that even seeing they did not see. For they saw a living body which they had bewailed as dead, they saw standing in their midst him whom they had grieved over as hanging on the cross; they saw, then, and because they did not believe their own eyes that they saw what was true, they thought they were being deceived. *For they supposed*, as you heard, *that they were seeing a spirit*[^1]. What the worst heretics afterward believed about Christ, this the faltering apostles first believed. For there are today those who do not believe that Christ had flesh, because they both destroy the Virgin's childbearing and refuse to believe him born of a woman. *The Word was made flesh*[^2]—they utterly alienate this from their faith, or rather from their unbelief. This whole dispensation of our salvation, that he was made man for the finding of the man whom God had made man; this whole thing, that Christ for the forgiveness of our sins poured out true, not false, blood, and with his own true blood blotted out the bond of our sins—this whole thing the damnable heretics try to empty out; this whole thing, like the Manichees, they do not believe; they say that what appeared to the eyes of men was a spirit, was not flesh.

**2.** See, the Gospel speaks. The Lord stood among his disciples, who did not yet believe that he had risen. They saw him and thought they were seeing a spirit. If there is nothing wrong in believing that Christ was a spirit, not flesh; if there is nothing wrong, let the disciples be left in that opinion. Attend, that you may understand what I wish to say; and may God grant that I may say it, that is, that I may say it as it is good for you to hear it. See, I repeat this very thing. Sometimes those detestable people, detesting the flesh and living according to the flesh, sometimes say this and so deceive: Who believe better about Christ, those who say that he had flesh, or we who say: He was God, he was a spirit, and to the eyes of men not a body but God appeared? Which is better, flesh or spirit? What shall we answer, except that the spirit is better than the flesh? If, then, he says, you confess that the spirit is better than the flesh, I think better about Christ who say that he was a spirit, not flesh. O wretched error! Why do I say that Christ was flesh? You say a spirit, I a spirit and flesh; you do not say better, but you say less. Hear, then, the whole of what I say, that is, what the Catholic faith says, what the most firmly grounded and most serene Truth says. You who say that Christ was only a spirit—which is also our spirit, that is, our soul—you say that Christ was only this. Hear what you say. I say what you say. He himself was a spirit of that nature and substance from which our spirit too is. How much less you say, attend: there was the Word there, there was flesh there. You say: a human spirit only. I say: Word, spirit, body: God and man. If I want to say two things, if I want to name two things, I use this shorthand: God and man. Both true God and true man. Nothing false in the humanity, nothing false in the divinity: God and man. But if you ask me about the man himself, again I say two things, a human soul and human flesh. You are a man because of soul and flesh; he is Christ because of God and man. See what I say.

**3.** But you think you say better, because you say: He was a spirit, a spirit appeared, a spirit was seen, a spirit dwelt among men. This you say. This I have said: this the disciples too thought. If you say nothing wrong, if what you say is good, then what the disciples thought was good too. If the Lord left them so, that they should think this, you too are to be left so. What you believe, this they believed. Is what you believe good? Then what they believed was good. But it was not good. The Lord said to them: *Why are you troubled?* Trouble believed what you believe; what? *they believed they were seeing a spirit.* And the Lord to this: *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart?* These thoughts are earthly. For if they were heavenly, they would descend into your heart, not arise. For why is it said to us: *Lift up your heart*, except that earthly thoughts may not find our heart before themselves, since we have set it on high? Therefore: *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet, handle and see*[^3]. It is too little for you to look, put forth your hands; if it is too little to look and it does not suffice to touch, handle. Nor did he say only *touch*, but *handle* and feel all over. Let your hands test for you, whether your eyes are lying. *Handle and see*, have your eyes in your hands. Why handle and see? Why? *Because a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^4]. You were erring with the disciples, be corrected with the disciples. It is human, I grant. You thought Christ a spirit; this Peter too, this the others too: *They thought they were seeing a spirit*; but they did not remain in this error. That you may know that this was utterly false in their hearts, the physician did not leave them so. He came near, he applied the medicine, he saw the wounds in their hearts, and the very means by which he would heal the wounds of hearts, he bore as scars in his body.

**4.** So, then, let us believe. I know that you do believe so. But lest perhaps in this field of the Lord there be some bad weed, I address even those whom I do not see. Let no one believe about Christ anything but what Christ wished to be believed about himself; and how it is good for us to believe what he wished to be believed about himself, who redeemed us, who sought our salvation, who poured out his blood for us, who bore for us what was not owed by him, who brought to us what was not owed to us: let us believe this. Christ, what is he? The Son of God, the Word of God. What is the Word of God? What the word of man cannot speak, that is the Word of God. You ask me what the Word of God is? If I wished to tell you what the word of man is, I cannot explain it, I grow weary, I hesitate, I give way; I cannot explain the force of the human word, how much less of the Word of God. See, before I say to you what I wish to say, the word is already in my heart; it has not yet been spoken by me and is with me; it is spoken by me and reaches you and does not depart from me. You are intent on hearing the word from me; I feed your minds when I speak. You would divide the food among yourselves, if I brought it to your stomachs, nor would it all reach each one; but the more of you there were, into the more pieces you would divide what I set down, and the less each would receive, the greater the number of recipients. But now I have brought food to minds; I say: Receive, take, eat. You have received, you have eaten, and you do not divide. Whatever I speak is both whole to all and whole to each. See how the force of the word of man cannot be sufficiently explained, and you say to me: What is the Word of God? The Word of God feeds so many thousands of angels. For they are fed by mind, they are filled by mind. He fills the angels, he fills the world, he fills the Virgin's womb, and neither ranges abroad there nor is straitened here. What is the Word of God? Let him say it himself, the only-begotten, the only Son, let him say what is the Word of God. He says of himself briefly, but great is what he says: *I and the Father are one*[^5]. Do not count words about the one Word. Surely all the words of men that can be cannot, in explaining, explain the one Word. *The Word*, then, which cannot be explained, *was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^6]; he took up, as it were, a whole and full man, the soul and body of a man. And if you wish to hear something more precisely—because a beast too has soul and flesh; when I say a human soul and human flesh, he took up a whole human soul. For there were those who made a heresy from this and said that the soul of Christ did not have mind, did not have understanding, did not have reason, but the Word of God was to it in place of mind, in place of understanding, in place of reason. I do not want you to believe so. He who created the whole redeemed the whole; the Word took up the whole, freed the whole. There is the mind and understanding of man, there is the soul giving life to the flesh, there is true and whole flesh, only sin is not there.

[^1]: Luke 24:37
[^2]: John 1:14
[^3]: Luke 24:38-39
[^4]: Luke 24:39
[^5]: John 10:30
