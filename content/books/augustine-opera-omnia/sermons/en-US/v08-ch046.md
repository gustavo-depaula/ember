# Sermon 304: On the Feast of the Martyr Lawrence

**1. 1.** The Roman Church commends to us today the triumphal day of blessed Lawrence, on which he trampled the world that raged, spurned the world that flattered, and in both conquered the devil who persecuted. For how glorious, and with how great a multitude of virtues, as it were with a variety of flowers, the crown of the martyr Lawrence is adorned, all Rome is witness. For in that Church, as you are wont to hear, he held the office of deacon. There he ministered the sacred blood of Christ: there for the name of Christ he poured out his own blood. He had wisely come to the table of the mighty one. To that table, of which just now the proverbs of Solomon were speaking to us, where it is written: *If you sit down to dine at the table of a mighty one, consider carefully what is set before you; and so stretch out your hand, knowing that you ought to prepare the like*[^1]. The mystery of this dinner the blessed apostle John clearly set forth, saying: *As Christ laid down his soul for us, so we too ought to lay down our souls for our brethren*[^2]. This Saint Lawrence understood, brethren; he understood, and he did it: and indeed he prepared such things as he had taken at that table. He loved Christ in his life, he imitated him in his death.

**2. 2.** And so we too, brethren, if we truly love, let us imitate. For we cannot render a better fruit of love than the example of imitation; *for Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow his footsteps*[^3]. In this saying the apostle Peter seems to have seen that Christ suffered only for those who follow his footsteps, and that the suffering of Christ profits nothing except those who follow his footsteps. The holy martyrs followed him, even to the shedding of their blood, even to the likeness of his suffering: the martyrs followed, but not they alone. For the bridge was not cut after they passed over; nor was the fountain itself dried up after they drank. For what is the hope of the good faithful, who either under the bond of marriage bear the yoke of matrimony chastely and in concord, or under widowed continence tame the enticements of the flesh, or even, raising themselves more loftily to the summit of holiness and flourishing in new virginity, follow the Lamb wherever he goes? What hope, I say, is there for these, what for us all, if none follow Christ except those who pour out their blood for him? Is mother Church then going to lose her sons, whom she has borne the more fruitfully, the more securely, in time of peace? Must persecution be prayed for, must temptation be prayed for, lest she lose them? Far be it, brethren. For how can one pray for persecution, who daily cries: *Lead us not into temptation?*[^4]

**3. 2.** That garden of the Lord has, brethren, it has, it has not only the roses of martyrs, but also the lilies of virgins, and the ivy of the married, and the violets of widows. Altogether, dearly beloved, let no kind of men despair of their vocation: Christ suffered for all. Truly it was written of him: *Who wishes all men to be saved, and to come to the knowledge of the truth*[^5].

**3. 3.** Let us understand, then, apart from the shedding of blood, apart from the peril of suffering, how a Christian ought to follow Christ. The apostle says, speaking of the Lord Christ: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^6]. How great a majesty! *But he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man*[^7]. How great a humility! Christ humbled himself: you have, Christian, what to hold to. Christ *was made obedient*: why are you proud? How far was Christ made obedient? Even to the incarnation of the Word, even to the sharing of human mortality, even to the threefold temptation of the devil, even to the mockery of the Jewish people, even to the spittings and bonds, even to the blows and scourges; if that is little, *even to death*; and if something is still to be added even about the kind of death, *even the death of the cross*[^8]. We have such an example of humility, a medicine for pride.

**4. 3.** Why then do you swell, O man? O dead skin, why are you stretched? O fetid corruption, why are you puffed up? You pant, you grieve, you seethe, because someone or other has done you a wrong. From this you demand vengeance, you thirst with parched throat for revenge; nor do you cease from your intent until you are avenged on the one who injured you. If you are a Christian, await your king: let Christ first be avenged. For he has not yet been avenged, who suffered such great things for you. And surely that majesty could either suffer nothing, or be avenged at once. But since there was in him so great a power, therefore there was also so great a patience: because *he suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow his footsteps*[^9]. You see surely, dearly beloved, that apart from the shedding of blood, apart from bonds and prisons, apart from scourges and hooks, there are many things in which we can follow Christ. Then, this humility run through, and death laid low, Christ ascended into heaven: let us follow him. Let us hear the apostle saying: *If you have risen with Christ, savor the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things on earth*[^10]. Whatever delightful thing among temporal things the world brings forward, let it be spurned: whatever harsh and terrible thing roars, let it be despised. And he who acts thus, let him not doubt that he cleaves to the footsteps of Christ, so that he may deservedly dare to say with the apostle Paul: *Our conversation is in heaven*[^11].

**5. 4.** But there can be unconquered virtue in these things only if love is not feigned. So he gives us true virtue who pours love into our hearts[^12]. And when would blessed Lawrence not have feared the fires applied from without, unless within the flame of love had been burning? Therefore, my brethren, the glorious martyr did not dread the fierce flames of the burnings in his body, because he was on fire in his mind with the most ardent longing for heavenly joys. In comparison with the fervor with which his breast burned, the outer flame of the persecutors was cold. For when would he bear the stings of such great pains, unless he loved the joys of eternal rewards? Finally, when would he despise this life, unless by loving a better life? *And who can harm you*, says the apostle Peter, *who*, he says, *can harm you, if you are lovers of good?*[^13] Let the persecutor do you evil though he may: do you not fail in loving good. For if you truly love with your whole heart what is good, you will bear every evil patiently and with an even mind. For what did those torments, which were brought upon him by the persecutors, harm blessed Lawrence, except that they made him more illustrious by the very punishments, and made for us this most festive day from his precious death?
