# Sermon 84: On the Words of the Gospel, Matthew 19:17: "If You Wish to Come to Life, Keep the Commandments"

**1.** The Lord said to a certain young man: *If you wish to come to life, keep the commandments*[^1]. He did not say: If you wish to come to eternal life; but: *If you wish to come to life,* defining as life that which would be eternal life. Let us, then, first commend the love of this life. For even a life of whatever sort is loved; and that life, of whatever sort, full of toil and miserable, people are afraid and dread to end. From this it must be seen, from this it must be considered, how eternal life ought to be loved, when this miserable life, destined someday to end, is loved so. Consider, brothers, how much that life ought to be loved where you never end life. You love, then, this life, where you toil so much, run, are busy, pant; and the things that are necessary in this miserable life are hardly to be counted: to sow, to plow, to plant vines, to sail, to grind, to cook, to weave—and after all these, you are to end life. Behold what you suffer in this miserable life that you love; and do you think you will live forever and never die? Temples, stones, marbles, made solid with iron and lead, nevertheless fall; and does a man never think he will die? Learn, then, brothers, to seek eternal life, where you will not endure these things, but will reign forever with God. For *he who wants life,* as the prophet says, *loves to see good days*[^2]. For in evil days death is rather to be wished for than life. Do we not hear and see people, when they are set in some tribulations and straits, conflicts and sicknesses, and see themselves toiling, saying nothing else but, God, send me death, hasten my days? And sometimes the sickness comes; there is running about, physicians are brought, solid pledges and gifts are promised. Death itself says to you: Behold I am present, whom a little while ago you were asking of the Lord; why do you now want to flee me? I have found you a liar, and a lover of a miserable life.

**2.** But of these days that we spend, the Apostle says: *Redeeming the time, for the days are evil*[^3]. Are not the days evil that we spend in the corruption of this flesh, in so great—or under so great—a burden of a corruptible body, amid so many temptations, amid so many difficulties, where there is false pleasure, no security of joy, tormenting fear, greedy desire, parched sorrow? Behold how evil the days are; and no one wants to end those very evil days, and people greatly entreat God for this, that they may live long. But what is it to live long, except to be tormented long? What else is it to live long than to add evil days to evil days? And when boys grow up, days seem to be added to them; and they do not know that the days are being lessened; and that very reckoning is false. For with those growing up, days rather depart than are added. Set, for example, eighty years for some man who is born: whatever he lives, he subtracts from the sum. And foolish people congratulate themselves on very many birthdays, both their own and their sons'. O prudent man! If wine were lessened for you in the skin, you would be saddened; you lose days, and you rejoice? Evil, then, are the days; and the worse, because they are loved. So this world flatters, that no one wants to end this toilsome life. For the true and blessed life is this: when we shall rise again and reign with Christ. For the ungodly too will rise again, but to go into the fire. So there is no life except the blessed life. And the blessed life cannot be except eternal, where there are good days; not many, but one. From the custom of this life they are called days. That day knows no rising, knows no setting. To that day no tomorrow succeeds, for no yesterday precedes it. This day, or these days, and this life, and the true life we have in the promises. So it is the reward of some work. For if we love the reward, let us not fail in the work; and we will reign with Christ forever.

[^1]: Mt 19:17
[^2]: Ps 34:12 (33:13)
[^3]: Eph 5:16
