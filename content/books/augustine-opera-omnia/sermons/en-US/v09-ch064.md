# Sermon 340/A: On the Ordination of a Bishop

**1.** Today, since God deigned to lead us to you, a third sermon is rendered to Your Charity; but in the past two days you heard that which especially pertains to you; for today indeed a bishop is ordained for you by the gift and mercy of God; so we ought to say this, by which we may both exhort ourselves, and inform him, and instruct you. For he who is over a people ought first to understand that he is the servant of many. And let him not disdain this: let him not, I say, disdain to be the servant of many; because the Lord of lords did not disdain to serve us. For from the carnal dregs there had crept upon the Apostles, the disciples of the Lord Jesus Christ, a certain appetite for loftiness, and the smoke of pride had begun to go into their eyes. For, as we read written in the Gospel: *There arose a contention among them, which of them was the greater*[^1]. But the Lord, the physician present, repressed their swelling. And when he saw from what vice that contention had come, he said to them, and setting little children before him: *Unless one becomes like this child, he will not enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^2]. In the child he commended humility. For he did not want his own to have such minds as children have, when the Apostle says in another place: *Do not become children in your minds.* And he added: *But be infants in malice, that you may be perfect in your senses*[^3]. But pride is a great malice, and the first malice, the beginning and origin, the cause of all sins; it cast down the angel, and made him the devil. The cup of pride that fallen one offered too to the man standing: he raised into pride him who was made to the image of God; now unworthy too, because proud. He envied, and persuaded him to despise the law of God, and to enjoy his own power. And how did he persuade him? *If you eat,* he says, *you will be like gods*[^4]. See, then, whether pride did not persuade him. He had been made a man, he wanted to be a god: he took on what he was not, he lost what he was; not that he lost human nature, but he lost blessedness both present and future. He lost that by which he was to be lifted up, deceived by him who had been cast down from there.

**2.** Therefore the apostle Paul, when among the other episcopal virtues he recalled, in that reading which we just heard read, this too he added: *Not a neophyte,* as if new in the faith, *lest, lifted up in pride, he fall into the judgment of the devil*[^5]. What is: *fall into the judgment of the devil?* Not to be judged by the devil, but to be condemned with the devil; for the devil will not be our judge; but because he himself fell through pride, and on account of pride, made impious, will be condemned with everlasting fire. Let him beware, he says, to whom a lofty place is given in the Church, lest, lifted up in pride, he fall into the judgment into which the devil fell. So the Lord, addressing the Apostles, and confirming them in holy humility, after he set out the example from the child, said to them: *Whoever wants to be greater among you, will be your servant*[^6]. Behold, I have done no injury to my brother, your future bishop, because I wanted him to be and admonished him to be your servant. For if I did it to him, I did it first to myself; for I speak not as just anyone about a bishop, but as a bishop; and what I admonish, I myself fear, and recall to mind what the holy Apostle himself said: *I do not so run, as if at an uncertain mark: I do not so fight, as one beating the air; but I chastise my body, and bring it into servitude, lest perhaps, preaching to others, I myself be found a reprobate*[^7].

**3.** So, that you may hear it briefly, we are your servants: your servants, but also your fellow servants; we are your servants, but we all have one Lord; we are your servants, but in Jesus, as the Apostle says: *But we ourselves your servants through Jesus*[^8]. Through him we are servants, through whom we are also free; for he himself says to those who believe in him: *If the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed*[^9]. Shall I, then, hesitate to become a servant through him, through whom, unless I became free, I would remain in a ruinous servitude? We are set over you, and we are servants; we are over you, but only if we profit you. In what, then, the bishop set over you is a servant, let us see. In that in which the Lord himself is too. For when he said to his Apostles: *Whoever wants to be greater among you, will be your servant,* lest human pride should be indignant at the servile name, he at once consoled them, and, setting himself forth as an example, exhorted them to what he had commanded. *Whoever among you wants to be greater, will be your servant.* But see how: *As the Son of man came not to be ministered to, but to minister*[^10]. Let us ask what he ministered. If we attend to bodily ministrations, we see the disciples ministering to him; but he was sending them, to buy food, to prepare food. Finally it is written in the Gospel, when the day of his passion drew near, his disciples said to him: *Lord, where do you want us to prepare for you to eat the Passover?*[^11] And he commands where it is to be prepared: and they go, and prepare, and minister. What, then, is it that he says: *As the Son of man came not to be ministered to, but to minister?* Hear what follows: *He came not,* he says, *to be ministered to, but to minister, and to give his soul a redemption for many*[^12]. Behold how the Lord ministered: behold what sort of servants he commanded us to be. He gave his soul a redemption for many: he redeemed us. Who of us is fit to redeem anyone? Indeed by his blood, by his death we were redeemed from death, by his humility we who were lying down were raised up; but we too ought to confer our little portions on his members, because we have been made his members: he is the head, we are the body. Finally the apostle John, exhorting us in his epistle to the example of the Lord, who had said: He will be your servant, whoever among you wants to be greater; *as the Son of man came not to be ministered to, but to minister,* and to give his soul a redemption for many; exhorting us, then, to the likeness, says: *Christ laid down his soul for us: so we too ought to lay down our souls for the brothers*[^13]. The Lord himself too, speaking after the resurrection: *Peter, do you love me?* And he answered: *I love you.* This he said three times, this he answered three times; and the whole three times the Lord: *Feed my sheep*[^14]. Where do you show me that you love me, except by feeding my sheep? What are you going to bestow on me by loving me, when you expect all things from me? What, then, you may do by loving me, you have: *Feed my sheep.* This once, and again, and a third time. *Do you love me? I love you. Feed my sheep.* For three times he had denied through fear: three times he confessed through love. Then, when the Lord had commended his sheep to him a third time, to him answering and confessing love, and condemning and blotting out fear, he at once added: *When you were young, you girded yourself, and went where you wanted; but when you have become older, another will gird you, and carry you where you do not want. But this he said signifying by what death he was to glorify God*[^15]. He proclaimed to him his cross, foretold to him his passion. So, going there, the Lord says: *Feed my sheep;* suffer for my sheep.

**4.** Such ought the good bishop to be; if he will not be such, he will not be a bishop. What does it profit a wretched man, if he is called Felix (Happy)? If you see some wretched, troubled man, who is called Felix, and you say to him: Come here, Felix, go, Felix, rise, Felix, sit, Felix, and he, amid all these names, is always unhappy; something like this it is, when one is called a bishop, and is not. What does the honor of the name confer on him, but an increase of guilt? But who is the one who is called a bishop, and is not? He who rejoices in the honor more than in the salvation of the flock of God, who in this loftiness of ministry seeks his own things, not the things of Jesus Christ; he is called a bishop, but is not a bishop, the name is empty for him. And you see men speaking nothing else. Did you see the bishop? did you greet the bishop? Where do you come from? from the bishop. Where are you going? to the bishop. So that he may be what he is called, let him hear, not me, but with me; let us hear together, together let us as fellow disciples in one school learn from one master, Christ, whose chair is in heaven for this reason, because first it was a cross on earth. He taught the way of humility: descending to ascend, visiting those who lay in the depths, and lifting up those who wanted to cleave to him.

**5.** Finally, perceive it most evidently by hearing. Two of his disciples, brothers, the sons of Zebedee, John and James, beyond the rest desired his loftiness, and through their mother, because they themselves were ashamed, they said to him, sending her in, that she might speak their desires: *Lord,* she said, *grant that in your kingdom one son of mine may sit at your right hand, the other at the left*[^16]. And the Lord to them, not to her: *You do not know what you ask.* And he added: *Can you drink the cup which I am to drink?*[^17] What cup, but that of which he says, drawing near to his passion: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me?*[^18] *Can you,* he says, *drink the cup which I am to drink?* And they at once, greedy for loftiness, forgetful of weakness: *We can,* they said. And he: *My cup indeed you will drink: but to sit at my right hand or at my left is not mine to give to you; it is prepared for others by my Father*[^19]. For whom is it prepared, if not prepared for the disciples? Who will sit there, if the Apostles will not sit? It is prepared for others, it is not prepared for you; for others, not for the proud. And well does he too set forth humility, when he said: It is prepared *for others by my Father;* when surely he himself was preparing it, he said it was prepared by his Father; lest here too he should seem arrogant, and should not build up to humility, for the sake of which he was saying all these things. For the Father does not prepare what the Son does not prepare, nor does the Son prepare what the Father does not prepare, since he himself says: *I and the Father are one*[^20]; he himself also says: *Whatever things the Father does, these same the Son likewise does*[^21]. The teacher of humility in word and work: for in word, always from the beginning of the creation, he never was silent, through angels, through prophets, to teach man humility; he deigned to teach it also by his own example. Our Creator came humble, created among us: he who made us, who was made for our sake: God before the ages, man in time, that he might free man from time. The great physician came to heal our swelling. From the East to the West the human race lay like a great sick man, and required a great physician; first this physician sent his servants, and afterward he himself came, when by some he was despaired of. As a physician too, when he sends his servants, as if to do something easy; but when there is a great danger, he himself comes; so the human race labored in great danger, wrapped in all vices, especially flowing from the fountain of pride: and therefore he came to cure that very pride by his own example. Blush still to be proud, O man, for whose sake God was humbled. God would have humbled himself much, if he were only born for your sake; he deigned even to die for you. So he was here on the cross in the man, when the persecuting Jews shook their heads before the cross, and said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross, and we will believe him*[^22]. But he kept his humility, therefore he did not come down; he had not lost his power, but was demonstrating his patience. For consider his very effect and power, and see how easily he could come down from the cross, who could rise from the tomb. But humility was for you, patience was for you; if it was not to be demonstrated, it was not to be commanded; but if it was to be commanded by word, it was to be demonstrated and commended by example. This, then, let us attend to in the Lord: let us see his humility, let us drink the cup of his humility, let us press ourselves close to him, let us meditate on him. It is easy to think of loftiness, it is easy to rejoice in honors, it is easy to lend the ears to flatterers and adulators. To bear reviling, to hear reproach patiently, to pray for the insolent, this is the cup of the Lord, this is the banquet of the Lord. *Are you invited by a greater? Consider that you ought to prepare such things*[^23].

**6.** The Apostle, describing a bishop, set this first: *He who desires the episcopate, desires a good work*[^24]. What is this? As though he inflamed all to desire the episcopate, and the ambitious will be better than the modest, and those usurping for themselves with a certain arrogance things not even owed to them, will be better than those who avoid even owed things through fear? Far be this, this is not so; he did not teach this, that we should be ambitious to attain the episcopate. But hear what he said, if I can explain what I feel. The Apostle's sense is clear to those who understand, but to the proud and ambitious cloudy and obscure. So the Apostle says this: *He who desires the episcopate, desires a good work.* It is not to desire the episcopate, to desire the episcopate: it is to desire a good work. But does he want to be a bishop, who does not do a good work, but his own work? That man does not desire the episcopate. This is what I was saying a little before: he seeks the name, not the reality. I want to be a bishop: oh, if I were a bishop! Would that you were! Do you seek the name, or the reality? If you seek the reality, you desire a good work; if you seek the name, you can have it even in a bad work, but with a worse punishment. What, then, shall we say? are there bad bishops? Far be it, there are not: plainly I dare to say, there are no bad bishops; because if they are bad, they are not bishops. You again call me back to the name, and say: He is a bishop, for he sits in the chair. There is even a straw-watchman in the vineyard.

**7.** He said among other things: *The husband of one wife*[^25]; but how much better of none? He said how far one must go, that it be not more than from one; but much better, if not even one. *Having children that obey*[^26]: that, if he has them, he have them obedient; not that he should try to have them, if he does not have them. For he commended discipline over children, for the sake of ruling the house: *For he who does not know how to be over his own house, how will he have diligence for the Church of God?*[^27] They are the words of the Apostle himself. And how will a bishop be without children, if he is a good bishop? Finally your bishop, in the name of Christ, helped by the grace of Christ, refused to have carnal children, that he might have spiritual ones. It pertains to you to obey him worthily, to obey worthily, to serve him with worthy service; and he will have obedient children, for few so many, for earthly heavenly ones, for heirs coheirs.

**8.** We have spoken of good bishops, and of bad bishops; we have said what we ought to be, and what we should avoid being. But what is this to you, O people of God? Something to you too. For we want you to be built on the rock, to rise a temple to God, to become fit for receiving God, that your hope not fluctuate in the uncertain, but be placed on the solid. Whatever sort we are, you ought to be secure. Indeed it is good for us that we ought to be good bishops set over you, not only to be called so, this is good for us; for to such a great reward is promised. But if we will not be such, but bad, which far be it, and seek our honors for our own sake, neglect the commands of God, count your salvation as nothing, greater punishments await us, than the rewards which are promised. But far be it from us, and pray for us; for the higher the place we are in, the greater the danger we are in. For we think what account we render of the obedience of men, what of the cursings of men. Many obey us, many detract from us, and curse us. Those who obey make us run more danger than those who curse; for the obedience of men titillates our pride, the cursings of men exercise our patience: in one place I fear a fall, in another I fortify the foundation. For: *The reproaches of men,* says one of the servants of God to me, *do not fear*[^28]. The Lord Jesus Christ too says: *You will be blessed, when men curse you, and say every evil against you, lying, for my sake*[^29]. For if those who curse also speak true things, they do not speak evil, because they speak true things; but they speak evil who speak false things. But what did the Lord promise us? *Rejoice and exult, because your reward is abundant in heaven*[^30]. My curser increases my reward: my flatterer wants to lessen my reward. But what shall I say, brothers? That our reward may be increased, ought we to wish that you be cursers? We do not want our reward increased by your evil. Be blessers, obey; let us be in danger, and you not be lessened. What then, if a people falls in with a bad bishop? The Lord and the bishop of bishops made you secure, that your hope be not in man. Behold in the name of the Lord I speak to you a bishop; what sort I am, I do not know: how much less you? For what I am at this hour, I can somehow perceive: what I will be at some time, how do I know? As Peter presumed, and Peter was shown to himself; the sick man did not know himself, but he was not hidden from the physician. He spoke, presumed, dared to promise: *With you even to death. I will lay down my soul for you.* And that physician, inspecting the vein of his heart: *You will lay down your soul,* he says, *for me? Amen I say to you, before the cock crows, you will deny me three times*[^31].

**9.** So may the Lord grant, your prayers helping us, both that we be this, and that we persevere to the end in being this, which you want us to be, all of you who wish us well, and which he wants us to be, who called us and commanded us; may he help us to fulfill what he commanded. But whatever sort we are, let your hope not be in us. I derogate from myself, that I as a bishop say this; I want to rejoice over you, not to be puffed up. Whomsoever at all I find placing hope in me, I do not congratulate him; he is to be corrected, not made firm; he is to be changed, not stabilized. If I cannot admonish him, I grieve; but if I can admonish him, I no longer grieve. As now in the name of Christ I speak to the people of God, I speak in the Church of God, I speak as a servant of God of whatever sort: let your hope not be in us, let your hope not be in men. If we are good, we are ministers; if we are bad, we are ministers. But the good are faithful ministers, truly ministers. What we minister, attend to; if you hunger, and do not want to be ungrateful, mark from whose storeroom it is brought forth. In what sort of little vessel it is set before you, what you are eager to eat, let it not pertain to you. *In a great house* of a householder, *there are not only vessels of gold and silver, but also of earthenware*[^32]. There is a silver vessel, a golden vessel, an earthen vessel; do you attend to whether it has bread, and whose this bread is, by whose gift it is ministered. Attend to him of whom I speak, by whose gift this bread is ministered. He himself is the bread: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^33]. So we minister to you Christ for Christ, himself under himself; that he may reach you, that he may be the judge of our ministry. For if a bishop is a thief, he will never say to you from this seat: Commit theft; but he will say to you only: Do not commit theft. For this he receives from the storeroom of the Lord. If he wants to say something else, you spurn him, and say: This is not from the storeroom of the Lord, you are telling me your own things. *He who speaks a lie, speaks of his own*[^34]. So let him say to you according to God: Do not commit theft, nor adultery, nor murder; let him say to you according to God, that you fear, that you not be exalted, that you turn from the love of the world, that you place your hope in the Lord. These things let him say to you according to God. If he himself does not do them, what is it to you? The Lord your God is Christ, he has made you secure. *The scribes,* he says, *and Pharisees,* in the image of those set over you, *have sat in the chair of Moses; what they say, do: but what they do, do not do; for they say, and do not do*[^35]. What will you say to this? whence will you excuse yourself in the judgment of Christ? Will you say: Therefore I did evil, because I saw my bishop not living well? It is answered you: You chose for yourself one with whom you would be condemned, not one with whom you would be freed. You imitated him living badly; why did you rather imitate him, and not hear me through him? For did I not say to you in my Gospel, that, when you see bad superiors, you should do what they say, but not do what they do? You would hear me through them, and not perish through them.

**10.** If, then, even the bad can say good things, let us now answer Christ too, and say, for the sake of learning, not of despising or harassing: Lord, if the bad can say good things—whence you admonished, and commanded, saying: *What they say, do, but what they do, do not do*—if, then, the bad can say good things, how do you say in another place: *Hypocrites, you cannot speak good things, since you are bad?*[^36] Attend to a thing bound, until by his help you see it loosed. Again I set out the question. Christ says: *What they say, do: but what they do, do not do; for they say, and do not do.* What then, but that they say good things, and do bad things? And therefore what they say, we ought to do; what they do, we ought not to do. In another place: *Surely they do not gather grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles? Every tree is known by its fruit*[^37]. What then, how shall we obey? how shall we understand? Behold they are briars, they are thorns. Do it. You bid me gather a grape from thorns; in one place you bid, in another you forbid: how shall I obey? Hear, understand. When I say: *What they say do, but what they do do not do,* attend first to what I say: *They have sat in the chair of Moses,* I said. When they say good things, not they themselves say it, but the chair of Moses. He put "chair" for doctrine: not that the chair speaks, but the doctrine of Moses; it is in their memory, it is not in their works. But when they themselves say it, when they themselves speak, that is, when they speak of their own, what do they hear? *How can you speak good things, since you are bad?* For attend to another likeness. From thorns do not pluck a grape: for a grape can never be born of thorns. But have you not noticed the branch of a vine going by growth into a hedge, and wrapping itself in thorns, and putting forth a shoot among the thorns, sending down a cluster? You are hungry, and you pass by, and you see a cluster hanging among the thorns; you do not do it, you do not pluck it. You are hungry, and you want to pluck it; pluck it, stretch out your hand diligently and cautiously; beware the thorns, pluck the fruit. So too when a most wicked or bad man speaks to you the doctrine of Christ, hear it, receive it, do not despise it. If he is a bad man, the thorns are his; if he says good things, that is the cluster hanging among the thorns, it is not born of thorns. So, if you are hungry, pluck it, but watch the thorns. For if you begin to imitate his deeds, while you gladly hear him, you have incautiously stretched out your hand; you ran into the thorns before you reached the fruit; you come away wounded, you come away torn; the fruit, which is of the grape, no longer profits you, but the thorns, which are of its own root, harm you. For, lest you be deceived, attend to where you plucked the fruit: there is the branch. Direct your eyes to the branch, and see that it pertains to the vine, goes out of the vine, proceeds from the vine, but runs into the thorns. Surely, then, ought the vine to draw in its branches? So too the doctrine of Christ, by growing and proceeding, has inserted itself into good trees, has inserted itself into bad thorns; it is said by the good, it is said by the bad. Do you see from where the fruit is, from where is born what feeds you, and from where is born what pricks you; they are mingled in the public face, but distinguished in the root.

**11.** But attend to this, my brothers, that we may say something too of our sharper grief, attend to this, why our brothers separated themselves from us. Let them tell us, why? The bishops were bad. They sat in their chairs, they sat in the chairs of Christ, they were in the unity of Christ: one ought not to be separated from the unity. They themselves were bad: you would do what the Lord commanded: *What they say do, but what they do do not do*[^38]. Why did you separate yourself from the chair of Christ? If a pestilent man sat there, you would hear through him, you would not imitate him. And yet can you prove it, when you say: A pestilent man sat there? But I prove you pestilent, who have left the chair of Christ. What you say is hidden; what I say, I prove. Your separation punishes you, your tearing apart punishes you. We were bought together, purchased at one price; the tablets of our price are recited, the instrument of our purchase is the holy Gospel. I am opened, I read. What do I open? what do I read? Where we were bought, where we are brothers and fellow servants, where we are established in unity. For what Christ bought, he did not keep silent, lest someone should take from him his possession, and substitute another's; he plainly did not keep silent about what he bought. Open the tablets, read; the documents are drawn up, he did not buy without writing, he foresaw there would be future slanderers; he plainly did what may be objected to the slanderers. What is read, is believed; but it is read—see by whom it was written, see by whom spoken, by whom taken down. He was speaking, the Apostles were taking it down: they left it written for us. Let us read the instrument, then, brothers: why do we quarrel? What if the tablets of our Lord, our buyer, take away the lawsuit from us? You say the Church of Christ is among the Africans and in Africa; I say the Church of Christ is spread through all nations. Behold where the question is, behold whence the brawl among brothers. You quarrel for the part: you quarrel, that you may remain in the part. I contradict you, that you may possess the whole. Understand a harmonious lawsuit, understand a lawsuit of love. I do not say to you: You are conquered, withdraw. For from the beginning the dividers of the inheritance displeased our Lord Jesus Christ. For when he preached the truth among the peoples, one from the crowd said: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*[^39]. And the Lord, who refused to confirm the division, who had come to make unity—for we heard just now too in the Gospel about that very unity: *I have other sheep, which are not of this fold; I must bring them too, that there may be one flock, and one shepherd*[^40]—the Lord, then, who loved unity, hated division, said to that man: *Tell me, man, who appointed me a divider of the inheritance among* you? I say to you: *Beware of all greed*[^41]. He refused to be a divider of the inheritance: he had come to gather unity, to give one inheritance throughout the lands. Let the instruments of his inheritance be read; let them be read, as I had begun to say. He rose from the dead, showed himself to his disciples, not only to be seen, but also to be touched and handled. *Handle,* he says, *and touch, and see that a spirit does not have bones and flesh as you see me to have*[^42]. For they had thought him to be a spirit, not a body; a phantasm, not the truth. *And while they still marveled for joy, he said to them: Did you not know that, when I was still among you, I said these things to you, that all things must be fulfilled that are written in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms concerning me?*[^43] What is this? what is written of him in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms? Hear: *That Christ must suffer*[^44]. I believe it, he says. Rightly, brothers: attend to the rest. I read the tablets of the Lord, I read the instrument or rather the testament of our inheritance; let us read, let us understand: why do we quarrel? Behold I read, hear the rest: *Christ must suffer.* Do you believe with me? I believe it, he says. *And rise from the dead on the third day.* Do you believe with me? I believe it plainly. Believe the rest too, and the discord is finished. What is that in the rest? *And that repentance and the forgiveness of sins be preached in his name through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^45]. Behold what I read, this is the Church of Christ: *Through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem.* Hold it with me, and you will take away the discord. If you are not in it, you will be in the part. By your loss you conquer, by your gain you are conquered. Acknowledge that you are conquered, and you will hold it with me, spread through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem. The tablets of the Lord are read, the Lord's Gospel speaks; why do you slander me, that I burned those tablets? Who ought to be believed to have burned them: he who obeyed them, or he who despises them? Whoever burned them, from wherever they were brought forth, let us read, let us hear, let us do, let us be in harmony; let us leave the past to the past; let us leave the passing things to those who have passed.

**12.** Their advocate, their defender, in the midst of the proceedings of our conference cried out in his straits: "Neither does a cause prejudge a cause, nor a person a person." Did Caecilian sin? Indeed Caecilian did not sin, but suppose Caecilian sinned; hear at least your own defender. "Neither a cause a cause, nor a person a person." One person would not prejudge one person: and will it prejudge the ends of the earth? Will it prejudge the inheritance of Christ going through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem? Did Caecilian sin: does Christ therefore lie? And yet Caecilian did not sin; but you do not want to be a good Christian. What is it to me about a man? This I was speaking to you, to this I was building you up. My hope is not in Caecilian, I have not placed my hope in man. If Caecilian was good, I will congratulate a good brother; if he was bad, I am not the judge of the hidden things of a brother. So, the honoring of Caecilian and his memory set aside for a little, I appeal to my Lord, I appeal to Christ against my brother. Not as that man did, I do not say to him: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*[^46]; but I say: Lord, tell my brother to hold the inheritance with me. So that I have appealed to the Lord against my brother is not against my brother, but for my brother; I do not want him disinherited, I do not want to possess it alone; for I know that what I possess will not become narrow, if many possess it with me. What I possess is called love, which is the more enlarged by a numerous possessor.
