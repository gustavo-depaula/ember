# Sermon 45

**1** In all the readings we have heard recited—if your Charity has attended to the first reading from the prophet Isaiah (for we can neither remember nor speak of all that was read)—I think the most recent could have remained in your hearts, with which the reader concluded: *But those who are devoted to me will possess the land and inhabit my holy mountain*[^1]. Then the apostolic reading came up and began from there: *Having these promises, then, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting sanctification in the fear of God*[^2]. By the providence of the divine mercy, which rules us and prepares food for us when we hunger—not only for our bodies to be restored, on whose account he makes the sun rise upon the good and the evil and rains upon the just and the unjust, but also on account of the hunger of our heart which we suffer in this desert and die unless manna rains down—the Lord, then, preparing his table for us, with no human industry providing it, but as he himself knew, it came about that the readings so followed one another that in Isaiah something was promised us, but in the Apostle it was said: *Having these promises, then, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting sanctification in the fear of God*, as though it were one reading of the Prophet and the Apostle. For what does the Apostle say? *Having these promises, then, dearly beloved*, and there it is not said which promises—not because they are not there, but because the reader did not begin from there; and as the hearer's mind was, as it were, asking of which promises the Apostle speaks: *Having these promises, then, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit*. For a great task is laid upon us, and no small labor, to cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit. And no one takes up this labor unless he hears the reward of the promise. Since, then, no one takes up the labor of cleansing flesh and spirit unless invited by a reward, somehow it came about that the reader began from the imposition of the labor and did not begin from the promise of the reward; but God, who would not cheat the attentive hearer—if perhaps he hesitated to take up the labor of cleansing flesh and spirit because he had not heard the reward, let him arm himself by the beginning of the apostolic reading. But if he seeks the promises, let him attend to the end of the prophetic reading. For where the prophetic reading ended, there is the promise; whence the reading of the Apostle began, there is the imposition of the work.

**2** Let us, then, rouse ourselves and cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit, having these promises. What promises? *But those who are devoted to me*, says the Lord through Isaiah, *will possess the land and inhabit my holy mountain. Having these promises, then, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit*. Someone will say: "And shall I therefore cleanse myself from defilement of flesh and spirit, that I may possess the land and inhabit the mountain?" Without doubt it must be asked what the possession of the land and the dwelling on the mountain mean, lest perhaps men hope they will receive broad possessions, and not end their desire but defer it—rather, increase it—and despise small things, thinking they will receive greater things of the very same kind. For who would not despise one acre, if it is promised him that he will possess a hundred? Or who would not despise the delights of a single lunch, perhaps poorer and more frugal, if it were said to him: "Unless you restrain yourself, you will not come to that most ample and rich dinner"? Those who so restrain themselves from certain present things do not end their desires. But because their desires are greater, fearing lest they lose what they desire more, they despise the things that are smaller. Yet it is desire itself. Is not a man greedy who despises a hundred coins to acquire a thousand? Do not think he has now ended avarice because you find him despising a hundred coins. He is thinking of a thousand, and therefore despised the hundred. There are men who often wait upon old men who have no sons, and despise many things in them, but hope for greater things from them. Do we think these merciful or greedy? Therefore the sons of the poor are more approved when they wait upon their poor parents, because devotion leads them, not reward. But when the sons of the rich wait upon their parents, devotion is not proved, and if it is there, it lies hidden, because, even if it is seen by God, it cannot be seen by men. So the parents themselves, often thinking ill of their sons, when they think their sons wait upon them for the sake of money—when it sometimes profits their sons to be emancipated from their parents, when perhaps some advantage demands it, one about to take a wife, or to attain some honor, that they should give them their goods—say: "I will not give it, for now he will no longer wait upon me." What a sentence he passed on his son, that he waits upon him for money, not attending to a father's love! If, then, you fear lest, money received, your son should no longer wait upon you, the devotion is not lovable, but venal. How much better the son of a poor man, the son sometimes of a wretched and needy old man, who, expecting nothing from his father because he has nothing to leave him, by labors and the hardship of sweat feeds his father! Sometimes the sons of the rich too, attending to the fear of God, not because they expect anything from their parents, but because they are parents and begot and raised them, and God commanded, saying: *Honor your father and your mother*[^3], therefore wait upon them. But the affection lies hidden where the reward is set forth. But these are the more acceptable to God, because men cannot see their mind, and God alone sees it, nor can they be praised by men, like Job who worshipped God. For the demons thought he worshipped God for the sake of reward. But where was it proved that he worships freely? Because, when all was lost, he said: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done; blessed be the name of the Lord*[^4].

**3** Why have I said these things, brothers? Because Scripture does not cease daily to admonish us, that, despising temporal things, we love eternal things. Every divine page does not cease to speak to us, sometimes openly, sometimes obscurely in a mystery. But let no one think himself cheated when the divine page speaks obscurely. Where the will of God comes forth to you—that is, where it is open—there love it. There love it, when it openly admonishes. And such as it is in the open, such it is in the obscure; such as it is in the sun, such it is in the shade. You will follow it such, if you read it such. For it is obscure, as I said: He will possess the land and inhabit my holy mountain. For if we take it carnally, we will not cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit, and God provided in vain that the end of the prophetic reading be joined to the beginning of the apostolic reading, if for the sake of possessing an earthly mountain we prepare ourselves for avarice, not for devotion. But what ought we to understand by the mountain? It is obscure what it meant by "mountain." But if God had deserted us, he would nowhere say openly what the mountain is. But where he says it openly, there love the mountain. Where he openly commends the mountain to you, and Scripture itself opens up what it means by the mountain, there love it. Even where you hear such a mountain promised, follow it. Such as you loved in the open, understand it in the obscure. Where do we suppose the mountain was named, that we may be able to cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit? What mountain has been promised us?

**4** First let us know what sort of land is promised us, for which the prophet David sighs in a certain place and says: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^5]. Without doubt, then, there is a certain land of the living, because this is the land of the dying. Or if anyone were born in this land not to die, he would not call that the land of the living, except by comparing this land and finding it to be the land of the dying. There is, then, a land of the living. For though it is eternal and heavenly, it is called land because it is possessed, not because it is plowed. For it has a possessor without labor, because this land too exercises its possessor in labor and wearies him in fear. What is said to you? "Rise, plow the land, that you may have whence to live." And willing or unwilling, groaning and sighing, you rise and work, because the sentence on the condemned Adam pursues you: *In the labor of your face you will eat your bread*[^6]. But when all labor and groaning have passed, we shall be in the land of the living. Nothing there is born and grows. Whatever is there is in the same way, is so always. There winter and summer, night and day, do not alternate. Here one sows that there one may reap, if indeed one sows. For who is it that sows here, that he may reap there? He who gives out to the poor. The giving-out to the poor is the sowing on the land. You sow here, and there you will find the harvest. In summer it is not cut to pass away, but it is eaten and abides with joy. For there you are fed full with justice. That land has its bread. What is this bread? The one who came from there to us. *I am the living bread who came down from heaven*[^7]. What is this bread like? *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they will be satisfied*[^8].

**5** We have heard of the bread from that land, let us hear also of the mountain. *They will inhabit*, it says, *my holy mountain*. For I think we find in another holy Scripture that this mountain too is Christ. He who is the bread is himself the mountain. But the bread, because he feeds the Church; the mountain, because the Church is his body. The Church itself is the mountain. And what is the Church itself? The body of Christ. Join to it the head, and one man comes to be. Head and body, one man. Who is the head? This one who was born of the Virgin Mary, who took up mortal flesh without sin, who by the Jews was struck, scourged, despised, crucified, *who was delivered up for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^9]. He is the head of the Church, he is the bread from that land. But what is his body? His wife, that is, the Church. *For the two will be in one flesh. This is a great sacred sign; but I speak of Christ and of the Church*[^10]. So too the Lord in the Gospel, when he spoke of husband and wife: *Therefore now they are not two, but one flesh*[^11]. He willed, then, Christ God and the Church to be one man. There the head, and here the members. He did not want to rise with the members, but before the members, that the members might have what to hope for. But for this reason the head wished to die, that the head might first rise; that the head might first go into heaven, that in their head the other members might have hope, and await fulfilled in themselves what was sent before in the head. For what need was there for Christ to die, the Word of God, through whom all things were made, of whom it is said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. All things were made through him*[^12]? And he is crucified, and mocked, and struck with a spear, and buried. *And all things were made through him*. But because he deigned to be the head of the Church, the Church would despair of itself rising, if it did not see that the head had risen. The head, then, rose, and the head was seen to rise. First it was seen by women, and it was announced to the men. First the women saw the Lord rising, and to the future Evangelists, the Apostles, the Gospel was announced by the women, and through the women Christ was announced to them. For "Gospel" in Latin is "good news." Those who know Greek know what Gospel is. The Gospel, then, is "good news." What good news can we name so great as that our Savior rose? Or what greater thing were they to preach than what the women announced to them? But why did a woman announce the Gospel? Because through a woman death was amended. For the woman who announced death was consoled by the woman who announced life, because the dead woman was the one who handed out death. Through a woman Adam was seduced to fall into death; through a woman Christ was announced, now rising, no more to die. So too we are to rise, and we shall be the holy mountain of God. On this mountain dwells the one who is devoted to God. *But those who are devoted to me will possess the land and inhabit my holy mountain*: that is, they will not depart from the Church. Now let us labor in the Church, afterward we will inherit the Church. For when our everlasting joy is there, we shall be there only as possessors, but we shall not labor.

**6** But let us find this mountain evidently elsewhere, for it seems somewhat obscure. Someone can say: "Where is the Church a mountain? And when is Christ a mountain? And when is the body of Christ a mountain?" Daniel says it most evidently, no one doubts it. Daniel saw a vision; does it now need an expositor? Let your Charity see. Certain words there perhaps need exposition, which, expounded in the name of Christ, will be opened to you. See whether it can be said except of Christ. *I saw*, Daniel said, *and behold, a stone cut from the mountain without hands*[^13]. He did not say this: that stone was without hands, but a stone cut from the mountain without human work. No human hands came to cut the stone from the mountain. Your Charity knows that stones are not cut from a mountain except by human hands coming to them. But that one was cut from the mountain without hands, and came and shattered all the kingdoms of the earth. I do not know whether anyone is set before your eyes except Christ, of whom it is said: *All the kings of the earth will adore him*[^14]. He shattered all the kingdoms of the earth. The proud king wants to have no king before him. Now all kings have Christ as king. He shattered, then, all the kingdoms of the earth, that he might reign. And what does it say? *This stone grew and became a great mountain, so that it filled the whole face of the earth*[^15]. Now I think you recognize Christ. You have heard of the land: *Those who are devoted to me will possess the land*. You have heard also of the mountain: *And they will inhabit my holy mountain. Having these promises, then, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit*. But perhaps you want to know what it is to be cut without hands. For this is set there as it were obscurely. But to some it has already occurred, before I say it. But let them bear some delay on account of others who cannot think of it unless they have heard something from us. What is "without hands"? Without the work of man. And let your Charity attend to this, brothers, that the stone was cut from the mountain, and became a mountain. Cut from the mountain, and it became a mountain by growing. But what sort of mountain did it become? Not such as the one from which it was cut. For of that mountain, from which it was cut, it was not said: *It grew and filled the whole earth*. There are, then, two mountains. The first mountain, the Synagogue, the second, the Church. The first, the people of the Jews; the second mountain, the Christian people. But that the Christian people might become a great mountain, and fill the whole earth, the stone was cut from that mountain, because Christ came from the Jews. Why, then, without hands? Without human work. For Christ, born of a Virgin, was conceived without marital embrace.

**7** Therefore we have this manifest mountain. Let us not set before ourselves mountains, whether such as Giddaba is, or such as any others that are named to us. For sometimes men, taking it carnally—suppose—read: *He will hear him from his holy mountain*[^16]. Well, sometimes it is of a mountain, and it speaks of Christ. And men run to a mountain to pray, as though God hears there. Carnally wise, because they often see clouds clinging to the sides of mountains, they go up into mountains, that they may be near to God. Do you want to touch God with your prayer? Humble yourself. Again, because we said: "Do you want to touch God? Humble yourself," do not take it carnally, going down into underground vaults, to ask God there. Seek neither vaults nor mountain. Have humility in your heart, and God will give you height. He will come to you, and will be with you in your bedroom. Since, then, we have such a mountain as Christ, we have the Church, let us love the Church. This mountain grew and filled the whole world. It is manifest that they are not on this mountain who are in a part and do not hold the whole earth with us. Remember, brothers, that every page arms and fills us against the tongues of men, which we suffer daily. If it had said, "That mountain grew and filled the whole of Africa," would they not say that it was the party of Donatus? It pressed their tongues by growing. It grew so much as to stop the mouths of the wordy. To where did it grow? Through the whole earth. For that mountain, from which it was cut, did not by growing fill the whole earth. For even though the Jews filled the whole earth, because they were conquered and lost their land, they were scattered through the earth by the punishment of their merits, not by the greatness of their increase. But the Lord Christ, the cornerstone, subdued the kingdoms of men, shattered the kingdoms of demons, humbled all kings that he might grow, grew and filled the whole face of the earth. I dare to say: It still grows, there are still places it fills.

**8** Do you, then, love this mountain and prepare yourself to inhabit this mountain forever; and cleanse yourself from all defilement of flesh and spirit, having these promises. What promises? If you want to possess the land and inhabit the holy mountain, cleanse yourself from all defilement of flesh and spirit. What are the defilements of flesh? Let your Charity attend, and this we ought to say. What are the defilements of flesh? Not where a man by chance passes by and touches something either with his foot or even with his face, or if it happens to a man to slip with his foot and so fall that he comes into mud or filth, and his face is defiled. That defilement is easy, as it is usually said: "He washes and goes out, it is nothing." But the defilement of flesh that must be guarded against does not come out here, except from defilement of spirit, and so defiles the flesh. What is the defilement of spirit? Lust. The defilement of flesh? Adultery perpetrated. You have two things. Lust has risen, now the spirit is defiled. It has not yet come to adultery, the flesh is not yet defiled. But what does it profit that the flesh is clean, and the inhabitant of the flesh is unclean? He who is perhaps clean in the flesh, an adulterer when God holds him in the heart, not yet a husband, as the Lord says: *Amen I say to you, whoever has looked at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart*[^17]. This is the defilement of spirit. But when does the perfect sanctification come about? When it is both of flesh and of spirit. For there are men who restrain themselves from deeds and do not restrain themselves from evil thoughts. They make the cleansing of flesh, and do not make the cleansing of spirit. Such men do not act for this reason, that they fear men. Lust burns, fear calls back. What do you fear? To be found out and condemned, to be found out and made a public show. Therefore the flesh seems undefiled, but the sanctification is not perfect, because what does the Apostle say? *Let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit*, that as you restrain yourself from evil deeds, so you may restrain yourself from evil will, from evil thoughts. Restrain yourself from the evil deed, and you cleanse yourself from defilement of flesh; abstain from the evil will, and you cleanse yourself from defilement of spirit.

**9** And it follows: *Perfecting sanctification in the fear of God*[^18]. Magnificently he added: *In the fear of God*. See, that a man does not perfect sanctification except in the fear of God. What is perfect sanctification? Both of body and of spirit. If it is of body and is not of spirit, it is imperfect. For sanctification cannot be of spirit and not be of body. To be of body and not be of spirit is possible. To be of spirit and not be of body is not possible. For he who is clean in spirit cannot work disgraceful deeds. Whence this? *For from the heart proceed*, says the Lord, *adulteries, murders*[^19]. For a man cannot perpetrate through the members what he has not said to himself in the heart. He conceived the word in the heart, and it was reckoned as the deed. Therefore in a certain place the Lord: *Cleanse the things that are within*, he says, *and the things that are without will be clean*[^20]. He did not say: Cleanse the things that are without. If he had begun from the body, it would remain for him to admonish us to cleanse the mind too; but if from the mind, there is no need that we cleanse the body, because the cleanness of the mind is followed by the cleanness of the body. Therefore the apostle Paul, because he began from the flesh, needed to say something of the spirit too: *Perfecting sanctification. Let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit*, because the flesh can be undefiled, not doing adulteries, fornications, and such things; but yet there can be lusts and thoughts and evil wills in the soul. And he added: *Perfecting sanctification in the fear of God*. Who, then, makes the sanctification of body, and does not make that of soul? He who fears men, and not God. For he who is in the fear of God perfects sanctification. Surely you refused to perpetrate adultery for this reason, lest a man should know. By the fear of men you held your flesh from committing evils; therefore you refused to commit it where a man sees. If you fear God too, do not commit it there too where God sees, and you have perfected sanctification. Attend: "Oh, if I could," someone says, "come to that woman! But I cannot, she is diligently guarded, she has a watchful husband, I have no go-between. If I dare it, I am caught." He makes, as it were, the cleansing of body; but within, because he wants to, he does not make the cleansing of spirit. Therefore he feared to do it in the body lest a man see, and he does not fear to do it within where God sees. He avoids the eyes of man, and does not fear God's. Who, then, perfects sanctification? He who is in the fear of God. *Perfecting sanctification in the fear of God*. The fear of men can perhaps restrain the body from uncleanness; but the mind, only the fear of God. Has he sanctified the mind? He is secure about the body. Let the one who is clothed be clean, and the garment itself will be clean. Let the inhabitant be good and sound, and let him not fear the ruin of his house.

**10** For what of this flesh? We ought not to despise it. What is it? Hay, but it will be gold. Do not despise hay. And it is changed into gold. For he who was able to change water into wine is able to change hay into gold, and to make an angel from flesh. If from filth he made man, will he not from man make an angel? Let your Charity attend to from what man was made, and see whether we even want to think of it. From that filth he made man, and set him over the other animals. From man will he not make an angel? He will plainly. He made men his friends; will he not make them angels? *Now I will not call you servants, but friends*[^21]. He said it to those still bearing flesh, still dying, still dwelling in this neediness and frailty of life. *Now I will not call you servants, but friends*. And what will he give his friends? What he showed in himself when he rose. They will be crowned and transformed into heavenly glory and will be equal to the angels of God. There will be no corruption, no titillation. There it will not be said to us: Cleanse yourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit. We will not labor, nor will a reward be promised us, because we will have already received it. Nor will it be said to us to groan, because we will already be praising. For so mortal flesh will be transformed into the body of an angel. So too groaning will be transformed into praises. Here penance and distress and groaning. There praises, gladness, and joy. Afterward, then; now there is not gladness. But where? In hope. You do not yet hold it, but by hoping you rejoice, because he who promised cannot deceive, because he who promised has it and gives it.
