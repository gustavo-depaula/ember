# Sermon 14/A: (Dolbeau 20)

**1.** Brethren, let us attend to what we sang. Whence the psalm took its theme, thence the psalm is set before us. With the Lord's help let us say something from what is written, and what is fresh in our voices and ears: *Your discipline has directed me unto the end, and your discipline itself will teach me*[^1]. For we seek unto what end we are directed, and what is the discipline that directs us, and what is the teaching of this matter. We wish to be directed, and perhaps we inquire whither we are directed. We have heard: *unto the end.* But the end, when it is said, is either of consumption or of consummation. Consumption makes a thing not to be; consummation makes it to be perfect. Food is ended by eating, a garment is ended by weaving. Food is ended by eating, so that it is not; a garment is ended by weaving, so that it is perfect. Unto such an end, then, without doubt, we seek to be directed, whereby we may be perfected, not whereby we may be consumed.

**2.** What, then, is this end, and what the discipline? The end is Christ, the discipline is the law. Hear the apostle: *The end of the law is Christ, unto justice for everyone who believes*[^2]. This is, then—that we may now say it more plainly and expound what we sang—this is *your discipline has directed me unto the end,* which is, your law has directed me unto the end, your law has directed me unto Christ. The Jews have the law, but it did not direct them unto the end. They came indeed to the end, because they saw Christ, but by stumbling on the final stone they transgressed, by stumbling on Christ they fell and fell outside the end. For they rejected him to whom they ought to have come. Finally, that very end was made to those not believing a stone of stumbling, to those believing a cornerstone. *To you,* says the apostle Peter, *who have believed, the stone which the builders rejected has been made the head of the corner, but to those not believing a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal*[^3].

**3.** Who the end is, we know; what it is to be directed unto the end, we have already spoken on a past Lord's day. For to be directed unto the end is to come to Christ, that is, to believe in Christ. Are the same things to be repeated more often? For you are to be reminded, not burdened. Although to say the same things to you is indeed not irksome to us, but safe for you. For we treated, on account of those who think it enough for themselves to believe and love to live badly, thinking that by believing they will be saved and by living badly will not perish. This, then, we treated, that there ought to be a difference between the faith of Christians and the faith of demons, because they too believe—they said to Christ: *We know who you are*[^4]. They believed Christ, but did not believe in Christ. Whence, then, is one distinguished who believes Christ from one who believes in Christ? Because everyone who believes in Christ at once without doubt believes Christ, but not everyone who believes Christ at once believes in Christ. But the whole work of God the Son of God defined in this, by saying: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*[^5]. By what end, but himself? Do not seek the end apart from him, lest by seeking the end apart from him you be consumed, not consummated. For what is the end, but that to which we wish to arrive, to stand, and to seek nothing beyond? For if you come, but still seek, you have not yet arrived at the end. Therefore to arrive at the end is to arrive where you say: "It suffices."

**4.** Since Philip thought the Father alone to be and said: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^6], the Lord showed him that the end is God, but God the Trinity. Accordingly, when you say: "The end is Christ," you do not separate from it God the Father, nor when you say: "The end is God the Father," do you separate from it Christ. He who wished as it were to separate, thinking that this alone was Christ which he knew by his eyes, joyfully says: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us.* What is: *It suffices us?* There is the end of desire: we will seek nothing further; there will be our satiety, there we will say: "It suffices, I wish nothing more." Why this? Because we now know you; show us the Father. For when we see you and do not see him, it does not suffice us. We rejoice that we see you, but show him, and it suffices us: we will seek nothing more. And the Lord to him, because he too is the end, because he too is the one who satisfies, that he might take from him what he thought—for he thought that he did not see the Son of God, when he saw the form of a servant—said to Philip: *So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me?* When you seek the end and what you see you do not see—therefore surely you seek the end, because you do not see the end standing before you—*Do you not believe,* he says, *that I am in the Father and the Father in me?*[^7]

**5.** To this we too exhort by the reading which follows, which today was read from the Gospel: *This is the will of the Father, that whoever sees the Son and believes in him have eternal life. And I will raise him up on the last day*[^8]. You were seeking the end: do you seek more than eternal life? *This is the will of the Father, that whoever sees the Son and believes in him have eternal life. And I will raise him up on the last day.* What do we say, my brethren? For this vision what eyes do we seek? For see another definition to be demonstrated today, like indeed the past one, of which we have already spoken: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent.* And here what does he say? *This is the will of the Father,* as if he said: *This is the work of God.* For he who does the work of God does the will of God. *This is the will of the Father, that whoever sees the Son and believes in him have eternal life.* He said two things. *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent.* But here he added: *whoever sees and believes.* The Jews saw, but did not believe: they had the one, the other was lacking; how without the other could they arrive at eternal life? If, then, those who saw therefore did not arrive because they did not believe, we who have believed but have not seen—if those two merit eternal life: to see and to believe, and those to whom one is lacking do not arrive at the reward of eternal life—what shall we do? One was lacking to the Jews, the other to us. To them was present seeing, but lacking believing. To us is present believing, but lacking seeing.
