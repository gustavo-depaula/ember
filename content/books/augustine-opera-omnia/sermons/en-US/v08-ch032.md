# Sermon 297: On the Birthday of the Apostles Peter and Paul

**1. 1.** The blood of the Apostles has made this day a feast for us. The servants repaid what had been spent for them in the Lord's blood. Blessed Peter, as we have just heard, is commanded to follow; yet he was planning to go ahead when he said to the Lord, *I will lay down my life for you*. He carried presumption within him; he did not know his own fear. He wanted to go before the one whom he ought to follow. He desired something good, but did not keep the proper order. He felt in bitter fear how bitter death was, and washed away the sin of bitter fear with bitter tears. Fear was questioned by a servant girl; love by the Lord. What did fear answer but human trembling? What did love answer but a divine confession? For loving God is God's gift. When the Lord questioned Peter about his love, he was asking for what he had given.

**1. 2.** Yet what did the Lord foretell to Peter that gives us this feast day? *When you were younger, he said, you used to gird yourself and go wherever you wished; but when you grow old, someone else will gird you and take you where you do not wish to go*. Where is *I will be with you even to death*? Where is *I will lay down my life for you*? See, you will be afraid; see, you will deny; see, you will weep. And the one for whom you were afraid to die will rise, and you will be strengthened. For what wonder is it that Peter was afraid before Christ rose? See, Christ has now risen; the reality of his soul and flesh is now manifest; what is promised is now confirmed by an example. The Lord is seen alive after the cross, after death, after the tomb. It is not enough that he is seen: he is touched, handled, tested. He spent forty days with the disciples, coming in and going out, eating and drinking—not from need, but by power; not from necessity, but from love. He ate and drank not because he was hungry or thirsty, but to teach and demonstrate. Proven real and truthful, he ascends into heaven, sends the Holy Spirit, fills those who believe and pray, and sends them to preach. Yet after all this, someone else girds Peter and takes him where he does not wish to go. You should have wished, when you had to follow, for what you wished when the Lord was foretelling it.

**2. 3.** *Someone else girds you and takes you where you do not wish to go*. The Lord comforts us about this by representing our weakness in himself and saying, *My soul is sorrowful even to death*. The martyrs are great because they trampled on this world's sweetness; the martyrs are great because they endured the most unyielding harshness of bitter death. For if it is easy to endure death, what great thing did the martyrs endure in return for the Lord's death? What makes them great, what makes them exalted, what gives them crowns that flourish so much more than those of other people? Why, as the faithful know, are the martyrs named in their own place, distinct from the other departed, with no prayer offered for them, but with the Church commended to their prayers? Why is this, unless because the death they chose to undergo in confessing the Lord, rather than deny Christ, is assuredly bitter? Nature assuredly shrinks from death. Examine every kind of animal: you will find none that does not want to live, that does not fear destruction. The human race has this feeling. Death is hard; but, I say, because death is hard, life must not therefore be denied. Even in old age Peter did not want to die. He did not want to die, indeed, but he preferred to follow Christ. He preferred following Christ to avoiding death. If there had been a broad road by which he could follow Christ without dying, who doubts that he would have taken it, would have chosen it? But there was no way to follow Christ where he wanted to go except by the road he did not want to endure. Finally, when the rams had passed through that harshness of death, the sheep followed. The holy Apostles are the rams of the sheep. Death's road is rough and full of thorns; but as the Rock and Peter passed along it, those thorns were crushed by feet of rock.

**3. 4.** We do not rebuke, we do not accuse, even if this life is loved. Yet let this life be loved in such a way that no sin is committed in loving it. Let life be loved, but let life be chosen. I question lovers of life and say, *Who is the person who wants life?* Even in silence you all answer: Who is the person who does not want life? I add what the Psalm added: *Who is the person who wants life and loves to see good days?* The answer comes: What person does not want life and does not love to see good days? If, then, you want to reach life and see good days, since this is the reward, attend to the work that earns this reward: *Restrain your tongue from evil.* This follows in the Psalm. *Who is the person who wants life and loves to see good days?* It adds: *Restrain your tongue from evil and your lips from speaking deceit; turn away from evil and do good*. Now say, I want to. I asked, Do you want life? You answered, I do. I asked, Do you want to see good days? You answered, I do. *Hold your tongue back from evil.* Now say, I want to. *Turn away from evil and do good.* Say, I want to. If this is what you want, seek the work, and you are running toward the reward.

**4. 5.** Attend to the apostle Paul, for today is his feast day too. Both led harmonious lives; both shed their blood in fellowship; both received the heavenly crown; both consecrated this day. Attend, then, to the apostle Paul; recall the words we heard a little earlier when his Epistle was read. *I,* he says, *am already being offered in sacrifice, and the time of my departure is at hand. I have fought the good fight, completed the course, kept the faith. Henceforth, he says, there remains for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will repay me on that day*. For he who gave what was not owed will not deny what is owed. The just judge will repay the crown; he will repay it, for he has someone to repay. *I have fought the good fight, completed the course, kept the faith:* he will repay a crown for these merits; as I said, he who gave what was not owed will not deny what is owed. What did he give that was not owed? *I who was formerly a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an insolent aggressor.* What, then, did he give that was not owed? Let us hear Paul himself confessing, and praising the giver of grace by confessing his own life. *Formerly,* he says, *I was a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an insolent aggressor.* Was it therefore owed to you that you should be an apostle? What was owed to a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an insolent aggressor? What but eternal condemnation? And what did he receive in place of eternal condemnation? *I obtained mercy because I acted ignorantly in unbelief*. This is the mercy God gave without its being owed. Hear something else the same man says elsewhere: *I am not,* he says, *worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted God's Church.* So I see, Apostle, that you were not worthy. How did worthiness come to you? Why, then, are you what you are not worthy to be? Listen: *But by God's grace I have obtained what I am.* Through my punishment I was what I was; by God's grace I am what I am. *By grace,* he says, *God's grace, I am what I am, and his grace in me was not empty; rather, I labored more than all of them.* So you repaid God's grace? You received and gave back? Consider what you have said. I am considering it, he says. *Yet not I, but God's grace with me*. Will the just God, then, deny the crown owed to this hardworking Apostle, fighting the good fight, completing the course, keeping the faith, when he gave him grace that was not owed?

**4. 6.** To what, then, will he repay the crown that is owed, O Paul, little and great—to what will he repay it? Surely to your merits. You fought the good fight, completed the course, kept the faith: he will repay the crown owed to these merits of yours. But for your crown to be repaid to you, your merits are God's gifts. See, you fought the good fight, completed the course. For you saw another law in your members, fighting against the law of your mind and taking you captive in the law of sin that is in your members. From where did your victory come, unless from what follows? *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? God's grace, through Jesus Christ our Lord*. This is how you fought, this is how you labored, this is how you did not fail, this is how you conquered. Look at him fighting: *Who will separate us from Christ's love? Tribulation? Or distress? Or hunger? Or persecution? Or nakedness? Or the sword? As it is written: For your sake we are put to death all day long; we have been counted as sheep for slaughter*. Here are weakness, toil, misery, dangers, trials. From where comes the fighters' victory? Hear what follows: *But in all these things we more than conquer through him who loved us*. You completed the course: with whom leading, whom directing, whom helping? What do you say here? *The course,* he says, *I have completed;* but *it belongs neither to the one who wills nor to the one who runs, but to God who shows mercy*. You kept the faith; that is true. First, what faith? One you gave yourself? Was what you said false: *As God has apportioned to each a measure of faith*? Do you not address certain fellow contestants of yours, who labor and run with you in the stadium of this life, saying to them, *For it has been granted to you on Christ's behalf?* What has been granted? *Not only to believe in him, but also to suffer for him*. See, both have been granted: believing and suffering for Christ.

**5. 7.** But perhaps someone says: I received faith, certainly, but I myself kept it. Perhaps you say this, whoever you are who hear these things foolishly: I received faith, but I myself kept it. Our Paul does not say, I myself kept it. For he considers: *Unless the Lord guards the city, its guardian has labored in vain*. Labor, keep guard; but it is good for you to be guarded. For you are not sufficient to guard yourself. If you are abandoned, you will doze and sleep. But the one who guards Israel *does not* *doze or sleep*.

**5. 8.** We love life, then, and we have no doubt at all that we love life; nor can we possibly deny that we love life. So let us choose life, if we love life. What are we choosing? Life. First a good life here; after this, an eternal life. First a good life here, but not yet a happy one. Let a good life be lived now, for which a happy life is reserved afterward. A good life is the work; a happy life is the reward. Live a good life, and you will receive a happy life. What could be more just, what better ordered? Where are you, lover of life? Choose a good life. If you wanted a wife, you would want only a good one; do you love life and choose a bad one? Tell me what you want to be bad. Whatever you want, whatever you love, you want it to be good. You certainly do not want a bad beast of burden, a bad slave, bad clothing, a bad estate, a bad house, a bad wife, or bad children. You seek everything good: be good yourself, you who seek it. How have you offended yourself, that among all the things you want to be good, you alone want to be bad? Your estate, your wife, your clothing, and, to come down to the lowest thing, your shoe are precious to you; has your soul become worthless to you? Surely this life is full of labors, hardships, trials, miseries, pains, and fears; surely it is clear that it is full of all these evils. Yet, full of all these evils as it is, if someone gave it to us eternally just as it is, how grateful we would be to remain miserable forever! That is not the sort of life promised—not by some human being, but by the true God. True truth promises life not only eternal but also happy, where there is no trouble, no toil, no fear, no pain. There is full and utterly certain security there. Life under God, life with God, life from God, life that is God himself: such an eternal life is promised to us. And do we prefer a temporal life to it, this life, miserable and full of hardship? Do we prefer it, I ask, or not? We do prefer it when you want to commit murder to avoid dying. You fear that your slave will kill you, and you kill your slave. You fear that your wife will kill you, perhaps suspecting her falsely, and after dismissing your wife, you desire an adulterous marriage with another. See, by loving life you have lost life; you preferred temporal life to eternal life, miserable life to happy life. And what have you found? Perhaps while preserving your life, you unwillingly breathe your last. You do not know when you will depart from here. With what face do you go to Christ? With what face do you refuse punishment? I do not say, With what face do you demand a reward? You will be condemned to eternal death, you who choose temporal life and by choosing it despise everlasting life.

**6. 9.** But you do not listen to counsel. You seek life; you seek good days. What you seek is good, but it is not here. This precious stone has its own region; it does not grow here. However hard you labor at digging, you will not find here what is not here. But do what is commanded, and what you love will be given in return. For however long this life may be, will you find good days here? Notice what he added: *Life and good days:* so that there may not be life made miserable by evil days. Evil days abound here; but evil days are not made by that sun which runs from east to west and comes forth the next day. We make evil days, brothers. If we lived well every day, we would have good days even here. For where does evil come to a human being, if not from a human being? Count how many things people suffer from outside themselves. Those that do not seem to be done by human beings are very few. Evils come abundantly to a person from another person. Theft comes from a person; he suffers adultery against his wife from a person; his slave is enticed away by a person; he is deceived by a person, proscribed by a person, attacked by a person, led captive by a person. *Deliver me, Lord, from the evil person*. Now, whoever you are who hear this, you think only of an enemy whom you endure as an evil neighbor, powerful person, partner, or fellow citizen. Perhaps you think these things about a robber when you hear, *Deliver me, Lord, from the evil person;* and when you pray, you pray this way, that God may deliver you from an evil person, this or that enemy of yours. Do not be evil to yourself. Listen to me: may God deliver you from yourself. For when God, by his grace and mercy, makes you good instead of evil, from where does he make you good, from whom does he deliver you, if not from yourself, the evil person? Absolutely, my brothers, this is true, this is certain, this is settled: if God delivers you from yourself, the evil person, no other evil person, whoever he may be, will harm you.

**7. 10.** I will present an example drawn from our subject, from the apostle Paul himself, whose day of suffering we celebrate. He was a persecutor, a blasphemer, an insolent aggressor. He was an evil person; he was his own punishment. But while he breathed murder and thirsted for the blood of Christians, though he would shed his own, carrying letters from the chief priests to bring back bound for punishment any followers of the Christian way he found at Damascus, traveling the road of cruelty, ignorant of devotion, he heard a voice from above, our Lord Jesus Christ himself speaking from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad*. Struck by that voice, the persecutor was thrown down and a preacher was raised up. He was blinded in the flesh so that he might see in his heart; he was given light in the flesh so that he might preach from his heart. What do you think, brothers? Saul was delivered from an evil person. From whom but Saul himself? Therefore, because he was delivered from himself, the evil person, what did any other evil person do to him? These are the apostle Peter's words: *And who will harm you if you are lovers of the good?* An evil person persecuted him, an evil person stoned him, an evil person beat him with rods; finally an evil person seized him, bound him, dragged him away, and killed him. As many evils as that man added, so many goods did God prepare. Whatever he suffered was not the torment of punishment, but an occasion for a crown. See what it means to be delivered from the evil person, that is, from oneself. *Who,* he says, *will harm you if you are lovers of the good?*

**8. 11.** But look, evil people do harm. They have done so much to you, Paul. Paul answers you: What was needed was that I should be delivered from the evil person, that is, from myself. Apart from that, what do these evil people do to me? *The sufferings of this time are not comparable to the future glory that will be revealed in us. For the lightness of our tribulation produces for us, beyond an incredible degree, an eternal weight of glory, as we attend not to what is seen. For what is seen is temporal, but what is unseen is eternal*. You have truly been delivered from the evil person, that is, from yourself, so that other evil people would not hurt you, but rather help you. Therefore, dearly beloved, let us celebrate the feast day of the saints, who fought against sin even to bloodshed and conquered by their Lord's gift and help, in such a way that we love them; let us love them in such a way that we imitate them, so that by imitating them we may deserve to reach their rewards.
