# Sermon 46

**1** That all our hope is in Christ, and that all our true and salutary glory is he himself, your Charity has not now learned for the first time. For you are in his flock, who attends to and feeds Israel. But since there are shepherds who want to hear the names of shepherds, but refuse to fulfill the office of shepherds, let us review what he says to them through the prophet, as we heard read. Hear, you, with attention; let us hear, we, with trembling.

**2** *And the word of the Lord came to me, saying: Son of man, prophesy concerning the shepherds of Israel, and say to the shepherds of Israel*[^1]. This reading we heard just now when it was read; from it we have resolved to speak something with your Holiness. He himself will help us to say true things, if we do not say our own. For if we say our own, we will be shepherds feeding ourselves, not the sheep; but if what we say is his, then through whomever he feeds you. *Thus says the Lord God: O shepherds of Israel, who feed themselves alone! Do not the shepherds feed the sheep?*[^2] That is, the shepherds do not feed themselves, but the sheep. This is the first cause why these shepherds are reproached, because they feed themselves, not the sheep. Who are they who feed themselves? Those of whom the Apostle says: *For all seek their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^3]. For we, whom the Lord has set in this place—of which a perilous account is rendered—according to his condescension, not according to our merit, have two things plainly to be distinguished: one, that we are Christians; the other, that we are set over you. That we are Christians is for our own sake; that we are set over you is for your sake. In that we are Christians, our own benefit is regarded; in that we are set over you, only yours. And there are many who, being Christians and not set over others, come to God by a perhaps easier road, and walk perhaps the more unencumbered, the lesser the burden they carry. But we, besides that we are Christians, whence we will render an account to God of our life, are also set over you, whence we will render an account to God of our stewardship. I set forth this difficulty for this reason, that, having compassion on us, you may pray for us. For the day will come on which all things are brought into judgment. And that day, if it is far for the world, is near for every man, the last day of his life. Yet God willed both to lie hidden, both when the end of the world comes, and when there is in each man the end of this life. Do you want not to fear the hidden day? When it comes, let it find you ready. Since, then, those set over others are for this purpose, that they may take counsel for those over whom they preside, and not in being set over others attend at all to their own benefit but to that of those whom they serve, whoever is so set over others that in being set over others he rejoices and seeks his own honor and looks only to his own advantages, he feeds himself, not the sheep. To these the discourse is directed. Hear it as the sheep of God, and see how God has made you secure, whatever they may be who preside over you—that is, whatever we are—he gave you security who feeds Israel. For if God does not desert his sheep, both the evil shepherds will pay due punishments, and the sheep will receive the promises.

**3** Let us see, then, what the divine discourse, flattering no one, addresses to the shepherds who feed themselves, not the sheep. *Behold, you consume the milk, and clothe yourselves with the wool, and what is fat you kill, and you do not feed my sheep. What was weak you have not strengthened; and what is sick you have not made strong; and what was broken you have not bound up; and what was straying you have not called back; and what perished you have not sought; and what was strong you have worn out; and my sheep have been scattered, because there is no shepherd*[^4]. It is said to the shepherds who feed themselves, not the sheep, what they love, what they neglect. What, then, do they love? *You consume the milk, and clothe yourselves with the wool*. On account of which the Apostle says: *Who plants a vineyard and does not take of its fruit? Who feeds a flock, and does not partake of the milk of the flock?*[^5] We find, then, that the milk of the flock is whatever is given by the people of God to those set over them for the support of temporal sustenance. For this is what the Apostle was speaking of when he said these things which I recalled.

**4** Indeed the Apostle, although he chose to support himself by his own hands, and not even to seek that milk from the sheep, yet said that he had the power of receiving milk, and that the Lord had so arranged that those who announce the Gospel should live by the Gospel. And he says that other fellow apostles of his used this power, not usurped but given. He did more, that he might not receive even what was owed. He himself forgave even the debt, but another did not exact what was not owed: he did more. For perhaps that one signified him who, when he brought the sick man to the inn, said: *If you spend anything more, on my return I will repay you*[^6]. Of those, then, who do not need the milk of the flock, what more shall we say? They are more merciful, or rather they bestow the office of mercy itself more generously. They can, and what they can, they do. Let these be praised, and let those not be condemned. For the Apostle himself did not seek the gift. Yet he desired the sheep to be fruitful, not barren, without abundance of milk. And so, when at a certain time he was in great need, bound in the confession of the truth, there was sent to him by the brothers wherewith his need and want might be supplied. But he answered them, giving thanks, and said: *You did well to share in my needs. For I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be self-sufficient. I know both how to abound and how to suffer want. I can do all things in him who strengthens me. Yet you did well to send for my use*[^7]. But to show, in that they did well to him, what he himself sought, lest he be among those who feed themselves, not the sheep, he rejoices not so much that his need was supplied, as he congratulates their fruitfulness. What, then, did he seek there? *Not that I seek*, he says, *a gift, but I require fruit*[^8]. Not that I, he says, may be filled, but that you may not remain empty.

**5** Those, then, who cannot do as Paul, to support themselves by their own hands, let them receive of the milk of the sheep, support their need, but not neglect the weakness of the sheep. Let them not seek this there as their own advantage, that out of the need of their own want they may seem to announce the Gospel, but let them, for the enlightening of men, provide the light of the word of truth. For they are as lamps, as it was said: *Let your loins be girded, and your lamps burning*[^9]; and: *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house: so let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^10]. If, then, a lamp were lit in your house, would you not add oil, lest it be extinguished? Moreover, if the lamp, oil received, did not give light, it was plainly not worthy to be set on a lampstand, but to be at once broken. Whence one lives, it belongs to need to receive; it belongs to love to provide. Not as though the Gospel were for sale, so that this should be its price, which they receive who announce it, that they may live. For if they so sell it, they sell a great thing cheaply. Let them receive the support of need from the people, the reward of stewardship from the Lord. For the people are not fit to render reward to those who serve them in the love of the Gospel. Let those not expect reward, except whence these too expect salvation. But why are these reproached, whence are they convicted? Because, while they took the milk and clothed themselves with the wool, they neglected the sheep. They sought, then, only their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ.

**6** But since we have said what it is to consume the milk, let us ask what it is to clothe oneself with the wool. He who provides milk provides sustenance, and he who provides wool provides honor. These are two things which those who feed themselves, not the sheep, seek from the peoples: the advantage of supplying need, and the favor of honor and praise. For a garment is well understood as honor, because it covers nakedness. For every man is weak. And what is anyone who presides over you, except what you are? He bears flesh, he is mortal, he eats, he sleeps, he rises; he was born, he is to die. If, then, you consider what he is in himself, he is a man. Yet you, by honoring more, as it were cover what is weak.

**7** See what sort of clothing the same Paul received from the good people of God, when he said: *You received me as an angel of God. For I bear you witness that, if it could be, you would have plucked out your eyes and given them to me*[^11]. But when so great honor was shown him, did he, on account of that very honor shown to him—lest perhaps it be denied and he be the less praised when he reproved them—spare those who erred? For if he had done this, he would be among those who feed themselves, not the sheep. He would say within himself: "What is it to me? Let each do what he wants; my sustenance is safe, my honor is safe: both milk and wool, it is enough for me; let each go where he can." Is everything intact for you, then, if each goes where he can? I do not want to make you one set over others, I set you as one of the people itself: *If one member suffers, all the members suffer together*[^12]. Accordingly the Apostle himself, when he recalled how they had been toward him, lest he seem as if forgetful of their honoring, bears witness that they received him as an angel of God, that, if it could be, they would have wished to pluck out their eyes and give them to him. And yet he approaches the sick sheep, the rotting sheep, to cut the wound, not to spare the rottenness. *Have I then*, he says, *become your enemy by preaching the truth?*[^13] See, he both received of the milk of the sheep, as we recalled a little before, and was clothed with the wool of the sheep, but yet did not neglect the sheep. For he did not seek his own things, but the things of Jesus Christ.

**8** Far be it, then, that we should say to you: "Live as you wish, be secure, God will destroy no one, only hold the Christian faith. He will not destroy what he redeemed, he will not destroy those for whom he shed his blood. And if you wish to delight your souls with spectacles, go. What evil is there? And these feasts that are celebrated throughout the city, with the joy of those who feast, and of those who, as they think, make merry at public tables, but in truth perish—go, celebrate securely. Great is the mercy of God, which forgives everything. Crown yourselves with roses before they wither. In the house of your God feast whenever you wish; be filled with food and wine with your own. For this creation was given for this, that you might enjoy it. For God did not give it to pagans and the impious, and not give it to you." If we say these things, perhaps we will gather larger crowds. And if there are some who perceive that we, saying these things, do not savor rightly, we offend a few, but we win over the multitude. But if we do this, saying not the words of God, not the words of Christ, but our own, we will be shepherds feeding ourselves, not the sheep.

**9** But when he had said what these shepherds love, he says also what they neglect. For the vices of the sheep lie widely about. Sound and fat sheep are very few—that is, solid in the food of truth, using the pastures well by the gift of God. But those evil shepherds do not spare even such. It is little that they do not care for those weak and sick and straying and lost. They even kill these strong and fat ones, as far as is in them. And those live. They live by the mercy of God. Yet, as far as pertains to the evil shepherds, they kill them. "How," you say, "do they kill them?" By living badly, by giving a bad example. Or was it said in vain to the servant of God, eminent among the members of the supreme Shepherd: Showing yourself in all things an example of good works; and: *Be a model to the faithful*[^14]? For by attending to his superior living badly, even a strong sheep, if he turn his eyes from the rules of the Lord and fix them on the man, begins to say in his heart: "If my superior lives so, who am I that I should not do what he does?" He kills the strong sheep. If, then, he kills the strong sheep, what will he do about the rest, who by living badly killed the one he had not himself strengthened, but had found strong or robust? I say to your Charity, I say again—even if the sheep live, even if the sheep are strong in the word of the Lord and hold to what they heard from their Lord: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^15]—yet he who lives badly in the sight of the people, as far as is in him, kills the one who attends to him. Let him not, then, flatter himself because that one is not dead. Both that one lives, and this one is a murderer. Just as, when a lascivious man attends to a woman to desire her: see, she is chaste, and this one is an adulterer. For the Lord's sentence is true and open: *Whoever has looked at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart*[^16]. He has not come to her bedroom, and he already wallows in his own inner bedroom. So everyone who lives badly in the sight of those over whom he is set, as far as is in him, kills even the strong. He who imitates him dies; he who does not imitate him lives. Yet, as far as pertains to him, he kills both. *And what is fat*, it says, *you kill, and you do not feed my sheep*.

**10** Now you have heard what they love. Hear what they neglect. *What was weak you have not strengthened; and what was sick you have not made strong; and what was broken*—that is, what was shattered—*you have not bound up; and what was straying you have not called back; and what perished you have not sought; and what was strong you have worn out*[^17]—that is, you have killed and slain. A sheep is weak—that is, it bears a weak heart, so that it can yield to temptations, if temptation comes upon it unwary and unprepared. The negligent shepherd, when such a one believes, does not say to him: *Son, approaching the service of God, stand in justice and fear, and prepare your soul for temptation*[^18]. For he who says these things strengthens the weak and makes the weak strong, so that he may not, when he believes, hope for the prosperity of this world. But if he is taught to hope for the prosperity of the world, by that very prosperity he is corrupted; when adversities come upon him, he is wounded, or perhaps extinguished. He, then, who so builds does not build him upon the rock, but sets him upon the sand. *But the rock was Christ*[^19]. The passions of Christ are to be imitated, not delights to be sought out by Christians. The weak one is strengthened, when it is said to him: "Hope indeed for the temptations of this world, but the Lord will deliver you from them all, if your heart does not turn back from him. For to strengthen your heart he came to suffer, he came to die, he came to be smeared with spittle, he came to be crowned with thorns, he came to hear reproaches, he came finally to be fixed to the wood. All these he for you, you nothing. Not for him, but for you."

**11** But what sort are they who, fearing to offend those to whom they speak, not only do not prepare them for imminent temptations, but even promise the happiness of this world, which God did not promise to the world itself? He foretells labors upon labors to come even to the end to the world itself, and do you want the Christian to be exempt from those very labors? Because he is a Christian, he is to suffer something more in this world. For the Apostle says: *All who wish to live devoutly in Christ will suffer persecution*[^20]. Now if it pleases you, shepherd seeking your own things, not the things of Jesus Christ—let him say, *All who wish to live devoutly in Christ will suffer persecution*, and do you say: "If you live devoutly in Christ, all good things will abound to you. And if you have no sons, you will take in and rear all, and no one will die for you." Is this your building? Attend to what you do, where you set it. He whom you set up is on the sand. Rain will come, the river will flow in, the wind will blow, they will strike that house and it will fall, and great will be its ruin. Lift it from the sand, set it on the rock: let the one you want to be a Christian be in Christ. Let him attend to the undeserved passions of Christ, let him attend to him paying without any sin what he did not steal, let him attend to the Scripture saying to him: *He scourges every son whom he receives*[^21]. Either let him prepare to be scourged, or let him not seek to be received. *He scourges*, it says, *every son whom he receives*. And do you say: "Perhaps you will be exempt?" If exempt from the suffering of scourges, exempt from the number of sons. "So," you will say, "does he scourge every son?" He plainly scourges every son, as even the Only-begotten. That Only-begotten, born of the Father's substance, equal to the Father *in the form of God*[^22], the Word through whom all things were made, had nowhere to be scourged. For this he was clothed with flesh, that he might not be without scourge. He, then, who scourges the Only-begotten without sin, does he leave the adopted with sin? The Apostle says we have been called to adoption. We received the adoption of sons, that we might be of the Only-begotten, co-heirs, that we might be even his inheritance: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance*[^23]. He set forth for us an example in his passions.

**12** But plainly, lest the weak one fail in future temptations, he must neither be deceived by a false hope, nor broken by terror. Say to him: *Prepare your soul for temptation*[^24]. But perhaps he begins to slip, to tremble, to refuse to approach. You have something else: *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear*[^25]. For to promise that, and to foretell coming passions, is to strengthen the weak. But to one who fears too much and is for this reason deterred, when you promise the mercy of God—not that temptations will be lacking, but that he does not permit one to be tempted beyond what one can bear—is to bind up the broken. For there are those who, when they hear of coming tribulations, arm themselves the more and as it were thirst for their cup. For they think the medicine of the faithful too small for themselves, but seek the glory of the martyrs too. But there are others who, hearing of future and necessarily coming temptations—which properly must come to the Christian, which no one feels except one who has wished to be truly a Christian—when some come upon them, are broken and limp. Offer the binding of consolation, bind up what is broken. Say: "Do not fear, he does not desert in temptations the one in whom you have believed. God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear. You do not hear this from me, the Apostle says it, who also says: *And do you want to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ?*[^26] When you hear these things, then, you hear them from Christ himself, you hear them from that Shepherd who feeds Israel. For to him it was said: *You will give us to drink in tears, in measure*[^27]. For what the Apostle says, *He does not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear*, this the prophet says: *In measure*. Only do not let go of the one who corrects and exhorts, who terrifies and consoles, who strikes and heals."

**13** *What was weak*, it says, *you have not strengthened*[^28]. He says it to the evil shepherds, the false shepherds, the shepherds seeking their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ, rejoicing in the advantage of milk and wool, not caring for the sheep at all, and not making strong what was sick. Between the weak—that is, the not strong (for even the sick are called infirm)—but between the weak and the sick, that is, the one who is unwell, this seems to me to be the difference. For these things, brothers, which we somehow try to distinguish, perhaps we ourselves can, with greater diligence, distinguish better, and another more skilled or fuller of the light of heart can; meanwhile, lest you be cheated, as far as pertains to the words of Scripture, I speak what I perceive. With the weak one it is to be feared lest temptation come upon him and break him. But the sick one is already ill with some desire, and by some desire is hindered from entering the way of God, from taking up the yoke of Christ. Attend to those men wishing to live well, now resolving to live well, but less fit to suffer evils than they are prepared to do good things. But it belongs to Christian firmness not only to work the things that are good but also to bear the things that are evil. Those, then, who seem to be fervent in good works, but unwilling or unable to bear imminent passions, are weak. But those who, by some evil desire, lovers of the world, are called back from those very good works, lie sick and ailing, since by that very sickness, as if without any strength, they can work nothing good. Such in soul was that paralytic, whom, when those who carried him could not bring him to the Lord, they opened the roof, and let him down. That is, as if you wished to do this in the soul, to open the roof and let down to the Lord a paralytic soul, dissolved in all its members and idle from every good work, weighed down surely by its sins and ailing with the sickness of its desire. If, then, all the members are dissolved and there is an inner paralysis, that you may come to the physician—for perhaps the physician lies hidden, and is within; that is, this true meaning is hidden in the Scriptures—by expounding what is hidden, open the roof and let down the paralytic. And those who do not do this and who neglect to do it, you have heard what they hear: *What was sick you have not made strong. And what was broken you have not bound up*: now of temptations. Something is added whence what is broken may be bound up, that consolation: *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, but will with the temptation also make a way out, that you may be able to endure it*[^29].

**14** *And what was straying you have not called back*[^30]. See whence we are in peril among the heretics. *And what was straying you have not called back; and what perished you have not sought*. Hence we somehow dwell among the hands of robbers and the teeth of raging wolves, and for these perils of ours we pray that you may pray. And the sheep are stubborn. Because the strays are sought, they say in their straying and their perdition that they are strangers to us: "Why do you want us? Why do you seek us?" As though that very thing were not the cause why we want them and why we seek them, because they stray and perish. "If I am in error," he says, "if I am in destruction, why do you want me? Why do you seek me?" Because you are in error, I want to call you back; because you have perished, I want to find you. "I want to stray thus, I want to perish thus." Do you want to stray thus, do you want to perish thus? How much better I do not want it. I dare to say plainly, I am importunate. I hear the Apostle saying: *Preach the word, be urgent in season, out of season*[^31]. In season for whom? Out of season for whom? In season surely for the willing, out of season for the unwilling. I am plainly importunate, I dare to say: You want to stray, you want to perish, I do not want it. Last of all, the one who terrifies me does not want it. If I should want it, see what he says, see what he reproaches: *What was straying you have not called back; and what perished you have not sought*. Shall I fear you more than him? *We must all be presented before the tribunal of Christ*[^32]. I do not fear you. For you cannot overturn the tribunal of Christ and set up the tribunal of Donatus. I will call back the straying, I will seek the lost. Willing or unwilling, I will do it. And if, as I seek, the brambles of the woods tear me, I will squeeze myself through all the narrow places; I will shake out all the hedges; as much strength as the terrifying Lord grants me, I will traverse all. I will call back the straying, I will seek the perishing. If you do not want me to suffer, do not stray, do not perish.

**15** It is little that I grieve over you straying and perishing. I fear lest, neglecting you, I kill even what is strong. For see what follows: *And what was strong you have worn out*[^33]. If I am indifferent to your error and your perishing, the one who is strong will be delighted to stray and perish. I desire outward gains, but I fear more the inward losses. If I treat your error as indifferent, the one who is strong attends to it, and thinks it is nothing to go into a heresy. When some advantage of the world shines forth whereby he may be changed, the strong one, about to perish, will at once say to me, when I do not seek you the lost: "God is both here and there, what does it matter? Men quarreling among themselves did this. Anywhere God is to be worshipped." If perhaps some Donatist says to him: "I will not give you my daughter unless you are of my party," he needs to attend and say: "If it were no evil to be of these men's party, our shepherds would not say such great things against them, would not busy themselves over their error." If, then, we cease and keep silent, he will say to the contrary: "Surely, if it were an evil to be in the party of Donatus, they would speak against it, would refute them, would busy themselves to win them. If they stray, they would call them back; if they perish, they would seek them." Not in vain, then, when he had said above, *What is fat you have killed*, he set down again at the end: *And what was strong you have worn out*. For it is the sentence repeated, except that it was born from what he said above: *What was straying you have not called back; and what perished you have not sought*; *and*, by doing this, *what is strong you have killed*.

**16** Accordingly hear what follows about this negligence of the evil—rather, the false—shepherds: *And my sheep have been scattered, because there is no shepherd, and have become food for all the beasts of the field*[^34]. Lurking wolves steal them, roaring lions snatch them, when the sheep do not cling to the shepherd. For the shepherd is present, but to those who act badly there is no shepherd. And they cling to shepherds who are not shepherds, who feed themselves, not the sheep. And a lethal error follows: they go into beasts that plunder them, and that desire to be satisfied by their death. For such are all who rejoice over others' errors: they are beasts fed on deaths.

**17** *And my sheep were scattered and strayed onto every mountain, and onto every high hill*[^35]. Beasts come from the mountains and hills—earthly swelling and the pride of the world. The pride of Donatus exalted itself, made for itself a party. Parmenian, following him, confirmed his error. That one is a mountain, that one is a hill. So every author of any error whatever, swelling with earthly elation, promises the sheep rest, good pastures. And sometimes the sheep find there pastures from the rain of God, not from the hardness of the mountain. For they too have the Scriptures, they have the sacraments. These are not of the mountains, but when they are found among the mountains, one badly remains among the mountains. For by straying among mountains and hills, they desert the flock, desert unity, desert the fortified cohorts against wolves and lions. From there, then, let God call them back, let him himself call them back. Now you will hear him calling them back: *My sheep strayed*, he says, *onto every mountain and onto every high hill*—that is, onto every swelling of earthly pride. For there are good mountains too: *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me*[^36]. And see that your hope is not in the mountains: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^37]. Do not think you do injury to the holy mountains, when you say: *My help* is not from the mountains, but *from the Lord who made heaven and earth*. The very mountains cry this to you. It was a mountain that cried out: *I hear there are schisms among you, and each of you says: I am of Paul, I of Apollos, I of Cephas, but I of Christ*[^38]. Lift up your eyes to this mountain, hear what it says, and do not remain even on this mountain. For hear what follows: *Was Paul crucified for you?*[^39] Therefore, after you have lifted your eyes to the mountains whence help will come to you—that is, to the authors of the divine Scriptures—attend to him crying out with all his marrow, with all his bones: *Lord, who is like you?*[^40], that secure, without any injury to the mountains, you may say: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*. Not only then will the mountains not be angry at you, but then they will love you, then they will favor you the more; if you place your hope in them, they will be saddened. An angel, showing many divine and wondrous things to a man, was being adored by the man, as by one lifting his eyes to the mountain. But that angel, calling him back from himself to the Lord, said: Do not *do it; adore him, for I am your fellow servant and that of your brothers*[^41].

**18** *Onto every mountain, and onto every hill, and onto every face of the earth they were scattered*[^42]. What is *Onto every face of the earth they were scattered*? Pursuing all earthly things, the things that shine on the face of the earth, these they love, these they cherish. They do not want to die, that their life may be hidden in Christ. *Over every face of the earth*, by love of earthly things, and because the straying sheep are over the whole face of the earth. Not all heretics over the whole face of the earth, but yet heretics over the whole face of the earth. Some here, some there, yet nowhere lacking. They themselves do not know each other: one sect in Africa, another heresy in the East, another in Egypt, another in Mesopotamia, for example. In different places there are different ones: but that one mother, pride, bore them all, just as our one mother, the Catholic, bore all the faithful Christians spread throughout the world. No wonder, then, if pride bears division, love unity. Yet that Catholic mother, that shepherd in her, everywhere seeks the straying, strengthens the weak, cares for the sick, binds up the broken, some from these, some from those, who do not know one another. But yet she knows them all, because she is spread with all. For example, in Africa there is the party of Donatus, the Eunomians are not in Africa, but with the party of Donatus the Catholic is here. In the East there are Eunomians, but there the party of Donatus is not, but with the Eunomians the Catholic is there. She is like a vine, spread everywhere by growing; they are like useless branches, cut off by the husbandman's pruning-hook for the merit of their barrenness, that the vine might be pruned, not amputated. Those branches, then, where they were cut off, there they remained. But the vine, growing through all, both knows its branches that remained in it, and, beside itself, those cut off from it. From there, then, it calls back the straying, because of the broken branches too the Apostle says: *For God is able to graft them in again*[^43]. Whether you say sheep straying from the flock, or you say branches cut off from the vine, neither to call back the sheep, nor again to graft in the branches, is God less fit, because he is the supreme shepherd, he the true husbandman. *And onto every face of the earth they were scattered; and there was no one to seek, no one to call back*, but among those evil shepherds; *there was no one*—but a man—*to seek*[^44].

**19** *Therefore, shepherds, hear the word of the Lord: As I live, says the Lord God*[^45]. See whence he began. It is, as it were, an oath of God, a testimony of his life. *As I live, says the Lord*. The shepherds are dead, but the sheep are secure; the Lord lives. *As I live, says the Lord God*. But which shepherds are dead? Those seeking their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ. Will there, then, be and be found shepherds seeking not their own things but the things of Jesus Christ? There will be plainly and will be found plainly, nor are they lacking nor will they be lacking. Let us see, then, what the Lord says, who says that he lives: whether he says that he will take away the sheep from the evil shepherds, who feed themselves, not the sheep, and give them to good shepherds, who feed the sheep, not themselves. *As I live*, says *the Lord God, because my sheep have become food for all the beasts of the field, because there was no shepherd*[^46]. Again he says shepherd, both a little before and now. He did not say: because there are no shepherds. For to such men, straying badly, perishing badly, there is no shepherd, even if the shepherd is present, because, even when the light is present, there is no light to the blind. *And the shepherds did not seek my sheep, and the shepherds fed themselves, but my sheep they did not feed*[^47].

**20** *Therefore, shepherds, hear the word of the Lord*[^48]. But which shepherds, hear? *Thus says the Lord God: Behold, I am against the shepherds, and I will require my sheep from their hands*[^49]. Hear and learn, sheep of God: from the evil shepherds God requires his sheep, and from their hands he requires their death. For he says in another place through the same prophet: *Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel. You will hear the word from my mouth, and you will forewarn them from me. When I say to the sinner: You will die the death, and you have not spoken that the impious man should beware of his way, that criminal will die in his crime, but his blood I will require from your hand. But if you forewarn the criminal of his way, that he may turn from it, and he does not turn from his way, he will die in his crime, and you will free your soul*[^50]. What is it, brothers? You see how perilous it is to keep silent? That one dies and rightly dies; he dies in his impiety and his sin; for his negligence killed him. For he would find the shepherd living who says: *As I live, says the Lord*; but when he has been negligent, with no one warning him who is set over him and is a watchman for this, that he may warn—both that one will justly die, and this one will justly be condemned. But if you say, it says, to the impious man: You will die the death—against whom I have threatened the sword, and he has neglected to avoid the imminent sword, and the sword comes and slays him—that one will die in his sin, but you have freed your soul. On account of this, it pertains to us not to keep silent; but to you it pertains, even if we keep silent, to hear from the holy Scriptures the words of the Shepherd.

**21** Let us see, then—for so I had proposed—whether he takes the sheep away from the evil shepherds and gives them to good shepherds. I see him taking the sheep away from the evil shepherds. For he says this: *Behold, I am against the shepherds, and I will require my sheep from their hands, and I will turn them away from them, that they may not feed my sheep; and the shepherds will no longer feed themselves*[^51]. For when I say: Let them feed my sheep, they feed themselves, not my sheep. *I will turn them away that they may not feed my sheep*. How does he turn them away that they may not feed his sheep? *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^52]. As though he said: "They say my things, they do their own." If he had said: "Do securely what they do, I will condemn them who live badly; but you I will spare, because you followed those set over you"—if he said this, he would deter the evil shepherds, who feed not the sheep but themselves. But since he terrifies not only the blind leader, but also the blind follower—for he did not say: The leader falls into the pit, and the follower does not fall, but: *The blind leading the blind, both fall into the pit*[^53]—he admonished the sheep and said: *What they say, do; what they do, do not do*. For when you do not do what the evil shepherds do, they do not feed you; but when you do what they say, I feed you. For they say my things and do them not. "Securely," they say, "we follow our bishops." The heretics often say this, when they are convicted by the most manifest truth: "We are sheep, they will render an account for us." They will render plainly a bad account for your death. For the death of a wicked sheep the evil shepherd will render a bad account. Does a sheep live, then, because its skin is handed in? The shepherd is reproached, because he neglected the straying sheep, and therefore it rushed into the jaws of the wolf to be devoured. What does it profit him that he brings the marked skin? The head of the household requires the life of the sheep. But see, the evil shepherd has brought the skin: let him render an account of the skin. Will he perhaps lie? He saw from above who afterward judges: to whom that one renders words, he counts deeds, he inspects thoughts. Let the evil shepherd render an account of the skin of the dead sheep. "I cried your words to it and it refused to follow; I made an effort that it not stray from the flock, and it did not obey": plainly, if he says this and says it truly—but he knows whether he says it truly—he renders a good account of a bad sheep. But if God inspects that he neglected the straying one, that he did not seek the perishing one, what does it profit that he found a skin to bring back? Let him call it back, that he not show the skin of a dead one. If, then, he who did not seek the straying one renders no good account, what sort will he render who made one stray? This is what I say: If a bishop set in the Catholic renders no good account of a sheep, if he has not sought the one straying from the flock of God, what sort of account will the heretic render, who not only will not call back from error, but even forced one into error?

**22** But let us see, as I said, how God calls back the sheep from the evil shepherds. I have already recalled: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^54]. And they do not feed you, but God, because, willing or unwilling, the shepherds, that you may come to the milk and wool, are going to say the words of God. *You who preach that one must not steal, you steal*[^55], says the Apostle to those who say good things and do evil. Do you hear the one preaching that you should not steal; do not imitate the one stealing. If you want to imitate the one stealing, he himself feeds you by his deed; he supplies you poison, not food. But if you hear from him what he says not from his own, but from God's—a grape indeed cannot be gathered from thorns; for it is the Lord's own sentence: *No one gathers a grape from thorns and figs from thistles*[^56]—yet do not for this reason as it were bring a false charge against your Lord and say: "Lord, you cheated me, because it cannot be that I gather a grape from thorns; and again you said to me of certain ones: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*—surely those doing evil are thorns. How do you want me to gather from thorns the grape of the word?" He will answer: "That is not a grape of thorns, but sometimes a branch growing entangles itself in the hedge, and the grape hangs amid the dense thorns, but it does not rise from the root of the thorns. If you are hungry, and have nothing else from which to take, carefully put forth your hand, lest you be torn by the thorns—that is, lest you imitate the deeds of the evil—and gather the grape hanging among the thorns, but born of the vine. The cluster, the nourishment, will reach you; for the thorns the torment of fire is kept."

**23** *And I will draw out my sheep*, he says, *from their mouth, and from their hands: and they will no longer be food for them*[^57]. This too is said in the psalm: *Will not all who work iniquity know, who devour my people as the food of bread?*[^58] *And they will no longer be food for them: because thus says the Lord God: Behold, I myself*[^59]. I have taken away the sheep from the evil shepherds, by admonishing, as I said, that they not do what they do: that is, that the unwary and negligent sheep not do what the evil shepherds do. And what does he say? To whom does he give what he took from them? Perhaps to good shepherds? This does not follow. And what shall we say, brothers? Are there not good shepherds? Is it not said in another place of the Scriptures: *And I will set over them shepherds according to my heart, and they will feed them with discipline?*[^60] How, then, the sheep which he takes from the evil shepherds, he does not give to good ones, but as though good ones remained nowhere at all, says: I will feed them? To Peter he had said: *Feed my sheep*[^61]. What, then, do we do? When the sheep are entrusted to Peter, the Lord does not there say: I will feed my sheep, not you, but: *Peter, do you love me? Feed my sheep*[^62]. Or is it perhaps that, because Peter is not now found—for he has already been taken up into the rest of the Apostles and martyrs—there is no one to whom the secure Lord of the sheep may say: *Feed my sheep*, and in a way, as if by necessity, he descends to the office of feeding his sheep, having no one to whom to entrust them, yet not deserting them? For this seems to follow: *Thus says the Lord God: Behold, I myself*—to whom we were saying: *You who feed Israel, attend; you who lead Joseph like sheep*[^63], the people set in Egypt: now Israel spread among the nations is himself Joseph. For you know that Joseph migrated into Egypt: it came about by his brothers selling him. The Jews sold Christ; not without cause among the Apostles Judas himself was the seller. Christ began to be among the nations, there he was honored, there his people grew, his shepherd does not desert him. *Stir up*, he says, *your power and come, that you may save us*[^64]. He plainly does and will do it. He says: *Behold, I myself, and I will seek my sheep, and visit them, as a shepherd visits his flock*[^65]. The evil shepherds did not care for them; for they did not redeem them with their own blood. *As a shepherd visits*, he says, *his flock on the day*—on what day? *When there is a storm and cloud*—that is, rain and mist[^66]. Rain and mist, the error of this world; a great gloom rising from the desires of men, and a strong mist covering the earth. And it is difficult for the sheep not to stray in this mist. But the shepherd does not desert them. He seeks them, he penetrates the mist with most keen eyes, he is not hindered by the gloom of the clouds. He sees, he calls back the straying from every side, only that there may come about what he says in the Gospel: *Those that are my sheep hear my voice and follow me*[^67]. *In the midst of the scattered sheep, so I will seek my sheep, and lead them out from every place where they were scattered, on the day of cloud and storm*[^68]. When it is difficult to be found, now I will find them. The mist is thick, the storm is heavy: nothing lies hidden from his eyes.

**24** *And I will lead them out from the nations, and gather them from the regions, and bring them into their land, and feed them upon the mountains of Israel*[^69]. He set up the mountains of Israel, the authors of the divine Scriptures. There feed, that you may feed securely. Whatever you hear from there, let this savor well to you; whatever is outside, spurn. Lest you stray in the mist, hear the voice of the shepherd. Gather yourselves to the mountains of holy Scripture. There are the delights of your heart, there nothing venomous, nothing foreign; the pastures are most abundant. Only come sound, sound be fed on the mountains of Israel. And in the streams, and in *every habitation of the land*[^70]. For from the mountains we have shown, the streams of evangelical preaching flowed, when their sound went out into all the earth, and every habitation of the land was made glad and fruitful for feeding the sheep. *In good pastures I will feed them, and on the high mountains of Israel. And their folds will be there*[^71]—that is, where they may rest, where they may say: It is well, where they may say: It is true, it is manifest, we are not deceived. In the glory of God they will rest, as in those folds. *And they will sleep*—that is, rest—*and rest in good delights*.

**25** *And in fat pastures they will be fed upon the mountains of Israel*[^72]. I have already said the mountains of Israel, the good mountains, to which we lift our eyes, that thence help may come to us. But our help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth. Therefore, lest our hope be even in the good mountains, when he had said, *I will feed my sheep upon the mountains of Israel*, again, lest you remain in the mountains, he added at once: I will feed my sheep. Lift up your eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to you, but attend to the one saying: I will feed them. For your help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth.

**26** *And I will make them rest, says the Lord God*[^73]. But that he may make them rest, what did he first care for? For what he first cared for, he says afterward: *Thus says the Lord God*: What perished, *I will seek*; *and what strayed, I will call back; and what was crushed, I will bind up; and what is lifeless, I will strengthen; and what is fat, and what is strong, I will guard*[^74]: which the evil shepherds did not do, feeding themselves, not the sheep. The Lord did not say: I will set up other good shepherds, who may do these things, but: I, he says, will do it; my sheep I will entrust to no one. Be secure, brothers; be secure, sheep. To us it seems we ought to fear, as though a good shepherd were lacking.

**27** He concludes thus: *And I will feed them with judgment*[^75]. See that he alone feeds thus, feeding with judgment. For what man judges of a man? All things are full of rash judgments. The one of whom we have despaired is suddenly converted and becomes the best. The one of whom we have presumed much suddenly fails and becomes the worst. Neither is our fear sure, nor is our love sure. What each man is today, the man himself scarcely knows. Yet somehow he knows what he is today. But what he will be tomorrow, not even he knows. He, then, feeds with judgment, distributing what is proper to the proper: these things to these, those to those, the things owed to those to whom they are owed, this or that. For he knows what he does. He feeds with judgment those whom, having been judged, he redeemed. He, then, feeds with judgment.

**28** For in the prophet Jeremiah a certain *partridge cried out, He gathered what he did not bring forth, making his riches not with judgment*[^76]. Against this partridge making his riches not with judgment, this shepherd feeds with judgment. Why is that one without judgment? Because he gathered what he did not bring forth. Why is this one with judgment? Because he cherishes what he brought forth. Yet we are speaking of the good shepherd. Good shepherds either are not, or lie hidden. If they are not, what do we do? If they lie hidden, why is there silence about them? That partridge indeed was understood by certain elders and treaters of the Scriptures before us as the devil, gathering what he did not bring forth. For he is not a creator but a deceiver, making his riches not with judgment. For it does not pertain to him whether one strays this way, another that. He wants all to stray, by whatever errors. How diverse are the heresies, how diverse the errors! He wants men to stray in all of them. The devil does not say: "Let them be Donatists and not Arians." Whether they are here or there, they pertain to him gathering without judgment. "Let him adore idols," he says, "he is mine; let him remain in the superstition of the Jews, he is mine; let him, deserting unity, go into this or that heresy, he is mine." He gathers, then, without judgment, making his riches. But what follows? *In the middle of his days they will desert him, and in his last days he will be foolish*[^77]. He comes gathering his sheep from every side. In the middle of his days, sooner than he hoped, before he thought, they will desert him, and he will be foolish in his last days. Why was he wise in his first days, and foolish in his last? Hear, brothers. Sometimes in the Scriptures wisdom is said for cunning, by an abuse of the word, not by its proper sense. Whence it is said: *Where is the wise man, where the scribe, where the disputer of this world? Has not God made foolish the wisdom of this world?*[^78] And that partridge, the same dragon, the same serpent, was as it were wise when he deceived Adam through Eve. He was thought to speak truly, he was esteemed to give good counsel, he was believed against God. But that wisdom is said by an abuse of the word and in a bad sense, indeed by the custom of our Scriptures—for what is it to us how the authors of the world speak?—you have in the same book: *There was there a serpent wiser than all the beasts*[^79]. This one, wiser than all the beasts, is recognized as keen and cunning to deceive. Afterward he is not believed. It is said to him: "We renounce you, it is enough that you first deceived the unwary." Therefore so in his last days he will be foolish. His frauds will be open, and therefore now they will be frauds no more. In his last days he will be foolish, who gathered what he did not bring forth, and made his riches not with judgment. Against him our Redeemer feeds with judgment.

**29** Let some heretic too come forward, if not the brother of the devil, surely his helper and son. And him too I would call a partridge, a contentious animal. For this animal, as fowlers know, is caught even by its zeal for contending. These men contend against the truth, and have contended ever since they divided themselves. Now they say: "We do not want to contend," because they are now caught. He has no ground to say: "I do not want to contend." O caught one, surely at some time you were the one who, in the first days of your sedition, accused the betrayers, condemned the innocent, sought the judgment of the Emperor, did not consent to the judgment of the bishops, when so often defeated appealed, most eagerly litigated before the Emperor himself. You were gathering what you did not bring forth. Where is your neck now? Where is your tongue? Where is your hiss? Surely in your last days you have been made foolish, you have fed without judgment. For you do not want to judge truly, either of your error or of the truth. Against you Christ feeds with judgment, he distinguishes his sheep from sheep not his own. *Those that are my sheep*, he says, *hear my voice and follow me*[^80].

**30** Here I find all the good shepherds in one shepherd. For good shepherds are not truly lacking, but they are in one. They are many who are divided. Here one is preached, because unity is commended. For it is not now truly the case that the shepherds are passed over in silence, and the shepherd is named, because the Lord found no one to whom to entrust his sheep. But then he entrusted them because he found Peter. Rather, in Peter himself he commended unity. There were many Apostles, and to one it is said: *Feed my sheep*[^81]. Far be it that good shepherds should now be lacking, far be it from us that they be lacking, far be it from his mercy that he not beget and set them up. Surely if there are good sheep, there are also good shepherds, for from good sheep good shepherds are made. But all the good shepherds are in one, they are one. They feed, Christ feeds. For the friends of the bridegroom do not speak their own voice, but rejoice with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom. Therefore he himself feeds, when they feed; and he says: I feed, because in them is his own voice, in them his own love. For Peter himself, to whom he entrusted his sheep as one to another, he wished to make one with himself, and so to entrust the sheep to him, that he should be the head, that the other should bear the figure of the body—that is, of the Church—and that, as bridegroom and bride, they should be two in one flesh. Accordingly, that he might entrust the sheep, what does he first say to him, lest he entrust them to him as to another? *Peter, do you love me? And he answered: I love you. And again: Do you love me? And he answered: I love you. And a third time: Do you love me? And he answered: I love you*[^82]. He confirms love, that he may consolidate unity. He, then, one, feeds in these, and these in one. And there is silence about the shepherds, yet there is no silence. The shepherds glory, but *let him who glories glory in the Lord*[^83]. This is for Christ to feed, this is to feed in Christ, this is also to feed with Christ, not to feed for oneself apart from Christ. For it was not truly from a lack of shepherds, as if the prophet were foretelling these coming evil times, that he said: *I will feed my sheep*, I have no one to whom to entrust them. Even when Peter himself was there, and when the Apostles themselves were still in this flesh and in this life, then that one, in whom one all are one, said: *I have other sheep, which are not of this fold; I must bring them also, that there may be one flock and one shepherd*[^84]. Let all, then, be in one shepherd, and speak the one voice of the shepherd which the sheep hear, and follow their shepherd, and not this one or that one, but one. And let all in him speak one voice, let them not have diverse voices. *I beseech you, brothers, that you all say the same thing, and that there be no schisms among you*[^85]. Let the sheep hear this voice, refined from all schism, purged from all heresy, and follow their shepherd saying: *Those that are my sheep hear my voice, and follow me*[^86].

**31** For do you want to know, heretic, how you do not have the voice of the shepherd, and how perilously the sheep follow you, covered with the garment of a sheep, and within a ravening wolf? Let them hear your voice; let us see whether it is Christ's. A weak sheep, straying from the flock, not knowing where the flock is, seeks the Church. It seeks where to gather itself, where to enter. Utter your voice: let us hear whether it is Christ's. Let us hear whether it is the lamb's, or the partridge's. The sheep of God seeks its flock. Suppose a sheep has come from the East to Africa; it seeks its flock. It runs upon you, it runs into your basilica, it wants to enter. You are moved by the unknown face, either you or your minister. Standing or sitting at the door, you question the sheep seeking its flock—rather, the flock of God. It wants to enter with its flock, it thinks it is there. You ask: "Are you a pagan, or a Christian?" It answers: "A Christian": for it is a sheep of God. You ask whether it is perhaps a catechumen, and rushes upon the sacraments. It answers: "A believer." You ask of what communion it is. It answers: "Catholic." You reject a Christian, a believer, a catholic. Who are those within whom you keep? Cast it out, then, reject it. Rejected by you, it is approved by Christ. Would that those who are with you might recognize you, and in the middle of your days desert you. Some of our brothers yesterday went to their basilica. Even though to evil brothers, yet to brothers. Attend, my brothers, to the difference between the confidence of truth and the fear of falsehood. When you recognize some of them in this congregation, how you rejoice! Because in you is the one who seeks what perished. It is sometimes suggested to you: "He will hear and depart." And you: "Let him hear and depart." "He will hear and mock." "Let him hear and mock. Sometime he will be wise and sometime he will know. Sometime he is left by his people, he remains with his heart. He renounces his error. He gives thanks to his God." But they, what? "Who are you?" "We are Christians." "No, but spies." And they: "We are catholics." They tried to do injury; by better counsel they regretted it. And would that they so regretted remaining there, as they regretted who went in to do injury. Yet whom they cast out—Christians, believers, catholics; whom they keep, I do not want to say. Whom they cast out, I see; whom they keep, let them say themselves.

**32** Let them, then, say their voice. Let us see whether it is Christ's voice, whether it is the shepherd's voice, which the sheep follow. Whether through a good man or through an evil man this voice comes, let us attend to whether it is the shepherd's voice. The weak one seeks the Church, the straying one seeks the Church. What do you say? "The Church is of the party of Donatus." I inquire the voice of the shepherd. Read this to me from the prophet, read it to me from the psalm, recite it from the Law, recite it from the Gospel, recite it from the Apostle. From there I recite the Church spread throughout the world and the Lord saying: *Those that are my sheep hear my voice and follow me*[^87]. What is the voice of the shepherd? *And that repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name among all the nations, beginning at Jerusalem*[^88]. See the voice of the shepherd, recognize yourself, and follow it if you are a sheep.

**33** "But those handed over the books, and those set incense before idols, this man and that man." What is it to me about this man and that man? If they did it, they are not shepherds. Do you utter the voice of the shepherd, because you do not announce the voice of the shepherd even concerning them. You accuse, not the Gospel; you accuse, not the prophet, not the Apostle; of whom this voice speaks to me, of him I believe; others I do not believe. But you will produce Acts; I produce Acts. Let us believe yours; do you believe mine too. I do not believe yours; do not believe mine. Let human papers be taken away, let the divine voices sound. Utter to me one voice of Scripture for the party of Donatus; hear innumerable ones, throughout the world. Who counts them? Who sets their limit? Yet, to recall a few, attend to the Law, the first Testament of God: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^89]. And in the psalm: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession. All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will adore in his sight, for the kingdom is his and he will rule over the nations. Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth. And all the kings of the earth will adore him, all the nations will serve him*[^90]. Who could count them? Almost every page sounds nothing else than Christ, and the Church spread throughout the world. Let one voice come forth for me for the party of Donatus: what great thing is it that I ask? They say the Church spread throughout the world was to perish. Was that prophesied to perish which was foretold by so many testimonies to abide? Not even that one voice is the shepherd's, through the Law, the Prophets, the Songs—for those men could not speak the truth without the Word of God, which is Christ—hear the voice of the Word, and from the mouth of the Word.

**34** Marveling at the faith of the centurion: *Amen*, he said, *I say to you, I have not found such great faith in Israel. Therefore I say to you that many will come from the East and the West and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven*[^91]. From the East and the West many will come. See the Church of Christ, see the flock of Christ. Do you see whether you are a sheep. For the flock which is everywhere does not lie hidden from you. You will have nothing to answer your judge, whom you do not want to be your shepherd; you will have nothing, I say, to answer your judge: "I did not know, I did not see, I did not hear." What is it you did not know? *Nor is there one who can hide himself from his heat*[^92]. What is it you did not see? *All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God*[^93]. What is it you did not hear? *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^94].

**35** But rightly is the voice of Christ sought from you, the voice of the shepherd, which the sheep hear and follow. You find nothing to say, you do not have the voice of the shepherd. Hear and follow. Let go of the voice of the wolf, follow the voice of the shepherd. Or give the voice of the shepherd. "We give it," they say. Let us hear: "We too give the voice of the shepherd." Let us hear. "In the Song of Songs," they say, "the bride speaks to the bridegroom, the Church to Christ." We know the Song of Songs, the holy Song, the love Song, of holy love, of holy charity, of holy sweetness. I plainly want to hear from there the voice of the shepherd, the voice of the sweetest bridegroom. Utter it, if you have it. Let us hear. "The bride," they say, "says to the bridegroom: *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down.* And he," they say, "answers: *At noon*[^95]." I was producing for you manifest testimonies; there was no way you could interpret them otherwise: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession. All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord*[^96]. What is it that you produce to me from the Song of Songs? Which perhaps you do not understand. For those Songs are riddles, known to few who understand, opened to few who knock. Hold and devoutly receive what is open, that the obscure things may deservedly be laid open to you. How will you be a penetrator of obscure things, a despiser of manifest ones?

**36** See, however, as we can, brothers, let us examine these words. The Lord will be present that you may see there a sound understanding. First, what is most easily judged by all, even the unskilled, they wrongly punctuate the very words. Now you will hear, now you will prove it. For the text of the reading itself stands thus. The bride speaks to the bridegroom: *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down*. That the bride says it to the bridegroom, that the Church says it to Christ, neither we doubt, nor they. But hear all the words of the bride. Why do you want to assign to the bridegroom a word that still belongs to the bride? Let her say all that the bride says. Then the bridegroom will answer. Hear more evidently by this punctuation which I am about to make: you will find nothing more. *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down at noon*. She herself still says: *Where you feed, where you lie down at noon*. And see that she herself still says it. For it follows: *Lest perhaps I become as one veiled over the flocks of your companions*[^97]. I think all, skilled and unskilled, distinguish the masculine and feminine gender. "Veiled" [operta]—I ask of what gender it is. Of every man I ask: Is it masculine or feminine? *Tell me*, she says, *you whom my soul has loved*. When she says *you whom*, she addresses a male, addresses the bridegroom. But that the woman addresses a man, the following words indicate: *Tell me where you feed, where you lie down at noon, lest perhaps I become as one veiled*. Hear "veiled," that these things may be unveiled to you. *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down at noon, lest perhaps I become as one veiled over the flocks of your companions*. Thus far the words of the bride. Now begin the words of the bridegroom, manifestly: *Unless you know yourself*—know yourself manfully, woman: *unless*, he says, *you know yourself*; hear also the rest: *O beautiful among women*—*Unless you know yourself, O beautiful among women, go out in the tracks of the flocks and feed your kids in the tents of the shepherds*[^98], not in the tent of the shepherd. See how the bridegroom threatens; see how, in peril, although that sweet one, he took away the blandishments from the midst. How sweetly she! *"Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down at noon.* For the midday comes, when the shepherds run together to the shade; and perhaps it will be hidden from me where you feed and where you lie down; and I want you to tell me, lest perhaps I become as one veiled—that is, as one hidden, and not known. For I am manifest, but lest, as one veiled, as one concealed, I fall upon the flocks of your companions." For all heretics went out from Christ; all who became evil shepherds, having their own flocks under the name of Christ, were his companions, received his banquet. For "companions" [sodales] are so called, as it were, of one banquet. In the Latin tongue they are called sodales, as if "co-eaters" [simul edales], because they eat together. Hear him in the psalm reproaching his evil companions—that is, of one banquet: *If my enemy*, he says, *had reproached me, I would surely have borne it, and if he had spoken great things against me*. *But you, my soul-mate, and my familiar, my guide, who together with me took sweet food*[^99]. Therefore many ungrateful companions of the Lord's table went out; the evil companions made themselves their own tables, set up altars against the altar: upon these she fears to fall.

**37** And if you think that noon is Africa—although I could maintain rather that the noon of the world is the regions of Egypt, and those regions parched by the sun where rain does not appear, because that is the noon where the midday is hot. But there is the desert full of thousands of servants of God. Whence, if we want to attend to the noon of places, why should he not feed there rather and rest there, when it was foretold: *The breasts will be the deserts of the wilderness*?—But see, I consent, let noon be Africa, let Africa be noon. Here are the evil companions. The Church beyond the sea, in some one of hers sailing to Africa, anxious lest he stray, calls upon her bridegroom and says to him: "I hear there are heretics abounding in Africa, I hear there are rebaptizers abounding in Africa. But that your own are there, I hear no less. And I hear this, and I hear that. But who are yours, I want to hear from you. *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down at noon*, in that noon where I hear there are two parties, one the party of Donatus, the other cleaving to your whole. Do you tell me where I should go, *lest perhaps as one veiled*—that is, unknown—*I become over the flocks of your companions*, fall upon the flocks of heretics, who try to set stone upon stone that may be destroyed, lest I rush upon rebaptizers, *tell me*." And he who commends the unity of the shepherd, who in this reading said: *I will feed them*—but rejects the shepherds who wanted to be many, who lost unity—answering most severely, not with blandishment, but according to the greatness of the peril: *Unless you know yourself*, he says, *O beautiful among women*: you are beautiful among women, but know yourself. Where do you know yourself? In the whole world. For if you are beautiful, unity is in you. Where there is division, there is foulness, not beauty. *Unless you know yourself*: you believed in me, know yourself. How did you believe in me? Just as those evil companions too, confessing the Word made flesh, born of a Virgin, crucified, risen, ascended into heaven: in such a one you believed me, such they too proclaim. Know yourself and me: me in heaven, you in the whole world." Christ addresses any one out of the Church as the Church.—For how does the Church seek the Church? I speak according to them. *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down.* What does it seek? The Church. And he, as it were showing the Church, says: *At noon*, as they want. Let them answer me, how the Church seeks the Church. *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved.* Who speaks? The Church. What does she want told her? *Where you feed, where you lie down*—that is, where the Church is. The Church speaks, and asks where the Church is; and he answers, as they think: *At noon*. If it is only at noon, as they say, in Africa, how does she herself ask where she herself is? But indeed a portion of the Church beyond the sea well asks about the noon, lest it stray here.—Christ addresses each member of his Church as his Church. And what does he say? *Unless you know yourself, O beautiful among women, go out.* To go out belongs to heretics. Either know yourself, or go out, because if you do not know yourself, you will go out. Go out where? In the tracks of the flocks, by following evil flocks. Lest perhaps you think you follow sheep, if you go out, hear what follows: *Go out in the tracks of the flocks, and feed your kids*, now not sheep. You know, brothers, where the kids will be. At the left will be all who have gone out from the Church. To Peter, abiding, it is said: *Feed my sheep*[^100]; to the heretic, going out: *Feed your kids*.

**38** "There is," they say, "another testimony too." No less against you. Say it, let us hear. It will be against you, just as this which you thought was for you. "What if," they say, "you interpret noon as Egypt?" In many ways indeed we interpret noon: we can understand both Egypt as a place of the world, and Africa itself, thus. Hear what I understand by noon; I understand the fervor of spiritual men, blazing with the fire of love, shining with the light of truth. For it is said in a certain psalm: *Make known to me your right hand, and those instructed in heart in wisdom*[^101]—the right hand, not the kids; *and those instructed in heart in wisdom*, these are the noon. Whence it is said to the prophet: *And your darkness will be as noon*[^102]. In many ways, then, we can understand noon. But I plainly understand Africa, altogether, I understand Africa. I receive from you something perhaps better than I would savor, unless reminded by you. Let Africa be noon. The Church beyond the sea fears to fall upon rebaptizers; she fears to fall, as one unknown, upon the flocks of the companions; she asks of her bridegroom that he tell her where he feeds, where he lies down at noon, because in that very noon he feeds in some, does not feed in others; he lies down in some, does not lie down in others.—Let her hear the counsel, let her come to the catholic Church, let her not fall upon the flocks of the companions, let her not feed her kids.—But say the other thing you said you were going to say. "The prophet," he says, "says: *God will come from the south*; and where the south is, surely Africa." O testimony! God will come from the south, and from Africa God will come! The heretics announce that another Christ is born in Africa and goes through the world! I ask, what is it: God will come from Africa? If you said: God remained in Africa, you would surely say it shamefully. But now you even say: He will come from Africa. We know where Christ was born, where he suffered, where he ascended into heaven, where he sent his disciples, where he filled them with the Holy Spirit, where he commanded them to evangelize through the whole world, and they obeyed, and the world is filled with the Gospel.

**39** "Then do you," he says, "expound to me what it is: *God will come from the south*." Say the whole, and perhaps you will understand. *God will come from the south, and the holy one from the shady mountain*[^103]. Do you expound to me, if from Africa, how from the shady mountain? From Numidia the party of Donatus was born. They were sent first into dissension and tumult and scandal, seeking a huge wound. Numidians sent. Secundus of Tigisi sent. Where Tigisi is, is known. The clergy who were sent gathered outside, away from the Church; they refused to approach the clergy of Carthage, set up a visitor, were received by Lucilla. The author of all this evil was a Numidian heretic. In Numidia, whence one came here with such great evil, a fly-whisk is scarcely found, they dwell in huts. How a shady mountain in Numidia? Tell me, then. Do not recite only this far: *God from the south*; I require what follows: *And the holy one from the shady mountain*. But show me the party of Donatus coming from Numidia, from the shady mountain. You find everything bare—fat plains indeed, but for grain, not fertile with olive groves, not pleasant with other groves. Whence, then, a shady mountain in the parts of Numidia, whence this scandal came?

**40** "Do you, then," he says, "expound to me what it is: *God will come from the south, and the holy one from the shady mountain*." See how easily I expound it. First hear what the Lord said: *Christ had to suffer and rise again on the third day, and repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name among all the nations, beginning at Jerusalem*[^104]. See whence he will come: when he said *beginning*, he surely foretold that thence, in his saints, he would come to the other nations. Read the division of the land of the children of Israel among all the tribes in the book of Joshua. There it is openly said: *Jebus from the south, which is Jerusalem*[^105]. Read, seek, and you will find. Would that, when you find it, you would believe; would that you would lay aside your animosity. *Jebus from the south, which is Jerusalem.* And the Lord: *Beginning at Jerusalem*: this is: *God will come from the south*. How, then, from the shady mountain? Now read the Gospel. From the Mount of Olives Christ ascended into heaven. Follow on. And what is clearer? You hear: *from the south*; you have heard: *from the shady mountain*. We recite the Law. We recite the Gospel. You heard: *Beginning at Jerusalem*; hear: *Among all the nations*. In the same prophet follow those words which you despised, those words which you passed over: *God will come from the south, and the holy one from the shady mountain; his shadow will cover the mountains, and his glory fills the earth*[^106]. Among all the nations, then, beginning at Jerusalem. How, beginning at Jerusalem? *God will come from the south, and the holy one from the shady mountain*—that is, from the Mount of Olives, where he ascended into heaven, whence he sent his disciples, where, when about to ascend, he also said: *It is not for you to know the times which the Father has set in his own power, but you will receive power from on high, and you will be my witnesses*—see how the Gospel begins—*and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in Judea and Samaria, and to the whole earth*[^107]. Therefore, when God comes, Christ, both his name and the preaching of his Gospel *from Jerusalem*—that is, *from the south, and from the shady mountain*—that is, from the Mount of Olives, because the Gospel was spread among all the nations—*his shadow will cover the mountains*—that is, his refreshment, his protection—*and the earth is full of his praise. Sing*, then, with the whole earth, *a new song*, not the old song with a corner of the earth.

**41** They say another thing too. "A certain Simon of Cyrene," they say, "was compelled to carry the cross of the Lord." We read it, but how it helps you I want to know. "The Cyrenian," he says, "is an African. Therefore he himself was compelled to carry the cross." Where Cyrene is you perhaps do not know: it is Libya, it is the Pentapolis, it is contiguous to Africa, it belongs rather to the East. Or recognize it in the distribution of the provinces of the Emperors: the Eastern Emperor sends a judge to Cyrene. Briefly I answer. Where the party of Donatus is, Cyrene is not found; where Cyrene is, the party of Donatus is not found. The manifest truth convicts the error. Let him give me Cyrene, where the party of Donatus is; let him give me the party of Donatus, where Cyrene is. For it is manifest, brothers, that in the Pentapolis the Church is catholic, and the party of Donatus is not there. But let us, secure, mock those to be wept over, and weep over those to be mocked. What do you say? You recall the great merit of this Cyrenian, because he carried the cross of the Lord, and you call him an African. He is an Easterner. For Libya is named in two ways, either this which is properly Africa, or that part of the East which is contiguous to Africa, and altogether sharing its borders. But suppose the Cyrenian was an African. Do you think him blessed, that, compelled, he carried the cross? How much better would another perhaps say that the Church of Christ remained in Arimathea? Because that rich Joseph of Arimathea, having the kingdom of God before his eyes, came to the cross of the Lord not compelled, not forced. When the rest dreaded, he asked of Pilate the Lord's body to bury, took it down from the wood, attended the funeral, laid it in the tomb, was praised in the Gospel. Because, then, this devout man, showing such great service to the Lord's body, was of Arimathea, did the Church remain in Arimathea? Or if the one compelled—that is, who is forced to carry the cross—rather delights you, then the catholic Emperors do rightly, who compel you to unity.
