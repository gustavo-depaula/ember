# Sermon 111

**1.** . . . whom there arrogant pride had taken captive. *But now*, he says, *knowing God, or rather known by God* [^1]. Therefore knowing, because known. For how has God now known? *He predestined before the foundation of the world* [^2]. But what is: He has now known? He has now made you know.

**2.** Therefore let the Church of Christ say, let mother Church the Catholic say, let the body of that head which ascended into heaven say, the holy body, the great body, the body spread throughout the whole world, let that harvest say which the grain made that fell into the earth—for it says, as you know, that grain drawing near to its passion: *The grain*, he says, *unless it falls into the earth, remains alone; but when it has fallen into the earth, it brings forth much fruit* [^3]; one grain fell into the earth, and made a most copious fruit; this harvest is the whole world—let it say, and let it not say it arrogantly, let it acknowledge from whom it receives what it says, what it says. What, then, should it say? I have known that the Lord is great. I have known. I was a little flock when my head was crucified. In the few I was terrified. While Christ spoke, Peter presumed; with Christ seized, Peter denied. Because the rock was laid hold of, the feet wavered, and in Peter the rest fled. He alone denied, because he alone had remained with the one he denied. How much more easily could those deny, who before they were questioned fled, and did not hold fast. The harvest did not yet glory, because the grain was being sown. He is seized, afflicted with insults, hung on the wood, mocked by the bystanders as though by victors. Where are the supports of the Apostles? Where are the rams, the future leaders of the flock? Where, in short, is that great multitude, going before and following the Lord's mount with branches of trees, and saying: *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord* [^4]? Then all the many were lukewarm in love, pressed down by fear. The Church does not yet say, with Christ hanging: *I have known that the Lord is great*. Where do we find this? There, in those two with whom, as they made their way after the resurrection, he joined himself, and as a third traveler began to converse with them. They were going along the way, and the Way was going with them, and yet they did not recognize the Way. The Way questioned them on the way: *What is this that you discuss among yourselves?* And they: Are you alone *a stranger in Jerusalem? Do you not know the things that have been done concerning Jesus of Nazareth, who was a great prophet in words and deeds, and our rulers seized and crucified him? But we were hoping that he was the one who would redeem Israel* [^5]. You were hoping, you hope no longer. Where is what you were? But behold, in the crowd of gainsayers, of those raging and silencing the flock of Christ and awaiting what would come to be, behold, off to the side, someone hangs with Christ, and believes in Christ. I mean that thief, brothers, the one who knew the giver of grace, and did not despise the sharer of his penalty. He denies who followed, he acknowledges who is fixed. The rest are silent, all despair, and that one says: *Lord, remember me, when you come into your kingdom* [^6]. The one whom he discerns fastened, he presumed would reign. Behold, there was at least one to say: *I have known that the Lord is great*. Great grace. Then he knew him great when the Jews thought him conquered. What greatness, my brothers, what greatness in the one hanging, in the crucified? Hanging with the hanging one, fixed in the abiding one, he knew that he was a grain of mustard seed [^7]. The tree itself he did not yet see, and the seed itself he already knew. But behold, let the things that follow come to pass. Let him lay down his soul, because he has the power to take it up again [^8]. Let him be taken down from the wood, let him be laid in the tomb. Let him sit in heaven, let him send the Holy Spirit. Let the few gathered be filled, let them speak in all tongues, let them signify the nations everywhere about to believe. Let this come to pass. Let this too be fulfilled. Let it be preached in Jerusalem. Let Stephen be kindled by the fire of love . . . and the crowds of the Jews. Let them rage, let them in frenzy kill the physician, because they already killed his master. Let him pardon the raging, let him pray for those who stone him, let him sleep. Let the rest come and confess Christ, let them be killed for Christ. Let Peter himself, a little before the great denier, become by love a confessor. Let so many thousands of the peoples of the Jews believe, let them lay all the prices of their goods before the feet of the Apostles. Let the Church grow from Jerusalem into all Judea and Samaria, by growing let it come to us, that I may run through it briefly. Let the martyrs be killed everywhere, sown with the grain from which the fertility of the harvest may sprout. Let the nations be converted, let the temples of demons be cast down, let the images be broken, let the worshipers of idols believe. Let the three measures, leavened, cry out [^9]: *I have known that the Lord is great*. By the leaven it knew. (The three measures are the whole human race; recall the flood, from which the rest were restored; three remained; Noah had three sons, from them the human race was restored; that woman who hid the leaven is wisdom.) Behold, the whole world cries out in the Church of God: *I have known that the Lord is great*.

**3.** Certainly few are those who are saved. You recall the question from the Gospel just now recited to us. It was said to the Lord: *Are those few who are saved* [^10]? The Lord to this, what? He did not say: Not few, but many are those who are saved. He did not say this. But what did he say, when he had heard: *Are those few who are saved? Strive to enter through the narrow gate* [^11]. When, then, he had heard: *Are those few who are saved?* the Lord confirmed what he heard. Through the narrow gate few enter. In another place he himself says this: *The way is hard and narrow that leads to life, and few are they who enter by it. But broad and spacious is the way that leads to destruction, and many are they who walk by it* [^12]. Why should we rejoice at the multitudes? Hear me, you few. I know that many hear, and few obey. I see a threshing floor, I seek grains. And the grains are scarcely seen, when the floor is threshed, but it is to come that it be winnowed. Few, then, are those who are saved in comparison with the many lost. For those few will make a great heap. When the winnower comes, bearing *the winnowing fan in his hand, he will cleanse his threshing floor; the wheat he will store in the granary, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire* [^13]. Let not the chaff mock the grain. He speaks this truly, he will deceive no one. Be, then, among the many, many, but in comparison with certain of the many, few. So great a heap is to come forth from this floor, that it will fill the granary of heaven. For he is not contrary, who said: Few are they who enter through the narrow gate, many perish by the broad way. Would he be contrary to himself who once said: *Many will come from East and West* [^14]? Many will come, certainly few. Both few and many. Some, then, the few, others the many? No, but the same are the few who are the many: few in comparison with the lost, many in the fellowship of the angels. Hear, dearly beloved, hear what is written: *After this I saw, coming from every tongue and nation and tribe, with white robes and palms, a multitude which no one could number* [^15]. This is the heap of the saints. With how much clearer a voice will the winnowed floor speak, separated from the shipwrecked crowd of evil and false Christians, set apart from the chaff separated for the eternal fire—those who press, do not touch (for a certain woman touched the hem, the crowd pressed Christ [^16])—all the damnable being separated, then, the purged heap standing at the right hand, fearing none of the wicked to be mingled with it, fearing to lose none of the good, about to reign with Christ, with how much confidence will it say: *I have known that the Lord is great*.

**4.** If, then, my brothers, I speak to grains, if those predestined to eternal life acknowledge what I say, let them speak by works, not by words. We are compelled to say to you what we ought not. For we ought to find in you what we might praise, not to seek what we might admonish. Yet behold, I say it briefly, I do not linger. Acknowledge hospitality; through it one comes to God. You receive a guest, of whom you too are a companion on the way, because we are all pilgrims. He is a Christian who recognizes himself to be a pilgrim both in his own house and in his own homeland. Our homeland is above, there we shall not be guests. For each of us here is a guest even in his own house. If he is not a guest, let him not pass on from there; if he is going to pass on, he is a guest. Let him not deceive himself, he is a guest. Willy-nilly, he is a guest. But he leaves that house to his sons, a guest to guests. Indeed, even if you were in an inn, would you not depart when another came? This you do in your own house too. Your father gave place to you, you are going to give place to your sons. Neither remaining do you remain, nor do you leave it to those who will remain. If we all pass on, let us work something that cannot pass on, so that when we have passed on and come there from where we do not pass on, we may find our good works there. Christ the Lord is the keeper. Why do you fear lest you lose what you dole out? Turned toward the Lord, and so on.  
We bring to Your Charity's knowledge what it knows. The anniversary day of the ordination of the lord elder Aurelius dawns tomorrow. Through my humility he asks and admonishes Your Charity that you deign to assemble most devoutly at the basilica of Faustus. Thanks be to God.
