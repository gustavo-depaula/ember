# Sermon 272/A: On the Fiftieth Day of the Resurrection, a Fragment

**1.** The Lord Christ was humbled, that we might know to be humble: he who contains all things was conceived, he who begets all things was born, he who gives life to all things died; but after three days he rose, and ascended into heaven, and placed the human flesh which he had assumed at the right hand of the Father. It is marvelous, brothers—and this is what the impious refuse to believe—it is marvelous that a man rose in the flesh, and ascended into heaven with the flesh; but it is far more marvelous that the whole world believed a thing so incredible. What is more incredible, that God did such things, or that the world could believe? What if the very manner too, by which the world believed, be considered by us? It too is truly seen to be divine, and is found greatly marvelous. Men untaught in the liberal disciplines, and altogether, as far as pertains to the doctrines of the world, unpolished, not skilled in grammar, not armed with dialectic, fishermen, with the nets of faith Christ sent in fewest number into the sea of the age. Why do I say "in fewest number"? He sent twelve. And yet through them he so filled the churches with every kind of fish, that very many even of those wise of the world, to whom the cross of Christ seemed ignominious, are marked with it on their forehead, and set on the citadel of honor that of which they thought one ought to blush, and over which they insulted us.
