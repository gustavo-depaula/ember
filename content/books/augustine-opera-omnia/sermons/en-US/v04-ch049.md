# Sermon 131

**1.** We have heard the truthful Master, the divine Redeemer, the human Savior, commending to us our price, his blood. For he spoke to us of his body and blood; he called the body food, the blood drink. The faithful recognize the sacrament of the faithful. But those who hear, what else do they do than hear? When, then, commending such food and such drink, he said: *Unless you eat my flesh, and drink my blood, you will not have life in you* (and this he said of life—who else but life itself? But it will be death to that man, not life, who has thought life a liar); his disciples were scandalized, not indeed all, but very many, saying within themselves: *This saying is hard, who can hear it* [^1]? But when the Lord had known this within himself, and had heard the murmurs of their thought, he answered those thinking, and not making a sound with their voice, that they might know they had been heard, and cease to think such things. What, then, did he answer? *Does this scandalize you? If, then, you should see the Son of man ascending where he was before* [^2]? What does he mean: *Does this scandalize you?* Do you think that of this body of mine which you see, I am going to make parts, and cut up my members, and give them to you? What is: *If, then, you should see the Son of man ascending where he was before?* Surely he who could ascend whole, could not be consumed. So both from his body and blood he gave us a saving refreshment, and so great a question he resolved briefly from his wholeness. Let those eat, then, who eat, and those drink who drink; let them hunger and thirst: let them eat life, let them drink life. That eating is to be refreshed: but you are so refreshed, that that from which you are refreshed does not fail. That drinking, what is it, except to live? Eat life, drink life: you will have life, and life is whole. But then this will be, that is, life will be to each one the body and blood of Christ; if what in the sacrament is taken visibly, in the very truth is eaten spiritually, drunk spiritually. For we have heard the Lord himself saying: *It is the Spirit that gives life, but the flesh profits nothing. The words I have spoken to you are spirit and life. But there are*, he says, *some who do not believe* [^3]. They said: *This saying is hard, who can hear it?* It is hard, but to the hard: that is, incredible, but to the unbelieving.

**2.** But that he might teach us that believing itself too is of gift, not of merit: *As*, he says, *I told you, no one comes to me, unless it has been given him by my Father* [^4]. But where the Lord said this, if we recall the earlier parts of the Gospel, we shall find that he said: *No one comes to me, unless the Father who sent me draws him* [^5]. He did not say: "Leads him"; but: *draws him*. That violence is done to the heart, not the flesh. Why, then, do you wonder? Believe, and you come; love, and you are drawn. Do not think this a harsh and troublesome violence: it is sweet, it is pleasant; that very sweetness draws you. Is not a sheep drawn, when grass is shown to it as it hungers? And I think it is not driven by the body, but bound by desire. So you too, come to Christ: do not meditate long journeys; where you believe, there you come. For to him who is everywhere one comes by loving, not by sailing. But since even in such a journey waves and storms of various temptations abound; believe in the crucified, that your faith may be able to ascend the wood. You will not be drowned, but will be carried by the wood. So, so on the waves of this world sailed the one who said: *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ* [^6].

**3.** But it is a wonder that, Christ crucified being preached, two hear, one despises, the other ascends. Let him who despises impute it to himself; let him who ascends not arrogate it to himself. For he heard from the truthful Master: *No one comes to me, unless it has been given him by my Father* [^7]. Let him rejoice, because it has been given: let him give thanks to the giver with a humble heart, not an arrogant one; lest what the humble deserved, the proud lose. For even those who already walk in that just way, if they attribute it to themselves and their own strength, perish from it. Therefore, teaching us humility, the holy Scripture through the Apostle says: *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation*. And lest they ascribe anything from it to themselves, because he said: *work out*, he at once added: *For it is God who works in you both to will and to work, for good will* [^8]. *It is God who works in you*; therefore: *with fear and trembling*, make a valley, receive the shower. The low places are filled, the high are dried up. Grace is rain. Why, then, do you wonder, if God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble [^9]? Therefore: *with fear and trembling*; that is: "with humility." *Do not be high-minded, but fear* [^10]. *Fear*, that you may be filled; *do not be high-minded*, lest you be dried up.

**4.** But now, you say, I walk this way; it was necessary that I learn, it was necessary that I know through the teaching of the Law what I should do: I have free choice of will; who will separate me from this way? If you read diligently, you will find that someone, out of a certain abundance of his, which yet he had received, began to lift himself up; but the merciful Lord, to teach humility, took away what he had given; and he suddenly remained destitute, and, confessing through remembrance the mercy of God, said: *I said in my abundance: I shall not be moved forever. I said in my abundance*. But *I said*; I, a man, said it: *Every man is a liar* [^11]. *I said*. Therefore: *I said in my abundance*; so great was the abundance, that I dared to say this: *I shall not be moved forever*. What then? *Lord, in your will you bestowed strength on my beauty. But you turned away your face from me, and I was made troubled* [^12]. You showed me, he says, that that in which I abounded was of yours. You showed me from where I should ask, to whom I should attribute what I had received, to whom I ought to give thanks, to whom I should run thirsting, from whom I should be filled, and, filled, to whom I should keep it. *For my strength I will keep to you* [^13], so that by your bestowing I may be filled, by your keeping I may not lose. *My strength I will keep to you*. That you might show me this: *You turned away your face from me, and I was made troubled. Troubled*, because dried up; dried up, because exalted. Say, then, dry and parched, that you may be filled again: *My soul is like land without water to you*. Say: *My soul is like land without water to you* [^14]. For you had said, the Lord had not said: *I shall not be moved forever*. You had said it presuming upon yourself; but not upon your own, and you thought it as it were of your own.

**5.** What, then, does the Lord say? *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult to him with trembling* [^15]. So too the Apostle: *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation, for it is God who works in you* [^16]. Therefore: *Exult with trembling. Lest at any time the Lord be angry*. I see that by crying out you anticipate me. For you know what I am going to say, by crying out you anticipate. And whence have you this, except that he taught you to whom by believing you have come? This, then, he says: Hear what you know; I do not teach, but recall by preaching: rather I do not teach, because you know it; nor recall, because you remember; but let us say together what you hold with us. This the Lord says: *Lay hold of discipline, and exult*, but: *with trembling*, that you may always humbly hold what you have received. *Lest at any time the Lord be angry*, surely at the proud, attributing to themselves what they have, not giving thanks to him from whom they have it. *Lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you perish from the just way*. Did he say: Lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you not come to the just way? Or: Lest at any time the Lord be angry, and he not lead you to the just way, or not admit you to the just way? You now walk in it, do not be proud, lest you perish even from it. *And you perish*, he says, *from the just way. When his anger blazes up shortly* upon you. Not for long. Where you are proud, there you lose what you had received. Frightened by these things, a man, as though saying: What then shall I do? There follows: *Blessed are all who trust in him* [^17]; not in themselves, but: *in him*. By grace we have been saved, not of ourselves, but it is the gift of God [^18].

**6.** Perhaps you say: What does it mean that he says this often? Again this, and a third time this: and almost never does he speak, except when he says this. Would that I do not say it without cause. For there are men ungrateful to grace, attributing much to a needy and wounded nature. It is true, man received great powers of free choice when he was created; but by sinning he lost them. He slipped down into death, was made weak, was left half-alive on the road by robbers; the passing Samaritan, which is interpreted "Keeper," lifted him onto his mount; he is still being led to the inn [^19]. Why is he lifted up? He is still being cured. But it suffices, he says, that in Baptism I received the remission of all sins. Is it that, because iniquity was blotted out, weakness is ended? I received, he says, the remission of all sins. It is utterly true. All sins were blotted out in the sacrament of Baptism, all utterly, spoken, done, thought, all were blotted out. But this is what was poured in on the road, oil and wine. You retain, dearly beloved, how that half-alive man wounded by robbers on the road was consoled, receiving oil and wine for his wounds [^20]. Now surely his straying had been pardoned, and yet the languor is healed in the inn. The inn, if you recognize it, is the Church. The inn now, because by living we pass on: it will be a house, from which we shall never migrate, when we come whole to the kingdom of heaven. Meanwhile in the inn let us be willingly cured, let us not, still languid, glory in health; lest by being proud we do nothing else, than that by never being cured we are healed.

**7.** *Bless the Lord, my soul*. Say to your soul, say: You are still in this life, you still bear fragile flesh, the body that is corrupted still weighs down the soul [^21]; still, after the wholeness of remission you have received the remedy of prayer; still surely you say, until your languors are healed: *Forgive us our debts* [^22]. Say, then, to your soul, a humble valley, not an upraised hill; say to your soul: *Bless the Lord, my soul, and do not forget all his repayments*. What repayments? Say, enumerate, give thanks. What repayments? *Who is merciful to all your iniquities*. This was done in Baptism. What comes to be now? *Who heals all your languors*. This comes to be now: I acknowledge it. But as long as I am here, the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul. Say, then, also what follows: *Who redeems your life from corruption* [^23]. After the redemption from corruption what remains? *When this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality, then will come to pass the saying that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where is, O death, your contention* [^24]? There rightly: *Where is, O death, your sting?* You seek its place, and do not find it. What is the sting of death? What is: *Where is, O death, your sting?* Where is sin? You seek it, and it is nowhere. *For the sting of death is sin* [^25]. They are the Apostle's words, not mine. Then it will be said: *Where is, O death, your sting?* Nowhere will there be sin, neither that may seize you, nor that may attack you, nor that may tickle the conscience. Then it will not be said: *Forgive us our debts* [^26]. But what will be said? *O Lord our God, give us peace: for you have repaid us all things*.

**8.** In short, after the redemption from all corruption, what remains except the crown of righteousness? It surely remains, but even in it, or under it, let not the head be swollen, so as to receive the crown. Hear, attend to the Psalm, how that crown does not want a swollen head. When it had said: *Who redeems your life from corruption; Who crowns you*, it says. Now here you were going to say: *He crowns you*, my merits confess it, my virtue did this: a debt is repaid, not granted. Hear rather the Psalm. For this too you say: *Every man is a liar* [^27]. Hear what God says: *Who crowns you in compassion and mercy* [^28]. Out of mercy he crowns you, out of compassion he crowns you. For you were not worthy that he should call you, and call you justify you, and justified glorify you. *A remnant has been saved through the election of grace. But if by grace, then no longer of works: otherwise grace is no longer grace* [^29]. *For to the one who works, the wage is not reckoned according to grace, but according to debt* [^30]. The Apostle speaks: *Not according to grace, but according to debt*. But he crowns you in compassion and mercy: and if your merits preceded, God says to you: Examine well your merits, and you will see that they are my gifts.

**9.** This, then, is the righteousness of God. Just as it is said: *the salvation of the Lord* [^31], not by which the Lord is saved, but which he gives to those whom he saves: so too the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord is called the righteousness of God, not by which the Lord is just, but by which he justifies those whom he makes just from the ungodly. But some, just as the Jews once, both want to be called Christians, and, still being ignorant of the righteousness of God, want to establish their own, even in our times, in the times of open grace, in the times when the grace once hidden is now revealed, in the times when grace is manifest now on the threshing floor, which once lay hidden in the fleece. I see that few have understood, more have not understood, whom I will by no means defraud by being silent. One of the ancient just men, Gideon, asked a sign of the Lord, and said: *I ask, Lord, that this fleece which I set on the threshing floor be drenched, and the threshing floor be dry*. It was done: the fleece was drenched, the whole threshing floor was dry. He wrung out the fleece in the morning into a basin; since to the humble grace is given: and in the basin you know what the Lord did to his disciples [^32]. Again he asked another sign: *I want*, he says, *Lord, that the fleece be dry, the threshing floor drenched* [^33]. And this too was done. Recall the time of the Old Testament, grace is hidden in the cloud, like rain in the fleece. Attend now to the time of the New Testament, examine the nation of the Jews, you will find it like a dry fleece: but the whole world, like that threshing floor, is full of grace not hidden, but manifest. Whence we are compelled to lament much our brothers, who contend not against hidden grace, but against open and manifest grace. The Jews are pardoned. What of the Christians? why are they enemies of the grace of Christ? Why presuming upon themselves? why ungrateful? For why did Christ come? Was not nature here? Was not nature here, which you deceive by praising it much? Was not the Law here? But the Apostle says: *If righteousness is through the Law, then Christ died in vain* [^34]. What the Apostle says of the law, this we say to these of nature: If righteousness is through nature, then Christ died in vain.

**10.** What, then, was said of the Jews, this we see altogether in these. They have zeal for God. *I bear them witness, that they have zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*. What is: *Not according to knowledge?* *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wanting to establish their own, they are not subject to the righteousness of God* [^35]. My brothers, suffer with me. Where you find such men, do not conceal it, let there not be in you a perverse mercy: utterly, where you find such men, do not conceal it. Refute the gainsayers, and bring those who resist to us. For already on this cause two councils have sent to the Apostolic See: thence too rescripts have come. The cause is finished: would that the error might one day be finished! Therefore we admonish that they may take note, we teach that they may be instructed, we pray that they may be changed.
