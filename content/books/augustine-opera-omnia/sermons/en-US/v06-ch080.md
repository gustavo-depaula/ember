# Sermon 229/K: A Tractate on the Thursday of Easter

**1.** It is good that you should be reminded of what you are accustomed to hear every year; for just as what is written is not enough to be read once, so what is not understood is not enough to be expounded once. Let those, then, who hold in mind and remember well what they have heard and understood, listen patiently, while those also who have perhaps forgotten are instructed, or those hear who have perhaps not yet heard. For it raises a question, why the Lord Jesus Christ said to the faithful woman who sought to reach the body of her Lord, which she could not find laid in the tomb: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*[^1]. For if he was unwilling to be touched before he had ascended to the Father, then surely he had not yet ascended to the Father when he said to the disciples: *See my hands and my feet; handle and see*[^2]. He was unwilling to be touched, who was willing to be handled. Hence arises a question certainly to be resolved: for no one has heard it said that he was willing to be touched by men before he had ascended to the Father, but kept himself to be touched by women only when he had ascended to the Father. When he was ascending to the Father, or when he had ascended to the Father, who indeed touched him? Did he then keep himself to be touched by women, when he could not have been touched even by men? And yet, on account of love, Saul the persecutor not only touched him, but even laid him waste on earth, when he said from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^3] For the Lord Christ is both there at the right hand of the Father, and here labors on earth; there he is, but here are his members; there he is who is to judge the living and the dead, and the same one is here in his own, of whom he is to say: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^4]. What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*? Christ is better touched by faith than by the flesh; to touch Christ by faith, this is to touch him. So that woman who suffered from a flow of blood drew near with faith, and touched the garment with her hand, the majesty by her faith. And see what it is to touch. Then the Lord, who was being pressed by the crowds, was touched by one. For he said: *Who touched me?* The disciples, marveling because the multitude pressed him on every side, answered: *The crowds press you, and you say: Who touched me?* And he: *Someone touched me*[^5]. For if the crowds press you, they do not touch you: how did she touch you, except that she believed?

**2.** And now, my brothers, Jesus is in heaven. When he was before the disciples, according to the visible flesh, according to the touchable bodily substance, he was both seen and touched; but now, since he sits at the right hand of the Father, who of us can touch him? And yet woe to us, unless we touch him by faith. We all touch him, who believe. Certainly he is in heaven, certainly he is far off, certainly it cannot be estimated by how great an interval of space he is distant from us. Believe, and you touch. Why do I say, you touch? Because you believe, you have with you the one in whom you believe. If, then, to believe is to touch, or rather if to touch is to believe, what does this mean: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*? What is this? Why do you seek my flesh, you who do not yet understand my divinity? Do you wish to know how that woman wanted to touch him? She sought him as one dead, whom she did not believe would rise. *They have taken away my Lord from the tomb*[^6]; and she weeps for him as for a man. O what a touch! Because, then, he saw her occupied with the form of a servant, and she knew not how to taste, or believe, or understand the form of God, by which he is equal to the Father, he defers the touch, that he may fulfill the touch. *Do not touch me,* he says, *for I have not yet ascended to my Father.* You touch me before I ascend to the Father, and you believe in me as only a man; what does it profit you, that you believe? Let me, then, ascend to the Father. From there I never departed; I ascend for you, if you have believed me equal to the Father. For our Lord Jesus Christ did not so descend from the Father as to forsake the Father; for even what ascended from us did not depart from us. For about to ascend and to sit at the right hand of the Father, he yet says to the disciples far off: *Behold, I am with you, even to the consummation of the age*[^7].

[^1]: John 20:17
[^2]: Luke 24:39
[^3]: Acts 9:4
[^4]: Matt 25:40
[^5]: Luke 8:45-46
[^6]: John 20:13
[^7]: Matt 28:20
