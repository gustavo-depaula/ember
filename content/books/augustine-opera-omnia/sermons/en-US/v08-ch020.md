# Sermon 290: On the Birthday of John the Baptist

**1. 1.** Saint John—not the Evangelist, but the Baptist—was sent before Christ’s face to prepare his ways. Christ’s testimony about John is, *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*. John’s testimony about Christ is, *He who comes after me is greater than I, and I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandal*. Let us consider both testimonies, the Lord’s about the servant and the servant’s about the Lord. What is the Lord’s testimony about the servant? *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist.* What is the servant’s testimony about the Lord? *He who comes after me is greater than I.* If, then, among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist, what is the one who is greater than he? John is a great man, but a man; Christ is greater than John because he is God and man. Both were born wondrously: the herald and the judge, the lamp and the day, the voice and the Word, the servant and the Lord. The servant was born of a barren woman, the Lord of a virgin. The Lord himself made his servant for himself in a barren womb, from an aged father and an elderly mother. And that same Lord made flesh for himself in a virgin’s womb, without a human father—he who made the first man without father or mother. *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist.* John seemed so great that some even thought he was the Christ. He did not follow another’s error through his own pride, nor dare to say, “I am what you think.” Instead, for his own good, he recognized himself, so that the servant might humble himself at the Lord’s feet, even at the strap of his sandal, and the lamp might not be extinguished by the wind of pride.

**2. 2.** Finally, because John was born in a great mystery, his alone among the righteous is the birthday the Church celebrates. The Lord’s birthday is celebrated too, but as the Lord’s. Show me another servant besides John, among the patriarchs, prophets, or apostles, whose birthday Christ’s Church celebrates. We celebrate the day of suffering of very many servants, but the day of birth of none except John. When the Gospel was read, you heard the order of the two births, of the forerunner and the ruler, or, as I said a little earlier, of the herald and the judge, of the voice and the Word. The angel Gabriel announces John; that same angel Gabriel announces the Lord Jesus Christ. The one goes before, the other follows; the one goes before in service, the other follows in sovereignty. For he follows in being born, but goes before in ruling, because Christ himself created John, after whom Christ was created: both Creator and created, Creator before his mother, Creator of his mother, created in his mother. And why do I say, “Creator before his mother”? *Before Abraham, I am,* he himself said. The Gospel speaks: listen or read. But “Creator before Abraham” says little: Creator before Adam, Creator before heaven and earth, Creator before all angels and the entire spiritual creation, Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, and Powers—Creator before everything. For *in the beginning* the Word was not made, but *was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him*. If all things, then visible and invisible things, heaven and earth, and the Virgin Mary; for the Virgin Mary too was of the earth, and Christ the maker of the earth was made from the earth, because *truth has sprung from the earth*.

**3. 3.** I therefore briefly commend a great mystery to Your Charity. Because there would be many who thought Christ was merely a man, nothing more than a man, John—a great man, than whom there was no greater among human beings—bore witness to him in submission, bowing and humbling himself. How greatly he would have humbled himself if he had said he was worthy to untie the strap of Christ’s sandal! Consider the strap of the sandal in this great mystery. How humble John would have been even if he had said he was worthy! What did he do by saying he was unworthy? For this reason the day of his birth was marked and commended to the Church’s celebration.

**4. 4.** Yet there is a very great difference, not only between the mothers, one a virgin and the other a barren wife: the one bearing God’s Son, our Lord, through the Holy Spirit, the other bearing the Lord’s forerunner by her aged husband. Consider this too. Zechariah did not believe. How did he fail to believe? He asked the angel how he would know what the angel promised, since he himself was old and his wife advanced in years. And the angel said to him, *Behold, you will be silent and unable to speak until the day these things happen, because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their time.* That same angel comes to Mary and announces that Christ will be born of her in the flesh, and Mary says something similar. For Zechariah said, *How shall I know this? For I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in years.* And he is told, *Behold, you will be silent and unable to speak until the day these things are fulfilled, because you did not believe my words.* He received the punishment of silence as the due reward of unbelief. What had the prophet said about John? *The voice of one crying in the wilderness*. Zechariah, who is to beget the voice, is silent. Because he did not believe, he was silent; fittingly, he became mute until the voice was born. For if it is rightly said—or rather, because it is very rightly said—in the holy psalm, *I believed, therefore I spoke*, then because he did not believe, fittingly he did not speak. But I ask you, Lord, together with my hearers I knock: open to us, explain to us what this question means. Zechariah asks the angel for the grounds on which he will know what was announced to him, since he was old and his wife advanced in years. He is told, *Because you did not believe, you will be silent.* Christ is announced to the Virgin Mary, and she too asks how it can be, saying to the angel, *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* He said, *How shall I know this? For I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in years.* She said, *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* He is told, “You will be silent because you do not believe.” But to her the explanation is given, and silence is not imposed. *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* And the angel says, *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.* This is how what you ask about will happen; this is how you will give birth without knowing a man. This is how: because *the Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.* Do not fear the heat of lust under the shade of such holiness. Why this difference? If we consider their words, either both believed or both doubted, Zechariah and Mary. But we can hear words; God can also question hearts.

**5. 5.** We understand, dearly beloved, that when Zechariah said, *How shall I know this? For I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in years,* he spoke out of despair, not inquiry. But when Mary, in contrast, said, *How will this happen, since I do not know a man,* she spoke out of inquiry, not despair. In asking her question, she did not doubt the promise. O truly full of grace! For that is how the angel greeted her: *Hail, full of grace.* Who can explain this grace? Who can give sufficient thanks for this grace? Man is made, and through free choice man perishes; and the one who made him is found to have become man, so that the one he made might not perish. In the beginning the Word, God with God, through whom all things were made, becomes flesh: *The Word became flesh and dwelt among us*. The Word becomes flesh, but the flesh is added to the Word; the Word does not perish in the flesh. O grace! What had we deserved, that we should have this?

**6. 6.** But see what holy Mary herself says, full of faith, full of grace, destined to become a mother and remain a virgin. What does she say among the other things, each of which would take a very long time to discuss? What does she say? *He has filled the hungry with good things, and sent the rich away empty*. Who are the hungry? The humble, the needy. Who are the rich? The proud and puffed up. I do not send you far: I will show you now, in a single temple, a rich man among those sent away empty and a poor man among those filled with good things. *Two men went up to the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector. The Pharisee said.* What did he say? Listen to a rich man belching up what he has not digested, breathing out his overindulgence—but in pride, not righteousness: *God,* he says, *I thank you that I am not like other people, robbers, unjust, adulterers, or like this tax collector. I fast twice a week; I give tithes of everything I possess.* Had you come to ask, or to praise yourself? You said you had everything; you asked for nothing as a person in need. How, then, had you come to pray? *I thank you, Lord.* He does not say, “Lord, give me grace.” *For I am not like other people, robbers, unjust, adulterers.* Are you alone righteous, then? *For I am not like this tax collector.* You are taunting, not rejoicing. *I fast twice a week; I give tithes of everything I possess.* O rich man destined to be emptied! Come, come, poor, hungry tax collector—or rather, stand where you are standing. For *the tax collector* *stood at a distance.* But the Lord was drawing near to the humble man. *He did not dare even to raise his eyes to heaven.* His heart was where he did not raise his eyes. *But he struck his breast, saying, Lord, be merciful to me, a sinner.* O hungry man destined to be filled with good things!

**6. 7.** You have heard the dispute, Lord; pronounce the verdict. Hear the judgment pronounced between the parties. The defeated man does not appeal, because there is no one to whom he can appeal. He cannot appeal from the Son to the Father, for God *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*. Let Truth therefore pronounce the verdict between the parties. *Amen,* he says, *I tell you that this man went down from the temple justified, rather than that Pharisee.* Why this, I ask you? By what justice? Do you want to hear? *Because everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted*. By the one who exalts this man, the one who exalts himself will also be humbled. For *he has filled the hungry with good things, and sent the rich away empty.* Go now and parade your riches; boast and say, “I am rich.” How rich? “If I wish, I am righteous; if I do not wish, I am not righteous. It is in my power to be righteous or not to be righteous.” Do you not hear in the psalm, *Those who trust in their own strength*? So God gave you flesh, God gave you sense, God gave you a soul, God gave you a mind, God gave you understanding—and you give yourself righteousness? What is flesh, what are the senses, what is the soul, what is the mind, what is understanding without righteousness? Will not all these things, if they lack righteousness, serve for punishment? Are you so rich, then, that while God gave you the lesser things, you give yourself the greater? Unhappily rich, rich man destined to be emptied! Yet even if you have what you said you had, *what do you have that you did not receive*? You have not even learned from that proud, rich Pharisee to thank the Lord for the things you said you possessed.
