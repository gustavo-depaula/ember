# Sermon 95

**1.** In expounding the holy Scriptures to you, we are as it were breaking bread for you. Receive it, you who are hungry, and belch forth the fat of praise from your heart; and you who are rich at the banquet, do not be lean in good works and deeds. What I dispense to you is not my own. What you eat, I eat; from what you live, I live. We have a common storeroom in heaven, for from there comes the word of God.

**2.** The seven loaves signify the sevenfold working of the Holy Spirit; the four thousand men, the Church established under the four Gospels; the seven baskets of fragments [^1], the perfection of the Church. For perfection is very often figured by this number. For why is it that it was said: *Seven times a day I will praise you* [^2]? Does a man go astray who praises the Lord so often? What then is the meaning of *seven times I will praise*, except, I will never cease from praise? For the one who says *seven times* signifies the whole of time. Hence by the cycle of seven days the ages roll on. What then is the meaning of *seven times a day I will praise you*, except what is said in another place: *His praise shall be always in my mouth* [^3]? It is on account of this very perfection that John writes to seven churches. The Apocalypse is a book of the holy evangelist John: he writes to seven churches [^4]. Be truthful; recognize the baskets. For those fragments did not perish; rather, since you too belong to the Church, they have benefited you. As for the fact that I expound these things to you, with Christ as minister—you, while you listen quietly, are reclining at table. I sit in body, I stand in heart, and anxiously I serve you, lest perhaps the little vessel, rather than the food, offend any of you. You know the banquet of our God, you have often heard of it; it is minds that seek it, not bellies.

**3.** Certainly the four thousand men were filled from seven loaves: what could be more wonderful? And yet it would have been little, unless seven baskets had also been filled from the remaining fragments. O great mysteries! They were performing deeds, and the deeds were speaking. If you understand those deeds, they are words. And you too belong to the four thousand, because you live under the fourfold Gospel. The boys and the women were not counted in the number. For it was said thus: *Now those who ate were four thousand men, besides boys and women* [^5]. As though the foolish and the effeminate were without number. Yet let these too eat. Let them eat: perhaps the boys grow up, and will be boys no longer; perhaps the effeminate are corrected and made chaste. Let them eat: we dispense, we expend. But who they are, God examines at his own banquet, and if they do not correct themselves, he who knows how to invite knows also how to separate.

**4.** You know, dearly beloved: call to mind the gospel parable; for the Lord came in to inspect those reclining at a certain banquet of his. The householder who had given the invitation, as it is written, *found there a man not clothed in a wedding garment* [^6]. For that bridegroom, beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, had invited them to a wedding. That bridegroom became disfigured on account of his disfigured bride, so as to make her beautiful. How did the disfigured one become beautiful? If I do not prove it, I blaspheme. One prophet gives me testimony of his beauty, saying: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men* [^7]. Another prophet gives me testimony of his disfigurement, saying: *We saw him, and he had no form or comeliness; but his face was cast down, his bearing disfigured* [^8]. O prophet, you who said, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*, you are contradicted: another prophet comes forward against you and says: You lie: *We saw him*. What is this that he says, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men? We saw him having no form or comeliness*. So do these two prophets disagree in the corner of peace? Both spoke of Christ, both spoke of the cornerstone. In the corner the walls come together. If they did not come together, it is not a building but a ruin. The prophets agree; let us not leave them in a quarrel. Rather, let us recognize their peace: for they do not know how to quarrel. O prophet, you who said, *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*, where did you see him? Answer, answer, where did you see him? *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped* [^9]. There I saw him. Or do you doubt that he who is equal to God is beautiful beyond the sons of men? You have answered: let the one answer who said, *We saw him, and he had no form or comeliness*. You have spoken: say where you saw him. He takes his beginning from the other's very words: where the one ended, there the other begins. Where did the one end? *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped* [^10]. See where he saw him beautiful in form beyond the sons of men: now you, say where you saw that he had no form or comeliness. *But he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man* [^11]. Of his disfigurement he says still more: *He humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross* [^12]. See where I saw him. So both peacemakers agree, and both are at peace. What is more beautiful than God? What more disfigured than the Crucified?

**5.** This bridegroom, then, *beautiful in form beyond the sons of men* [^13], made disfigured so as to make his bride beautiful—she to whom it is said, *O beautiful among women* [^14], of whom it is said, *Who is this that comes up made white* [^15], made bright, not darkened by the color of a lie?—this one, then, who had called them to the wedding, found a man not having a wedding garment, and said to him: *Friend, how did you come in here without a wedding garment? But he was struck dumb*. For he found nothing to answer. And the householder who had come in said: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness: there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth* [^16]. So great a penalty for so small a fault? Great indeed. It is called a small fault, not to have a wedding garment: small, but only to those who do not understand. Why would he be so angry, why would he pass such judgment, that on account of a wedding garment which the man did not have he should send him with bound hands and feet into the outer darkness, where there would be weeping and gnashing of teeth, unless it were a very grave fault not to have a wedding garment? I say this: because you were invited through me, and if he invited you, he invited you through us. You are all at the banquet; have a wedding garment. I expound what it is, so that you may all have it; and if anyone is now listening to me who does not have it, before the householder comes and inspects his guests, let him change for the better, let him receive the wedding garment, let him recline in security.

**6.** For in truth, dearly beloved, that man who was cast out from there does not signify one single man: far from it. They are many. And the Lord himself, who put forward this parable—the Bridegroom himself who called the guests together and gives the guests life—he himself expounded to us that that man signifies not one man but many, there, in that very place, in the same parable. I do not go far afield, I expound it right there, right there I break it and set it out to be eaten. For when that man who did not have a wedding garment had been sent into the outer darkness, he then went on and added: *For many are called, but few are chosen* [^17]. You cast out one from here, and you say: *For many are called, but few are chosen*. Without doubt the chosen were not cast out: and they were the few who had remained reclining; and there were many in that one man, because that one man is one body of the wicked, who does not have a wedding garment.

**7.** What is the wedding garment? Let us seek it in the holy Writings. What is the wedding garment? Without doubt it is something that the wicked and the good do not have in common: let us find this, and we find the wedding garment. Among the gifts of God, what is there that the good and the wicked do not have in common? That we are men and not beasts is a gift of God: but this we have in common with the good and the wicked. That light rises for us from heaven, that rains come down from the cloud, that springs flow, that fields bear fruit—these are gifts, but common to good and wicked. Let us enter into the wedding; let us leave outside the others who, though called, did not come. Let us consider the guests themselves, that is, the Christians. Baptism is a gift of God; the good and the wicked have it. The good and the wicked alike receive the sacraments of the altar. Wicked Saul, hostile to a holy and most just man, prophesied; while he was persecuting him, he prophesied [^18]. Are only the good said to believe? *The demons also believe, and tremble* [^19]. What am I to do? I have shaken out everything, and I have not yet reached that garment. I have unrolled my bundle, I have considered everything, or nearly everything, and I have not yet reached that garment. In a certain place the apostle Paul brought me a great bundle of great things; he laid it out before me, and I said to him, Show me whether perhaps here you have found that wedding garment. He began to shake out each thing and to say: *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, if I have all knowledge and prophecy and all faith, so as to move mountains; if I distribute all I have to the poor, and hand over my body to be burned* [^20]. Great garments: yet none of them is the wedding garment. Now bring out to us the wedding garment. Why do you keep us in suspense, Apostle? Prophecy perhaps is a gift of God that the good and the wicked do not have. *If I do not have love*, he says, *I am nothing, it profits me nothing* [^21]. Behold the wedding garment: clothe yourselves, O guests, so that you may recline in security. Do not say: We are too poor to have that garment. Clothe others, and be clothed. It is winter: clothe the naked: Christ is naked; and whoever of you does not have the wedding garment, he will give it. Run to him, ask him: he knows how to sanctify his faithful, he knows how to clothe his naked ones. So that, having the wedding garment, you may not fear the outer darkness, the binding of limbs and hands and feet; let your works not fail. If they fail, what will he do whose hands are bound? Where will he flee whose feet are bound? Hold fast that wedding garment, clothe yourselves with it, and recline in security, when he comes to inspect. The day of judgment will be here: he who once was naked is now granted a great span of time in which to be clothed.
