# Sermon 44

**1** Of our Lord and Savior, dearly beloved brothers, it was prophesied long ago: *He will come up like a shoot, and like a root in thirsty ground*[^1]. Why like a root? For this reason: *He has no comeliness, nor honor*[^2]. He suffered, he was humbled, he was spat upon: he had no comeliness; he appeared as man, though he was God. But just as a root is not beautiful, yet within has the power of its own beauty. Attend, my brothers, see the mercy of God. You attend to a beautiful tree, pleasant with green leaves, you praise it rich with fruits. It delights you to pluck some of its fruit, to sit under its shade and rest from the heat: you praise all that beauty. If the root be shown you, there is no beauty in it. Do not despise what is abject: from it came forth what you marvel at. *Like a root in thirsty ground*. Now attend to the splendor of the tree.

**2** The Church has grown, the nations have believed, the princes of the earth have been conquered under the name of Christ, that they might be conquerors in the world. Their neck has been set under the yoke of Christ. Before, they persecuted Christians on account of idols; now they persecute idols on account of Christ. All flee to the help of the Church in every distress, in every tribulation of theirs. That grain of mustard has grown, has become greater than all the herbs: the birds of heaven, the proud of the world, come and rest under its branches. Whence this great beauty? It rose from some root or other: and this beauty is in great glory. Let us seek the root. He was spat upon, he was humbled, he was scourged, he was crucified, he was wounded, he was despised. See, here there is no comeliness: but in the Church the glory of the root prevails. So it describes the bridegroom himself, that despised, dishonored, abject one. But now you have to see the tree, which rose from that root, and filled the world. *A root in thirsty ground*.

**3** *He has no comeliness, nor honor: and we saw him, and he had no comeliness nor beauty*[^3]. *Is not this the carpenter's son?*[^4] How he had no beauty, that it should be said: *Do we not say truly that you have a demon?*[^5] In his name demons were put to flight, and it is cast at him that he had a demon. But why this? *We saw him, and he had no comeliness nor beauty*. What is his comeliness within, where he was not seen? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^6]. What is his comeliness? *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^7].

**4** And where was he seen to have no comeliness nor beauty? *And he had no comeliness: but his face was abject, and his condition deformed beyond all men. A man under blows*[^8]. A man under blows, God before the blows, God-man after the blows. *A man under blows, and knowing how to bear weaknesses*[^9]. The weaknesses of whom? Of those very ones from whom he suffered. The physician bore the weaknesses of the frenzied; and when he himself was being crucified, he prayed and said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^10]. Attend: let us love the bridegroom. The more deformed he is commended to us, the dearer, the sweeter he has been made to the bride. *Wherefore he also turned away*[^11]. He turned away, that those who were crucifying him might not understand him. *His face was insulted, and was not greatly esteemed*[^12].

**5** *He bears our weaknesses, and is in sorrows for us: and we esteemed him to be in sorrows, and under blows, and in punishment. But he was wounded for our sins, and made weak for our iniquities. The chastisement of our peace was upon him, by his bruise we have been healed. We all like sheep went astray, and the Lord delivered him up for our sins*[^13]. Is it Gospel, or prophecy? What do the Jews say against these things? Is it not a wonder to hear them have these things, read these things, and find no one of whom it could be said except that one alone who is preached in the Gospel throughout the world, and yet not be Christians, but be so blind against the most evident utterances of the Prophets? Do not wonder at the blindness of the Jews concerning Christ. See, what is said of the bridegroom passes over, and begins to be said of the bride too; and as in the bridegroom you marveled at the blindness of the Jews, so in the bride you will marvel at the blindness of the heretics.

**6** Now let us marvel at the blindness of the Jews. *The Lord delivered him up for our sins; and he, because he was badly treated, did not open his mouth. He was led as a sheep to the slaughter; and as a lamb before its shearer was without voice, so he did not open his mouth. In his humiliation his judgment was taken away*[^14]. And lest you despise him, *Who will declare his generation?*[^15] What generation? *Before the morning star I begot you*[^16]. See, one generation: *Before the morning star*, before all things that were made, before all the angels, before all creation. Why? Because *all things were made through him*[^17]. But perhaps his second generation is narrated. Who narrates it? It is conceived by faith, no male approaches, the womb of a Virgin swells: he comes forth like a bridegroom from his chamber. Wondrous is this generation. The human one is wondrous, because without a father: that one is wondrous, because without a mother. *He was led as a sheep to the slaughter, as a lamb before its shearer, so he did not open his mouth. In his humiliation his judgment was taken away. Who will declare his generation? For his life will be taken from the earth*[^18]. He proclaims his resurrection. You see that truly the Lord—as though anything could be true except the Truth—said this: *The things that are written in the Law, and the Prophets, and the Psalms concerning me*[^19]. That *Christ had to suffer, and to rise again*[^20], you have heard. And to rise again, you have now heard: *For his life will be taken from the earth. And that repentance and remission of sins be preached in his name among all the nations, beginning at Jerusalem*[^21]: you will hear this from that Prophet too; not that we ought to set the Prophet before the Lord. The herald went before, the judge followed. The herald spoke not his own words, but the judge's, and the judge following confirmed his own words in the herald. *For his life will be taken from the earth. From the iniquities of my people he was led to death*[^22]. You were hearing him say to them just now, What have I done to you? If you have found sin in me, convict me. And they, *Crucify, crucify*[^23]: as they thought him a man, yet an innocent one. Therefore: *From the iniquities of my people he was led to death*.

**7** *I will therefore give the evil for his burial*[^24]. What is this: *I will give the evil for his burial, and the rich for his death*[^25]? The evil on account of the burial, the rich on account of the death. That rich man of Arimathea, Joseph, when the Lord was hanging on the cross, went in to Pilate, and asked for his body: it was granted, that he might bury him. The rich were given for his death: he buried the poor man, in whom he sought riches. Therefore, the rich on account of his death. What he said afterward happened first: what he said first happened afterward. *The evil for his burial*. Where do we show this? The Jews went in to Pilate, and said to him: Lord, we have heard that that deceiver—the impostor—said to his disciples that he, once killed, would rise again: command that the tomb be guarded, lest perhaps his disciples come by night and take him away, and the last error be worse than the first. Pilate said to them: You have soldiers, go, guard it as you wish. They took soldiers, set them there. These are the evil: they themselves were given for his burial, to guard. But whence do we prove that they are evil? Innocent soldiers were sent: the judge commanded them; they came to the tomb, they guarded it. Hear that they are evil: read the Gospel. After the Lord rose, and they saw the angel, they were terrified and astonished. When it was said to others, *Do not fear*[^26], these were struck with fear, because they were not lifted up by faith. And yet, though they knew these things, they came to the Jews, and told them all these things. The Jews said: We give you money. Therefore they were evil: they hid the truth, they sold a lie. And how did they sell a lie? No wonder, they sold a lie, the blind a lie to the blind. Say (it was said to them) that, while we slept, his disciples came and took him away. O vanity selling vanity to vanity! Vain men will hear it and believe it. And to this day this is among the Jews, so runs that report—how vain, how false, how empty. They refuse to hear the testimony of the martyrs, that they may live; and they hear the testimony of sleepers, that they may perish. If the guards were sleeping, how could they know who took him from the tomb? Or why did you keep watch, evil man? O evil man, of whom the Prophet said, not without cause, *I will give the evil for his burial*. O evil men, O worst men: either you were keeping watch, and ought to have guarded him; or you were sleeping, and do not know what was done. For there was fulfilled what the Holy Spirit foretold long before through the Psalmist: *They devised a plan which they could not establish*[^27].

**8** Let us, then, dearest brothers, for whose salvation all these things were both foretold and fulfilled, give thanks to the divine mercy, and, as much as we can, with all our strength labor, that the benefits of God may bring us not judgment but advancement: so that when the dread day of judgment and the time of rendering an account come, whatever our Lord and Savior, having been judged, conferred on us, he may, as he is about to judge, find whole. And indeed, when he comes, he will render what he promised, but he will require what he redeemed: and what he conferred at his first coming, he will exact at his second. Although we ought to presume much of the mercy of God, yet we ought not negligently to fear his justice. For he will judge you with justice, who redeemed you with mercy. For that, while we sin so long a time, he spares us, is not negligence, but patience. He has not lost his power, but reserved us for repentance. Let us, then, fear the justice of him whose mercy we desire. For now he spares, but does not keep silent: and even if he keeps silent, he will not always keep silent. Let us, then, hear him while he does not keep silent in the precept, if we want him to spare us, when he has not kept silent in the judgment. For now mercy is dispensed to us beforehand, then justice will be exacted from us, and he will render to each according to his works; and there will come about what the Apostle said: *Judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy*[^28].
