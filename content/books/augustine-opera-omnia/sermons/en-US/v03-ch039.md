# Sermon 77: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 15:21-28: "Jesus, Going Out from Gennesaret, Withdrew Into the Regions of Tyre and Sidon, and Behold a Canaanite Woman," and What Follows

**1. 1.** That Canaanite woman, who has just now been commended to us by the Gospel reading, offers us an example of humility, and the way of godliness: she shows that one rises from humility to the heights. But she was, as it appears, not of the people of Israel, whence the Patriarchs, whence the Prophets, whence the parents of our Lord Jesus Christ according to the flesh; whence the Virgin Mary herself, who bore Christ. So this woman was not of that people: but she was of the Gentiles. For, as we heard, the Lord withdrew into the regions of Tyre and Sidon, and thence the Canaanite woman, coming out from those borders, sought most urgently the benefit of the healing of her daughter, who was badly vexed by a demon[^1]. Tyre and Sidon were not cities of the people of Israel, but of the Gentiles; although near to that people. She cried out, then, eager to obtain the benefit, and knocked strongly: and she was put off, not that mercy might be denied, but that desire might be kindled; and not only that desire might be kindled, but, as I said before, that humility might be commended. She cried out, then, as if the Lord did not hear, but was arranging in silence what he was going to do. The disciples asked the Lord for her, and said: *Send her away, for she cries after us*[^2]. And he: *I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^3].

**2. 2.** Here a question of these words arises: Whence did we come to the sheepfold of Christ from the Gentiles, if he was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel? What does so deep a dispensation of this mystery mean, that, although the Lord knew why he came, namely that he might have a Church in all nations, he said he was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel? We understand, then, that he ought to show the presence of his body, his birth, the display of miracles, and the power of resurrection in that people; that it was so arranged, so commended from the beginning, this foretold, this fulfilled: because Christ Jesus had to come to the nation of the Jews to be seen, to be killed, and to win thence those whom he had foreknown before. For that people was not condemned, but winnowed. There was the multitude of chaff, there the hidden dignity of the grains: there what might be burned, there whence the barn might be filled. For whence the Apostles except thence? whence Peter? whence the rest?

**2. 3.** Whence Paul himself, first Saul? that is, first proud, afterward humble. For when Saul, the name was derived from Saul. But Saul was a proud king: but in the kingdom he persecuted David, the humble one[^4]. When, then, Saul, who was afterward Paul, then surely proud, then a persecutor of the innocent, then a devastator of the Church[^5]. For he had received letters (as it were burning with zeal for the synagogue, and pursuing the Christian name) from the priests, that he might bring whatever Christians he found to suffer punishments. As he went, as he panted for slaughter, as he thirsted for blood, the persecutor was prostrated by the heavenly voice of Christ, the preacher was raised up. There was fulfilled in him what is written in the prophet: *I will strike, and I will heal*[^6]. For God strikes this, what exalts itself in a man against God. The physician is not impious who strikes the swelling, who cuts or burns the rottenness. He brings pain: he brings it, but that he may lead to health. He is troublesome: but unless he were, he would not be useful. So Christ prostrated Saul by one voice, raised up Paul: that is, he prostrated the proud one, raised up the humble one. For what was the reason for changing his name, so that, although before he was called Saul, afterward he wished to be called Paul, except because he recognized in himself that the name of Saul, when he persecuted, had been of pride? He chose, then, a humble name, that he might be called Paul, that is, least. For "Paulus" is "least." "Paulus" is nothing else than "small." Of which name now glorying, and commending humility: *I*, he says, *am the least of the Apostles*[^7]. Whence, then, whence was he, except of the people of the Jews? Thence the other Apostles, thence Paul, thence those whom the same Paul commends, that they saw the Lord after the resurrection. For he says that he was seen by about *five hundred brothers at once, of whom most remain even now, but some have fallen asleep*[^8].

**2. 4.** But of that people too were those who, when Peter was speaking, commending the passion, resurrection, and divinity of Christ, having received the Holy Spirit—when all those upon whom the Holy Spirit came spoke in the tongues of all nations—pricked in spirit, who were hearing of the people of the Jews, sought counsel for their salvation, understanding that they were guilty of the blood of Christ: that they themselves crucified him, they themselves killed him, in whose name they saw so great miracles done by him whom they had killed, saw the presence of the Holy Spirit[^9].

**3. 4.** Seeking counsel, then, they received the answer: *Do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ; and your sins will be forgiven you*[^10]. Who would despair that sins would be granted to him, when the crime of killing Christ was granted to the guilty? They were converted of that very people of the Jews: they were converted, they were baptized. They approached the table of the Lord, and the blood which, raging, they shed, believing they drank. But how they were converted, how plainly and perfectly, the Acts of the Apostles indicate. For all that they possessed they sold, and laid the prices of their things at the feet of the Apostles; and it was distributed to each as each had need: and no one said anything was his own, but all things were common to them[^11]. *And they had*, as it is written, *one soul and one heart toward God*[^12]. Behold the sheep of whom he said: *I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^13]. For to them he displayed his presence, for them, raging against him, crucified he prayed, saying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^14]. The physician understood that the frenzied, with mind lost, killing the physician, and by killing the physician not knowing it, were making a medicine for themselves. For by the Lord killed we were all healed, redeemed by his blood, freed from hunger by the bread of his body. This presence, then, Christ displayed to the Jews. This, then, he says: *I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel:* that he might display to them the presence of his body; not that he might despise and pass over the sheep he had among the Gentiles.

**4. 5.** For to the Gentiles he himself did not go, but sent the disciples. And there was fulfilled what the prophet said: *A people whom I did not know served me*[^15]. See how deep, how evident, how express the prophecy: *A people whom I did not know*, that is, to whom I did not display my presence, *served me.* How? It follows: *By the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^16]: that is, not by seeing, but by hearing they believed. Therefore the praise of the Gentiles is greater. For those saw, and killed: the Gentiles heard, and believed. But to call and gather the Gentiles, that there might be fulfilled what we just now sang: *Gather us from the Gentiles, that we may confess to your name, and glory in your praise*[^17], that Paul the Apostle was sent. He, made least, became great, not through himself, but through him whom he persecuted; he was sent to the Gentiles, from a robber a shepherd, from a wolf a sheep. He was sent to the Gentiles, that least apostle[^18], and labored much among the Gentiles, and through him the Gentiles believed. His epistles are witnesses.

**4. 6.** You have this too most sacredly figured in the Gospel. The daughter of a certain synagogue ruler had died, her father was asking the Lord to come to her: he had left her sick and set in peril. The Lord was going to visit and heal the sick girl: meanwhile she was announced dead, and it was said to the father: *The girl is dead, do not trouble the Master further*[^19]. But the Lord, who knew that he could raise the dead, did not take away hope from the despairing one, and said to the father: *Do not fear, only believe*[^20]. He was proceeding to the girl: and on the way, among the crowds, a certain woman, as she could, pressed herself in, who suffered a flow of blood, and in that long-lasting languor had spent in vain on physicians all she had. When she touched the fringe of his garment, she was made whole. And the Lord: *Who touched me?*[^21] The disciples, marveling, who did not know what had been done, and saw him pressed by the crowds, and that he was anxious about one who had lightly touched him, answered: *The crowd presses you, and you say, Who touched me?* And he: *Someone touched me*[^22]. For these press, that one touched. The body of Christ, then, many press troublesomely, few touch wholesomely. *Someone*, he says, *touched me. For I felt power go out from me.* But when she saw that she had not been hidden, she fell down before his feet, and confessed what had been done. After these things he proceeded, and reached where he was heading, and raised up the girl, the daughter of the synagogue ruler, found dead[^23].

**5. 7.** It was done indeed, and fulfilled as it is narrated: but yet the very things which were done by the Lord were signifying something, as it were words, if it may be said, visible and signifying something. Which especially appears in that, out of season, he sought fruit on the tree, and because he did not find it, cursing the tree he made it dry[^24]. This deed, unless it is taken as figured, is found foolish: first, that he sought fruit on that tree, when it was not the season for there to be fruit on any tree; then, if it were already the season of fruit, what fault of the tree was it not to have fruit? But because it signified that he sought not only leaves, but also fruit, that is, not only words, but also the deeds of men; by making dry where he found only leaves, he signified the punishment of those who can speak good things, are unwilling to do good things. So then here too. For surely it is a mystery. The one foreknowing all says: *Who touched me?* The Creator makes himself like one unskilled, and asks—he who knew this, and even foreknew the rest. There is surely something which Christ speaks to us by a signifying mystery.

**5. 8.** That daughter of the synagogue ruler signified the people of the Jews, for whose sake Christ had come, who said: *I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^25]. But that woman who suffered a flow of blood figured the Church of the Gentiles, to whom Christ had not been sent by the presence of his body. To the former he was going, her healing he intended: this one runs in between, touches the fringe of him, as it were, not knowing, that is, she is healed as by one absent. He says: *Who touched me?* as if he said, I do not know this people. *A people whom I did not know served me. Someone touched me. For I felt power go out from me*[^26], that is, the Gospel sent forth has filled the whole world. But the fringe is touched, the small and outermost part of the garment. Make the garment of Christ to be, as it were, the Apostles. There Paul was the fringe: that is, the last and least. For he said both of himself: *I am the least of the Apostles*[^27]. For he was called after all, believed after all, healed more than all. The Lord had not been sent except to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel. But because a people too whom he had not known was going to serve him, was going by the hearing of the ear to obey him, he was not silent about it even while established there. For the same Lord says in a certain place: *I have other sheep that are not of this fold; I must bring these too, that there may be one flock and one shepherd*[^28].

**6. 9.** Thence was this woman: therefore she was not despised, but deferred. *I was*, he says, *sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^29]. And she, crying out, pressed on, persevered, knocked, as though she had already heard, Ask, and receive; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. She pressed on, she knocked. For the Lord too, when he said these words: *Ask, and you will receive; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you*[^30]; had said above: *Do not give what is holy to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine; lest perhaps they trample them with their feet, and, turning, tear you*[^31]: that is, after the contempt of your pearls they even become troublesome to you. Do not, then, cast to them what they despise.

**6. 10.** And whence do we distinguish (as if they answered) who are the swine, who are the dogs? This was shown in that woman. For to that woman pressing on he answered this: *It is not good to take the bread of the children and cast it to the dogs*[^32]. You are a dog, you are one of the Gentiles, you adore idols. But what is so familiar to dogs, as to lick stones? *It is not good, then, to take the bread of the children, and cast it to the dogs.* If she had withdrawn after these words, a dog had approached, a dog had departed: but by knocking, she was made a human being from a dog. For she pressed on in asking, and from that as it were reproach she showed humility, she obtained mercy. For she was not moved, nor did she grow angry, that she had been called a dog while asking a benefit, begging mercy: but she said: *Yes, Lord*[^33]: you have said I am a dog; plainly I am a dog, I acknowledge my name; the Truth speaks: but not for that reason am I to be repelled from the benefit. Utterly a dog: *but the dogs too eat of the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters*[^34]. I desire a certain small and slight benefit: I do not invade the table, but seek the crumbs.

**7. 11.** See how humility was commended. The Lord had called her a dog; she did not say: I am not; but she said: I am. And the Lord at once, because she acknowledged herself a dog: *O woman, great is your faith! be it done to you as you asked*[^35]. You acknowledged yourself a dog, I now acknowledge you a human being. *O woman, great is your faith!* you asked, you sought, you knocked; receive, find, let it be opened to you. See, brothers, how in this woman who was a Canaanite, that is, who came from the Gentiles, and bore the type, that is, the figure of the Church, humility was especially commended. For the Jewish nation, that it might be repelled from the Gospel, was puffed up with pride, because it had merited to receive the Law, because from that very nation the Patriarchs had come forth, the Prophets had arisen, Moses the servant of God had done great miracles, which we heard in the Psalm[^36], in Egypt, had led the people through the Red Sea with the waters receding, had received the Law, which he gave to that people. There was whence the Jewish nation might be exalted, and through that very pride it came about that it was unwilling to be humbled to Christ, the author of humility, the represser of swelling, the physician God, who for this reason, since he was God, became man, that man might recognize himself a man. A great medicine. If this medicine does not heal pride, I do not know what may heal it. He is God, and becomes man: he sets aside his divinity, that is, in a way sequesters, that is, hides what was his, appears what he had assumed. He becomes a man, when he is God: and a man does not acknowledge himself a man, that is, does not acknowledge himself mortal, does not acknowledge himself frail, does not acknowledge himself a sinner, does not acknowledge himself sick, that even as sick he might seek a physician! but, what is more dangerous, he seems to himself well!

**8. 12.** Therefore on this account that people did not approach, that is, on account of pride: and they are said, of the olive tree, that is, of that people created from the Patriarchs, to be the natural branches broken off, that is, the Jews deservedly barren by the spirit of pride; and into that olive tree the wild olive was grafted. The wild olive is the people of the Gentiles. So the Apostle says, that the wild olive was grafted into the olive, but the natural branches were broken off. Those broken off on account of pride: the wild olive grafted in on account of humility[^37]. This humility the woman showed, saying: *Yes, Lord*[^38], I am a dog, I desire the crumbs. In this humility that Centurion too pleased him: who, when he desired his servant to be healed by the Lord, and the Lord said: *I will come and heal him;* answered: *Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof; but only say the word, and my servant will be healed*[^39]. I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof. He did not receive him in his roof, he had received him in his heart. The more humble, the more capacious, the more full. For the hills repel the water, the valleys are filled. What then, what to this did the Lord say, after he said: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof*, to those who followed: *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel*[^40]: that is, in that people to which I came, *I have not found such faith. Such*, what is it? So great. Whence great? From the least, that is, from humility, grand. *I have not found such faith:* like a grain of mustard, the smaller, the more fervent. So the Lord was now grafting the wild olive into the olive. Then he was doing this, when he said: *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel.*

**9. 13.** Finally, see what follows. *Therefore I say to you* (because *I have not found such faith in Israel*, that is, so great a humility with faith): *Therefore I say to you, that many will come from the east and west, and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven*[^41]. *They will recline*, he says, they will rest. For we ought not to think there of carnal feasts, or crave anything of the sort in that kingdom, so that we do not change vices for virtues, but substitute vices. For it is one thing to desire the kingdom of heaven for the sake of wisdom and eternal life; another, for the sake of earthly happiness, as if we should have it there more opulent and greater. If you think you will be rich in that kingdom, you do not cut off craving, but change it: and yet you will be rich, and only there will you be rich. For here your neediness gathers very many things. Why do the rich have much? Because they need much. Greater neediness, as it were, acquires greater means: there neediness itself will die. Then you will truly be rich, when you will be in need of nothing. For you are not rich, and the angel poor, who does not have beasts of burden and carriages and households. Why? Because he does not need them: because the stronger one is, the less needy. So there are riches, and true riches. Do not think of the feasts of this earth there. For the feasts of this earth are daily medicines; they are necessary for a certain sickness of ours, with which we are born. This sickness everyone feels, when the hour of refreshment has passed. Do you wish to see how great this sickness is, that, like an acute fever, it kills in seven days? Do not think yourself well. Health will be immortality. For this is a long sickness. Because by daily medicines you prop up your disease; you seem to yourself to be well: withdraw the medicines, and see what you can do.

**10. 14.** For from the time we are born, it is necessary that we die. This disease must necessarily lead to death. Surely the physicians, when they inspect the sick, say this. For example, this man is dropsical, he is dying; this disease cannot be cured. He is leprous; nor can this disease be cured. He is consumptive; who cures this? It is necessary that he perish, it is necessary that he die. Behold the physician has now said, He is consumptive, he can only die; and yet sometimes the dropsical man does not die of it, and the leprous man does not die of it, and the consumptive man does not die of it: and yet it is necessary that whoever is born die of it. He dies of it, it cannot be otherwise. This both the physician and the unskilled pronounce: but even if he dies later, does he therefore not die? When, then, is there true health, except when there is true immortality? If, then, there is true immortality, no corruption, no failing, what need will there be of nourishment? So when you hear: *They will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob*[^42]; prepare not the belly, but the mind. You will be filled there: and the inner belly itself has its own foods. According to this belly it is said: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^43]. And truly they shall be filled, so that they no longer hunger.

**10. 15.** So the Lord was now grafting in the wild olive, when he said: *Many will come from the east and west, and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven*[^44], that is, they will be grafted into the olive. For the roots of this olive are Abraham and Isaac and Jacob: *but the sons of the kingdom*, that is the unbelieving Jews, *will go into the outer darkness*[^45]. The natural branches will be cut off, that the wild olive may be grafted in. But whence did the natural branches merit to be cut off, except by pride? Whence the wild olive to be grafted in, except by humility? Whence this woman too said: *Yes, Lord: for the dogs too eat of the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters*[^46]. And thence she hears: *O woman, great is your faith!*[^47] So too that Centurion: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof*[^48]. *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel*[^49]. Let us learn, or keep, humility. If we do not yet have it, let us learn it: if we have it, let us not lose it. If we do not yet have it, let us have it, that we may be grafted in: if we already have it, let us keep it, lest we be cut off.
