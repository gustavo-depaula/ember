# Sermon 228/B: On the Sacraments, on the Day of Easter

**1.** The duty of rendering a sermon, and the care by which we travailed with you, that Christ might be formed in you[^1], compels us to admonish your infancy — you who, now reborn of water and the Spirit, behold this food and drink upon this Lord's table with a new light, and receive it with a new piety — what so great and divine a sacrament means, so clear and noble a medicine, so clean and easy a sacrifice, which now is offered not in one earthly city, Jerusalem, nor in that tabernacle which was built through Moses, nor in that temple which was built through Solomon, which were shadows of things to come[^2], but *from the rising of the sun even to its setting*[^3], as was foretold by the Prophets, is sacrificed, and, according to the grace of the New Testament, a victim of praise is offered to God. No longer is a bloody victim sought from the flocks of cattle, no longer is a sheep or a goat brought to the divine altars, but the sacrifice now of our time is the body and blood of the priest himself. For of him it was foretold so long before in the Psalms: *You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek*[^4]. But that Melchizedek, the priest of the most high God, brought forth bread and wine, when he blessed our father Abraham, we read and hold in the book of Genesis[^5].

**2.** Christ therefore, our Lord, who offered by suffering for us what he received by being born from us, made prince of priests forever, gave the order of sacrificing which you see, that, namely, of his body and blood. For his body, struck by a lance, sent forth water and blood, by which he forgave our sins. Mindful of this grace, working out your own salvation, since it is God who works in you[^6], with fear and trembling draw near to the participation of this altar. Recognize in the bread what hung on the cross; in the cup what flowed from the side. For those old sacrifices of the people of God figured, by manifold variety, this one thing to come. For Christ himself is both a sheep, on account of the innocence of his simple soul, and a goat, on account of the likeness of sinful flesh[^7]. And whatever else in many and diverse ways was foretold in the sacrifices of the Old Testament pertains to this one thing which was revealed in the New Testament.

**3.** Receive therefore and eat the body of Christ, you yourselves too, now made members of Christ in the body of Christ; receive and drink the blood of Christ. Lest you be dissolved, eat your bond; lest you seem cheap to yourselves, drink your price. As this is changed into you, when you eat and drink it, so you too are changed into the body of Christ, when you live obediently and piously. For he himself, his passion now drawing near, when he kept the Passover with his disciples, took bread, blessed it, and said: *This is my body, which will be handed over for you*[^8]. Likewise he gave the blessed cup, saying: *This is my blood of the New Testament, which will be shed for many unto the forgiveness of sins*[^9]. This in the Gospel you either read or heard, but you did not know that this Eucharist is the Son: but now, sprinkled in heart unto a pure conscience, and washed in body with clean water[^10], *draw near to him, and be enlightened, and your faces will not blush*[^11]. For if you receive this worthily, which pertains to the New Testament, through which you hope for an eternal inheritance, keeping the new commandment that you love one another[^12], you have life in you. For you take up that flesh of which Life itself says: *The bread which I will give is my flesh for the life of the world*[^13]; and: *Unless one eats my flesh and drinks my blood, he will not have life in himself*[^14].

**4.** Having, then, life in him, you will be in one flesh with him. For this sacrament does not so commend the body of Christ as to sever you from it. For the Apostle recalls that this was foretold in holy Scripture: *The two will be in one flesh. This sacrament*, he says, *is great; but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^15]. And in another place, of this very Eucharist he says: *One bread, one body, we many are*[^16]. You begin therefore to receive what you have also begun to be, if you do not receive it unworthily, lest you eat and drink judgment to yourselves. For thus he says: *Whoever eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily, will be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. But let a man prove himself, and so eat of the bread, and drink of the cup; for he who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^17].

**5.** But you receive worthily, if you guard against the leaven of evil doctrine, that you may be unleavened with sincerity and truth[^18]; or if you keep that leaven of love, which a woman hid in three measures of meal, until the whole was leavened[^19]. For this woman is the Wisdom of God, made through the Virgin in mortal flesh, who in the whole world, which she restored from the three sons of Noah after the flood, as in three measures, sows abroad her Gospel, until the whole is leavened. This is that whole which in Greek is called *holon*, where, keeping the bond of peace, you will be according to the whole, which is called *catholon*, and whence the "catholic" Church is named.
