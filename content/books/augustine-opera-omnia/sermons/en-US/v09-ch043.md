# Sermon 333: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ gave great security to his witnesses, that is, to his martyrs, anxious because of human frailty, lest perhaps by confessing him and dying they should perish, saying to them: *A hair of your head will not perish*[^1]. Do you, then, fear lest you perish, whose hair will not perish? If your superfluous things are so guarded, in how great safety is your soul? Does the hair not perish, which when it is cut you do not feel, and your soul perishes, through which you feel? He foretold indeed that they would suffer many hard things, that by foretelling them he might make them more prepared, and that they might say to him: *My heart is ready*[^2]. What is: *My heart is ready,* but: My will is ready? So the martyrs have a ready will in martyrdom, but: *The will is prepared by the Lord*[^3]. But to those evil, hard, and harsh things to come, recalled, he added: *In your patience you will possess your souls*[^4]. *In your patience,* he says; for it would not be your patience, if your will too were not there. *In your patience;* but from where ours? Ours is what is had from us, ours is also what is given to us; for if it be not ours, it is not given. For how do you give anything, except that it become his to whom you give it? Open is that confession: *Shall not my soul be subject to God? For from him is my patience*[^5]. He himself says to us: *In your patience.* Let us too say to him: *From him is my patience.* He made it yours by giving: do not be ungrateful by assigning it to yourself. Do we not in the Lord's Prayer say that what is from God is ours too? Daily we say: *Our daily bread.* You have already said: *our;* and you say: *give us.* Behold *our,* behold *give us;* by his giving it becomes ours. If by his giving it becomes ours, by our being proud it becomes another's. You say: *our,* and you say: *give us.* Why then do you assign to yourself what you did not give yourself? *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^6] You say: *our;* and: *give us.* Acknowledge the giver: confess that you receive, that he may gladly deign to give. What if you did not need, you who beg, and are proud? Or do you not beg, who ask for bread? Our eternal bread, Christ in the equality of the Father; our daily bread, Christ in the flesh; eternal without time, daily in time. Yet he himself is the *bread which came down from heaven*[^7]. The martyrs are strong, the martyrs are firm, but *bread strengthens the heart of man*[^8].

**2.** So now let us hear the apostle Paul saying, when he drew near to his passion, presuming on the crown prepared for him. *I have fought,* he says, *the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; for the rest there remains for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render to me on that day; and not only to me, but to all who love his appearing*[^9]. *The Lord will render to me* the crown, *the righteous judge.* So he owes what he will render: *therefore the righteous judge will render* it; for, the work inspected, he cannot deny the wage. What work does he inspect? *I have fought the good fight,* that is a work; *I have finished the course,* that is a work; *I have kept the faith,* that is a work; *there remains for me a crown of righteousness,* that is the wage. But in the wage you do nothing; in the work you do not act alone. The crown is to you from him; but the work is from you, yet only with him helping. But since the apostle Paul, before, when he was Saul, was a most cruel and savage persecutor, he merited absolutely no good, indeed he merited very much evil; for he merited to be condemned, not chosen. And behold, suddenly, while he was doing evil, and meriting evil, by one heavenly voice he is laid low; the persecutor is cast down, the preacher is raised up. Hear him confessing this very thing: *Who before was a blasphemer and a persecutor and insolent, but I obtained mercy*[^10]. Surely there he did not say: *The righteous judge will render to me? I obtained mercy,* he says; I merited evils, I received good things. *He has not dealt with us according to our sins*[^11]. *I obtained mercy,* the things owed me were not rendered; for if the things owed were rendered, punishment would be rendered. I did not, he says, receive what was owed; but *I obtained mercy. He has not dealt with us according to our sins.*

**3.** *As far as the East is from the West, so far has he removed our iniquities from us*[^12]. Turn away from the West, turn to the East. Behold one man: Saul and Paul; Saul in the West, Paul in the East; the persecutor in the West, the preacher in the East. There the sins set, from there righteousness rises. In the West the old, in the East the new; in the West Saul, in the East Paul. From where this to Saul, from where this to the cruel one, from where this to the persecutor, from where this to the non-shepherd? For he was a *ravening wolf, of the tribe of Benjamin*, he himself said it. But it had been said in prophecy: *Benjamin a ravening wolf, in the morning he seizes, and at evening he divides the food*[^13]. First he devoured, afterward he feeds. He seized, he plainly seized. Read it, he seized: read the book of the Acts of the Apostles. He had received letters from the priests, that whomever he found following the way of Christ, he might bring bound to be punished. He went, he raged, he panted for slaughter and blood: behold he seizes. But it is still morning, there is vanity under the sun; evening comes to him, when he is struck with blindness. His eyes are closed to the vanity of this world, others within are illumined. A vessel a little before of perdition, he becomes a vessel of election, and behold it is fulfilled: *he will divide the food;* his divisions of the food are recited daily. See how he divides the food. He knows what suits whom: he divides, not haphazardly, he does not dispense confusedly. He divides, that is, he distributes, distinguishes; he does not dispense haphazardly and confusedly. He speaks *wisdom among the perfect*[^14]; but to certain ones unable to take solid food, dividing he says: *I gave you milk to drink*[^15].

**4.** Behold, he who a little before was doing—what? I do not want to recall it. Rather let me recall the man's wickedness, that I may approve God's mercy. From him Christ was suffering, he suffers for Christ; Paul is made from Saul, a true witness from a false one. He who scattered, gathers; he who attacked, defends. From where this to Saul, which we say? Let us hear him. You ask, he says, from where this to me? It is not, he says, from me: *I obtained mercy.* It is not, he says, to me from me: *I obtained mercy. What shall I render to the Lord for all the things he has rendered to me?*[^16] For he rendered, but not evils for evils; he rendered plainly, but not evils for evils, he rendered good things for evils. What, then, shall I render? *I will take the cup of salvation*[^17]. Surely you were going to repay? Still I have received: but now plainly, my passion drawing near, I will render good things for good things, not good things for evils. So first the Lord owed evils for evils; but he refused to render evils for evils, but rendered good things for evils. By rendering good things for evils, he found how to render good things for good things.

**5.** For behold, in Paul, before Saul, he found nothing good. Since he had found nothing good in him, he forgave the evils, he rendered good things. Since, then, you say, he first rendered him good things, he went before; but by giving good things, by which he might render good things, behold he rendered the wage for these good things, for the good works; to the one fighting the good fight, finishing the course, keeping the faith, he renders good things. But what good things? Those which he himself gave. Did he not himself give it, that you might fight the good fight? If he did not himself give it, what is it that you say in another place: *I labored more than all of them, yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^18]? Behold again you say: *I have finished the course.* Did he not himself give it that you might finish the course? If he did not himself give it that you might finish the course, what is it that you say in another place: *It is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy*[^19]? *I have kept the faith.* You kept it, you kept it: I acknowledge it, I approve it; I confess it, you kept it; but: *Unless the Lord guards the city, in vain does he watch who guards it*[^20]. So with him himself helping, and him himself giving, you both fought the good fight, and finished the course, and kept the faith. Pardon me, Apostle, I know nothing of your own things, except evils. Pardon me, Apostle; we say it, because you taught it; I hear you confessing, I do not find you ungrateful. We know absolutely nothing of your own things prepared by you for yourself, except evils; since, then, God crowns your merits, he crowns nothing but his own gifts.

**6.** Let no arguments tear this faith and true godliness from your hearts—lest anyone be exalted over free choice in good works (works which, whoever receives them, let him so receive them that he knows the giver, that whoever it is may not be ungrateful to the giver, may not be proud against the physician, being either still sick, or not made well by himself)—this faith, I say, and true godliness let no arguments tear from your hearts. Keep what you have received. *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^21] This is to confess to God, to say what the apostle Paul says: But we have not received the spirit of this world. The spirit of this world makes men proud, the spirit of this world makes them puffed up, the spirit of this world makes a man *think himself to be something, when he is nothing*[^22]. But against the spirit of this world, what does the Apostle say? Against the spirit of this world, puffed up, proud, given over, swollen, not solid, what does he say? *We have not received the spirit of this world, but the Spirit which is from God*[^23]. How do you prove it? *That we may know the things that have been given us by God*[^24]. So let us hear the Lord saying: *Without me you can do nothing*[^25]. And that: *No one has anything, unless it has been given him from above*[^26]. And: *No one comes to me, unless the Father who sent me draws him*[^27]. And that: *I am the vine, you are the branches; as a branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it remains in the vine, so neither can you, unless you remain in me*[^28]. And that which the apostle James testifies, saying: *Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights*[^29]. And that which the apostle Paul, to check the presumption of those who glory in free choice, cries out and says: *What do you have that you have not received? But if you have received it, why do you glory as if you had not received it?*[^30] And that: *By grace we have been saved through faith, and this not of ourselves; for it is the gift of God, that no one may be exalted*[^31]. And that: *To you it has been given on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in him, but also to suffer for him*[^32]. And that: *God, who has begun a good work in you, will perfect it*[^33]. So, diligently and faithfully thinking on these things and the like, let us not agree with those who, exalting free choice into pride, try rather to cast it down than to lift it up; but humbly let us consider what the Apostle says: *It is God who works in you both to will and to perfect*[^34].

**7.** Let us give thanks to our Lord and Savior, who, with no preceding merits, healed us when wounded, and reconciled us when enemies, and redeemed us from captivity, led us back from darkness to light, recalled us from death to life; and, humbly confessing our frailty, let us beseech his mercy, that, because according to the psalmist his mercy goes before us, he may deign not only to keep in us, but also to increase his gifts or benefits, which he himself deigned to give; who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns forever and ever. Amen.
