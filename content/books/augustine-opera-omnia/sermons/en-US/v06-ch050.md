# Sermon 223: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** In the book which is called Genesis, the Scripture says: *And God saw the light, that it was good. And God divided between the light and the darkness: and God called the light day, and the darkness he called night*[^1]. If, then, God called the light day, without doubt those to whom the apostle Paul says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^2], were day: since he who commanded the light to shine out of darkness[^3] enlightened them. These infants, whom you behold whitened outwardly and cleansed inwardly, who by the whiteness of their garments prefigure the splendor of their minds, when they were pressed by the night of their sins, were darkness. But now, because they have been cleansed by the laver of pardon, because they have been watered by the fountain of wisdom, because they have been suffused with the light of righteousness: *This is the day which the Lord has made, let us exult and rejoice in it*[^4]. May the day of the Lord hear us, may the day made by the Lord hear us: may it hear, and obey; that we may exult and rejoice in it. Since, as the Apostle says, this is our joy and crown, if you stand in the Lord[^5]. Hear us, then, O new sons of the chaste mother: rather, hear us, sons of the virgin mother. Since *you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord: walk as children of light*[^6], cleave to the children of light; and, that I may say this very thing more plainly, cleave to the good faithful. For there are, what is worse, evil faithful. There are those who are called faithful, and are not. There are faithful in whom the sacraments of Christ suffer injury: who so live that they both perish themselves, and lose others. For they perish themselves by living badly: but they lose others by offering examples of living badly. You, then, dearest ones, do not be joined to such. Seek the good, cleave to the good: be good.

**2.** And do not wonder at the multitude of evil Christians, who fill the church, who communicate at the altar, who with great voices praise a bishop or presbyter disputing of good manners: through whom is fulfilled what our gatherer foretold in the Psalm: *I announced and spoke, they were multiplied above number*[^7]. In the Church of this time they can be with us: but in that assembly of the saints which will be after the resurrection, they will not be able to be. For the Church of this time is compared to a threshing floor, having grains mixed with chaff, having the evil mingled with the good; about to have, after the judgment, all the good without any evil. This floor contains the harvest sown by the apostles, watered by the good teachers who followed up to the present time, not a little ground down too by the persecution of enemies; but, what alone remains, not yet purged by the heavenly winnowing. But he will come of whom you gave back in the Creed: *Thence he will come to judge the living and the dead*: and, as the Gospel says, *he will have his winnowing fork in his hand, and will cleanse his threshing floor; and will gather his wheat into the granary, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire*[^8]. What I say, let the old faithful too hear. He who is a grain, let him rejoice with trembling, and remain, and not depart from the floor. Let him not try by his own judgment to strip himself, as it were, from the chaff: since, if he should now wish to separate himself from the chaff, he will not be able to remain on the floor. And when he comes who without error distinguishes, what he does not find on the floor, he will not lift to the granary. In vain then will those grains boast themselves of the ear, whatever ones have now departed from the floor. That granary will be filled, and closed. Whatever remains outside, the flame will lay waste. He who is good, then, dearest ones, let him bear with the evil: he who is evil, let him imitate the good. For on this floor grains can fail into chaff; again, grains can be roused up from chaff. These things happen daily, my brothers: this life is full of punishments and consolations. Daily those who seemed good slip and perish: and again those who seemed evil are converted and live. *For God does not will the death of the ungodly, but only that he turn back and live*[^9]. Hear me, grains; hear me, you who are what I desire to be: hear me, grains. Do not be saddened at the mixing of the chaff: they will not be with you forever. How great is this that the chaff presses? Thanks be to God, because it is light. Let us only be grains, and however great it be, it will not weigh us down. For God is faithful, who will not permit us to be tempted beyond what we can bear; but will with the temptation also make a way of escape, that we may be able to endure[^10]. Let the chaff too hear us; wherever they are, let them hear. I do not wish them to be here: yet let us address them, lest perhaps they be. So hear me, chaff: although, if you hear, you will not be chaff. Hear, then. May the patience of God profit you. May the conjunction, the admonition of the grains, make you grains. The rains of the words of God are not lacking to you: let not the field of God be barren in you. Therefore grow green again, become grain, ripen. For he who sowed you wishes to find ears, not thorns.
