# Sermon 260/B: A Sermon Given in the Days of the Octave of Easter, in Which He Addresses Especially the Infants

**1.** The sermon by which a good life is commanded and commended, that eternal life may be obtained and received, pertains indeed to all; nevertheless we address especially you, new shoots of holiness, reborn of water and the Spirit, planted and watered through our ministry in the field of God, who gives the increase. Consider yourselves thus, as it were freed from Egypt, from the hard slavery in which iniquity ruled over you; as having also passed through the Red Sea, namely through baptism, marked by the bloody cross of Christ. The enemies who were pursuing you from behind, reckon as past sins: for, as when the people of God were passing through the Egyptians perished, so for you, when baptized, those sins were blotted out. Now, then, seek the heavenly kingdom, to which you are called, as a land of promise; and, making your way through this earthly life as through the desert, resist temptations vigilantly. For you receive your manna from the participation of the holy altar, and from the rock flows what you drink. Recalling all this, and preaching it by teaching, the apostle Paul says: *I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink; for they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them; and the rock was Christ*[^1]. By these apostolic words you surely recognize clearly that we have suggested these things to your ears and minds not by our own conjecture, but taught by holy Scripture.

**2.** But what I admonished, saying, resist temptations vigilantly, look at what the same Apostle says next and terribly: *But with most of them God was not well pleased; for they were laid low in the desert. But these things were made figures of us, that we should not be desirous of evil things, as they too desired; nor servants of idols, as some of them, as it is written, the people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play. Nor let us fornicate, as some of them fornicated, and there fell in one day twenty-three thousand. Nor let us tempt Christ, as some of them tempted, and perished by serpents. Nor murmur, as some of them murmured, and perished by the destroyer. But all these things happened to them in figure: and they were written for our correction, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^2].

**3.** You, then, beloved, avoiding the preceding bad examples, imitate those who pleased God, not those who perished by offending God. For what did it profit to escape from the Egyptians through the Red Sea, and to perish from serpents in the desert? Such are those who, baptized and freed from past sins, neglect so great a grace, and, cut off by the venomous bites of deadly seductions, cannot come to the promised life. Fleeing their examples, cling with persevering obedience to him who redeemed you, that you may come to the kingdom; not such as was given to the first people in a shadow of what was to come, but where, with Christ, there will be no end, and you will live in eternal happiness.

[^1]: 1 Cor 10:1-4
[^2]: 1 Cor 10:5-11
