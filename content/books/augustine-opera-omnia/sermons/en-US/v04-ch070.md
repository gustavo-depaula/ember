# Sermon 145

**1.** When the holy Gospel was being read, we heard what ought truly to move every attentive soul to seek, not to fail. For one who is not moved is not changed either. But there is a dangerous movement, of which it is written, *Do not give my feet to movement*[^1]. But there is another movement, that of one who seeks, knocks, asks. What was read, then, we all heard; but I think that we did not all understand. He reminds us of what you may seek with me, ask with me, knock with me for receiving. For the grace of the Lord will be present, as we hope, so that, when I wish to minister to you, I too may deserve to receive. What is it, I beseech you, that we just heard the Lord say to his disciples, *Until now you have asked nothing in my name*[^2]? Is he not speaking to those disciples whom, when he had sent them, having given them power to preach the Gospel and to do great deeds, returned rejoicing and said to him, *Lord, behold, in your name even the demons are subject to us*[^3]? You recognize, you recall, what I have recalled from the Gospel, which is truthful in every place and every saying, nowhere false, nowhere deceiving. How, then, is it true, *Until now you have asked nothing in my name*; and, *Lord, behold, in your name even the demons are subject to us*? It certainly moves the mind, to know the secret of this question. So let us ask, seek, knock. Let this be in us faithful piety, not restlessness of the flesh, but submission of the mind; that he may open to us, who sees us knocking.

**2.** What, then, the Lord may give for me to minister to you, receive attentively, that is, hungering; and when I have said it, you will doubtless approve, with the sound palate of the heart, what is set before you from the Lord's storeroom. The Lord Jesus knew whence the human soul, that is, the rational mind made in the image of God, could be satisfied, so as to be satisfied by that very thing. He knew this, and he knew that it still lacked that fullness. He knew himself to appear, he knew himself to lie hidden. He knew what was being shown in him, what was being concealed. He knew this. *How great*, says the Psalm, *is the multitude of your sweetness, O Lord, which you have hidden from those who fear you, but have perfected for those who hope in you*[^4]. Your sweetness, both great and abundant, you have hidden from those who fear you. If you hide it from those who fear you, to whom do you open it? *You have perfected it for those who hope in you*. A twofold question has arisen, but each is solved from the other. If anyone inquire into the one, what is this: *You have hidden it from those who fear you, you have perfected it for those who hope in you*? Are some who fear and others who hope? Are not the same persons fearing God and hoping in God? Who hopes in him who does not fear him? Who piously fears him and has no hope in him? So let this first be solved. About those who hope and those who fear I wish to say something.

**3.** The Law has fear, grace has hope. But what is the difference between the Law and grace, since there is one giver both of the Law and of grace? The Law terrifies the one who presumes on himself, grace helps the one who hopes in God. The Law, I say, terrifies; do not despise it because it is brief; weigh it, and it is great. See what I have said, take what we minister, prove from where we take it. The Law terrifies the one who presumes on himself, grace helps the one who hopes in God. What does the Law say? Many things, and who can count them? I recall one small and modest precept, which the Apostle recalled, very small; let us see who upholds it. *You shall not covet*. What is it, brothers? We have heard the Law; if grace be not present, you have heard your penalty. Why do you boast to me, whoever you are who, hearing this, presume about yourself, why do you boast to me of your innocence? Why do you flatter yourself about it? You can say: I have not seized others' goods; I hear, I believe, perhaps I even see; you do not seize others' goods, *You shall not covet*, you have heard. I do not go to another's wife; this too I hear, I believe, I see. *You shall not covet*, you have heard. Why do you look around outside yourself, and not look inside? Look, and you will see another law in your members. Look inside; why do you pass yourself by? Go down into yourself. You will see another law in your members warring against the law of your mind, and taking you captive in the law of sin which is in your members. Rightly is the sweetness of God hidden from you. The law set in your members takes you captive, warring against the law of your mind. Of that sweetness which is hidden from you, the holy angels drink; you cannot endure and taste the sweetness while a captive. *You did not know covetousness, except that the Law said: You shall not covet*. You heard, you feared; you tried to fight, you could not overcome. *For taking occasion, sin through the commandment worked death*. Surely you recognize, these are the Apostle's words: *Taking occasion, sin through the commandment worked in me every covetousness*[^5]. Why did you boast, proud one? See, with your own arms the enemy overcame you. You indeed sought the commandment for warning; see, through the commandment the enemy found the occasion of entering. *For taking occasion, sin through the commandment deceived me*, he says, *and through it killed me*. What is it that I said: With your own arms the enemy overcame you? Hear the same Apostle following and saying: *And so the Law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*. Now answer the detractors of the Law; answer by the Apostle's authority: *The commandment is holy, the law holy, the commandment just and good. Did then that which is good become death to me? God forbid; but sin, that it might appear, through that which is good worked death in me*[^6]. Why this, except because, having received the commandment, you feared, you did not love? You feared the penalty, you did not love righteousness. He who fears the penalty wishes, if it could be, to do what he pleases and not have what he fears. God forbids adultery; you coveted another's wife, you do not approach, you do not do it, occasion is given, you have the time, the place lies open, a witness is lacking, yet you do not do it; why? Because you fear the penalty. But no one will know. Surely not even God? Yes, indeed, because God knows what you would do, you do not do it; but you also tremble before God himself threatening, you do not love him commanding. Why do you not do it? Because if you do it, you will be cast into hell. You fear the fire. Oh, if you loved chastity, you would not do it, even if you were to be entirely unpunished. If God said to you: See, do it, I will not damn you; I will not damn you to hell, but I will deny you my face. If for the sake of this threat you would not do it, you would not do it out of love of God, but out of fear of judgment. But you would do it—for perhaps you would do it so; for it is not mine to judge. Grace, which makes men holy, helps you if you do not do it in this way, because you shudder at the defilement of adultery, because you love the Commander, so that you may claim the Promiser, not because you fear the Damner; now it is of grace, do not take this to yourself, do not attribute it to your own powers. You act from delight, well; you act from love, well; I grant it, I consent. Love works through you, when you act willingly. Now you taste sweetness, if you hope in the Lord.

**4.** But where do you get this love? if indeed you have it; for I fear that you may still act out of fear, and seem great to yourself. Now if you act not out of love, you are truly great. Do you have love? I have it, you say. Whence? From myself. You are far from sweetness, if you have it from yourself. You will love yourself; because you will love the one from whom you have it. But I prove that you do not have it. For because you think you have so great a thing from yourself, I do not believe you have it. For if you had it, you would know whence you had it. Do you have love from yourself, as something light, as something small? If you spoke with the tongues of men and of angels, but had not love, you would be sounding brass and a clanging cymbal. If you knew all mysteries and had all knowledge, all prophecy and all faith, so as to move mountains, but had not love, these things could not profit you. If you distributed all your goods to the poor, and handed over your body to be burned, but had not love, you would be nothing[^7]. How great is this love, which, if it be lacking, all things profit nothing? Compare it not to your faith, not to your knowledge, not to your tongue; compare love to lesser things, to the eye of your body, to the hand, the foot, the belly, to some lowest member; are these least things in any part to be compared to love? So you have the eye and nose from God, and love from yourself? If love, which surpasses all things, you gave to yourself, you have made God cheap to yourself. What more can God give you? Whatever he gives is less. Love conquers all things, the love you gave to yourself. But if you have it, you did not give it to yourself. *For what do you have that you did not receive?*[^8] Who gave it to me, who gave it to you? God. Acknowledge the giver, lest you feel the damner. By believing the Scriptures, God gave you love, a great good, love surpassing all things. God gave it to you: *for the love of God has been poured out in our hearts*; perhaps from yourself? God forbid; *through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^9].

**5.** Return with me to that captive, return with me to my proposition. The Law terrifies the one who presumes on himself, grace helps the one who hopes in God. For see that captive. He sees another law in his members warring against the law of his mind, and leading him captive in the law of sin which is in his members. See, he is conquered, see, he is dragged, see, he is taken captive, see, he is subjugated. What did *You shall not covet* profit him? He heard, *You shall not covet*, that he might know the enemy, not that he might conquer him. *For he did not know covetousness*, that is, his enemy, *except that the Law said: You shall not covet*[^10]. Now you have seen the enemy; fight, free yourself, claim yourself into liberty. Let the pleasant suggestion be pressed down, let the illicit delight be destroyed. Arm yourself, you have the Law; go forth, conquer, if you can. For what is it that, by some little grace of God already, you delight in the law of God according to the inner man? But you see another law in your members warring against the law of your mind[^11]; not warring and prevailing nothing, but leading you captive in the law of sin. See, whence from you who fear, that multitude of sweetness is hidden; it is hidden from one who fears—how is it perfected for one who hopes? Cry out under the enemy, because you have an assailant; you have also a helper who awaits you fighting, lifts you up laboring; but if he finds you hoping; for he hates the one who is proud. Under the enemy, then, what do you cry? *Wretched man that I am*. Now you see, since you have cried out. Let this be your cry, when perhaps you labor under the enemy, say it, say it in your innermost heart, say it with sound faith: *Wretched man that I am*. Wretched I, wretched for this reason, because I. Wretched man that I am, both because I, and because a man. For in vain is man disturbed. For although man walks in an image[^12]; *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* Surely you? Where are your powers, where is your presumption? Surely you are silent about something; you are silent, but you are silent from exalting yourself, not from calling upon God. Be silent and cry out. For God himself too is silent and cries out; he is silent from judgment, he is not silent from precept; so you too, be silent from elation, do not be silent from invocation; lest God say to you, *I have been silent, shall I be silent always?*[^13] Cry out, then, *Wretched man that I am*. Acknowledge yourself conquered, confound your own powers, and say, *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* What had I said? The Law terrifies the one who presumes on himself. See, the man presumed on himself, he tried to fight, he could not overcome; he was conquered, he was laid low, he was subjugated, he was taken captive. He learned to presume on God, and he whom the Law terrified while presuming on himself, it remains that grace help him while hoping in God. Trusting in this he says, *Who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^14]. Now see the sweetness, taste it, let it be savory to you; hear the Psalm, *Taste and see that the Lord is sweet*[^15]. He has become sweet to you, because he freed you. You were bitter to yourself, when you presumed on yourself. Drink the sweetness, receive the pledge of so great a storehouse.

**6.** So the disciples of the Lord Jesus Christ were still under the law of the world, still to be nourished, still to be corrected, still to be guided. For they still coveted; though the Law says, *You shall not covet*[^16]. By the leave of those holy rams, the leaders of the flock; by their leave let me say it, because I say what is true; the Gospel speaks of it: they contended which of them was the greater[^17], and, with the Lord still placed on earth, they were tossed about by dissension over the primacy. Whence this, except from the old leaven? Whence this, except from the law in the members warring against the law of the mind? They sought the summit. Indeed they coveted; they pondered who was the greater; therefore their loftiness is confounded by a child. Jesus calls to himself the humble age, that he may tame the swollen covetousness[^18]. Rightly, then, even when they returned and said, *Lord, behold, the demons are subject to us in your name*. (They rejoiced over nothing; how much was it, what was it, compared to what God was promising?) So the Lord, the good Master, calming fear, building up firmness, said to them, *Do not rejoice in this, that the demons are subject to you*. Why this? Because, *Many will come in my name saying: Behold, in your name we cast out demons; and I will say to them: I never knew you*[^19]. *Do not rejoice in this, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven*[^20]. You cannot yet be there, and yet you are already written there. Rejoice, then. And that: *Until now you have asked nothing in my name*[^21]. For what you have asked, compared to what I wish to give, is nothing. For what have you asked in my name? That the demons be subject to you? *Do not rejoice in this*, that is, it is nothing that you asked; for if it were something, he would bid you rejoice. So it was not altogether nothing, but, compared to that greatness of God's rewards, it was slight. For indeed the apostle Paul was not nothing; and yet, in comparison with God, *neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters*[^22]. And to you we say, and to ourselves we say, both to us and to you we say, when we ask in the name of Christ these temporal things. For surely you have asked. For who does not ask? One asks health, if he is ill; another asks deliverance, if he is set in prison; another begs a harbor, if he is tossed in a ship; another begs victory, if he contends with an enemy; and in the name of Christ he begs it all, and what he begs is nothing. What, then, is to be begged? *Ask in my name*. And he did not say what, but in his words we understand what we ought to ask. *Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full*. *Ask, and you will receive, in my name*[^23]. But what? Not nothing; but what? *That your joy may be full*; that is, ask this which suffices you. For when you ask anything, he who drinks of this water will thirst again[^24]. He lowers into the well the water-jar of covetousness, draws up something to drink, that he may thirst again. *Ask that your joy may be full*; that is, that you may be satisfied, not that you may be delighted for a time. Ask what suffices you; say the voice of Philip, *Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us*. The Lord says to you, *Have I been with you so long a time, and you have not known me? Philip, he who sees me sees also the Father*[^25]. So give thanks to Christ for you who are weak, and prepare your throat to be satisfied by the divinity of Christ. Turning to the Lord, etc.
