# Sermon 350/A: Here Begins on Love and on Loving God Alone

**1.** We are not unaware that the hearts of your love are daily well fed by the exhortations of the divine readings and by the nourishments of the word of God; yet on account of the desire by which we are kindled toward one another, something must be said about love to your love. What else, than about love itself? For it is the thing about which, if anyone wishes to speak, he does not choose for himself a reading to be recited, that there may be an occasion for discoursing about love: for every page, whatever is opened, sounds this. To this matter the Lord himself is witness, and we are reminded from the Gospel, that when it was asked of him which were the greatest commandments in the law, he answered: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*, and *you shall love your neighbor as yourself*. And lest you should seek anything else in the holy pages, he subjoined and said: *On these two commandments the whole Law depends, and the Prophets*[^1]. If the whole Law and the Prophets depend on these two commandments, how much more the Gospel? For love makes a man new: for as greed makes a man old, so love makes him new. Hence, groaning in the struggle with greed, he says: *I have grown old among all my enemies*[^2]. But that love pertains to the new man, the Lord himself signifies in this way: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*[^3]. If, then, the Law and the Prophets depend on love—since in the Law and the Prophets the old testament seems to be commended—how much more does the Gospel, which is most plainly called the new testament, pertain to nothing but love, since the Lord too gave his commandment as nothing but that you love one another? And he both called the commandment itself new, and came for our renewal, and made us new men, and promised a new and likewise eternal inheritance.

**2.** For if perhaps you think how both the old testament is called law, and depends on love, although love makes a man new and pertains to the new man, this is the reason. The old testament is held forth there, because the promise is earthly, and the Lord promises an earthly kingdom to those who worship him there. But there existed even then lovers of God, who loved him freely, and cleansed their hearts with chaste sighing toward him; who, the coverings of the old promises removed, came to the prefiguration of the future new testament, and grasped that all those things which in the old testament according to the old man were either commanded or promised, are figures of the new testament, which in the last time the Lord was going to fulfill, the Apostle saying most plainly: *But these things happened to them in a figure; but they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^4]. And so the new testament was secretly foretold, and was foretold in those old figures. But when the time of the new testament came, the new testament began to be openly preached, and those figures to be narrated and expounded, so that there might be understood as new what there had been promised as old. Moses, then, was a herald of the old testament; but he who was a herald of the old was an understander of the new: to the carnal people he announced the old, he himself spiritual understood the new. But the apostles were heralds and ministers of the new testament, not because then there was not that which afterward through the apostles would be made manifest. Love, then, there; love here: but there love more hidden, fear more open; but here love more manifest, fear less. For as much as love increases, so much is fear diminished. For with love increasing the soul becomes secure; but where there is full security, there is no fear, as the apostle John too says: *Consummate love casts out fear*[^5].

**3.** Speaking, then, of this love with your holiness—because, as I said, whatever page of the Lord were read, it would admonish us of nothing but love itself—we have taken occasion also from the present psalm. See whether the divine words do anything else than make us love: see whether they do anything else than that we be kindled, that we burn, that we desire, that we groan, that we sigh, until we arrive. For men laboring on earth, and set amid the greatest temptations, attending most often with a mortal heart and weak thought to how here the wicked are wont, according to the time, to be powerful, and to be proud with transitory happiness—and this thought is wont to tempt the servants of God, as though they worshiped God without cause, if they see to be lacking to themselves what they behold abounding to the impious—for men so situated, then, the Holy Spirit, foreseeing this temptation of ours, and changing our love, lest impious and wicked men should seem to us the more to be imitated, the more we saw them to be happy according to the world, by the love of such things as those abounded in to their swelling, says: *Do not be envious of the malicious*—it is the heading of the psalm—*nor be jealous of those who work iniquity; for like grass they will quickly wither, and like the herbs of the meadow they will quickly fall*[^6]. Does the grass never flower? But it is little that flowers, the withering of that flower will quickly come; and that it may flower, cold air does it. The coming of the Lord Jesus Christ will be as the warm air of the year; this time is as the cold air of the year: but let us beware lest in the cold air of the year our love grow cold. Our honor has not yet appeared: there is cold on the surface, let there be warmth in the root. For so the trees in summer are leafy, and are beautiful, and are fruitful, which through the winter seemed dry. Was all that you see on the branches in summer there through the winter? It was, but it was hidden in the root. Our honor, then, which is promised us, is not yet: let our summer come: it is not yet, it is hidden. We say better, it does not appear, than, it is not yet; for the Apostle says openly: *For you are dead*. He was speaking as to trees through the winter. But that you may know that the surface seemed dead, while the inner parts lived, he at once subjoined and said: *And your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^7]. We seem to dwell on this earth: consider where we have fixed our root. The root of our love is with Christ, is in God: there is the opulence of our honor; but it does not yet appear now.

**4.** But what does he say next? *When Christ your life appears, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^8]. Now, then, is the time of groaning, then of rejoicing: now of desiring, then of embracing: what we now desire is not present; but let us not fail in desiring, let long desire exercise us, because he who promised does not defraud us. We do not say, brothers, let no one grow cold: let no one even grow lukewarm. And if lovers of the world insult those serving God: Behold what we have, and what we enjoy: where is your happiness? You do not have what you may see, but you have what you may believe: but those do not believe, to whom it is not shown; rejoice that you have believed: you will rejoice more when you will see. And if you groan, because you cannot now show it, the groaning of your grief will profit toward salvation, but also toward everlasting glory. There is nothing for them to show us as great: their happiness seems present, ours future; but we say more truly that neither is theirs present, nor will it be future: for because they love the false present, they will not come to the true future. But if they neglect the false present, and find what to do with that which they have, and know what to procure from it: let them hear the counsel of the blessed Apostle, which he charges to be commanded to the rich through Timothy. For he says: *Charge the rich of this world not to be high-minded, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy. Let them be rich in good works, let them give easily, share: let them lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^9]. If, then, brothers, the Apostle turned those who seemed to themselves to be happy in present things away from the earthly aim, and directed them to heaven: he did not wish them to rejoice over present things, but to hope for future ones; if the Apostle says such things to those who have these things, how much more ought he who has resolved to have nothing on this earth to stretch his whole heart to that which is to come? But to have nothing superfluous, to have nothing that burdens, to have nothing that binds, to have nothing that hinders. For that is more truly fulfilled even in this time in the servants of God, *as having nothing, and possessing all things*[^10]. Let there be nothing you call yours, and all things will be yours: if you cling to a part, you lose the whole; for as much of riches as would suffice you, so much suffices of poverty.
