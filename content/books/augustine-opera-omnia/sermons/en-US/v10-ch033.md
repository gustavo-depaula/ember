# Sermon 358: On Peace and Love

**1.** May the prayers of your holiness help our care for you, and for our enemies and yours, for the salvation of all, for quiet, for the common peace, for the unity which the Lord commanded, which the Lord loves, that we may again and again speak to you about it, and rejoice with you. For to speak of peace and love, if we always love it, we always ought, much more then at this time, when peace is so loved that those, set in the peril of loving and keeping it, to whom we do not return evil for evil[^1], and with whom, as it is written, hating peace we are peaceable[^2], and because we speak to them, they wish to war against us without cause: those, then, who are such, are imperiled between the love of peace and the confusion of shame, nor do they act, when they will not be conquered, so as to be unconquered. For those who will not be conquered by the truth, are conquered by error. O if love rather than animosity overcame them! Thence they would become victors, whence they had been conquered. We, for the catholic Church, to whose peace and reconciliation we invite its enemies, not by human opinions but by divine testimonies, love, hold, defend it. What shall I do with him who clamors for a part, and quarrels against the whole? Is it not good for him to be conquered, because if he is conquered, he will hold the whole; if he conquers, he will remain in a part? rather, if he will seem to himself to conquer, for none conquers but the truth: the victory of truth is love.

**2.** Why, then, should I commend to you, brothers, the catholic Church fruitful and growing throughout the whole world, with many words, and my own? We have the words of the Lord for it, and for us. *The Lord*, he says, *said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession*[^3]. Why, then, brothers, do we quarrel about the possession, and not rather recite the holy tablets? Let them suppose we have come to a judge. There is a contention about possession: and this contention is not of quarrel, but of love. Indeed, the quarreler over an earthly possession quarrels for this, that he may shut out his adversary: we, that we may let him in. The quarreler over an earthly possession, when he hears his adversary saying: I wish to possess; answers: I do not permit it. But I say to my brother: I wish you to possess with me; he, quarreling, says: I will not. And so I do not fear lest the Lord despise me and rebuke me, as he rebuked those brothers, or that brother, who appealed to him among the people, and said: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*. At once the Lord, bringing forth a rebuke, because he hated division: *Tell me, man*, he said, *who set me as a judge, or a divider of the inheritance among you? I tell you, beware of all greed*[^4]. This rebuke I do not fear. For I appeal to my Lord: I confess it, I appeal. Yet I do not say: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*; but I say: Lord, tell my brother to keep unity with me. Behold, I recite the tablets of this possession, not for this, that I alone may possess, but that I may convince my brother, unwilling, to possess with me. Behold the tablets, brother: *Ask of me*, it says, *and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession*. It was said to Christ. To us, then, it was said, because we are members of Christ. Why do you run into a part? or remain in a part? Behold, hold the whole that is in the tablets. You seek between whom and whom you may possess, as possessors are wont to be sought by documents, between whom there may be neighbors. He who gave you all the bounds left no neighbors.

**3.** Hear another testimony of the holy tablets. It is said of the Lord Christ in the figure of Solomon: *He will have dominion from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the world. Before him the Ethiopians will fall down, and his enemies will lick the earth. The kings of Tarshish and the islands will offer gifts: the kings of the Arabs and of Sheba will bring presents. And all the kings of the earth will adore him: all nations will serve him*[^5]. When it was said, it was believed: when it is fulfilled, it is denied. Hold with me, then, the inheritance *from sea to sea, and from the river*, namely the Jordan, where the teaching of Christ began, *to the ends of the world*. Why do you not want it? Why are you an enemy to this promise and inheritance, your riches? Why do you not want it? On account of Donatus? On account of Caecilian? Who was Donatus? Who Caecilian? Surely men. If good, by their own good, not mine: therefore if evil too, by their own evil, not mine. Do you receive Christ, and attend to his Apostle, zealous for Christ: *Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?* But that he might say this, see what he shuddered at: *Each one of you says: I am of Paul, but I of Apollos, but I of Cephas, but I of Christ. Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^6] If not in the name of Paul, much less in the name of Caecilian; much more, and much less, in the name of Donatus. And yet still, after the apostolic words, after the declaration of the Church and its spread through the whole world, it will be said to me: I do not let go Donatus, I do not let go some Gaius or other, some Lucius, Parmenian. A thousand names, a thousand splits. You will deprive yourself, by following a man, of so great an inheritance, of which you just now heard: *From sea to sea, from the river to the ends of the world*. Why do you not hold it? Because you love a man. What is man, except a rational animal made from earth? Therefore you are an enemy because you lick the earth. Despise this rather. Do not lick the earth, that you may place your hope in him who made heaven and earth. This is our hope, these our testimonies: *The God of gods, the Lord, has spoken, and called the earth, from the rising of the sun to its setting*[^7]. Do not remain in the earth, but where the earth has been called.

**4.** And who can recite all the testimonies of the holy tablets of this possession? Why, then, are they not converted to the Church, except because it is the very voice of the Church: *Let those who fear you be converted to me, and let them know your testimonies*[^8]? The Church saw this, which she said in the Psalm. Just now you heard, the words are fresh in your ears and hearts: *Of all consummation I have seen the end*. What is, *Of all consummation I have seen the end*? *Of consummation*, of perfection, not of consumption; and *the end*, of perfection, not of abolition. *Of all consummation I have seen the end: your commandment is exceedingly wide*[^9]. *Of all consummation I have seen the end*. What is this end? *Your commandment is exceedingly wide. For the end of the precept is*—now say it with me. All have said it, what you have always heard not unfruitfully. *The end of the precept is love from a pure heart*[^10]. The end by which we are perfected, not by which we are consumed. This end is wide, because it is the commandment of God, and to whom it was said: *Your commandment is exceedingly wide. A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*[^11]. See the width of this commandment. Where is it wide? In the flesh? In the heart rather. For if it were wide in the flesh too, you eager hearers would not suffer crowding. It is wide in the heart. See where it is wide, if there is anything whence you may see; and hear from this the Apostle, how wide is the commandment of love: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts*[^12]. He did not say, enclosed, but *poured out*. For the word "enclosed" sounds as it were of narrows: while "poured out" suggests width. *Wide*, then, *is your commandment exceedingly*. Lord our God, approve that on account of this very width we invite our brothers to the possession of peace. Do you wish to be bishops? Be it with us. Does the people not want two bishops? Be with us brothers in the inheritance. Let us not, on account of our honors, hinder the peace of Christ. What honor shall we receive in heavenly peace, if we now defend our honor in earthly quarrel? Let the wall of error be removed, and let us be together. Recognize me as a brother: I recognize you as a brother: but excepting the schism, excepting the error, excepting the dissension. Let this be corrected, and you are mine. Or do you not wish to be mine? I, if you correct yourself, wish to be yours. Therefore, with error removed from the midst, as it were the wall of the partition of contradiction and division, be my brother, and let me be your brother, that both may be his, who is the Lord both of me and of you.

**5.** We say these things out of love of peace, not out of distrust of the truth. For this we wrote back, this proposal you read: that we do not flee the cause to be conferred about, rather we insist that it be conferred; so that, when I have demonstrated the possession, I may so share the inheritance with him. Let him come undaunted, let him come secure, let him come learned: I do not wish to prejudge by authority. Let us open our eyes to him who cannot err: let him teach us what the Church is. You have heard his testimonies. Human offenses do not contaminate her whom human justice does not redeem. And yet, since the cause of the Church is one, that of men another, and they are altogether distinct, we do not even fear the cause of those men, whom they accused, and could not convict. We have known them cleared, we have read them cleared. Who, if they were not cleared, I would not establish the Church in their cause, nor build upon sand, and cast it down from the rock: because *upon this rock*, he says, *I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not overcome it*[^13]. *But the rock was Christ*[^14]. Was Paul crucified for you? Hold these things, love these things, say these things in a brotherly and peaceable way.

**6.** Let none of you rush to the place of the conference, my brothers. Plainly, if it can be done, avoid even passing through that place, lest perhaps some entrance of contention and quarrel be found, or some occasion be afforded, and those who seek an occasion find that occasion, especially since those who fear God too little, or count our admonition as of little worth, or because they love present things, ought at least to fear the severity of the earthly power. You have read the edict of the illustrious man publicly posted: which indeed was not posted on account of you who fear God; and you do not despise the admonition of your bishops; but lest anyone not care for these things, lest anyone despise them. Let those see, then, who are such: lest perhaps there befall them what the Apostle said: *For he who resists the power resists the ordinance of God. For princes are not a terror to the good work, but to the evil*[^15]. Let us avoid all sedition, all occasions of sedition. Perhaps you will say: We are intent on what we are going to do. There is something for us to enjoin on you: rich shares perhaps of piety. We dispute for you: do you pray for us. Help your prayers too, as we already forewarned, with fasts and alms. Add to them wings, by which they may fly to God. Doing this business, perhaps you will be more useful to us, than we to you. For none of us in this disputation presumes of himself: in God is the whole hope. For we are not better than the Apostle, who says: Pray for us. *Pray*, he says, *for me, that speech may be given me*[^16]. Therefore ask him for us, in whom we have placed our hope, that you may rejoice over our disputation. Hold these things, brothers, we beseech you, by the name of the Lord himself, by the author of peace, the establisher of peace, by the planter of peace, we pray you, that you pray to him peaceably, beseech him peaceably; and remember that you are sons of him of whom it was said: *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God*[^17].
