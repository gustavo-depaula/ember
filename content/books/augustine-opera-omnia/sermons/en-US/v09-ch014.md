# Sermon 313: On the Feast of Cyprian the Martyr

**1.** A most holy and most solemn day, and to this Church, by reason of its adornment, more familiar and more illustrious, has dawned today for our gladdening, which the most blessed Cyprian illumined for us by the glory of his passion. For the praises of this reverend bishop and venerable martyr no tongue would suffice, not even if he praised himself. So in this our sermon, which as owed we render to your ears concerning him, approve rather the affection of will than exact the effect of ability. For so too with the praises of God, for which not only speech but not even any thought is enough, when the holy praiser saw himself less able, he said: *Make the freewill offerings of my mouth pleasing, O Lord*[^1]. This I too would say: let this also be my devotion, that if I am not equal to unfolding what I want, it may be reckoned to my account, because I want to.

**2.** For what are the praises of so great a Martyr but the praises of God? Or whose honor is Cyprian turned to God with his whole heart, but his to whom it was said: *God of hosts, turn us*[^2]? Whose work is Cyprian the teacher, but his to whom it was said: *Teach me your statutes*[^3]? Whose work is Cyprian the pastor, but his who said: *I will give you pastors after my heart, and they will feed you with discipline*[^4]? Whose work is Cyprian the confessor, but his who said: *I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which your enemies will not be able to resist*[^5]? Whose work is Cyprian, the endurer of so great a persecution for the truth, but his to whom it was said: *The patience of Israel, O Lord*[^6]; and of whom it was said: *For from him is my patience*[^7]? Lastly, whose work is Cyprian, the victor in all things, but his of whom it was said: *In all things we more than conquer through him who loved us*[^8]? So we do not withdraw from the praises of God, when we praise the works of God, and the battles of God in the soldier of God.

**3.** For thus the Apostle exhorts: *Stand, having girded your loins in truth, and clothed with the breastplate of righteousness, and your feet shod in the preparation of the gospel of peace: in all things taking the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the fiery darts of the evil one; and take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God*[^9]. What is it to be clothed with the breastplate of righteousness, and to take the shield of faith, and the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God, but to be armed by the Lord with his gifts? Nor would it suffice for this soldier to be armed only, unless he had obtained to be helped by the very armed one by whom he had been armed. For surely the most godly Martyr, in that conflict of his passion, did pray and say: *Judge, O Lord, those who harm me, fight against those who fight against me. Take hold of arms and shield, and rise up to my help. Pour out the sword, and shut up the way against those who persecute me: say to my soul, I am your salvation*[^10]. How could he be conquered, whom the Lord so led forth armed, the armed one so helped?

**4.** But far be it that we should believe with a childish heart that God is armed with certain bodily instruments. Of what kind those arms are, by which God's soldiers are usually helped by the armed God, they themselves confess, being helped, where exclaiming and giving thanks they say: *Lord, you have crowned us as with the shield of your good will*[^11]. But the sword of God, that is, the blade of God, which sword the body of Christ, which is the Church, prays to be poured out and shut up against those who persecute it; can indeed be understood where the Savior himself says to his body: *I have not come to bring peace upon the earth, but a sword*[^12]. By which spiritual sword he separated from his martyrs, longing for heavenly joys, the earthly affections that wickedly flatter, by which they would be drawn back, bound, from heaven to earth, unless the sword of Christ cut between. But there is also another most evident sword of God: the soul of the righteous man in the hand of God; of which it is said to him in the Psalm: *Rescue my soul from the impious, your sword from the enemies of your hand*[^13]. What he said, *my soul,* this he repeated, *your sword;* what he said, *from the impious,* this he repeated, *from the enemies of your hand.*

**5.** This sword he poured out by scattering his martyrs everywhere: and shut up the way against those who persecuted the Church; so that, because they were not bent by the voices of preachers, they might be broken by the virtues of those who died. For God forges for himself strong arms against his enemies—those very ones whom he makes friends. So the soul of the most blessed Cyprian was a great sword of God, splendid with love, sharp with truth, driven and brandished by the power of the fighting God: what wars did it complete? what bands of gainsayers did it overcome by refuting them? how many hostile ones did it strike? how many adversaries did it lay low? In the hearts of how many enemies did it slay the very enmities by which it was assailed, and make them friends, by whom God might fight more abundantly against others? But when the time came that, as though the enemies were prevailing, he was seized, then indeed, lest, oppressed and conquered, he should yield to the hands of the impious, there was present the one through whom he was rendered unconquered: he received the victory, after no further contest remained for him to win except, namely, over this world and over the prince of this world. He was altogether present to his most faithful witness, contending even to death for the truth, he did what he had been entreated to do, he rescued his soul from the impious, his sword from the enemies of his hand. The holy flesh of which conquering soul, as the scabbard of that sword, we adorn in this place with the loftiness of the divine altar; to be rendered to that same triumphal soul in the resurrection, and thenceforth to be laid down in no death.
