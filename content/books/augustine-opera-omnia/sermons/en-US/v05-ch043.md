# Sermon 179

**1.** The blessed apostle James addresses the assiduous hearers of the word of God, saying, *But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves*[^1]. For not him whose word it is, or through whom the word is spoken; but you deceive yourselves. From this sentence, then, flowing from the fountain of truth, through the most truthful apostolic mouth, we too dare to exhort you; and while we exhort you, to look upon ourselves. For the preacher of the word of God is empty outside, who is not within a hearer. Nor are we so turned away from humanity and faithful consideration, that we do not understand our perils, we who preach the word of God to the peoples. But it consoles us that, where we are in peril in our ministries, we are helped by your prayers. For that you may know, brothers, in how much safer a place you stand than we, I bring forth another sentence of the same apostle, saying, *But let each of you be swift to hear, but slow to speak*[^2]. So first I will speak of this office of ours, on account of this sentence, by which we are admonished to be swifter to hear, slower to speak; that, when I have excused our office, who often speak, then I may come to what I first proposed.

**2.** It is fitting that we exhort you not to be hearers only of the word, but also doers. So, that we often speak to you, who does not judge us, little attending to our necessity, when he reads, *But let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak*[^3]? Behold, your zeal does not permit us to keep this sentence. You ought, then, to pray, to relieve, the one whom you compel to be in peril. Yet, my brothers, I will tell you—believe what I wish—because you do not see this in my heart. I who speak to you assiduously, by the command of my lord and brother your bishop, and at your demanding, then have solid joy, when I hear. My joy, I say, is then solid, when I hear, not when I preach. For then I am delighted in security. That pleasure has no puffing-up. There the precipice of elation is not feared, where there is the rock of solid truth. And that you may know it is so, hear where it was said, *To my hearing you will give exultation and gladness*. There I rejoice, where I hear. Then it follows and adds, *The humbled bones will exult*[^4]. Where we hear, then, we are humble; but where we preach, even if we are not endangered by elation, surely at least we are reined in. And if I am not exalted, I am in peril lest I be exalted. But where I hear, I enjoy without a defrauder, I am delighted without a witness. This joy that friend of the bridegroom too knew, who said, *He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom stands, and hears him*. And therefore he stands, because he hears him. Because the first man too by hearing God stood, by hearing the serpent fell. Therefore *the friend of the bridegroom stands, and hears him; and with joy*, he says, *he rejoices on account of the voice of the bridegroom*[^5]. Not on account of his own voice, but on account of the voice of the bridegroom. Yet the voice of the bridegroom which he heard within, he did not shut from the peoples outside.

**3.** This part Mary too had chosen for herself, who, while her sister ministered and was occupied about much ministry, sat at the feet of the Lord, and at leisure heard his word. John stood, she sat; but she stood in heart, and he sat in humility. For standing signifies permanence; sitting, humility. And that you may know that standing signifies permanence, the devil is said not to have had this permanence; of whom it was said, *He was a murderer from the beginning, and did not stand in the truth*[^6]. Likewise, that sitting signifies humility, that Psalm shows, where it admonishes about penitence and says, *Rise after you have sat, you who eat the bread of sorrow*. What is, *Rise after you have sat*? *He who humbles himself will be exalted*[^7]. What good hearing has, the Lord himself is witness, speaking of Mary who sat at his feet, and heard his word. For when her sister, most occupied about ministry, complained that she was deserted by her own sister, she heard from the Lord, appealed to, *Martha, Martha, you are occupied about many things; but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken from her*[^8]. Was what Martha was doing evil? Who of us could sufficiently unfold in words how great a good it is to minister hospitality to the saints? If to any saints whatever, how much more to the head and the chief members, to Christ and the Apostles? Does not each one of you who has this good of hospitality, when he hears what Martha was doing, say within himself: O blessed, O happy woman, who deserved to receive the Lord; whose guests the Apostles were made, walking in the flesh! Nor do you fail, because you cannot do what Martha did, receive Christ into your house with his Apostles; he himself makes you secure: *When you did it to one of my least, you did it to me*[^9]. It is a great work, then, a very great work, that the Apostle commands, saying, *Sharing in the necessities of the saints, pursuing hospitality*[^10]. Praising which in *the Epistle to the Hebrews* he says, *Through this some, unknowing, received angels in hospitality*[^11]. A great ministry, then, a great gift. And yet *Mary has chosen the better part*; because, with her sister anxious, laboring, caring for many things, she was at leisure, she sat, she heard.

**4.** Yet the Lord showed whence that part was better. For at once, when he had said, *Mary has chosen the better part*; as though we should ask, wishing to know whence the better, he added and said, *which will not be taken from her*. What do we understand, my brothers? If she chose the better part for this reason, because it will not be taken from her, without doubt Martha had chosen the part which will be taken from her. Plainly it will be taken from every man who ministers to the saints the things necessary to the body; it will be taken from him what he does. For he will not always minister to the saints. For to whom does he minister, but to infirmity? to whom does he minister, but to mortality? to whom does he minister, but to one hungering and thirsting? All these things will not be, when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality. For when that very necessity has passed away, there will be no ministry of necessity. Labor will be taken away, but the reward will be rendered. To whom then will food be ministered, where no one hungers? to whom drink, where no one thirsts? to whom hospitality, where no one is a pilgrim? The Lord, then, with his disciples, that he might render the reward of this work, deigned to be in need. He himself hungered and thirsted; not because he was forced, but because he deigned to. For it was good that the one through whom all things were made should hunger; for so the one who fed him would be happy. And when anyone fed the Lord, what did he give? who gave? whence did he give? to whom did he give? What did he give? He gave food to the Bread. Who gave? He surely gave who wished to receive more. Whence did he give? Surely not from his own? for what did he have, that he had not received? To whom did he give? Surely to the one who had created both what he received, and the one from whom he received? A great ministry this, a great work, a great gift. And yet *Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken from her*. Martha's part, then, passes; but, as I said, the reward given for it does not pass.

**5.** But Mary's part does not pass. See how it does not pass. Whence was Mary delighted when she heard? What did she eat? what did she drink? Do you know what she ate, what she drank? Let us question the Lord himself, who prepares such a table for his own, let us question him. *Blessed*, he says, *are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled*[^12]. From that fountain, from that storehouse of righteousness, holy Mary, sitting at the feet of the Lord, received certain crumbs, hungering. For the Lord then gave as much as she could take in. But the whole, as much as he is going to give at that future table of his, neither the disciples themselves, nor the Apostles themselves could then take in, when he said to them, *I still have many things to say to you; but you cannot bear them now*[^13]. Whence, then, was Mary, as I said, delighted? What did she eat, what did she drink with the most eager throat of the heart? Righteousness, truth. By truth she was delighted, truth she heard; for truth she gaped, for truth she sighed. Hungering she ate truth, thirsting she drank it; and she herself was refreshed, and that whence she was fed was not lessened. Whence was Mary delighted? what did she eat? I linger, because I am delighted. I dare to say, she ate him whom she heard. For if she ate truth, did he not himself say, *I am the truth*[^14]? And what more shall I say? He was eaten, because he was bread: *I am*, he says, *the bread that came down from heaven*[^15]. This is the bread that refreshes, and does not fail.

**6.** Let your Charity attend, then. Behold, we say to minister to the saints, to prepare food, to minister drink, to set the table, to wash the feet, to spread the bed, to receive under one's roof; is not all this going to pass away? But who dares say that now we are fed by truth, but will not be fed when we come to immortality? If now we are fed with crumbs, will we not then have a full table? For of that very spiritual food the Lord spoke, when he praised the faith of the centurion, and said, *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel. And therefore I say to you, that many will come from East and West, and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven*[^16]. Far be it from our thoughts, that we think of those foods at the table of that kingdom, of which kind the Apostle says, *Food for the belly, and the belly for food; but God will destroy both this and these*[^17]. Why *will he destroy*? Because there will be no hunger there. What is eaten there is not ended. For promising this very reward to his holy ones in that kingdom, he says, *Amen I say to you, that he will make them recline; and he will pass over, and will minister to them*[^18]. What is, *he will make them recline*, but, he will make them rest, will make them be at leisure? What is, *he will pass over*, or *he will pass on, and will minister to them*? After this passing over he will minister to them. For here Christ made a passing over; we shall come to him to where he passed over, there he no longer passes over. For "Passover" too in the Hebrew language is interpreted "Passing over." The Lord shows this—rather, the Evangelist, where he said of the Lord, *But when the hour had come for him to pass over from this world to the Father*[^19]. If, then, he feeds us here, and feeds us thus; how will he feed there? What Mary chose, then, was growing, not passing away. For the delight of the human heart from the light of truth, from the affluence of wisdom—the delight of the human heart, of a faithful heart, of a holy heart—there is found no pleasure to which it can in any part be compared, so as even to be called less. For what you say, It is less, as if by growing it would be equal. I do not wish to say: Less; I do not compare, it is of another kind, it is far other. For what is it now, that you all attend, you all hear, you are all roused, and when something true is said, you are delighted? What did you see? What did you hold? What color appeared to your eyes? What form, what figure, what stature, what lineaments of members, what beauty of body? None of these. And yet you love. For how would you so praise, if you did not love? How would you love, if you saw nothing? So, though I do not show the form of a body, the lineaments, the color, the beautiful movements, though I do not show it, yet you see, love, praise. If this delight of truth is sweet now, much sweeter will it be then. *Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken from her*.

**7.** I have shown, as I could, as far as the Lord deigned to help me, to your most sweet Charity, in how much safer a place you stand by hearing than we by preaching. For in this way you do now what we all are going to do then. For then there will not be any master of the word, but the Word the master. There follows, then, what pertains to you to do, to us to admonish. For you are the hearers of the word, we the preachers. But within, where no one sees, we are all hearers; within, in the heart, in the mind, where he teaches you who admonishes you to praise. For I speak outside, he within rouses. Therefore within we are all hearers; and we all, both outside and within in the sight of God, ought to be doers. How doers within? *Because whoever sees a woman to covet her has already committed adultery with her in his heart*[^20]. And he can be an adulterer with no man seeing, but God punishing. Who, then, is a doer within? He who does not look to covet. Who is a doer outside? *Break your bread to the hungry*[^21]. For when this is done, the neighbor too sees; but with what spirit it is done, none but God sees. *Be*, then, my brothers, *doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves*; not God, not the one who preaches. For I, or whoever preaches the word to you, do not see your heart; what you do within in your thoughts, I cannot judge. Because man cannot, God beholds it, from whom the human heart cannot be hidden. He sees with what zeal you hear, what you think, what you hold, how much you profit from his supplements, how earnestly you pray, how you beseech God for what you do not have, how you give thanks for what you have; he knows, who is going to exact. We can pay out the Lord's money; the exactor will come who said, *Wicked servant, you should have given my money to the bankers, and I coming would exact it with interest*[^22].

**8.** Do not, then, my brothers, deceive yourselves, that you have come eagerly to hear the word, if you do not do what you hear, by failing. Consider, if it is beautiful to hear, how much more to do. If you do not hear, you neglect the hearing, you build nothing. If you hear and do not do, you build a ruin. The Lord Christ has on this matter a most fitting similitude set forth: *He who hears*, he says, *these my words, and does them, I will liken him to a prudent man who builds his house on rock. The rain came down, the rivers came, the winds blew, and dashed against that house, and it did not fall*. Why did it not fall? *For it was founded on rock*. Therefore to hear and to do is to build on rock. For the very hearing is to build. *But he who*, he says, *hears these my words, and does not do them, I will liken him to a foolish man who builds*. He too builds. What does he build? Behold, *he builds his house*; but because he does not do what he hears, by hearing too *he builds on sand*. Therefore on sand he builds who hears and does not do; on rock, who hears and does; neither on sand nor on rock builds he who does not hear at all. But see what follows: *The rain came down, the rivers came, the winds blew, and dashed against that house, and it fell; and its ruin was great*[^23]. A pitiable spectacle!

**9.** So someone says: What I am not going to do, what need have I to hear it? For by hearing, he says, and not doing, I will build a ruin. Is it not safer to hear nothing? That very part the Lord did not wish to touch in his similitude set forth, but gave it to be understood. For in this age, rain, winds, rivers do not rest. Do you not build on rock, so that, when they come, they do not cast you down? Do you not build on sand, lest, when they come, they cast down the house? Therefore without any roof, because you hear nothing, so you will remain. The rain comes, the rivers come; are you therefore safe, because you are swept away naked? Consider, then, what kind of part you have chosen for yourself. You will not be, as you suppose, secure by not hearing; you must necessarily, naked without any roof, be overwhelmed, swept away, drowned. If, then, it is evil to build on sand, it is evil to build nothing; it remains that there is no good but to build on rock. It is evil, then, not to hear; it is evil to hear and not do; it remains to hear and to do. *Be*, then, *doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves*.

**10.** After this exhortation I fear lest I not raise you up by word, but break you down by despair. For perhaps someone, either one, or two, or surely more, in this frequent presence of yours, judges me, and says: I would like to know whether this one who speaks to me does all the things that he either himself hears, or says to others. To him I answer: *But it is a very small thing to me to be judged by you, or by a human day*[^24]. Since even I myself, in some part, can know what I am now; what I will be tomorrow, I do not know. But the Lord has given you security about me, O whoever you are who are so moved. For if I do what I say or what I hear, *be imitators of me, as I too am of Christ*[^25]. But if I say, and do not do, hear the Lord: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*. Therefore if you think well of me, you praise me; if you think badly, you accuse me, but you do not excuse yourself. For how will you excuse yourself, if you twist an accusation against an evil preacher of the truth speaking the word of God to you, and doing his own evil works; when your Lord, your redeemer, the one who poured out the price, gathering you into his army, and making his servant his brother, does not cease to admonish you, and says, *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*? *For they say*, he says, *and do not do*[^26]. They say good things, they do evil things; do you hear the good, and do not do the evil. Here you will answer: How do I hear good things from an evil man? *Surely they do not gather grapes from thorns*[^27]?
