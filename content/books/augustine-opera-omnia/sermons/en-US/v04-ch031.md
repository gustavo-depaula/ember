# Sermon 115

**1.** The reading of the holy Gospel builds us up to pray and to believe, and to presume not upon ourselves, but upon the Lord. What greater exhortation to praying, than that a likeness about an unjust judge should be set before us? For the unjust judge, neither fearing God nor reverencing man, yet heard the widow appealing to him, overcome by weariness, not inclined by devotion. If, then, he heard who hated what he was asked, how does he hear who exhorts us to ask? Since, then, the Lord persuaded us by this contrary comparison: *That we ought always to pray, and not to fail* [^1]; he added and said: *Yet when the Son of man comes, do you think he will find faith on earth* [^2]? If faith fails, prayer perishes. For who prays for what he does not believe? Whence the blessed Apostle too, when he exhorted to praying, said: *Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved* [^3]. And to show that faith is the fountain of prayer, and that the stream cannot go where the head of the water is dried up, he added and said: *But how will they call on him in whom they have not believed* [^4]? Therefore, that we may pray, let us believe; and that the very faith by which we pray may not fail, let us pray. Faith pours forth prayer, the poured-forth prayer obtains the firmness of faith. Faith, I say, pours forth prayer, the poured-forth prayer obtains firmness even for faith itself. For lest faith fail in temptations, therefore the Lord says: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation* [^5]. *Watch*, he says, *and pray, lest you enter into temptation*. What is it, to enter into temptation, except to go out from faith? For temptation prevails as much as faith fails; and temptation fails as much as faith prevails. For that Your Charity may know more plainly, that the Lord said about faith, lest it fail and perish: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*: in that place of the Gospel he says: *This night Satan has asked to vex you like wheat; and I have prayed for you, Peter, that your faith may not fail* [^6]. Does the one who protects pray, and the one who is in peril not pray? But what the Lord said: *When the Son of man comes, do you think he will find faith on earth?* he said of faith that is perfect. For that itself is scarcely found on earth. Behold, the Church of God is full: who would come here, if there were no faith? Who would not move mountains, if his faith were full? Attend to the Apostles themselves: having left all their own, having trampled the hope of the world, they would not follow the Lord, unless they had great faith; and yet if they had full faith, they would not say to the Lord: *Increase faith for us* [^7]. See too that man confessing both of himself (see faith, and not full faith), who, when he had offered his son to the Lord to be healed of an evil demon, and was asked whether he believed, answered and said: *I believe, Lord, help my unbelief* [^8]. *I believe*, he says: *I believe, Lord*: so there is faith. But *help my unbelief*: so the faith is not full.

**2.** But because faith is not of the proud, but of the humble: *He said to certain men who seemed to themselves just, and despised the rest, this likeness: Two men went up into the temple to pray, one a Pharisee, and the other a publican. The Pharisee said: Thanks to you, God, that I am not like the rest of men* [^9]. Let him say at least, Like many men. What is, *like the rest of men*, except all besides himself? I, he says, am just, the rest sinners. *I am not like the rest of men, the unjust, plunderers, adulterers*. And behold, from the nearby publican an occasion of greater swelling. *Like*, he says, *this publican*. I, he says, am alone, this man is of the rest. I am not, he says, such as this man, by my righteous deeds, by which I am not unjust. *I fast twice on the Sabbath, I give tithes of all that I possess* [^10]. What he asked of God, seek in his words, you will find nothing. He went up to pray: he refused to ask God, but to praise himself. It is too little, not to ask God, but to praise himself; over and above to insult the one asking. *But the publican stood afar off*: and yet he himself drew near to God. The conscience of the heart removed him, devotion brought him close. *But the publican stood afar off*: but the Lord regarded him from close at hand. *For the Lord is high, and regards lowly things* [^11]. But the high ones, such as that Pharisee was, he knows from afar. The high things indeed he knows from afar, but he does not pardon. Hear still the humility of the publican. It is too little, that he stood afar off: *he did not even lift his eyes to heaven?* That he might be regarded, he did not regard. He did not dare to look up: conscience pressed him down, hope lifted him up. Hear still: *He beat his breast*. He exacted penalties from himself: therefore the Lord spared the one confessing: *He beat his breast, saying: Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*. Behold one who asks. Why do you wonder, if God pardons, when he himself acknowledges himself? You have received the dispute about the Pharisee and the publican; hear the sentence: you have heard the proud accuser, you have heard the humble defendant; hear now the judge. *Amen I say to you*. The Truth says, God says, the judge says: *Amen I say to you, that publican went down justified from the temple, rather than that Pharisee* [^12]. Tell, Lord, the cause. Behold I see the publican go down from the temple more justified than the Pharisee. I ask why. You ask why? Hear why. *Because everyone who exalts himself will be humbled; and he who humbles himself will be exalted* [^13]. You have heard the sentence, beware the evil cause: I say another thing, You have heard the sentence, beware pride.

**3.** Let those now see, let them hear these things, I know not what men prattling impiously, and presuming on their own strength let them hear, who say: God made me a man, I make myself just. O worse and more detestable than the Pharisee! That Pharisee indeed proudly said he was just, but yet he gave God thanks for it. He said he was just; but yet he gave God thanks. *Thanks to you, God, that I am not like the rest of men. Thanks to you, God*: he gives thanks to God, that he is not like the rest of men: and yet he is reproved as proud and puffed up; not because he gave God thanks, but because, as it were, he desired nothing to be added to himself. *Thanks to you, that I am not like the rest of men, the unjust*. So you are just; so you ask nothing; so you are now full; so the human life on earth is not a temptation [^14]; so you are now full; so you now abound; so there is now no reason for you to say: *Forgive us our debts* [^15]. What, then, is the one who impiously fights against grace, if the one who proudly gives thanks is reproved?

**4.** And behold, after the dispute spoken and the sentence pronounced, even little ones come forward, rather are brought and offered to be touched. To be touched by whom, except the physician? Surely the healthy: to whom are infants offered to be touched? To whom? To the Savior. If to the Savior, surely to be saved. To whom, except him who came to seek and to save what had perished [^16]. Where had these perished? As far as pertains to themselves properly, I see them innocent, I seek the guilt. Whence? I hear the Apostle: *Through one man sin entered the world. Through one*, he says, *man sin entered the world, and through sin death; and so it passed to all men, in whom all sinned* [^17]. Let the little ones, then, come, let them come: let the Lord be heard: *Let the little ones come to me* [^18]. Let the little ones come, the sick to the physician, let the lost come to the redeemer: let them come, let no one forbid. In the branch they have committed nothing yet: but in the root they have perished. Let the Lord bless the little with the great [^19]: let the physician touch both little and great. We commend the cause of the little ones to the elders. Speak for those who are silent, pray for those who weep. If you are not in vain elders, be guardians: protect those who cannot yet plead their own cause. The perdition was common, let the finding be common: together we had perished, together let us be found in Christ. The merit is unequal, but the grace is common. They have nothing of evil, except what they drew from the fountain: they have nothing of evil, except what they drew from their origin. Let them not hinder from salvation those who have added much to what they drew. He who is greater in age, is greater also in iniquity. But the grace of God blots out what you drew, blots out also what you added. For where sin abounded, grace superabounded [^20].
