# Sermon 335/D: On the Martyrs

**1.** The holy martyrs, when they suffer evils here, hope in the Lord. For by hope they were saved: they walk toward the promises, they do not yet hold what is given. Because, then, they hoped under the cover of his wings, that they might bravely and patiently sustain the evils of the world, what will you give them? *They will be intoxicated by the abundance of your house*[^1]. If he had more to say, he would say it. For what would he say? "They will drink"? Even a little is drunk. What would he say? "They will be filled"? Even the sober are filled. They will drink so much that they will be intoxicated. For in that intoxication they forget all past evils. O holy intoxication! Would that in this intoxication we were all drunk.

**2.** Behold what kind of intoxication those desire who get drunk in the places of the martyrs, and those whom the martyrs were persecuted with stones, they persecute with cups; moreover they even dance, and give the members of Christ for the sport of demons, and think they please the martyrs while they please the unclean spirits. How great things are these we say? Let them at least attend to those at whose memorials they get drunk: if these had loved such things, they would not be martyrs.

**3.** Do not think, then, most beloved brothers, that you cannot have the merits of the martyrs because there are no persecutions, such as there were in those times when the martyrs were crowned. There are no persecutions now, but the trials never cease. Surely, my brothers, there are Christian men of those sons of men who hope under the cover of the wings of God. And when the time of hope has passed, and the joy of the reality has come, they will be intoxicated by the abundance of his house and he will give them drink from the torrent of his delight. From where will he give them drink? From where will they be intoxicated? Hear from where. *For with you is the fountain of life*[^2]. The intoxication of life is to live without end. This intoxication is undigestible: no one digests it and returns to thirst. So there are men of that number of the sons of men hoping under the cover of the wings of God, who wrestle in their bed and conquer, conquer and are crowned. But men pertaining to "man," not sons of man, because they care for nothing but this salvation which, when it passes, they reckon none will follow, even if they are called Christians, when this salvation begins to be in danger for them, seek soothsayers, send to astrologers, hang unlawful remedies on their necks. They desire salvation and bind their own neck, they bind the neck of the outer man and choke the throat of the inner. But he who says: "I will not"—a friend suggesting, and a neighbor muttering, or a neighbor woman or maid, sometimes even from his own household register—he who says: "I will not: I am a Christian; God forbids this; they are the sacraments of demons; hear the Apostle: *I do not want you to become partners of demons*[^3]"—it is answered him by the one who suggests it: "Do it and you will be well; this man and that man did it. What? Are they not Christians? Are they not faithful? Do they not run to church? And yet they did it and are well. That man did it and was at once healed. Do you not know him, that he is a Christian, faithful? Behold he did it and is well." But that sick man, because he does not love the salvation common to men and beasts, says: "If that man was made well from there, I refuse to be made well from there. For he can make me well to whom it was said: *You will save men and beasts, Lord, as you have multiplied your mercies, O God*[^4]." See the athlete of God, hear the athlete of Christ! O man sick and well! O weak and strong! O lying in bed and reigning in heaven! "But behold he refuses. Surely he does not take from me that divine salvation? It follows: *But the sons of men will hope under the protection of your wings*[^5]. But this salvation you do not see because *the salvation of the righteous is from the Lord*[^6]. I know that he keeps it for me. He who made me himself remakes me. But by death do you terrify me, for whom Life itself died? Christ gave his death to the impious, will he not give life to the godly?" He who says these things, and spurns such remedies, the more approved, the more they are called as if proven by experience, who flees such things, is a martyr in his bed. He grows faint and conquers, he barely moves his limbs and carries through battles. So this man suffers no evil, even if he dies of that disease. For he does not perish, but loses nothing, then rather he finds what he seeks. He will go out to his Lord, his forehead signed with the cross of Christ, to whom by unlawful amulets he did no injury. Will he, then, not give him what he promised, whom he himself protected as he fought? Surely the Lord protected him lest he suffer any evil, and helped his contest that the devil might be conquered by him. For many say, my brothers: "How did the martyrs conquer the devil?" As the good faithful conquer him. Hear the Apostle saying: *Your struggle is not against flesh and blood*[^7]. For flesh and blood raged against the holy martyrs. What is flesh and blood? Mortal men. Emperors, prefects, judges, soldiers, were flesh and blood. The raging peoples, the furious crowds, what else are they but flesh and blood? So lest the martyrs hate these furious and raging men, and, rendering in the heart evil for evil, be conquered by evil, the Apostle instructs them whom they have for their battle. *Your struggle is not,* he says, *against flesh and blood.* Those whom you see raging, raving, crying: *Crucify, crucify*[^8], are flesh and blood. They are not alone, they are not those whom you see, the devil rages through them. Do you beware the devil the thief in your gold. Why do you fear in hay the hay? Why do you fear earth for the sake of earth. What is the impious man but earth? For what do you fear him, except for your earth, that is, for your flesh which was made of earth? Another's earth passes away and your earth will rise again. So do not fear these men, but pray for them and speak to them: "Why do you rage, brother, why are you stirred up? To me indeed you rage, but to yourself you perish. Oh, if you would change your mind! Oh, if you would change your life! Because we all are going to die and rise again. I indeed have hope in God, for whom I suffer these things. But you, if you remain in this faithlessness, a little while, and you will not be, and would that you were not, for your own good, but you will be, for your own harm, with inextinguishable fire and the worm that does not die!" But that man says this: "These are the very words of proud Christians boasting about the resurrection. Who came from there and made known to you that what you say is true?" "You speak truly: your grandfather did not come from there, but your Lord returned from there, the very God whom you persecute in me, and for his sake you kill me as guilty, by whom God is not offended."

**4.** Let us return to our order, my brothers. *Your struggle is not against flesh and blood.* They rage indeed but they are vessels of another. Fear the armed one, not the bow; that is, the devil, not the man through whom the devil works. Yet do not fear even him, but beware. How will you not fear him? By hoping under the cover of the wings of God, from whom is the *salvation of the righteous,* because *he is their protector in the time of tribulation*[^9]. *For your struggle is not,* he says, *against flesh and blood, but against the principalities and powers and rulers of the world*[^10], that is, of men loving the world. He did not call those unclean spirits rulers of heaven and earth, but rulers of the world, because by "world" he meant men, lovers of the world, because by them they are ruled to rage. As they have been instigated, so they go to you. *The prince of the power of this air,* says the Apostle, that is, the devil. And what did he add? *Who now works in the children of disobedience*[^11]. These children of disobedience: flesh and blood. So let them be guarded against, because in them the devil works to rage against you. Beware him, love these; assail him, pray for these, that even from them he may be driven out, and that those who were before your persecutors may become your imitators. So they are to be guarded against. But you will say: "I do not see them. How do I fight against them?" *Against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places,* he said. By "in the heavenly places" he means this air. For not in that heaven where are the sun, moon, stars, where birds fly and yet are called the birds of heaven; so the unclean spirits, since they were lofty angels, thrust down into this air, indeed as into their prison, seduce those with whom they may burn.

**5.** Do not, then, say that there are no persecutions, because the trials do not cease. So abstain from unlawful things, from amulets, from incantations, from astrologers, from soothsayers. When you fall sick, do not seek such things and do not withdraw from God, lest you perish. But you will say with the Apostle: *For when I am weak, then I am strong*[^12]. You lie in bed and you are an athlete of God. You do not move your limbs and you carry through battles. The fever does not withdraw and faith toward God goes forward. But behold a neighbor stands by, and a friend, and a maid—I have already said it, perhaps from the household register—carrying wax or an egg in her hands and says: "Do this and you will be saved. Why do you prolong your sickness? Make this amulet. I have heard one who there invokes the name of God and of the angels and you will be well. To whom do you leave your widowed wife, to whom your tender children?" But that man: "I will not do it, because I am a Christian. May I rather die than perish by this." Hear the word of a martyr. See whether this is not what the pagan said: "Sacrifice and you will live." But that man: "I will not do it." O merits of the martyrs that never withdraw! Those conquered in the arena, this man in his bed, those over the slayer, this man over the seducer. But he is not conquered, with him protecting who for him hung on the wood.
