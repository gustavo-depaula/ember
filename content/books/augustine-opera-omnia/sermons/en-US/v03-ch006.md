# Sermon 55: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 5:22: "Whoever Says to His Brother, 'You Fool,' Shall Be Liable to the Fire of Hell," and What Follows

**1. 1.** The chapter of the holy Gospel which we just heard read greatly terrified us, if we have faith: but it did not terrify those who do not have faith. And because it does not terrify them, they wish to be perversely secure, not knowing how to apportion and distinguish the times of fear and of security. Let him fear, then, who now leads a life with an end, by which he may have in that life security without end. So we have feared. For who would not fear the Truth speaking and saying: *Whoever says to his brother, You fool, shall be liable to the fire of hell?*[^1] *But no man can tame the tongue*[^2]. And man tames a wild beast, but does not tame the tongue: he tames a lion, and does not curb his speech: he tames another, and does not tame himself: he tames what he feared; and to tame himself, he does not fear what he ought to have feared. But what happens? The sentence is true, and this too came forth from the oracle of truth: *But no man can tame the tongue.*

**2. 2.** What, then, shall we do, my brothers? I see that I am speaking to a multitude: but because we are all one in Christ, let us take counsel as if in secret. No stranger hears us, we are one, because we are unto one. What shall we do? *Whoever says to his brother, You fool, shall be liable to the fire of hell: But no man can tame the tongue.* Shall all, then, go into the fire of hell? Far be it. *Lord, you have become our refuge, from generation to generation*[^3]. Your anger is just: you send no one into hell unjustly. *Where shall I go from your spirit, and where shall I flee from you*[^4], except to you? Let us understand, then, dearly beloved, that if no man can tame the tongue, we must take refuge in God, who may tame our tongue. For if you wish to tame it, you cannot, because you are a man. *No man can tame the tongue.* Attend to a likeness from the very beasts that we tame. The horse does not tame itself; the camel does not tame itself; the elephant does not tame itself; the asp does not tame itself; the lion does not tame itself: so too man does not tame himself. But that the horse, the ox, the camel, the elephant, the lion, the asp may be tamed, a man is sought. Therefore let God be sought, that man may be tamed.

**3. 3.** So: *Lord, you have become our refuge.* We betake ourselves to you, and it will be well with us from you. For it is ill with us from ourselves. Because we forsook you, you forsook us to ourselves. Let us be found, then, in you, because we had perished in ourselves. *Lord, you have become our refuge.* What, then, my brothers, ought we to doubt that the Lord will make us meek, if we offer ourselves to him to be tamed? You tamed a lion, which you did not make; will he not tame you, who made you? For whence were you able to tame beasts so monstrous? Are you equal to them in bodily strength? By what power, then, were you able to tame huge beasts? The very animals that are called beasts of burden are wild beasts. For untamed they are not borne with. But because habit does not know them except in the hands of men and under the bridles of men and under the power of men, do you think they could have been born tame? Surely, attend to the monstrous wild beasts. The lion roars, who would not fear? And yet, whence do you understand yourself stronger? Not by bodily strength, but by the inner reason of the mind. You are stronger than the lion, whence you were made to the image of God. The image of God tames a wild beast; and will God not tame his own image?

**4. 4.** In him is hope, let us submit to him, and pray for mercy. In him let us place our hope, and until we are tamed and thoroughly tamed, that is, perfected, let us bear the tamer. For very often our tamer brings forth even scourges. For if you, to tame your beasts of burden, bring forth a rod, bring forth a scourge; does God not bring forth, to tame his beasts of burden—which we are—he who will make sons of his beasts of burden? You tame your horse: what are you going to give your horse, when, tame, it begins to carry you, to bear your discipline, to obey your command, to be a beast of burden, that is, an aid (adiumentum) of your weakness? What do you repay it, which you do not even bury when it is dead, but throw out to be torn by the birds? To you, when tamed, God reserves an inheritance, which is God himself: and you, dead for a time, he raises up. Your flesh, even to the number of the hairs, he will restore to you: and he will set you with the angels forever, where you no longer need to be tamed, but only to be possessed by the most loving One. For then God will be all in all[^5]: nor will there be any unhappiness to harass us, but only a happiness to feed us. He himself is our shepherd, our God: he himself our drink, our God; our honor, our God; our riches, our God. Whatever varied things you seek here, he himself, one, will be all to you.

**5. 5.** For this hope is a man tamed, and is the tamer reckoned intolerable? For this hope is a man tamed, and against this useful tamer, if perhaps he brings forth a scourge, is there murmuring? You have heard the Apostle exhorting: *If you are apart from discipline, then you are bastards, and not sons*[^6]. Bastards are children of adultery. *For what son is there to whom his father does not give discipline? And we had fathers of our flesh*, he says, *as correctors, and we revered them; shall we not much more be subject to the Father of spirits, and live?*[^7] For what could your father grant you, in that he corrected you, in that he beat you, in that he brought forth a scourge and struck you? Could he grant that you live forever? What he could not grant himself, when would he grant it to you? For his money, however little, which he gathered from interest and labor, he trained you with scourges, lest his labor, left to you, be squandered by your living ill. And he struck his son, fearing that his labors would perish: since he left to you what he could neither keep here nor take away. For he left you here nothing that could be his own: he yielded it, that you might so receive it. But your God, your redeemer, your tamer, your chastiser, your father, trains you. To what end? That you may receive an inheritance where you do not bury your father, but have your father himself as the inheritance. For this hope you are trained, and you murmur? And if anything sad happens, perhaps you blaspheme? Where will you go from his spirit? Behold, he lets you go, and does not scourge you: he deserts you blaspheming, will you not feel him judging? Is it not better that he scourge you and receive you[^8], than that he spare you and desert you?

**6. 6.** Let us say, then, to the Lord our God: *Lord, you have become our refuge, in generation and generation*[^9]. In the first generation and the other generation, you have become our refuge. You our refuge that we might be born, who were not: you our refuge that we might be reborn, who were evil: you our refuge that you might feed your deserters: you our refuge that you might raise up and direct your sons: you have become our refuge. From you we will not depart, when you have freed us from all our evils, and filled us with your good things. You give good things, you coax us, lest we grow weary on the way: you correct, beat, strike, direct us, lest we stray from the way. Whether, then, you coax us, lest we grow weary on the way; or chastise us, lest we stray from the way; *Lord, you have become our refuge.*
