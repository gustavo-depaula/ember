# Sermon 293/E: On Two Men: Christ and John

**1.** *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*. Did he grant that it should be said of each of them, *everything was made through him*? He retained divinity within himself and gave grace to us. Let us recognize the man for our sake, and recognize God above us: the same one man, the same one God. But you see both as human, John and Christ; yet the one you see as human is greater than John . . . and nevertheless John *is not* *worthy to untie his sandal strap.* Therefore, understand Christ as greater, so much greater that John is less than he, although greater than all the righteous. Christ, however, is greater than earth and heaven, greater than angels, greater than all powers, thrones, authorities, and dominions. Why greater? Because *everything was made through him, and without him nothing was made.* In this he is equal to the Father; through his humanity he is less than the Father. For the same one said, *I and the Father are one,* who said, *The Father is greater than I*. Both statements seem contrary, and both are true. Let your heart not quarrel, and the Lord's words do not quarrel. *I and the Father are one* shows equality; *The Father is greater than I* shows inequality.

**2.** Concerning *I and the Father are one,* hear the Apostle interpreting both statements in one passage: *Though* *he was in God's form, he did not regard being equal to God as robbery,* because he was born as this. Robbery belongs to what is usurped, as with Adam, for whom these things became robbery. Therefore, because he wanted to seize what he was not, he lost what he had. How did he desire robbery? The serpent told him what had caused his own fall: *I will place my throne toward the north, and I will be like the Most High*. This thought cast down the devil; this suggestion, through that pride, made the human being a partner of the devil. For the one already fallen envied the one standing and cast him down by the same means through which he himself fell. See for whom it was robbery. For Christ it was not, because he was born equal and remains from eternity to eternity. There was never a time when he was not equal; there will never be a time when he is not. Nor should we say, 'He was, he is, and he will be,' but 'He is.' For what is said to have been is no longer; what is said to be in the future is not yet. Therefore, when he commissioned his servant Moses and Moses asked him, 'What are you called?' and said, *What shall I tell the children* *of Israel?* he answered: *I am who I am. Therefore, you will tell the children* *of Israel: The one who is sent me to you*. Where 'is' is said, it is genuine and pure; it can never change anywhere. This is God, this God's Son, this the Holy Spirit. Therefore, brothers, this is above all, and thus the Son is equal to the Father. Hence the Apostle: *He did not regard being equal to God as robbery.*

**3.** Whence, then, *The Father is greater than I?* *But he emptied himself*—see, distinguish the words—*taking a slave's form*. Fix this in your ears and discern how, when speaking of a slave's form, he said *taking,* but of God's form he did not say 'taking,' but said: *Though he was in God's form, taking a slave's form, becoming in human likeness, and being found in appearance as a man, he humbled himself, becoming obedient to death, even death on a cross. Therefore, God also exalted him and gave him the name above every name*. Insofar as he was made, he was exalted; exalted insofar as he was humble. For insofar as he is equal to the Father, he was not exalted, because he was never lowly. Insofar as he died and rose, *he gave him the name above every name.* See Christ coming, yet he was already there; he rose and ascended into heaven, yet had not departed from there. Do you think him merely human? Do not think so. Here is a man, than whom *no greater has arisen among those born of women*. Hear him about that very man: *I am not worthy to untie his sandal strap*. Therefore, understand two human beings, but one a human being who is God, the other a human being good because of God; one a human being who is truth, the other a human being truthful from truth.
