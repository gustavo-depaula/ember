# Sermon 303: On the Birthday of the Martyr Lawrence

**1.** The martyrdom of blessed Lawrence is illustrious, but at Rome, not here: for I see how few you are. As Rome cannot be hidden, so neither can the crown of Lawrence be hidden. But why it should still lie hidden from this city, I cannot tell. So, you few, hear a few things: since we too, in this weariness of body and the heat, cannot say much. He was a deacon, a follower of the apostles: he lived in the time after the apostles. So when the persecution—which just now you heard from the Gospel had been foretold to Christians—was burning fiercely at Rome, as in other places, and the wealth of the Church was demanded, as it were, from the archdeacon, he is said to have answered: *Let wagons be sent with me, in which I may bring the wealth of the Church*. Avarice opened its jaws: but wisdom knew what it was doing. At once it was ordered: as many wagons as he asked, so many went. And he asked for many: and the more the wagons were, the greater was the hope of plunder conceived in the heart. He filled the wagons with the poor, and returned with them: and it was said to him, What is this? He answered: *These are the riches of the Church*. The persecutor, mocked, demanded the flames; but he was not so cold as to fear the flames: he was burning almost with frenzy, but more in soul with love. Why say more? The gridiron was brought, and he was roasted. And when he had burned on one side, he is said to have borne those torments with such tranquillity, that there was fulfilled in him what just now we heard in the Gospel: *In your patience you will possess your souls*[^1]. In short, burned by the flame, but tranquil in patience: *Now*, he said, *it is cooked; what remains, turn me, and eat*. Such was the martyrdom he passed through: with this glory he was crowned. His benefits at Rome are so famous that they cannot be counted at all. This is he of whom Christ said: *Whoever loses his soul for my sake will save it*[^2]. He saved it by faith, he saved it by contempt of the world, he saved it by martyrdom. How great is his glory before God, when his praise is so great among men?

**2.** Let us follow his footsteps by faith, let us follow also by contempt of the world. Heavenly rewards are promised not only to martyrs, but also to those who follow Christ with whole faith and perfect love. For he was honored among the martyrs, with the truth itself promising, and saying: *There is no one who leaves house, or field, or parents, or brothers, or wife, or children, and does not receive sevenfold in this time, and in the age to come will have eternal life*[^3]. What is more glorious for a man than to sell his own things, and buy Christ; to offer God the most acceptable gift, the uncorrupted virtue of the mind, the unimpaired praise of devotion; to accompany Christ, when he begins to come to receive vengeance on his enemies; to stand at his side, when he sits to judge; to become a coheir of Christ, to be made equal to the angels, to rejoice with the patriarchs, with the apostles, with the prophets, in the possession of the heavenly kingdom? What persecution can conquer these thoughts, what torments can overcome them? A mind hard, strong, and stable, founded on religious meditations, and a soul unmoved against all the terrors of the devil and the threats of the world, stands firm, which a sure and solid faith in things to come strengthens. The eyes are closed in persecutions; but heaven lies open. Antichrist threatens; but Christ protects. Death is brought on; but immortality follows. The world is taken from the one slain; but paradise is shown to the one restored. Temporal life is extinguished; but eternal life is repaired. How great is the dignity, and how great the security, to go out from here joyful, to go out glorious amid pressures and straits; to close in a moment the eyes by which men and the world were seen; to open them at once, that God may be seen, even by a happy passing! How great the swiftness! You are suddenly drawn back from the lands, that you may be set in the heavenly kingdoms. These things one ought to embrace in mind and thought, these one ought to meditate day and night. If persecution find such a soldier of God, his courage, ready for battle, cannot be conquered. Or if the summons comes before persecution; to the faith that was prepared for martyrdom, without loss of time, the reward is rendered with God as judge. In persecution one is crowned by soldiering, in peace by constancy.

[^1]: Luke 21:19
[^2]: Matt 16:25
[^3]: See Luke 18:29-30; Matt 19:29
