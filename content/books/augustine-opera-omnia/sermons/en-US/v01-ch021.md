# Sermon 16/B: A Sermon on the Response of Psalm 40

**1.** With one voice many of us have sung, because in Christ we are one. For the people who say in the plural number, *Our Father*[^1], are the same who say, *I said, Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^2]. For many wish to sin, and are unwilling that their sins be attributed to themselves. But let your Holiness attend to proud men, how they are unwilling to confess to God. For all the evils that they do they are unwilling to attribute to themselves, and they begin to say that either fortune did it or fate did it. But he says, "The devil did it," lest he say, "I did it." Let him, then, who says these things, remove them from the midst, because both fortune is a vanity of men and fate an emptiness, and whoever thinks that fate is anything himself becomes a fool. And the devil, although he is our enemy, yet trips up only the one who consents. He does not have the force of compelling, but the cunning of persuading. But if the devil, persuading evil, spoke alone, and God, teaching good things through the Scriptures, did not speak, you would have an excuse before God. You would say, "To whom was I going to consent, except to him who was speaking to me, since you were silent toward me?" But since the devil is not silent in persuading evil, and God is not silent in admonishing good, why are your ears, midway between the devil persuading evil and God commanding good, inclined to the words of the devil, and turned away from the words of God? He says to you, "Commit theft"; God says to you, "Do not commit theft." If you heard both, you would be most wicked. And yet how could you obey both, commanding different things, when God Christ cries out, *No one can serve two masters*[^3]? Now see what kind of man you are, who despise God admonishing, and consent to the devil deceiving. When you do this, at least attend, and now do not do it. And when you have seen that you did wrong, confess to God, do not accuse the devil, that you may be able to say truly, *I said, Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^4]. Fate did not sin, fortune did not sin: *Heal my soul, for I have sinned against you.* And what shall I do? Because I have sinned, I am sick. If I am sick, *heal my soul.* This is to confess to the physician, and to call upon the physician. If you wish to attribute your sins to others, as I said, either to fortune or to fate or to the devil, not to yourself, and again wish to attribute your good deeds to yourself, not to God, you are perverse. For, on the contrary, whatever evil you do, you do by your own malice; whatever good you do, you do by the grace of God.

**2.** But see how certain men, I know not who, unwilling, turn to blasphemies, so that they wish to accuse God himself. For when one has begun to accuse fortune, because it itself compelled him to sin, and it itself sinned in him, he begins to accuse fate; it is asked of him, "What is fortune, or what is fate?" And he begins to say that the stars compelled him to sin. See how little by little his blasphemy runs to God. Who set the stars in heaven? Is it not God the creator of all? If, then, he set there such stars as compel you to sin, does he not seem to be himself the author of your sins? See how perverse you are, O man, who, when God accuses your sins, not that he may punish you, but that, those sins being punished, he may free you, you, on the contrary, by your own perversity, where you do something good attribute it to yourself, where you do something evil attribute it to God. Turn yourself, then, from this perversity. Be corrected, and begin to contradict yourself, and to speak to yourself from the contrary. For what did you say before? "The good that I do, I do; the evil that I do, God does." Rather, it is true thus: the good that you do, God does; the evil that you do, you do. By saying these things you do not sing in vain, *I said, Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^5]. For if where you do evil it is God, and where you do good it is you, you speak iniquity against God. Hear from here too what the psalm says: *Do not,* it says, *lift up your horn on high, nor speak iniquity against God*[^6]. For this iniquity you were speaking against God, by which you wished to attribute all good things to yourself, and all evil things to him. By lifting up the horn of pride, you were speaking iniquity against God. With humility you speak equity. What is the equity that you speak with humility? *I said, Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^7].

**3.** Therefore, when that psalm had said, *Do not lift up your horn on high, nor speak iniquity against God*, it added at once, *For neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the desert mountains; because God is the judge, he humbles this one and exalts that one*[^8]. He sees two men, that is, two kinds of men. What two men, then, does he see? One being proud, the other confessing; one speaking equity, the other speaking iniquity. Who speaks equity? He who says, "I have sinned." But who speaks iniquity? He who says, "Not I sinned, but fortune sinned, fate sinned." When, therefore, you see two men, one speaking equity, the other iniquity, one humble, the other proud, do not wonder that what it says follows: *For God is the judge, he humbles this one and exalts that one.* And it is little, what I said: Brother, do not so speak as to attribute to yourself the good things you do, but to God the evil things. But even the good things themselves that you do, if you so attribute to God and give thanks to God for them, yet so that you exalt yourself above others who do not yet do good works, and seem to yourself as though of already perfect justice, because you do not commit murder, or do not commit adultery, or do not commit theft, or you fast, or you give alms, and you seem to yourself thence to have already perfected justice, and despise those who do not do these things, and are proud as one healthy looking at the sick, then God disapproves you. For you ought, however much you have advanced, not to attend to how far you have passed, but to what remains to you, until you both finish the way and rejoice in the homeland, exalted in the king of that homeland, who for your sake became humble.

**4.** Therefore two men are shown in the temple by the Lord, and the Gospel says thus: *And he spoke also against those who seemed to themselves just and despised the rest, this likeness. Two men went up into the temple to pray, one a Pharisee, and the other a publican*[^9]. The Pharisees were, as it were, the chiefs of the Jews, either as their learned or as their holy ones; but the publicans they held to be the most worthless sinners. Both, then, went up into the temple to pray, and the Pharisee began to say, *God, I give you thanks*. See that from the good which he had, he gave thanks to God. But see where he is reproved, because he despised the one whom he saw to be a sinner. Therefore see the following words: *I give you thanks, that I am not like the rest of men, unjust, robbers, adulterers, like this publican too*[^10]. He attended to him and despised him. And he exalted himself above, and asked nothing to be given him, but only gave thanks for what he had, as though he were already perfect. And he began now to enumerate to God, as it were, his merits: *I fast twice on the sabbath, I give tithes of all that I possess*[^11]. He had come to the physician to be cured, and he showed his healthy members, he covered his wounds. *But the publican stood afar off, nor did he dare to lift his eyes to heaven, but struck his breast, saying: Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^12]. See how he did not seek not to be accused. He accused himself and beat himself. With his fist he struck his breast, his conscience with fear, and confessed to God. Let your Holiness attend how he humbles this one and exalts that one. Hear the following words of the Lord: *Amen I say to you, the publican went down from the temple justified rather than that Pharisee*[^13]. And as though you said, "Lord, why so?" and he said to you, *For whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted*[^14]. Having, then, this way of humility, dearest brothers, advance, abstain yourselves from all wickedness and malignity. Cleanse again and again your ways, with the help of God, to whom you confess. Turned.
