# Sermon 59A

〈…〉 **1.** Let us, then, praise our God, brothers, because he sent his only Son for us, and did not wish the only Son he had to remain alone. From wicked servants he made good brothers for him, not by our merit but by his grace. Great is his mercy! Before he promised us anything, he owed us nothing; indeed, we would perish if he paid us what was due. It was not enough for him to make himself a debtor by promising: lest we fail to believe, he made a written bond. God's written bond is the holy Gospel, in which, through his disciples, who are our apostles, he transmitted to us for our salvation the prayer uttered by his own mouth. Let us explain this prayer, handed down to us, to Your Holiness, as far as he deigns to grant us.

**2.** Our Father, who art in heaven. We have found a Father in heaven; we have a father on earth. But generation has been condemned; regeneration has been promised. We change our father; let us change our inheritance. Our father from the earth left us an inheritance: his inheritance is called sin. Sin and death, guilt and punishment—this comes from Adam. *Through one man sin entered the world, and through sin death, and so it passed to all people, in whom all sinned*. An evil inheritance, a ruinous inheritance! Orphan, wake up; renounce it before the debt collector comes. Because I said “orphan,” seek a father for yourself. For you have been left an orphan and poor: an orphan by losing your father, poor by renouncing the inheritance. Seek, then, the one to whom you may say: *The poor man has been left to you; you will be the orphan's helper*. Seek the one to whom, or of whom, you may say: *My father and my mother have abandoned me, but the Lord has taken me up*. Seek, and if you have found him, say: Our Father. [^1]

**3.** Ask for the inheritance too, and say: Hallowed be thy name. We do not say this because his name is not yet holy, for it is always holy, but so that it may be hallowed in us. For through this sanctification we who are dirty are washed, we who are guilty receive pardon, we who are crooked are corrected, and we who have been stripped naked by unbelief are clothed in the white garment of faith. This is the inheritance our heavenly Father bestows on us. [^2]

**4.** We say: Thy kingdom come*.* We say to the Lord, “Thy kingdom come,” and at the end he himself will say: “*Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom*. Receive my fulfillment of what you hoped for when I promised it. I have cheated no one. I fulfill my promise, because you have shown your obedience.” This kingdom has been promised *from the beginning of the world*. What good does this kingdom hold? God's kingdom is eternal life. It is not a kingdom where a barbarian is feared; not a kingdom where a provincial supplies a soldier's rations; not a kingdom where the Lord says, *Render to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's*; not a kingdom where the Apostle says, *Render to everyone what is due: taxes to whom taxes are due, revenue to whom revenue is due, honor to whom honor is due, fear to whom fear is due*; *owe no one anything except to love one another*. Of all these things I have said, nothing will be there *except to love one another*. No tax, no revenue, no \*honor due, no judicial inquiry. Truth is not sought through torture, cases are not handled \*by legal experts, nor will each person be defended by advocates as best he can arrange. There will be a judge and a witness: Wisdom the judge, conscience the witness. Conscience without fear, life without sorrow, truth without error, charity without offense, health without illness. No hunger, no thirst, no need to eat. Only the love of seeing and the happiness of \*enjoying God. [^3]

**5.** We say: Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. We ask that, just as the angels have clung to 〈God〉 in holy charity from the moment they were made, so we too may cleave to him in chaste love, that we may do at least after our fall what they did without any fall. Let us say to God: *My soul has clung closely after you*. And let us not proudly claim the credit for ourselves, but attribute it to his praise and grace. For the passage continues: *Your right hand has upheld me*. *For I was wandering like a lost sheep*. That I was found was because the good shepherd sought me; that I was carried in my weakness was granted by his strength; that I was called back to the flock of blessedness was the gift of divine charity. [^4]

**6.** Give us this day our daily bread. When we say this, we ask for everything we need, whether for the heart or for the flesh. For just as our flesh is fed with earthly bread, so our mind is satisfied with heavenly bread. But this very bread was given to the Jews in manna and is given to us in grace: to them in a figure, to us in its reality. Yet because they used it badly, they perished; by using it well, we live. Here a Jew objects and says: “It was said of us: *He gave them the bread of heaven; man ate the bread of angels*.” I answer: it is true that you ate manna and the bread of heaven, but you did not deserve even to taste the bread of angels. Because you did not believe in him when he was present, you perished in your misery and do not know it. And you do not know it because you perished. For if you knew even in part, you would not have perished entirely. Do you want to know why you perished? Because, though you were Christ's own, you did not receive him when he came to his own. For the moment, this question of manna and the bread of angels is being discussed. I appeal to the bread himself and say to him: Lord, judge between me and the Jew, guilty above all sinners, who crucified you, God. Tell the Jews whether the manna that satisfied their bodies profited them, and what profit the bread of angels brings us. The Lord answers them: *Your fathers ate manna and died*. Dead as you are, you have heard about your dead parents; hear what he says about us, whom Christ, life itself, has brought to life. The Lord continues: *The bread*, he says, *that I will give is my flesh for the life of the world*. O Jew, this Christ, this life born among you, abandoned you who hated him; but by his grace he came over to me who love him. [^5]

**7.** And forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors. Here, when we ask concerning debts, we ask concerning the punishments for our sins, that they may be remitted. But what we ask of God, God commands of us. We say, “Forgive us sinners.” He \*answers: “Certainly I forgive, but those who serve me. Forgive other people, and I forgive you. Remit, and I remit. Hear me exhorting you, and I will hear you asking. For how do you want me to have compassion on your misery when you do not gladden me with the fruit of obedience? I have put the door of pardon into your hands. If you want to enter into my forgiveness, do not ask me at length or judge me difficult to move. Forgive your brothers, and open the door for yourselves. But if with perverse hands you cruelly shut it against yourselves, why do you spend so long asking me in vain?” *Does a man preserve anger against another and seek healing from God? Does he have no mercy on a man like himself, yet pray concerning his own sins? Has he begun to hold on to anger, yet seek pardon from God? Who will plead for his offenses?* O shameless soul! O brazen, unblushing conscience! Are you not ashamed to entreat the one whose commands you proudly despise, to wish to receive from the one \*at whose command you refuse to give? [^6]

**8.** When we say, “Do not allow us to be led into temptation,” we ask not that we should not be tested, but that we should not be led into temptation. For if we are not tested, we do not know our hidden weakness, if there is any, and we do not give thanks for whatever strength we have received as God's gift, or we even neglect to ask God for strength yet to be received. The prophet says: *By day the Lord commanded his mercy, and by night he made it known*. Day is spoken of for a time of security, night for a time of trouble. In a secure time we either advance through eager diligence or decline through idle neglect, and, worse still, we do not know which. In adversity, however, we reveal to ourselves and to others what we \*are within. This is the night's revelation, this is the usefulness of testing: that a person may know what he is through the one who made him. Of this testing the Apostle says: *God is faithful and will not allow you to be tested beyond what you can bear, but with the testing will also provide a way out, so that you may endure*. Hence to that same man who was once Saul and afterward Paul, once proud and afterward humbled, who from seeing outwardly was blinded and inwardly enlightened, God gave a thorn in the flesh, an angel of Satan to strike him, not in hostile hatred but in divine love, not to harm a bad man but through a great evil to bestow greater goods on a good man. He disregarded the request's desire and granted the petitioner's benefit. He did not remove the enemy's cruel persecution, so that he might perfect the son serving his Father's command. [^7]

**9.** But deliver us from evil. In these words we ask that the Lord strip us [of] the evil of our sin and punishment and \*clothe us in the good of full forgiveness and his everlasting kingdom. We ask, then, that he free us from the evil of corruption and mortality and fill us with the good of incorruption and \*immortality, because according to God's promise this corruptible thing will put on incorruption. For as long as we are in this world, we are pilgrims; but we shall reach God's homeland, and we shall see God, cleave to God, and enjoy him, where God will be all in all. [^8]
