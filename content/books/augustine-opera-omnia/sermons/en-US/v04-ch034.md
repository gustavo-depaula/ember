# Sermon 118

**1.** All you who seek the many words of man, understand the one Word of God: *In the beginning was the Word*. But *in the beginning God made heaven and earth* [^1]. But the Word was, when we heard: *In the beginning God made*. Let us acknowledge the Creator; for he is the Creator who made, but the creature what he made. For the creature that was made was not, as the Word God always was, through whom it was made. But when we heard: *The Word was*, with whom was it? We understand the Father, who did not make, nor create that same Word, but begot it. For *in the beginning God made heaven and earth*. Through what did he make them? *The Word was, and the Word was with God*; but what kind of Word? Did it sound and pass away? Was it thought and turned over? No. Was it remembered, and uttered? No. What kind of Word, then? Why do you ask me many things? *God was the Word*. When we hear: *God was the Word*, we do not double God; but we understand the Son. For the Son is the Word of God. Behold the Son, and what except God? For *God was the Word*. What of the Father? Surely God. If the Father is God, and the Son is God, do we double God? Far be it! The Father is God, the Son is God; but the Father and the Son are one God. For the Only-begotten was not made, but born. *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*; but the Word was from the Father. Was the Word, then, made by the Father? No. *All things were made through him* [^2]. If *all things were made through him*, was he too perhaps made through himself? Through whom you hear all things were made, do not think him made among all things. For if he too was made, all things were not made through him, but he himself was made among the rest. You say: He was made; surely through himself? Who is it who makes himself? If, then, he was made, how were all things made through him? Behold, he himself too was made, as you say, not I, because I do not deny him begotten. If, then, you say he was made, I ask through what, I ask through whom. Through himself? Then he was, before he came to be, to make himself. But if *all things were made through him*, understand that he himself was not made. If you cannot understand, believe that you may understand. Faith precedes, understanding follows; since the Prophet says: *Unless you believe, you will not understand* [^3]. *The Word was*. Do not, then, seek time for him, through whom times were made. *The Word was*. But you say: At some time the Word was not. You lie, nowhere do you read it. But I read to you: *In the beginning was the Word*. Why do you seek before the beginning? But if you can find anything before the beginning, that itself will be the beginning. He is mad who seeks anything before the beginning. What, then, says he was before the beginning? *In the beginning was the Word*.

**2.** But you say: And the Father was; and before the Word? What do you seek? *In the beginning was the Word*. What you find, understand; do not seek what you cannot find. There is nothing before the beginning. *In the beginning was the Word*. The Son is the splendor of the Father. Of the Wisdom of the Father, which is the Son, it was said: *For it is the brightness of eternal light* [^4]. Do you seek the Son without the Father? Give me light without brightness. If at some time the Son was not, the Father was a dark light. For how was the light not dark, if it had no brightness? Therefore always the Father, always the Son. If always the Father, always the Son. Do you ask me whether the Son was born? I answer: Born. For he would not be the Son, if not born. But when I say: Always the Son; I say this: Always he is born. And who understands: Always he is born? Give me everlasting fire, and I give you everlasting splendor. Let us bless God, who gave us the Sacred Scriptures. In the splendor of light do not be blind. Brightness is begotten from light, and yet the brightness is coeternal with the begetter. Always the light, always its brightness. It begot its brightness; but was it ever without its brightness? Let it be allowed to God to beget everlastingly. I ask, hear of whom we speak; hear, attend, believe, understand. We speak of God. We confess and believe the Son coeternal with the Father. But man, he says, when he begets a son, the one who begets is greater, and the one who is begotten is less. Behold, it is true; in men the one who begets is greater, and the one who is begotten is less, and he comes to the strength of his father. But why, except because, while the one grows, the other grows old? Let the father stand still in time, and by growing the son follows him, and you will see him equal. But behold, I give you from which to understand. Fire begets a coeval splendor. You do not find in men except lesser sons, greater fathers; you do not find coeval ones; but I give you, as I said, a splendor coeval with the fire its father. For fire begets splendor, but never without splendor. Since, then, you see splendor to be coeval with fire, allow God to beget a coeternal one. Let him who understands rejoice; but let him who does not understand believe. For the word of the Prophet cannot be made void: *Unless you believe, you will not understand* [^5].

[^1]: See Gen 1:1
[^2]: See Jn 1:3
[^3]: See Isa 7:9
[^4]: See Wis 7:26
[^5]: See Isa 7:9
