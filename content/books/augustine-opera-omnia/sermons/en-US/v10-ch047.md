# Sermon 365: On Verse 7 of Psalm 16 (15):

"I Will Bless the Lord, Who Gave Me Understanding," and the Rest

**1.** We have sung, and said: *I will bless the Lord*: would that with the heart, not only with the mouth: for if with the mouth and not with the heart, the praise of the Lord grows foul in the mouth of the sinner[^1]: nor does the confession of the mouth profit, if there is not within the profession of love. See, brothers: love is not a virtue of the mouth; there it fixes its seat, where the friend of the bridegroom is beautiful. But where is this beauty? *All the glory of the king's daughter is from within*[^2]. That glory is nothing other than beauty, that beauty is nothing other than love, love is nothing other than life. Therefore, that you may live, love. If you love, you are beautiful: love is good, love is beautiful. If that beautiful thing is absent, you do not live; you have indeed an appearance, but not within. Let that tomb be opened, propped with so many columns, let the marble be broken; what else will meet you but a horrible corpse, stinking bones, ashes, worms? And so there is an appearance, but it covers a dead thing, at the sight of which you shudder, you groan. But do you think that the dead man says: *I will bless the Lord*? Rather, with Scripture as witness: *The dead will not praise you, O Lord, nor all who go down into hell*[^3]. Open the Gospel, you will hear the Lord rebuking and saying to the devil: *Be silent*[^4]. Why? Because *the dead will not praise you, nor all who go down into hell.* No one praises him whom he does not love; or if an enemy praises, he loves the virtue which he praises in the enemy. He who sins exercises enmities with God: nor, therefore, does he praise God, nor does he praise the virtue of God; because praise is some good thing, which does not fall in sin. He who praises someone and lies, slanders, or mocks rather than praises. Take love away from the heart, only the lie remains. Do you wish the lie to praise the truth, and that God should take a tribute from there, whence blasphemy proceeds? Wise men do not greatly esteem being praised by the insane and the evil: shall God be praised by an impure and most shameless heart, by a blasphemous and most senseless mind? Say, then: *I will bless the Lord, who gave me understanding*[^5]. You are not insane, you have been healed, you have understanding; praise your God, who gave you understanding, who furnishes the eye: understanding, that you may grasp; the eye, that you may behold; that you may grasp how great is the breadth and length of the love of God, that you may look upon the author and finisher of faith; that you may grasp love, that you may look upon Christ; love, that you may love; Christ, that you may bless, and by loving and blessing may know the Lord, who gave you understanding; and by knowing may live: because: *This is eternal life, that they may know you the only God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^6].

**2.** But from where, I ask, is this understanding for you? Someone will come from across, and will say: He who gave nature gave understanding; good nature, good understanding; this good one I shall obtain, it is enough: for the understanding will not be degenerate; and from there I will bless the Lord, from where he gave me understanding, that is, from nature. O heretic, did you then obtain a good nature? Let the Apostle be silent and be confounded: *Not the good which I will, this I do*[^7]; and yet he obtained a good nature, and in this good nature the good which he wishes to do, he cannot do. He wishes, desires, tries: he wishes weakly, desires in vain; he tries fruitlessly; and against what he wishes, what he desires, what he tries, this good he does not accomplish. O good nature, whose will is feeble, desire void, efforts useless! Let the philosophers play their parts, and they will be most grateful to you, who so freely grant them a good nature. What of it? They have known the heaven, known the earth, known the sea, they explore the abysses, scrutinize the hidden things of nature, they know the distinctions of things, the differences, the essences; of all these they render an account; what of it? Go on, say more. After so many and so great things they fail, and did not glorify God as God, and became vain in their thoughts, and were made foolish, while they thought themselves wise, and changed the likeness of God into the likeness of a calf eating hay, and poured themselves out in the error of the Baals; and the god of this age blinded them, and they were handed over to ignominious passions, and were made like the things which they adored[^8]. And yet they rejoiced in a good nature, and through nature in understanding. O madman, do you see whether this nature grants understanding by which to bless God? rather it grants understanding by which they curse God, and rise up against the Creator? Say rather, brothers: *I will bless the Lord, who gave me understanding*: unless he had given it, I would not have understood; unless I had understood, I would never have blessed: he gave understanding, he furnished the eye. Blind nature does not provide what it itself lacks. Make for yourself understanding, and you will make yourself blind. The light which is in you is darkness[^9]. Say, then: *Enlighten my darkness, lest I ever sleep in death*[^10]. What is it to sleep in death? To hope something from nature, to make one's own arm flesh, to fashion for oneself understanding: he who does these things is not only in death, but sleeps in death, is more than four days dead, more than stinks[^11]. If he were only in death, he could be raised: but in death itself he sleeps more than an iron sleep, in it he rests. It is over with him.

**3.** But do you wish to know what he did who had obtained a good nature? Did he say like you: In a good nature, good understanding is mine, and he who granted the one did not deny the other? Let us dash the scorpion, let us crush it, let its mouth be stopped against iniquity. Read: *I obtained a good soul*. Go on, let us hear what treasure you have come upon, which perhaps will be turned into coals, and would that into coals which, heaped upon your head, consume the superfluous things[^12]. *And being rather good, I came to an undefiled body: and when I knew that I could not otherwise be continent, unless God grant it: and this very thing was of wisdom, to know whose gift it was: I approached the Lord, and besought him*[^13]. Most truly you obtained a good nature, who denies it? For the nature of the devil too is good, and all things which God made are good: but in this nature, although good, will you have wise understanding, by which you can abstain from the concupiscences and desires which war against the soul, if God does not give it? Learn, heretic, learn at last. *And I knew that I could not otherwise be continent, unless God grant it*: and in this is the chief thing of wisdom, which is wholly understanding, to know from where you may be continent, and that you may not otherwise be continent than from God alone. O excellent nature, which can abstain from nothing, nor otherwise can it but fall, if God does not give understanding by which it may not fall! Let us sing, then, brothers, let us sing. *I will bless the Lord, who gave me understanding*: he gave nature, he gave understanding; he healed nature, he healed understanding. In both the kind Samaritan showed mercy; he came down, bound the wounds, with wine which we understand, he cleansed, healed, carried into the inn, that is, into the Church, commended to the host[^14]. To what host? To the Holy Spirit, the inhabitant of the holy Church. He, with the coin received, but which he who pays for the wretched poured out from a torn purse, with his own oil, with his own ointment, healed the wounds of nature lying and breathing out, and with the same oil which he kindled, enlightened my darkness, and made my understanding clear. If you do not believe this, the Samaritan will not be yours, and you will perish in your wound, you who refuse to admit the hand of the one healing.

**4.** Let the Apostles be questioned, and let them say who, on that fiftieth day from the resurrection of the Lord, gave them understanding. Or did they perhaps stir up that terrible noise, by which the place was shaken, in which they awaited the promises of the Father? Did they perhaps kindle that supernal fire, with which they were wholly burning? Did they perhaps create those fiery tongues themselves? Did they perhaps procure for themselves those divine gifts, of which understanding is one, and pour them out into themselves according to the desires of the heart? Whoever says this has no part in this sacred fiftieth day; nor does he receive understanding, who thinks understanding is given according to nature. When, then, was understanding given, except when the Spirit too was given? Let us deal together, now you are confounded, now you blush; and would that you be so confounded that you may be built up, so blush that you may be glorified. What is the Holy Spirit, except the promised gift of the Father, except the gift of the Son? If it is promised, you have one by whom it is promised, for the things which are promised are outside us; and what was either in our power when it is promised, or possessed when one pleased, would be promised in vain. I promise you this which you do not have, and it is in me when I promise, that what I promise may descend into you. The Holy Spirit is also a gift, and above every gift, and a gift owed to none, and a gift granted freely. Christ was born, and laid in the manger that he might become the food of the pious beasts: yet he suffered thirst, weariness, was sold by his own, betrayed and accused by the Jews, scourged by the Gentiles, crowned with thorns, crucified, at last shut in by a stone: to what end all these things? That he might purchase for you this gift, which he afterward dispensed to the believer; or rather dispensed, that you might believe. For unless he had first given it, you would have believed neither before nor after. Whence if you have this gift from nature, you render the death of Christ empty, his passion, his cross. For he dies in vain, to acquire for you what is in you, and to give what you possess. Render to the Lord the humility of the manger, render the curse of the cross, render the abundance of the poured-out blood, in vain are all these things: you suffice for yourself, all these things are empty; you are rich, and among the goods of a rich nature you count understanding, which nature gave, not grace, and for which you are no debtor to the Creator, except in so far as he granted nature, which made understanding for you.

**5.** You recognize, I think, the error: and would that your reins might amend you[^15], so that you may better recognize it, and by recognizing ask, and by asking obtain the spirit of understanding and counsel and fear, by which you may turn out wiser, by which you may become more cautious and more subject to the Lord. These things, brothers, even if I did not treat them, yet there would be another who would instruct you: and among yourselves you would be treaters. For if, which God avert, you had slipped into so gross and stupid an error, as to think understanding was given you from nature, which you have from grace and the mercy of God alone, your reins would amend you, and would cry out: Why do thoughts of this kind ascend into your heart[^16]? Let each one consult himself, palpate himself, search himself, nor seek himself outside; let him question his reins, and they will tell him: that there was a law in my members contrary to the law of my mind[^17]; I am prone to every disgrace, so do the waves of concupiscence overwhelm me, that daily I am submerged, and fall into the depth of sins. Envy ascends, pride ascends, luxury ascends, in a formed column they assail the wretched man: if I avoid Charybdis, I run into Scylla; if I drive off lust, I succumb to anger; if greed does not subdue me, drunkenness strikes me down; if I abstain from robberies, I close my hand and my heart to the poor; if I shut my ears to slanders, death enters through the windows, that is, through the eyes I drink in the incentives of lusts and pleasures; finally, if I stop up one crack, a hundred open, by which I receive the hostile flood, and at last grow faint. These are the things which your reins treat with you, and conscious of their own weakness instruct you, rebuke you, amend you, even into the night. What night? Of your dark conscience, with whose darkness wrapped, you would wish, if you could, to hold these up as it were a shield against the light of truth, and to hope well of your own strength. But your reins amend you. *In the reins and in the loins is strength*, as it is somewhere in Scripture[^18]: but since the loins are so feeble, the reins so flabby, they instruct and say: If in the reins, from where comes strength, there is so great weakness, so great infirmity, what will become of the rest? When, then, our loins have instructed us, and taught how vain is the salvation of men; what remains, except that we gird our loins, that they may not flow apart? When our floors, either by too great age, or too great heat, or the impulse of storms, or for some other cause, gape open, we at once join them with nails and sinews: let this be done with your loins, that is, with your strength; they are loosened, they fall, they grow faint, the whole frame is dissolved; you have nails in the Crucified, drive them in firmly, and the better the deeper; do not place them under what is tottering, that it may be propped up; so will the loosened frame be strengthened. The nails of the cross have wherewith to raise the prostrate, wherewith to restore the weak, wherewith to hold back those slipping. Drive, strike, even to the marrow, even to the soul itself; from this amendment you will be healed, and healed you will say to the Lord: *My strength, I will keep for you*[^19]: from you my strength, to you my strength. Turned to the Lord, etc.
