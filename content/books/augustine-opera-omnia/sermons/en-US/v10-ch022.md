# Sermon 350/E: On the Ministry of Carnal Things Done to the Saints

**1.** The Apostle says: *But let him who is catechized in the word share with him who catechizes him in all good things*[^1], surely admonishing that which in another place too he confirms with the law as witness, that the mouth of the threshing ox is not to be muzzled. For he says thus: *Let the presbyters be honored with double honor, especially those who labor in the word and in teaching. For Scripture says: "You shall not muzzle the mouth of the threshing ox"*[^2]. This to Timothy; but to the Corinthians, when he was dealing with this matter and proclaiming that this was lawfully owed to him, but that he did not demand it: *Who*, he says, *ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? who plants a vineyard and does not eat of its fruit? who shepherds a flock and does not partake of the milk of the flock? Do I say these things according to man? or does not the law also say this? For in the law of Moses it is written: "You shall not muzzle the mouth of the threshing ox." Is it about oxen that God is concerned? or does he say it altogether for our sake? For it was written for our sake, because he who plows ought to plow in hope, and he who threshes in hope of sharing. If we sow spiritual things for you, is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things? If others share in this power over you, do not we more? But we have not used this power*[^3].

**2.** Since, then, these things are so, most beloved brothers, since absolutely no one of the faithful and good sons of the Church doubts that carnal things must be rendered with due service, for necessary uses, to the servants and ministers of God, the dispensers of spiritual things, it can seem a wonder how those very carnal things which are provided are sown not in the flesh but in the spirit. For when, writing to the Galatians, he had said what I mentioned a little before, *But let him who is catechized in the word share with him who catechizes him in all good things*, then, following, he added: *Do not err: God is not mocked. For what a man sows, this he will also reap; because he who sows in his flesh, from the flesh will reap corruption; but he who sows in the spirit, from the spirit will reap eternal life. But in doing good let us not grow weary; for in due time we shall reap. Therefore, while we have time, let us untiringly work good toward all, but especially toward those of the household of faith*[^4]. If, then, he who sows in the flesh will reap corruption from the flesh, it is manifest that those carnal things which are ministered for the food of the dispensers of God, each one sows not in his own flesh, since indeed from it he will reap not corruption but eternal life, the Lord saying: *For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^5], and answering those who asked: *When you did it for one of my least ones, you did it for me*[^6], and commending the reward itself at the last: *So shall the impious go into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^7]. But those who give food to the hungry Christ and drink to the thirsty Christ in the least ones of Christ minister carnal things, according to the Apostle who says: *Is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things?*, and yet from the spirit they reap eternal life, surely not by sowing in their own flesh but in the spirit, although they sow carnal things. So much does it avail, not what is given, but with what mind it is given: for what is given is earthly food, which, as the Lord mentions and the thing itself proclaims, goes into the belly and is sent out into the latrine[^8]; but the intention of the giver, sowing the carnal thing in the spirit, is added, and a tiny portion of earthly fruits becomes the price of the kingdom of heaven.

**3.** What are these things but the great and wonderful works of God? Spiritual things are sown and carnal things are reaped: for what else is, *If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things?* Likewise carnal things are sown and spiritual things are reaped: for what else is, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world, for I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^9]? O the venerable commerce of the saints! Great things are given and small things received, and again for those very small things great things are repaid—if indeed those are to be called great and these small, as the sun is great and the moon small, when both are heavenly luminaries; but this is not even to be so compared, as if a small star were compared to the great sun, because both of these too are from heaven. Far be it that we should so speak of the great teaching of wisdom which the apostles of Christ preached, as though, being of that kind, the human kindness of the table which believers ministered to the preachers were small, when the former is the illumination of the mind, the latter the sustenance of the belly; when it is the least thing for the former to go before even the sun, while the latter cannot endure under the sun. But now, if the spiritual things which the holy teachers sow incomparably surpass these carnal things which they reap from the devout faithful, what is to be said of that eternal life which in the end is repaid, most dear, for these most cheap things? For God is then to be seen, he is to be clung to, he is to be enjoyed without end. *For this is*, he says, *eternal life, that they may know you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^10]. For this he openly promised, when he said: *He who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me; and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and will show myself to him*[^11]. For these earthly, small, and temporal things, then, for that the hungry Christ is given food in his least ones, he is going to repay not something of his own, but himself. How great will the victor be in that judgment, whose prize the Judge himself will be!

**4.** Knowing this, what reward awaited these works, the Apostle rejoiced not for his own convenience when these carnal things were provided for his temporal needs. Therefore, when he spoke of it, he said: *Not because I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit*[^12]. For the worker of the Lord's field could obtain food from elsewhere too, but it was bad for the tree to remain barren. For the Lord fed Elijah with ravens ministering to him, whom he afterward gave to a widow to be fed[^13], not because there was nothing else from which the servant of God might be nourished, but that she might have something by which she might deserve well of his Lord. The dispensers of God, then, rejoice, sowing spiritual things and reaping carnal things, but they rejoice not from greed for what is given but from love for the givers. Otherwise far happier would be those who, by providing earthly things, are going to receive heavenly things in the end, than those who sow spiritual things and reap carnal things. But because, again, those very carnal things which they reap they refer to the advancement of those from whom they reap them, love embraces all things in one end, that at once by all, with no end, the kingdom may be enjoyed. For from this it is that God wills to teach his little ones through the dispensation of men and to sustain his dispensers through the ministry of men, so that, while they supply one another's need, they may bring to birth benevolence and bear beneficence. Otherwise, just as he fed the hungry, when he willed, from where he willed, with no one bestowing anything on anyone—as Elijah through the birds of heaven, the people of Israel through manna from heaven[^14]—so too he can teach the unlearned even with no words of men, as Moses, as the other prophets, as the Apostle who says, *Not from men nor through man*[^15], as that other Apostle to whom it is said, *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^16]. That, then, he teaches some through whom he may teach others, and enriches some through whom he may feed others, when he could without the ministry of man both teach and feed all, he wishes the works of mercy to be exercised by men, that one from another's temporal want may obtain the merit of eternal abundance: for carnal supplies Elijah is sent to the widow to whom the birds were ministering food, the tithes are commanded to be provided to the Levites by the people whom all together the manna had satisfied[^17]; finally the Lord himself, made man for our sake, now a certain Zacchaeus[^18], now a certain Martha[^19] receives, and they feed, with very few loaves, the one who satisfies so many thousands, yet feed him as a poor man; and likewise, in the dispensation of spiritual things, Ananias receives Saul called from heaven to be instructed[^20], the angel sent to Cornelius sends him to Peter[^21], the Holy Spirit comes upon all and yet appoints some as teachers for others[^22]. Thus weakness makes those temporally needy necessary to one another, that love may make all rich, that eternity may receive all after the time.

**5.** Buy, therefore, the field with the treasure while there is time, buy the incorruptible pearl of perpetual whiteness: it is the kingdom of heaven of which I speak, preached by the Lord's mouth in the Gospel[^23]. Our fathers bought it and left it still to be bought by us. By the buyer it is kept whole for the one who will buy; in turn it is bought by all, the whole is possessed by each. Nor let the care of the price torment you; for it has not been fixed so that the richer can easily reach it and the poorer cannot stretch himself to it: as much as each one has, for so much it is worth to him. He received half the goods of the most wealthy Zacchaeus, he did not exclude the nets of the fisherman[^24], he admitted even the two coins of a certain needy widow, and weighing them in the scale of love he found them heavier than the many gifts of many rich men[^25]; nor would you separate from this commerce even a cup of cold water, where the love of the buyer is fervent[^26]. Even if perhaps it is lacking, let him not despair: let him love and he buys. There are certain great resources of the poor—pious wills. For what is more precious than peace, or to what do we tend through all our labors except to eternal peace? Hear, then, at the birth of the Savior, the testimony of the angels on this matter, *praising God and saying*: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^27]. This is brought forth not from a money-bag, not from a storehouse: it is kept in the heart, it is paid out from the heart, neither corrupted when kept nor lost when paid out.
