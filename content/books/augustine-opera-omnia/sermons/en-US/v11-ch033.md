# Sermon 218: Augm. (éTaix 5)

**1.** The passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, by whose blood our offenses have been blotted out, is solemnly read, solemnly celebrated, that by an annual devotion our memory may be more gladly renewed and that, by the very frequency of the peoples, our faith may be more clearly proclaimed. The solemnity itself, then, requires of us that we render you such a sermon on the passion of the Lord as he himself grants. And indeed, for our salvation and the usefulness of passing through this life, in those things which our Lord suffered from his enemies, he deigned to offer us an example of patience, that for the sake of evangelical truth, if he himself shall so will, we may refuse to suffer nothing of the kind. But yet, because not even in that very mortal flesh did he suffer anything by necessity, but all things by will, it is rightly believed that he willed to signify something to us also in each of the several deeds which were done and written about concerning his passion.

**2.** And first, that, handed over to be crucified, he himself carried his cross, he gave a sign of continence and demonstrated, going before, what he ought to do who should wish to follow him. This too he admonished by word, where he says: *He who loves me, let him take up his cross and follow me*[^1]. For he in a manner takes up his cross who rules his own mortality.

**3.** That he was crucified in the place of Calvary signified in his passion the remission of all sins, of which it is said in the psalm: *My iniquities have been multiplied above the hairs of my head*[^2].

**4.** That two were crucified with him on either side showed that some would be at his right hand, others at his left—those at the right of whom it is said: *Blessed are they who suffer persecution for justice's sake*[^3], but those at the left of whom it is said: *If I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^4].

**5.** That a title was set above his cross, in which was written: *King of the Jews*[^5], showed this, that not even by killing him could they bring it about that they should not have him as king, who by a most manifest and most eminent power, according to their works, will render to them. Whence it is sung in the psalm: *But I have been appointed king by him over Sion his holy mountain*[^6].

**6.** That the title was written in three tongues, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, declared that he was to reign not only over the Jews, but also over the nations. Accordingly in the same psalm, when he had said: *But I have been appointed king by him over Sion his holy mountain*, where, namely, the Hebrew tongue reigned, he at once, as it were adding the Greek and Latin, says: *The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you; ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance and the ends of the earth as your possession*[^7]. Not that Greek and Latin are the only tongues of the nations, but because these especially excel, Greek on account of the study of letters, Latin on account of the empire of the Romans. Although in these three tongues the totality of all the nations was shown to be subjected to Christ, yet there it was not written "and king of the nations," but only "of the Jews," that the origin of the seed might be commended in the propriety of the name. *For the law went forth from Sion,* as it was said, *and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem*[^8]. For who are they who say in the psalm: *He has subjected peoples to us and nations under our feet*[^9], but those of whom the Apostle says: *For if the nations have shared in their spiritual things, they ought also to minister to them in carnal things*[^10]? Or do we not see the nations subjected to the most excellent grace of the apostles, or ought we to attend to the broken branches, who today are called Jews, or ought we rather to hear that *Israelite of the race of Abraham*, from Saul Paul, and therefore from small great, admonishing and saying to the engrafted wild olive: "Understand! *You do not bear the root, but the root you*"[^11]? Therefore Christ is the King of the Jews, under whose mild yoke the nations too were sent unto salvation. That this was granted them by a greater mercy, the Apostle himself shows more openly where he says: *For I say that Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers, but that the nations glorify God for his mercy*[^12]. For it was not right to take the bread of the children and cast it to the dogs, unless the dogs, humbled to gather the crumbs which they pick up from the table of their masters, through that very humility being exalted and made men, should merit to approach the table itself.

**7.** That the chief priests of the Jews suggested to Pilate that he should not write absolutely that he is *King of the Jews,* but that he himself said he was king of the Jews, but Pilate answered: *What I have written, I have written*[^13]—just as the chief priests of the Jews figured those broken branches, so Pilate figured the engrafted wild olive, because he was a man from the nations writing the confession of the nations, convicting the denial of the Jews, to whom the Lord himself rightly said: *The kingdom shall be taken from you and given to a nation doing justice*[^14]. Nor therefore is he not the King of the Jews. For the root bears the wild olive, not the wild olive the root. And although those branches were broken through unbelief, *not* therefore *has God rejected his people whom he foreknew. For I too,* he says, *am an Israelite*[^15]. And although the sons who were unwilling that the Son of God reign over them go into the outer darkness, yet many will come from the east and the west and recline not with Plato and Cicero, but with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of God. For Pilate wrote King of the Jews, not King of the Greeks or Latins, although he was to reign over the nations. And what he wrote, he wrote, nor did he change it at the suggestion of the unfaithful, which was foretold so long before in the psalms: *Do not corrupt the inscription of the title*[^16]. All the nations believe in the King of the Jews, over all nations he reigns, but yet *King of the Jews.* So much did that root avail, that it could change the engrafted wild olive into itself, but the wild olive could not take away the name of the olive.

**8.** That the soldiers took away his garments divided into four parts signified his sacraments, to range through the four parts of the world.

**9.** That they cast lots for the one tunic, seamless, woven from above, rather than divided it, was sufficiently demonstrated that the visible sacraments, although they too are the garments of Christ, can yet be had by any whatever, whether good or bad; but that the most sincere faith which works through love, the integrity of unity, which love has been poured out from above in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us, does not pertain to any whatever, but is granted as it were by lot by the hidden grace of God. Whence to Simon, who had baptism and did not have this, it was said by Peter: *You have neither lot nor part in this faith*[^17].

**10.** That from the cross he commended his recognized mother to his beloved disciple, he fittingly showed human affection then, when he was dying as a man. That hour had not yet come, when, about to turn water into wine as God, he had said to that same mother: *What is it to me and to you, woman? My hour has not yet come*[^18]. For he had not taken from Mary what he had in his divinity, just as he had taken from Mary what hung on the cross.

**11.** That he said: *I thirst*[^19], he was seeking faith from his own. But because *he came to his own and his own did not receive him*[^20], for the sweetness of faith they gave the vinegar of perfidy, and this in a sponge. Truly to be compared to a sponge, not solid but swollen, not opened by a straight approach of confession, but hollow with the tortuous windings of snares. Yet that drink had hyssop too, which, a humble herb, is said to cling to the rock by a most firm root. For there were in that people those for whom this crime was reserved for the humbling of their soul by repentance, after casting it off. He himself knew them, who received the hyssop with the vinegar. For he prayed for them too, as another evangelist testifies, when, hanging on the cross, he said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^21].

**12.** That he said: *It is finished, and with his head bowed he gave up the spirit*[^22], showed not the necessity but the power of his death, who, waiting until all the things which had been prophesied of him should be fulfilled—because this too was written: *And in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink*[^23]—as it were having the power of laying down his soul, as he himself testified of himself, gave up the spirit through humility, that is, with head bowed, to receive it again through resurrection, with head exalted. That this death and the bowing of the head was of great power, that patriarch Jacob foretold in the blessing of Judah: *You have ascended,* he says, *reclining, you have slept like a lion*[^24], signifying by the ascent the cross, by the reclining the bowing of the head, by the sleeping death, by the lion power.

**13.** But now that the legs of those two were broken, but his were not, because he was dead, why this was done the Gospel itself declared. For it was fitting that by this sign too it should be shown that in the prophecy to be foretold of him the Pasch of the Jews was commended, where this was commanded, that they should not break the bones of the sheep[^25].

**14.** That his side, pierced with a lance, poured out blood and water upon the earth is, beyond doubt, the sacraments by which the Church is formed, just as Eve was made from the side of the sleeping Adam, who was *a type of the one who was to come*[^26].

**15.** That Joseph and Nicodemus bury him: as some have interpreted the names, Joseph means "increased," while Nicodemus, since it is a Greek name, is known to many to be compounded from "victory" and "people," because *nikos* means victory and *demos* people. Who, then, was increased by dying, if not he who said, *Unless a grain of wheat dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it is multiplied*[^27]? And who likewise by dying conquered the people of his persecutors, if not he who by rising again will judge them?
