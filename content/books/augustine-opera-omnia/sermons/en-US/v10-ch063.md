# Sermon 376/A: The Sunday in the Octave of Pasch

**1.** [2.2.] Today is called the octave of the infants; their heads are to be uncovered, which is a sign of liberty. For this spiritual birth has liberty, but the birth of the flesh has, properly, slavery. There are indeed two births of man: to be born and to be reborn. We are born to toil, we are reborn to rest; we are born to miseries, we are reborn to eternal happiness. For those children, infants, little ones, sucklings, clinging to their mothers' breasts, and not knowing how much grace is conferred upon them, since, as you yourselves see, they are called infants, they too have their octave today; and these old men, young men, youths—all are infants. For the one infancy of those belongs to oldness, the other to newness. For those whom you see fresh from the womb are born old. Our old man is called Adam, from whom we are born; the new man is Christ, through whom we are reborn. These, then, are both new, and reborn to another life, and there is in them, if it may be said, when they are born, a new oldness.

**2.** [2.3.] Behold, today our infants are mingled with the faithful, and as it were they fly out of the nest. It is necessary, then, that we who gave them birth address them. For just as you recall, my brothers, when the young of swallows or of house sparrows have begun to fly from the nest, their mothers fly around them with a clamor, and with loving cries bear witness to the dangers of their offspring.  
**[3. 3.]** We know, then, that many who are called faithful live badly, and that their conduct does not accord with the grace they have received; they praise God with their tongue and blaspheme him with their life. But we know others among these many, like grains among a great abundance of chaff, groaning under the threshing but consoling themselves with the hope of the granary. We know that these two kinds of people are in the Church. We know the Church as the Lord's threshing floor: we await the winnowing on the day of judgment; we long for the gathering of the wheat at the resurrection; we desire to reach the granary in eternal life. There will be no chaff there, just as there will be no grain in hell. Now, my brothers, although we know that these two kinds of people are in the Church, the devout and the impious, the good and the bad, those who fear and those who despise, we do not know to which these newly baptized will attach themselves. They themselves know what we desire; but whether our hopes for them will be fulfilled, human ignorance is worn out by anxiety, and sometimes disturbed even by false suspicions. We learn from these things on this earth, where life is never without temptation. I therefore admonish you, holy shoots; I admonish you, new plants in the Lord's field, lest what was said of the vineyard of the house of Israel be said of you: *I expected it to produce grapes, but it produced thorns*[^1]. May he who was crushed like a cluster for us find a cluster in you. Bear grapes; live well. For *the fruit of the spirit is*, as the Apostle says, *charity, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, gentleness, faith, self-control, chastity*[^2]. When our husbandman comes to us, whose laborers we are, he who gives growth within—for we know how to plant and water externally, but the Apostle says: *Neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything, but God who gives the growth*[^3]—he who now sees how you listen, who now examines how you fear, or are beginning to fear: when that husbandman comes to you, may he find in you what the Apostle said: *My joy and my crown, all you who stand firm in the Lord*[^4].

**3.** [4.4.] Brothers, sweetest sons, dearest sons, imitate the good, beware of the wicked. For I know that wicked men will come to you and will try to persuade you to drunkenness, and they will say to you: Why? Are not we too most faithful? I know it, and from that I grieve, from that I fear. Now if you say steadfastly: "Brother, I would not even want you to do what you do; but if I cannot lead you to my good, at least do not drag me to your evil"—such men, as usually happens, will have a headache. Your neighbor, man or woman, will say to you: "There is here an enchanter, there is here a charmer, and somewhere an astrologer." You say: "I am a Christian, it is not lawful for me." And if he says to you: "Why? Am I not a Christian?" you will say: "But I am one of the faithful." And he to you: "And I have been baptized." Members of Christ become angels of the devil. Because the enemy possesses that man, he seeks to drag another too. Let him who has prepared these things for you find you ready. For this reason I speak, for this reason I bear witness, for this reason I am not silent, for this reason I shake out my garments, for this reason at the tribunal of my God I have myself excused. I will say to my God: Lord, I have not been silent; Lord, the talent you gave me I did not hide, but paid out. This he could say to me: *Wicked servant, you should have given it, that I might demand it*[^5]. Behold, Lord, I have given; do you demand it. And if perhaps an old habit tickles you, you have one to call upon. The helper is greater than the assailant. For this reason you groan, for this reason you pray, for this reason you say: *Lead us not into temptation*[^6]. Observe also that, my brothers, which you say above: *Forgive us our debts*, so that you may do what follows: *as we also forgive our debtors*[^7]. You give alms, you receive alms. You pardon, you will be pardoned. You give out, it will be given out to you. Hear God saying: *Forgive, and you will be forgiven; give, and it will be given to you*[^8]. Keep the poor in mind. To all I say: give alms, my brothers; do it, and you will not lose. Trust God. Not only do I tell you that you do not lose what you do for the poor, but I plainly tell you that this alone you do not lose, while the rest you do lose. Behold, let us see whether you gladden the poor today; you are their storehouses, that God may give you the means to give, and may forgive what you perhaps sin. Shut up your alms in the heart of the poor, and it will itself plead with the Lord for you—to whom be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen.

[^1]: Isa 5:2
[^2]: Gal 5:22
[^3]: 1 Cor 3:7
[^4]: Phil 4:1
[^5]: Luke 19:22-23
[^6]: Matt 6:13
[^7]: Matt 6:12
[^8]: Luke 6:37-38
