# Sermon 335/I: On the Birthday of the Martyrs

**1.** We know what we have sung, and we hold it well: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^1]. For by his price the death of the saints is precious. So it is not to be wondered at that the death of those is precious who were bought at so great a price. For the world is not weighed against the blood of him through whom the world was made. But that he might have blood which he might shed for us, *the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us*[^2]. So his blood is the price of us all, which blood nevertheless was shed for the forgiveness of sins. For what were sinners worth, or how much were they worth? Was that blood truly the price of sinners? Indeed Christ died for the impious. Hear the Apostle: *God commends his love toward us,* he says, *because when we were still sinners, Christ died for us*[^3]. *When we were still sinners,* were we worth so much? Indeed, we would be worth nothing, if we remained sinners. That buyer of ours by his price cleansed what he had bought. For how would a sinner be bought at so great a price, unless he were cleansed by his price?

**2.** So what we were before he bought us, let us not look back upon, lest we remain on the way. And let us not look back, and yet hold it in memory. For if we look back, we return to it; if we forget, we are ungrateful. So what we were, it is both good to remember and quick to hate: to remember that we may give thanks, to hate lest we return to the old things. For Jesus did not die for sinners by loving sinners. Which indeed, if we seek it on the surface, presents some difficulty of question. For how did he not love those for whom he willed to die? And who are they for whom he willed to die? You heard the Apostle: *When we were still sinners, Christ died for us.* If, then, he loved those for whom he died, and they are sinners for whom he died, then he loved sinners. Let the sinner be silent, let the Savior speak. I know from where comes what you say, O sinner: you love to be a sinner. For you would not say: the Savior loved sinners, unless you loved to be a sinner. So let the sinner, as I said, be silent; let the Savior speak. What does the Savior say? *Behold you have now been made well, sin no more, lest something worse befall you*[^4]. How threateningly he forbade what you loved! So he loves what you wished to be, to whom he threatens such things, if you roll back to what you were?

**3.** How then did he die for sinners, if he does not love sinners? Plainly not loving sinners, he died for sinners. Understand, and you do not labor. You ask me how he did not love sinners who died for sinners. Tell me first what I ask you, and you yourself will answer yourself about what you had asked. What each one loves, does he want it to exist, or not want it to exist? I think that, if you love your children, you want them to exist; but if you do not want them to exist, you do not love them. And whatever you love, you want it to exist, nor do you at all love what you desire not to exist. What then did the Lord want, when he died for us? That we be sinners, or that we be freed from sins? If he died for us for this reason, that he might blot out our sins, surely he did not love what he blotted out? Who blots out what he loves? If you are faithful, if you have believed in him, if you have your heart on high, that he might write what you are, he blotted out what you were.

**4.** See him. Hear him speaking in the psalm through prophecy: *Their weaknesses,* he says, *were multiplied, afterward they made haste*[^5]. What is: *Their weaknesses were multiplied? The law entered in, that the offense might abound*[^6]. And what: *afterward they made haste? Where the offense abounded, grace superabounded*[^7]. What then? *Afterward they made haste,* because, filled with diseases, they sought the physician. What then? *I will not gather their assemblies,* he says, *of bloods*[^8]. What is: *I will not gather their assemblies of bloods?* I will not gather them through those sacrifices of cattle, not of bloods, but of one blood, on account of which blood *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^9]. So *now I will not gather their assemblies of bloods.* For by those bloods they were convicted, not cleansed. There was given, there was shed one cleansing blood, for many convicting bloods. One blood was given, and what then? *Nor will I be mindful of their names through my lips*[^10]. Before that one blood was shed, when their assemblies were gathered of bloods, their names did not die: the adulterer was an adulterer; the thief was a thief; the striker was a striker; the sacrilegious was sacrilegious. One blood was shed: *I will not be mindful of their names through my lips. Do not err,* says the Apostle, *neither fornicators, nor those serving idols, nor adulterers, nor the effeminate, nor liers with males, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor the rapacious, nor the slanderous, will possess the kingdom of God*[^11]; these were their names. And that you may know that these were their names, hear what follows: *And these things indeed you were*[^12]. And where is: *Nor will I be mindful of their names through my lips? But you were washed, but you were sanctified*[^13]. So now what you were you are not.

**5.** Surely God loves sinners: surely sinners, through his blood, what they were they are not. How are they loved, since the Lord Christ loved the righteous, not sinners? What he wanted to make he loved, not what he found. The physician, if he fulfills what he is called, loves the healthy, not the sick. And I plainly dare to say, and it is true, he came to the sick for this reason, because he does not love the sick. What I said seems contrary, but I ask: Why did he come to the sick? To make him well. So he does not love the sick. What he wants to make he loves, what he wants to take away he does not love. So the holy martyrs were made well, bought at so great a price, redeemed by their Creator. Twice servants, because both made and bought. And from where twice servants, thence free: bondsmen of the Creator, brothers of the Redeemer. That you may see the servants, read the Gospel as an instrument; there see where they are bought. Attend to the very transaction: the purse of their price hung on the wood, was struck, was poured out. He poured out, and bought: you read, and you find the instrument of servants. He made them brothers from servants, and what was the instrument of servants became the testament of sons. Amen.
