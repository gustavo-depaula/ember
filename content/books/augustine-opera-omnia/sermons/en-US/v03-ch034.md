# Sermon 73: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 13: Where the Lord Jesus Expounds the Parable of the Sower

**1.** Both yesterday and today, as our Lord Jesus Christ spoke, we heard the parables of the sower. You who were present yesterday, recall it today. Yesterday it was read about that sower who, when he scattered seeds, one fell on the path, which the birds gathered; another in stony places, which dried up in the heat; another among thorns, which was choked, and could not be made fruitful; another into good ground, and it brought forth fruit a hundredfold, sixtyfold, thirtyfold[^1]. But today the Lord again narrated another parable about a sower, who sowed good seed in his field. While men slept, the enemy came, and oversowed tares. When it was grass, it did not yet appear: when the fruit of the good seed began to appear, there the tares too became clear. The servants of the householder were offended, seeing in the good crop many tares, and wished to root them out, and were not permitted[^2]: but it was said to them: *Let both grow until the harvest*[^3]. But the Lord Jesus Christ also expounded this parable: and said that he himself is the sower of the good seed, showed that the sower of the tares is the enemy man, the devil; the time of harvest, the end of the age; his field, the whole world. But what does he say? *At the time of harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather first the tares to burn them, but my wheat store in the barn*[^4]. Why do you hasten, he says, servants full of zeal? You see tares among the wheat, you see bad Christians among the good; you wish to root out the bad: be still, it is not the time of harvest. Let it come, and let it find you wheat. Why are you vexed? why do you bear it ill that the bad are mixed with the good? In the field they can be with you, in the barn they will not be.

**2.** But you know those three things mentioned yesterday, where the seed did not succeed, the path, the stony places, the thorny places; these are the tares. They received another name in another likeness. Because, when likenesses are given, the property itself is not expressed, by them not the truth, but the likeness of the truth is expressed. What I say, I know few have understood: but we speak for the sake of all. In visible things a path is a path, stony places are stony places, thorny places are thorny places: what they are, that they are; because they are properly named. But in parables and likenesses one thing can be called by many names: therefore it is not incongruous that I should say to you, That path, those stony places, those thorny places are bad Christians; they are also the tares. Is not Christ the lamb? is not Christ also the lion? Among the wild beasts and flocks, what is a lamb is a lamb, what is a lion is a lion: Christ is both. Those single things by property: these both by likeness. There is even more, which happens, that by likeness things very distant from one another are called by one name. For what is so distant from one another as Christ and the devil? Yet a lion is called both Christ, and the devil. Christ a lion: *The lion has conquered, of the tribe of Judah*[^5]. The devil a lion: *Do you not know that your adversary the devil goes about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour?*[^6] So both that one is a lion and this one is a lion: that one a lion for fortitude; this one a lion for ferocity: that one a lion for conquering; this one a lion for harming. The devil himself is a serpent, the ancient serpent[^7]: surely we were not commanded to imitate the devil, when our Shepherd said to us: *Be simple as doves, cunning as serpents?*[^8]

**3.** So yesterday I addressed the path, I addressed the stony places, I addressed the thorny places: and I said: Be changed, while you can; turn the hard ground with the plough, throw the stones out of the field, pluck the thorns out of the field. Do not have a hard heart, whence the word of God quickly perishes. Do not have thin soil, where the root of love does not sit deep. Do not choke with worldly cares and cravings the good seed which is scattered to you by our labors. For the Lord sows: but we are his laborers. But be good ground. We said it yesterday, and today we say to all: Let one bear a hundredfold, another sixtyfold, another thirtyfold. In one the fruit is greater, in another smaller: but all will belong to the barn. Yesterday we said these things, today I address the tares: but the very tares are sheep. O bad Christians! O you who, by filling, press the Church by living badly! correct yourselves before the harvest comes! Do not say: *I have sinned, and what has happened to me?*[^9] God has not lost his power: but he demands of you penance. This I say to the bad, and yet Christians; this I say to the tares. For they are in the field: and it can come about that those who are tares today, tomorrow may be wheat. Therefore I address the wheat too.

**4.** O you Christians who live well, few among many, you sigh, few among very many you groan. The winter will pass, the summer will come, behold the harvest will be at hand. The angels will come, who can separate and do not know how to err. We at this time are like those servants, of whom it was said: *Do you wish that we go and gather them?*[^10] For we would wish, if it could be done, that no bad man remain among the good. But it was said to us: *Let both grow until the harvest*[^11]. Why? For you are such that you can be deceived. Finally hear: *Lest perhaps, while you wish to root out the tares, you root out together also the wheat*[^12]. What good do you do? Will you not by your diligence lay waste my harvest? The reapers will come: and he expounded who the reapers are: *But the reapers are the angels*[^13]. We are men, the angels are reapers. We too indeed will be, if we complete the course, equal to the angels of God: but now, when we are vexed against the bad, we are still men. And we now ought to hear: *Wherefore let him who thinks he stands beware lest he fall*[^14]. For do you think, my brothers, that these tares do not climb up to the apses? Do you think that they are below, and not above? Would that we not be such. *But to me it is a very small thing to be judged by you*[^15]. I say plainly to your love, both in the apses there is wheat, there are tares; and among the peoples there is wheat, there are tares. Let the good bear with the bad: let the bad be changed, and imitate the good. Let us all, if it can be done, belong to God: let us all escape the malice of this world in his mercy. Let us seek good days[^16], because we are in evil days: but in the evil days let us not blaspheme, that we may be able to reach the good days.
