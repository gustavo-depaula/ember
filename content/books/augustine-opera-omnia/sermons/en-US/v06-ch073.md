# Sermon 229/D: On Holy Easter

**1.** You ought always, brothers, to remember that Christ *was handed over for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^1]; but especially in these days, admonished by so great a grace, on which we are not permitted to forget, by the yearly festival, this very thing that was done once for all. Informed by faith, made firm by hope, set aflame by love, let us solemnly observe what is temporal, and without ceasing long for what is eternal. For if God *did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all, how has he not also given us all things with him?*[^2] Christ suffered: let us die to sin; Christ rose: let us live to God. Christ passes from this world to the Father[^3]; let our heart not cling here, but follow to the things on high. Our Head hung upon the wood; let us crucify the concupiscence of the flesh. He lay in the tomb; buried with him, let us forget the things that are past. He sits in heaven; let us transfer our longing to the things above. He is to come as judge; let us not draw the yoke together with unbelievers. He is to raise up even the corpses of the dead; let us, by changing our mind, do something for our body that is to be changed for the better. He is to set the wicked on the left, the good on the right; let us choose our place by our works. Of his kingdom there will be no end; let us not in the least fear the end of this life. The whole *training of our peace is in him, by whose bruises we have been healed*[^4].

**2.** Therefore, dearly beloved, let the most persevering meditation upon all these things be for us a daily celebration of Easter. For we ought not to hold these days so special that we neglect the memory of the Lord's passion and resurrection, since we have his body and blood as our daily feast: yet this festival recalls them more clearly, stirs them up more fervently, renews them more joyfully; since by the yearly cycle of the seasons it represents, as it were to our very sight, the remembrance of what was done. Keep this festival, then, as one that will pass, and ever think of the coming kingdom that will abide. For if days that pass away, on which we recall with devout solemnity the passion and resurrection of Christ, so gladden us, how will the eternal day make us blessed, where we shall see him and abide with him whom now we rejoice in by longing and hoping? How great an exultation will he give to his Church, from which, regenerated through Christ, he takes away in a manner the foreskin of carnal nature, that is, the reproach of birth? Hence it is said: *And you, when you were dead in your offenses and in the foreskin of your flesh, he made alive together with him, forgiving us all our debts*[^5]. *For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*[^6]. Therefore in the baptism of Christ is revealed what was covered in the shadow of the old circumcision; and this very thing pertains nonetheless to that same circumcision not made by hand, when the covering of carnal ignorance is taken away. *When you pass over,* he says, *to Christ, the veil is taken away*[^7].

[^1]: Rom 4:25
[^2]: Rom 8:32
[^3]: See John 13:1
[^4]: Isa 53:5
[^5]: Col 2:13
[^6]: 1 Cor 15:22
[^7]: See 2 Cor 3:16
