# Sermon 265/F: On the Day of the Fortieth of the Ascension

**1.** Today, beloved, as you know, we celebrate the solemnity of the Ascension of the Lord. For he ascended, as you heard, to his Father and to our Father, to his God and to our God. From where did we merit the brotherhood of Christ? In no way would we attend to his brotherhood, if he had not taken up our weakness. For therefore we are brothers, because he is man. Therefore he who was Lord condescended to be a brother; Lord always, brother from a certain time; Lord from the form of God, brother from the form of a servant. *For since he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal to God*[^1]: there Lord. From where, then, brother? *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^2]. If he were only a brother, it would be much. And he took the form of a servant, condescended to be a servant. Is he ours, or not? He is ours too. For he himself said of himself: *I came not to be ministered to, but to minister*[^3]. Of him the prophet foretold that he would justify the just one, *serving many well*[^4]. But let us not exalt ourselves. Very often a master too serves sick servants, that he may have healthy servants to serve him: that they may be healed, he serves the sick. Our Lord serves the sick. Did he not from his weakness prepare medicines for the sick? For the sick he poured out his blood, with the salve of his blood he anointed the eyes of the blind.

**2.** He became for us, then, by condescension a brother, who by nature is Lord. He said: *I will ascend to my God and your God, to my Father and your Father*[^5]. To whom did he command this to be said? *Go*, he says, *tell my brothers*, and because I am a brother, *I will ascend to my Father and your Father, my God and your God.* He did not say: I ascend to our Father; nor did he say: I ascend to our God. It is not idle, *my Father and your Father, my God and your God.* This distinction indicates something I ought not to be silent about. *My Father*, because I am the only one; *your Father*, because you are adopted through me. *My God*, from where? Christ is not a creature. But according to that he is the Only-begotten. From where, then: *my God?* The Psalm indicates it. From there, my God, because I took the form of a servant: *From my mother's womb you are my God*[^6]. If you remove the mother's womb where he was made man, he is not his God, but his Father. His Father, then, always, the eternal Father of the eternal Son. But that he might be his God, the mother's womb was added, and it was said in the prophet: *You are my God.* But not so as ours. For he is our God, because he made sinners whole! But he is his God because he was made a man without sin. Therefore, when *he had come to God*, he distinguished: *my God and your God. My God*, how? Because I am a man. Why, then, not at the same time ours, since you too are a man, and we are men? But one thing is a man without sin, who came to blot out sins; and another is a man with sin, to whom it was come that he might be freed from sin. This, then, is a distinction, not a separation. We have a Father in heaven, but Christ otherwise, because he without sin, the only one, adopted us. We have a God in heaven, but Christ otherwise, because he without sin, we sinners.

**3.** Yet that is the condescension which the Apostle speaks of: *Heirs indeed of God, but coheirs of Christ*[^7]. We find a Father in heaven, we belong to a great family. The Son came to us from there that he might become a brother. He did not leave the Father when he came to us, nor returning to the Father did he leave us. Let us believe Christ to be in heaven, let us believe him to be with us. How in heaven, if with us? How as God. My word both is with me and is with you; and it is with me in my heart, and is with you in your ears. If this my word can do it, could not the Word of God? He came down indeed, when he was here. What, then, is to come down? For Christ Jesus to appear. How did Jesus appear? Because he was made man. What, then, is to ascend? For the body of Christ to be lifted into heaven, not for the majesty to migrate. To where he ascended, from there he is to come down; and as he ascended, so he is to come down. It is the voice of the angels, not ours. For the disciples stood, and escorted him ascending by seeing. And it was said to them: *Men of Galilee, why do you stand? This Jesus will so come, as you see him going into heaven*[^8]. What is: *he will so come?* in that very form he is to judge, in which he was judged. Visible to none but the just, visible also to the unjust, he will come to be seen by the just and the unjust. The unjust will be able to see him, they will not be able to reign with him.

**4.** Let us celebrate, then, the holy day of the Fortieth; for this day the whole world celebrates with us. And the Fiftieth the Church spread through the whole world celebrates with us; the celebration, then, of the twenty and the thirty is a custom of Africa, not a sacred sign of the Church.

[^1]: Phil 2:6
[^2]: Phil 2:7
[^3]: Matt 20:28
[^4]: See Isa 53:11
[^5]: John 20:17
[^6]: Ps 22:10 (21:11)
[^7]: Rom 8:17
[^8]: Acts 1:11
