# Sermon 21

**1** *The just man will rejoice in the Lord and will hope in him, and all the upright in heart will glory*[^1]. This surely we have sung with voice and heart. These words a Christian conscience and tongue have spoken to God: *The just man will rejoice*[^2], not in the world, but *in the Lord. Light has dawned for the just*, it says elsewhere, *and gladness for the upright in heart*[^3]. You would ask whence the gladness. Here you hear: *The just man will rejoice in the Lord*[^4]. And elsewhere: *Delight in the Lord, and he will give you the petitions of your heart*[^5]. What is enjoined upon us? What is given? What is commanded? What is granted? That we should rejoice in the Lord. Who will rejoice in a thing he does not see? Or do we perhaps see the Lord? This we hold in promise. But now *we walk by faith; as long as we are in the body, we are away from the Lord*[^6]. By faith, not by sight. When by sight? When what the same John says is fulfilled: *Beloved, we are children of God, and it does not yet appear what we shall be. We know that when he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^7]. Then, therefore, there will be great and perfect gladness, then full joy, where hope no longer suckles us, but the reality nourishes us. Yet even now, before the reality itself comes to us, before we come to the reality itself, let us rejoice in the Lord. For hope, whose reality will come afterward, holds no small gladness. And in these temporal things too—in the gladness not of the Lord but of the world—many love various things and have not yet attained the things they love. And yet their ardor runs on in hope; it does not yet hold the reality. For example, you love money; you would not love it if you did not hope. You love a wife not yet wed but still to be wed, and perhaps when she is to be wed she is loved, but once wed she will be held in hatred. Why is this? Because when wed she did not appear such as the mind had painted her when she was to be wed. But God does not grow cheap when present, and is loved when absent. For however much the human mind heaps up the good that God is, it falls short and is very far below, and necessarily the attainment finds more than the thought formed. We shall therefore love more when we see, if we were able to love even before we saw. Now, then, we love in hope. So, it says, *the just man rejoices in the Lord*[^8]. And at once, because he does not yet see, *and he will hope in him*[^9].

**2** Yet we have the firstfruits of the Spirit, and perhaps from another source we draw near to him whom we love, and what we are eagerly to eat and drink we now lick and taste beforehand, even if thinly. Whence do we prove this? For God, whom we are commanded to love, in whom we are commanded to rejoice, is not gold, nor silver, nor earth, nor heaven, nor this light of the sun, nor whatever shines from heaven, nor whatever gleams from the earth flooded with light. He is no corporeal thing. *God is spirit*[^10]. Therefore, he says, *those who worship must worship in spirit and in truth*[^11]. Not in any place of the body, because he is not a body; not as on a high mountain, that by the mountain's height you should think you draw near to God. The *Lord* is indeed *exalted, but he regards the lowly; the lofty, however, he knows from afar*[^12], the lowly not from afar. Surely he is exalted, and if indeed he knows the lofty from afar, he ought to perceive the lowly from farther still. "If by his exaltation he is far from the lofty, so as to know them from afar, how much more," someone says, "has his exaltation withdrawn far from the lowly." It is not so. For the *Lord* is *exalted, and he regards the lowly*[^13]. How does he regard them? *The Lord is near to those who have crushed their heart*[^14]. Do not, then, seek a high mountain from which you may seem to be nearer to God. If you exalt yourself, he withdraws far from you; if you humble yourself, he bends down to you. The publican stood at a distance, and therefore God drew nearer to him more easily; he did not dare lift his eyes to heaven, and already he had with him the one who had made heaven. Whence, then, do we rejoice in the Lord, if the Lord is so far from us? Let him not be far! That he is far, you bring about. Love, and he will draw near; love, and he will dwell with you. *The Lord is at hand; have no anxiety about anything*[^15]. Do you want to see that if you love he is with you? *God is love*[^16]. Why do the phantasms of your thought flit far and wide, and you say to yourself: "What do you suppose God is? What do you suppose God is like?" Whatever you imagine, it is not he; whatever you grasp by thought, it is not he. For if it were he, it could not be grasped by thought. But that you may receive some taste, *God is love*[^17]. You will say to me: "What do you suppose love is?" Love is that by which we love. What do we love? An ineffable good, a beneficent good, the good Creator of all good things. Let him delight you, from whom you have whatever delights you. I do not mean sin, for sin alone you do not have from him. Apart from sin, you have from him whatever else you have.

**3** Therefore, what I said—"Let him delight you, from whom you have whatever delights you"—do not understand as sin and say: "See, sin delights me; do I have sin from God?" See first whether perhaps it is not the sin that delights you, but something else delights you in which you commit the sin. By loving the creation inordinately, then, contrary to honorable use, contrary to what is permitted, contrary to the law and will of the Creator himself, by loving the creation you sin. For you do not love the sin itself; but by loving badly what you love, you are ensnared by sin. You go after the bait in the net, and unknowingly you feed on sin. Then you defend yourself thus: "If it is a sin to drink much, why did God establish wine? If it is a sin to love gold—I am a lover of gold, not a creator; the creator of gold is God—why did he create what it is evil to love?" So with the other things you love badly, in which is all extravagance, where all disgraceful acts are committed. Attend, examine, consider, and see that *every creature of God is good*[^18]. And there sin is not, except that you use it badly. Hear this, then, O man. You say: "Why did God establish what he forbids me to love? Let him not have established it, and there would be nothing for me to love. Let him not have established the creation he commands me not to love, and there would be nothing for me to love, and by loving I would be condemned." If the creature itself could have a voice—the creature you love badly because you do not even love yourself—it would answer you: "Would you not want God to make me, lest there be something for you to love? Would you not want him to make you either, lest there be one who loves?" Now, then, see how unjust you are, and in your very words you are caught most unjust. You would want God to make you, who is above you, and you would not want him to make some good thing beneath you. Whatever God made is good. But some are great goods, others small goods, all of them goods nonetheless. Some are heavenly goods, others earthly goods. Some spiritual goods, others bodily goods. Some everlasting goods, others temporal goods. All of them goods nonetheless, because the good one made good things. This is why in a certain place of the divine Scriptures it is said: *Set love in order within me*[^19]. God made you some good thing beneath himself; he made something lower and beneath you. You are beneath one thing, above another. Do not, leaving the higher good, bend yourself down to the lower good. Be upright, that you may be praised, because *all the upright in heart will be praised*[^20]. For whence do you sin, except that you handle inordinately the things you have received for use? Be one who uses lower things well, and you will be one who rightly enjoys the higher good.

**4** Now hear and examine your own recognitions, and question yourself who do the handling, and the things you handle. See, if in your business you set silver before gold, lead before silver, dust before lead, will you not, if perhaps you are a merchant, be judged utterly mad by all your associates, and shut out from their company, and called ruinous, and perhaps even to be cured by having your head treated? For what else will all your associates say when you say, "Silver is dearer than gold," or "Silver is better than gold"? Will they not cry out this: "You are insane, you are out of your senses, what is wrong with you?" Everyone in your house cries out, "What is wrong with you?" when you set silver before gold. And no one says to you, "What is wrong with you?" when you set gold before God. "How," you say, "do I set gold before God? For if through some madness I set silver before gold, I am called mad because, of two kinds of thing, both of which I see, both of which I behold, both of which I handle with my hand, I set the cheaper before the dearer. How do I set gold before God? Gold I see, God I do not see." Nor will you excuse yourself on this ground. Why do you love silver? Because it is dear, because it is worth dearly. Why gold the more? Because it is dearer. Silver is dear, gold dearer. *God* is *love itself*[^21].

**5** See, I will name something of God's gift, that I may convince you more surely how you set gold before God, even though you see gold and do not see God; and therefore you do not seem to yourself to be setting it before him, since, as it were, no one sets a thing he sees before a thing he does not see. See, I name something. What do you think? Is faith silver? Is it gold? Is it coin? Is it cattle? Is it land? Is it heaven? It is none of these, and yet it is something. Not only something, but even something great. For now I am not speaking of that higher faith by which you are called faithful, approaching the table of the Lord your God, answering out of faith to the words of faith. For now, if I set this aside a little, let me speak of that faith which is also commonly called faith—not the great faith your Lord commands of you, but the faith you demand from your servant. I mean that very faith, because your Lord commands this too of you, that you defraud no one, that you keep faith in business, that you keep faith with your wife in bed. This faith too your Lord commands of you. What is this faith? Surely you do not see it. If you do not see it, why, when it is broken for you, do you cry out? By your own cry I convince you that you see it. You were saying, "How do I set gold before God? Gold I see, God I do not see." See, you see gold, you do not see faith. Or, what is truer, you do see faith. But when you demand it, you see it; when it is demanded of you, you refuse to see it? With the eyes of the heart open you cry out, "Return the faith you promised." With the eyes of the heart shut you cry out, "I promised you nothing." In both cases open your eyes. Unjust man, destroy not faith, but injustice itself. Return what you demand.

**6** You bring a servant to the church to be set free by your hand. Silence falls. Your document is read out, or the carrying out of your wish takes place. You say that you are freeing the servant because he has kept faith with you in all things. This you love, this you honor, this you reward with the prize of liberty. Whatever you can do, you do. You make him free, because you cannot make him eternal. Your God cries out to you, and in your servant he convicts you. He says to you in your heart: "You brought your servant from your house to my house. You want to call him back from my house, free, into your house. Why do you yourself serve badly in my house? You give him what you can. I promise you what I can. You make free the one who keeps faith. I make you eternal, if you keep faith with me. Why do you still argue against me in your mind? Render to your Lord what you praise in your servant. Or do you perhaps claim so much for yourself that you think yourself worthy to have a faithful servant, of whom you say, 'I bought him,' and that I am not worthy to have a faithful servant whom I created?" These things the Lord your God speaks to you within, where no one hears but you, and he himself speaks there, who speaks true things. For what is truer than this saying? Do not grow deaf. See, you love faith in your servant. Surely you do not see faith. Why do you love it in another—and all that I have said, in another, and in a servant whom you bought with money, yet did not create? With a twofold claim your Lord deals with you. "I both created you and bought you. Before you were," he says to you, "I made you. When you had been sold under sin from yourself, I redeemed you." That you may free your servant, you break his deed of bondage. God will not break your deed. Your deed is the Gospel, where there is the blood by which you were bought. It remains, it is read daily, you are reminded of your condition, your price is recalled to you.

**7** If your servant whom you are freeing did not show you faith, and did not make himself worthy of your manumission by keeping faith, and you found him guilty of some frauds in your house, what would you cry out? "Bad servant, do you not keep faith with me? Do you not know that I bought you? Do you not know that I counted out my blood for you?" You cry out as much as you can, and you strike heaven with your indignant cries: "I gave my blood for you, bad servant." And all who hear say: "He speaks the truth." If your servant himself dared to answer you when you inveigh and cry out so, would you not blush if he said to you: "What blood, I ask you, did you give for me? When you bought me, you did not even let your own blood." But you call your money your blood. So much do you love your money that you call it your blood. From your own words your Lord convicts you. You call your money your blood, and therefore you demand faith from the servant you bought, because for him you gave not blood, to be sure, but coin, or gold. What I gave, you recall. If you do not recall it, I read your deed. If you do not recall it, read—read for yourself the death of the Savior, the spear of the one who struck him, the price of the Redeemer. A living man too can, as I said, when his vein is opened, give his blood and yet live. Greater is what your Lord says to you: "My blood was not pressed out of me while I lived. With my blood I bought you. I add, with my death I bought you." What have you to say? Render to your Lord the faith you demand from your servant. You see gold, you also see faith. You would not demand it if you did not see it; you would not praise it if you did not see it; you would not reward it with liberty if you did not see it. But gold you see with the eyes of the flesh, faith you see with the eyes of the heart. The better the eyes of the heart are, the better is what you see with the eyes of the heart. But you set gold before this faith which your Lord commands of you. And what was entrusted you do not return, and you say, "You gave me nothing." Or to one to whom you entrusted nothing you say, "Return what I entrusted to you." You do not give what you received, you demand back what you did not give. See, gain. Take it thus, and heap up against yourself the mire by which you are weighed down, by saying "Give" what you did not entrust, by denying what you received in trust. Take, gather ruinous profits. See, you have filled your chest, you have gained much gold. Shake out the chest of your heart, you have lost faith.

**8** Return, then, if you have felt something, if you have blushed, if you have corrected what was crooked and bent—return, *delight in the Lord*[^22], rejoice in the Lord. Rejoice in the things the Lord has commanded, that you may rejoice in the Lord. Rejoice in faith, rejoice in hope, rejoice in love, rejoice in mercy, rejoice in hospitality, rejoice in chastity. All these things are goods, inner treasures, jewels, not of your chest but of your conscience. Love to be rich in these, riches you cannot lose even by shipwreck, so that if you go out naked, you go out full. For so you will also be upright in heart, so that you may be praised for not blaming your Lord, if anything adverse befalls you in this world, and may praise the scourge of the Father whose inheritance you await. Flee under the hand of the one who corrects you. Do not turn away from discipline, because he who corrects cannot err. He who made you knows what to do with you. Or do you perhaps think your craftsman so unskilled that he would know how to make you and forget what to do with you? Before you existed, he thought of you; for unless he had thought of you before you existed, you would not exist. And now that you exist, abide, live, and serve him, will he despise you, will he look down on you? "He has looked down on me," you say, "for I prayed and he did not hear me." What if there you were asking for what, had you received it, you would have received to your harm? "I wept before him and he did not give it to me." O foolish child, for what did you weep? To receive carnal happiness, temporal happiness, earthly happiness. What if that happiness, which you longed for and asked for and wept for, would have cast you down? Just now I was speaking of your servant; now take a comparison from your son. Your little son weeps before you to be lifted onto a horse. Do you hear him? Do you grant it? Are you hard, or rather merciful? What is it, tell me, by what counsel do you act? Surely this is the counsel of love, who can doubt it? You who keep your whole house for him when grown do not lift the little weeping one onto a single horse. All that you have, both house and whatever is in the house, and field and whatever is in the field, you keep for him. And yet you do not lift the little weeping one onto a horse. Let him weep as much as he wants, let him weep all day, you do not hear him, and out of mercy you do not hear him, and if you heard him you would be cruel. See, then, and consider whether perhaps your Lord does this to you, when you ask for unsuitable things and do not receive them. For perhaps want would instruct you, abundance corrupt you. You seek the abundance of corruption, when perhaps the want of instruction is necessary for you. Leave it to your God, who knows what to give you, what to take from you. For if he gives you what you ask badly, perhaps he gives it in anger. Hear examples from the Law. The Israelites craving the cravings of belly and throat he heard in anger; Paul saying, "Take from me the thorn of the flesh," he did not hear, in mercy.

**9** Therefore *delight in the Lord*[^23], rejoice in the Lord, not in the world. For that man rejoiced in the Lord who, when he had lost all the gladness of the world, still had the Lord remaining in whom he might rejoice. There remained to him a wondrous, simple, perfect, unchangeable gladness of his heart. The things he had he possessed, he was not possessed by them, but he was possessed by the Lord. Those things he trod underfoot, on the Lord he depended. When those things he trod underfoot were taken away, he clung to him on whom he depended. See what it is to rejoice in the Lord. *The Lord gave*—see the gladness—*the Lord has taken away*[^24]. Did he take himself away? What he gave he took away; he who gave offered himself. He rejoices, then, in the Lord: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^25]. Why should it displease the servant what pleased the Lord? "Gold," he says, "I have lost; my household I have lost, my cattle I have lost, whatever I had I have lost. The one by whom I am possessed I have not lost. I have lost what was with me, I have not lost the one whose I myself am. He is my delight, he is my riches." But why? Because he was not perverse, not upside down, he did not neglect the one who is above him and love the things below him. For this is perversity: to use the creation badly.

**10** Why do you accuse the one who gave gold, you who are rightly accused of loving gold badly? Have gold, God says to you, I gave it to you, use it well. Do you want to be adorned with gold? Rather adorn the gold; you want honor, you want glory from gold—give glory to the gold, lest you be the disgrace of gold. He has gold: he whores, he fornicates, he lives in luxury, he puts on pompous shows, he gives mad gifts to actors, he gives nothing to the hungry poor—he is no glory to the gold. Does not one who attends rightly say this: "I grieve for the gold that fell to such a man"? And you, if you had gold? For now you say: "I grieve for the gold that fell to such a man. Oh, if I had it!" What would you do? "I would take in pilgrims, feed the needy, clothe the naked, ransom captives." You speak good things before you have it; how will you speak when you have had it? If you are such, gold will be among your adornments. If you truly so use gold, because you love more the one who created gold, you will be upright, loving the higher things more, rightly using the lower. And you will delight in the Lord, you will rejoice as a just man in the Lord. There will be in you no accusation of the Creator; there will be thanksgiving to the Redeemer. Amen.
