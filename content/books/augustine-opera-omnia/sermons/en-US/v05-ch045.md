# Sermon 180

**1. 1.** The first reading which was recited to us today, of the apostle James, has been offered to us for discussion, and in a way imposed. For it made you attentive, admonishing before all things that you not swear. It is a difficult question. Whom would this sin not hold guilty, if to swear is a sin? For that perjury is a sin, and a great sin, let no one doubt. But the Apostle, of whose reading we treat, does not say: *Before all things, my brothers*, do not perjure; but: *do not swear*[^1]. A similar admonition of the Lord Jesus Christ himself in the Gospel went before: *You have heard*, he says, *that it was said to the ancients: You shall not perjure. But I say to you: do not swear, neither by heaven, for it is the throne of God; nor by earth, for it is the footstool of his feet; nor shall you swear by your head, for you have no power to make one hair white or black. But let your speech be: Yes, yes; No, no. If anything is more than this, it is from evil*[^2]. To this dominical admonition the mentioned reading of the Apostle so wholly agrees that it seems God commanded nothing else; because no other said this than the one who said through the Apostle: *Before all things*, he says, *my brothers, do not swear, neither by heaven, nor by earth, nor by any other oath. But let your speech be: Yes, yes; No, no*. Except that this one added: *Before all things*; whence he made you very attentive, and increased the difficulty of the question.

**2. 2.** For we find that the saints swore, that first the Lord himself swore, in whom there is no sin at all. *The Lord swore, and will not repent: You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek*[^3]. He promised the Son the eternity of priesthood with an oath. You have too: *By myself I swear, says the Lord*[^4]. And that is an oath: *As I live, says the Lord*[^5]. As a man swears by God, so God by himself. Is it, then, not a sin to swear? It is hard to say this; and since we said that God swore, how blasphemous it is to say this! God swears, who has no sin; therefore it is not a sin to swear; but rather it is a sin to perjure. Perhaps someone says that the example of swearing should not be set forth concerning the Lord God. For he is God, and perhaps it befits him alone to swear, who cannot perjure. For men swear falsely either when they deceive, or when they are deceived. For either a man thinks true what is false, and swears rashly; or he knows or thinks it false, and yet swears it for true, and swears no less wickedly. But these perjuries, which I have mentioned as two, differ. Suppose one swears who thinks true that for which he swears; he thinks it true, and yet it is false. This man does not perjure from the soul; he is deceived, he holds for true what is false; he does not knowingly interpose the oath for a false thing. Take another who knows it is false, and says it is true; and swears as if it were true what he knows to be false. You see how detestable this beast is, and to be exterminated from human affairs? For who would wish this to be done? All men detest such things. Take another, he thinks it false, and swears as if it were true, and perhaps it is true. For example, that you may understand: Did it rain in that place? you question a man; and he thinks it did not rain, and it suits his business that he say: It rained. But he thinks it did not rain. It is said to him: Truly did it rain? Truly, and he swears; and yet it rained there, but he does not know it, and thinks it did not rain; he is a perjurer. It matters how the word proceeds from the soul. A guilty mind alone makes the tongue guilty. But who is there who is not deceived, even if he was unwilling to deceive? Who is the man upon whom deception does not creep? And yet the oath does not depart from the mouth, it is frequent; oaths are very often more than the words. If a man examines how often he swears throughout the whole day, how often he wounds himself, how often he strikes and transfixes himself with the sword of the tongue, what place is found in him sound? Because, then, it is a grave sin to perjure, Scripture gave you a shortcut: Do not swear.

**3. 3.** What shall I tell you, O man? Swear truly? Behold, swear truly, you do not sin; if you swear truly, you do not sin. But a man set among temptations, wrapped in flesh, treading the earth under the earth, while the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind thinking many things[^6]; amid these your many uncertain thoughts, flighty things, human conjectures, human deceptions, when does there not creep upon you what is false, set in the region of falsehood? Do you wish, then, to be far from perjury? Do not swear. For he who swears can sometimes swear truly; but he who does not swear can never swear a lie. Let God swear, then, who swears securely, whom nothing deceives, from whom nothing is hidden, who wholly knows not how to deceive, because he cannot be deceived. For when he swears, he produces himself as witness. As you, when you swear, produce God as witness; so he, when he swears, produces himself as witness. You, when you produce him as witness, perhaps over your lie, take in vain the name of the Lord your God[^7]. Lest, then, you swear a lie, do not swear. This is the strait. Perjury is the precipice. He who swears is near it; he who does not swear is far from it. He sins, and gravely, who swears falsely; he does not sin who swears truly; but neither does he sin who does not swear at all. But he who does not swear, and does not sin, is far from sin; but he who swears truly does not sin, but is near sin. Suppose you to walk in some place where on the right hand the land is spacious, nor do you anywhere suffer straits; on the left there is a precipitous place. Where do you choose to walk? On the edge of the land at the precipitous brink, or far from it? I think far from it. So too he who swears walks on the edge; and walks with infirm feet, because human. If you stumble, you go down; if you slip, you go down. And what receives you? The punishment of perjury. Therefore you wished to swear truly; hear the counsel of God: Do not swear.

**4. 4.** If swearing were a sin, it would not be said even in the old law: *You shall not perjure; but you shall render to the Lord your oath*[^8]. For a sin would not be commanded to us. But your God says to you: If you swear, I will not condemn you; if you swear truly, I will not condemn you. But surely if you do not swear will I condemn you? There are two things, he says, that I never condemn: a true oath and no oath; but I condemn a false oath. A false oath is destructive, a true oath is dangerous, no oath is secure. I know it is a difficult question, and I confess to your Charity that I always avoided it. But now, when on the Lord's day of the sermon owed, the same reading was recited, I believed it was divinely inspired to me that I should treat of it. From here God wished me to speak; from here you to hear. I beseech you not to despise it, I beseech you to make your heart firm, to change the mobilities of your tongue. It is not wholly without cause, it is not idle, that, since I always wished, as I said, to avoid that question, it has been imposed on my necessity, that it may be imposed on your Charity too.

**5. 5.** That you may know that to swear truly is not a sin, we find that the apostle Paul too swore: *I die daily, by your glory, brothers, which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord*. *By your glory* is an oath. He does not say it as though: *By your glory I die*, as though your glory makes me die; as if he said: He died by poison, he died by the sword, he died by a beast, he died by an enemy; that is, the enemy doing it, the sword doing it, the poison doing it, and the like; he did not say it so: *By your glory*. The Greek text resolves the ambiguity. It is examined in the Greek Epistle, and there is found an oath which is not ambiguous: Nē tēn hymeteran kauchēsin; where a Greek says this, he swears. You hear Greeks daily, and you who know Greek: Nē ton Theon; when one says Nē ton Theon, it is an oath: By God. Therefore let no one doubt that the Apostle swore, when he said: *By your glory, brothers* (and lest we suppose he swore by human glory) *which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord*[^9]. There is in another place an oath wholly certain and express: *I call God as witness upon my soul*. The Apostle says: *I call God as witness upon my soul, that, sparing you, I have not yet come to Corinth*[^10]. And in another place, to the Galatians: *But what I write to you, behold, before God, that I do not lie*[^11].

**6. 6.** Attend, I beg, and notice; and even if the sermon is not so pleasing to you, on account of the straits of the question, yet it is useful, if it reaches your inmost parts. Behold, the Apostle swore. Let those not deceive you who, I know not how, wishing to distinguish—or rather, not to understand—oaths, say it is not an oath, when a man says: God knows, God is witness, I call God upon my soul that I speak the truth. He called upon God, they say, made God a witness; did he swear? Those who say this wish nothing else but, with God called as witness, to lie. Is it really so, whoever you are of a depraved heart and a perverse heart, that if you say: By God, you swear; but if you say: God is witness, you do not swear? For what is, By God, but, God is witness? Or what is, God is witness, but, By God?

**6. 7.** But what is it to swear, but to render right ("ius") to God, when you swear by God; to render right to your salvation, when you swear by your salvation; to render right to your children, when you swear by your children? But what right do we owe to our salvation, to our children, to our God, but of love, of truth, and not of falsehood? But most of all when it is done by God, that is the true oath; because even when anyone says: By my salvation, he obligates his salvation to God; when he says: By my children, he pledges his children to God, that this come upon their head, which goes out of his mouth; if true, true; if false, false. When, therefore, anyone, naming his children, or his head, or his salvation in an oath, obligates to God whatever he names; how much more when he perjures by God himself? For he fears to swear falsely by his son, and does not fear to swear falsely by his God? Perhaps saying this in his mind: I fear to swear falsely by my son, lest he die; but to God, who does not die, even if a false oath be sworn by him, what evil happens? You say well indeed, no evil happens to God, when you swear falsely by God; but much evil happens to you, who deceive a neighbor to whom you produce God as witness. If you did something with your son as witness, and said to a friend or your neighbor or any man whatever: I did not do it, and you touched your son's head, by whose witness you did it, and said: By his salvation, I did not do it; your son, trembling under your paternal hand, yet trembling not at your paternal hand, but at the divine, would perhaps cry out: Do not, father, let my salvation not be cheap to you; you called God upon me, I saw you, you did it, do not perjure; I have you indeed as my begetter, but I fear more both your Creator and mine.

**7. 8.** But because God, when you swear by him, does not say to you: I saw you, do not swear, you did it; but you fear lest that one kill you, you kill yourself first; because, then, he does not say: I saw you, do you suppose he did not see? And where is what he says: *I was silent, I was silent; shall I always be silent*[^12]? And yet very often he does say: I saw you; but in another way, when he takes vengeance on the perjurer. But he does not take vengeance on all; therefore men are built up by the example. I know, that man swore falsely to me, and he lives. That man swore falsely to you, and he lives? He swore falsely, and he lives; that man swore falsely. You are deceived. If you had eyes whence you could see his death, if you too in that which is to die and not to die were not deceived, you would see his death. And now attend to Scripture; and there you will find him lying dead, whom you think living. Because he walks with his feet, because he handles with his hands, because he sees with his eyes, and hears with his ears, and uses well enough the other offices of the members, you think him living. He lives, but his body; but his soul is dead, what is better in him is dead. The dwelling lives, the dweller is dead. How, you will say, when the body lives, is the soul dead; when the body would not live, unless given life by the soul? How, then, is the soul dead, by which the body lives? Hear, then, and learn: the body of man is a creation of God, and the soul of man is a creation of God. From the soul God gives life to the flesh; that same soul he gives life to from himself, not from itself. The life, then, of the body is the soul; the life, then, of the soul is God. The body dies when the soul withdraws; the soul dies, then, if God withdraws. The soul withdraws when the body is struck with the sword; and do you suppose that God does not withdraw when the soul itself is struck with perjury? Do you wish to see that the one of whom you speak is dead? Read Scripture: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^13]. But you think God a present avenger, if the one who deceived you by a false oath at once breathes his last. If he breathes his last before your eyes, his flesh has breathed its last. What is, his flesh has breathed its last? It has cast out the spirit by which it was given life. This is, it breathed its last, the spirit being shut out by which the flesh lived. He perjured, he shut out the spirit by which the soul lived. He breathed his last, but you do not know it; he breathed his last, but you do not see it. For you see the flesh lying without the soul; the wretched soul without God you cannot see. Believe, then, apply the eyes of faith. No perjurer is unpunished; absolutely none; with him is his own punishment. If he had in his chamber a torturer of the flesh, he would be punished; he has in the secret of his heart the torturer of his conscience, and is he called unpunished? And yet what do you say? He lives, rejoices, lives luxuriously who swore me a lie; why do you send me to invisible things? Because God himself, by whom he swore, is invisible. He swore by the invisible, he is struck by an invisible punishment. But he lives, he says, and in a way teems and bubbles with luxuries. If this is so, that he teems with luxuries, that he bubbles with luxuries, they are the worms of a dead soul. Finally every prudent man, who attends to such luxuriating perjurers, by the sound smell of his heart turns himself away, does not wish to see, does not wish to hear. Whence does this soundness turn itself away, unless because it thinks the soul dead?

**8. 9.** Briefly, then, hear, my brothers, I will conclude the sermon, fixing in your hearts a wholesome care: *Before all things do not swear*[^14]. Why, *Before all things*? If it is a great crime to perjure, but there is no fault in swearing truly, why *Before all things do not swear*? For he ought to have said: Before all things do not perjure. *Before all things*, he says, *do not swear*. Is swearing, then, worse than stealing? Is swearing worse than committing adultery? I do not say swearing falsely; I say swearing: is swearing worse than killing a man? Far from it. To kill a man, to commit adultery, to steal, is a sin; to swear is not a sin; but to swear falsely is a sin. Why, then, *Before all things*? By this word which he said, *Before all things*, he made us cautious against our tongue. *Before all things*, he says, that you attend before the rest, that you keep watch lest the habit of swearing creep upon you. As in a mirror he so set you against yourself: *Before all things*, he lifted you above the rest, whence you may attend to yourself. For he considers you swearing: By God, by Christ, I kill him; and these, how often a day, how often an hour? You do not open your mouth except for such an oath. Would you not wish him to say to you: *Before all things*, that he might make you most attentive against the habit, that you might inspect all your doings, most diligently guard all the movements of your tongue, be a guard of your evil habit, to restrain it? Hear: *Before all things*. You were sleeping, I prick, *before all things*, I apply thorns. What is, *Before all things*? Before all things keep watch, before all things be attentive.

**9. 10.** We too swore here and there, we had that most foul and deadly habit. I tell your Charity: from the time we began to serve God, and saw how great an evil is in perjury, we feared vehemently, and reined in by fear that most inveterate habit. Reined in, it is restrained; restrained, it grows weak; growing weak, it dies, and a good habit succeeds the evil one. Finally we do not say to you that we do not swear. For if we say this, we lie. As far as concerns me, I swear; but, as it seems to me, compelled by great necessity. When I see that I am not believed unless I do it, and that it does not profit the one who does not believe me that he does not believe it, with this reason weighed and consideration balanced, with great fear I say: Before God; or: God is witness; or: Christ knows it is so in my mind; and I see that it is "more"—that is, that it is beyond *Yes, yes; No, no*; but *what is more is from evil*; and if not from the evil of the one swearing, it is from the evil of the one not believing. Finally he did not say: If one does more, he is evil; and: Let there be in your mouth: Yes, yes; No, no; if anyone does more, he is evil; but: *Let there be in your mouth: Yes, yes; No, no; but what is more is from evil*[^15]. But seek whose. Yet the worst human habit has another thing. Both when you are believed, you swear; and when no one demands it, you swear; and to people who shudder, you swear; you are not silent by swearing, you are scarcely sound by not perjuring. Unless perhaps you think, brothers, that if the apostle Paul knew that the Galatians believed him, he would add an oath and say: *But what I write to you, behold, before God, that I do not lie*[^16]. He saw there those who believed; he saw also others who did not believe. So do not say: I do not swear, if perhaps it is demanded. For what you do is from evil; but from the evil of the one who demands it. For you have no way to clear yourself, you find no way to satisfy the business at hand. But it is one thing when an oath is demanded; another when it is offered; and this very thing that is offered—it is one thing when it is offered to one who does not believe; another when it is bandied about even to one who believes.

**10. 11.** Hold your tongue and habit, then, as much as you can; not like certain ones, when it is said to them: Do you speak the truth? I do not believe it. You did not do it? I do not believe it: let God judge, swear to me. And he himself who demands the oath—it makes much difference whether he does not know the man is going to swear falsely, or knows it. For if he does not know it, and therefore says: Swear to me, that he may be assured; I dare not say it is no sin, yet it is a human temptation. But if he knows the man did it, knows he did it, saw he did it, and forces him to swear, he is a murderer. For that one slays himself by his perjury; but this one both forced out and pressed down the hand of the slayer. But when some wicked thief hears: Swear if you did not take it, swear if you did not do it, from one who does not know whether he did it; then that one—it is not lawful for a Christian to swear; when an oath is demanded of him, it is not lawful to swear; I am a Christian, it is not lawful for me. Catch such a one, turn yourself away from him, dissemble about the business of which you were speaking; mix in other tales, and you will find swearing a thousand times the one who was unwilling to swear once. This daily, frequent habit of swearing, then—without cause, with no one extorting it, no one doubting your words—turn away from yourselves, cut it off from your tongues, circumcise it from your mouth.

**11. 12.** But it is a habit, it is wont to be said. It is wont to be said, when I do not say it. This is: *Before all things*. What is, *Before all things*? Be cautious before the rest, be more attentive to this than to other things. A greater habit demands a greater attention, not the habit of a light matter. If you were doing something with your hand, you would more easily command your hand not to do it; if you had to go somewhere with your feet, with laziness slowing you, you would rouse yourself to rise and go. The tongue has an ease of movement, it is set in a moist place, it easily slips on the slippery. The more quickly and easily it moves, the more you be fixed against it. You will tame it, if you keep watch; you will keep watch, if you fear; you will fear, if you consider that you are a Christian. For swearing has so much evil in it that those who worship stones fear to swear falsely by stones; do you not fear God present, God living, God knowing, God remaining, God taking vengeance on the despiser? That man shuts the temple over the stone, and goes to his house; he himself shut it over his god, and yet, when it is said to him: Swear by Jupiter, he fears the eyes of the present one.

**12. 13.** And behold, I say to your Charity, that even he who swears falsely by a stone is a perjurer. Whence do I say this? Because many are deceived even in this, and think, because there is nothing by which they swear, that they are not held by the crime of perjury. You are plainly a perjurer, because you swear a lie by what you think holy. But I do not think that holy. The one to whom you swear thinks it holy. For when you swear, you do not swear to yourself, or swear to the stone; but you swear to your neighbor. You swear to a man before the stone; but is it not before God? The stone does not hear you speaking; but God punishes you deceiving.

**12. 14.** Before all things, then, my brothers, I beseech you, let God not have compelled me to speak these things without cause. For I say before him what I said, that I often avoided this question; I feared lest, by admonishing and commanding, I make more guilty those who would not hear; but today I feared more lest I refuse to speak what I was commanded to speak. As if indeed the fruit of this sweat of mine were small, if all who acclaimed me cry out also against themselves, lest they swear falsely against themselves; if so many men who heard me most attentively be attentive against their own habit, and admonish themselves today, when they come to their houses, when they have resumed their habit by a slip of the tongue; let neighbor admonish neighbor: This is what we heard today, this is what we are bound to. Let it not be done today, surely while the sermon is fresh: I speak from experience; if it is not done today, it is done more sluggishly tomorrow. If it is not done tomorrow either, the one who guards labors less; for he is helped by the habit of the previous day. In three days the plague dies of which we labor; and we will rejoice over your fruit; because you will abound in a great good, if you lack so great an evil. Let us turn to the Lord, etc.
