# Sermon 100

**1.** Hear about the passage of the Gospel that the Lord has granted. For it was read that the Lord Jesus acted differently when one man offered himself to follow him, and was rejected; another did not dare, and was urged on; a third put it off, and was blamed. For that he said, *Lord, I will follow you wherever you go* [^1]; what is so prompt, what so eager, what so ready, and what so fitted for so great a good, as to follow the Lord wherever he goes? You marvel at this, saying: What is this, that to the good master, the Lord Jesus Christ, inviting disciples to whom he would give the kingdom of heaven, one so ready was displeasing? But because he was such a master as foresees things to come, we understand, brothers, that this man, if he followed Christ, would have sought his own, not the things of Jesus Christ. For he himself said: *Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven* [^2]. And this man was of those very ones, and did not know himself as the physician saw him. For if he were already looking on himself as feigning, if he already knew himself deceitful and treacherous, he did not know to whom he was speaking. For he is the one of whom the evangelist says: *He had no need that anyone should bear him witness of man: for he himself knew what was in man* [^3]. What then did he answer? *The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests: but the Son of man has nowhere to lay his head* [^4]. But where does he not have it? In your faith. For the foxes have holes in your heart, you are deceitful: the birds of the air have nests in your heart, you are puffed up. Deceitful and puffed up, you will not follow me. How does the deceitful man follow simplicity?  
And to the other, immediately, who was silent and saying nothing, promising nothing, he says: *Follow me* [^5]. As much evil as he saw in the one, so much good he saw in the other. *Follow me*, you say to one unwilling. Behold, you have a man ready. *I will follow you wherever you go*: and you say to the unwilling one: *Follow me?* This one, he says, I turn away; because I see holes there, I see nests. Why then is he troublesome to this one, whom you provoke and who excuses himself? Behold, you even compel him, and he does not come; you exhort, and he does not follow. For what does he say? *I will first go to bury my father* [^6]. The faith of his heart showed itself to the Lord: but devotion put it off. But the Lord Christ, when he prepares men for the Gospel, wants no excuse of this carnal and temporal devotion to be interposed. This indeed the law of God also has, and the Lord himself reproved the Jews, because they were destroying that very commandment of God [^7]. And the apostle Paul set it down in his Letter, and said: *This is the first commandment with a promise* [^8]. What? *Honor your father and your mother* [^9]. Surely God said it. So this young man wanted to obey God, and to bury his father: but there is a place, and there is a time, and there is a matter, which serves this matter, this time, this place. The father must be honored, but God must be obeyed. The begetter must be loved, but the Creator must be preferred. I, he says, call you to the Gospel; for another work I have need of you: this is greater than what you want to do. *Let the dead bury their dead* [^10]. Your father is dead: there are other dead who may bury the dead. Who are the dead burying the dead? Can a dead man be buried by dead men? How do they wrap, if they are dead? how do they carry, if they are dead? how do they mourn, if they are dead? And they wrap, and they carry, and they mourn, and they are dead: because they are unbelievers.

**2.** He taught us what is written in the Song of Songs, where the Church says: *Set love in order within me* [^11]. What is: *Set love in order within me?* Make steps, and render to each what is owed. Do not subject the former things to the latter. Love your parents, but set God before your parents. Consider the mother of the Maccabees: *My sons*, she says, *I do not know how you appeared in my womb* [^12]. I was able to conceive you, I was able to bear you; I was not able to form you: hear him, then, set him before me; do not consider how I shall remain without you. She commanded, and they followed. What the mother taught her sons, this the Lord Jesus Christ was teaching this man to whom he said: *Follow me* [^13].  
*The third, looking back to his own, is blamed. Election according to grace.* For now another disciple put himself in the midst, to whom no one had said anything: *I will follow you, Lord*, he said; *but I will first go to take leave of those who are at home* [^14]. I believe the sense is this: Let me give word to mine, lest perhaps, as is wont to happen, they should look for me. And the Lord: *No one who puts his hand to the plow and looks back is fit for the kingdom of heaven* [^15]. The East calls you, and you attend to the West. In this passage we learn this: that the Lord chose those whom he willed. And he chose them, as the Apostle says, both according to his grace, and according to their righteousness. For these are the words of the Apostle: *Consider*, he says, *what Elijah says: Lord, they have killed your prophets, they have overthrown your altars, and I am left alone, and they seek my life. But what does the divine answer say to him? I have left myself seven thousand men, who have not bent the knee before Baal* [^16]. You think you alone are a servant laboring well: there are others who fear me, and not few. For I have seven thousand there. And he added: *So then also at this present time*. For some Jews believed, and many were rejected: like that man too who carried the holes of foxes in his soul. *So then*, he says, *also at this present time, a remnant has been saved through the election of grace* [^17]; that is, it is the same Christ now as then, who also said then to that Elijah: *I have left myself*. What is: *I have left myself?* I chose them, because I saw their minds presuming upon me, not upon themselves, nor upon Baal. They were not changed, they are as they were made by me. And you who speak, unless you presumed upon me, where would you be? Unless you were full of my grace, would you not yourself too bend the knee before Baal? But you are full of my grace; because you presumed nothing of your own strength, but the whole of my grace. Do not, then, glory in this, as to think that in your service you have no fellow servants: there are those whom I chose, as I chose you, namely presuming upon me: as the Apostle says: *And now a remnant has been saved through the election of grace*.

**3.** Beware, O Christian, beware of pride. For though you be an imitator of the saints, always attribute the whole to grace: because, that there might be a remnant, the grace of God in you, not your merit, brought it about. Of this very remnant the prophet Isaiah had spoken, recalling: *Unless the Lord of hosts had left us seed, we would have become as Sodom, and would have been made like Gomorrah* [^18]. *So then*, he says, *also at this present time a remnant has been saved through the election of grace. But if by grace*, he says, *then no longer of works*; that is, no longer be lifted up by your merit: *otherwise grace is no longer grace* [^19]. For if you presume upon your own work, then a wage is paid you, grace is not bestowed. But if it is grace, it is given freely. I now ask: Do you believe in Christ, O sinner? You say: I believe. What do you believe? That all your sins can be freely remitted you through him? You have what you believed. O grace freely given! What do you, righteous man, believe—that you cannot keep righteousness without God? Then attribute the whole, that you are righteous, to his goodness: but that you are a sinner, ascribe to your own iniquity. Be your own accuser, and he will be your pardoner. For every crime, every misdeed, or sin, is of our negligence: and all virtue and holiness is of God's indulgence. Turned toward the Lord.
