# Sermon 275: On the Feast Day of the Martyr Vincent

**1.** Our mind has taken in a great and most wonderful spectacle: and we have drawn in with our inner eyes a pleasure not utterly empty and ruinous, such as is usual in the theaters of trifles of every kind, but plainly most useful and most fruitful, when the glorious passion of blessed Vincent was read. It was something to see the unconquered soul of the martyr striving in fiercest conflict against the snares of the ancient enemy, against the savagery of the impious judge, against the pains of mortal flesh, and overcoming all things by the help of the Lord. So indeed it was, dearly beloved, so it was through and through: let us in the Lord praise this soul, *that the meek may hear, and be glad*[^1]. What words he heard, what answers he gave, what torments he overcame, the reading just gone through has declared, and has set before us, as it were in our sight, the things that were done. So great was the pain in his limbs, so great the security in his words, as though it were one man being tortured and another speaking. And truly it was another: for the Lord foretold this, and promised it to his martyrs, saying: *It is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^2]. In the Lord, then, let this soul be praised. For *what is man, that you are mindful of him?*[^3] Or what strength has the dust, except by the help of him who made us from the dust? *That he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^4]. For if the diabolical and seducing spirit often fills either his lying soothsayers or his false martyrs, so that they either inflict bodily torments on themselves or despise those inflicted on them: what great thing is it for the Lord our God, to confirm the preaching of his name, to hand over indeed the flesh of his very preachers into the hands of their persecutors, but to take up their minds into the citadel of freedom; so that even while the flesh suffers wrong, the mind asserts the truth? Namely, so that he makes them conquerors not by mere endurance but by justice: since it is the cause, not the punishment, that distinguishes martyrs. For many have endured pains through obstinacy, not constancy; through vice, not virtue; through a depraved error, not right reason; with the devil possessing them, not persecuting them. But in our conquering Vincent, it was indeed he who conquered who possessed him: but the one who possessed him had cast out the prince of this world[^5], so that even fighting from without, he might be overcome, who had already been overcome lest he should rule within. For he who was cast out, never ceasing, like a roaring lion goes about seeking whom he may devour[^6]. But the one who reigns in us, having shut the other out, fights him for us.

**2.** In the end the devil was more tormented by the unconquered Vincent than Vincent was by the persecuting devil. For the more truculent and exquisite those torments were, the more the tortured man triumphed over the torturer; and from that flesh, as from earth watered with his own blood—the very thing over which the enemy grieved the more—a palm was growing. But because the enemy rages in hiding, and in hiding is afflicted when conquered, it appeared openly in the governor, a man, what the devil was suffering in secret; and that invisible adversary was betrayed through the cracks of his little vessel, which he had filled, as it burst. For the voices of this man, his eyes, his face, and the turbulent movement of his whole body indicated how much heavier the torments were that he felt within than those which he was inflicting on the martyr without. If we consider the agitation of the one torturing and the tranquillity of the one suffering the torments, it is most easy to see who was beneath the punishments and who was above them. What joys will there be in the strength of those who reign, when so great are those of the ones dying for the truth? What will the fountain of life be, with the incorruption of the body, when its dew is so sweet amid torments? And what will the eternal flame do to the impious, when the madness of an angry heart so devastates them? What will they suffer when they are judged, who even while they judge are tormented? What power will the future judgments of the saints have, when in this life the judge's tribunal has tortured the martyr's rack?

**3.** Now the Lord gives a great testimony to his witnesses, since he who governed the hearts of those contending does not abandon even the bodies of the dead; as he showed by a most splendid miracle concerning the body of this same blessed Vincent: so that what the enemy had wished, striven, and contrived should not appear at all, was, by so present a nod of the divine will, betrayed and shown forth to be buried more reverently and venerated, so that in him a splendid memory of victorious piety and of vanquished impiety might endure. Truly *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^7]: when not even the earth of the flesh is despised, though life has left it; and when, the invisible soul departing from its visible house, the dwelling of the servant is guarded by the Lord's care, and is honored to the Lord's glory by faithful fellow servants. For what does God do, in working marvelous works concerning the bodies of the dead saints, except bear witness that what dies does not perish to him; and so that from this it may be understood in what honor he holds with himself the souls of the slain, when their lifeless flesh is adorned with so great an effect of divinity? For just as the Apostle, speaking of the members of the Church, used a likeness from the members of our body, since *upon those parts of ours that are less honorable we bestow more abundant honor*[^8]: so the providence of the Creator, by granting to the corpses of the martyrs such splendid testimonies of miracles, bestows more abundant honor upon the bloodless remains of men, and there, where what remains seems already unsightly with life having departed, there the giver of life appears more evidently present.
