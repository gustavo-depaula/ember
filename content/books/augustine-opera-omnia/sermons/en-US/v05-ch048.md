# Sermon 183

**1. 1.** Your Charity's expectation is the demanding of my debt. I do not doubt that you remember what I promised, with the Lord's help, concerning the reading of holy John. So when you heard the reader, I believe you thought that I ought to be the payer of the debt. For we deferred a great question, the sermon proceeding to length, how it can rightly be understood what blessed John—not the Baptist, but the Evangelist—says in his Epistle: *Every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is from God*[^1]. For we see that many heresies confess Christ to have come in the flesh, and yet we cannot say that they are from God. The Manichean denies that Christ came in the flesh. There is no need to labor, nor to persuade you longer, that this error is not from God. But the Arian confesses that Christ came in the flesh, the Eunomian, the Sabellian, the Photinian. Why do we seek witnesses to convict these? Who counts so many plagues? But meanwhile let us treat of those that are better known. For many of those heresies which I named are unknown, and this ignorance is safer. Surely, what we know, the Donatist confesses that Christ came in the flesh: and yet far be it that this error be from God. To speak of more recent heretics, the Pelagian confesses that Christ came in the flesh: yet this error is by no means from God.

**2. 2.** Accordingly, dearly beloved, let us consider diligently, since we do not doubt that the sentence is true—*Every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is from God*—these men are to be convicted of not confessing that Christ came in the flesh. For if we grant them this confession, we shall confess that they are from God. How do we either forbid you their errors, or deter you, or defend you against them with the shield of truth? Let the Lord help us, because your expectation too is a prayer for us, that these men be convicted of not confessing that Christ came in the flesh.

**2. 3.** The Arian hears, and preaches the birth of the virgin Mary. Does he, then, confess that Christ came in the flesh? No. How do we prove it? If the Lord help your understanding, most easily. What is it that we require? Whether he confesses that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. How can he confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, who denies Christ himself? For who is Christ? Let us question blessed Peter. Just now, when the Gospel was read, you heard, when the Lord Jesus Christ himself had asked whom men said that he, the Son of man, was; the disciples answered the opinions of others, and said: *Some John the Baptist, others Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the Prophets*. Those who confessed or confess these things did not know Jesus Christ as more than a man. But if they did not know Jesus Christ as more than a man, they surely did not know Jesus Christ. For if he is only a man, and nothing more, he himself is not Jesus Christ. *But you*, he says, *who do you say that I am*? Peter answered, one for all, because there is unity in all: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^2].

**3. 4.** Behold, you have a true confession, a full confession. For you ought to join both: what Christ said of himself, and what Peter said of Christ. What did Christ say of himself? *Whom do men say that I am, the Son of man*? What did Peter say of Christ? *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*. Join both, and Christ came in the flesh. This of himself Christ said, which is less; this of Christ Peter said, which is greater. Humility answered from the truth, and truth from the humility: that is, humility from the truth of God, and truth from the humility of the man. *Whom do men say that I am*, he says, *the Son of man*? I say what I was made for your sake: do you say, Peter, who it is that made you. He who, then, confesses Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh, surely confesses the Son of God to have come in the flesh. Let the Arian now say whether he confesses that Christ came in the flesh. If he confesses the Son of God to have come in the flesh, he confesses Christ to have come in the flesh. If he denies the Son of God to be Christ, he does not know Christ; he says one for another, he does not say him himself. For what is the Son of God? As we were asking what Christ is, and were hearing that he is the Son of God; let us ask what the Son of God is. Behold the Son of God: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God*[^3]. *In the beginning was the Word*. What do you say, Arian? *In the beginning*, as Genesis says, *God made heaven and earth*[^4]; but you say: In the beginning God made the Word. For you say the Word was made, you call the Word a creature. You, then, say: In the beginning God made the Word; but the Evangelist says: *In the beginning was the Word*. And therefore in the beginning God made heaven and earth, because the Word was. *All things were made through him*[^5]. You say he was made. If you say he was made, you deny the Son.

**4. 5.** For we seek a Son by nature, not by grace; an only Son, only-begotten, not adopted. Such a Son we seek, so true a Son we seek, *who, when he was in the form of God*—they are the Apostle's words; I mention it for the sake of the unlearned, lest they be thought my words: that Son we seek, *who, when he was in the form of God*, as the Apostle says, *did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*[^6]. Not robbery, because by nature. It was nature, it was not robbery. *He did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*. It was not robbery to him, it was nature; so he was from eternity, so he was coeternal with the one begetting, so he was equal to the Father, so he was. *He emptied himself*: that we may confess Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh. *He emptied himself*. How? By losing what he was, or by assuming what he was not? Let the Apostle follow; let us hear: *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^7]. So he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, not losing the form of God. The form of a servant came, the form of God did not depart. This is to confess Christ to have come in the flesh. But the Arian, who does not confess him equal, does not confess the Son. If he does not confess the Son, he does not confess Christ. He who does not confess Christ, how does he confess Christ to have come in the flesh?

**5. 6.** So too the Eunomian, his equal, his associate, not much different. For the Arians are said to have confessed that the Son is at least like the Father; and if they did not say equal, yet like. That one, not even like. So this one too denies Christ. For if the true Christ is equal and like the Father, surely he who denies him equal denies Christ; he who denies him like denies Christ. He, then, who denies him equal and like, denies that Christ came in the flesh. For I ask: Did Christ come in the flesh? He answers: He came. And we think that he confesses it. I question: Which Christ came in the flesh? Equal to the Father, or unequal? He answers: Unequal. You say, then, that one unequal to the Father came in the flesh: you deny that Christ came in the flesh, because Christ is equal to the Father.

**5. 7.** Hear the Sabellian. He himself is the Son, who is also the Father. This he says, with this he stings, from this he spreads poisons. He himself, he says, is the Father. When he wishes, he is the Son: when he wishes, he is the Father. He himself is not Christ. And you err, if you say this one came in the flesh; because this is not Christ, you deny that Christ came in the flesh.

**5. 8.** What do you say, Photinus? Photinus says: Christ is only man, he is not God. You confess the form of a servant, you deny the form of God. And Christ in the form of God is equal to the Father, in the form of a servant is a consort with us. And you deny that Christ came in the flesh.

**5. 9.** What of the Donatist? Most of the Donatists confess of the Son what we do, that the Son is equal to the Father, and of the same substance: but others of them confess him indeed of the same substance, but deny him equal. What need is there to dispute about those who deny him equal? For if they deny him equal, they deny the Son. If they deny the Son, they deny Christ. If they deny Christ, how do they confess that Christ came in the flesh?

**6. 10.** About those there is a subtler discussion, who confess what we do—the only-begotten Son equal to the Father, of the same substance, coeternal with the eternal one; and yet are Donatists. Let us say to them: With words you confess, with deeds you deny. For someone denies by deeds. Not everyone who denies, denies by word. Plainly there are men who deny by deeds. Let us question the Apostle: *All things*, he says, *are clean to the clean; but to the unclean and unbelieving nothing is clean, but both their mind and their conscience are polluted. For they confess that they know God, but by deeds they deny him*[^8]. What is it, to deny by deeds? To be proud, and to make schisms; to glory not in God, but in man. So Christ is denied by deeds: for Christ loves unity. Finally, behold how these too deny Christ, to speak more openly. We say he is the Christ of whom John the Baptist says: *He who has the bride is the bridegroom*[^9]. A good marriage, a holy wedding. The bridegroom Christ, the bride the Church. From the bridegroom we know the bride. Let the bridegroom himself tell us what bride he has; let him tell us, lest perhaps we err, and, invited to the wedding, disturb the holy vows; let him tell us, let him first show himself the bridegroom.

**7. 11.** After the resurrection he said to his disciples: *Did you not know that all things must be fulfilled that are written in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms about me*? Then, the Evangelist follows and says: *Then he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures, and said to them: Because so it behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day*[^10]. Behold the bridegroom, whom Peter confessed—that is, the Son of the living God—it behooved that he suffer, and rise on the third day. And it had happened: they saw it fulfilled, they held the head, they sought the body. What is the head? Christ himself: he suffered, he rose on the third day: he is the head of the Church. What is the body? The Church itself. The disciples, then, saw the head, they did not see the body. Therefore, since they did not see the body, let the head teach them. Tell us, Lord Jesus; tell us, holy bridegroom, instruct us about your body, about your bride, about your beloved, about your dove, whom you endowed with your blood; tell us: *It behooved that Christ suffer, and rise from the dead on the third day*. Behold the bridegroom: tell us of the bride, fill in the marriage tablets. Hear of the bride: *And that it be preached*, he says. For this follows. *It behooved that Christ suffer, and rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all nations*. Where do you hide yourself? *Through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^11]. So it was done. We read the promise, we see it fulfilled. Behold my light; where is your darkness? Therefore Christ is the bridegroom of this Church, which is preached through all nations, and sprouts and grows even to the ends of the earth, beginning from Jerusalem: of this one the bridegroom is Christ. What do you say? Of whom is Christ the bridegroom? Of the party of Donatus? He is not the one, he is not the one. Good man, he is not the one; rather, bad man, he is not the one. We come to the wedding, let us read the tablets, and let us not litigate. Therefore if you say, Christ is the bridegroom of the party of Donatus; I read the tablets, and I find that Christ is the bridegroom of the Church spread throughout the whole world. If you say: He is the one, and he is not the one, you deny that Christ came in the flesh.

**8. 12.** The Pelagian remains—not out of all the heresies, but those which I have mentioned for the short time. For I have already said: Who counts so many plagues? What do you say, Pelagian? Hear what he says. He seems to confess that Christ came in the flesh: but, examined, he is found to deny it. For Christ came in the flesh which would be the likeness of sinful flesh, but would not be sinful flesh. They are the Apostle's words: *God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh*. Not in the likeness of flesh, as though the flesh were not flesh; but *in the likeness of sinful flesh*, because it was flesh, but it was not sinful flesh. But this Pelagius tries to equate the rest of the flesh, the flesh of every infant, to the flesh of Christ. It is not so, dearly beloved. The likeness of sinful flesh would not be commended in Christ as a great thing, unless all the rest of the flesh were sinful flesh. What does it profit, then, that you say Christ came in the flesh, and try to equate him to the flesh of all infants? And to you I say this too which I said to the Donatists: He is not the one. Behold, I see mother Church bearing testimony by her very breasts. Mothers run with their little sons, they thrust the ones to be saved upon the Savior, not upon Pelagius the condemner. Every woman, a mother running by devotion with her little son says: Let him be baptized, that he may be saved. Pelagius, on the contrary: What is he to be saved from? There is nothing in him to be saved; he has no vice, he drew nothing of damnation from his descent. If he is equal to Christ, why does he seek Christ? Behold, I say to you: The bridegroom, the Son of God, who came in the flesh, is the savior both of the great and the small, is the savior both of the grown and of infants, and he is Christ; but you say that Christ is the savior of the great, not of the small: he is not the one. If he is not the one, you too deny that Christ came in the flesh.

**9. 13.** And if we examine all the heresies, we find that they deny that Christ came in the flesh. All heretics deny that Christ came in the flesh. Why do you wonder if pagans deny that Christ came in the flesh? Why do you wonder if Jews deny that Christ came in the flesh? Why do you wonder if the Manicheans most openly deny that Christ came in the flesh? But I tell your Charity, even all evil Catholics confess with words that Christ came in the flesh; but by deeds they deny it. Do not, then, be as it were secure about faith. Add to right faith a right life, that you may confess Christ to have come in the flesh, both by speaking true things with words, and by living well in deeds. For if you confess with words, and deny by deeds, the faith of such evil men is near the faith of demons. Hear me, dearly beloved, hear me, lest this sweat of mine be a witness against you; hear me. The apostle James, when he spoke of faith and works against those who thought faith sufficed for them, and were unwilling to have good works, says: *You believe that God is one; you do well: the demons too believe, and tremble*[^12]. Will the demons, then, be freed from the eternal fire because they believe and tremble? Behold, what you just now heard in the Gospel, what Peter said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^13]; read, and you will find that the demons said: *We know who you are, the Son of God*[^14]. Yet Peter is praised, the demon is restrained. One voice, different deeds. Whence are these two confessions separated? Love is praised, fear is condemned. For the demons did not say this out of love: *You are the Son of God*. They said this out of fear, not out of love. Finally in their confession they said: *What have we to do with you*[^15]? But Peter: *I am with you even to death*[^16].

**10. 14.** But Peter himself—from where, my brothers, from where did he have it to say out of love, *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^17]? From where did he have it? Was it from himself? By no means. It is good that this very chapter of the Gospel shows both things: what Peter had from God and what from himself. You have both there: read it, there is no reason for you to wait to hear it from me. I recall the Gospel: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*. And the Lord to him: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona*[^18]. Why? Blessed from yourself? No. *For flesh and blood has not revealed it to you*: for this is what you are. *Flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^19]. And it goes on, and says the rest, which would take long to recall. A little later the Lord, after these words of his by which he approved Peter's faith and showed it to be the rock, began to show his disciples that he had to go to Jerusalem and suffer many things and be rejected by the elders and scribes and priests, and be killed, and rise again on the third day. There Peter, out of himself, took fright and shrank in horror from the death of Christ; the sick man took fright at his own medicine. *Far be it from you, Lord*, he said: *be merciful to yourself, let this not happen*[^20]. And where is this: *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again*[^21]? Have you forgotten, Peter? You have forgotten: *Greater love has no one than this, that one lay down his soul for his friends*[^22]? You have forgotten. That forgetfulness comes from himself; the trembling, the horror, and the fear of death, all of it comes from Peter—rather, from Simon, not from Peter. And the Lord: *Get behind me, Satan. Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona: Get behind me, Satan. Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona*; but from God. *Get behind me, Satan*; from where? Recall from where he was blessed. I have already said it: *For flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*. From where, Satan? Let the Lord say it: *For you do not savor the things that are of God, but the things that are of men*[^23].

**10. 15.** Hope in the Lord, and join good deeds to your true faith. Confess that Christ has come in the flesh, both by believing and by living well, and hold both as received from him; hope that both will be increased and perfected by him. For cursed is everyone who places his hope in man[^24]. And it is good for a person that he who glories should glory in the Lord[^25].  
Turning to the Lord God, the Father almighty, let us with a pure heart give him our greatest and truest thanks, as far as our littleness can; praying with our whole soul for his singular kindness, that he may deign to hear our prayers according to his good pleasure, that by his power he may also drive the enemy from our deeds and thoughts, multiply our faith, govern our mind, grant us spiritual thoughts, and bring us to his blessedness; through Jesus Christ his Son. Amen.
