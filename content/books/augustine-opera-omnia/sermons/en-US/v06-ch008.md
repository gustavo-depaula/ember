# Sermon 191: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1. 1.** The Word of the Father, through whom *times were made*[^1], made flesh, made his own birthday for us in time: and in his human rising he willed to have one day, without whose divine nod no day rolls round. He himself, with the Father, precedes all the spans of the ages: he himself, of his mother, on this day entered into the courses of the years. Made man, the maker of man: that he might suck the breast, who rules the stars; that the Bread might hunger, that the Fountain might thirst, that the Light might sleep, that the Way might be wearied by the journey, that the Truth might be accused by false witnesses, that the Judge of the living and the dead might be judged by a mortal judge, that Righteousness might be condemned by the unrighteous, that Discipline might be scourged with whips, that the Cluster of grapes might be crowned with thorns, that the Foundation might be hung upon a tree, that Strength might be weakened, that Health might be wounded, that Life might die. To bear these and such unworthy things for us, that he might free the unworthy; since neither did he, who for our sake bore such evils, deserve any evil, nor did we, who through him received such goods, deserve any good: for these things, then, he who was before all the ages, without beginning of days, the Son of God, deigned in the last days to be the son of a man; and he who was born of the Father, not made by the Father, was made in the mother whom he made; that from her there might at some time arise the one who, except through him, could never and nowhere have existed.

**1. 2.** Thus was fulfilled what the Psalm had foretold: *truth has sprung up from the earth*[^2]. Mary a virgin before conception, a virgin after the birth. For far be it that in that earth, that is, in that flesh from which truth sprang up, integrity should perish. Indeed, after his resurrection, when he was thought to be a spirit, not a body: *Handle me*, he said, *and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see that I have*[^3]. And yet the solidity of that youthful body brought itself in to the disciples through doors that were not open[^4]. Why, then, could he who was able to enter, fully grown, through closed doors, not also go forth, an infant, through uncorrupted members? But the unbelieving will believe neither this nor that. Therefore faith rather believes both; because unbelief does not believe either. For it is unbelief itself to which no divinity seems to be in Christ. But further, if faith believes that God was born in the flesh, it does not doubt that both are possible for God; that both his body of greater age might present itself to those placed within, with no door of the house unbarred, and the bridegroom, an infant, might proceed *from his chamber*[^5], that is, from the virginal womb, with the mother's virginity unharmed.

**2. 3.** For there the only-begotten Son of God deigned to join human nature to himself, that to himself, the immaculate Head, he might unite the immaculate Church: which Paul the apostle calls a virgin, considering in her not only those who are virgins also in body, but desiring the uncorrupted minds of all. *For I have betrothed you*, he says, *to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ*[^6]. The Church, then, imitating the mother of her Lord, since she could not in body, is nevertheless in mind both mother and virgin. In no way, therefore, did Christ, in being born, take away virginity from his mother, who, by redeeming his Church from the fornication of the demons, made her a virgin. From her uncorrupted virginity were begotten holy virgins, who, despising earthly marriages, have chosen to be virgins also in the flesh: rejoicing, solemnly celebrate on this present day the Virgin's childbearing. For he was born of a woman who was not begotten by a male in a woman. He who brought you something to love did not take from his mother what you love. He who heals in you what you drew from Eve, far be it that he should mar what you loved in Mary.

**3. 4.** She, then, whose footsteps you follow, both did not remain with a man in order to conceive, and remained a virgin when she bore. Imitate her as much as you can; not in fruitfulness, for this you cannot, with virginity preserved. She alone was able to do both, of which you have wished to have the one; for you lose this if you should wish to have both. She alone was able to do both, who bore the Almighty, through whom she was able. For it was fitting that the only-begotten Son of God alone should become the son of a man in this unique way. Nor yet for that reason is Christ nothing to you, because he is the offspring of one virgin. For the very one whom you could not bring forth as a son in the flesh, you have found as a bridegroom in the heart: and such a bridegroom that your happiness so holds him as Redeemer that your virginity need not fear him as a slayer. For he who did not take away virginity from his mother even by a bodily birth, much more in you preserves it by a spiritual embrace. Nor on that account should you consider yourselves barren, because you remain virgins. For that holy integrity of the flesh itself belongs to the fruitfulness of the mind. Do what the Apostle says: that you do not *think of the things of the world, how you may please your husbands*; think of *the things of God, how you may please him in all things*[^7]; so that you may have a womb not fruitful with offspring, but a mind fruitful with virtues. Finally, I address all, I speak to all; the whole chaste virgin whom the Apostle betrothed to Christ I summon with this voice. What you marvel at in the flesh of Mary, do in the inmost places of the soul. He who *believes with the heart unto righteousness* conceives Christ: he who *confesses with the mouth unto salvation*[^8] bears Christ. So in your minds let both fruitfulness abound and virginity persevere.
