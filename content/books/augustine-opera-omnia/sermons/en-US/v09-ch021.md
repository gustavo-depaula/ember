# (Erfurt 6 = Sermon 313/G): A sermon of Saint Augustine

**1.** I give thanks to the Lord our God because, that I might keep this day with you, he deigned to grant it both to me and to you. With a most devoted gathering we celebrate the solemnities of a most illustrious martyr. The adornment of confession, the ruler of the Africans and teacher of the Church, a most true and most sincere martyr and both instructor and ruler, teaching what he was to do, doing what he had taught, sending many before himself by precept, drew many after himself by example. But who did this in him, except he who made him? For God made both the man and the believer and the martyr: the man when he created him, the believer when he called him, the martyr when he crowned him. Such martyrs we venerate without scruple; for illustrious and clear to all are the passions which they bore for the Lord. This blessed Cyprian too was sent like a sheep into the midst of wolves; by his word he chastised the wolves, and like a shepherd he answered for the sheep and shed his blood for the sheep. He held the simplicity of the dove and the cunning of the serpent: by the simplicity of the dove he harmed no one, by the cunning of the serpent he guarded his own head. When serpents are struck, they set all the coils of their members against the blow for the sake of the head, and with great zeal guard the place where they live. How did holy Cyprian guard his head? *The head of the man is Christ*[^1]; so he guarded his head, when, his body set against the persecutors, he did not deny Christ.

**2.** &lt;...&gt; eager and glad, let us celebrate the sincerity of devotion; let it suffice us, brothers! Let us not persecute the martyrs with cups, whom the pagans persecuted with stones! I see, by God's favor, this place full with the eagerness of your gathering. Surely now we are not eating—do not question your bellies, but your minds!—surely now we are not drinking? Our food and drink ought to be that of the inner man. For the burdened outer man weighs down the inner, whence the Lord says: *Let not your hearts be weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness*[^2], because by drunkenness bodies are filled and hearts are weighed down. How much we ought to avoid this, when we celebrate the birthdays of the martyrs! For that they might reach those crowns, they despised such things as are now celebrated as things to be longed for, when men set about celebrating their solemnities. For if we love the martyrs, let us follow the footsteps of the martyrs! Imitate the one you love: this is the fruit of love, the footstep of imitation.

[^1]: 1 Cor 11:3
[^2]: Luke 21:34
