# Sermon 202: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1. 1.** What this day's solemnity, known throughout the whole world, brings us of festivity, or what it commemorates in its yearly recurrence, the season admonishes us to tell also in a yearly sermon. "Epiphany," in Greek, in Latin can be called "manifestation." For on this day the Magi are reported to have adored the Lord; admonished, namely, by the appearing of the star, and led by it going before. For on that day on which he was born, they saw the star in the East; and they recognized whose birth it had indicated. From that day, then, to this day they hastened, terrified King Herod with the news, and, the Jews answering from the prophetic Scripture, found Bethlehem, the city where the Lord had been born. Then, the same star leading, they came to the Lord himself, adored him when pointed out; offered gold, frankincense, and myrrh; returned by another way[^1]. The Lord was indeed made manifest also on the very day of his nativity to the shepherds, admonished by the angel; on which day too, through the star, he was announced to those far off in the East: but on this day he was adored by them. The whole Church of the Gentiles, then, most devoutly took up this day to be celebrated: for what were those Magi too, except the firstfruits of the Gentiles? The shepherds Israelites, the Magi Gentiles: the former near, the latter far: yet both ran together to the cornerstone[^2]. *For coming*, as the Apostle says, *he announced peace to us who were far off, and peace to those who were near. For he himself is our peace, who made the two one, and created the two in himself into one new man, making peace, and reconciled both in one body to God, slaying the enmities in himself*[^3].

**2. 2.** Rightly have the Donatist heretics never willed to celebrate this day with us: because they neither love unity, nor are in communion with the Eastern Church, where that star appeared. But let us celebrate the manifestation of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, by which he tasted the firstfruits of the Gentiles, in the unity of the Gentiles. For then the child, before he knew how to call upon father or mother, as had been prophesied of him, *received the power of Damascus and the spoils of Samaria*[^4]: that is, before he brought forth human words through human flesh, he received the power of Damascus, that, namely, on which Damascus presumed. For in riches that city, flourishing according to the world, had once presumed. But in riches the chief place is given to gold, which the Magi suppliantly offered to Christ. But the spoils of Samaria were those very ones who inhabited it. For Samaria is put for idolatry. For there the people of Israel, turned away from the Lord, were turned to the worshiping of idols. About to war down, namely, the kingdom of the devil with the spiritual sword throughout the whole world, the child Christ first drew these spoils from the dominion of idolatry, that he might turn the Magi, converted to adore him, away from the pestilence of that superstition, and, not yet speaking in this land through the tongue, might speak from heaven through the star; that both who he was, and whither, and on whose account he had come, he might show not by the voice of the flesh, but by the power of the Word, which was made flesh. For this Word, which in the beginning was God with God, now also made flesh, that it might dwell among us[^5], had both come to us and remained with the Father; above not deserting the angels, and below gathering men to himself through the angels; and to the heavenly dwellers, according to the Word, it shone with unchangeable truth, and on account of the narrow lodging lay in a manger. By it the star was pointed out in heaven, and it pointed out itself, to be adored, on earth. And yet the infant so powerful, so great, a little one, fled into Egypt, his parents carrying him, on account of the enmities of Herod: thus, not yet by speech, but already by his deeds, his own members speaking, and, silent, saying: *If they persecute you in one city, flee to another*[^6]. For he bore mortal flesh, in which he prefigured us, in which he was also, for our sake, going to die at the opportune time. Whence from those Magi he had received not only gold, to be honored, and frankincense, to be adored, but also myrrh, to be buried. Of what kind those would be who were to die for his name, how innocent, how humble, he showed in the little ones whom Herod killed. For by their being two years old he signified also that number of precepts on which the whole Law and the Prophets hang.

**3. 3.** But now whom does it not make attentive, what it means that to the inquiry of the Magi, where Christ would be born, the Jews answered from Scripture, and themselves did not adore with them? Do we not see this even now, when, out of the very sacraments to which their hardness is subjected, nothing else is shown than Christ, in whom they are unwilling to believe? Do they not, even when they kill the sheep and eat the Passover[^7], show Christ to the Gentiles, whom they themselves do not adore with them? For what kind of thing is it, that often, concerning the prophetic testimonies by which Christ was foretold, to certain men who doubt lest perhaps those things were written by Christians, not as still future, but as done, we appeal to the books of the Jews, that we may confirm the minds of the doubters? Do not even then the Jews show to the Gentiles the Christ whom they are unwilling to adore with the Gentiles?

**3. 4.** We therefore, dearest ones, whose firstfruits those Magi were, we the inheritance of Christ to the ends of the earth, on whose account a partial blindness has happened in Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter[^8], having come to know our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who for the sake of consoling us then lay in a narrow lodging, now for the sake of exalting us sits in heaven; let us so announce him in this land, in this region of our flesh, that we may not return by the way we came, nor seek again the traces of our former conduct. For this is what those Magi too did: they did not return by the way they had come[^9]. The way changed, the life changed. To us too the heavens have declared the glory of God[^10]; and us too the gleaming truth from the Gospel, as a star from heaven, led to adore Christ; we too received with a faithful ear the prophecy celebrated in the Jewish nation, as an indication of the Jews not proceeding with us; we too, recognizing and praising Christ as king and priest and dead for us, have honored him as it were in gold and frankincense and myrrh: it remains that, announcing the gospel of him, we take a new way, and not return by the way we came.
