# Sermon 335/A: On the Solemnity of the Martyrs of Whom One Was Called Primus, Another Victoria Perpetua

**1.** "Martyr" is a Greek name, in Latin they are called "witnesses"; if, then, they are witnesses, for the truth of their testimony they suffered so much. Truth served God, iniquity lied to itself. For so it is written; the body of Christ, which is the Church, speaks in the Psalm: *Unjust witnesses rose up against me, and iniquity lied to itself*[^1]. Witnesses and witnesses: unjust witnesses, and just witnesses; both witnesses of the devil, and witnesses of Christ. We saw, we watched, we heard both kinds of witness, when the passion of the blessed martyrs, whose day of solemnity is kept, was read. Questioned, they answered that they had held an assembly, because they were Christians. This is the testimony of truth. The judge said: "Since you have confessed to a crime." This is the testimony of iniquity. God was preached, and it was called a crime. In the preaching of God truth obeyed God; in the calling it a crime iniquity lied to itself. For what they said turned back upon themselves, and the false crime was condemned by a true crime. Our martyrs did not commit a crime; the martyrs of Christ did not commit a crime by gathering themselves in an assembly to praise God, to hear the truth, to hope for the kingdom of heaven, and to despise the present most wicked world. These did not commit a crime. This is called godliness, the name of this thing is religion, the name of this thing is devotion, the name of this thing is true testimony. What crime, then, did they commit, who moreover killed those who confessed their godliness? "Let that man and that man," says the judge of iniquity, the witness of falsehood, "let that man and that man be punished by the sword." Behold the crime. Hear too the voice of godliness: *Thanks be to God,* said the testimony, Primus first; Victoria Perpetua closed the testimony. I believe Your Charity noticed, when the passion of the saints was read, that he who confessed first was called Primus before the last; Victoria Perpetua at the end. O victory without blemish! O end without end! For what is perpetual victory, but victory without end? This is to conquer the enticements of the flesh, to conquer the threats of the perverse judge, to conquer the pain of the body, to conquer the love of life.

**2.** I will say, if I can, with the Lord's help, my brothers, what I feel: in the holy martyrs the love of life was conquered by the love of life. You who applauded, understood; but for the sake of those who did not understand, suffer me, who understood, to open a little what I said. This I said: in the holy martyrs the love of life was conquered by the love of life; by loving life they despised life. Who, by loving silver, despises silver? who, by loving gold, despises gold? who, by loving estates, despises estates? No one despises what he loves. We find martyrs who loved life and despised life. They would not come to that life, if they did not trample this one. They knew what they were doing, who by spending acquired. Do not think, dearly beloved, that they lost their judgment, when they loved life, and despised life; they did not lose their judgment. They scattered the seeds and sought the harvest. I see the farmer's judgment, and I recognize in the martyrs wisdom. The farmer, by loving wheat, scatters wheat. You who do not know what the sower is doing, perhaps would rebuke him, and say: What are you doing, madman? what you gathered with such labor, you bring out, you scatter, you take from your eyes, you cast into the earth, moreover you bury it? He answers you: I love the wheat, therefore I cast away the wheat; if I did not love it, I would not cast it away; I want it to grow, not to perish. Behold what our martyrs did, incomparably wiser than the farmers. For the sowers scatter few grains, and the reapers gather many. But both what they scatter and what they gather has an end; what is scattered is not much, what is gathered is much, yet both are finite. And would you not have our martyrs scatter a life that would someday be ended by death, that they might receive a life that has no death? Good lenders, good sowers; he who multiplies is God. He himself multiplies the crops in the field; he himself nourishes whatever is born of the earth. God can multiply grains: can he not preserve his martyrs? Behold I tell you, hear what they heard.

**3.** You too heard just now, when the Gospel was read; what was promised them, you received: *They will hand you over,* he says, *in councils, and in their synagogues they will scourge you, and some of you they will kill; but I say to you: A hair of your head will not perish, and in your patience you will possess your souls*[^2]. You will possess, and you do not lose; for there no enemy persecutes, no friend dies. There you will be, where the perpetual day has no yesterday preceding, nor tomorrow following. There you will be good lenders, where the devil cannot follow you. Bear for a time; rejoice forever. Hard are the things you endure, but great fruit has what you sow. Read what was said of you, when you sowed: *Going they went and wept, casting their seeds*[^3]. To what fruit? to what end? to what consolation? *But coming they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^4]. From these sheaves crowns are made. So let us celebrate the days of the martyrs, by honoring the passions of the martyrs, not by loving the drinking-cups. Turned to the Lord.

[^1]: Ps 27:12 (26:12); Ps 26:12 (LXX)
[^2]: See Matt 10:17; Luke 21:16-19
[^3]: Ps 126:6 (125:6)
[^4]: Ps 126:6 (125:6)
