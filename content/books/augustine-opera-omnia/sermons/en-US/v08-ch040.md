# Sermon 299/F: On the Birthday of the Holy Martyrs of Scilli

**1.** The day of this feast urges us to speak of the martyrs of Christ, that is, of the witnesses of Christ, who were not ashamed to confess his name before men. May he who said to them: *Do not think about what you are to say, for the Holy Spirit will teach you what you ought to say*[^1], grant us to say to you what is profitable for you. "Martyrs" is a Greek word; in Latin they are called "witnesses." So the holy martyrs, witnesses not false but true, by their blood bore witness that there is another life to be preferred to this life, because they bravely despised this passing one. You heard the confessions of the martyrs whose feast is celebrated today, when they were recited. What constancy burned in them, what longing for the kingdom of heaven appeared in the fire of their words, where they were when they were heard, to what they clung, from which perhaps they drew what they said—since we cannot recall all, let us at least recall something.

**2.** Remember, dearly beloved, how, when the judge who was listening called their confession "the persuasion of vanity," one of them answered: *The persuasion of vanity is to commit murder, to bear false witness*. In these two things what is to be understood as the persuasion of vanity? Evil, surely. For were they not evils that vanity persuaded? Not for nothing did he—to whom it had been foretold that he should not think what he was to say, because he was to receive it at that hour from the Holy Spirit—not without cause say these two things. For this was what was at issue. The very case was being handled in their midst. *The persuasion of vanity*, he says, *is to commit murder, to bear false witness*. This is to say: You wish to commit murder, and you compel me to bear false witness. To deny Christ is false witness. "Say that Christ is not God, and that those we worship are gods." Both are false: for he is God and they are not gods. "Deny what you believe." What you do not want me to have in my mouth, take from my heart; but why do you forbid me to utter what you cannot take from my heart? What is within, this he who is a true witness brings forth without. For I heard from the apostle, or rather from Christ through the apostle: *With the heart one believes unto righteousness, with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^2]. But the mocker of truth, the lover of vanity, said: "How is confession made with the mouth unto salvation? They speak and they die. How do they speak unto salvation, when, as they speak, they are killed? If they did not speak, they would not be killed." How? Confession is made unto salvation—the salvation those who bore witness saw, which those who frightened them with death did not see. For the adversary threatened that he would take away their health, but they saw another which they were to receive and would never lose. Gazing on it, they were brave, and inflamed by its beauty, they despised the transitory health, shared with the beasts. There is one health shared with the angels, another shared with the beasts. In the middle is man. He has something like the angels. What does he have? Mind, reason, understanding, wisdom. What has he like the beasts? Flesh, weakness, want, mortality. Let him gaze on the former, disregard the latter. Let him love the former, spurn the latter. The former abide, the latter pass away. There will come the salvation which the Savior himself promised, who by his death taught that this health is to be despised, and by his resurrection taught what ought to be loved by those who despise it. The whole has been shown to me in my Lord. The whole has been shown to me in the Word that was made flesh and dwelt among us[^3]. For he deigned to have flesh for our sake, that he might show us in the very flesh he took from us what we should love, what we should despise. For there was no blood in the Word that he might shed for us, for there was no death in life. For *life was the light of men*[^4]. Whence then blood, whence death, whence suffering for us, except because *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^5]? This he had of ours, giving us great things of his own. We knew how to die. Whence to live we did not have. For we had remained poor in this land. For this is what is usually said of the very poor: He is very poor, he has nothing to live on. This we all were, both poor and rich we were thus. For even the rich man who does not know the eternal life has nothing to live on. Consider us, the sons of man, below; the Word of God the Son, above. We had nothing to live on, nor he anything to die by. The Word, the only-begotten Son of God, equal to the Father and coeternal, he above, we below. Sons of men, mortal, helpless, needy, swollen, covetous, truly sad, vainly glad, we had nothing to live on, nor had he anything to die by. What did he take from us, what did he give us? He took from us wherewith to die. He gave us wherewith to live. *For the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*. Being the Son of God, he was made the Son of man. He hungered that he might feed, thirsted that he might satisfy, slept that he might awaken, was wearied on the way that he might offer himself as provision to the weary, finally was dishonored that he might honor, died that he might give life.

**3.** This the true witnesses held; they beheld his future gifts in their mind. Therefore they despised all passing things: *Vain is the salvation of man*[^6]. So one was not frightened when he heard: "If you confess Christ you will be punished," because he attended to this: *A false witness will not go unpunished*[^7]. The blessed saints spoke the truth and were killed. For what then? If no one killed them, would they still be alive? How much better did the witness of truth accomplish what a fever was soon going to do anyway. Life is spared, that the true life may perish. The passing one is spared, that the eternal one may not be received. Buy, you rich; buy, you poor. Let no one say: I have nothing to buy with. Let him not seek the price in his strongbox. Let no one say: I have nothing, I am poor. He who is to be bought gives that with which he may be bought. He speaks to you: "I will dwell in you, that you may have wherewith to buy me; confess me and you will possess me."

**4.** May the holy martyrs pray for us, that we may not only celebrate their feasts, but also imitate their ways. Let us love their confessions, praise their crowns, and not despair. For we too are men as they were, and we too were created by him by whom they were. We have one fountain, we have one storehouse from which we are fed, from which we drink, and from which we have all that we live by. Let no one say: He was able, I am not able. Whence was he able? What could he do, unless he had granted it who said to his own: *Without me you can do nothing*[^8]? Therefore the apostle says: *Who*, he says, *shall separate us from the love of God? tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or the sword, as it is written: For your sake we are put to death all the day long?*[^9] O good cause: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long*. So fruitfully, so happily, because *for your sake*. Because the cause is good, therefore the crown. *For your sake we are put to death all the day long, we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter*[^10]. They who were everywhere killed and offered up by those who knew not, were publicly laid low and secretly crowned.

**5.** *For your sake we are put to death all the day long*[^11]. What love itself is worth, it itself declares; for you it itself came from the Spirit of God. The covetousness of the age is of the world, not of God, and yet it too is strong in its own way. How much do men suffer for money, how many perilous things! They commit themselves to the waves, to storms. They are willing to die rather than to live poor. And yet, loving life, when they have begun to be in danger, they cast away their goods. Whatever they carry they throw into the sea, and to live they fling away what they live by. There life is sweet and is preferred to money; but when the sailor has escaped, naked, he upbraids God: "See where you have brought me; why did you not drown me in the sea?" Madman, when you were in danger you did not ask for this. Behold, how much do the lovers of money suffer for their beloved! Some are worn down by labors and fail by toiling; some are slaughtered by robbers; some are sunk by the waves; some perish by various deaths. They too can say to money what the martyrs say to wisdom: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long*[^12]; they can, indeed, in the very same words: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long*, but they say it to one that does not hear. And if it heard and felt and answered, perhaps it would rebuke you and say to you: "Madman, wisdom will say to the martyrs: When you have died for me, you will have me. But I say to you: When you have died for me, you lose both yourself and me."
