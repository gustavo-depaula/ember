# Sermon 79/A: A Sermon on the Gospel Where Jesus Was Transfigured on the Mountain

**1.** Before the Lord showed himself on the mountain, as we heard when the holy Gospel was read, he had said: *There are some of those standing here who will not taste death until they see the Son of man in his kingdom*[^1]. But we know that at the end of the age the Lord Jesus Christ will come as judge and will give the kingdom to those on the right, punishment to those on the left[^2], *who*, as we believe and confess, *will come to judge the living and the dead*. But all those who stood around him when he was speaking, when the condition of death came, fell asleep, and they will not see the Lord as judge in his kingdom, except when the time of resurrection comes. What, then, does it mean that he says: *There are some of those standing here who will not taste death until they see the Son of man in his kingdom?* Without doubt the holy Gospel opened the question by what follows. For he took three up the mountain with him, Peter and James and John, and before them he transfigured himself, so that his face shone like the brightness of the sun[^3]. They, then, were of those standing around who were not going to see death until they saw the Lord in his kingdom. But the brightness of all will be at the end of the ages. Which brightness the Lord showed in himself. His members will shine as the head shone. It is written: *He will transfigure the body of our lowliness, conformed to the body of his glory*[^4]. Behold, he himself on the mountain *shone like the sun*[^5], and had not yet risen. He had not yet tasted death but was God in the flesh, and of the flesh not yet raised he did what he wished by divine power. For, that you may know it is not proud if we too hope for this, hear him himself and do not doubt. When he expounded the parable of the tares he said: *He who sows the good seed is the Son of man. The field is the world. But the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom. The tares are the sons of the evil one. The enemy who sowed it is the devil. The harvest is the end of the age. But the reapers are the angels*[^6]. *When, then, the end of the age comes, then the Son of man will send his angels, and they will gather out of his kingdom all scandals, and cast them into the furnace of burning fire, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^7]. What of the wheat? Hear what follows: *Then the just will shine like the sun in the kingdom*[^8]. And because they will be such in the kingdom, therefore the Lord said: *There are some of those standing here who will not taste death until they see the Son of man in his kingdom*[^9].

**2.** But now, brothers, what does it mean? Moses and Elijah appeared, set the Lord in the middle and were conversing with him[^10]. Holy Peter was delighted with the solitude, he had weariness of the turbulent human race. He saw the mountain, saw the Lord, saw Moses, saw Elijah. There were alone those who did not trouble him. He was delighted with a quiet, leisurely, blessed life and said to the Lord: *Lord, it is good for us to be here*[^11]. Why do we come down from the mountain to be disturbed and not choose to rejoice here? *It is good for us to be here. Let us make here three tabernacles, if you command: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah*[^12]. Peter, not yet knowing how he ought to speak, wished to divide. He thought what he said was good. But what did the Lord do? He sent a cloud from heaven and covered them all, as if saying to Peter: Why do you wish to make three tabernacles? Behold one. Then they heard the voice from the cloud: *This is my beloved Son*[^13], lest they compare Moses and Elijah to him and think the Lord was to be received as if one of the Prophets, when he was the Lord of the Prophets: *This is my Son: hear him*[^14]. By this voice they, terrified, fell down. *The Lord approached and raised them up and they saw no one except Jesus alone*[^15].

**3.** A great mystery, if the Lord grants, I will now speak. Moses and Elijah speak with the Lord. In Moses the Law, in Elijah the Prophets. When we set forth anything from the Gospel, we prove it from the Law and the Prophets. And Moses and Elijah speak with the Lord, but the ministers at the sides, in the middle the ruler. What is it for Moses and Elijah to speak with the Lord? Hear the Apostle: *Through the Law*, he says, *is the knowledge of sin, but now without the Law the justice of God has been manifested*[^16]. Behold the Lord, where Moses and Elijah are, having testimony from the Law and the Prophets. In this time the testimonies of the Law and the Prophets are necessary. But when we rise again, what need is there of the Law, what need of the Prophets? We do not seek testimonies because we will see him himself. But when will this be? After the resurrection. Therefore those who fell rose up and saw the Lord alone.
