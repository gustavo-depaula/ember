# Sermon 337: On the Dedication of a Church

**1.** The good works of the faithful from their temporal and earthly substance, when they are stored up in the heavenly treasuries, faith sees this, which has the eye of godliness in the heart. Whence even these buildings, which are erected for gathering the religious assemblies, when faith has inspected them with the eye of the flesh, it praises inwardly what it discerns outwardly, and receives by the visible light that in which it may rejoice by the invisible truth. For faith is not occupied in inspecting how beautiful are the members of this dwelling; but from how great a beauty of the inner man these works of love proceed. So the Lord will render to his faithful, who work these things so godly, so cheerfully, so devoutly, that he may set them too in the construction of his own building, where the living stones run, formed by faith, made solid by hope, joined together by love. There the wise architect, the Apostle, laid the foundation Christ Jesus, himself the chief *cornerstone,* as Peter too recalls from the prophetic Scripture, *rejected indeed by men, but chosen and honored by God*[^1]. By cleaving to him, we are made peaceful; by leaning on him, we are made firm. For he is at once both foundational, because he himself rules us; and angular, because he himself joins us. He is that rock, on which the wise man building his house perseveres most safely against all the trials of this world: neither does it slip when the rain rushes in, nor is it overturned when the river floods, nor is it moved when the winds blow. *He is also our peace, who made both one*[^2]: for in him *neither circumcision avails anything, nor uncircumcision, but a new creation*[^3]. For these two, as it were walls coming from opposite directions, had been far from one another, until, led to him as to a corner, they were even in him joined to one another.

**2.** And so, just as this visible building was made for gathering us bodily; so that building, which we ourselves are, is constructed for God to dwell in spiritually. *For the temple of God is holy,* says the Apostle, *which you are*[^4]. As this we construct with earthly masses, so let us raise that with well-ordered conduct. For this now, while we are visiting it, that will be dedicated at the end of the world when the Lord comes, when this our corruptible will put on incorruption, and this our mortal will put on immortality: because he will conform the body of our humility to the body of his glory. For see what he says in the Psalm of dedication: *You have turned my mourning into joy for me; you have torn my sackcloth and girded me with gladness: that my glory may sing to you, and I may not be pricked*[^5]. For when we are being built, our humility groans to him; but when we will be dedicated, our glory will sing to him: because in the building-up there is labor, in the dedication gladness. While stones are cut from the mountains, and timbers from the woods, while they are formed, hewn, fitted together; there is labor and care: but when the dedication of the finished building is celebrated, joy and security succeed the labors and cares. So also the spiritual building, whose inhabitant is God, will be not for a time, but forever; while men are segregated from an unbelieving life to faith, while whatever in them is not good and is perverse is cut off and pruned away, while they become fit, peaceful, and godly joinings; how many trials are feared, how many tribulations sustained? But when the day of the dedication of the eternal house comes, when it will be said to us: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^6]; what will that exultation, what security will there be? Brightness will sing, and weakness will not be pricked. When he will show himself to us who loved us, and handed himself over for us; and who appeared to men as what he was made in his mother, will appear to them God the maker as what he was in the Father: when the eternal inhabitant himself will enter his house, perfect and adorned, established in unity, clothed with immortality; he will fill all things, will shine in all things, *that God may be all in all*[^7].

**3.** This one vision a certain man sought from the Lord; and that certain man, if we will, is we ourselves. For desire of this he labored in his groaning, for this he washed his bed every night, and watered his couch with tears. For on account of this his tears were bread to him day and night, while it was said to him every day: *Where is your God?*[^8] For he himself says: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord, and be protected, his temple*[^9]. He himself the inhabitant of his own, himself the dwelling. For those who dwell in the house of God, are themselves also the house of God: which contemplates his delight, and is protected, his temple, and is hidden in the hiding of his face. This hope we hold, the reality we do not yet see. *But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^10], and by patience we are built up.

**4.** Come, then, brothers, *if you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God: savor the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^11]. For Christ our foundation too was placed there for this reason, that we might be built up toward what is above. For just as in earthly masses to be constructed, whose heavy bodies surely sink only to the depths, the foundation is placed at the bottom: so for us, on the contrary, that foundational stone is placed above, that it may snatch us upward even by the weight of love. So eagerly *work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God who works in you to will and to work according to his good will*[^12]. *Do all things without murmuring*[^13]. And *as living stones be built up together into a temple of God*[^14]: and as incorruptible timbers make of yourselves the house of God. Be made square, be hewn, in labors, in necessities, in vigils, in tasks, be prepared for every good work: that in eternal life you may merit to rest as in the framework of the fellowship of the angels.

**5.** For this place was built temporally, nor will it last forever: just as our very bodies too, for whose necessity, through the works of mercy, it was made, are surely not everlasting, but temporal and mortal. *But we have a dwelling from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens*[^15]: where our very bodies too, by the change of the resurrection, will be heavenly and everlasting. And now, although not yet through sight, as it will be face to face, yet through faith God dwells in us: and to him so dwelling we are made a dwelling-place through good works; which works are not eternal, but lead to eternal life. Of these is also this work, by which this basilica was built: for we will not work such buildings there. No place to fall in ruin is built there, into which no inhabitant to die enters. Yet now let your temporal work be good, that your reward may be eternal. Now, I say, build the house of faith and hope by spiritual love in every good work, which then will not be; because there will be no need. So lay in your hearts the foundations, the apostolic and prophetic admonitions; spread out your humility like a pavement without stumbling; fortify the saving doctrine in your heart with prayers and sermons as with firm walls, illumine them with the divine testimonies as with lamps, support the weak as columns, protect the needy as roofs: that the Lord our God may restore eternal things for temporal goods, and may possess you, perfect and dedicated, forever.
