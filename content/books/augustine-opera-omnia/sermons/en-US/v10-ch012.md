# Sermon 347: On the Fear of God

**1.** Many precepts have been given us, brothers, about the fear of God, and how useful it is to fear God the divine words have proclaimed innumerable times. From this most abundant store, attend willingly as I recall a few things, and discourse on them, as much as I shall be able for the brevity of the time. Who would not be glad to be wise, or, if he is not yet, desire to be? But what does Scripture say? *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^1]. Whom would it not delight to reign? But let us hear what the Spirit admonishes in the Psalm: *And now, kings, understand; be instructed, you who judge the earth; serve the Lord in fear, and exult before him with trembling*[^2]. Whence the Apostle too says: *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation*[^3]. We read also what is written: *You have desired wisdom; keep justice, and the Lord will furnish it to you*[^4]. For we find many most negligent of justice, and most avid for wisdom. These divine Scripture teaches that they cannot arrive at what they desire, except by keeping what they neglect. *Keep*, it says, *justice, and the Lord will furnish you* the wisdom *which you have desired*. But who can keep justice unless he fears God? For it says in another place: *For he who is without fear cannot be justified*[^5]. Further, if the Lord does not furnish wisdom except to one keeping justice, but he who is without fear cannot be justified, we run back to that sentence: *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*.

**2.** The prophet Isaiah too, when he commended those seven well-known spiritual gifts, beginning from wisdom, came down to the fear of God, as though descending from on high to us, so that he might teach us to ascend. From there, then, he began, where we wish to arrive; and there he arrived, from where we ought to begin. *The Spirit of God will rest upon him*, he says, *the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and fortitude, the Spirit of knowledge and piety, the Spirit of the fear of the Lord*[^6]. As, then, that one, not by failing but by teaching, descended from wisdom down to fear; so we, not by being proud but by advancing, must ascend from fear up to wisdom. *For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^7]. For this is the valley of weeping, of which the Psalm says: *He has set up ascents in his heart, in the valley of weeping*. For by the valley humility is signified. But who is humble, except one fearing God, and by that fear crushing his heart in the tears of confession and repentance? Because *a contrite and humbled heart God does not despise*[^8]. But let him not fear that he will remain in the valley. For in that very contrite and humbled heart, which God does not despise, he himself has set up the ascents by which we may rise toward him. For so the Psalm says: *He has set up ascents in his heart, in the valley of weeping, to the place which he has appointed*[^9]. Where do the ascents take place? *In the heart*, it says. But from where must one ascend? Surely from the valley of weeping. And to where must one ascend? *To the place*, it says, *which he has appointed*. What is that place, except of rest and peace? For there is that bright wisdom which never withers. Hence, to exercise us, by certain steps of teaching Isaiah descended from wisdom down to fear, that is, from the place of everlasting peace down to the valley of temporal weeping: so that we, in the confession of repentance, by grieving, groaning, weeping, may not remain in grief and groaning and weeping; but, ascending from this valley to the spiritual mountain, where the holy city Jerusalem our eternal mother is founded, may fully enjoy untroubled gladness. Therefore that one, having placed wisdom first, namely the unfailing light of the mind, joined understanding: as though answering those who ask from where one comes to wisdom: From understanding; from where to understanding: From counsel; from where to counsel: From fortitude; from where to fortitude: From knowledge; from where to knowledge: From piety; from where to piety: From fear. Therefore to wisdom from fear; because *the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*. From the valley of weeping up to the mountain of peace.

**3.** *For blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^10]. They are the humble in the valley, they in trembling sacrifice to God a contrite and humbled heart: from where they ascend to piety, so as not to resist his will, whether in his words, where they do not grasp his meaning, or in the very order and governance of creation, when most things happen otherwise than the private will of a man demands: for there it must be said: *But not what I will, but what you will, Father*[^11]. *For blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the land as an inheritance*[^12]: not the land of the dying, but the land of which it was said: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^13]. For from this piety they will merit the step of knowledge, so that they may know not only the evils of their past sins, over which in the first step of repentance they wept with grief, but also in what evil of this mortality and pilgrimage from the Lord they are, even when worldly happiness smiles. For this reason it is written: *He who adds knowledge, adds also grief*[^14]. *For blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted*[^15]. From there they rise to fortitude, so that the world is crucified to them, and they to the world, so that in the perversity of this life and the abundance of iniquity love does not grow cold; but that hunger and thirst for justice may be endured, until one comes to be filled with it in that immortality of the saints, and the fellowship of the angels. *For blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^16]. Nevertheless, on account of the restlessness of temptations, and what was said, *Woe to the world because of scandals*[^17], if perhaps some sins creep in little by little and stealthily, by which human weakness is overtaken, counsel ought not to be lacking. For in this mortal life that step of fortitude cannot avail so much that one who fights in continual struggle with a most cunning adversary is never struck: especially through the temptations of the tongue, where *if anyone says to his brother, Fool, he shall be liable to the fire of hell*[^18]. What, then, is the counsel, except what the Lord says: *Forgive, and it shall be forgiven you*[^19]? And therefore, as in the steps which we learn through Isaiah, counsel is fifth; so in the Gospel, in those praises of beatitude, it is placed in the fifth place: *Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy*[^20]. The sixth in Isaiah is understanding: where hearts are cleansed from all the falsehood of carnal vanity, that pure intention may be directed to the end. Therefore in the sixth place the Lord too said: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^21]. But when the end is reached, one now takes a stand, one now rests, one now triumphs in secure peace. And what is the end, except God-Christ? *For Christ is the end of the law, unto justice for everyone who believes*[^22]. And who is the wisdom of God, except Christ? And who is the Son of God, except Christ? In him, then, whoever become wise become so, and in him they become sons of God: and this is full and perpetual peace. Therefore, since in Isaiah wisdom is the seventh for those ascending upward, from where he himself began by teaching to descend to us; in the seventh place too the Lord, who lifts us up, placed: *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God*[^23]. Having these promises, then, and tending to the Lord by these steps, let us endure all the harsh and hard things of this world, nor let its cruelty break us, over which, once conquered, we shall rejoice in eternal peace. For the eighth sentence, the end now having been shown, exhorts us to this: *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of justice, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^24].
