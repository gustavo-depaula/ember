# Sermon 196: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1.** Today the birthday of our Lord Jesus Christ has shone forth festal for us. The birthday on which the day was born. And it is on this day, because from today the day grows. The nativities of our Lord Jesus Christ are two; one divine, the other human: both wonderful; that one without a female mother, this one without a male father. What the holy prophet Isaiah says, *His generation, who shall declare?*[^1], can be referred to both generations. Who can worthily declare God begetting? Who can worthily declare a virgin's childbearing? That one without a day, this one on a certain day: both beyond human estimation, and with great wonder. Attend to that first generation: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^2]. Whose Word? The Father's own. What Word? The Son himself. Never the Father without the Son. And yet he who is never without the Son begot the Son. And he begot, and did not begin. To one begotten without a beginning there is no beginning. And yet he is the Son, and yet begotten. A man is going to say: How begotten, and yet he has no beginning? If begotten, he has a beginning: if he has no beginning, how begotten? How, I do not know. Do you ask of a man how God was begotten? I labor at your question; but I appeal to the Prophet: *His generation, who shall declare?* Come with me to this human generation, come with me to this one, in which he *emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^3]; to see whether we may be able to grasp even this one, to see whether we may be able to say even something of this one. For who could grasp, *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^4]? Who could grasp this? Who could worthily think it? Whose mind would dare to search this out? Whose tongue would dare to pronounce it? Whose thought could grasp it? Meanwhile let us pass this over: it is too much for us. But that it might not be too much for us, *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men*[^5]. Where? In the Virgin Mary. From there, then, let us say something, if perhaps we can. The angel announces, the virgin hears, believes, and conceives. Faith in the mind, Christ in the womb. The virgin conceived; marvel: the virgin bore; marvel more: after the birth, she remained a virgin. This generation, then, who shall declare?

**2.** I say what may delight you, dearest ones. There are three lives in the Church among the members of Christ: married, widowed, virginal. Because these very lives, these very chastities, were to be in the holy members of Christ; all these three lives bore witness to Christ. The first, the married: when the virgin Mary conceived, Elizabeth, the wife of Zechariah, had herself also conceived; she bore in her womb the herald of this Judge. Holy Mary came to her, as to greet her kinswoman. The infant leaped in the womb of Elizabeth. He leaped, she prophesied[^6]. You have one bearing witness to married chastity. Where is the widowed? In Anna. You heard just now, when the Gospel was read, that there was a holy prophetess, a widow of eighty-four years, who had lived seven years with her husband; frequenting the temple of the Lord, serving in prayers night and day. And she too, a widow, recognized Christ. She saw the little one, recognized the great one. And she too bore witness[^7]. You have in her the widowed life. In Mary, the virginal. Let each one choose for himself of these three whichever he wishes. He who wishes to be apart from these does not order himself to be among the members of Christ. Let not married women say: We do not belong to Christ. Holy women had husbands. Let not virgins exalt themselves. The greater they are, let them humble themselves in all things[^8]. All the examples of salvation are set before our eyes. Let no one stray. Let no one be apart from a wife: it is better without a wife. If you seek married chastity, you have Susanna: if widowed, you have Anna: if virginal, you have Mary.

**3.** The Lord Jesus willed to be man for our sake. Let mercy not become cheap: Wisdom lies on the earth. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^9]. O food and bread of the angels: with you the angels are filled, with you they are satisfied, and do not grow weary; on you they live, by you they are wise, in you they are blessed. Where are you for my sake? In a cramped lodging, in swaddling clothes, in a manger. For whose sake? He who rules the stars sucks the breast: he fills the angels, speaks in the bosom of the Father, is silent in the bosom of the mother. But he will speak at the fitting age, fulfilling the Gospel for us. He will suffer for us, die for us, rise again as an example of our reward, ascend into heaven before the eyes of the disciples, come from heaven to judgment. Behold, he who lay in a manger was diminished, but did not lose himself: he received what he was not, but remained what he was. Behold, we have the infant Christ; let us grow with him.

**4.** Let this be enough for your Charity. Because I see many here on account of the solemnity, I must speak. The Kalends of January are coming. You are all Christians; God being favorable, the city is Christian. There are two kinds of men here, Christians and Jews. Let not those things be done which God hates: through play, iniquity; through jesting, wickedness. Let men not make themselves judges, lest they come into the hands of the true Judge. Hear, you are Christians, you are members of Christ. Think what you are, think at what price you were bought. Finally, if you wish to know what you do: I say it to those who do these things. Do not take it as an injury to yourselves, you to whom these things are displeasing: I speak to those who do them, and to whom they are pleasing. Do you wish to know what you do, and what sadness you bring upon us? Do the Jews do that? Even so, blush, that it may not be done. On the birthday of John, that is, six months earlier (for the herald and the Judge have that many months between them), from a superstitious pagan solemnity, Christians used to come to the sea and there baptize themselves. I was absent: but, as I have learned, the presbyters, moved by Christian discipline, gave certain people a worthy and ecclesiastical discipline. Men murmured at this, and some said: How much would it have cost to make it known to us? If we had been warned beforehand, we would not have done it. Had the presbyters themselves warned us beforehand, we would not have done it. Behold, the bishop warns you beforehand; I admonish, I proclaim, I declare. Let the bishop be heard commanding, let the bishop be heard admonishing, let the bishop be heard pleading, let the bishop be heard adjuring. I adjure you by him who was born today: I adjure, I bind you, let no one do it. I absolve myself. It is better that I be heard admonishing than felt grieving.
