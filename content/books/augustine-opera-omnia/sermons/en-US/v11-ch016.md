# Sermon 72: Augm. (Dolbeau 16)

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ admonished us to be good trees and to be able to have good fruits. For he says: *Either make the tree good, and its fruit good; or make the tree bad, and its fruit bad. For the tree is known by its fruit*[^1]. Where he says: *Make the tree good, and its fruit good,* this is surely not an admonition, but a healthful precept, to which obedience is necessary. But what he says: *Make the tree bad, and its fruit bad,* is not a precept, that you do it, but a warning, that you beware. For he said this against those who thought that they, being evil, could speak good things or have good works. This the Lord Jesus says cannot be. For the man must first be changed, that the works be changed. For if the man remains in what is evil, he cannot have good works; if he remains in what is good, he cannot have evil works.

**2.** But who was found good by the Lord, since Christ died for the impious? He found, then, all the trees bad. But he gave power to become sons of God to those believing in his name. Whoever, therefore, is today a good man, that is, a good tree, was found bad, and was made good. And if, when he found the trees bad, he had wished to root them out rather than that they remain, which would not have been worthy to be rooted out? But he came to dispense mercy first, that afterward he might exercise judgment, he to whom it is said: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, Lord*[^2]. He gave, then, to believers the remission of sins, he was unwilling to keep account with them about the past records. He gave the remission of sins: he made the trees good. He put off the axe: he gave security.

**3.** Of this axe John speaks, saying: *Now the axe is laid to the root of the trees. Every tree that does not bear good fruit will be cut down and cast into the fire*[^3]. Of this axe the householder threatens in the Gospel, saying: *See, it is three years that I come to this tree and find no fruit on it. Now I ought to clear the ground: therefore let it be cut down*[^4]. And the vinedresser intercedes: *Lord, let it be this year too: I will dig around it and apply a basket of dung; if it bears fruit, well; if not, you will come and cut it down*[^5]. As if through three years the Lord visited the human race, that is, in three certain times. The first time is before the law; the second, in the law; the third is now, which is the time of grace. For if he did not visit the human race before the law, whence Abel? whence Enoch? whence Noah? Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob? whose God he wished to be called, whose were all the nations, as though he were the God of three men: *I am,* he says, *the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob*[^6]. But in the law, if he did not visit, he would not give the law. After the law the householder himself too came: he suffered, died, and rose, gave the Spirit, made the Gospel preached throughout the whole world. And still a certain tree has remained unfruitful. There is still a certain part of the human race: it still does not correct itself. The vinedresser intercedes: the Apostle prays for the people: *I bend,* he says, *my knees for you to the Father, that, rooted and founded in love, you may be able to grasp with all the saints what is the breadth and length, the height and depth, and to know also the surpassing knowledge of the love of Christ, that you may be filled unto all the fullness of God*[^7]. By bending the knees, he intercedes for us before the householder, that we be not rooted out. Therefore it is necessary that he come: let him find us fruitful. The digging around the tree is the humility of the penitent: for every ditch is humble. And the basket of dung is the filth of the penitent: for what is filthier than dung? and yet, if you use it well, what is more fruitful?

**4.** Let each one, then, be a good tree. Let him not think that he can have good fruits, if he remains a bad tree. There will be no good fruit but of a good tree. Change the heart, and the work will be changed. Root out greed, plant love. For just as greed is the root of all evils, so love is the root of all good things. Why, then, do men mutter among themselves or contend: "What is good?" O if you knew what is! The good which you wish to have is not greatly good. The good which you do not wish to be, this is the great good! For do you wish to have health of body? Be it so, let it be a good. Yet it is not a great good which even an evil man can have. Do you wish to have gold and silver? See, this too I say: it is a good, but if you use it well; but you do not use it well, if you are evil. And through this, gold and silver to the evil is evil, to the good is good: not because gold and silver makes them good, but because, finding them good, it is turned to good use. Do you wish to have honor? It is a good, but this too if you use it well. To how many has honor been an occasion of ruin! to how many has honor been a service of good work!

**5.** Let us distinguish, then, these goods, if we can, because we are speaking of good trees. And there is nothing that anyone ought to think of, except to strive, as far as he can, to turn back from the rest by which the eyes are corrupted, to turn his eyes upon himself, to descend into himself, to examine himself, to inspect himself, to seek himself and find himself; and what displeases, let him kill, and what pleases, let him adopt, and what he adopts, let him plant. For when a man has found himself empty of the better goods, why is he greedy for exterior goods? See, what does a chest full of goods profit, with an empty conscience? You wish to have goods, and you do not wish to be good! Do you not see that you ought to blush at your goods, if your house is full of goods and has you, its owner, evil? For what is there that you wish to have evil? Tell me. Nothing at all: not a wife, not a daughter, not a son, not a servant, not a farm, not a tunic, finally not a boot; and yet you wish to have an evil life! I beg you, prefer your life to your boot. All the things that lie around your eyes, elegant and beautiful, are dear to you; and to yourself you are cheap and foul! If your goods could answer you, the goods with which your house is full, which you wished to have, which you trembled to lose, would they not cry out to you: "As you wish to have us good, so we too wish to have you a good master"? With a silent voice they plead against you before your Lord: "See, you gave such great goods to this man, and he himself is evil! What does it profit him to have what he has, when he does not have the one who gave all?"

**6.** He asks, then, admonished by these words of mine, and perhaps pricked, he asks what is good, what kind of good, whence one is good. You have understood well that you ought to ask this. I will answer the one who asks, and say: This is good, which you cannot lose unwillingly. For you can lose gold even unwilling; you can lose your house, your honors, you can lose the very health of the flesh. But the good by which you are good, you neither receive unwilling nor lose unwilling. I ask, then, what kind of good this is. The psalm indeed admonishes us of a great thing, perhaps what we are seeking. For it says: *Sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart?*[^8] How long that tree in the three years? *Sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart?* What is *heavy of heart?* *Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?*[^9] And after this it says what is to be sought: *Know that the Lord has magnified his holy one*[^10]. Now Christ too has come, now he has been magnified, now he has risen and ascended into heaven, now throughout the whole world his name is preached: *How long will you be heavy of heart?* Let past times suffice: now, with that holy one magnified, *how long will you be heavy of heart?* After the three years what remains, but the axe? *How long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?* Still vain things, still useless things, still pompous things, still fleeting things—thus, with Christ the holy one magnified, are these things still sought! Now truth cries out, and still vanity is sought! *How long will you be heavy of heart?*

**7.** Deservedly is this world strongly scourged: for the world has now come to know the words of the Lord. *And the servant,* he says, *not knowing the will of his lord and doing things worthy of stripes, will be beaten with few*[^11]. Why? That he may seek the will of his lord. The *servant,* then, *not knowing the will:* this was the world, before the Lord magnified his holy one: it was *a servant not knowing the will of his lord,* and therefore was beaten with few. But the *servant* now *knowing the will of his lord:* this is now, from the time the deity magnified his holy one, and not doing his will, *will be beaten with many.* What wonder, then, if the world is much beaten? It is a *servant knowing the will of the lord and doing things worthy of stripes.* Let it not, then, refuse to be beaten with many, since, if it is unwilling to hear the teacher unjustly, it will justly suffer the avenger; let it not murmur against the chastiser, when it sees itself worthy of stripes, that it may merit mercy.

**8.** &lt;...&gt; ...  
/ *beyond peace?* To those who here have peace in love, he will give them peace also; he will give them peace in immortality, not to those who say: "*Peace, peace,* and there is no peace[^12]. *Seek,* therefore, *peace and pursue it*[^13]." Thus, then, in this psalm *he has magnified his holy one.* Therefore there is one to hear us, because we have one who intercedes for us. So, when he had said: *The Lord has magnified his holy one,* he added: *The Lord will hear me, when I cry to him*[^14]. For now I have there an advocate, *who sits at the right hand of God and intercedes for us*[^15].

**9.** But because he does not hear so as to give full peace, except him who withdraws from evil and does good—and to withdraw from evil is to be angry with oneself, to be pricked on one's couch; and to do good is to sacrifice the sacrifice of justice; to seek peace is to hope in the Lord—when he had said: *Sacrifice the sacrifice of justice,* he added: *And hope in the Lord*[^16]. You have now been angry with yourself, you have now been pricked, you have now withdrawn from evil, you have now offered the sacrifice of justice still in hope in good works: one thing you do not yet hold, lest perhaps you say: "I have now done all these things and have as yet received nothing"—there remains for you the work of endurance. For *tribulation works patience, patience proof, proof hope. And hope does not confound*[^17]. Be secure: you will not be confounded. Hope in him. Now it is still as it were night: you do not yet see God nor hold what he promised, but in this night do what the psalm says: *In the day of my tribulation I sought God with my hands in the night before you, and I was not deceived*[^18]. *In the day of my tribulation:* reckon this whole life as the day of tribulation, and say: *I sought God with my hands,* not with my eyes. For it is still night. Seek with your hands, merit by your works, do good things and say that in this night you sought the Lord with your hands. There follows what follows for you too: *And I was not deceived.* By night I sought, by groping I sought, and yet I found. This is *I sought with my hands:* diligently and by works I sought. But how with those very *hands did I seek? Before him,* on account of that: *Do not do your justice before men*[^19], not that you avoid men's faces, but that you not seek your own praise. For if you avoid the faces of those watching when you do good things, where is that: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds?* But see what follows: *And they may glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^20]—that he may be glorified in you who made you good. For you were fit to make yourself bad, you are not fit to make yourself good. You were fit to wound yourself, you are not fit to heal yourself. Therefore when you do good works, wish this, that God may be glorified in you, because, when you have sacrificed the sacrifice of justice—that is, good works—it follows that you hope in the Lord.  
**10.** "And what shall I hope for? how long shall I hope?" Here do not; it is not of this earth what you seek. You wish to be happy, I know. For who does not? You seek a good thing, but you do not seek it in its own region. As if you heard that gold is good and cannot be found except under the earth, and you snatched up a hoe and dug in your house. Asked what you seek, you would answer: "Gold." But he who knows where gold is found would answer you: "You seek a good thing, but not where you ought." You seek to be happy, you wish to be full of joys, you wish to be satisfied with goods so that you want for nothing at all and nothing is lacking to you: a great thing, but it is not here. How you may arrive, I tell you. What shall I say, but what I said? Be angry with yourself, be pricked on your couch, change yourself. Sacrifice the sacrifice of justice and hope in the Lord. In him you will have what you seek; when you come to him, there you will have it, because you will have him himself, of whom it is said: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^21].

**11.** Finally, here too what follows? When he had said *be pricked*—that is, may it repent you that you were bad—*sacrifice the sacrifice of justice*—be fervent in good works—he added: *Hope in the Lord,* and he says: *Many say: "Who shows us good things?"*[^22] See, they have been pricked. "On my couch I was bad: I was angry with myself. I was a thief, I was a plunderer, I was this and that: I was angry with myself, I was pricked on my couch, I changed myself. Now I do good works: when I see a hungry man, I hold out bread; when I see a naked man, I clothe him; when I see a stranger, I take him in; when I see men quarreling, I reconcile them; when I see one oppressed, I redeem him; when I see a sick man, I visit him; when I see a dead man, I bury him: where is the good that was promised me? I have now amended myself, now I have changed, now corrected from evil works I do good ones: where are the things promised me?" *Many say*—would that few said it—*many say.* What do many say? "*Who shows us good things?*"—where is what was promised me? "I dispense my gold and see nothing. What I dispense, I see; what is promised me, I do not see." It is night. Seek: your hands will not fail, and you will not be deceived. *Many say: "Who shows us good things?"*

**12.** What was he going to say, the one to whom they were saying: "*Who shows us good things?*" And what is the good itself? *The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord*[^23]. See the good. *The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord.* We were made to the image of God, and we are not re-engraved except by him who made us, nor re-formed except by him who created us. Therefore *the light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord,* that we may be your coin and be stored in your treasury. *The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord.* This is the coin of Christ: the face of our emperor is stamped on us. This coin Christ as a tax-collector sought, when to those slandering him about the tribute he said, as they showed the coin and answered that it had the image of Caesar: *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's and to God the things that are God's*[^24]. Caesar seeks his own image in your gold; will God not seek his in your soul? See what the good things are. Why do *many* still *say: "Who shows us good things?" How long will you be heavy of heart?*[^25] You seek good things in which you may fear many things. You seek transitory good things, you seek temporal good things, you seek good things by which, living, you are sent away, or, dying, you let them go. *How long,* then, *will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?* Do you wish me to show you a good? Have it, and you will know. It can be had in the heart, it cannot be shown to the eyes.

**13.** Finally, see what follows *the light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord: You have given,* he says, *gladness in my heart*[^26]. *In my heart,* not in my storeroom, not in my chest, but *in my heart.* See that *the light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord.* For one acquires gold too and puts it in his chest: his heart rejoices, but over vanity, not over truth. Do you wish to see how vainly he rejoices who so rejoices and who supposes the false to be true? This man has a full chest: he goes out from his house; wherever he turns, he rejoices, because his chest is full. Another had a full chest and lost by a thief what he had: he does not know it, he still rejoices; he does not know it has perished, he still rejoices. How was that yours which was not in you? See, now it perishes, and you still rejoice. You feed on empty hopes. Whence, but because you seek *vanity? Why?* To what good, with what fruit? See, you have lost it, even though you loved what you lost. And how have you perished? Return, then, to the highest good, that you may be able to say: "*The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord.* We have been made your coins: gather us to your treasuries, and we render the tribute of Caesar, ourselves to you, to Caesar what you gave, to you what you made, to you who made us. *You have given gladness in my heart:* there is that from which I rejoice, the good is within, it is stored in the inner chest." This you cannot lose unwilling; there is he himself who made it, delighted in his temple, because you were pricked on your couch.

**14.** Finally, after he said: *The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord; you have given gladness in my heart,* he looked back at those many who were saying: "*Who shows us good things?*", and said: *From the time of their grain, wine, and oil they have been multiplied*[^27]—their grain, their wine, their oil. These things they attended to and coveted earthly things. And when it was well with them, they were filled with these earthly things—*they were multiplied*—*they called the people happy who have these things.* But you, what do you say? *Happy is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^28]. Because *the light of your countenance is sealed upon us, Lord.* But those, *multiplied from the time of their grain, wine, and oil,* let them withdraw from me; let those who love such things not say to me: "*Who shows us good things?*" There is what I would show, but there are not those to whom I would show it. Therefore *from the time of their grain, wine, and oil they have been multiplied.*

**15.** You, what? *In peace, in the selfsame.* What is *in the selfsame?* What is not changed, what is not variable, what is not temporal, what is not now thus, now otherwise. *In the selfsame I will sleep*[^29]. It is difficult to explain what I feel about what he said: *I will sleep in the selfsame.* *I will sleep in peace: in the selfsame I will sleep.* He who sleeps in a manner has a holiday from the senses of the flesh. Why, then, do you seek outside, for your blessed life, forms and colors for your eyes, songs and sweet melodies for your ears, various flavors for your taste, various pleasures for your touch? Not such is the good by which you are good, not such the good of which you said: "*Who will show us good things?*" Within, in the image of God, is the good, whence is formed this good *which neither eye has seen nor ear heard*[^30]. Therefore sleep, take yourself away from the desire of the eyes, do not converse with the senses of the flesh, that your Lord may say to you: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^31]. Therefore *in peace, in the selfsame I will sleep and rest*[^32].

**16.** Whence does one come there? *For you, Lord, have made me dwell singularly in hope*[^33]. This which was being said: *Hope in the Lord,* was said with iniquity now strained off, with the sacrifice of justice now offered. *Hope in the Lord,* but *you have made me dwell singularly in hope.* What is *singularly?* Not like the *many* who *say:* "*Who will show us good things?*", coveting many things and dissipated by various pleasures—one seeks this, another that, they do not seek one thing. From another's evil one wishes his own good. The one cannot grow rich, unless the other comes to want. The bigger fish cannot be satisfied, unless the smaller fish is devoured. Those who seek these goods do not seek goods, because they seek non-goods. *Singularly,* then, you will receive what the impious do not receive. O you singular man—the whole body of Christ—O you singular man—the body whose head is Christ—dwell *singularly in hope,* do not seek the goods of the nations. *For all these things the nations seek. Seek first the kingdom and the justice of God*[^34]—this is the sign of the countenance of God. Seek this in yourself and, if need be, *all these things will be added to you*[^35]. Does he feed the robber and will he forsake you? *The earth is the Lord's and its fullness*[^36].

**17.** Do not you withdraw from your God, love your God. To whom you say: "Give me this and give me that," say to him sometimes: "Give me yourself." If you love, love freely, do not be an unchaste soul. Your wife would not please you if she loved your gold, if she loved you for this reason, that you gave gold, that you gave a good tunic, that you gave a most excellent farm, a choice servant, a handsome eunuch, because, if she loved these things in you, she would not love you. Do not rejoice in such a love: an adulterer can often give more. You wish your wife to love you freely, and do you wish to sell your faith to God? "Because I believe in you," you say to your God, "give me gold." Do you not blush? "Because I believe," you say, "give me gold." You have pledged your faith: see its price. It is not worth so much, it is not worth gold or silver, your faith is not worth so much; it has a great price: God himself is its price. Love him and love him freely. For if you love him for the sake of something else, you do not love him. You ought not to wish him for the sake of something else, but whatever else you wish you ought to wish for his sake, so that all things are referred to the love of him, not he referred to other loves, but he preferred to other loves. Love him, love him freely. To this that great athlete was provoked by a great adversary saying: "*Does Job worship God for nothing?*[^37]"

**18.** Therefore if you wish a good, when you say: "*Who shows us good things?*", God himself is, who made you good and who makes you better, he himself is good by no other good, but good by himself. For to you, that you be good, another good comes; to him, that he be good, no good comes. For he himself is good to himself by himself. Commending this great good, the Lord says: "*Why do you ask me about the good? No one is good but God alone*[^38]." Are there not other good things? Is it not written: *God made all things very good?* But all these things are good through God. But God is not good through these, nor is he good through other things which have goods, but he is the good of goods. See what kind of good: seek it. *Why do you still love vanity and seek a lie?* See what good: seek the good of goods. You seek riches: it is not singular. Attend: *You have made me dwell singularly in hope.* Why is it not singular? Both the good and the bad have it. You seek health of body: both the good have it and the bad, both men and beasts, it is not singular. You seek honors: even the bad attain them, it is not singular. That is singular which only the good will have. To the good here God gives goods, and to the bad; he himself gives evils both to the good and to the bad: to the one for torment, to the other for exercise. Evils are common to the good and the bad. The singular evil, the bad eternal fire; the singular good, the good eternal God. For whatever else there is of creatures, God gives those even to his enemies: earth and heaven; they use the lights of heaven, the fruits of the earth, health of body, the honors of the world, his enemies use them. It is a creature: what he created he gives them. He who created keeps himself for you, but if you love freely. For the time will come when he gives himself to you, and to the impious it will be said: *Let the impious be taken away, that he see not the glory of God*[^39]. Therefore, brethren, let us love God freely, let us do good things, let us endure evils, let us hope in him, that someday, with himself given back, we may be satisfied with him alone, who lives and reigns for immortal ages. Amen.
