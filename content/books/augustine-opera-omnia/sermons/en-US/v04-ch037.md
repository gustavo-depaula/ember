# Sermon 121

**1.** Christ, then, *was in the world, and the world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^1]. Which world was made through him, which world did not know him? For the world that was made through him did not itself fail to know him. What is the world that was made through him? Heaven and earth. How did heaven not know him, when in his passion the sun was darkened, how did the earth not know him, when at his hanging it trembled [^2]? But *the world did not know him* whose prince is the one of whom it was said: *Behold, the prince of this world comes, and finds nothing in me* [^3]. Evil men are called "the world," unbelieving men are called "the world." From this they received the name, from what they love. By loving God, we are made gods. So by loving the world, we are called "the world." But *God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself* [^4]. *The world*, then, *did not know him*, all of it surely?

**2.** *He came into his own, and his own did not receive him* [^5]. *Into his own* . . . All things are his, but those are called properly his own from whom his mother was, from whom he had taken flesh, to whom he had sent ahead the heralds of his coming, to whom he had given the law, whom he had freed from the slavery of Egypt, whose carnal father he chose, Abraham. Because he spoke truly: *Before Abraham, I am* [^6]. Nor did he say thus: Before Abraham was, I am, or: Before Abraham was, I was made, or: Before Abraham was, I am. For *in the beginning was the Word* [^7], it was not made. Therefore: *He came into his own, and his own did not receive him*.

**3.** *But as many as received him . . .* [^8]. For surely there the Apostles who received him, there those who carried branches before his mount, who went before and followed and spread their garments and said with a loud voice [^9]: *Hosanna to the Son of David, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!* Then the Pharisees said to him [^10]: *Restrain the boys, let them not cry these things to you*. And he: *If these are silent, the stones will cry out* [^11]. He saw us when he said these things: *If these are silent, the stones will cry out*. What stones except those who worship stones? If the little Jews are silent, the nations, great and small, will cry out. What stones except those of whom this John speaks, who came *to bear witness to the light* [^12]? For when he saw those Jews priding themselves on the race of Abraham, he said to them: *Brood of vipers*. They called themselves sons of Abraham and he said to them: *Brood of vipers*. Did he do wrong to Abraham? Far be it! From their conduct he gave the name, who, if they were sons of Abraham, would imitate Abraham. As he himself too says to those who say to him: *We are free and have never served anyone, we have Abraham for father*. And he: *If you were sons of Abraham, you would do the deeds of Abraham. You want to kill me, because I tell you the truth, this Abraham did not do* [^13]. From him you were born but you have degenerated. So what does John say? *Brood of vipers, who showed you to flee from the wrath to come*? Because they came to be baptized with John's baptism unto repentance. *Who showed you to flee from the wrath to come? Bear, then, fruit worthy of repentance and do not say in your hearts: We have Abraham for father. God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham* [^14]. *God is able from these stones* which he saw in spirit; he was speaking to them and foreseeing us. *For God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*. From which stones: *if these are silent, the stones will cry out*. Now you have heard and you cry out. It is fulfilled: *the stones will cry out*. For we come from the nations. In our forefathers we adored stones, therefore we were called dogs. Recall what that woman heard who cried out after the Lord because she was a Canaanite, a worshiper of idols, a handmaid of demons. What did Jesus say? *It is not good to take the bread of the children and throw it to dogs* [^15]. Have you never noticed how dogs lick anointed stones? So are all worshipers of images. But grace has come to you. *But as many as received him, he gave them power to become sons of God*. Behold, you have now the newly born: *He gave them power to become sons of God*. To whom did he give it? *To those who believe in his name* [^16].

**4.** And how do they become sons of God? *Who were born not of bloods, not of the will of man, nor of the will of the flesh, but of God* [^17]. Attend, then; these were born of God, having received power to become sons of God. They were born of God, not of bloods. What is the first birth? What is the wretched birth, coming from wretched ones? But those who were born of God, what was that from which they were first born? Mingled of bloods, of the bloods of male and female, of the mingling of the flesh of male and female, thence they were born. Now from what were they *born of God?* The first birth from male and female, the second birth from God and the Church.

**5.** Behold, *they were born of God*. How did it come about that they were born of God who first were born of men? How did it come about that *he dwelt among us* [^18]? A great change; he made flesh, they spirit. What is this? What condescension, my brothers! Lift up the mind to hoping for and grasping better things. Do not give yourselves over to worldly desires. *You were bought with a price* [^19]; for your sake *the Word was made flesh* [^20], for your sake he who was the Son of God was made the Son of man, that you who were sons of men might become sons of God. What was he? What was he made? What were you? What were you made? He was the Son of God who was made the Son of man. You were sons of men who are made sons of God. He shared evils with us and is to give us goods. But by that very thing by which he was made the Son of man, he differs from us. We are sons of men through the desire of the flesh. He is the Son of man through the faith of the Virgin. The mother of any man lay with a man and conceived. But each one is born of a man his father and of a woman his mother. But Christ was born of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary. He came to us but did not much depart from himself. Rather, from himself, in that he was God, he never departed but added what was of our nature. For there came to be what was not, he did not lose what he was, made the Son of man, but did not cease to be the Son of God. Through this he is the Mediator in the middle. What is: in the middle? Neither above nor below. How neither above nor below? Not above because flesh, not below because not a sinner, but yet insofar as God, always above. For he did not so come to us that he forsook the Father; he went from us, he went and did not forsake us; he will come to us and will not forsake him.
