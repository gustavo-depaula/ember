# Sermon 85: On the Words of the Gospel, Matthew 19:17-25: "If You Wish to Come to Life, Keep the Commandments," and the Rest

**1. 1.** The Gospel reading, brothers, which just now sounded in our ears, desires a hearer and a doer rather than an expositor. What is clearer than this light: *If you wish to come to life, keep the commandments*[^1]? What, then, am I to say? *If you wish to come to life, keep the commandments.* Who is there who does not want life? And yet who is there who wants to keep the commandments? If you do not want to keep the commandments, why do you seek life? If you are lazy toward the work, why do you hasten toward the reward? That rich young man said he had kept the commandments; he heard greater precepts: *If you wish to be perfect, one thing you lack; go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor;* nor will you lose it, but *you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^2]. For what does it profit you, if you do this and do not follow me? But he went away sad and grieving, as you heard; for he had many riches. What that man heard, we too have heard. The mouth of Christ is the Gospel. He sits in heaven, but he does not cease to speak on earth. Let us not be deaf, for he cries out. Let us not be dead, for he thunders. If you do not want to do the greater things, do the lesser. The burden of the greater is too much for you—at least take hold of the lesser. Why are you lazy toward both? Why are you opposed to both? The greater are: *Sell all that you have, and give to the poor, and follow me*[^3]. The lesser are: *You shall not commit murder, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not bear false witness, You shall not steal. Honor your father and mother, You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^4]. Do these. Why do I cry out to you to sell your things, when I cannot wring from you that you not seize the things of others? You heard: *You shall not steal;* you rob. Before the sight of so great a Judge, I now hold you not as a thief, but as a robber. Spare yourself, have mercy on yourself. This life still gives you a reprieve; do not cast away the rebuke. You were a thief yesterday; do not be one today. Now perhaps you have been one even today; tomorrow do not be one. Sometime end the evil, and demand the good as a reward. You want to have good things, and you do not want to be good: your life is contrary to your prayers. If it is a great good to have a good estate, how great an evil is it to have a bad soul?

**2. 2.** The rich man went away saddened, and the Lord said: *How hard it is that one who has riches should enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^5]! And how hard it would be, he showed by setting forth a comparison, to be so hard as to be altogether impossible. For everything impossible is hard; but not everything hard is impossible. Of what kind the hardness is, attend to the comparison: *Amen I say to you, it is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^6]. A camel to enter through the eye of a needle: if he had said a flea, it would be impossible. Then, hearing this, the disciples were saddened, and said: *If it is so, who then can be saved*[^7]? Which of the rich? You poor, hear Christ; I am speaking to the people of God. You are more numerous, the poor; at least take hold of this; and yet hear. Whoever of you glory in poverty, beware of pride, lest humble rich men outdo you; beware of impiety, lest godly rich men outdo you; beware of drunkenness, lest sober rich men outdo you. Do not glory in poverty, if they ought not to glory in riches.

**3. 3.** Let the rich hear, if indeed there are any; let them hear the Apostle: *Charge the rich of this world*[^8]—for there are rich of another age. The poor are rich of another age, the Apostles are rich of another age, who said: *As having nothing, and possessing all things*[^9]. That you may know of what rich he speaks, he added, *of this world.* Let the rich of this world, then, hear the Apostle: *Charge,* he says, *the rich of this world not to be proud*[^10]. The first worm of riches is pride. A bad moth, it gnaws everything, and reduces it even to ashes. *Charge,* then, *not to be proud, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*[^11]; lest perhaps you go to sleep rich and rise poor. *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches* (these are the Apostle's words); *but in the living God,* he says. A thief takes your gold from you; who takes God from you? What does the rich man have, if he does not have God? What does the poor man not have, if he has God? *Not,* then, *to hope in riches,* he says; *but in the living God, who furnishes us abundantly with all things to enjoy*[^12]—and with all those things, himself as well.

**3. 4.** If, then, they ought not to hope in riches, nor to trust in them, but in the living God, what are they to do with their riches? Hear what: *Let them be rich in good works*[^13]. What is this? Expound, Apostle. For many refuse to understand what they refuse to do. Expound, Apostle: do not give occasion for a bad work through the obscurity of your speech. Say what you have said: *Let them be rich in good works.* Let them hear, let them understand; let them not be permitted to excuse themselves, but rather let them begin to accuse themselves, and to say what we heard just now in the Psalm: *For I acknowledge my sin*[^14]. Tell what it is:

**4. 4.** What is *let them give readily?* Is this too not understood? *Let them give readily, let them share*[^15]. You have, another does not have: share, that it may be shared with you. Share here, and you will share there. Share bread here, and you will receive bread there. What bread here? The bread you gather with sweat and toil, from the curse of the first man. What bread there? He who said: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^16]. You are rich here, but you are poor there. You have gold, but you do not yet hold Christ present. Pay out what you have, that you may receive what you do not have. *Let them be rich in good works, let them give readily, let them share.*

**4. 5.** So are they to lose their possessions? *Let them share,* he said; not, Let them give all. Let them keep for themselves what is enough, let them keep more than enough. Let us give some part of it. What part? A tenth part. The scribes and Pharisees gave tithes. Let us be ashamed, brothers; they gave tithes, for whom Christ had not yet shed his blood. The scribes and Pharisees gave tithes; lest perhaps you think you are doing something great because you break bread for a poor man—and this is scarcely a thousandth of your means. And yet I do not reproach you: at least do this. So thirsty am I, so hungry, that I rejoice even at these crumbs. But still I will not be silent about what the living one said, who died for us. *Unless your righteousness abounds,* he says, *beyond that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^17]. He does not stroke us; he is a physician, he reaches all the way to the living flesh. *Unless your righteousness abounds more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.* The scribes and Pharisees gave tithes. What of it? Question yourselves. See what you do, out of how much you do it; what you give, what you leave for yourselves; what you expend on mercy, what you reserve for luxury. So: *Let them give readily, let them share, let them treasure up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^18].

**5. 6.** I have admonished the rich: hear, you poor. You, do not rob; you, do not seize. You, bestow your means; you, bridle your desires. Hear, you poor, that same apostle: *But there is great gain.* Gain is the acquisition of profit. *But there is great gain,* he says, *in godliness with sufficiency*[^19]. You have the world in common with the rich; you do not have a house in common with the rich; but you have heaven in common, light in common. Seek sufficiency, seek what is enough, want no more. The rest weigh down, do not lift up; they burden, do not honor. *Great gain, godliness with sufficiency.* First of all, *godliness.* Godliness is the worship of God. *Godliness with sufficiency. For we brought nothing into this world*[^20]. Did you bring anything here? But neither did you, the rich, bring anything. You found everything here, naked you were born along with the poor. The infirmity of the body is common to both; common is the wailing, witness of our miseries. *For we brought nothing into this world* (he speaks to the poor); *but neither can we carry anything away. Having food and covering, with these let us be content. For those who want to become rich*[^21]—*those who want to become*; not, Those who are. For let those who are, be so. As to what pertains to them, they have heard, that *they be rich in good works, give readily, share.*

**6. 7.** These have heard. You who are not yet rich, hear. *Those who want to become rich fall into temptation and a snare, and many harmful desires*[^22]. Do you not fear? Hear what follows: *which plunge men into ruin and perdition.* Do you not fear? *For greed is the root of all evils*[^23]. Greed is to want to be rich, not already to be rich. That is greed itself. Do you not fear to be plunged into ruin and perdition? Do you not fear greed, the root of all evils? From your field you root out the root of thorns, and do you not root out from your heart the root of evil desires? You cleanse your field, that your belly may take fruit from it; and do you not cleanse your heart, where your God may dwell? *For greed is the root of all evils: which some, following after, have strayed from the faith, and entangled themselves in many sorrows*[^24].

**6. 7.** You have heard what you are to do, you have heard what you are to fear, you have heard whereby the kingdom of heaven is bought, you have heard whereby the kingdom of heaven is hindered. Be all in concord in the word of God. God made both the rich man and the poor man. Scripture says: *The rich and the poor have met one another; the Lord made them both*[^25]. *The rich and the poor have met one another.* On what road, if not in this life? A rich man was born, a poor man was born. You have met one another walking the road together. You, do not oppress; you, do not defraud. The one is in need, the other has. *The Lord made them both.* Through the one who has, he helps the needy; through the one who has not, he proves the one who has. We have heard, we have spoken: let us fear, let us beware, let us pray, let us arrive.
