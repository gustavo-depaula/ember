# Sermon 104

**1.** When the holy Gospel was being read, we heard that the Lord was received into hospitality by a devout woman, and she was called Martha. And while she herself was occupied in the care of serving, her sister Mary sat at the Lord's feet and heard his word. The one labored, the other was at leisure; the one dispensed, the other was filled. Yet Martha, laboring much in that occupation and business of serving, appealed to the Lord, and complained about her sister, that she did not help her laboring. But the Lord answered Martha on Mary's behalf; and he himself became her advocate, he who had been appealed to as judge. *Martha*, he says, *you are occupied about many things, whereas one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken from her* [^1]. We have heard both the appeal to the judge, and the sentence; which sentence answered the one who appealed, and defended the one who was accused. For Mary was intent on the sweetness of the Lord's word. Martha was intent on how she might feed the Lord; Mary intent on how she might be fed by the Lord. By Martha a banquet was being prepared for the Lord, in whose banquet Mary was already taking delight. So when Mary was sweetly hearing the most sweet word, and was being fed with a most intent heart, the Lord being appealed to by her sister, how must we suppose she feared, lest the Lord should say to her: Rise, help your sister? For she was held by a wondrous sweetness, which is surely greater of the mind than of the belly. She was excused, she sat more secure. But how was she excused? Let us attend, let us look, let us examine what we can; let us too be fed.

**2.** For what? Do we suppose that Martha's service was reproved—she whom the care of hospitality had occupied, who had received the Lord himself into hospitality? How was she rightly reproved, who rejoiced in so great a guest? If this is true, let men leave off what they minister to the needy; let them choose for themselves the better part, which will not be taken from them. Let them be at leisure for the word, let them long for the sweetness of doctrine, let them be occupied about saving knowledge; let them have no care who is a stranger in the village, who needs bread, who clothing, who is to be visited, who redeemed, who buried; let works of mercy be at leisure, let knowledge alone be pursued. If it is the better part, why do we not all seize it, when we have the Lord himself as our patron in this cause? For we do not fear in this matter that we offend his justice, when we hold his sentence as patron.

**3.** And yet it is not so; but as the Lord said, so it is. In the way you understand it, it is not so; but it is so, in the way you ought to understand. Behold, attend. *You are occupied about many things, whereas one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the better part* [^2]; not you the bad, but she the better. Why the better? Because you about many things, she about one. The one is preferred to the many. For the one is not from the many, but the many from the one. Many are the things that were made; one is he who made them. Heaven, earth, sea, and all that is in them, how many they are! Who would number them? who would conceive their multitude? Who made these? *And God made all things, and behold, very good* [^3]. Very good the things he made: how much better is he who made them? Let us attend, then, to our occupations about many things. Service is necessary for those who will refresh bodies. Why this? Because there is hunger, because there is thirst. Mercy is necessary for misery. You break bread for the hungry, because you have found one hungry. Take away hunger, if you can: for whom do you break bread? Take away pilgrimage: to whom do you show hospitality? Take away nakedness: for whom do you prepare a garment? Let there be no sickness: whom do you visit? Let there be no captivity: whom do you redeem? Let there be no quarrel: whom do you reconcile? Let there be no death: whom do you bury? In that future age these evils will not be: therefore neither these services. Rightly, then, Martha was about the bodily—I know not what to call it, necessity, or will, or will of necessity?—of the Lord; she was serving mortal flesh. But who was in mortal flesh? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^4]. Behold what Mary heard. *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us* [^5]. Behold whom Martha served. Therefore *Mary chose the better part, and it will not be taken from her*; for she chose what will always remain: therefore *it will not be taken from her*. She wanted to be occupied about one thing; she already held it: *But for me, to cling to God is good* [^6]. She sat at the feet of our head; the more humbly she sat, the more she received. For water flows together to the lowness of the valley; it runs down from the swellings of the hill. So the Lord did not reprove the work, but distinguished the gift. *You are occupied*, he says, *about many things; but one thing is necessary*. Mary has now chosen this for herself; the labor of multiplicity passes, and the love of unity remains. Therefore what she chose *will not be taken from her*; but from you, what you chose—this surely follows, this is understood—from you, what you chose, will be taken away. But it will be taken away for your good, that what is better may be given. For labor will be taken from you, that rest may be given. You sail, she is already in the harbor.

**4.** You see, then, dearly beloved, and, as I judge, you now understand, in these two women, who were both pleasing to the Lord, both lovable, both disciples; you see, then, and understand something great—whoever of you understand—which you ought to hear and know, even you who do not understand; in these two women two lives are figured, the present and the future, the laborious and the restful, the toilsome and the blessed, the temporal and the eternal. There are two lives, which I have described as briefly as I could: now think on them more copiously yourselves. What this life has—I do not say a bad, unjust, wicked, luxurious, impious life; but a laborious one, full of toils, chastened by fears, anxious with temptations; this very innocent life I mean, such as it was fitting for Martha to have—this, then, as far as you can, examine, and on this, as I said, think more copiously than we speak. But the wicked life was absent from that house, it was neither with Martha nor with Mary; and if it ever was, it fled when the Lord entered. There remained, then, in that house which had received the Lord, in the two women two lives; both innocent, both praiseworthy; one laborious, the other at leisure; neither criminal, neither slothful. Both innocent, both, I say, praiseworthy; but one laborious, as I said, the other at leisure; neither criminal, which the laborious must beware; neither slothful, which the leisurely must beware. There were, then, in that house these two lives, and the very fountain of life. In Martha was the image of present things, in Mary of things to come. What Martha did, there we are; what Mary did, this we hope for; let us do this well, that we may have that fully. For what do we have of it, in what measure do we have it, as long as we are here? How much is it that we have of it? What is it that we have of it? For even now something of it is done. Withdrawn from business, household cares set aside, you have gathered, you stand, you hear; in as much as you do this, you are like Mary. And you do what Mary does more easily than I what Christ does. Yet if I say anything of Christ, for that reason it feeds you, because it is Christ's, because it is the common bread, from which I too live, if I live. *But now we live, if you stand fast in the Lord* [^7]; not in us, but in the Lord; because *neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase* [^8].

**5.** Yet how much is it that, by hearing and understanding, you take and grasp of that life, whose image Mary bore—how much is this? Let this night of the world pass. *In the morning I will stand before you, and I will contemplate* [^9]. *To my hearing you will give joy and gladness, and the humbled bones will exult* [^10]. Humbled bones, like the limbs of one clinging. This Mary did: she was humbled, and was filled. She was sitting; what, then, is it that I said: *In the morning I will stand and contemplate?* How does she sit like one standing, if morning signifies the future age? The night of the present age being past: *I will stand*, he says, *and I will see, I will stand and contemplate*. He did not say: I will sit. How does Mary, sitting, bear the image of so great a thing, if it is, *I will stand and contemplate?* Do not let these things trouble you, it is the poverty of the flesh; both cannot be required of the body, that at the same time, at one moment, it both stand and sit. For if it sits, it does not stand; if it stands, it does not sit; the body cannot do both at once. But if I prove that the mind can, will there be any cause for us to doubt? Because if even now it can do something of the sort, much more easily then will man be able, every difficulty being ended. Behold, from where you may take an example. Paul himself says: *Now we live, if you stand fast in the Lord* [^11]. So great an Apostle, rather Christ through the Apostle, commanded us to stand. How does that same Apostle likewise, rather Christ himself through the Apostle, say to us: *Yet in what we have attained, let us walk in it* [^12]? Here to stand, here to walk; it is too little to walk, *so run, that you may attain* [^13]. Attend, then, dearly beloved, and understand; he commands us both to walk, and commands us to stand; not that, when we stand, we should cease to walk, or, when we walk, we should cease to stand; but both at once, let us both stand and run. What is it: both stand and run? Both remain and make progress. *Make your ways, Lord, known to me* [^14]. Surely, by the ways of the Lord being made known, what are we commanded, except to walk? *Lead me, Lord, in your way* [^15]; what do we wish, except to walk? And again, as though to be fixed in one place: *Do not give my feet to be moved* [^16]. And rejoicing in another place and giving thanks: *And he did not give my feet to movement* [^17]. If it were said to him: How did you wish the ways of the Lord to be made known to you, how did you wish to be led by him in his way, and you wish your feet not to be moved, and you give thanks that your feet were not given to movement? how did you walk, who did not move your feet? he would answer you: I both walked, because I acted; and I stood, because I did not depart. Do not wonder, then, brothers; behold, what the body cannot do, the mind can. According to the body, when you walk, you do not stand; when you stand, you do not walk; according to the mind, according to faith, according to the intentness of the mind, both stand and walk, both remain and make progress, because *Now we live, if you stand fast in the Lord*, and *so run, that you may attain*. So then, dearly beloved, you will both sit and stand. We shall sit, because we shall humbly gaze on the Creator; we shall stand, because we shall remain forever.

**6.** For I add more: we shall also recline, which surely is neither to sit nor to stand. We shall recline. I would not dare to say it, unless the Lord promised it: *He will make them recline*. Promising a great reward to his servants: *He will make them recline*, he says, *and passing by he will minister to them* [^18]. This life is promised us, that the Lord will make us recline, and passing by will minister to us. This too was said, when the centurion's faith was marveled at and praised: *Amen I say to you, that many will come from East and West, and will recline with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven* [^19]. A great promise, a happy repayment. Let us act so as to deserve it, let us be helped that we may be able to come there, where, as we recline, the Lord will minister to us. For what will it be to recline, except to rest? and what will it be to minister, except to feed? What is that food? what is that drink? Surely Truth itself. That food refreshes, and does not fail: it feeds, and by feeding makes whole; nor is it consumed in the one it feeds, but remaining whole, it makes whole. Do you not believe that God can feed thus, when even now your eye is thus fed by this light? Your eye is fed with light. Let many see, it is so great; let few see, it is so great; and those eyes are refreshed, and it itself does not fail. It takes from it, and does not diminish it; it plucks, and does not cut it off. Can this light do this for the eye, and God not be able to do it for changed man? He can, clearly he can; why do you not yet grasp it? Because you are occupied about many things; Martha's business has occupied you, rather all of us. For who is at leisure from this service of helping? who breathes free from this care? Let us do this innocently, let us do it in love; that too will come, that we should recline, and he passes by, and will minister to us. For he would not then minister to us, unless he passed over from here to the Father; for he was here, when he was promising. And lest we should think he would display to us something such as the form of servant we were contemplating: *Passing by*, he says, *he will minister to them*. And the Evangelist concerning this passing over: *But when the hour had come, that Jesus should pass over from this world to the Father* [^20]. *So long a time*, he says, *I am with you, and you have not known me* [^21]? If he knew what he heard, he would answer: I have not known you, because you have not yet passed over. Whence also to Mary it is said after the resurrection: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father* [^22].

**7.** Therefore, dearly beloved, I beseech you, I exhort you, I admonish, I charge, I ask, let us together desire that life, let us run together by coming to it, that we may stand fast in it by persevering. The hour comes, and that hour will be without end, when the Lord will make us recline, and will minister to us. What, except himself? Why do you ask what you may eat? You have the Lord himself. For what will there be from which we may be fed, what, except *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^23]? What will it be to recline, except to rest? what will it be to be fed, except to be delighted ineffably by his contemplation? *Delight is at your right hand* [^24]. *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek*; not many things, about which I am occupied: but *one thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord* [^25]. This is not the blessedness of those who labor. *Be still and see*: what? *that I am the Lord* [^26]. A great vision, a happy contemplation. But what else is it: Recline and eat, except: Be still and see? Therefore let us not be carnally minded, nor think, so to speak, of luxurious banquets. These things will pass: they are to be endured, not loved. If you want to fulfill in them the office of Martha, let moderation be present, let mercy be present; moderation in tempering, mercy in dispensing. Labor passes, and rest will come; but to rest only through labor. The ship passes, and comes into the homeland; but to the homeland only through the ship. For we sail, if we attend to the waves and storms of this world. Nor do I doubt that we are not drowned for this reason, that we are carried by the wood of the cross.
