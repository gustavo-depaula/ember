# Sermon 225: On the Day of Easter

**1. 1.** The Son of God, born of the Father without time, commends to us the loftiness of the divine grace toward us. For what was he before he was in man? Suppose you had asked and said. For let us suppose, my brothers, before Christ was born of the virgin Mary, was he, or was he not? Suppose we ask, where it is not permitted to doubt. To these thoughts the Lord himself answered, when it was said to him: *You are not yet fifty years old, and have you seen Abraham?* For he answered, and said: *Amen, amen, I say to you, before Abraham was made, I am*[^1]. Therefore he was, but man he was not yet. Lest perhaps anyone say, He was an angel; the holy Gospel told you that Christ was. And do you ask what he was? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God.* Behold what he was: *In the beginning was the Word.* The Word was not made in the beginning, but *was the Word*. But of this world hear what the Scripture says: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^2]. Do you ask through what he made it? *In the beginning was the Word*, through which heaven and earth were made. It was not made, but *was the Word*. Now it remains to ask what kind of Word: because our words too are called words. Our words indeed are conceived by thought, brought forth by the voice; and yet, thought and uttered, they pass away. But that one, what? *And the Word was with God.* Say where it was, say what it was. I have already said. The holy Gospel told you: *In the beginning was the Word.* Say where it was, say what it was. *And the Word was with God.* But I asked what kind of Word. Do you wish to hear what it was? *And the Word was God*[^3]. O Word! what kind of Word? Who could unfold the word: *And the Word was God.* But perhaps made by God? Far be it. Hear what the holy Gospel says: *All things were made through it*[^4]. What is, *All things?* Whatever was made by God, was made through it. And how was he himself made, who made all things? Did he perhaps make himself? Finally, if he made himself, there was one who might make him. If, then, there was one who might make himself, he never was not.

**2. 2.** How is such a Word in a virgin? *All things were made through it*[^5]. What is, *All things?* Whatever was made by God, was made through it. Do not, brother, separate the Holy Spirit from so great a work. From what so great work? Not a small work, a great work are the angels: the flesh of Christ sitting at the right hand of the Father the angels adore. So great a work, then, the Holy Spirit especially wrought. In this work he was named, when to the holy Virgin through the angel a son was announced as to come. She, because she had purposed virginity, and her husband was not the remover, but the guardian of her modesty — rather not the guardian, because God was guarding her; but the husband was the witness of her virginal modesty, lest she be thought pregnant from adultery — when the angel announced it to her, she said: *How will this be, since I do not know a man?* If she were disposed to know one, she would not wonder. That wonder is the testimony of her purpose: *How will this be, since I do not know a man? How will it be?* And the angel to her: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you.* Behold how will be what you ask: *And the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore what will be born of you holy will be called the Son of God*[^6]. And he said well: *Will overshadow you*: lest your virginity feel the heat of lust. And when she was pregnant, it was said of her: *Mary was found having of the Holy Spirit in her womb*[^7]. The Holy Spirit, then, wrought the flesh of Christ. The only-begotten Son of God himself too wrought his flesh. Whence do we prove it? Because of this the Scripture says: *Wisdom has built herself a house*[^8].

**2. 3.** Therefore mark this. So great a God, God with God, the Word of God through whom all things were made, how is he enclosed in a womb? First, that the Word might be there, did he desert heaven? That the Word might be in the womb of the virgin, did he desert heaven? And whence would the angels live, if the Word deserted heaven? But that man might eat the bread of the angels, the Lord of the angels was made man. Let human thought still wander through its mists, fail, seek, say, that it may find, how the Word of God in the womb of the virgin, through which all things were made, did not desert the angels, did not desert the Father. How could he be enclosed in that womb? He could be there, he could not be enclosed. How, he says, could so great a one be in so tiny a place? Therefore the womb held what the world does not hold. Nor was he lessened, that he might be in the womb. He was in the womb, and he was so great. How great was he? Say how great he was, say what he was.

**3. 3.** *And the Word was with God.* Say what it was. *And the Word was God*[^9]. And I too know, I say, who speak with you; nor do I comprehend it: but thought makes us extend ourselves, the extension enlarges us, the enlargement makes us capacious. Yet, made capacious, we will not be able to comprehend the whole: but I deal with my word among you. Behold what I say, what I am about to say, this hear, this comprehend, my word, this is a human word. But if you cannot comprehend even this, see how far you are from that. Surely we wonder how Christ took flesh, was born of a virgin, and did not depart from the Father: behold, I who speak with you, before I came to you, thought beforehand what I would say to you. When I thought what I would say to you, already in my heart there was a word. For I would not speak to you, unless I first thought. I found you Latin, a Latin word is to be brought forth to you. But if you were Greek, I should have to speak to you in Greek, and bring forth to you a Greek word. That word in the heart is neither Latin nor Greek: that which is in my heart altogether precedes these tongues. I seek for it a sound, I seek, as it were, a vehicle; I seek whence it may reach you, when it does not depart from me. Behold, you have heard what is in my heart, now it is in yours too. It is in mine and is in yours: both you have begun to have it, and I have not lost it. As my word took up a sound, through which it might be heard: so the Word of God took up flesh, through which it might be seen. As much as I could, I have said. And what have I said? Since who said it? A man, I wished to speak of God. So great is he, such is he, that we can neither speak him, nor ought we to be silent of him.

**4. 4.** Thanks to you, Lord, because what I say, or wished to say, you know: yet from the crumbs of your table I have fed my fellow servants; feed you too and nourish inwardly those whom you have regenerated. Behold, what was this multitude? *Darkness: but now light in the Lord.* For to such the Apostle says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^10]. O you who have been baptized, *you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord.* If light, surely day: *for God called the light day*[^11]. You were darkness, he made you light, he made you day: of you we sang: *This is the day which the Lord has made, let us exult and rejoice in it*[^12]. Flee the darkness. Drunkenness pertains to darkness. Do not depart sober, and return drunk: and after midday we will see you. The Holy Spirit has begun to dwell, let him not migrate: do not shut him out of your hearts. A good guest, he finds you empty, he fills you: he finds you hungry, he feeds you: finally, he finds you thirsty, he makes you drunk. Let him make you drunk: for the Apostle says: *Do not be drunk with wine, in which is all luxury.* And, as if wishing to teach us with what we ought to be made drunk: *But be filled*, he says, *with the Holy Spirit; singing to yourselves in hymns and psalms and spiritual songs, singing in your hearts to the Lord*[^13]. He who rejoices in the Lord, and sings praises to the Lord with great exultation, is he not like one drunk? I approve this drunkenness: *For with you, God, is the fountain of life, and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight.* Whence? *Because with you, God, is the fountain of life, and in your light we will see light*[^14]. The Spirit of God is both drink and light. If you found a fountain in the darkness, you would light a lamp, that you might reach it. Do not light a lamp at the fountain of light: it itself shines for you, and will lead you to itself. When you come to drink, draw near and be enlightened. *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*[^15]: do not withdraw, lest you be darkened. Lord God, call, and let us draw near to you; strengthen us, lest we withdraw. Make your sons new, from little ones old men, but not from old men dead. For in this wisdom it is permitted to grow old, it is not permitted to die.
