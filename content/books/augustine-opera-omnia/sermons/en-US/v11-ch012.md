# Sermon 354/A: (Dolbeau 12)

**1.** The Lord's readings and, in the holy Scriptures that have just been read, the divine word and heavenly authority exhort the human race to remember its mortality in this, that an end is to come. For to every man, that is, to each individual man, the end is near, even if perhaps the end of the human race itself is far off. This, then, as I had begun to say, the divine word seems to exhort, that we remember our mortality in this, that an end is to come. But let him meditate on the life where there is no end: he will deserve to attain immortal life who remembers that he is mortal.

**2.** You have heard that the Pharisees asked the Lord, testing him, whether it was lawful to dismiss a wife for any cause whatever. He answers the truth, because he is the truth. For those who tested him did not make the Lord lie, so that both the faithful and the unfaithful might learn the truth; and that the worshiper of God might be instructed, the tester hears the truth. This I have said for this reason, lest men think that the Lord God said anything otherwise than the matter stands, since they were asking not faithfully but testing through a question. Let it, then, make no difference to us of what sort were those who asked, but what he said; not what sort of man struck the rock, but what sort of water flowed forth. Therefore in the Lord's answer the married have something to learn, and those too who are not yet married, or who have already from a good resolve despised marriage. According to these words of the Lord, the time too admonishes us to say something, because, as is thought, it is the last.

**3.** What the apostle says now pertains more to us, with greater care and fuller solicitude and richer devotion: *For the rest, brethren, the time is short. It remains that those who have wives be as though not having them,* and the rest, *and those who buy as though not buying, and those who rejoice as though not rejoicing, and those who use this world as though not using it. For the figure of this world is passing away. I wish you to be without solicitude*[^1]. Then he adds: *He who is without a wife thinks of the things that are God's, how he may please God; but he who is bound in marriage thinks of the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife*[^2]. A great difference: to think of the things that are God's, and to think of the things that are of the world. It cannot be that comparison join what thought so divides. But if some married man has burned to promise continence, let him look to his side, let him see whether she follows, and if she follows, let him lead her on; if she does not follow, let him not dismiss her. Perhaps he can and she cannot, or she can and he cannot; let him understand that he is one flesh. You have heard not some man, but the very Lord of men, in answering the Jews, giving precepts to Christians: *Have you not read,* he says, *that from the beginning God made them male and female, and said: For this reason a man shall leave father and mother and shall cleave to his wife, and the two shall be in one flesh? Therefore now they are not two, but one flesh. What, therefore,* he says, *God has joined, let not man separate*[^3].

**4.** It seems shameless to dispute somewhat carefully about this. But we ought not to think ourselves so healthy that we do not feel compassion for the sick. For what are we compared to the holiness of the apostle Paul? And yet with pious humility, with healthful speech, with divine medicine, he entered human bedchambers. And such great holiness drew near to the beds of the married, looked upon them lying there, did not lay aside the habit of holiness, and yet gave counsel for weakness: *Let the husband,* he says, *render to the wife her due*[^4]. It is a *due:* let him *render* it. *Likewise also the wife to the husband. The wife has not power over her own body, but the husband*[^5]. This is no wonder. For the woman is subject to the man, and in the man is authority, in the woman compliance. But yet in this matter, in which both sexes are joined, although in other things the woman ought to be the man's handmaid, in this matter, I say, the condition is equal. It was too little, then, for the apostle to say: *The woman has not power over her own body, but the husband*—decently he named the sex by the word of the body, to avoid obscenity. What he meant, he gave to be understood: *The woman has not power over her own body, but the husband. Likewise the husband has not power over his own body, but the woman*[^6]. In which matter the sex is set apart from the wife. It is another's: it is owed to the woman.

**5.** The husband cannot have such authority thence as to say: "I can now be continent. If you can, do this with me; if you cannot, you shall in no way hinder me: I do what I can." What, then? Do you wish, O husband, that your other side perish? For if the weaker flesh cannot be continent, the more languid will will commit fornication, and by fornicating will be damned. Far be it that her punishment be your crown! "You are mistaken: it will not be, it will not be so. It will not be as you say to me, that, since she will be damned by fornicating, better she alone than both together." If you say this, you are mistaken. For marriage is not condemned, that *which God has joined* is not condemned, only *let not man separate* it. You are a man; by seizing continence without the consent of your spouse, you, like a man, wish to separate what God has deigned to join. "But God," he says, "separates it, because I do it for God's sake." Clearly, if you read anywhere that God said: "If you have intercourse with your wife, I will damn you," do what you wish, lest you be damned together. But when you hear the apostle of Christ saying: *The wife has not power over her own body, but the husband. Likewise also the husband has not power over his own body, but the woman. Do not defraud one another . . .*[^7].

**6.** He said *defraud:* by denying the due, not by committing adultery. For he was speaking of dues to be rendered, and was binding the married to render their dues to one another. For he would not have permitted adulteries in the following words, where, when he had said: *Do not defraud one another,* he added, saying, *except by consent for a time*[^8]. Are adulteries, then, to be committed by consent? If you think that what was said, *Do not defraud one another,* pertains to adulteries, what does *except by consent* mean? Far be it that by consent husband and wife permit adulteries to be committed between them. Modest and patient matrons are wont to bear such things. To tolerate such a husband pertains to a woman's chastity. But let not the husband therefore be secure; let him rather beware lest he be afterward condemned, who is now said to be tolerated. But the matter is plain, nor does the apostolic word require exposition; since *do not defraud one another* he says to this end, that they not deny one another the conjugal dues, *except by* their *consent for a time, that you may have leisure,* he says, *for prayer*[^9]. You see, then, that the apostle commanded a certain continence, indeed a truce of continence, for stirring up and offering prayers, and with that solicitude with which such great health did not disdain to approach the beds of the weak: *And again be together*[^10].

**7.** When you have been continent *for a time, that you may have leisure for prayer, again be together.* Do you command this, apostle? "This," he says. And where is shame, where the modesty of such great holiness? "But I," he says, "know the danger of weakness." Then he did not keep silent the reason for his counsel. "Do you wish to hear," he says, "why I said: *Again be together? Lest Satan tempt you because of your incontinence*[^11]." Then it follows, lest he seem to have not permitted but commanded this—for one thing is what is permitted to weakness, another what is commanded to faith—: *But this,* he says, *I say by way of indulgence*[^12]. "What I say, *Again be together, I say by way of indulgence, not by way of command.* I do not command this to chastity, but permit it to weakness. It is not praiseworthy, but pardonable: *by way of indulgence.* *For I would that all were as myself, but each has his own gift from God, one thus, another thus*[^13]." Whence the Lord too: *Let him who can grasp it, grasp it*[^14].

**8.** Here someone perhaps will say: "If the apostle granted this *by way of indulgence* and yielded to the weakness of men, marriage is a sin. For to whom is indulgence granted, if not to sin?" Clearly this which the apostle granted to weakness *by way of indulgence*—I dare to say—is a sin, but there is not the good of marriage. Distinguish, then, brethren, and aid me by your attention as I labor in a most difficult place and toil for you before the Lord. He grants indulgence to such a deed; surely he attends to the intercourse of the married, not of adulterers, and yet he says *by way of indulgence, not by way of command.* "I pardon, I do not command." Let the married man say: "O apostle, if you pardon, I sin. I do not, therefore, wish a defender of the marriage good to bring me this reasoning, so as to say: Marriage is a good for this reason, that adulteries not be committed. I wish it to be a good, not a small evil. For he who says: Marriage ought to be entered for this reason, that adulteries not be committed; for marriage too is pardoned, and to marriage indulgence is granted, names two evils, not one good and one evil, but two evils, one small and the other great."

**9.** But the apostle was not reproaching the good of marriage, but the evil of incontinence, which is to use one's wife even beyond the necessity of begetting children. For why do you take a wife? Read not my disputations, but your own tablets. Read, attend, and if you do anything more, blush. Read, and let me too hear: for your sake it is necessary to me. Surely you read thus: "for the sake of begetting children." *If, then, there is anything more, it is from evil*[^15]. See, examine, test yourself. If you do nothing more than what must be done "for the sake of begetting children," you have nothing for the apostle to pardon you. But if you do anything more, it is evil that you do, it is sin that you do. But yet do you wish to know what the good of marriage is? Through the marriage good the evil of incontinence is pardonable. Concupiscence was stirred: you were overcome, you were drawn, but you were not drawn away from your wife. Penalties would have received you, overcome by incontinence, unless marriage interceded for you. Therefore, if you are married and wish that there be nothing for the apostle to grant you by way of indulgence, do not exceed the limits of your tablets.

**10.** But you are not yet bound: do not even seek this. For he who can use his wife only "for the sake of begetting children" can also not use her. He is a victor over lust, he masters the motions of the flesh, he firmly holds the reins of temperance, he sends that motion off like a horse where he wishes, he does not lose the right of holding it. Therefore, such as you are and not yet bound and not yet tied, *loosed from a wife, do not seek a wife*[^16]. For if it is said to the married: *The time is short, it remains that even those who have wives be as though not having them,* why do you wish to have, when you can, with continence safe, not have?

**11.** For why must a people still be propagated for God, through whom Christ, the salvation of the nations, might come? For when that people was being propagated, those who could also be continent were compelled by the very duty of piety to take wives. By a duty of piety the holy fathers begot sons, by a duty of piety the holy mothers bore sons: they could also have been continent. They served the people to be propagated: it was a duty. Now already a multitude lies open on every side, from which spiritual sons may be chosen and a holy people made for Christ in the adoption of immortality unto the heavenly inheritance. Therefore then was the time for embracing, now is the time for refraining from embrace. The prophet says, let us hear: *There is a time for everything. A time for embracing, a time for refraining from embrace*[^17]. *A time for embracing:* the prophetic time; *a time for refraining from embrace:* the evangelical time. *A time for scattering stones, a time for gathering them*[^18]. It *was the time for scattering stones,* for propagating men. For *God is able to raise up sons of Abraham from these stones*[^19]. Why are they still scattered? Let those now who have been scattered be gathered. It was said that stones should be scattered: *Cursed be he who does not raise up seed for Israel*[^20]. It is said that the stones should be gathered: *The time is short, it remains that even those who have wives be as though not having them.* Now, then, *let him who can grasp it, grasp it.* Then he who could grasp it did not undertake the office of continence: yet the virtue was in the man. Thus they had wives for God's sake—unless perhaps we think that if it had been said to Abraham that he should be continent, so great a pious virtue could tremble, which was able, bidden by the Lord, even to offer that same heir to be slain by the one from whom he had received him to be reared? Their time, then, was another.

**12.** Let no one, then, prescribe from this, let no one think that the necessity of this matter is laid upon him. *Let him who can grasp it, grasp it.* "But I cannot," he says. You cannot? "I cannot." A certain nurturing authority of the Apostle has taken you up, that *if they cannot contain themselves, let them marry*[^21]. Let something be done whence one may come to indulgence. Let it pertain to indulgence, lest one rush into eternal punishment. Let what is lawful be done, whence what is not lawful may be pardoned. This is what follows indicates: *I would rather they marry than burn*[^22]. "I granted," he says, "something to incontinence, because I feared something greater: I feared eternal punishments, I feared what awaits and remains for adulterers." Even this, by which the married, overcome by concupiscence, use one another beyond the necessity of begetting children, let them put among those things for which it is daily said: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^23]. But those who can grasp it, let them grasp it, and that they may be able, let them pray. Faith obtains even continence: *When I knew,* says holy Scripture, *when I knew that no one can be continent unless God grant it, this very thing too was wisdom, to know whose gift this was*[^24]. When you fear continence as a punishment, you do not knock at grace. Do not think it a punishment. When you are able, it will not be troublesome. He will grant it, whose gifts must be obtained. Knock: you will receive; ask, seek: you will find. The fountain always flows, let not faith be sluggish. And yet *let him who can grasp it, grasp it;* let him who cannot, *if he cannot contain himself, marry.*

**13.** Love one another. The husband can, the wife cannot: do not demand the due, render it. And in rendering it, you who do not demand, if you do not demand, you do an act of mercy. I dare to say outright: it is mercy. For if you do not render it, your spouse, overcome by concupiscence, or if you, woman, do not render it, your husband, overcome by concupiscence, will become an adulterer. I do not wish you to be so honored that you would wish him to be condemned. Indeed, if you no longer demand, but only render: it is reckoned to continence. For it is not exacted by lust, but rendered by mercy. Say outright to your God: "Lord, you know in me what you have given, but I hear too what you have admonished, that you made both me and my spouse, and willed that no one perish."
