# Sermon 97

**1.** Brothers, as for what you have just heard Scripture warning and saying, that we should keep watch on account of the last day, let each one think about his own last day, lest perhaps, when you suppose or imagine that the last day of the world is far off, you doze toward your own last day. Concerning the last day of this world, hear what he said: that no one knows it, *neither the angels of heaven, nor the Son, but the Father* [^1]. Here indeed there is a great question, lest, being carnally minded, we suppose that the Father knows something that the Son does not know. For surely when he said, *The Father knows*, he said it because in the Father the Son too knows. For what is there in the day that was not made in the Word, through whom the day was made? Let no one, he says, ask about the last day, when it will be; but let us all keep watch by living well, lest the last day of any one of us find us unprepared, and lest, in whatever state each one departs from here on his own last day, in that state he be found on the last day of the world. It will be of no help to you what you have not done here. Each one's works will help him, or his works will weigh him down.

**2.** And how did we sing to the Lord in the psalm? *Have mercy on me, Lord, for man has trampled me* [^2]. He is called man who lives according to man. Indeed, to those who live according to God it is said: *You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High* [^3]. But to the reprobate, who were called to be sons of God, and chose rather to be men, that is, to live according to man: *But you*, he says, *will die like men, and fall like one of the princes* [^4]. For the fact that man is mortal ought to serve him for discipline, not for boasting. On what does a worm, who will die tomorrow, pride himself? I say to Your Charity, brothers: proud mortals ought to blush before the devil. For though he is proud, yet he is immortal; he is a spirit, though a malignant one. For him the last day of punishment is reserved unto the end, yet he does not suffer the death that we suffer. But man heard: *You will die the death* [^5]. Let him make good use of his punishment. What do I mean: Let him make good use of his punishment? Let him not go from it into pride, from which he received his punishment; let him acknowledge himself mortal, and break his haughtiness. Let him hear it said to him: *Why does earth and ashes pride itself?* [^6] Even if the devil prides himself, he is not earth, he is not ashes. Therefore it was said: *But you will die like men, and fall like one of the princes* [^7]. You consider only that you are mortal, and like the devil you are proud. Therefore let man make good use of his punishment, brothers; let him make good use of his evil, so as to profit toward his good. Who does not know that it is a punishment that we must die; and what is worse, when we do not know it? The punishment is certain, the hour uncertain; and of this punishment alone are we certain in human affairs.

**3.** Our other things, both good and evil, are uncertain; death alone is certain. What do I mean? A child is conceived; perhaps it is born, perhaps a miscarriage occurs. So it is uncertain: perhaps it grows, perhaps it does not grow; perhaps it grows old, perhaps it does not grow old; perhaps it will be rich, perhaps poor; perhaps honored, perhaps brought low; perhaps it will have children, perhaps it will not; perhaps it will take a wife, perhaps it will not; and whatever else you may name among good things. Look also to the evils: perhaps it falls sick, perhaps it does not fall sick; perhaps it is struck by a serpent, perhaps it is not struck; perhaps it is devoured by a beast, perhaps it is not devoured. And consider all evils: everywhere it is, perhaps it will be, perhaps it will not be. But can you say: Perhaps it dies, perhaps it does not die? Just as physicians, when they have examined a sickness and recognized it to be mortal, pronounce this: He dies, he will not escape it. From the moment a man is born, it must be said: He will not escape. When he was born, he began to fall sick. When he has died, he indeed ends his sickness; but he does not know whether he passes on to a worse one. That rich man had ended his luxurious sickness, he came to a tormenting one. But that poor man ended his sickness and came to health [^8]. But he chose here what he would have afterward; and what he reaped there, he sowed here. Therefore while we live we ought to keep watch, and we ought to choose what we may hold in the future.

**4.** Let us not love the world. It oppresses its lovers, it does not lead them to good. One must labor in it rather lest it seize us, than fear lest it fall. Behold, the world falls: the Christian stands, because Christ does not fall. For why does the Lord say: *Rejoice, because I have conquered the world* [^9]? Let us answer him, if it please him: Rejoice, but you yourself! If you have conquered, you rejoice. Why we? Why does he say to us, *Rejoice*, except because he conquered for us, fought for us? For where did he fight? Because he took on a man. Take away that he was born of a virgin, take away that he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man [^10]; take this away, and where is the wrestling? where the contest? where the temptation? where the victory, which no battle preceded? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made* [^11]. Would a Jew crucify this Word? Would an impious man insult this Word? Would this Word be struck with blows? Would this Word be crowned with thorns? But that he might suffer these things, *the Word was made flesh* [^12]; and having suffered these things, by rising again he conquered. Therefore he conquered for us, to whom he showed the security of the resurrection. So you say to God: *Have mercy on me, Lord, for man has trampled me* [^13]. Do not you yourself trample yourself, and do not let man conquer you. For behold, a powerful man frightens you. With what does he frighten you? I despoil, I harm, I torture, I kill. And you cry out: *Have mercy on me, Lord; for man has trampled me*. If you speak truly, you are attending to yourself; because you fear the threats of man, a dead man tramples you; and because you would not fear unless you were a man, man tramples you. What then is the remedy? O man, cling to God, by whom you were made man: cling to him, presume upon him, call upon him, let him be your strength. Say to him: *In you, Lord, is my strength* [^14]. And you will sing in the face of the threats of men; and what you will then sing, the Lord himself says: *In God I will hope, I will not fear what man can do to me* [^15].
