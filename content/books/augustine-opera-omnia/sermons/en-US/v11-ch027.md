# Sermon 360/C: (Dolbeau 27)

**1.** For nothing is sweeter than the zeal of the brethren, but nothing is more dangerous than the dissension of peoples. In the sound of the tongue of our mouth, indeed, "flesh" (caro) and "love" (caritas) seem near to one another: they are near in the sound of the tongue. For what sounds so near as "flesh" and "love"? Yet they differ very much from one another, even in this time. How much these two differ which sound alike, appears to you sufficiently, since indeed, where love is, the heart is enlarged, the flesh is straitened. But because even our love itself labors in the flesh, and the spacious fields of divinity have not yet received you, as long as the bond of weakness still holds, yet, dearly beloved, consider how ample the spaces of these buildings have been made! Do you think that those placed far off hear with more difficulty? The way of our voice is your quiet. See how quickly the quiet hear, even what is not said with so great a voice! Aid yourselves, then, and, as it is written, *bear one another's burdens*[^1], that you may grasp together what is given to all.

**2.** After a long and lasting desire we see your love bodily present; in spirit neither did you ever depart from us, nor we from you. When we have our "hearts up," there you dwell with us, where no one presses upon another. Yet we ask pardon, brethren, if perhaps to some of you we seem to have come to you later than both we and you would wish. Great necessities held us in the summer time, and those very necessities surely were not hidden from you. For we were aided too by your prayers, that what had been necessities might become pleasures. In the city of Hippo where I serve my sons, your brethren, we long labored in birth, at last we saw unity. Again and again aid us by your vows and prayers, that *the Lord may confirm what he has wrought in us*[^2]. But now, although we were still doing these things there, and in the region of Hippo the peoples have now begun to be converted, by whom unity is held the more slowly the more difficultly rusticity understands it. Now, then, by your desire we have been not led, but snatched, that we may come again, desired, to those people. Receive, then, the petition for pardon for the delay. For then the venerable brother and my colleague Maximinus too was converted to the catholic Church. Then, therefore, his newness in the catholic Church, through which he ground down the hard kingdoms of oldness, surely could not and ought not to be deserted by me, nor was it right for him at once to withdraw thence. When, therefore, the Lord judged it opportune, he permitted us both to come. I think, then, that you easily pardon my delay, because I came afterward with him, on whose account at first I did not come.

**3.** Now then, dearly beloved, receive what now presses. We heard, absent, your fervors; you heard, absent, our fervors. You love unity, you love peace, you keep peace, you hunger for peace. We approve and rejoice at the healing of your palate, by which you taste *how sweet the Lord is*[^3]. For good bread is good to the healthy. But the sick man, though he can praise good bread when seen, cannot eat it when offered. For who is our bread, but he who said: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^4]? Is he perhaps bread and not peace? Let us prove that he is peace too. For we have proved that he is bread by his most manifest testimony: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven.* Let the Apostle too say: *For he himself is our peace*[^5]. He, then, who says: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven,* of him it is said: *He himself is our peace.* We have, then, the bread, peace, but if we are healthy, let us eat.

**4.** You have heard that very bread speaking, just now from the Gospel. The disciples were seeking the first and high place, and there was a contention of glory among the sons of love. They were seeking *who was greater among them*[^6]. Weakness sought the greater place, which surely love obtains. They did not yet know by what way they should go, although they understood whither they should go. Through humility one comes to loftiness. The way is Christ. He is the bread, he is the peace, he is also the way. Ask of him whither you wish to go, he answers: "To me." Ask by what way you wish to go, he answers: "Through me." And he remained whither we should go, and he came by what way we should go. Therefore, dearly beloved, sons of peace, sons of light, sons of love, offshoots of the catholic Church, if we are firm, let us serve the weak; if we are healthy, let us serve the sick. Our Lord served. You attend to the fact that the Lord serves; the servant is sick, whom the Lord serves. See, let us praise our bread, with all the strength we can. *See how good and how pleasant for brethren to dwell in unity*[^7]. Be delighted, you just, in the Lord.

**5.** *Good* clearly is it *for brethren to dwell in unity.* All concede that it is *good,* not all grasp that it is *pleasant.* Ask of anyone, even though he be still a heretic or already show his face and cover his mind; ask, question one who loathes, refuses, repels with the hand the sickness of one serving and wishing to feed; yet hold him, ask of him: "Is unity good?" If he can, let him say: "It is evil." I altogether do not spare, I ask: Is unity good? He answers: "Good." Like it or not, he answers this: "Unity is good." Or are you silent? Even if you are silent, surely you are silent for this reason, that you cannot say: "It is not good." Iniquity does not permit to say it is good, but truth does not allow to deny it is good. Yet I press, that I may extort the voice, I will not desist, I will not withdraw, you will not be rid of me unless you say something. I have found at last your ears; if I do not hold you loving, I hold you at least fearing. Say, answer me. Easy is what I ask, brief is what I question. Is unity good? What is he to do? By no means is he going to say: "It is not good." Therefore, even to be rid of me, he is going to say: "Good." And I answer: What you praise, if it is a possession, hold it with me; if it is a garment, be clothed with me; if it is bread, eat with me. "It is good," he says, "I do not deny, but because I am compelled to it, therefore I am unwilling for it." Therefore it is good, but because you are compelled to the good, therefore you do not wish the good: as if indeed I were troublesome in compelling, if you were eager in asking. If it is good and you do not wish it, therefore I compel you. For what you confess to be good, you do not refuse out of truth, but out of weakness. I serve the weak: you are sick, I am your minister. I offer food, receive the food you praise. Do you perhaps, as the sick are wont to refuse the foods set before them, slander it as badly cooked? You will be able to say none of these things to the food I offer. Christ is the bread, Christ is the peace. This food was formed in the womb of virginity, cooked by the fire of the passion. Take, brother; receive, brother, receive something lest you die. You surely praise unity. Your weakness is against me, not your judgment. I offer food, not only that which strengthens the sick man, but also that which sustains the sick man. I am troublesome when I press it in, but impious if I withdraw it. "See," he says, "I receive it."

**6.** What sick men do we endure, brethren? "See," he says, "I receive it." Some have come, yielding to the troubles of those serving, to the solicitude, though importunate, yet of those displaying the maternal love of parents. Why do I say "of parents," brethren? I do not mean myself, I do not mean any man. Our parents, feeding the healthy, refreshing the sick, are God the Father and the mother Church. This pious mother, then, both bearing her conceived sons and bringing to birth those in peril, did not spurn the sickness of her own; though troublesome, though importunate, she approached those lying down; she pressed food on those refusing.
