# Sermon 28/A: (Dolbeau 9)

**1.** The apostle says: *But having the same spirit of faith, according to what is written, "I believed, therefore I spoke," we too believe, therefore we also speak*[^1]. He who wishes to speak about what he does not believe wishes to pour out what he has not filled. It must be filled, that you may pour out. But it must be so poured out to others that you do not remain empty. Therefore the Lord, promising the abundance of his Holy Spirit to believers: *There will be in him,* he says, *a fountain of water springing up to eternal life*[^2]. For it is proper to a fountain so to pour forth that it is not emptied. And if God grants this to us, what shall we render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to us? Therefore, full of the grace of God, he who was speaking in the psalm as we have heard, and considering in himself the gift of God, looking around for his recompense and not finding himself a fit one to respond, hesitated, asking, and said: *What shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me?*[^3] For he did not give to me, but *rendered to me,* because I deserved evil for evil, and he rendered good for evil. What shall I, he says, render to the Lord, surely if I am to render anything to the Lord, when to him it is said in another psalm: *I said to the Lord: You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^4]? If, then, he is Lord because *he has no need of my goods, what shall I render to the Lord?* And yet he seemed to have found what to render. *I will take,* he says, *the cup of salvation, and I will call upon the name of the Lord*[^5]. This is to render, indeed this is to receive again.

**2.** But he had said above: *I said in my alarm: every man is a liar*[^6]. In which matter he seems to have given to men—but to those who understand little and do not carefully attend—not a confession but an occasion. *Every man is a liar,* Scripture said. Someone says: "I will lie securely, because *every man is a liar.* For I am not a man unless a liar." You have received security, hear also the warning: *You will destroy all who speak a lie*[^7]. Carefully, then, return and inquire, because even he who said this did not say "in security," but *in alarm.* I could therefore say to you: he said it *in alarm,* he said it trembling, he does not know what he said, he said it troubled; I could say this, were it not that the apostle Paul confirmed this sentence, saying: *God alone is truthful, but every man a liar, as it is written*[^8]. Therefore if *God is truthful* and *alone truthful, but every man a liar,* whence will a man be truthful, unless he comes to him who is not a liar? Finally to men it is said: *You were once darkness*[^9]. See, *every man is a liar.* But to God it is said: *With you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light*[^10]. And because *God alone is truthful,* since *God is light, and there is no darkness in him at all*[^11], men are darkness, God is light; man a liar, God truthful. When will a man be truthful? *Draw near to him and be enlightened*[^12]. This, then, Scripture wished to show, that every man, absolutely every man, as far as the man himself is concerned, is a liar. For he is a liar only from what is his own, that is, from the body, which consists of earthly matter. For the soul is divine; and from it he is truthful, not a liar. Nor does he have anything of his own, except to be a liar, not because he cannot be truthful, but because he will not be truthful from what is his own. Therefore, that he may be truthful: *I believed, therefore I spoke.* Take away believing: *Every man is a liar.* For when he has withdrawn from the truth of God, he will remain in his own lie, since *he who speaks a lie speaks from what is his own*[^13]. Therefore if you have now been made truthful, learn from what you were made so, lest in that very thing you be a liar. For a truthful man, when you say "I am truthful from what is my own," in that very thing you will be a liar. If, then, you are truthful, you have been filled, you have been made a partaker of truth. You were empty, but you have been filled with truth from the heavenly fountain; therefore you will say: *What shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me?* *In my alarm I said,* and I said the truth: *Every man is a liar.* But he rendered to me not punishment for the lie, but good for evil, and by justifying the impious he made out of a liar a truthful man.

**3.** *What,* then, *shall I render to the Lord?* *I will take the cup of salvation, and I will call upon the name of the Lord.* *I will take the cup of salvation:* I will take the cup of Christ. For he is the salvation of God. For of him that Simeon said: *Now you dismiss your servant, Lord, in peace, for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^14]. You, then, if you ask: *What shall I render to the Lord?*, *for all that he has rendered* to you, take *the cup of salvation.* What *cup?* *Of salvation.* Whose *salvation?* Christ's. What *cup of the salvation* of Christ, but that of which he answered certain men seeking lofty things and despising humble ones: *Can you drink the cup that I am to drink?*[^15] Take this cup, if you wish to render *to the Lord for all that he has rendered* to you. *For just as Christ laid down his life for us, so we too ought to lay down our life for the brethren*[^16]. This is to render to him, by taking *the cup of salvation.* But why do you fear, why do you tremble at such a cup? He himself will be the help, who went before as the example. For see what that inquirer renders, and as it were, in that very recompense, the finder of the cup, as if saying: "I have found what to render: *I will take the cup of salvation*"; and being fit, he dared to drink and say: *And I will call upon the name of the Lord.* Accordingly, when you seek to render what you received, you have not rendered, but received yet more in addition. You received once what you believed, again what, by drinking *the cup of salvation,* you drank. From him, then, all things have been given us; through him we have what we are, if we are anything good.

**4.** Whence also that reading which preceded the psalm seems to us to hint at something of this kind, if only we are able to grasp the will of the one who hints. For it says: *If you sit down to dine at the table of a powerful man, consider sensibly what is set before you, and put forth your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things yourself*[^17]. What is this? *If you sit at the table of a powerful man,* you are bidden *sensibly* to understand *what is set before you;* then next you are bidden to put forth *your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things yourself.* Who is more powerful than Christ? Sit down *to dine at* his *table:* approach his altar humbly, for sitting is a sign of humility. *Sensibly,* then, *understand what is set before you.* What you receive, the inviter himself is. You have sat down at the table of your shepherd: for you find the death of your inviter. Those who sit at this table know what I am saying. It is no light thing: he who called you sets himself before you. *Sensibly,* then, *understand what is set before you, and put forth your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things yourself.* What this is, I have already said a little before: *For just as Christ laid down his life for us, so we too ought to lay down our life for the brethren.* This is the table of that powerful one: to this supper you have come, if you have sat down humbly; rather, at this supper you have sat down, if you have come humbly. See what is prepared for you: *Christ laid down his life for us.* This you receive, see whither you put forth your hand, *knowing that you must prepare such things yourself.* Say, then, what follows: *So we too ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.* Therefore you take *the cup of salvation* and call upon *the name of the Lord:* for you received a precious death, therefore you drank the cup. What follows? *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^18]. See how you eat such things, you prepare such things.

**5.** But what is it that follows in the same reading? Let us notice, let us see; if we can, let us examine it. *But if you are too greedy,* it says, *do not covet his foods: for these have a deceitful life*[^19]. I see: it is closed. And where is the knocking? Knock with me, that it may be opened to us. He is powerful, it is a powerful man's table; you sit at it, you are humbled before it, putting forth your hand, *knowing* what kind of things *you must prepare:* to suffer for the name of the Lord who suffered for you. *But if you are too greedy,* it says, *do not covet his foods.* What is *If you are too greedy?* *If you are too greedy,* do you wish to know what it is? If you give yourself more than you are. In so great a depth of this mystery, to me knocking as best I can, this for now occurs as what he deigns to grant; this for now he has opened. See, the powerful one's table, Christ's table, full of the grace of the Lord's passion. Sit at it: do not approach proudly. *Put forth your hand:* let not the tongue sound while the deed is silent. For this is to put forth the hand: let there be no voice except with solidity, no faith without works. *Put forth your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things.* From what will you put it forth, you who are poor? Is it from what is your own? Woe to you, if you think this! You will display nothing, you will have nothing. *If,* then, *you are too greedy*—you give yourself more than you are—*do not covet* his foods. For if you give yourself more than you are and covet his foods, see what follows: *For these have a deceitful life.*

**6.** *These,* it says, *have a deceitful life.* What *have a deceitful life?* That you are too greedy, give yourself more, assign yourself more than you know yourself to be, is a deceitful life, it is hypocrisy, boasting, not obedience. It is altogether a deceitful life. *For he who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, deceives himself*[^20]. Will you render to so powerful a one from what is your own, will you, taking such things from so powerful a one, prepare such things from what is your own? O detestable greed, O deceitful life! Then, when it had said: *For these have a deceitful life,* if you give yourself more than you are, and in this are too greedy and do not understand grace, approaching the table of grace, see what it adds and how it admonishes you. For what were you saying? "I will render from my own, I will repay from my own, I will prepare from my own and will prepare such things as that rich man displayed." See what follows: *Do not stretch yourself out against the rich man, since you are poor*[^21]. A most healthful admonition, if there is one to hear it, if the poor man hears it, if he does not consider himself rich from what is his own, if he does not stretch himself out against the rich man. For by stretching himself out he is inflated more easily than he is filled. You say: "From my own, from my own"; against whom do you say it? *For what do you have that you did not receive?*[^22] He is full, you are inflated. *Do not stretch yourself out against the rich man, since you are poor.* This is the deceitful life, you remain in what you said *in alarm:* *Every man is a liar.* *Do not stretch yourself out against the rich man, since you are poor:* do not against God, since you are a man; do not against the just one, since you are a sinner; do not against him who had no sin, you a man who do not live without sin. *Do not stretch yourself out against the rich man, since you are poor; but restrain yourself by your own sense*[^23]: measure yourself, do not go outside to mock me, but enter into yourself and see yourself.

**7.** *For if you fix,* it says, *your eye on him, he will nowhere appear. For he has made himself wings like an eagle's, and goes into the house of his superior*[^24]. Who is that powerful one, to whose table you were approaching, from whom you were taking, and such things, as if from your own, you were arranging to render? For you stretch *yourself,* poor man, *against the rich one,* and do not rather restrain *yourself by your own sense,* so that he may know you as one to be helped, lest he find you one to be condemned. But who is this powerful one, whom you seek as it were to feed from your own, and to feed in the way he himself fed you? Who is this, do you wish to know? *If you fix your eye on him, he will nowhere appear.* What is this? He terrified, when he said: *he will nowhere appear.* But when *will he nowhere appear?* *If you fix your eye on him,* that distorted eye of yours, by which you see badly, by which you do not rightly understand, that eye *if you fix on him, he will nowhere appear.* For he will be hidden, he will not show himself; you will not grasp him, you will not understand him; acknowledge yourself the lesser, that you may be able to understand him the greater. It can also be understood thus: *If you fix your eye on him,* the human eye, seeking him as if a man, *he will nowhere appear,* because he is not a man. *He will nowhere appear:* seek in the east, seek in the west, seek in the patriarchs, seek in the prophets, seek in the apostles, *nowhere* will he *appear* to you: for he is not only a man, but God-man. Accordingly, *if you fix your eye on him,* that is, the human eye, not the divine Spirit, *he will nowhere appear:* if you seek him as a man, you do not find him; if you seek as a man the one who fed you, you do not find him: *he will nowhere appear.*

**8.** *For he has made himself wings like an eagle's.* He did this: *He made himself wings like an eagle's:* he was taken up, he flew, since of him it had been said: *His life shall be lifted up from the earth*[^25]. *And he will go into the house of his superior.* He returns to the Father, you remained among men. He who made you surpasses you. For he is despised by you, because he was made for your sake. If you wish to repay him anything, receive it from him. Poor men do this, when they are invited by their rich patrons and wish themselves also to invite them: they ask from those very men what they may be able to display to them. His Father begot one equal to himself, begot one coeternal with himself, begot one of one substance with himself; but for your sake he made him lesser, and became his superior, so that his Son, his only one, coeternal with him, equal to him, says of him: *The Father is greater than I*[^26]. What he begot, do you wish to know? Ask and hear: *I and the Father are one*[^27]. What he was made for your sake, if you wish to know, ask and hear: *For the Father is greater than I.* Acknowledge his justice, love his obedience, beware of pride, praise his power, marvel at his patience, obtain his grace.
