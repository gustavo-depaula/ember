# Sermon 143

**1.** The medicine for all wounds of the soul, and the one propitiation for the sins of men, is to believe in Christ; nor can anyone at all be cleansed, whether from the original sin which he drew from Adam, in whom all sinned and became by nature children of wrath, or from the sins which they themselves added by not resisting fleshly desire but following it, and serving it in shameful deeds and crimes—unless through faith they are joined and fitted together to the body of him who was conceived without any fleshly allurement or deadly delight, whom his mother did not nourish in the womb in sins, and who committed no sin, and in whose mouth no guile was found[^1]. For those who believe in him become children of God; because they are born of God through the grace of adoption, which is in faith in Jesus Christ our Lord. Therefore, dearly beloved, with reason does this same Lord and Savior of ours call this the one sin, of which the Holy Spirit convicts the world, namely that it does not believe in him. *I*, he says, *tell you the truth, it is expedient for you that I go away. For if I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you; but if I go away, I will send him to you. And when he comes, he will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment. Of sin, because they did not believe in me; of righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me; of judgment, because the prince of this world has been judged*[^2].

**2.** Of this one sin, then, he willed the world to be convicted, that they do not believe in him; namely because, since by believing in him all sins are loosed, he willed this one to be charged, by which the rest are bound together. And because by believing they are born of God and become children of God: *For he gave*, he says, *to those who believe in him the power to become children of God*[^3]. So he who believes in the Son of God, insofar as he cleaves to him and becomes himself too by adoption a son and heir of God, and a coheir with Christ, to that extent does not sin. Hence John says, *He who is born of God does not sin*[^4]. And therefore the sin of which the world is convicted is this, that they do not believe in him. This is the sin of which he likewise says, *If I had not come, they would have no sin*[^5]. For did they not have other innumerable sins? But by his coming this one sin was added to the unbelievers, by which the rest were held. But in believers, because this one was absent, it came about that all were forgiven to believers. And for no other reason does the apostle Paul say, *All*, he says, *have sinned and need the glory of God*[^6]; so that whoever believes in him may not be confounded[^7]; as the Psalm also says, *Come to him, and be enlightened, and your faces will not be confounded*[^8]. So he who glories in himself will be confounded; for he will not be found without sins. Therefore only he will not be confounded who glories in the Lord. *For all have sinned and need the glory of God*. And so, when he was speaking of the unbelief of the Jews, he did not say: For if some of them have sinned, will their sin make void the faith of God? For how would he say, If some of them have sinned, when he himself had said, *For all have sinned*? But he said, *If some of them have not believed, will their unbelief make void the faith of God?*[^9] So as to set forth more expressly this sin, by which alone the rest are shut off, lest by the grace of God they be remitted. Of this one sin, through the coming of the Holy Spirit—that is, through the gift of his grace which is given to the faithful—the world is convicted, the Lord saying, *Of sin, because they did not believe in me*[^10].

**3.** But the merit of believers would not be great, and their blessedness glorious, if the Lord were always to appear to human eyes in his resuscitated body. So the Holy Spirit brought this great gift to those who were to believe, that they might sigh after him whom they did not see with fleshly eyes, with a mind sober from fleshly desires and inebriated with spiritual longings. Hence that disciple too, who had said that he would not believe unless he had touched his scars with his hand, when, after handling the Lord's body, he cried out as though awaking, *My Lord and my God!*—the Lord said to him, *Because you have seen me, you have believed; blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*[^11]. This blessedness the Holy Spirit the Paraclete brought, that, the form of a servant being removed from the eyes of the flesh—which he received from the Virgin's womb—the purified gaze of the mind might be stretched toward the very form of God, in which, equal to the Father, he remained even when he deigned to appear in the flesh; so that the Apostle, filled with the same Spirit, might say, *And even if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we no longer know him so*[^12]. For he knows the flesh of Christ not according to the flesh but according to the spirit who recognizes the power of his resurrection, not curious by handling, but sure by believing; not saying in his heart, *Who will ascend into heaven? That is, to bring Christ down; or, Who will descend into the abyss? That is, to bring Christ back from the dead. But the word is near*, he says, *in your mouth*, that Jesus is Lord: *and if you believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^13]. These, brothers, are the words of the Apostle, of the Holy Spirit himself belching forth in holy inebriation.

**4.** Since, then, we would in no way have this blessedness, by which we do not see and yet believe, unless we received it from the Holy Spirit, it was rightly said, *It is expedient for you that I go away. For if I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you; but if I go away, I will send him to you*[^14]. He is indeed always with us by his divinity; but unless he departed from us bodily, we would always see his body in a fleshly way and would never believe spiritually—and by that faith justified and made blessed, we would deserve to contemplate, with a cleansed heart, that very Word, God with God, through whom all things were made, and which was made flesh that it might dwell among us. And if it is not by touching with the hand but by believing with the heart that one is brought to righteousness, then the world is rightly convicted concerning our righteousness, the world that is unwilling to believe except what it sees. But that we might have the righteousness of faith, of which the unbelieving world would be convicted, therefore the Lord says, *Of righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me*[^15]. As if he were saying: This will be your righteousness, that you believe in me the mediator, whom you will most certainly hold to have gone, resuscitated, to the Father, although you do not see him in a fleshly way; so that, reconciled through him, you may be able to see God spiritually. Hence to the woman who bore the figure of the Church, when she fell at his feet after the resurrection, he said, *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^16]. This is understood to have been said mystically. Do not believe in me in a fleshly way through bodily contact; but you will believe spiritually, that is, you will touch me by spiritual faith, when I have ascended to the Father. For blessed are those who do not see and yet believe. And this is the righteousness of faith, which the world lacking, it is convicted concerning us who do not lack it; for *the righteous lives by faith*[^17]. Whether, then, because rising again in him and coming to the Father in him we are invisibly perfected also in justification; or because, not seeing and believing, we live by faith, since the righteous lives by faith; therefore he said, *Of righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me*.

**5.** Nor let the world excuse itself on the ground that it is hindered by the devil from believing in Christ. For in the case of believers the prince of the world is cast out[^18], so that he no longer works in the hearts of those whom Christ has begun to possess through faith; just as he works in the children of unbelief[^19], whom he often stirs up to tempt and trouble the righteous. For because he who within held sway has been cast out, he wars from without. So although the Lord, through his persecutions, guides the meek in judgment[^20], yet now by this very fact, that he has been cast out, he has been judged. And of this judgment the world is convicted; because in vain does he who is unwilling to believe in Christ complain of the devil—the devil whom, judged, that is, cast out, and permitted to assail from without for our training, not only men but also women, and boys, and girls, the martyrs have overcome. But in whom have they overcome, except in him in whom they believed, and whom, not seeing, they loved, and by whose reigning in their hearts they were freed from that worst tyrant? And all this through grace, that is, through the gift of the Holy Spirit. Rightly, then, does the same Spirit convict the world, both *of sin*, because it does not believe in Christ; *and of righteousness*, because those who willed believed, although they did not see him in whom they believed, and through his resurrection hoped to be perfected themselves too in the resurrection; *and of judgment*, because they themselves, if they were willing to believe, would be hindered by none, *since the prince of this world has now been judged*[^21].
