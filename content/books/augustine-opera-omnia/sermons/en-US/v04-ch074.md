# Sermon 147a

**1.** All the things that have just been read from the holy Gospel were done and said after the Lord's resurrection. We have heard, then, the Lord Jesus Christ questioning the apostle Peter, questioning whether he loved him. So the Lord was questioning the servant, the master the disciple, the creator the man, the redeemer the freed one, firmness the trembling one, the foreknowing the ignorant one; and where he made himself the questioner, there he showed himself the teacher. For Christ did not fail to know anything that Peter carried in his heart. He questions once, the other answers. And it is not enough, he questions again, and not something else, but the very thing he had questioned; this same thing the other too answers. A third time the question is repeated, a third time love answers; for he was questioned three times because of love, who had denied three times through fear. When the Lord was dying, he feared, he feared and denied; but the Lord rising implanted love, put fear to flight. For what would Peter now fear? For when he denied, he certainly denied because he feared to die; with the Lord rising, what would he fear, in whom he found death dead? Surely it was indeed the very one living who questioned him, who was buried dead; he was present who had hung on the wood. When our Lord Jesus Christ was being judged by the Jews, then Peter, questioned, and—what is worse—by a woman, and—what is more disgraceful—by a maidservant, feared and denied; under a maidservant he trembled, under the Lord he stood firmly. But to the one confessing his love once, and again, and a third time, he commended his sheep. *Do you love me?* he says. *Lord, you know that I love you*. And he: *Feed my lambs*. This once, this again, this a third time[^1]; as if there were nowhere for Peter to show his love toward Christ, except by being a faithful shepherd under the prince of all shepherds. Do you love me? I love you. And what will you bestow, loving me? what will you, a man, confer on your creator? what will you confer of your love, you redeemed, on your redeemer—as if much, a soldier on your king? what will you confer? This alone I require: *Feed my sheep*[^2].

**2.** See, nevertheless, brothers, because of bad men, the servants who made themselves private flocks out of the Lord's flock, and divided what they had not bought. For there arose certain unfaithful servants, they divided the flock of Christ, and out of his very flock made themselves, in a manner, private flocks by their thefts, and you hear them saying: Those sheep are mine. Why do you reach for my sheep? I will not find you at my sheep. If both we say "mine" and they say "theirs," Christ has lost his sheep. Set before your eyes the prince of shepherds, the Lord of his flock, standing and discerning and judging between his servants. You—what do you say? These sheep are mine. And you—what do you say? These are my sheep. The ones I bought, where are they? Bad servants, you call the sheep yours, and you claim for yourselves what I bought, when you yourselves, if I had not bought you, would perish. Far be it from us to say that you are our sheep; that is not a catholic voice, it is not genuine, it is not Peter's, because it is against the Rock. You are sheep, but of him who bought both us and you. We have one Lord: he is a shepherd, but not a hired one. He feeds his own, and—what no one does with sheep—he both gave the price and drew up the document. Seek the price: it is his blood; seek the document: it is the Gospel, which you have just heard read. What did he say to Peter? *Do you love me? I love you. Feed my sheep*[^3]. Surely not yours? Do you wish to know to whom he says yours? Hear in the holy book that is called the *Song of Songs*; there are read holy love-songs, the bridegroom and the bride, Christ and the Church. And that whole book is, as it were, a wedding-song, what they call an epithalamium, but of the holy bed, the chaste bed: *For in the sun he has set his tabernacle*, that is, in the light, in public, where it would lie open and not be hidden. *And he himself, like a bridegroom, came forth from his chamber*[^4]; for he took a spouse, human flesh. His chamber was the virginal womb; there he joined the Church to himself, that what was foretold might be fulfilled, *And the two shall be in one flesh*[^5].

**3.** So these lovers, Christ and the Church, were speaking with one another. The Church says to him, *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down at midday*? Why do I wish you to tell me where you feed, where you lie down at midday? *Lest perhaps I become as one veiled over the flocks of your companions*[^6]. Therefore, she says, I wish you to tell me where you feed, where you lie down at midday, so that, when I come to you, I may not go astray, lest perhaps I become as one veiled over the flocks of your companions, that is, lest I rush in among the flocks not yours, but of your companions. *As one veiled.* What else is "veiled," except as it were hidden and unknown? The Donatists are wont in these words to say their own sense, not the sense of the Scriptures. For this they are wont to say. Africa is the south, Africa is the south of the world; therefore the Church asks the Lord, *Where do you feed? Where do you lie down?* and he answers, *At midday*; as if to say, Do not seek me except in Africa. Read and understand, heretical mind. A mirror is now set before you, here I have found you; understand that the bride is still asking; why do you make the bridegroom already answering? Or at least recognize the feminine gender. *Where do you feed, where do you lie down at midday? Lest perhaps I become as one veiled.* One veiled, I think, is a woman, not a male. So, O Lord, let the south be Africa; let it be understood as they understand it. Africa is the south; here a faction was made, the Donatists; here a great division and the saw of dissension was drawn through the flock of Christ. Therefore the Church across the sea asks, as it were, where this division has not been made: *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you lie down at midday?* For I hear that there the party of Donatus is spoken of, some catholics, others Donatists; tell me where you feed, lest I come and go astray. There I seek a report, where I fear uncertainty. Tell me where you feed, where you lie down at midday? Why do I wish you to tell me? Lest perhaps I become as one veiled; because to the party of Donatus I am as it were veiled, as it were unknown; there I am preached, and from them I lie hidden.

**4.** The Scriptures say this: *In the last times the mountain of the Lord will be manifest, prepared on the top of the mountains, and it will be exalted above the hills, and all the nations will come to it*[^7]. It is called a mountain, and to the party of Donatus the mountain is veiled. He who stumbles on a stone is to be pardoned; he who stumbles on a mountain, what kind of eyes does he have? My brothers, the Jews are more excusable; for the Jews stumbled on a stone, the heretics stumble on a mountain. How did the Jews stumble on a stone? Because Christ, when he was suffering, was still small; and it was said, *They stumbled on the stone of stumbling*[^8]. But holy Daniel saw a vision, and wrote what he saw, and said that he saw a stone cut from a mountain without hands. It is Christ, coming from the nation of the Jews; for that too was a mountain, because it has a kingdom. What is, *Without hands*? A stone cut without human work, because no male work approached the Virgin, that he might be born without human work. A stone cut from the mountain without hands; and it broke the statue, in which the kingdoms of the earth were signified. And what was said? This is the stone on which the Jews stumbled; they stumbled on the stone of stumbling. What is the mountain on which the heretics stumbled? Hear Daniel himself: *And that stone grew*, he says, *and became a great mountain, so that it filled the whole face of the earth*[^9]. Rightly does the Psalm say to Christ the Lord rising again, *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and your glory above all the earth*[^10]. What is, *Your glory above all the earth*? Over all the earth your Church, over all the earth your bride. And yet she says, *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved*. Now I am everywhere, now I hold all lands, and to the Africans I am veiled. So tell me, lest perhaps I become as one veiled over the flocks, not of your sheep, but of your companions. For the companions made the schisms. Who are the companions? Those who approached the table of the Lord, of whom the Psalm says in another place, *Who ate my bread*[^11]; of whom it says, *If an enemy had reproached me, I would have borne it; and if he who hated me had spoken great things against me, I would have hidden myself from him; but you, a man of one mind, my guide and my familiar, who together with me took sweet food; in the house of the Lord we walked in agreement*[^12]. Once in agreement, now in disagreement, because without sense. These are the companions, into whom she feared lest she fall. I fear, she says, lest I go astray; I fear lest, as one veiled, I rush into the flocks of your companions, lest by going astray I perish, lest by repeating the baptism I received I lose it all.

**5.** You have heard the solicitude of the bride, hear the answer of the bridegroom. When this had been said by the bride, immediately the bridegroom: *Unless you know yourself, O beautiful among women*, catholic, beautiful among heresies, *Unless you know yourself*, unless you attend to knowing yourself there where you learned me, unless you set my Scriptures before human rumors, unless you know yourself, that you are everywhere, unless you know yourself, that you were designated where it was said, *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance*[^13]; unless, then, you know yourself, what? *Go out*; unless you know, go out. An evil word, a mournful word, Go out. May God turn it away from us! See, of whom it was said, *They went out from us, but they were not of us*[^14]. To the bad servant it is said: Go out, because *The servant does not remain in the house forever, the son remains forever*[^15]. Do you wish to see that it is to the bad servant that it is said, Go out? To the good servant, what is said? *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^16]. So let everyone who hears, everyone who is a member of his bride, fear what is said, *Unless you know yourself, O beautiful among women, go out in the footprints of the flocks*[^17]. What is, *in the footprints of the flocks*? Through the errors of men, not in the voice of the shepherd. Let us, brothers, not go out in the footprints of the flocks; we have the footprints of the shepherd, by following which we do not go astray. *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow in his footsteps*[^18]. So, unless you know yourself, go out in the footprints of the flocks, *And feed your kids*[^19]. Both kids, and yours. You know the sheep at the right hand, the kids at the left. And feed your kids. Why, your kids? Because you go out, you feed, like Donatus, your kids; but if you do not go out, you feed, like Peter, my sheep. The end.
