# Sermon 227: Delivered on the Holy Day of Easter

**1.** I am mindful of my promise. For I had promised you, who have been baptized, a sermon in which I would expound the sacrament of the Lord's table, which now too you see and of which last night you were made partakers. You ought to know what you have received, what you are going to receive, what you ought to receive daily. That bread which you see on the altar, sanctified through the word of God, is the body of Christ. That cup, rather what the cup holds, sanctified through the word of God, is the blood of Christ. Through these the Lord Christ willed to commend his body and blood, which he shed for us unto the forgiveness of sins. If you have received well, you are what you have received. For the Apostle says: *One bread, one body, we many are*[^1]. So he expounded the sacrament of the Lord's table: *One bread, one body, we many are.* In this bread it is commended to you how you ought to love unity. For was that bread made of one grain? Were there not many grains of wheat? But before they came to the bread, they were separate; through water they were joined after a certain crushing. For unless the wheat is ground and sprinkled with water, it does not come to that form which is called bread. So you too, before, by the humiliation of fasting and by the sacrament of exorcism, were as it were ground. There came baptism, and you were, as it were, sprinkled with water, that you might come to the form of bread. But it is not yet bread without fire. What, then, does the fire signify, that is, the chrism of oil? For fire, the nourisher of the Holy Spirit, is its sacrament. Note in the Acts of the Apostles when it is read; for that book is now beginning to be read: today began the book which is called Acts of the Apostles. He who wishes to advance has whence. When you assemble at the church, take away empty fables; be intent on the Scriptures. We are your books. Attend, then, and see, that the Holy Spirit is to come at Pentecost. And so he will come: in tongues of fire he showed himself. For he breathes in love by which we may burn for God and despise the world, and our hay may be burned up, and the heart purged as it were like gold. So the Holy Spirit comes, after water fire, and you are made bread, which is the body of Christ. And therefore unity is in a certain manner signified. You hold the sacraments in their order. First, after the prayer, you are admonished to have your heart on high; this befits the members of Christ. For if you have been made members of Christ, where is your head? Members have a head. If the head had not gone before, the members would not follow. Where did our head go? What did you give back in the Creed? *On the third day he rose from the dead, ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father.* Therefore our head is in heaven. Therefore, when it is said: *Hearts on high*, you answer: *We have them with the Lord.* And lest this very thing, that you have your heart on high with the Lord, you should attribute to your own strength, your merits, your labors, because it is the gift of God to have the heart on high, therefore the bishop, or the presbyter who offers, goes on and says — when the people have answered: *We have them with the Lord*, hearts on high — : *Let us give thanks to the Lord our God*, because we have our hearts on high. *Let us give thanks*, because, unless he granted it, we would have our heart on earth. And you bear witness, saying: *It is fitting and right*, that we give thanks to him who made us have our heart on high with our head. Then, after the sanctification of the sacrifice of God, because he willed that we ourselves be his sacrifice — which was shown, where that first sacrifice of God was set, and we too, that is, the sign of the thing, what we are — behold, where the sanctification has been accomplished, we say the Lord's Prayer, which you have received and given back. After it is said: *Peace be with you*, and the Christians kiss one another with a holy kiss. It is a sign of peace: as the lips show, let it be done in the conscience, that is, as your lips draw near to the lips of your brother, so let your heart not withdraw from his heart. Great sacraments, then, and very great. Do you wish to know how they are commended? The Apostle says: *He who eats the body of Christ or drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily, is guilty of the body and blood of the Lord*[^2]. What is to receive unworthily? To receive contemptibly, to receive mockingly. Let it not seem cheap to you, because you see it. What you see passes away, but what is signified, invisible, does not pass away, but remains. Behold, it is received, eaten, consumed. Is the body of Christ consumed? is the Church of Christ consumed? are the members of Christ consumed? Far be it. Here they are cleansed, there they are crowned. What is signified, then, will remain, although that which signifies seems to pass away. So receive it, then, that you may consider yourselves, may have unity in the heart, may always fix your heart on high. Let your hope be not on earth, but in heaven; let your faith be firm in God, acceptable to God. Because what now here you do not see and believe, you are to see there, where you will rejoice without end.

[^1]: 1 Cor 10:17
[^2]: See 1 Cor 11:27
