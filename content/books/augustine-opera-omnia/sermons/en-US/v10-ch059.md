# Sermon 375/A: Another Sermon on the Same Day, on the Sacraments, Begins

**1.** As the truth resounded through the Apostles, and *their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^1], so *Christ our paschal lamb has been sacrificed*[^2]. Of whom the prophet had foretold beforehand: *Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer he was without voice, so he opened not his mouth*[^3]. Who is this? Surely he of whom it follows and says: *In his humility his judgment was taken away. Who shall declare his generation?*[^4] I see an example of such great humility in a king of such great power. For this one, like a lamb before its shearer not opening his mouth, is himself the lion of the tribe of Judah. Who is this lamb and lion? He bore death like a lamb, he consumed it like a lion. Who is this lamb and lion? Gentle and strong, lovable and terrible, innocent and powerful, silent when judged, roaring when about to judge. Who is this lamb and lion? In the passion a lamb, in the resurrection a lion. Or rather both in the passion a lamb and a lion, and in the resurrection a lamb and a lion? Let us see the lamb in the passion. It has just now been said: *Like a lamb before its shearer he was without voice, so he opened not his mouth*. Let us see the lion in the passion. Jacob said: *You went up; lying down, you slept like a lion*[^5]. Let us see the lamb in the resurrection. The Apocalypse, speaking of the everlasting glory of virgins: *they follow*, it says, *the lamb wherever he goes*[^6]. Let us see the lion in the resurrection. The same Apocalypse says, what I have already set down above: *The lion of the tribe of Judah has prevailed to open the book*[^7]. Why a lamb in the passion? Because he took up death without iniquity. Why a lion in the passion? Because, slain, he slew death. Why a lamb in the resurrection? Because his innocence is everlasting. Why a lion in the resurrection? Because his power is everlasting.

**2.** Who is this lamb and lion? Why do you ask who he may be? If you ask what he was before: *In the beginning was the Word*. If where he was: *and the Word was with God*. If of what sort the Word was: *and the Word was God*. If of how great power he is: *all things were made through him*. If what he himself was made: *and the Word was made flesh*[^8]. If in what way he was born—either of the Father without a mother, or of a mother without a father: *Who shall declare his generation?*[^9] Begotten from eternity, coeternal with his begetter; the Word abiding, made flesh; the creator of all times, created at a fitting time; the prey of death, the despoiler of death; *without form in his lowliness beyond the sons of men, beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^10]; knowing how to bear weakness, and to take it away; lofty in making lowly things, lowly in making lofty things; God of man, and man God; both firstborn, and creator of the firstborn; both the only one, and brother of many; born of the Father's substance, and made a sharer with the adopted; both Lord of all, and servant of very many. This is the lamb who takes away the sins of the world; this is the lion who has conquered the kingdoms of the world. We were asking who this one is: let us ask who they are for whom he died. Perhaps for the righteous and the holy? The Apostle does not say this, but rather that *Christ died for the impious*[^11]: not, indeed, that the impious should remain impious, but that by the death of the righteous one the impious might be justified, and that by the shedding of blood, without sin, the bond of sin might be blotted out. Here ends the sermon on the sacraments, on the day of Pasch.
