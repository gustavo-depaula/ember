# Sermon 169

**1. 1.** Let your Holiness direct your ears and mind to the apostolic reading, helping us by your affection before the Lord our God, that the things he deigns to reveal to us we may be able to set forth to you fittingly and wholesomely. So when it was read, you heard the apostle Paul saying, *For we are the circumcision, who serve the Spirit of God*[^1]. I know that very many codices have: *Who serve God in spirit*. But as far as we could examine, more Greek copies have this: *Who serve the Spirit of God*. But the question is not there. For both are manifest, and agreeable to the rule of truth, since we both serve the Spirit of God, and serve God not in the flesh, but in the spirit. For he serves God in the flesh who hopes to please God by carnal things. But when even the flesh itself is subjected to the spirit for good works, we serve God in the spirit; because we tame the flesh, that the spirit may obey God. For the spirit rules, the flesh is ruled; nor does the spirit rule well, if it is not ruled.

**1. 2.** When, then, he says, *We are the circumcision*; see what he wished to be understood in that circumcision, which was given in the signifying shadow, which was removed when the light came. But why did he not say: We have circumcision; but: *We are the circumcision*? take it that the Apostle wished to say this: We are righteousness. For circumcision is righteousness. But he commends what he says more by saying that we are righteousness than by saying that we are righteous; yet so that when he says we are righteousness, we understand the righteous. For we are not that unchangeable righteousness of which we have been made partakers; but just as it is said: There is great youth there, for many young men; so righteousness is said, that the righteous may be understood. Hear this very thing more evidently, the same Apostle saying, *That we*, he says, *may be the righteousness of God in him*[^2]. *That we may be righteousness*—not our own, but *God's*; received from him, not assumed from ourselves; imparted, not usurped; given, not snatched. For to a certain one it was robbery to be equal to God; and because he sought robbery, he found ruin. But our Lord Jesus Christ, *since he was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*. For to one whose nature was equality with God, it was not robbery. *But* yet *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^3], that we might be the righteousness of God in him. For if he avoided poverty, we would not be free of poverty. *For he became poor, though he was rich; that by his poverty*, as it is written, *we might be enriched*[^4]. What will his riches do for us, whose poverty makes us rich? The Apostle, then, did not deny you circumcision, but expounded it; he held forth the light, removed the shadow.

**2. 3.** *We are*, he says, *the circumcision, who serve God in spirit, and glory in Christ Jesus, and do not trust in the flesh*[^5]. He looked at certain ones trusting in the flesh; they were the ones who gloried in the circumcision of the flesh. Of whom he says in another place, *Whose God is the belly, and whose glory is in their shame*[^6]. Understand the circumcision, and be the circumcision; understand, and be: *For good understanding*, but *to all who do it*[^7]. Surely not in vain was the infant commanded to be circumcised on the eighth day[^8], except because the rock by which we are circumcised was Christ. For the people was circumcised with knives of stone[^9]: *but the rock was Christ*[^10]. Why, then, on the eighth day? Because in the weeks the same is both first and eighth. For when the seven days are completed, one returns to the first. The seventh is ended, the Lord buried; one returns to the first, the Lord raised up. For the Lord's resurrection promised us the eternal day and consecrated for us the Lord's day. That which is called "the Lord's" seems properly to pertain to the Lord; because on that day the Lord rose again. The rock has been restored, let them be circumcised who wish to say, *For we are the circumcision*. *For he was handed over for our sins, and rose again for our justification*[^11]. Your justification, your circumcision, is not from you. *By grace you have been saved through faith; and this not from yourselves, but it is the gift of God; not from works*[^12]. Lest perhaps you say: I earned it, and therefore I received it. Do not suppose that you received it by earning, you who would not earn it, unless you had received. Grace went before your merit; not grace from merit, but merit from grace. For if grace is from merit; you bought it, you did not receive it freely. *For nothing*, he says, *you will save them*[^13]. What is, *For nothing you will save them*? You find nothing in them whence to save, and yet you save. You give freely, you save freely. You go before all merits, that your gifts may follow my merits. Plainly you give freely, you save freely, you who find nothing whence to save, and find much whence to condemn.

**3. 4.** *We*, then, he says, *are the circumcision, who serve the Spirit of God, and glory in Christ Jesus*[^14]. *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^15]. *And do not trust in the flesh*. And what is it to trust in the flesh? Hear, he says. *Although I*, he says, *have confidence even in the flesh. If anyone else thinks he has confidence in the flesh, I more*[^16]. Do not suppose, he says, that I despise what I do not have. What great thing is it, if an abject, plebeian, ignoble man despises nobility, and then exhibits a true humility? *Although I*, he says, *have confidence even in the flesh*. Therefore I teach you to despise it, since you see that I have what I despise. *If anyone else thinks he has confidence in the flesh, I more*.

**3. 5.** And hear the confidence in the flesh: *In the circumcision of the eighth day*; that is, not a proselyte, not a newcomer to the people of God, not circumcised as a grown man, but a Jew born of Jewish parents, I have the circumcision of the eighth day. *Of the race of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews, according to the law a Pharisee*[^17]. There were certain leading men, as it were set apart for Jewish nobility, not mingled with the contemptible commons, who were called Pharisees. For this word is said to be interpreted as a kind of separation, as in the Latin language one says "egregius" ("distinguished"), as it were separated from the flock ("grex"). But there were Israelites—that is, of the race of Israel—even those who had been separated from the temple. But there remained at the temple the tribe of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin. The tribe of Levi in the priests, the tribe of Judah the royal one, and the tribe of Benjamin—this alone remained at Jerusalem and at the temple of God, when that separation was made under the servant of Solomon[^18]. Do not, then, take lightly what he says, *of the tribe of Benjamin*; cleaving to Judah, not withdrawing from the temple. *A Hebrew of Hebrews, according to the law a Pharisee, according to zeal persecuting the Church*[^19]. Among his merits he mentions that he was a persecutor: *according to zeal*, he says. What zeal? I was not, he says, a lazy Jew; whatever there was that seemed adverse to my Law, I bore impatiently, I pursued sharply. This is nobility among the Jews; but with Christ humility is sought. Therefore there he is Saul, here Paul. "Saul" is a name derived from Saul. Who Saul was, you know; his stature was chosen, tall. So Scripture describes him, that he was taller than all, when he was chosen to be anointed as king[^20]. Paul was not so, but became Paul. For "Paul" is little, therefore "Paul" is small. Therefore: *According to zeal*, he says, *persecuting the Church*. Hence let men understand what kind I was among the Jews, I who persecuted the Church of Christ out of zeal for ancestral traditions.

**4. 6.** He adds: *According to the righteousness that is in the law, having been without complaint*[^21]. Your Charity knows that Zechariah and Elizabeth were said to have walked without complaint in all the justifications of the Lord. *Walking in all*, says Scripture, *the justifications of the Lord without complaint*[^22]. Behold, this is what our Paul too was, when he was Saul. In the law he walked without complaint; and that which in him was without complaint, this made a great complaint about him. What, then, do we suppose, brothers—is it evil to be without complaint according to the righteousness that is in the law? If it is evil to be without complaint according to the righteousness that is in the law; then is the law something evil? But we have the same Apostle saying, *And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*[^23]. If the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good; how can it not be good to conduct oneself without complaint according to the righteousness that is from the holy law? how can it not be holy? Or is it perhaps holy? Let us hear the Apostle himself; see what he says: *The things that were gains to me, these I reckoned losses for the sake of Christ*[^24]. He calls them his losses, and counts among his losses that he was without complaint in the righteousness that is in the law. *But yet*, he says, *I also reckon all things to be losses on account of the surpassing knowledge of Christ Jesus our Lord*. I attend, he says, to my praises, I compare them to the surpassing greatness of our Lord Jesus Christ. For that I thirst, this I despise. This is too little: *On account of whom I reckon*, he says, *all things not only to be detriments, but I even considered them to be dung, that I might gain Christ*[^25].

**5. 7.** A greater question has arisen, O Paul! If you conducted yourself without complaint according to the righteousness that is in the law, and you count this among your detriments, your losses, your dung, that you may gain Christ, did that righteousness, then, keep you from Christ? I beseech you, expound this a little. Let us rather say to God, that he may enlighten us too, by whom he himself was enlightened who wrote this Epistle to us, not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God[^26]. You see, dearly beloved, how arduous, how difficult it is to understand this; when it is agreed that *the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*; and it is agreed entirely among the faithful catholics—so that no one dissents, except one who does not wish to be a catholic—that this law was given only by the Lord our God; that to conduct oneself without complaint according to that righteousness which is in the law was an impediment to the Apostle, that he should not come to Christ; and that he did not come to Christ, unless he had counted that which he was without complaint according to the righteousness that is in the law among his losses and detriments and dung. Let us follow, then, and approach a little, lest perhaps in the very words of the Apostle something may dawn on us, whence this obscurity may be removed and resolved. *Detriments*, he says, *I believed all these things, and considered them dung, that I might gain Christ*[^27]. Attend, I beseech you. I considered these losses, detriments, dung—among which I also mention that I was without complaint according to the righteousness that is in the law. *I considered*, then, *all these things detriments and dung, that I might gain Christ, and be found in him, not having my own righteousness which is from the law*[^28]. You who have anticipated the exposition with your understanding, suppose yourselves as it were swift on the road, walking with slower companions. Let speed be somewhat restrained, lest the slower companion be abandoned. *That I may gain Christ*, he says, *and be found in him, not having my own righteousness which is from the law*. If he had said *my own*, why did he add *from the law*? For if it is from the law, how is it yours? Did you impose the law on yourself? God gave the law, God imposed the law, God commanded you to obey his law. If the law did not teach you how you ought to live, how could you have righteousness without complaint according to the law? if you have it according to the law, how do you say, *not having my own righteousness which is from the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, which is from God*[^29]?

**6. 8.** Now then I will say it as I can; let him reveal it better who possesses you, let him grant both understanding and affection. For he will grant the effect, if he grants the affection. For this is what I wish to say: when the law of God is set forth—for it said, *You shall not covet*; when the law of God is set forth, then, apart from those carnal sacraments which were shadows of things to come; when the law of God is set forth, whoever fears it, and supposes that he can fulfill it by his own strength, and does what the law commands, not loving righteousness, but fearing punishment; that man is indeed without complaint according to the righteousness that is from the law; he does not steal, does not commit adultery, does not bear false witness, does not commit murder, does not covet his neighbor's property; he can do this, perhaps he can; whence? From fear of punishment. Although the one who does not covet from fear of punishment, I think still covets. By the immense terror of arms and weapons, and perhaps of a surrounding multitude or one coming to meet it, even a lion is called back from its prey; and yet the lion came, the lion returns; it did not seize the prey, it did not lay aside its malice. If you are such, it is still righteousness, the righteousness by which you take counsel for yourself not to be tortured. What great thing is it to fear punishment? Who does not fear it? What robber, what criminal, what wicked man? But this is the difference between your fear and the robber's fear: the robber fears the laws of men, and therefore commits robbery, because he hopes to deceive the laws of men; but you fear his laws, fear his punishment, whom you cannot deceive. For if you could deceive, what would you not do? Therefore your evil concupiscence too is not taken away by love, but pressed down by fear. The wolf comes to the sheepfold; by the barking of the dogs love does not take away, but fear presses down. The wolf comes to the sheepfold; by the barking of the dogs and the shout of the shepherds the wolf returns from the sheepfold; yet it is always a wolf. Let it be turned into a sheep. For the Lord does even this; but it is his righteousness, not yours. For as long as you have your own, you can fear punishment, not love righteousness.

**7. 8.** Therefore, my brothers, does iniquity have its delights, and righteousness not have them? Does evil delight, and good not delight? It delights entirely; but: *The Lord will give sweetness, and our land will give its fruit*[^30]. Unless he first give sweetness, our land will have nothing but sterility. This righteousness, then, the Apostle coveted, was delighted; he was mindful of God, and was delighted[^31]; his soul coveted, and grew hot for the courts of the Lord[^32]; and all the things he held for great became cheap, became losses, detriments, dung.

**7. 9.** For hence too came that he persecuted the Church, according to zeal for ancestral traditions[^33]; thence it came, because he was establishing his own righteousness, not seeking the righteousness of God. For see by what he was persecuting the Church. *What, then, shall we say*? the same Apostle says in another place: *That the gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, laid hold of righteousness*. But what? *The righteousness that is from faith. But the gentiles who did not pursue righteousness*—that which is from the law, as their own, which comes from fear of punishment, not from love of righteousness; because *they did not pursue righteousness, laid hold of righteousness; but the righteousness that is from faith. But Israel*, he says, *pursuing the law of righteousness, did not arrive at the law of righteousness. Why? Because not from faith*[^34]. What is, *Because not from faith*? He did not hope in God, did not ask it of God, did not believe in him who justifies the ungodly[^35]; he was not like the tax collector casting his eyes to the ground, striking his breast, and saying, *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^36]. Therefore: *Pursuing the law of righteousness, he did not arrive at the law of righteousness. Why? Because not from faith, but as if from works. For they stumbled on the stone of stumbling*[^37]. Behold whence Saul was persecuting the Church. For when he persecuted the Church, he stumbled on the stone of stumbling. The humble Christ lay on the earth; in heaven indeed he himself was too, that flesh of his lifted up, raised from the dead; but unless Christ also lay on the earth, he himself would not cry to Saul, *Why do you persecute me*[^38]? Therefore that one lay, because he preferred humility; this one stumbled, because he did not see. And all this not-seeing, whence was it? From the swelling of pride. What is, From the swelling of pride? As it were from his own righteousness. From the law indeed, but his own. What is, From the law? Because in the precepts of the law. What is, His own? As it were from his own strength. Love was lacking, the love of righteousness, the love of the love of Christ. And whence love to him? Fear alone possessed him, but kept a place in the heart for the love to come. When he raged, raised up, boastful, glorying among the Jews themselves that according to zeal for ancestral traditions he persecuted the Church; when he seemed to himself lofty, he heard from above the voice of our Lord Jesus Christ, already sitting in heaven, and still commending humility: *Saul*, he said, *Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad*[^39]. I could let you go; for you would be vexed by my prickings, I would not be broken by your kicks; but I do not let you go. You rage, and I have mercy. *Why do you persecute me*? For I do not fear you, lest you crucify me again; but I wish you to acknowledge me, lest you kill not me, but yourself.

**8. 10.** The Apostle, then, shuddered, struck down and laid low, raised up and instructed. For there was fulfilled in him: *I will strike, and I will heal*. For he did not say: I will heal, and I will strike; but: *I will strike, and I will heal*[^40]. I will strike you, and I will give you myself. Thus, laid low, he shuddered at his own righteousness, in which he was surely without complaint, praiseworthy, great, as it were glorious among the Jews; he considered it detriments, believed it losses, reckoned it dung, *that he might be found in him not having his own righteousness which is from the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, which is*, he says, *from God*[^41]. But of those who stumbled on the stone of stumbling, what does the same Apostle say? *Because not*, he says, *from faith, but as if from works*. Because they, as if by their own righteousness, *stumbled on the stone of stumbling; as it is written: Behold, I place in Zion a stone of stumbling, and a rock of scandal; and he who believes in him will not be put to shame*[^42]. For he who believes in him will not have his own righteousness which is from the law, although the law is good; but he will fulfill the law itself, not by his own righteousness, but by that given from God. For so he will not be put to shame. *For love is the fullness of the law*[^43]. And whence is this love poured out in our hearts? Surely not from ourselves, but through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^44]. Therefore they stumbled on the stone of stumbling, and the rock of scandal. And he says of them, *Brothers, the good will indeed of my heart, and my supplication to God for them is unto salvation*. The Apostle supplicates for unbelievers, that they may believe; for the turned-away, that they may be converted. You see that not even conversion itself is without the help of God. *My supplication*, he says, *to God for them is unto salvation. For I bear witness that they have a zeal for God*[^45]. So he too had it; he had a zeal for God. But how did he have it? How did they have it: *But not according to knowledge*. What is this: *Not according to knowledge? For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own*[^46]. Whence this man, corrected, says, *Not having my own righteousness*. They wish to establish their own; it still delights them to lie in the dung. I do not have my own righteousness, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness from God; the righteousness, I say, from God, who justifies the ungodly.

**9. 11.** Take yourself away, take, I say, yourself from yourself, you impede yourself; if you build yourself, you build a ruin. *Unless the Lord build the house, in vain have they labored who build it*[^47]. Do not, then, wish to have your own righteousness. Surely it is from the law, surely it is from the law; surely God gave the law, and because the righteousness is from the law, let it not be yours. The apostle Paul speaks; let not those who love their own righteousness slander me. Behold where you have him: open, read, hear, see. Do not wish to have your own righteousness; the Apostle reckons it dung, although it is from the law, yet because it is one's own. *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not submitted to the righteousness of God*[^48]. Do not suppose that because you are called a Christian, you therefore cannot stumble on the stone of stumbling. Whose grace you disparage, on him you stumble. It is less to stumble on Christ hanging on the cross than on Christ sitting in heaven. Let there be righteousness, but let it be from grace, let it be to you from God; let it not be your own. *Let your priests*, he says, *be clothed with righteousness*[^49]. A garment is received, it is not born with the hair; cattle are clothed from their own. This the apostle Paul preaches: let it be to you from God. Groan, that you may obtain it; weep, that you may obtain it; believe, that you may obtain it. *He who*, he says, *calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^50]. Or do you suppose it was said thus: *He who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*; because from a fever, or a plague, or gout, or some pain of the body? Not so, but *will be saved*, will be righteous. Because: *Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick*. He expounded it when he said, *I came not to call the righteous, but sinners*[^51].

**10. 12.** See, then, what follows. *And that I may be found*, he says, *in him, not having my own righteousness which is from the law*; although from the law, yet my own: *but that which is through the faith of Christ*; which is obtained from God, *which is from God, the righteousness in faith, to know him, and the power of his resurrection*[^52]. It is something great, to know the power of Christ's resurrection. Do you suppose this is great, because he raised up his own flesh? Did he call this the power of his resurrection? Will there not also be our resurrection at the end of the age? Will not this our corruptible also put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality? Will it not be that, just as he rose from the dead, and now does not die, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^53]; so we too—more wonderfully, so to speak? For his flesh did not see corruption, ours is restored from ashes. It is indeed great, because he went before in example, and gave us what we might hope for; but this is not the only thing for the one who was speaking of righteousness not his own, but that which is from God, and there named the power of Christ's resurrection; acknowledge there your justification. For from his resurrection we are justified, as though we were circumcised by the rock. Therefore he began from there: *We are the circumcision*. Whence the circumcision? From the rock. What rock? Christ. How? On the eighth day. As the Lord rose on the Lord's day.

**11. 13.** This justification, then, my brothers, let us both have insofar as we have it, and increase it insofar as we are deficient, and perfect it when we come there, where it will be said, *Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting*[^54]? But all from God; yet not as though sleeping, not as though we should not strive, not as though we should not will. Without your will the righteousness of God will not be in you. The will indeed is none but yours, the righteousness none but God's. The righteousness of God can be without your will, but it cannot be in you apart from your will. It has been shown what you ought to do; the law commanded: Do not do this, not that; do this and that. It has been shown to you, commanded to you, opened to you; if you have a heart, you understood what you should do; ask that you may do it, if you know the power of Christ's resurrection. *For he was handed over for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^55]. What is, *for our justification*? That he may justify us, that he may make us righteous. You will be the work of God, not only because you are a man, but also because you are righteous. For it is better to be righteous than for you to be a man. If God made you a man, and you make yourself righteous; you do something better than God did. But God made you without you. For you did not bring any consent, that God might make you. How did you consent who did not exist? Therefore he who made you without you, does not justify you without you. He made you, then, not knowing it; he justifies you willing it. Yet he himself justifies, lest it be your own righteousness, lest you return to losses, to detriments and dung, to be found in him not having your own righteousness, *which is from the law, but the righteousness through the faith of Christ, which is from God; the righteousness from faith, to know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings*[^56]. And this itself will be your power; the fellowship of the sufferings of Christ will be your power.

**11. 14.** But what will be: in the fellowships of the sufferings of Christ, if there is no love? Are not robbers found, tortured, with such fortitude of body that some of them not only refused to betray their accomplices, but did not even deign to confess their own names; amid the torments, amid the tortures, their sides dug out, and their limbs almost destroyed, their mind remained in a most wicked obstinacy? See, then, what they loved. Yet they could not do these things without great love. But the lover of God is not so. God is not loved except from God. That one loved I know not what else of the flesh, like a man. Whatever he loved—he loved his associates, he loved the consciousness of his crimes, he loved glory in misdeeds; whatever he loved, he loved much, who could be tortured and could not fail. If, then, that one could not—who could be tortured, could not fail; if, then, that one could not endure such things without love; neither will you be able to share in the sufferings of Christ without love.

**12. 15.** But I ask, by what love? Let it not be cupidity, but let it be love. *For if*, he says, *I hand over my body to be burned, and have not love, it profits me nothing*[^57]. That the fellowship of the sufferings of Christ may profit you, let love be present. Whence have you love? O most beggarly weakness, whence have you the love of God? Do you wish me to show you whence it is to you? Question the Lord's storehouse itself. For if the love of God is in you, you will share in the sufferings of Christ and you will be a true martyr. In whom love is crowned, he will be a true martyr. Whence, then, is it to you? *We have this treasure in earthen vessels*, says the Apostle himself, *that the eminence of the power may be God's, and not from us*[^58]. Whence, then, have you love, except because *it has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^59]? Behold, for this groan. Despise your own spirit, receive the Spirit of God. Let not your spirit fear lest, when the Spirit of God begins to dwell in you, it suffer straits in your body. When the Spirit of God begins to dwell in your body, it will not for that reason shut out your spirit; do not fear. If you receive some rich man into your lodging, you suffer straits; you do not find where you may stay yourself, where a bed may be prepared for him, where the wife, where the children, where the household. What do I do, you say? Where do I go? Where shall I migrate? Receive the rich Spirit of God; you will be enlarged, not straitened. *You enlarged my steps beneath me*: you say. To your guest you will say, *You enlarged my steps beneath me*[^60]. When you were not here, I suffered straits; you filled my cell, and you did not shut me out, but my straits. For when he says, *The love of God has been poured out*, that very pouring out signifies breadth. Do not, then, fear straits, receive this guest; and let him not be a guest as of those passing by. For he does not give by departing: coming, let him dwell in you, and he has given. Be his, let him not forsake you, let him not migrate from there; hold him entirely, and say to him, *O Lord our God, possess us*[^61].

**13. 16.** Therefore for this, he says, let us have the righteousness which is from God, to know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, conformed to his death. *For we have been buried*, he says, *with Christ through Baptism into death; that as Christ rose from the dead, so we too may walk in newness of life*[^62]. Die, that you may live; be buried, that you may rise again. For when you have been buried and have risen again; then it will be true: Lift up your heart. What I have said has been savory. Surely this sermon would not be savory, unless there were in you an inner sweetness? *Conformed*, he says, *to his death, if by any means I may attain to the resurrection of the dead*[^63]. He was speaking of righteousness, the righteousness that is from the faith of Christ, the righteousness that is from God, and so he carried it all through. And when he sought righteousness, saying, *That I may be found in him, not having my own righteousness which is from the law, but the righteousness which is from the faith of Christ, which is from God*; he now says, *If by any means I may attain to the resurrection of the dead*. Why did you say, *If by any means I may attain*? *Not that I have already received it, or am already perfect; but I follow, if by any means I may lay hold, in which I was also laid hold of by Christ Jesus*. His righteousness has gone before me, let mine follow him. But then mine will follow, if it is not mine. *If by any means I may attain to the resurrection of the dead. Not that I have already received it, or am already perfect*. They began to wonder, who heard the Apostle saying this: *Not that I have already received it, or am already perfect*. What is it that he had not yet received? He had faith, he had virtue, he had hope, he burned with love, he worked virtues, he preached most unconquerably, he bore all persecutions, patient in all, loving the Church, bearing in his heart the care of all the churches; what had he not yet received? *Not that I have already received it, or am already perfect*. What is it that you say? You say it, and we wonder; you say it, and we are amazed. For we know what we hear; what do you say? *Brothers*, he says. What is it that you say? What do you say? *I do not reckon that I have laid hold of it*[^64]. Do not, he says, be deceived about me; I know myself more than you. If I know what is lacking to me, I know what is present. *I do not reckon that I have laid hold of it myself. But one thing*; this I do not reckon that I have laid hold of. I have many things, and one thing I have not yet laid hold of. *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek*. What did you ask, or what do you seek? *That I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life*. For what? *That I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*[^65]. This is the one thing which the Apostle said he had not yet laid hold of; and as much as was lacking to him, by so much he was not yet perfect.

**14. 17.** You remember, my brothers, that gospel reading where two sisters received the Lord, Martha and Mary. Surely you recall it: Martha was occupied in much ministry and busied about the care of the house; for she had received the Lord and his disciples into her lodging. She busied herself with every most devout care, lest the holy ones should suffer any wrong with her. So when she was occupied about much ministry, Mary her sister sat at the feet of the Lord, and heard his word. The other, indignant in her labor, because she saw her sister sitting and caring nothing for her labors, appealed to the Lord: *Does it please you, Lord*, she said, *that my sister has left me, and behold I labor so much in ministry*? And the Lord: *Martha, Martha, you are occupied about much. But one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken from her*[^66]. You the good, but she the better. You the good (for it is good to be occupied in the service of the saints), but she the better. Finally, what you chose passes away. You minister to the hungry, you minister to the thirsty, you minister beds to those who will sleep, you offer a house to those who wish to dwell in it; all these things pass away. There will be a time when no one will hunger, no one thirst, no one sleep. Therefore your care will be taken from you. *Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken from her. It will not be taken*; she chose to contemplate, she chose to live by the word. What will the life from the Word be, without word? Now she lived by the Word, but with the word sounding. There will be a life from the Word, with no word sounding. The Word itself is life. *We shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^67]. This was the one thing, that she might contemplate the delight of the Lord. This we cannot do in the night of this age. *In the morning I will stand before you, and contemplate*[^68]. Therefore: *I*, he says, *do not reckon that I have laid hold of it. But one thing*[^69].

**15. 18.** What, then, do I do? *Forgetting what is behind, stretching out to what is ahead, according to my intention I follow*. Still I follow: *toward the palm of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^70]. Still I follow, still I make progress, still I walk, still I am on the way, still I stretch myself out, I have not yet arrived. Therefore if you too walk, if you stretch yourself out, if you think on the things that are to come; forget the past, do not look back at it, lest you remain there where you have looked back. Remember Lot's wife[^71]. *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind*. He had said: I am not perfect, and he says, *As many as are perfect, let us be of this mind. I do not reckon that I have laid hold of it. Not that I have already received it, or am already perfect*; and he says, *As many as are perfect, let us be of this mind*. Perfect, and not perfect; perfect travelers, not yet perfect possessors. And that you may know that he calls them perfect travelers; those who now walk on the way are perfect travelers; that you may know he said travelers, not dwellers, not possessors; hear what follows: *As many of us, then, as are perfect, let us be of this mind. And if you are of any other mind*, lest perhaps it creep upon you that you are something. But he who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, seduces himself[^72]. And he who thinks he knows something does not yet know as he ought to know[^73]. Therefore: *And if you are of any other mind*, as little ones; *that too God will reveal to you. But in what we have arrived, in it let us walk*[^74]. So that God may reveal to us even what we are of another mind about, and in what we have arrived, let us not remain in it, but in it let us walk. You see that we are travelers. You say: What is it to walk? I say briefly: To make progress; lest perhaps you not understand, and walk more lazily. Make progress, my brothers, examine yourselves always without deceit, without flattery, without indulgence. For there is not someone within with you before whom you may blush and boast of yourself. He is there, but one to whom humility is pleasing; let him prove you. Prove yourself too, you yourself. Let what you are always displease you, if you wish to arrive at what you are not yet. For where you have pleased yourself, there you have remained. But if you say: It is enough; you have perished too. Always add, always walk, always make progress; do not remain on the way, do not go back, do not turn aside. He remains, who does not make progress; he goes back, who returns to the things from which he had already withdrawn; he turns aside, who apostatizes. Better does the lame man go on the way than the runner off the way. Let us turn to the Lord, etc.
