# Sermon 384: On the Trinity, or on the Old and New Scriptures,

Against the Arians

**1.1.** The holy and divine words, brothers, are continually, indeed daily, read aloud to us for our health, that our souls may be fed; but in the age to come they will be fattened with eternal feasts, as the prophet says: *I shall be satisfied when your glory is made manifest*[^1]. But what this future glory may be, and with what riches it flourishes, and with how great splendor it shines, we can praise but cannot unfold. Why? Because we read: *Neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love him*[^2]. If, therefore, the eternal heavenly goods are so great and such as the almighty Lord has prepared for his holy catholic and faithful peoples, what is God himself, who prepared things so great and such? What, I say, is the almighty God? What, except inestimable, ineffable, incomprehensible, beyond all things, outside all things, apart from all things? For he exceeds his every creature, surpasses his every work, excels all things. For if you seek greatness, he is greater; if beauty, more beautiful; if sweetness, sweeter; if splendor, more shining; if righteousness, more righteous; if strength, stronger; if mercy, more merciful. For no reason allows that either the thing made be made equal to its maker, or the work be compared to the artificer: as is read in the prophet: *He who made strong things is stronger; and he who made beautiful things is more beautiful than they*[^3].

**2.2.** But we proclaim the lofty marks of this one godhead in such a way, however, that we confess equally—not like the Jews, but like Christians—the mysteries of the divine Trinity. For just as the Father is almighty and ineffable, so the Son is almighty and incomprehensible; so too the Holy Spirit, connected without distinction in the Father and the Son, is ineffable and immense. For the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one almighty God, one in trinity, one in power, unity, trinity, everlasting majesty, one powerful through all things; and the trinity consists in unity, and the unity in trinity: but neither is the trinity divided, nor the unity separated. Armed and instructed, then, with this catholic faith, dearest ones, let us briefly question the abominable Arian heretics, who in this time impiously boast of themselves, and by seducing corrupt and deceive many of our Christians, how they pray to the Lord who hold against the Lord. They answer us, saying: We pray to the Lord, indeed, in trinity, but, as we read, the Father greater, the Son less, the Holy Spirit inferior, because Christ himself says: *The Father is greater than I*[^4].

**3.2.** To these we answer: So, I say, do you pray to and worship God? Just so; thus we read, thus we worship, thus we pray. To these we say: If, then, you worship and pray to God thus, this is not to worship and entreat one great God, but to make three gods: and where is what is read in the divine Law: *Hear, O Israel; the Lord your God is one God*[^5]? And again elsewhere it says: *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*[^6]. For if the Father is one thing, the Son another, the Holy Spirit another, then it is no longer one trinity, but a divided power. But how can a discordant power stand, when, according to what we read in the Gospel: *A house divided against itself, or a kingdom, cannot stand*[^7]? But how is the divinity divided from itself, when the splendor of light or the heat of the sun is in no way separated? For behold, just as we see, in the sun there are three things, and they can in no way be separated. But let us see what the three are: the course, the splendor, and the heat. For we see the sun running in the heaven, shining, warming. Divide, then, if you can, Arian, the sun, and only then divide the Trinity. But perhaps the reasoning about distinguishing the sun is difficult, because it is in the heaven, and set far from us. Behold, we propose another element which is smaller, and is on earth with us: I mean fire, which is held in our hands, and yet is not divided. For fire too has three things, and cannot be divided: that is, motion, light, and heat. If, then, abominable heretic, you cannot divide the created sun and fire, how can you divide God the creator of all things?

**4.3.** But hear and learn that this great and unique Trinity was shown from the beginning of the human race. Hear it declared, without doubt, in the Law and the Prophets, in the Psalms and in the Gospel, hear it in the Apostle. Hear, I say, in Genesis: *God made man to the image of God*[^8]. That he might at the same time show the inseparable Trinity, he says in that book: *And the Spirit of God was borne over the waters*[^9]. Hear the prophet saying in the person of Christ: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me; to evangelize the poor he has sent me*[^10]. Hear in the Psalms: *By the word of the Lord the heavens were established, and by the spirit of his mouth all their power*[^11]; and again: *Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and strengthen me with a principal spirit*[^12]. Hear this same thing confirmed in the Gospel: the Lord Christ says to the Apostles: *All power has been given to me in heaven and on earth: go, baptize all nations in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit*[^13]. Hear the Apostle: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How inscrutable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, that it should be repaid him? For from him, and through him, and in him are all things: to him be glory forever and ever*[^14]. If, therefore, it has been proved sufficiently and openly, by the Old Scriptures as well as the New, that the divine unity is an inseparable Trinity, the heretics being utterly spurned, according to the Apostle who says, *Avoid a heretic after one admonition*[^15], let us now confirm our faithful peoples in this very catholic faith. For there are no greater riches, no treasures, no honors, no greater substance of this world, than is the catholic faith, which saves sinful men, enlightens the blind, cures the weak, baptizes catechumens, justifies the faithful, restores penitents, increases the just, crowns the martyrs, preserves virgins, widows, and the married in chaste modesty, ordains clerics, consecrates priests, prepares for the heavenly kingdoms, and gives a share in the eternal inheritance with the holy angels. As the Lord himself confirms by promising: *In the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but will be equal to the angels of God*[^16]; through Christ our Lord.
