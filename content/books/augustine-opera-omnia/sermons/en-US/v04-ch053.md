# Sermon 134

**1.** Your Charity knows that we all have one Master, and under him are fellow disciples. Nor are we for this reason your masters, because we speak to you from a higher place: but the master of all is he who dwells in all of us. He himself just now in the Gospel spoke to all of us, and said to us, what I too say to you: but he says of us, both to us and to you: *If you abide in my word*, not surely in mine, who now speak; but in his, who just now spoke from the Gospel: *If you abide in my word*, he says, *you are truly my disciples*. To be a disciple it is too little to approach, but to abide. He did not, then, say: If you hear my word; or: If you approach my word; or: If you praise my word: but see what he said: *If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will free you* [^1]. What do we say, brothers? To abide in the word of God, is it labor, or not? If it is labor, attend to the great reward; if it is not labor, you receive the reward freely. Let us abide, then, in him who abides in us. We, if we do not abide in him, fall; but he, if he does not abide in us, does not for that reason lose his house. For he knows how to abide in himself, who never forsakes himself. But far be it from a man, to abide in himself, who has lost himself. Therefore we abide in him out of need: he abides in us out of mercy.

**2.** Now then, if it is set forth what we ought to do, let us see what we are to receive. For he enjoined a work, and promised a reward. What is the work? *If you abide in my word*. A brief work; brief in word, great in deed: *If you abide*. What is: *If you abide?* If you build on the rock. O how great this is, brothers, to build on the rock, how great it is! *The rivers came, the winds blew, the rain came down, and struck against that house, and it did not fall: for it was founded upon the rock* [^2]. What is it, then, to abide in the word of God, except to yield to no temptations? What is the reward? *You will know the truth, and the truth will free you*. Suffer with us, because you perceive my voice to be blunt; help me with quietness. O reward! *You will know the truth*. Perhaps someone would say: And what does it profit me, to know the truth? *And the truth will free you*. If the truth does not delight, let liberty delight. In the custom of the Latin tongue "to be freed" is said in two ways; and most of all we are accustomed to hear this word, so that whoever is freed is understood to escape a peril, to be without troubles. But properly "to be freed" is said to be made free: just as to be saved, to be made safe; to be healed, to be made whole; so to be freed, to be made free. Therefore I said: If the truth does not delight, let liberty delight. This sounds more evidently in the Greek tongue, nor can it be understood otherwise. And that you may know that it cannot be understood otherwise; when the Lord spoke, the Jews answered: *We have never served anyone; how do you say: The truth will free you* [^3]? That is: The truth will make you free, how do you say it to us, who have never served anyone? Whom you see, they say, not to have the necessity of servitude, how do you promise them liberty?

**3.** They heard what they ought: but they did not do what they ought. What did they hear? Because I said: *The truth will free you*; you attended to yourselves, that you do not serve a man, and you said: *We have never served anyone. Every*: Jew and Greek, rich and poor, honored and private, emperor and beggar, *everyone who commits sin, is a servant of sin* [^4]. *Everyone*, he says, *who commits sin, is a servant of sin*. If men acknowledge their servitude, they will see from where they may receive liberty. Someone freeborn is taken captive by barbarians, from freeborn he is made a servant; a merciful man hears, considers that he has money, becomes a redeemer, goes to the barbarians, gives money, redeems the man. Plainly he gave back liberty, if he took away iniquity. But who took away iniquity? Man from man? He who served among the barbarians, was redeemed by his redeemer; and there is much difference between the redeemer and the redeemed; yet under the mistress iniquity perhaps they are fellow servants. I ask the redeemed: Do you have sin? I have, he says. I ask the redeemer: Do you have sin? I have, he says. Therefore neither do you boast yourself redeemed, nor do you lift yourself up, redeemer: but flee both to the true liberator. It is too little that those who are under sin are called servants; they are also called dead. What a man fears lest captivity do to him, iniquity has already done to him. For what, because they seem to live, did he therefore err who said: *Let the dead bury their dead* [^5]? Therefore all under sin are dead, dead servants, dead by serving, servants by dying.  
Who, then, frees from death and from servitude, except one free among the dead? Who is free among the dead, except one without sin among sinners? *Behold, the prince of the world comes*, our Redeemer himself says, our Liberator: *Behold, the prince of the world comes, and will find nothing in me* [^6]. He holds those whom he deceived, whom he seduced, to whom he persuaded sin and death; *in me he will find nothing*. Come, Lord; come, Redeemer, come; let the captive recognize you, let the captor flee you: be a liberator for me. He found me lost, in whom the devil found nothing of his own that he does. The prince of this world found flesh in the Lord, he found: and what kind of flesh? Mortal, which he could hold, which he could crucify, which he could kill. You err, deceiver, the Redeemer is not deceived: you err. You see in the Lord mortal flesh, it is not sinful flesh: it is the likeness of sinful flesh. *For God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh*. True flesh, mortal flesh, but not sinful flesh. *For God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, that from sin he might condemn sin in the flesh. For God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh*: in flesh, yet not in sinful flesh; but *in the likeness of sinful flesh*. For what? *That from sin*, which surely was none in him, *he might condemn sin in the flesh: that the righteousness of the Law might be fulfilled in us, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the spirit* [^7].

**4.** If, therefore, it was the likeness of sinful flesh, not sinful flesh; how is it: *That from sin he might condemn sin in the flesh?* The likeness too is wont to take the name of the thing of which it is the likeness. A man is called true: but even if you show one painted on a wall, and ask what it is, the answer is: A man. So flesh having the likeness of sinful flesh was called sin, that it might be a sacrifice for sin. The same Apostle says in another place: *Him who did not know sin, he made sin for us* [^8]. *Him who did not know sin*; whom *who did not know sin*, except him who said: *Behold, the prince of the world comes, and will find nothing in me* [^9]? *Him who did not know sin, he made sin for us*; that very Christ knowing no sin, God *made sin for us*. What is this, brothers? If it were said: He made sin in him, or: He made him have sin; it would seem intolerable: how do we tolerate what was said: *He made him sin*, so that Christ himself is sin? Those who know the Scriptures of the Old Testament recognize what I say. For not once was it said, but several times, very often sacrifices for sins were called sins. There was offered, for example, for sin a goat, a ram, whatever: that very victim which was offered for sin was named sin [^10]. So sin was called the sacrifice for sin: so that somewhere the Law says, that the priests ought to set their hands upon the sin [^11]. Therefore: *Him who did not know sin, he made sin for us*; that is: he was made a sacrifice for sin. Sin was offered, and sin was blotted out. The blood of the Redeemer was poured out, and the bond of the debtor was blotted out. This is the blood which was poured out for many for the remission of sins.

**5.** What is it, then, that you have foolishly exulted, my captor, because my Liberator had mortal flesh? If he had sin, look: if you found anything of yours in him, hold it. *The Word was made flesh* [^12]. The Word is creator, the flesh is creation. What is yours there, enemy? And the Word is God, and the soul of a man is a creature, and the flesh of a man is a creature, and the mortal flesh of God is a creature. Seek sin. But what do you seek? Truth speaks: *The prince of this world will come, and will find nothing in me* [^13]. Therefore he did not fail to find flesh, but nothing of his own, that is, no sin. You deceived the innocent, you made them guilty. You killed the innocent; you destroyed one whom you ought not, give back what you held. Why, then, for an hour did you exult, because you found mortal flesh in Christ? It was your trap: from where you rejoiced, from there you were caught. Where you exulted to have found something, from there now you grieve to have lost what you possessed. Therefore, brothers, we who believe in Christ, let us abide in his word. For if we abide in his word, we are truly his disciples. For not those twelve alone, but all of us who abide in his word, are truly his disciples. And we shall know the truth, and the truth will free us; that is, Christ the Son of God, who said: *I am the truth* [^14]. He will make us free; that is: he will free us, not from barbarians, but from the devil; not from the captivity of the body, but from the iniquity of the soul. He alone is the one who so frees. Let no one call himself free, lest he remain a servant. Our soul will not remain in servitude, because daily our debts are forgiven.
