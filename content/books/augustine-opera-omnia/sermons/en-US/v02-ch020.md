# Sermon 33

**1** Since it is written: *O God, I will sing a new song to you, on a psaltery of ten strings I will sing to you*[^1], by the psaltery of ten strings are understood the ten precepts of the law. But singing and playing are usually the business of lovers. For the old man is in fear, the new in love. So too we distinguish the two Testaments, the old and the new, which the Apostle says are prefigured allegorically even in the sons of Abraham, one of the slave woman, the other of the free: *which are*, he says, *two Testaments*[^2]. For slavery pertains to fear, freedom to love. For the Apostle says: *For you did not receive the spirit of slavery again unto fear, but you received the spirit of adoption of sons, in which we cry: Abba, Father*[^3]. John too says: *Fear is not in love, but perfect love casts out fear*[^4]. Love, then, sings the new song. For that servile fear, set in the old man, can indeed have a psaltery of ten strings—because to the carnal Jews too was given that very law of ten precepts—but it cannot sing the new song on it. For it is under the law and cannot fulfill the law. It carries the instrument itself, it does not play it, and is burdened by the psaltery, not adorned. But he who is under grace, not under the law, fulfills the law, because to him it is not a weight but an adornment, nor is it a torment to one who fears but an ornament to one who loves. For, kindled by the Spirit of love, he now sings the new song on the psaltery of ten strings.

**2** For thus the Apostle says: *For he who loves another has fulfilled the law. For: You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not steal, you shall not covet, and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. Love of the neighbor works no evil. But the fullness of the law is love*[^5]. The Lord too, because he had said, *I came not to abolish the law, but to fulfill it*[^6], therefore gave his disciples such a commandment, whence the law could be fulfilled by them: *A new commandment*, he says, *I give you, that you love one another*[^7]. No wonder, then, if the new commandment sings the new song, because, as has been said, the psaltery of ten strings is the ten precepts of the law, and the fullness of the law is love. But the Apostle wished to recall a few strings from it, that from them the rest might be understood, where he says: *For: You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill*, and so on. For just as there are two precepts of love, on which the Lord says the whole Law and the Prophets depend, showing well enough that love is the fullness of the law, so those ten precepts were given on two tablets. Three indeed are said to be written on one tablet, and seven on the other. And just as those three pertain to the love of God, so the other seven are assigned to the love of the neighbor.

**3** The first of those three is: *Hear, O Israel: the Lord your God is one Lord. You shall not make for yourself an idol, or any likeness, whether of things in heaven above, or of things on earth below*[^8], and the rest by which he binds them to the worship of the one God, abandoning the fornication of idols. The second precept is: *You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain*[^9]. The third, concerning the observance of the sabbath. I believe that on account of the Trinity three precepts pertain to the love of God. For the unity of the Godhead has its beginning from the Father; hence the first precept speaks chiefly of the one God. But we are warned by the second precept not to think the Son of God a creature, if we should take him as unequal to the Father. *For every creature*, as the Apostle says, *is subject to vanity*[^10]. But there it is commanded not to take the name of the Lord our God in vain. Now indeed the gift of God, which is the Holy Spirit, promises everlasting rest, which is figured by the sabbath. Whence we observe the sabbath spiritually, if we do not do servile works. For from these the Jews too, by a carnal understanding, are forbidden on the sabbath. But that he wants servile works to be understood spiritually, let him hear the Lord saying: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^11]. But sin is not only that which appears to men in a base or wicked deed, but even if it have the appearance of a good work, and yet be done for the sake of a temporal reward and not for the sake of everlasting rest. For whatever anyone does, if he does it with the intention of obtaining earthly gain, he does it slavishly, and therefore does not observe the sabbath. For God is to be loved freely, nor can the soul rest except in what it loves. But eternal rest is not given to it except in the love of God, who alone is eternal. And this is the perfect sanctification and the spiritual sabbath of sabbaths. Wherefore, since we are sanctified in the Holy Spirit, who is there who is not moved to understand a great sacred sign, that among the three precepts pertaining to God the third precept is concerning the sabbath? And among all the things which Scripture in the book of *Genesis* recalls that God made, it is not said there that he sanctified anything except the seventh day, which signifies the sabbath.

**4** But of the seven precepts that are assigned to the love of the neighbor, the first is: *Honor your father and your mother*[^12]. The second: *You shall not kill*[^13]. The third: *You shall not commit adultery*[^14]. The fourth: *You shall not steal*[^15]. The fifth: *You shall not bear false witness*[^16]. The sixth: *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*[^17]. The seventh: *You shall not covet your neighbor's property*[^18]. To this distribution the Apostle plainly attests where he says: *Honor your father and your mother, which is the first commandment*[^19]. For it is asked, and it is not found first in the whole Decalogue, because the first of the ten commandments is that in which it is commanded to worship the one God. And therefore what is written about honoring parents is on the other tablet, and it is first because from there begin the precepts that are referred to the love of the neighbor.

**5** Let us, then, sing a new song, playing on the psaltery of ten strings. This is the new song, the grace of the new Testament, which distinguishes us from the old man, who was first made earthy from the earth. For he was made from mud, and, having lost blessedness, was justly cast into misery, since he had become a transgressor of the precept. But what does he say in the prophet who gives thanks to the grace of God, reconciling us to God through the remission of sins, and renewing the things past in their oldness? *He led me up*, he says, *from the pit of misery and from the mire of the mud, and set my feet upon a rock, and directed my steps, and put into my mouth a new song, a hymn to our God*[^20]. This is the new song, which he plays on the psaltery of ten strings. For no one praises God—that is, says a hymn—unless he agrees with his mouth by his deeds, by loving God and neighbor. Nor let the rebaptizing Donatists think that they pertain to the new song. For they do not sing the new song, who, in proud impiety, have been cut off from the Church, which God willed to be in all the earth. For in another place the same prophet says: *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^21]. He, then, who does not want to sing with all the earth, not departing from the old man, does not sing the new song, nor play on the psaltery of ten strings, because he is an enemy of love, which alone is the fullness of the law, which we say is contained in the ten precepts pertaining to the love of God and neighbor.
