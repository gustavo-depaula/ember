# Sermon 229/L: A Tractate on the Thursday of Holy Easter

**1.** From the Gospel reading today according to John the resurrection of the Lord was recited, and we heard that the Lord was sought by the disciples, and not found in the tomb; and this the women announced, not believing that he had risen, but that he had been stolen from the tomb by theft. And when two disciples came—John the evangelist himself (for he is understood to be the one whom Jesus loved), and Peter with him—and they entered, and saw the linen cloths alone, but no body; what is written of John himself? If you have noticed: *He entered,* he says, *and saw, and believed*[^1]. This that you heard, *he believed,* is not praised faith; for both true things and false are believed. For if his faith were praised or commended in what was said: *he saw, and believed,* the Scripture would not go on to say: *For he did not yet know the Scriptures, that Jesus must rise from the dead*[^2]. Therefore *he saw, and believed*; what, then, did he believe? What did he believe, but what the woman had said, that the Lord had been taken away from the tomb? For she had said: *They have taken away the Lord from the tomb, and I do not know where they have laid him*[^3]. They ran, they entered, they saw the linen cloths alone, no body; and they believed that he had perished, not that he had risen. They saw that he had been taken from the tomb; the men believed he had been taken away, and they departed. The woman stood, and began to seek the body of Jesus with tears, began to weep at the tomb; they had less care, stronger in sex, but lesser in affection. The woman sought Jesus more, because she herself had first lost Jesus in paradise; because through her death had entered, the more she sought life. And yet how did she seek? As the body of a dead man, not the incorruption of the living God; for not even she herself believed that the body had therefore not been in the tomb because the Lord had risen. Entering, she saw angels. Behold, the angels did not show themselves to Peter and John, and they showed themselves to the woman. Plainly, dearly beloved, this is commended, that the weaker sex sought more, the sex that first, as we said, lost. The angels see her, and say: *He is not here, he has risen*[^4]. Still she stands and weeps, still she does not believe; she supposed that the Lord had perished from the tomb. She saw Jesus too: she does not think it Jesus, she thought him a gardener, she still demands the body of a dead man. *If you,* she says, *have taken him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away*[^5]. What need have you of what you do not love? Let it be given to me, she says. She who sought the dead thus, how did she believe in the living? Afterward the Lord calls her by her own name. Mary recognized the voice, looked back at the Savior; and she answered him as though to himself: *Rabboni, which means, Lord*[^6].

**2.** What does it mean, what follows: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father? Go, and tell my disciples: I will ascend to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God*[^7]? This indeed presents no question. *I will ascend to my Father,* because I am the Only-begotten; *and to your Father, because you have been adopted, and you all say together: Our Father who are in heaven*[^8]. And *my God and your God.* Not mine as yours: *my God,* because I was made man; *your God,* because you are always men. For in this respect God is the Father of Christ, because he was made man; therefore he is his God, the God of the creature of the only-begotten Word. Ask the psalm: *From my mother's womb you are my God*[^9]. Before the mother's womb, my Father; from the mother's womb, my God. This, then, presents no question. But that other thing somewhat troubles those of lesser understanding, what this means: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father.* On earth he was not being touched by her; when he had ascended, could he be touched? What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*? Recall, when he appeared to his disciples, and they thought he was a spirit, what he said to them. It was read yesterday. *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts rise up in your heart? See my hands and my feet: touch, and see*[^10]. To the men he said: *Touch,* before he ascended to the Father; and would the woman not touch him, except when he had ascended to the Father? What is it, then, that we should understand? Far be it from us to understand it so. Mary, to see, sought the body; let us, to understand, desire his spirit. *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father.* What is it, *Do not touch me*? Do not believe only this far, do not be fixed on the man; there is something greater, which you do not understand. You see me lowly on earth: you touch me, and you remain on earth. Touch me as higher, believe in me as higher, believe me to be the Only-begotten, equal to the Father; for when you have understood me to be equal, then I have ascended to the Father for you. But to touch with the heart, this is to believe; for that woman too, who touched the hem, touched with the heart, because she believed. So he feels the one touching, and did not feel the crowd pressing. *Someone touched me,* says the Lord: *he touched me,* he believed in me. And the disciples, not understanding what *he touched me* means, said: *The crowd presses you, and you say: Who touched me?* I do not know what I should say: Someone touched me? The crowd presses, faith touches. Let Christ, then, ascend for us, and let us touch him, if we believe in him, that he is the Son of God, eternal, coeternal, not from the time he was born of the Virgin, but eternal. For he will make us too eternal, not because we always were, but because we always will be. He coeternal, equal to the Father, without time, before all times, through whom times were made; before the day, day from day, who founded the day. Believe thus, and you have touched. Touch thus, that you may cling; cling thus, that we may never be separated, but may abide with him in the divinity, who for us died in weakness.
