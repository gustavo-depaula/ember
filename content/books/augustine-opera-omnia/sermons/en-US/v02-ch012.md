# Sermon 27

**1** Just as a door brings one into a house, so the title of a psalm brings one into the understanding of it. For it is headed thus: *When the house was being built after the captivity*[^1]. You ask what house; the psalm now indicates it to you: *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^2]. See what house. When all the earth sings a new song, it is the house of God. By singing it is built, by believing it is founded, by hoping it is raised, by loving it is perfected. Now, then, it is being built, but at the end of the world it is dedicated. Let the living stones, then, run together to the new song, let them run together and be fitted into the structure of the temple of God. Let them acknowledge the Savior, let them receive the indweller.

**2** It has been said what house. It is to be said after what captivity. And this too the psalm indicates to you. Follow a little: *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth. Sing to the Lord, bless his name, proclaim well from day to day his salvation. Announce among the nations his wonders, among all peoples his glory*[^3]. *For all the gods of the nations are demons*[^4]. See under whom the house lay captive. For from the first transgression of the first man, the whole human race, born with the obligation of sin, the victorious devil possessed. For if we were not held in captivity, we would not need a Redeemer. He came to the captives, not captive himself. He came to redeem the captives, having in himself nothing of captivity—that is, of iniquity—but bearing our price in mortal flesh. For if he did not have mortal flesh, whence in the Word would there be blood, which he might shed for the captives? But he who came to our captivity came with the likeness of sinful flesh, not with sinful flesh. For it was the likeness of sinful flesh: true flesh, but like sinful flesh; true flesh, but not the flesh of sin. He, then, who came thus, who was he? *Proclaim well from day to day*[^5]. See who he was. He was from day to day, he was God from God, he was light from light. But the Word became flesh, that he might dwell among us: majesty lying hidden, weakness appearing, that the weakness might die and the majesty be held.

**3** If, then, the whole world was held in captivity, it was well said: *I will have mercy on whom I have been merciful, and I will show mercy to whom I have had pity*[^6]. For if the whole world was under captivity, the whole world in sin, the whole world most justly destined to punishment, but in part freed through mercy, who would say to God, "Why do you condemn the world?" How is the judge God accused, when the guilty world is condemned? You are guilty. If you consider what you owe, it is called punishment, and when what is owed is exacted from you, the exactor is not justly reproached. Let the exactor be reproached, if he exacts what is not owed. But when he exacts what is owed, who would reproach the exactor, however much he awaits a forgiver? *He has mercy on whom he wills, and whom he wills he hardens. You will say therefore to me: Why does he still find fault? For who resists his will? O man, who are you that you should answer back to God?*[^7] Attend to who he is. Attend to who you are. He is God, you a man. But do you seem to yourself to speak justice, and is that fountain of justice dried up? If you speak what is just, whence is it to you? Either you speak what is unjust, and you ought to be silent; or you speak what is just, and you have it only from the fountain of justice. And who is the fountain of justice, except God? First, then, lay the foundation of faith: *Is there iniquity with God?*[^8] Equity can lie hidden from you; iniquity cannot be there.

**4** You expect from me, perhaps, that I should tell you why he has mercy on whom he wills, and whom he wills he hardens. Do you expect this from me, a man? If you too are a man and I am a man, we have both heard: *O man, who are you that you should answer back to God?*[^9] Better, then, is a faithful ignorance than a rash knowledge. God says to me, through the Apostle Christ speaks: *O man, who are you that you should answer back to God?* And shall I be indignant because I do not know the justice of God? If I am a man, let me not be indignant. Let me go beyond man, if I can, and reach the fountain. But even if I reach it, I will not tell a man. Let him too go beyond, and reach it with me. "And who is it," you say, "that a man should go beyond man?" Does not the Apostle then reproach certain men and say: *For when you say: I am of Paul, I of Apollos, are you not men?*[^10] What did he want them to be, whom he reproached for being men? You are a man, you belong to Adam. Belong to the Son of man.

**5** And perhaps he says to you: *Now I will not call you servants, but friends; because what I heard from my Father I have made known to you*[^11]. But he said this to the disciples, to those apostles, to them he said this. We ought not to be saddened that we are not yet such. And yet how did he say even to them: *All that I heard from my Father I have made known to you?* I think he said this in hope, not yet in reality. I think it of what he was going to do, not of what he had already done. And whence is it proved, since he says, *I have made known to you*, and does not say, I will make known to you? Because certain things are said in the Scriptures of the past which are to be understood of the future. How are they said of the past, when they are to be understood of the future? *They pierced*, he says, *my hands and my feet, they numbered all my bones*[^12]. It had not yet been done, and what was to come was announced as done. *He saved us through the washing of regeneration*[^13]. But in another place he himself says: *In hope we were saved. But hope that is seen is not hope*[^14]. *In hope we were saved*, we say only of the past. And since in hope we were saved, not yet in reality, what we hope for is still to come. For we already see it and hold it. But not yet the reality, only hope. *For what a man sees*, he says, *why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it with patience*[^15]. And yet we were saved, and yet we still hope for and await salvation, we do not yet hold it. So too the Lord to the disciples: *All that I heard from my Father I have made known to you*[^16]. If this was already done, why does he say in another place to them: *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^17]? Surely, *all that I heard from my Father I have made known to you*. But when he says, *You cannot bear them now*, and says, *I have things to say to you*, he defers, he does not take away. On account of the sure hope, then, because without doubt he knew that he was going to do it, with him it was reckoned as done. And therefore he was saying: *I have made known to you*.

**6** As long, then, *as we are in the body, we are away from the Lord. For we walk by faith, and not by sight*[^18]. As much as is given us, let us hold to faith, let us not doubt of the justice of God. Let us not at all believe that there is iniquity with him, lest we come into a great abyss of impiety. And when with perfect faith we have held that there is no iniquity with him, even if we do not now see it—that is, the equity that is with him—the way will be finished and we will come to the homeland. It cannot be seen in the time of faith; it will be seen in the time of sight. For now *we walk by faith*, then by sight. What is "by sight"? *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^19], because *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^20]. *He who loves me*, he says, *keeps my commandments*, and *he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him*[^21]. And what will you give him? *And I will show myself to him*[^22]. This will be the sight, when he does what he said: *And I will show myself to him*. There you will see the equity of God, there without a codex you will read in the Word. Therefore, *when we see him as he is*[^23], our pilgrimage will pass. And afterward we will rejoice with the joy of the angels. For these things are on the way. What is the way? Faith. On account of your faith Christ became deformed, but Christ remains beautiful. *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*, he will be seen after the pilgrimage. But now, in faith, how is he seen? *And we saw him, and he had no form nor comeliness, but his face was abject, and deformed his condition*—this is his strength—*despised and deformed his condition, a man set among blows, and knowing how to bear weaknesses*[^24]. The deformity of Christ forms you. For if he had not been willing to be deformed, you would not have received the form you lost. He hung, then, on the cross, deformed, but his deformity was our beauty. In this life, then, let us hold to the deformed Christ. What is "the deformed Christ"? *Far be it from me to glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^25]. This is the deformity of Christ. Did I say I knew anything among you, except the way? This is the way, to believe in the crucified. The sign of this deformity we carry on the forehead. Let us not be ashamed of this deformity of Christ. Let us hold to this way, and we will arrive at the sight. When we have arrived at the sight, we will see the equity of God. And then there will be no saying: "Why did he come to the aid of this one, and not this one? Why was this one led by the governance of God to be baptized, while that one, though he lived well as a catechumen, died by a sudden collapse and did not reach baptism? But that one, though he lived wickedly—a libertine, an adulterer, an actor, a hunter—fell sick, was baptized, departed, the sin in him was convicted, the sin in him was blotted out?" Seek merits, you will find nothing but punishment. Seek grace: *O the depth of the riches!*[^26] Peter denies, the thief believes. *O the depth of the riches!*

**7** Do you think we can search out what the blessed Apostle dreaded? And when, gazing on so great a profundity and height, he trembled, he cried out: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God!*[^27] For what had he said before, that he should come to this exclamation? He had said something where, if it is not believed of God that there is no iniquity with God, God will be judged unjust. He was speaking to the gentiles, he was speaking to the faithful, about the Jews: *As you*, he says, *did not believe God, but now have obtained mercy through their unbelief, so these too have not believed in your mercy, that they too may obtain mercy. For God shut up all in unbelief, that he might have mercy on all*[^28]. This Paul said afterward. And what is this account of the equity and justice of God, to shut up all in unbelief, that he might have mercy on all? You ask the account, I dread the height. *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God!* You reason, I will marvel. You dispute, I will believe. I see the height, I do not reach the depth. *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past tracing out!*[^29] Perhaps he is going to explain? *For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, that it should be repaid to him? For from him and through him and in him are all things. To him be glory forever and ever*[^30]. He rested, because he found—because he found wonder. Let no one ask of me the account of hidden things. He says: *His judgments are unsearchable*, and have you come to search them out? He says: *His ways are past tracing out*, and have you come to trace them? If you have come to search out the unsearchable, and come to trace the untraceable, believe, for you have perished. To wish to search out the unsearchable and to trace the untraceable is like wishing to see the invisible and to speak the unspeakable. Therefore let the house be built. When it has come to its dedication, then perhaps it will find the most open account of these hidden things.
