# Sermon 75: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 14:24-33: "but the Boat Was Tossed in the Midst of the Sea by the Waves," and What Follows

**1. 1.** The reading of the Gospel which we just heard admonishes the humility of us all, to see and recognize where we are, and to what we ought to direct ourselves and hasten. For that boat carrying the disciples, which labored against a contrary wind in the waves, signifies something. Nor without cause did the Lord, leaving the crowds, ascend the mountain to pray alone; then, coming to his disciples, he found them in peril, walking upon the sea, and strengthened them by ascending into the boat, and calmed the waves[^1]. But what wonder, if he can calm all things, who founded all things? Yet after he ascended into the boat, those who were carried came, saying: *Truly you are the Son of God*[^2]. But before this evidence they were disturbed, seeing him upon the sea. For they said: *It is a phantasm*[^3]. But he, ascending into the boat, took away the fluctuation of the mind from their hearts, where they were now more in peril by doubt in mind, than in body by the waves.

**2. 2.** Yet in all the things the Lord did, he admonishes us how we should live here. For no one in this world is not a pilgrim: although not all desire to return to the fatherland. But from the journey itself we suffer waves and tempests: but it is needful that we at least be in the boat. For if in the boat there are perils, without the boat there is certain destruction. For however great the strength of arms one has who swims in the sea, sometimes, overcome by the greatness of the sea, he is swallowed up and sunk. It is needful, then, that we be in the boat, that is, that we be carried on the wood, that we may be able to cross this sea. But this wood, by which our weakness is carried, is the cross of the Lord, by which we are sealed, and rescued from the submersions of this world. We suffer waves: but he is God, who may help us.

**2. 3.** For that the Lord, leaving the crowds, ascended the mountain alone to pray, that mountain signifies the heights of the heavens. For, leaving the crowds, the Lord alone after the resurrection ascended into heaven[^4], and there intercedes for us, as the Apostle says[^5]. There is, then, something that it signifies, that, leaving the crowds, he ascended the mountain to pray alone. For he alone is still the firstborn from the dead[^6], after the resurrection of his body at the right hand of the Father, high priest and advocate of our prayers. The head of the Church is on high, that the other members may follow to the end. If, then, he intercedes for us, as it were on the peak of the mountain, above the loftiness of all creatures, he alone prays.

**3. 4.** Meanwhile the boat carrying the disciples, that is, the Church, fluctuates and is shaken by the tempests of temptations: and the contrary wind does not rest, that is, the devil opposing it, and strives to hinder it from reaching rest. But greater is he who intercedes for us. For in this our fluctuation, in which we labor, he gives us confidence, coming to us, and comforting us: only let us not, disturbed in the boat, shake ourselves out, and throw ourselves into the sea. For although the boat is disturbed, it is yet a boat. It alone carries the disciples, and receives Christ. It is indeed in peril in the sea: but without it one perishes at once. Hold yourself, then, in the boat, and pray to God. For when all counsels fail, when neither the rudders have sufficed, and the very spreading of the sails has been a greater peril than help; all human aids and strengths dismissed, there remains for the sailors only the intent of beseeching and pouring out voices to God. He, then, who grants to those sailing that they reach the port, will he dismiss his Church, so as not to lead her to rest?

**4. 5.** Yet, brothers, the greatest disturbance in this boat is none other than in the absence of the Lord. Does one established in the Church have the Lord absent? When does he have the Lord absent? When he is conquered by some craving. For just as in a certain place it is taken as said in a sacred sense: *Let not the sun set upon your anger; nor give place to the devil*[^7]: but it is understood not of that sun which has a certain sublimity among the visible heavenly things, which can be seen in common both by us and by beasts; but of that light which only the pure hearts of the faithful see, as it was said: *It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^8]. For this light of the visible sun enlightens even the tiniest and smallest beasts. There is, then, a true light, justice and wisdom, which the mind ceases to see, when it has been overcome, as if by a cloud, by the disturbance of anger: and then, as it were, the sun sets upon the anger of the man. So too in this boat, when Christ is absent, each one is shaken by his own tempests and by his own iniquities and cravings. For the Law says to you, for example: *You shall not bear false witness*[^9]. If you understand the truth of the testimony, you have light in your mind: but if, overcome by craving for base gain, you have resolved in mind to bear false witness, you now begin, with Christ absent, to be disturbed by the tempest; you will fluctuate with the waves of your avarice, you will be in peril by the tempest of your cravings, and, as if Christ were absent, you will almost be submerged.

**5. 6.** How greatly to be feared it is, lest the boat be turned, and look back? which happens when, the hope of heavenly rewards being abandoned, one is turned, by craving twisting him aside, to the things that are seen and pass away. For one who is disturbed by the temptations of lusts, and yet looks at the things that are within, is not so utterly desperate, beseeching pardon for his offenses, and intent on overcoming and crossing the rage of the raging sea. But he who is so twisted away from himself that he says in his heart, God does not see; for he does not think of me, or care whether I sin: turns the prow, is carried by the storm, and is driven back whence he came. For there are many thoughts in the hearts of men: and by the waves of this world, and by many tempests the boat is disturbed when Christ is absent.

**6. 7.** But the fourth watch of the night is the end of the night: for one watch consists of three hours. So it signifies that now at the end of the age the Lord comes to the aid, and is seen walking upon the waters. For although this boat is disturbed by the storms of temptations, yet it sees God glorified walking upon all the swellings of the sea; that is, upon all the principalities of this world. For before it was said, in the voice of his passion, that, when according to the flesh he showed the example of humility, the waves of the sea prevailed against him, to which he yielded by will for us, that that prophecy might be fulfilled: *I have come into the depth of the sea, and the tempest has sunk me*[^10]. For he did not repel the false witnesses, nor the raging shout of those saying: *Let him be crucified*[^11]. The raging hearts and mouths of those raving he did not repress by power, but bore by patience. They did to him as much as they wished: because he was made *obedient even to death, the death of the cross*[^12]. But after he rose from the dead, that he might pray alone for the disciples established in the Church as in a boat, and carried by faith in his cross as by wood, and in peril from the temptations of this world as from the waves of the sea; his name began to be honored even in this world, in which he was despised, accused, killed: so that he who had come, according to the passion of the flesh, into the depth of the sea, and the tempest had sunk him, now treads down the necks of the proud, like the foam of the waves, by the honor of his name. As now we see the Lord, as it were walking upon the sea, under whose feet we behold the whole rage of this world made subject.

**7. 8.** But to the perils of the tempests are added also the errors of the heretics; and there are not lacking those who so tempt the souls of those who are in the boat, that they say Christ was not born of a virgin, nor had a true body, but that what was not was seen by the eyes. And these opinions of the heretics have now arisen, when the name of Christ is now glorified through all nations, as though Christ were now walking upon the sea. The tempted disciples said: *It is a phantasm*[^13]. But he strengthens us against these plagues by his voice, saying: *Have confidence, it is I; do not fear*[^14]. For by an empty fear men have thought these things about Christ, attending to his honor and majesty: and they do not think that he could be born so, who merited to be glorified so, as if terrified at him walking upon the sea. By which deed the excellence of his honor is figured: and so they think that he was a phantasm. But when he says: *It is I*; what else does he say, except that there is not in him what is not? And so if he showed flesh, it was flesh; if bones, they were bones; if scars, they were scars. For *there was not in him, Yes, and: No; but: Yes was in him*[^15], as the Apostle says. Hence is that voice: *Have confidence, it is I; do not fear.* That is, Do not so be terrified at my dignity that you wish to take away from me my truth. Even if I walk upon the sea, even if I have under my feet the elations and pomps of the world as raging waves, yet I appeared a true man, yet my Gospel preaches the truth about me, that I was born of a virgin, that I was the Word made flesh; that I spoke truly: *Handle, and see, that a spirit does not have bones, as you see me have*[^16]; that the hands of the doubter handled the true marks of my wounds. And so: *It is I; do not fear.*

**8. 9.** But this matter does not signify those alone, that the disciples thought it a phantasm; it does not designate those alone who deny that the Lord had human flesh, and sometimes also disturb by blind perversity even those who are in the boat: but also those who think that the Lord lied in something, and do not believe that the things he threatened the impious with will come to pass. As though he were in part truthful and in part a liar, like a phantasm appearing in words, as if what is and is not. But those who well understand the voice of the one saying: *It is I; do not fear*; now believe all the words of the Lord, so that, as they hope for the rewards he promises, so they fear the punishments he threatens. For just as it is true what he will say to those set on the right: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^17]: so it is true what those set on the left will hear: *Go into the eternal fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels*[^18]. For that opinion too, by which men think that Christ did not truly threaten the wicked and the lost, arose from this, that they see many peoples and innumerable crowds subject to his name: so that from this Christ seems to them a phantasm, because he was walking upon the sea; that is, he seems to lie in the threatening of punishments, because, as it were, he cannot destroy so innumerable peoples, who are subject to his name and honor. But let them hear him saying: *It is I.* Let those, then, not fear, who, believing Christ truthful in all things, not only seek what he promised, but also flee what he threatened: because, even if he walks upon the sea, that is, all kinds of men set in this world are subject to him; yet he is not a phantasm, and therefore he does not lie when he says: *Not everyone who says to me: Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven*[^19].

**9. 10.** What, then, does it also signify that Peter dared to come to him upon the waters? For Peter very often bears the person of the Church. What else, then, do we think is meant: *Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you upon the waters*[^20]: except, Lord, if you are truthful, and lie in nothing, let your Church too be glorified in this world, because this prophecy preached of you? Let her, then, walk upon the waters, and so come to you, she to whom it was said: *The rich of the people will entreat your face*[^21]. But since human praise does not tempt the Lord, but men are often disturbed in the Church by human praises and honors, and almost sink; therefore Peter trembled in the sea, shuddering at the great force of the tempest. For who does not fear that voice: *Those who call you happy send you into error, and disturb the paths of your feet*[^22]? And because the soul wrestles against the desire of human praise, it is good in such a peril that it turn to prayer and beseeching: lest perhaps he who is soothed by praise be undermined by blame and sink. In the wave let Peter, faltering, cry out, and say: *Lord, save me*[^23]. For the Lord holds out his hand: and although he rebukes, saying: *O you of little faith, why did you doubt?*[^24] why, not gazing on him to whom you were heading by a straight path, did you glory in nothing but the Lord? yet he snatches him from the waves, and does not let him perish, confessing his weakness and asking for his help. But the Lord received into the boat, the faith confirmed and all doubt taken away, and the tempests of the sea calmed, that one may now come to the stability and security of the land, all adore, saying: *Truly you are the Son of God*[^25]. For this is the everlasting joy, by which the clear truth, and the Word of God, and the Wisdom through whom all things were made, and the eminence of his mercy, is both known and loved.
