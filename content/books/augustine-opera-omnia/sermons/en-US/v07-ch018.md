# Sermon 245: In the Days of Easter

**1.** Today too the resurrection of the Lord has been recited from the holy Gospel. And the Gospel was read according to John. We have heard things that we had not heard in the other books of the Gospel. The preaching of the truth is indeed common to all, and they all drank from one fount: but in the preaching of the Gospel, as I have often reminded your Charity, some things all set down, some things three, some things two, some things the individual ones set down. So now what we have heard according to the Gospel of John—that Mary saw the Lord, and the Lord said to her: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^1]—the Evangelist John alone records. From this, then, I must speak to your Holiness. Even when they had seen the linen cloths in the tomb, they had believed that the Lord had not risen, but had been taken away. John himself (for he calls himself *the one whom Jesus loved*), when he had heard the women announcing and saying: *They have taken away my Lord from the tomb*, ran with Peter, and looked into the tomb, saw only the linen cloths, and believed. What did he believe? Not that he had risen, but that he had been taken away from the tomb. This the following words attest. For so it is written, what we just now heard: *He looked, he saw, and he believed: for he did not yet know the Scriptures, that he must rise from the dead*[^2]. So it appeared what he believed: what was not of faith, this he believed: he believed, but believed something false. The Lord appeared to him afterward, put the false to flight, implanted the true.

**2.** Yet that which is wont to disturb a reader and hearer who is not incurious or negligent—how it was said: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father*—let us see here, with the Lord's own help, what was meant. For this disturbs, what it means: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father.* For when did he ascend to the Father? As the Acts of the Apostles indicate, on the fortieth day after his resurrection, which day we are going to celebrate in his name: then he ascended to the Father; then the disciples, who had touched him with their hands, escorted him with their eyes. Then the angelic voice sounded: *Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you, will come in the same way as you saw him going into heaven*[^3]. If, then, he ascended to the Father then, what do we say, my brothers? Could Mary not touch him standing on earth, and could she touch him sitting in heaven? If she could not here, how much less could she there? What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me; for I have not ascended to the Father?* For the words sound as though he said, Touch me then, when I have ascended; before I ascend, do not touch me. O Lord, you are here, and I do not touch; when you have ascended, shall I touch? Then, if before he ascended to the Father he shrank from human touch, how did he offer himself to the disciples not only to be seen but even to be felt, when he said: *What do you think in your hearts? See my hands and my feet: handle, and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^4]? That disciple Thomas too, the unbelieving one, touched the pierced side, and exclaimed: *My Lord and my God!*[^5] When he touched, Jesus had not yet ascended to the Father. Or will some foolish person perhaps say: Before he ascended to the Father, men could touch him; but women could not, except when he had ascended to the Father? That thought is absurd, and the opinion perverse. By all means let the Church hear what Mary heard. Let all hear this, let all understand, let all do it. What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father?* What you see me to be, you think me only a man, you do not yet know that I am equal to the Father: do not touch me as such, do not believe in me as only a man, but understand the Word equal to the Begetter. What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me?* Do not believe. What do you mean, do not believe? That I am only what you see. I will ascend to the Father, and then touch. I ascend for you, when you have understood me to be equal. For when you think me less, I do not yet ascend for you.

**3.** But that to touch is to believe, I think we can easily understand from that woman who touched the hem of Christ's garment, and was made whole. Remember the Gospel: the Lord Jesus Christ was going to visit the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, who was first reported sick, afterward dead. As he went on his way, see, a woman came across his path, who had been afflicted with a flow of blood for twelve years, and, while the physicians cured her in vain and did not heal her, had consumed all she had: and she said in her heart: *If I touch the hem of his garment, I will be saved.* To say this very thing was already to touch. Finally, hear the verdict. When she had been made whole according to her faith, the Lord Jesus Christ said: *Someone touched me.* And the disciples: *The crowds press you, and you say, Who touched me?* And he: *Someone touched me; for I know that power has gone out from me*[^6]. Grace went forth, that she might be healed, not that he might be diminished. So the disciples say, The crowds press you; and have you felt one man or one woman? and he: *Someone touched me*: they press, this woman touched. What does it mean, they press, this woman touched? The Jews afflict, the Church believed.

**4.** According to this understanding, then, by which we see that the woman touched, which is, believed, according to this it was said to Mary: *Do not touch me*; I will ascend, and touch. For touch me then, when you have come to know: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^7]. The Word indeed was made flesh, the Word remains uncontaminated, immaculate, unchangeable, and whole. But because you see only the man, you do not see the Word; I do not want you to believe in the flesh, and abandon the Word. Let the whole Christ appear to you, because he is equal to the Father in the Word. Do not, then, he says, touch now: because you do not yet see who I am. So let the Church, whose figure Mary bore, hear what Mary heard. Let us all touch, if we believe. He has already ascended to the Father, he sits at the right hand of the Father. The whole Church confesses this today: He ascended into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father. This those who are baptized hear: this they believe before they are baptized. When, then, they believe, Mary touches Christ. The understanding is obscure, but sound: closed to unbelievers, open to faith that knocks. The Lord Jesus Christ himself, then, is both there and with us; both with the Father and in us; he neither withdraws from him, nor abandons us: and he teaches us to pray, as a master; and with the Father he hears, as a Son.
