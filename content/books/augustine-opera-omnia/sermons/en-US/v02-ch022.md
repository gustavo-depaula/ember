# Sermon 34

**1** We have been admonished to sing a new song to the Lord. The new man knows the new song. A song is a thing of cheerfulness, and, if we consider more diligently, a thing of love. He, then, who knows how to love the new life knows how to sing the new song. What the new life is, then, we must be reminded on account of the new song. For all things pertain to one kingdom: the new man, the new song, the new Testament. Therefore the new man will both sing the new song and pertain to the new Testament.

**2** There is no one who does not love, but it is asked what he loves. We are not, then, admonished not to love, but to choose what we love. But what do we choose, unless we are first chosen? Because we do not even love unless we are first loved. Hear the apostle John. He is that Apostle who reclined on the Lord's breast and at that banquet drank in heavenly secrets. From that draught and from that happy inebriation he poured forth: *In the beginning was the Word*[^1]. Lofty humility and sober inebriation. That great pourer-forth, then—that is, preacher—among the other things he drank from the Lord's breast, said also this: *We love because he first loved us*[^2]. For he had given much to man, since he was speaking of God, by saying, *We love*. Who? Whom? Men love God, mortals the immortal, sinners the just, the frail the immovable, the creation the maker. We loved. And whence is this to us? *Because he first loved us*. Seek whence it is to man to love God, and you will find nothing at all, except that God first loved him. He gave the one whom we loved, he gave that by which we might love. For what he gave by which we might love, hear more openly through the apostle Paul: *The love*, he says, *of God has been poured out in our hearts*[^3]. Whence? Perhaps from ourselves? No. Whence, then? *Through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^4].

**3** Having, then, such great confidence, let us love God from God. Rather, because the Holy Spirit is God, let us love God from God. For what more shall I say: let us love God from God? Surely, because I said, *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^5]; therefore it follows that, since the Holy Spirit is God, and we cannot love God except through the Holy Spirit, let us love God from God. Hence, then, it follows. Hear more openly John himself: *God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him*[^6]. It is too little to say: Love is from God. Which of us would dare to say what was said: *God is love*? He said it who knew what he had. Why, then, does human imagination and flitting thought fashion God for itself, and build an idol in its heart, composing such a one as it can think, not such a one as it has deserved to find? "Is God such? No, but he is such." Why do you arrange features? Why do you put together members? Why do you form a pleasing stature? Why do you imagine a beauty of body? *God is love*. What color is in love? What features? What form? None of these we see. And yet we love.

**4** I dare to say to your Charity: let us attend in lower things to what we may find in higher things. That very love, lowest and earthly, that very love, sordid and disgraceful, which pursues bodily beauties, warns us of something whence we may rise to higher and cleaner things. Some lascivious and unchaste man loves a most beautiful woman. The beauty of the body indeed moves him, but within is sought the return of love. For if he hears that she hates him, does not all that ardor and impetus around her beautiful members grow cold, and does he not in a way recoil from what he had aimed at, turn away, take offense, and even himself begin to hate what he loved? Surely her form is not changed? Are not all the things there that had allured him? They are there. And yet he was burning in what he saw, and demanded from the heart what he did not see. But if he comes to know that he is loved in return, how much more vehemently does he burn! She sees him, he sees her; no one sees love. And yet the very one who is not seen is loved.

**5** Raise yourselves from that muddy desire, that you may abide in the most illuminated love. You do not see God. Love, and you have him. How many things in damnable desires are loved, and not had! They are sordidly sought, yet not at once possessed. Is to love gold the same as to have gold? Many love it and do not have it. Is to have the most extensive and elegant estates the same as to love them? Many love them and do not have them. Is to love honor the same as to have honor? Many, without honor, burn to have it. They seek to have it, and often die before they find what they were seeking. God offers himself to us as a shortcut. He cries out to us: "Love me, and you will have me, because you cannot even love me unless you have me."

**6** O brothers, O sons, O catholic shoots, O holy and heavenly seeds, O you regenerated in Christ and born from above, hear me, or rather, through me: *Sing to the Lord a new song*[^7]. "See," you say, "I sing." You sing, plainly you sing, I hear it. But let your life not bear testimony against your tongue. Sing with your voices, sing with your hearts, sing with your mouths, sing with your conduct: *Sing to the Lord a new song*. Do you ask what you should sing of the one you love? Without doubt you want to sing of the one you love. You seek his praises to sing. You have heard: *Sing to the Lord a new song*. Do you seek praises? *His praise is in the Church of the saints*[^8]. The praise of the singing is the singer himself. Do you want to speak praises to God? Be yourselves what you speak. You are his praise, if you live well. For his praise is not in the synagogues of the Jews, is not in the madness of the pagans, is not in the errors of the heretics, is not in the applause of the theaters. Do you seek where it is? Attend to yourselves, be yourselves: *His praise is in the Church of the saints*. Do you seek whence you may rejoice when you sing? *Let Israel rejoice in the one who made him*[^9]—and it finds nothing whence to rejoice except God.

**7** Well, my brothers, question yourselves, examine your inner cells. See and attend to what you have of love, and what you find, increase. Attend to such a treasure, that you may be rich within. Other things, surely, that have a great price are called dear, not without reason. Look at the custom of your speech: "That is dearer than that." What is "it is dearer," except "it is more precious"? If whatever is more precious is called dearer, what is dearer than love itself, my brothers? What, do we suppose, is its price? Whence is its price found? The price of wheat, your coin; the price of an estate, your silver; the price of a pearl, your gold; the price of love, you. Do you ask, then, whence you may possess an estate, a gem, a beast of burden? You ask whence you may buy an estate, and you seek it from yourself. But if you want to have love, seek yourself, and find yourself. For why do you fear to give yourself, lest you consume yourself? Rather, if you do not give yourself, you lose yourself. Love itself, through Wisdom, speaks, and says something to you, by which you may not be terrified at what was said, "Give yourself." For if someone wanted to sell you an estate, he would say to you, "Give me your gold"; and if someone wanted to sell something else, "Give me your coin, give me your silver." Hear what love says to you from the mouth of Wisdom: *Give me, son, your heart*[^10]. *Give me*, it says. What? Son, *your heart*. It was ill when it was away from you, when it was for yourself. For through trifles and lascivious and ruinous loves you were dragged. Take it away from there. Where do you drag it? Where do you set it? *Give me*, it says, *your heart*. Let it be mine, and it does not perish for you. For see whether he wished to leave anything in you whence you might love even yourself, who says to you: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*[^11]. What remains of your heart, whence you may love yourself? What of your soul? What of your mind? *With your whole*, he says. He who made you demands the whole of you. But do not be sad, as though nothing remained in you whence you might rejoice. *Let Israel rejoice*, not in himself, but *in the one who made him*[^12].

**8** You will answer and say: "If nothing remains to me whence I may love myself, because I am commanded to love the one who made me *with my whole heart, and with my whole soul, and with my whole mind*, how am I commanded by the second precept to love my neighbor as myself?" This is rather why you ought to love your neighbor with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind. How? *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^13]. God with the whole of me, the neighbor as myself. Whence the "me," whence the "you." Do you want to hear whence you may love yourself? From this you love yourself, because you love God with the whole of yourself. For do you suppose it profits God that you love God? And because you love God, does anything accrue to God? And if you do not love him, you will have less. When you love, you profit; you will be there where you do not perish. But you will answer and say: "For when did I not love myself?" You did not at all love yourself when you did not love God, who made you. But when you hated yourself, you thought you loved yourself. *For he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^14].

**9** Turning to the Lord God, the Father almighty, with a pure heart, let us give him, as far as our littleness can, the greatest and richest thanks; praying with our whole soul his singular gentleness, that he may deign to hear our prayers in his good pleasure; that by his power he may also drive the enemy from our deeds and thoughts, multiply faith in us, govern the mind, grant spiritual thoughts, and lead us to his blessedness; through Jesus Christ his Son, our Lord, who lives and reigns with him in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, forever and ever. Amen.
