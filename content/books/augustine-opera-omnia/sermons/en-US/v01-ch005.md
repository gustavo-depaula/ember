# Sermon 4/A: From a Sermon on the Three Rods

[A Fragment]

**[2]** But why the Lord first chose the lowborn, the few, the unlearned, and the unrefined, when he had before his eyes a great crowd—fewer indeed in comparison with those poorer ones, but in their own kind many rich, noble, learned, wise men, whom he afterward also gathered in—the Apostle expounds the mystery: *God chose the weak things of this world to confound the strong; and God chose the foolish things of this world to confound the wise; and God chose the lowborn things of this world and the things that are not*, that is, are not counted, *that the things that are might be brought to nothing*[^1]. For he had come to teach humility and to subdue pride. God had come in humility: in no way would he here first seek the lofty, who had come so humble. First, because he chose to be born of that woman who was betrothed to a carpenter. He did not, then, choose a distinguished lineage, lest in this earth nobility should be proud. He did not even choose to be born in a most distinguished city, but was born in Bethlehem of Judah, which is not even called by the name of a city. To this day the inhabitants of that place call it a village: so small, so slight, almost nothing, were it not ennobled by the former birth of the Lord Christ. He chose, then, the weak, the poor, the unlearned: not because he abandoned the strong, the rich, the wise, the noble; but if he chose these first, they would seem to themselves to be chosen for the merit of their riches, for the merit of their possessions, for the merit of their birth, and, puffed up about these things, they would not receive the salvation of humility, without which no one can return to that life from which we would not have fallen except through pride.

**[3]** Therefore he did not say "mother," because sometimes mothers are either more delicate, or, loving their children less, when they have given birth, hand them over to others to be nursed. Again, if he had said only, *As a nurse cherishing*[^2], and had not added "her own children," he would seem to have received children from another woman who had given birth, to be nursed. He both called himself a "nurse," because he was feeding them, and said "his own children," because he himself had given birth to them, saying, *My children, of whom I am again in travail, until Christ be formed in you*[^3]. But he gives birth as the Church gives birth, from her womb, not from her seed.

[^1]: 1 Cor 1:27-28
[^2]: 1 Thess 2:7
[^3]: Gal 4:19
