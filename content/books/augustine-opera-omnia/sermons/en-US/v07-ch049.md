# Sermon 265/C: On the Fortieth Day, the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** We celebrate today the ascension of the Lord into heaven with the flesh in which the Lord rose; the anniversary solemnity does not recall the deed, but renews the memory. Let us meanwhile ascend with him in heart; we are sure that we will follow also in the flesh. Not in vain did we just now hear *heart on high*; nor without cause does the Apostle exhort us, saying: *If you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; set your minds on the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^1]. Lift yourselves from the earth: the body cannot, but the mind can. Lift yourselves from the earth: endure labors on earth, think of rest in heaven; let it be done well here, that it may be remained there forever. The heart's place is not on earth, where it can keep its integrity; if it is on earth, it is corrupted. What precious thing each one has, he lifts on high; many men, indeed all men, when they hear that some dangers of wars are imminent on earth, whatever dear thing they have, seek where to keep it. Is it not so? Can anyone in the human race be otherwise than I say? He has silver, he has gold, he has gems, he has precious necklaces, he has costly garments; he seeks where to keep them, surely lest he lose what he has. Better let him put on high what he has better, let him put it on high. What does he have better than his heart? From the heart earthly things are possessed. Finally, little children, because they do not yet have sense and understanding in use—for they have it set aside, what was created in them has not yet awakened—surely do they possess? An heir is born, the future heir of all things, and although all things are now his by right, yet he does not yet possess what is his, because he does not yet have that by which he may possess. Therefore the Apostle said: *As long as the heir is a little one, he differs nothing from a slave*[^2]. From where, then, do we possess anything on earth—it is the heart, it is the understanding, it is sense, it is talent, it is reason, it is thought, it is counsel. How many things I have said, and what have I said? For who comprehends himself? how much less him who made himself? There let us entrust what we hold dear. Look around at all your things, my brothers, and find what you hold more dear. I admonish even the greedy: how much more easily do the non-greedy hear me! I convict the greedy: O you greedy acquirer of a man, gathering gains from anywhere, whether honorably or shamefully, you heap up to yourself much mud; you collect mud, and you do not dread to be stuck there, earthly things are dear to you. You are a man, you have a body, you have a soul; in your very body first I ask what you hold more dear? I think you find nothing in your body dearer than your eyes. Finally, those who love a man much say: I love you so, as my own eyes. Let us make steps to what I desire to show. Among your members nothing is dearer to you than the eye. Attend now to your treasures, attend to what you keep. If someone said to you: Either you give me what you keep on earth, or I now take away your eyes; would you not give the whole for your eyes? You would give the whole, lest you remain blind amid your riches; for you would not possess what you did not yet see. And your avarice indeed possesses gold, some last and tiny part of the earth; with your eyes you possess heaven, with your eyes you look on the sun, with your eyes you behold the stars, through your eyes you are a possessor of the world. And why should I say much? Question yourself, your soul will answer you on behalf of its body: Give the whole, keep my windows. This your soul says to you: I have two windows in your face, through them I see this light: give the gold, lest my windows be shut. The whole, then, you give for your eyes.

**2.** Surely you have nothing dearer than your eyes: nothing indeed, but in the body. For I show you something you have dearer than your eyes: now you will confess, the one to whom I speak is dearer in you than your eyes. The one to whom I speak, I say, not that through which I speak; through the ear I address the mind, through the ear I rouse the mind, through sound I speak to the mind, I exhort the mind, I build up the mind. I ask of the mind itself, and about the mind itself, and I question the man in this way. A while ago I was saying, if someone wished to take from you your treasures or your eyes, one of the two, you would choose your eyes: though grieving, you would lose the treasures, lest you lose your eyes. Now I ask about the eyes themselves. If it is allowed to keep both, it is happiness, the eyes and the mind. If it is not allowed to keep both, and one of these is set forth: Choose which is better, to lose the eyes of the body, or the mind. If you lose the mind, you will be a beast; if you lose the eyes, you will have the mind, you will be a man. Tell me, choose what you wish. What do you want to be, a blind man, or a seeing beast? You cried out, you chose; what you chose, from where did you see it? what did I show, that you should cry out? Did I show some beautiful colors, some most lovely forms, gold, silver? did I bring forth some gems for you to look at? None of these: yet you cried out, and by crying out you signified that you had chosen. From where did you see what you chose? it is the mind itself, to which I speak. From where you chose what you heard through my word, this believe in the Word of God. This you hear and do, when it is said: *Heart on high.* Think of Christ sitting at the right hand of the Father; think that he will come to judge the living and the dead. Let faith think it: faith is in the mind, in the foundation of the heart is faith. See who died for you; attend to him ascending, love him suffering; attend to him ascending, and hold him dying. For so great a promise you have a pledge, what Christ promised you: what he did today, his ascension, is a promise to you. We ought to hope that we will rise, and ascend to the kingdom of God, and there be with God forever, going to live without end, going to rejoice without any sadness, going to remain without trouble. There it will not be said to you: Beware of evil; but: Hold the good. Great is what is promised; when would timid and feeble mortality dare to promise this to itself? when would this rottenness dare to pledge these things to itself? considering what it was, when would it promise these things to itself? God promised those things. That you may believe, he says, that you will ascend to me, I first descend to you; and that you may believe that you will live from me, I first die for you.

[^1]: Col 3:1-2
[^2]: Gal 4:1
