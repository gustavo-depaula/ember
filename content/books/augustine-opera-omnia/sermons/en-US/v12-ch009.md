# Sermon 225, Expanded Version

**1.** {What was read from the holy Gospel} commends to us the depth of divine grace toward us. For the Son of God, born of the Father outside time, what was he before he was in a human being {among human beings? The holy Gospel you heard has spoken.} Suppose \*we asked and said, “Do we think that before Christ was born of the Virgin Mary, he existed or did not exist?” Imagine us asking about something we must not doubt. The Lord himself answers these thoughts when he was told: *You are not yet fifty years old, and have you seen Abraham*? He answered: *Amen, I say to you, before Abraham, I am*. So he existed. But what was he? For he was not yet man. Lest anyone say, “He was an angel,” {hear the divine utterance.} The holy Gospel answers you: “You know that Christ existed, and you ask what he was? *In the beginning was the Word*.” Here is what he was: *In the beginning was the Word*. Not “In the beginning the Word was made,” but “the Word was.” What does Scripture say of this world? *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*. Do you ask through what he made them? *In the beginning was the Word*, through whom heaven and earth were to be made. {The Word was.} The Word was not made; the Word was. It remains for you to ask what kind of word, because ours too are called words, conceived in thought and born in speech, yet passing away both when thought and when uttered. But what of that Word? *And the Word was with God*. You have said where he was. {What was he?} Say what he was. {“You ask?” the Gospel tells you.} “I have already said: *In the beginning was the Word*.” But I asked what kind of word, {since words are common among us. Say what kind of word.} “Do you want to hear {what kind of word?} *And the Word was God*.” O Word! Who could explain such a Word with words? *The Word was God*. But perhaps a god made by God? “Certainly not!” {How do we know it is certainly not? “Do not say he was made.} Hear {what follows}: *All things were made through him*.” What are *all things*? Whatever God made was made through him. Did he perhaps make himself? If he made himself, was he there to make himself? If, then, he was there to make himself, was there ever a time when he was not? [^1]

**2.** How, then, is such a Word in a virgin? *All things were made through him*, {and is he himself made? He is made, but as man, not by the Father alone but also by himself. What does “by himself” mean? Because *all things were made through him*.} Do not separate the Holy Spirit from this great work. From what great work? From Christ's flesh. It is no small work. The angels are a great work of God, yet the angels too worship the flesh of Christ seated at the Father's right hand. The Holy Spirit, then, also made this work, and he in particular is named in it. For when an angel announced to the holy Virgin that she would have a son, she had resolved on virginity, and her husband was not the remover but the guardian of her chastity—or rather, not its guardian, since God guarded it; the husband was a witness to her virginal chastity, lest her pregnancy be attributed to adultery. When, then, the angel announced it to her, she said: *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* If she intended to know one, she would not have wondered. Her wonder is testimony to her resolve. *How will this happen? I do not know a man.* How will it happen? *The Holy Spirit will come upon you*—that is how what you ask will happen—*and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.* The Holy Spirit himself is called *the power of the Most High*. And well did he say, *He will overshadow you*, so that your virginity may not feel the heat of lust. {*He will overshadow you. Therefore the holy one born of you will be called the Son of God*.} When she was pregnant, it was said of her: *She was found with child of the Holy Spirit*. Thus the Holy Spirit fashioned Christ's flesh. Christ himself, God's only-begotten Son, also fashioned his own flesh. How do we prove it? Because Scripture says of this: *Wisdom has built herself a house*. [^2]

**3.** Attend, then, {to what we were saying}. How is so great a God, God with God, God's Word through whom all things were made, enclosed in a womb? First, did he leave heaven to be there? Did the Word leave heaven to be in the Virgin's womb? And from what would the angels live? {For God's Word, as he is with the Father, feeds the angels.} But so that man might eat the bread of angels, the Lord of angels became man. Let human thought still travel through its clouds, {let it fly \*to seek, fail so that it may find, seek to speak,} about how God's Word in the Virgin's womb did not abandon the angels, did not abandon the Father. How could he be enclosed in that womb? He could be there; he could not be enclosed. “How,” you ask, “could one so great be in so tiny a place? \*Did the womb, then, contain him whom the world cannot contain?” {It contained him, and the world does not contain him.} Nor was he diminished in order to be in the womb. He was in the womb and was as great as he was. What does “as great” mean? *In the beginning was the Word*. What does “as he was” mean? *The Word was God*. {“I do not comprehend,” you say.} Do I know? Even I who speak with you do not comprehend. But thought makes us stretch, stretching enlarges us, and enlargement makes us capable. Yet even made capable, we shall not be able to comprehend the whole. I will discuss my own word with you. Grasp what I am saying to you; {grasp what I am about to say about my word, that is, a human word.} But if you cannot comprehend even this, see how far away you are. {What do I want to say about my word? Listen:} this is certainly what we marvel at, how Christ took flesh and was born of a virgin without departing from the Father. See, I who speak to you, before I bring forth {a word to you, have a word in my heart. For I would not speak to you unless I thought first.} When I thought what I would say to you, the word was already in my heart. {And I sought how what was in me might come to you. I considered what language you spoke.} I found you to be Latin-speaking: I must bring forth the word to you in Latin. If you were Greek, however, I would have to speak to you in Greek and bring you a Greek word. {To one a Latin word, to another a Greek word.} The word in my heart is neither Latin nor Greek: it is simply a word. What is in my heart precedes these languages. I seek a sound for it as a kind of vehicle; I seek a vehicle by which what does not depart from me may reach you. See, you have heard, and what was in my heart is now in yours. It is in mine and yours; you have begun to possess it, and I have not lost it. Just as my word took on sound through which it could be heard, so God's Word took on flesh through which he could be seen. I have said what I could. And what have I said? For who am I who have spoken? A human being who wished to speak of God. He is so great, of such a kind, that we can neither express him nor ought to be silent about him. {May he receive in our voice the intention, not the accomplishment.} [^3]

**4.** Thanks to you, Lord, that I wished to speak. {How far what I have said falls short of your greatness,} you know; yet with a few crumbs from your table I have fed my fellow servants. Feed them inwardly yourself; nourish those you have given new birth. See, what was this multitude before? *Darkness, but now light in the Lord*. {What was this multitude of white-robed people, renewed in heart? What was it? Darkness.} For to such people the Apostle says: *You were* once *darkness, but now light in the Lord*. You who have been baptized, you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord: {darkness in yourselves, light in the Lord.} If light, then certainly day, for God called the light day. You were darkness; he made you light, made you day. Of you we sang: *This is the day the Lord has made*. Flee darkness. Drunkenness belongs to darkness. Do not leave sober and return drunk. We shall see you after midday too. The Holy Spirit has begun to dwell in you; let him not move away. Do not shut him out of your hearts. He is a good guest, {asking nothing from you, seeking you yourselves.} He finds you empty and fills you; finds you hungry and feeds you; finally, finds you thirsty and makes you drunk. Let him make you drunk. But the Apostle says: *Do not be drunk with wine, in which is every kind of debauchery*. And as though wishing to teach us what should make us drunk: *But be filled*, he says, *with the Spirit, singing among yourselves in hymns, psalms, and spiritual songs, singing* {*and making melody*} *in your hearts to the Lord*. Does someone who rejoices in God and sings God's praises with great exultation not resemble a drunken person? {He is not drunk with wine, and yet he is drunk.} I prove that there is holy drunkenness: *For with you is the fountain of life.* {Lord, make us drunk! Here we are. You certainly have what can make us drunk. The prophet says to you: *They will be drunk with the abundance of your house*}*, and you will give them drink from the torrent of your delight*. From where? *For with you is the fountain of life; in your light we shall see light*. God's Spirit is both drink and light. If you found a fountain in the dark, you would light a lamp to reach it. Do not seek a lamp to reach the fountain of light. It shines for you itself and leads you to itself. When you have come, drink and live; draw near and be enlightened. *Draw near to him and be enlightened*; do not withdraw, lest you grow dark. Lord God, call, and let people draw near to you; strengthen them, lest they withdraw {from you}. Make your new children grow from little ones into the aged, but not from the aged into the dead. For in this wisdom one may grow old, but one may not die. [^4]
