# Sermon 335/C: A Sermon of Blessed Augustine the Bishop on One Martyr

**1.** Because the birthday of the blessed martyr has dawned, the day on which the Lord wanted us to celebrate with you, let us, with his giving, discourse something of the glory and patience of the martyrs. The glory indeed was despised, the patience was proved. For the glory lay hidden in the heavens, the patience was exercised on earth. He who does not shudder at their patience, let him come to the glory. For it is thought misery to suffer hard and harsh things in the flesh, because it is truly troublesome. Unless it were troublesome to men, it would not be glorious to the martyrs.

**2.** Set before your eyes two persons, as it were: one of desire, one of love. By desire I mean the love of sinning, for there is sometimes a desire which is called in a good sense. Likewise by love I mean the love of living rightly, for sometimes love too is called in a bad sense. Therefore I wanted to define what I said. The faithful desire the kingdom of heaven. Even thieves are called dear (cari) to one another. But there is no love (caritas) in those whom a bad conscience joins to one another, but in those whom wisdom delights in fellowship.

**3.** Consider, then—and distinguish—how many evils greedy men suffer, and for the things they desire, how hard things they sustain, which seem intolerable to men who do not desire them. But love makes them strong. But the love of evil is called desire, the love of good love. There are many things which are loved by the greedy, sometimes so diverse, that they are even found to be contrary to one another. Avarice gathers money, luxury scatters it. The one is needy, the other prodigal, and what is so contrary to one another as to gather and to scatter? Yet avarice commands one to sustain, by bearing pain, both how great things are done and how hard and harsh things . . . the pleasure you love. Yet even that itself sometimes, in the madness of love, is both loved basely and for its sake many things are borne bravely.

**4.** It is no wonder, then, dearly beloved, if love too has the strength of its own love. This the martyrs had, in it they endured all hard things. They loved what they did not see, but they believed it certain, and as far as it can be discerned by a man bearing flesh, they discerned it with the heart. For does not this flesh truly have its own beauty? And does not unfading wisdom have its own beauty? But the beauty of wisdom the wicked perceive and at length sometimes desire. For they too would want to be wise, if it were permitted them both to hold what they love and at the same time to have wisdom. Without doubt they would want both, they did not reject wisdom. You find a lover of fleshly pleasure wanting to be wise too. But you find a wise man a despiser of fleshly pleasure. It is very rare that you find a lover of pleasure a despiser of wisdom. If he could, he would have both, but he prefers one to the other, and so he imposes on himself, a wretch. He defrauds himself who loses better things by loving worse, but he does not feel the fraud in heavenly things who is delighted in base things.

**5.** Give me, then, a lover of the good of whom the Apostle says: *And who can harm you if you are lovers of the good?*[^1] For in that which you love you suffer no loss. Whatever he who rages against you takes from you, he will not lose you, the lover, who made you. And as much as earthly things are taken away, so much are heavenly gifts increased, if for the love of them these are taken away. For it matters why you lose anything. And therefore let it be not the punishment that makes the martyr, but the cause. So we do not justify those martyrs who suffered many things, except when we attend to why they suffered. *For your sake,* he says—it is the voice of the martyrs—*for your sake we are put to death all the day long*[^2]. Take away: *for your sake;* what does it profit: *we are put to death all the day long?* Add: *for your sake;* what harm is it to be put to death all the day long? To be put to death all the day long for your sake, not only does no harm but even profits very much. The cause is in this which is said: *for your sake;* the suffering is in this which is said: *we are put to death all the day long.* You build the suffering well, if you do not take away the foundation, the love of God.

**6.** It matters what you say: "for your sake." For even that lascivious lover who ran into and clung to the beauty of the flesh, so boasts to his beloved: "For your sake, for your sake," he says, "I bore the anger of my father, for your sake I was beaten by a most severe father and by most harsh masters. For your sake I spent absolutely whatever I had, for your sake I remained in want." How many things you say: "for your sake"! And nothing for you? Indeed not only nothing for you but even everything "for your sake."

**7.** If money could hear its lovers, how many would say to it: "For your sake I bore a hard winter at sea, for your sake I sustained so many shipwrecks, for your sake, perishing in the waves, I made jettison, for your sake I lost both you; for, attending to what I still desired to have, I lost what I had." How many things: "for your sake"! But it is deaf, the thing to which you speak, nor does it hear you if for its sake you lose even yourself. And what does it profit, when for the sake of money you have perished? Both you perish and you do not find it. Indeed, if you have any, by perishing here you leave it behind. You pass on; then comes another lover of it. How many, loving it, left it behind, and by loving and by passing on perished. *For although man walks in an image, yet he is troubled in vain*[^3]. It is to be pitied that, although he walks in the image of God indeed, he is troubled in vain: *He treasures up, and does not know for whom he gathers*[^4]. For why is he troubled, except to treasure up? Behold, treasure up, but where wisdom commands, not where avarice abides.

**8.** Concerning money the Lord gave counsel, lest what has been acquired perish. *Make for yourselves,* he says, *friends of the mammon of iniquity, that they too may receive you into eternal dwellings*[^5]. The martyrs, having a good cause and for the love of God sustaining many things, were received by certain ones when hungry, were clothed when naked, were taken in when strangers. For there is service to those set in tribulation. They were made friends from the mammon of iniquity. So the Lord gave good counsel even about money, if anyone hears it. For surely, if you love your money, you ought to beware lest it perish. If it has perished to them, it perishes to you. For from you it falls, to another it accrues. Do something from it, that it may not perish to you. And since it will depart before you, treasure up in heaven, *where no thief comes near, nor moth corrupts*[^6]. The place is fortified, why do you hesitate to move? What you have, send ahead, that you may come where you have sent it. Buy something from it for yourself which cannot perish. You know, dearly beloved, with what counsel the greedy for money act when they see themselves to have some coins. What do they say? "It is a round thing, it rolls, it perishes, it must be bound by the purchase of some possession." And they want to bind their money by buying an estate. And behold they bought an estate, they will have an estate. Surely the estate will not always have them? But neither will they have it, for after a little time they will move on without delay. Where you have bound your money, you cannot bind your soul there. For the time will come when your soul will be required of you. What you bought, whose will it be? So you will neither have the estate, nor will the estate have you, unless perhaps according to the body you are there dead and buried. For then something marvelous happens: it will have you, you will not have it.

**9.** So good and golden counsel of the Lord is given to him to whom it is said: "Move to where you do not lose." "What counsel is given! But I will not see it," you say. Afterward you will see it. But this which you have sent ahead you will not see. For you have given it at interest. You gave one thing, another will be rendered to you. He is almighty whom you wanted to make your debtor. He receives small things, but gives great. He receives few things, he will render very many. He created for you such an earth: you will cast a few grains to fill your granaries. If he created for you such an earth, what does he keep for you sowing good works, who made heaven and earth?

**10.** But we speak to the deaf greedy, whether to the lascivious loving beautiful bodies, or to the avaricious gathering and treasuring up money on earth. We speak to the deaf, they do not hear us. Lord, let them be healed that they may hear. To you nothing is impossible. To you no disease is incurable, because you are a great physician, especially because you showed toward us your love going before, because you did not spare your own Son, but handed him over for us all. How have you not with him given us all things. Open wide your throat, greedy man, now despise the few things, you will have many. He had conquered, ground down, trampled greed who said: *As having nothing, and possessing all things*[^7].

**11.** So to many of the lascivious and avaricious the holy martyrs seemed to be mad when for the name of Christ they suffered so much and always truthfully confessed him. They were pressed to deny; confessing, they were killed, burned, exposed to beasts, openly they suffered horrible things; in secret they were crowned unspeakably. If earthly things were sought from them, what can be added to this glory by which their birthdays are celebrated? Many brave men went mad for glory, and said that blood must be shed for the homeland, nor did they hesitate to shed it, knowing that life itself passes away but that glory, even immortal, remains in them. What glory of theirs is to be compared to the glory of the martyrs? Who could find in the glory of this earth, who could find in the glory of human affairs, what an orator could find, what a fisherman could find? The tombs of brave men who died for the homeland are at Rome. To whose tomb has an Emperor deigned to enter? Behold, if earthly glory had been to be desired, not even of that were they defrauded who sought so great an honor among the angels. We see their glories on earth and are amazed. What would we feel if we saw them in heaven? How great an amazement of wonder would seize us if we saw the martyrs glorying among the angels, whose birthdays we see the peoples celebrating?

**12.** But seek the invisible things of the martyrs, my brothers. What they loved, love. What they endured, even if you do not endure it, prepare your minds to endure. First, as much as you can, choose the cause. For, the cause not being chosen, do not the martyrs suffer such things as thieves have often suffered, such as adulterers, such as evildoers, such as the sacrilegious? If you attend to the punishments, they are equal; if to the very many causes, they are far from one another. What has such proximity and likeness, and yet no nearness, as three crosses: one of the Lord and two of thieves? There were three, all were crosses, all were in one place, all those bodies hung on the wood, but the cause distinguished all. In the middle the Savior; on either side the guilty. That cross was a tribunal: he hung and distinguished; judged he hung and judged those hanging. Of those two guilty ones one merited punishment, the other a reward. Why did the other merit a reward? Because he changed his cause on the cross. Hanging he believed in him who was sent into far places; when the Lord came into his kingdom, then he wished to be kept in his mind. But what did the Lord say, when that one had said: *Lord, remember me when you come into your kingdom*[^8], as though saying: "I know my cause, I know my merits, so I am to be tortured for my deeds, but at least when you come, have mercy"; that one deferred, this one offered: *Amen, amen, I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise*[^9]; why do you send yourself far, when I come? *today you will be with me in paradise;* him whom you hope will come I am never absent, and I am everywhere and there I come; but *today you will be with me in paradise,* because where you will be happily, without me you will not be able to be happy. So all the souls of the blessed, their bodies not yet received, are happy with Christ, are happy only from Christ. For him they loved, him they cherished, in him they had righteousness, in him wisdom, in him knowledge, in him the hidden treasures of knowledge and wisdom. How many things they despised here by suffering. They strongly refused to be rich. For what does the poor man lack if he has God?

**13.** Love what is good, my brothers, nothing is more beautiful, even if it is not seen except with the eyes of the heart. To you I speak. Behold, all the things you see through the eye of the flesh are beautiful: heaven, earth, sea, and all that is in them, the stars gleaming from heaven, the sun filling the day, the moon tempering the night, the flying things, the swimming things, the walking things, men too, among all things made to the image of God, praisers of the creation, lovers of the creation, but only if they are lovers of the Creator. Whatever you love so as to neglect God, none but God made it. Whatever, I say, you love so as to neglect God, none but God made it. For if it were not beautiful, it would not be loved by you. And from where would it be beautiful, unless it were created by him who is invisibly beautiful? You love gold, God created it. You love beautiful bodies and the flesh, God created it. You love pleasant estates, God created them. You love this light as a great thing, God created it. If on account of what God created you neglect God, I beg you, love God himself too. For how worthy he is to be loved, how worthy he is to be loved because he created all that you love. So love it that you love him more. I do not want you to love nothing, but I want it ordered. Prefer the heavenly to the earthly, the immortal to the mortal, the everlasting to the temporal. Prefer the Lord to all, not by praising but by loving. For it is easy to prefer him by praising. Trial comes: I ask you whether you prefer by loving what you preferred by praising. For when you are asked: What is better: money or wisdom, money or righteousness, finally money or God? you do not hesitate to say: wisdom, righteousness, God. As you do not hesitate to say it, so do not hesitate to choose it. What is better: righteousness or money? And like boys, when they are questioned in school, you cry out vying with one another, "righteousness." I know you all, I hear your thoughts: righteousness is better. But trial will come. It sets out money on the other side. And trial says to you: "You can have this money; if you commit fraud the money accrues." But righteousness will say: "What do you choose? Now is the time for me to test your tongue." A while ago, questioned, you preferred righteousness to money; but now, the two set out: here money, there righteousness, as though blushing you close your eyes against righteousness, you stretch out your hand to the money. Ungrateful, foolish man, when, questioned by me, you preferred righteousness to money, you gave testimony against yourself. Surely God does not seek another witness after you convict yourself. In praising, righteousness was preferred, in choosing, money was preferred. Do you not see on whose side you wanted to be? On the perishing side of the perishing one. For money without doubt is perishing, because *both the world will pass away and all its desire*[^10]. Choose righteousness, because *he who does the will of God abides forever, as he himself abides forever*[^11].
