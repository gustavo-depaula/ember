# Sermon 63/A: On the Woman Who Suffered a Flow of Blood

**1.** The Church of God, the holy Church, of which he is the head. For if he is the head, we are the body; if indeed we are such as to suffer pressure from the crowds, not to make pressure on the Lord. A great multitude flows together to the Church spread through all lands, all nations believe; and in all nations one part presses, another is pressed; the part that is pressed bears it; the part that bears it will receive the reward, coming to the fruit of endurance, of which the Lord says in the Gospel: *It brings forth fruit with endurance*[^1]. For this is the part of the saints, spread everywhere; because it is wheat, and the wheat had to be sown through the whole field, that is, through the whole world; for the Lord said the field is the world[^2]. But all his faithful, approaching God not with lips but with the heart, he says are wheat; but all who so approach that they do not approach with the heart, he numbers as chaff and tares. Through the whole field, or through the whole threshing floor, both are present, both wheat and chaff; but the greater part is chaff, the lesser is wheat—lesser but more solid, lesser but heavier, lesser but more precious; for which one labors, for which one takes care; for which a barn, not the fire, is prepared. Let no one, then, congratulate himself because he enters within these walls; let him recall his attention to himself, let him question his heart; let him be to himself a most severe judge, that he may feel God a merciful father; let him not flatter himself, let him not show partiality to his own person, let him sit in the tribunal of his mind, let him apply to his conscience the torturers, fears, let him confess to God who he is: if he sees himself wheat, let him be pressed, threshed, let him endure, let him not be anxious that he is mixed with chaff: on the threshing floor the chaff can be with him, in the barn it will not be.

**2.** But since we have said, dearly beloved brothers, let us belong to the member of him whose type that woman bore. Your love awaits whose type that woman bore; for we say that she signified the Church which came from the Gentiles; for the Lord was going to the daughter of the synagogue ruler, to raise her up. The daughter of the synagogue ruler signifies the Jewish people; for the Lord came only to the Jews, saying: *I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^3]. He came as to the daughter of the synagogue ruler. Then she, coming as it were across, from I know not where, unknown, because she was unknowing, even touched the Lord by faith, saying: *If I touch the fringe of his garment, I shall be saved*[^4]. She touched, and was made whole. But there was in her a detestable disease, a flow of blood. And yet all shudder either to hear of this or to suffer it; they shudder at a flow of blood in the body, let them not, then, suffer it in the heart. The disease is more to be avoided in the heart. But in some way the perversity of the soul, the dweller, turned toward its own dwelling, has departed from itself. The lord, that is, the soul, wishes its body to be healed of a flow of the body, more than he who dwells in the very house, that is, itself. What does a house of marble and panels profit, where the householder is not well? What did I say? What does a body, sound and whole, profit, where the soul is sick, the inhabitant of the body? So a flow of blood in the soul is luxury: as the avaricious are like those with dropsy—for they crave to drink—so the luxurious are like a flow of blood. For the avaricious labor by craving, the luxurious by spending: there is the craving, here the flow; but each kills. There is need to have a physician, who comes to the diseases of souls: but for this reason he willed to heal the diseases of bodies, that he might show himself the Savior of the soul, because he is the creator of both. For he is not the creator of the soul, and not of the body; and therefore he wished to admonish the soul, that it be healed within. Therefore he healed the body: the soul was intent on the body, that what it saw Jesus working without, it might wish to be worked within. What is it that God worked? He healed the flow of blood, healed the leper, healed the paralytic. All these are diseases of the soul. The lame and the blind; for everyone limps who does not walk rightly in the way of life, and he is blind who does not believe God; and the luxurious suffers a flow of blood, and every man of two colors and a liar bears the spots of leprosy. And there is need that he heal within, who for this reason healed without, that he might be desired to heal within.

**3.** So this woman suffered a flow of blood, and is healed of carnal affection, because all her strength flowed away; so the soul, by pursuing carnal desires, spends all its strength. And this woman consumes all her substance on physicians; so it is written of her[^5]. As the Church of the Gentiles, wretched, seeking blessedness, seeking to have some strength, or seeking medicine, how much had it consumed on false physicians, on astrologers, on diviners, on the possessed, and on the seers of the temples? For all promise health, but cannot give it, because they themselves do not have what they may give. She had poured out all, then, and was not healed. She said: I touch his fringe; she touched, and was made whole. Let us seek what the fringe of the garment is. Let your love attend. By the garment of the Lord are understood the Apostles clinging to him. Seek which Apostle was sent to the Gentiles: you will find the apostle Paul sent; for his greatest work was in the apostolate of the Gentiles. So the apostle Paul, sent to the Gentiles, is the fringe of the Lord's garment, because he was the last of the Apostles. Is the fringe of a garment anything but both the last and least part? The Apostle says both of himself: *But I am the last of the Apostles*[^6], and: *I am the least of the Apostles*[^7]. He the last, he the least. This is the fringe of the garment: and the Church of the Gentiles, like the woman who touched the fringe, suffered a flow; she touched, and was made whole. And let us too touch, that is, believe, that we may be able to be saved.

[^1]: Luke 8:15
[^2]: See Matt 13:38
[^3]: Matt 15:24
[^4]: Matt 9:21
[^5]: See Mark 5:26
[^6]: 1 Cor 15:8
