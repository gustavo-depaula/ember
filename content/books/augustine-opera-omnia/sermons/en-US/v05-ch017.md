# Sermon 161

**1. 1.** When the Apostle was read, we heard him rebuking and restraining human lusts, and saying, *Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I, then, take the members of Christ and make them members of a harlot? Far from it*[^1]! He said, then, that our bodies are members of Christ; since Christ is our head, in that he was made man for us, the head of whom it was said, *He himself is the Savior of our body*. But his body is the Church[^2]. If, then, our Lord Jesus Christ took up only a human soul, his members would be only our souls; but because he took up a body too, through which he is also our head—we who consist of soul and body—surely our bodies too are his members. If, then, each one craving to fornicate became cheap to himself, and despised himself in himself; let him not despise Christ in himself; let him not say: I will do it, I am nothing: *All flesh is grass*[^3]. But your body is a member of Christ. Where were you going? Return. To where were you craving as it were to hurl yourself? Spare Christ in yourself, acknowledge Christ in yourself. *Shall I, then, take the members of Christ and make them members of a harlot*? For a harlot is one who consents with you to adultery; and perhaps she herself, a Christian woman, takes the members of Christ and makes them members of an adulterer. You despise Christ in one another, and do not acknowledge your Lord, nor consider your price. But what kind of Lord is he, who made his servants his brothers? But it was too little to make them his brothers, unless he made them his members. Has so great a dignity, then, become cheap? Because it was bestowed so kindly, is no honor rendered to it in return? If it were not bestowed, it would be longed for; because it is bestowed, is it despised?

**2. 2.** But these our bodies, which the Apostle says are members of Christ, on account of the body of Christ which he took from the kind of our body; these our bodies, then, the same Apostle says are a temple in us of the Holy Spirit, whom we have from God. On account of the body of Christ, our bodies are members of Christ; on account of the indwelling Spirit of Christ, our bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit. Which of these do you despise in yourself? Christ, of whom you are a member? Or the Holy Spirit, of whom you are a temple? The harlot herself, who consents with you to evil, you do not dare perhaps to bring into your bedroom, where you have your conjugal bed; but you seek some abject and foul place in your house, in which to wallow foully. You show honor, then, to the bedroom of your wife, and do not show honor to the temple of your God? You do not bring in the unchaste woman where you sleep with your spouse, and you yourself go to an unchaste woman, when you are the temple of God? I think the temple of God is better than the bedroom of your wife. For wherever you go, Jesus sees you; who made you, and redeemed you when lost, and for you, dead, died. You do not acknowledge yourself; but he does not turn his eyes from you—not to help, but to punish. *For the eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and his ears to their prayers*. At once he went on and terrified those who gave themselves an evil security, who said to themselves: I will do it; for God does not deign to attend to me doing such foul things. Hear what follows, attend to whose you are; since wherever you go, Jesus sees: *But the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil, to destroy their memory from the earth*[^4]. But from what earth? Where it is said, *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^5].

**3. 3.** For perhaps the evil, the unjust, the adulterer, the unchaste, the fornicator rejoices that he does it, and grows old while in him lust does not grow old, and says within himself: Surely it is true, *But the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil, to destroy their memory from the earth*. Behold, I have now grown old, who from my earliest age until this present day commit such things, I have buried many chaste men before me, I myself led to the grave the funerals of many chaste young men, and, unchaste, I have outlived the chaste. What is it that is said, that *the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil, to destroy their memory from the earth*? There is another earth where there is no unchaste man, there is another earth in the kingdom of God. *Do not err; neither fornicators, nor those who serve idols, nor adulterers, nor the effeminate, nor sodomites, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers will possess the kingdom of God*[^6]. This is, he will destroy their memory from the earth. For many committing such things place hope for themselves; on account of those who, living in ruin, place hope for themselves in the kingdom of God, to which they will not come, it was said, *He will destroy their memory from the earth*. For there will be a new heaven and a new earth, which the righteous will inhabit. There the ungodly, there the evil, there the most worthless are not allowed to dwell. Let him who is such now choose where he longs to dwell, while there is time that he may be able to be changed.

**4. 4.** For there are two dwellings; one in eternal fire, the other in the eternal kingdom. Suppose that in the eternal fire this one will be tormented in one way, that one in another; yet they will be there, there all will be tortured; this one less, that one more. For it will be more tolerable for Sodom on the day of judgment than for another city[^7]; and some go around sea and land to make one proselyte, and when they have made him, they make him a son of hell twice as much as they are themselves[^8]. Suppose that some twofold, others single; suppose that some more, others less; there is no region there where you may choose a place for yourself. Whatever milder torments are there, they are worse than what you dread in this age. Consider how you tremble if someone slanders you, lest you be sent to prison; and do you yourself live badly against yourself, that you may be sent into fire? You tremble, you are troubled, you grow pale, you run to the church, you long to see the bishop, you roll at his feet. He asks why. Free me, you say. What is going on? Behold, this man slanders me. And what is he going to do to you? Lord, I am shaken; Lord, I am being sent to prison; have mercy on me, free me. Behold how prison is feared, how confinement is feared; and the burning of hell is not feared! Finally, when calamity increases, and pressure rages more fiercely, and rages even to death, when it seems good to a man that he not die, not be killed, all cry out that help must be given, all aids are implored: come to the aid, run, for the sake of the soul. The whole heightening of the calamity is that it is said, for the sake of the soul. Help must indeed be given, nor must aid be denied to this fear; what can be done must be done, by him who can.

**5. 5.** Yet I wish to question the one in peril, and moving my inmost feelings by this word; since he says, Run, for the sake of the soul. I easily answer him: I indeed run for the sake of your flesh; would that you would run for the sake of your soul. And know that I run for the sake of your body, not for the sake of your soul. I would rather hear Christ saying true things than you murmuring out of a false fear. For the Lord himself says, *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^9]. Surely you wish me to run for the sake of your soul; behold, the one you fear, and under whose threats you grow pale, cannot kill your soul; he rages as far as the body; do not you rage against your soul. By him it cannot be killed, by you it can; not by a lance, but by a tongue. The enemy who strikes you ends this life: *but the mouth that lies kills the soul*[^10]. From these things, then, which in this time men fear, let them gather what they ought to fear. For he fears prison, and does not fear hell? He fears the torturers of the questioning, and does not fear the infernal angels? He fears temporal torment, and does not fear the punishments of the eternal fire? Finally, he fears to die for a little, and does not fear to die forever?

**5. 6.** That one who is going to kill you, whom you fear, whom you shudder at, whom you flee, from fear of whom you are not allowed to sleep, and if you see him in dreams when you sleep, you are terrified—what is he going to do to you? He is going to shut out your soul from your flesh; see where your soul, shut out, may go. For he cannot kill your flesh in any other way except by shutting out your soul from there, by which your flesh lives. For by the presence of your soul the flesh lives, and as long as your soul is present in your flesh, your flesh must necessarily live. But the one who seeks your death wishes to cast out from your flesh your life, by which your flesh lives.

**6. 6.** Do you suppose there is not some life by which your soul itself lives? For the soul is a certain life, by which your flesh lives. Do you suppose there is no other life by which your soul itself lives? Or, just as your flesh has a life—the soul by which your flesh lives—does your soul itself too come to have some life of its own? And just as the flesh, when it dies, breathes out the soul, its life; so the soul too, when it dies, breathes out some life of its own? If we find what this life is—not of your body, which is your soul, but the life of the life of your body, that is, the life of your soul; if we find it, then concerning this death by which you fear that your soul may be cast out of the flesh, I think you ought rather to fear that death, lest the life of your soul be cast out from your soul. Briefly, then, I will say it—and why am I held by many words? The life of the body is the soul, the life of the soul is God. The Spirit of God dwells in the soul, and through the soul in the body, so that our bodies too are a temple of the Holy Spirit, whom we have from God. For the Spirit comes to our soul; because *the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^11]; and he possesses the whole who holds the principal part. For in you that has the rule which is better. God, holding what is better—that is, your heart, your mind, your soul—surely through the better possesses also the lower, which is your body. Let the enemy, then, rage, let him threaten death, let him do it if it is permitted, let him shut out the soul from your flesh; let your soul not shut out from itself its own life. If you rightly lament, and think you say pitiably to your powerful enemy: Do not strike, spare my blood; does God not say to you, *Have mercy on your soul, pleasing God*[^12]? Your soul perhaps says: Ask him not to strike; for I dismiss you. For if he strikes, I cannot remain with you. Ask him not to strike, if you wish me not to dismiss you. Who says to you, if you wish me not to dismiss you? You yourself; for you who speak are a soul. If, then, he strikes the flesh, you flee, you go out, you migrate, earth lies in earth. Where will be what animated the earth? What was given to you by the breath of God, where will it be? If it did not breathe out its own life, that is, its God, it will be in the one it did not lose, it will be in the one it did not shut out from itself. But if you obey the infirmity of your soul, which says to you, He strikes, and I dismiss you; do you not fear God saying to you, You sin, and I dismiss you?

**7. 7.** From a vain fear let us take a useful fear. The fear of all men fearing to lose temporal things—men who are going to migrate at some time, and who tremble to migrate, wishing always to put off what they cannot keep from being taken away—is vain. This fear of men is vain; and yet it exists, and is vehement, and cannot be resisted. Hence men are to be reproached, hence rebuked, hence lamented, hence mourned: fearing to die, and doing nothing else but to die later. Why do they not bring it about that they not die? Because whatever they do, they do not bring it about that they not die. But can they do anything by which they may bring it about that they never die? In no way. Plainly, whatever you do, however much you keep watch, wherever you flee, whatever fortifications you seek, with whatever riches you ransom yourself, with whatever cunning you deceive the enemy, you do not deceive the fever. For you do nothing else, that you may not die soon by the enemy, except that you may die later sometime by the fever. You have something to do, that you may never die. If you fear death, love life. Your life is God, your life is Christ, your life is the Holy Spirit. You do not please him by acting badly. He does not inhabit a ruinous temple, he does not enter a filthy temple. But groan to him, that he may cleanse the place for himself; groan to him, that he may build a temple for himself; what you destroyed, let him construct; what you laid waste, let him reform; what you cast down, let him raise up. Cry to God, cry within, cry where he hears; for there too you sin, where he sees; cry there, where he hears.

**7. 8.** And when you have corrected your fear, and begun to fear usefully—not temporal torments, but the punishments of the eternal fire—and therefore have not been an adulterer (for this is what we were speaking of, on account of the Apostle, who said, *Your bodies are members of Christ*); when, then, you have begun not to be an adulterer because you fear to burn in the everlasting fire, you are not yet to be praised; not indeed so to be grieved over as before, but yet not yet to be praised.

**8. 8.** For what great thing is it to fear punishment? It is a great thing—but to love righteousness. I question you, and I find you out. Look at my question that sounds aloud, and make about yourself the question that is silent. I say to you, then: When, overcome by lust, you have a consenting partner, why do you not commit adultery? And you will answer: Because I fear hell, I fear the punishment of the eternal fire, I fear the judgment of Christ, I fear the fellowship of the devil, lest I be punished by him and burn with him. What? Shall I say: You fear badly? Just as I said to you about the adversary, that he sought to kill your body. For there I rightly said: You fear badly, your Lord made you secure, saying, *Do not fear those who kill the body*. Now, when you say to me: I fear hell, I fear to burn, I fear to be punished forever; what shall I say? You fear badly? You fear in vain? I dare not, since the Lord himself, having taken away fear, added fear; and he said, where he said, *Do not fear those who kill the body, and afterward have nothing they can do; but fear him who has power to kill both body and soul in the hell of fire; yes, I tell you, fear him*[^13]. When, then, the Lord struck fear, and struck it vehemently, and by repeating the word doubled the threat, shall I say: You fear badly? I will not say these things. Fear plainly, you fear nothing better; there is nothing you ought more to fear. But I question you: If God did not see you when you do it, nor would anyone convict you in his judgment, would you do it? Look at yourself. For you cannot answer all my words; look into yourself. Would you do it? If you would do it, then you fear punishment, you do not yet love chastity, you do not yet have love; you fear in a servile way; it is dread of evil, not yet love of good. But fear nevertheless, that this dread may guard you, that it may lead you to love. For this fear, by which you fear hell, and therefore do not do evils, restrains you; and so it does not allow the inner soul, willing to sin, to do so. For fear is a kind of guard, like the pedagogue of the law; it is the letter that threatens, not yet the grace that helps. Yet let this fear guard you, while you do not act through fearing, and love will come; it enters into your heart, and as much as it enters, so much does fear go out. For fear was working that you not do it; love works that you not wish to do it, even if you could commit it with impunity.

**9. 9.** I have said what you should fear, I have said what you should seek. Pursue love, let love enter; admit it, by fearing to sin, admit the love that does not sin, admit the love that lives well. As that one enters, as I had begun to say, fear begins to go out. The more that one enters, the less fear there will be. When that one has wholly entered, there will be no fear; because perfect love casts out fear[^14]. Love enters, then, it drives out fear. But it too does not enter unaccompanied. It has with it its own fear, which it itself introduces; but that chaste fear, remaining unto the age of the age[^15]. Servile fear is that by which you fear to burn with the devil; chaste fear is that by which you fear to displease God. Consider, dearly beloved, and question human affections themselves. A servant fears to offend his master, lest he command him to be flogged, command him to be put in fetters, command him to be shut in prison, command him to be ground down in the mill. Fearing these things the servant does not sin; but when he perceives that the eyes of his master are absent, and has no witness by whom he can be convicted, he does it. Why does he do it? Because he feared punishment, he did not love righteousness. But a good man, a just man, a free man (for only the just man is free; for everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin[^16]) is delighted by righteousness itself; and if he could sin without a witness, he fears a witness, even God; and if he could hear God saying to him: I see you when you sin, I will not condemn you, but you displease me; that one, unwilling to displease the eyes of the father, not of a fearsome judge, fears—not lest he be condemned, not lest he be punished, not lest he be tortured; but lest he offend the joy of the father, lest he displease the eyes of the one who loves. For if he himself loves, and perceives the Lord who loves him, he does not do what displeases the one who loves him.

**10. 10.** Attend to slippery and dishonorable lovers; if anyone, out of love for a woman, wanton and worthless, dresses himself otherwise than as she pleases, dresses himself otherwise than as it pleases his beloved, or adorns himself otherwise than as it pleases her. She has said: I do not want you to have such a cloak; he does not have it; if in winter she says to him: I love you in the mantle; he chooses to shiver rather than to displease. Surely she whom he displeases is not going to condemn him? surely not to send him to prison? surely not to bring in torturers? This alone is feared there: I will not see you; this alone is trembled at there: You will not see my face. If an unchaste woman says this, and terrifies; does God say it, and not terrify? Surely very much; but only if we love. But if we do not love, we are not terrified by that; but are we terrified as slaves, by fire, by hell, by the most atrocious threats of Tartarus, by the most heightened angels of the devil and their punishments? Let us at least be terrified by that. If we love that less, let us at least fear those things.

**11. 11.** Therefore let there be no fornications. *You are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you. If anyone corrupts the temple of God, God will corrupt him*[^17]. Marriages are lawful; require nothing more. For no great burden has been imposed. On virgins a greater love has imposed a greater burden. The virgins were unwilling to do what was lawful, that they might please the more him to whom they devoted themselves. They aspired to that greater beauty of their heart. What do you command? As though they should say: What do you command? That we not be adulteresses, is this what you command? By loving you, we do more than you command. *Concerning virgins*, says the Apostle, *I have no precept of the Lord*. Why, then, do they do this? *But I give counsel*[^18]. But those loving women, to whom earthly marriages had become cheap, who did not long for earthly embraces, so accepted the precept that they did not refuse the counsel; that they might please more, they adorned themselves more. For the more the adornments of this body—that is, of the outer man—are sought, the greater are the losses of the inner; but the less the adornments of the outer man are sought, the more the inner man is adorned with beautiful morals. Hence Peter too says, *Adorning themselves not in braided hair*. For when he had said, *Adorning themselves*, what else but these visible adornments would be supposed by the carnal? At once he took away from thought what desire was seeking. *Not*, he says, *in braided hair, nor gold, nor pearls, nor costly garment; but that hidden man of the heart, who is rich before God*[^19]. For God would not give riches to the outer man and leave the inner needy; he gave invisible riches to the invisible, and adorned the invisible invisibly.

**12. 12.** Eager for these adornments, the maidens of God, the holy virgins, neither sought what was lawful, nor consented to what they were forced to. For many overcame even the opposing attempts of their own parents by the fire of heavenly love. The father was angry, the mother wept; she did not care, before whose eyes was set the one *beautiful in form above the sons of men*[^20]. For she desired to be adorned for him, that she might take wholly his care. Because *she who is married thinks of the things of the world, how to please her husband; but she who is unmarried thinks of the things of God, how to please God*[^21]. See what it is to love. He did not say: She thinks how not to be condemned by God. For this fear is still that servile one, the guard indeed of the wicked, that they may abstain from evils, and by abstaining may be worthy to admit love into themselves. But those women think not how they may not be punished by God, but how they may please God, by inner beauty, by the comeliness of the hidden man, by the comeliness of the heart, where they are naked to his eyes; naked within, not without; whole both within and without. Let virgins at least teach the married men and women not to go into adultery. They do more than is lawful; let those not do what is not lawful.
