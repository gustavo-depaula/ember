# Sermon 139

**1.** The Lord God, Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, born of God the Father without any mother, and born of a virgin mother without a human father, you have heard what he said: *I and the Father are one* [^1]. So receive this, so believe it, that you may deserve to understand it. For faith ought to precede understanding, that understanding may be the reward of faith. For the Prophet most openly said: *Unless you believe, you will not understand* [^2]. What, then, is simply preached, must be believed; what is subtly discussed, must be understood. First, then, to imbue your minds through faith, we preach to you Christ the only Son of God the Father. Why is "only" added? Because he whose Only one he is has many sons by grace. All the rest of the saints, then, are sons of God by grace, he alone by nature. Those who are sons by grace are not what the Father is. In short, no one of the saints dared to say what that Only one says: *I and the Father are one*. Is he not the Father of us too? If he is not our Father, how do we say, praying: *Our Father who are in heaven* [^3]? But we are sons whom by his will he made sons, not of his nature did he beget sons. He begot us indeed, but, as it is said, adopted, begotten by the benefit of the one adopting, not by nature. In short, we are called this too, because God called us to the adoption of sons [^4]; we are adopted men. He is called the Only one, the Only-begotten; because this he is which the Father is; but we are men, God is the Father. Because, then, he is this which the Father is, he said, and said truly: *I and the Father are one*. What is: *we are one?* We are of one nature. What is: *we are one?* We are of one substance.

**2.** Perhaps you understand too little what it is to be of one substance. Let us labor, that you may understand; let God help both me speaking and you hearing; me, that I may say things that are true and apt for you; but you, before all and especially that you may believe; then that, as you can, you may understand. What, then, is it: of one substance? Let me apply likenesses to you, that what is understood too little may grow clear by an example. For instance, God is gold, his Son too is gold. If from earthly things likenesses to heavenly are not to be given, how is it written: *But the rock was Christ* [^5]? Therefore whatever the Father is, this the Son too is; as I said, for example: The Father is gold, the Son is gold. For he who says: The Son is not of the substance of which the Father is; what else does he say than: The Father is gold, the Son is silver? If the Father is gold, the Son silver, the only Son has degenerated from the Father. A man begets a man; of whatever substance is the father who begets, of that substance is the son who is begotten. What is, of that substance? He is a man, the other too a man; he has a soul, the other too a soul; he has flesh, the other too flesh; what the one is, this the other too is.  
But the Arian heresy answers me and says. What does it say to me? Attend to what you said. What did I say? That the son of man is to be compared to the Son of God. Plainly to be compared: but not, as you think, in propriety; but in likeness. But you, whatever you want to make of this, say it. Do you not see, it says, that the father who begot is greater, and the son who was begotten is less? How then do you say, tell me; how then do you say that the Father and the Son, God and Christ, are equal; when you see that a man, when he begets a son, the son is less, and the father is greater? Wise man, in eternity you seek out times; where there are no times, you ask for ages. When the father is greater, the son less, both are temporal; the one grows, because the other grows old. For by nature the man father, by nature, as I said, did not beget one less; but by age. Do you want to know that by nature he did not beget one less? Wait, let him grow, and he will be equal to the father. For a little boy by growing comes to the greatness of his father. But you so say that the Son of God was born less, that he never grows and even by growing comes to the greatness of his Father. Now then the son of man born of man, was born in a better condition than the Son of God. How? Because he grows, and comes to the greatness of his father. But Christ, as you say, was for this reason born less, that he should remain less, and not even the accession of age should be awaited. So then you say that there is in the nature a diversity. But why do you say this, except because you do not want to believe the Son to be of the same substance, of which substance is the Father? In short, first confess that he is of the same substance, and say he is less. Attend to a man, he is a man. What is his substance? He is a man. What of the one he begets? He is less, but he is a man. The age is unequal, the nature is equal. Say you too: What the Father is, this the Son is, but the Son is less. Say it, make the step, say of the same substance, but less, and you come to equal. For you do not approach a little, you do not come near a little to the truth, by which you would confess him equal, if you have confessed him of the same substance and less. But he is not of the same substance, this you say. Therefore because you say this: He is both gold and silver; what you say is such, as if a man begot a horse. For of one substance is a man, of another a horse. If, then, the Son is of another substance than the Father, the Father begot a monster. For when a creature, that is, a woman, bears what is not a man, it is called a monster. But that it may not be a monster, what is born is this which is the one who begot; that is, man and man, horse and horse, dove and dove, sparrow and sparrow.

**3.** To his creatures he gave, that what they are, this they should beget. To his creatures God gave, to mortal creatures, to earthly ones he gave, he bestowed, that they should beget this which they are; and do you think that he himself could not keep this for himself, who is before the ages? He who has no beginning of time, would beget a son not this which he himself is, would beget one degenerate? Hear how great a blasphemy it is to say that the only Son of God is of another substance. Utterly if it is so, he is degenerate. If you say to any son of man: You are degenerate; how great an injury is it? And how is a son of man called degenerate? For instance, his father is strong, he is timid and lazy. Whoever sees him, and wants to rebuke him, attending to his father a strong man, what does he say to him? Go on, degenerate one. What is: *degenerate?* Your father was a strong man, and you tremble with fear. He to whom this is said is degenerate by vice, equal by nature. What is: equal by nature? He is a man, which his father too is. But the one strong, the other cowardly; the one bold, the other timid; yet man and man. By vice, then, he is degenerate, not by nature. You, when you say the only Son, the one Son of the Father, is degenerate, say nothing else, except that he is not this which the Father is; and you say he was not now born degenerate, but was begotten degenerate. This blasphemy who could bear? With whatever sort of eyes, if they could see this blasphemy, they would flee it, and become Catholics.

**4.** But what shall I say, brothers? Let us not be angry at them: but let us pray for them, that the Lord give them understanding, because perhaps they were born this way. What is: they were born this way? They received this from their parents, which they hold. They prefer race to truth. Let them become what they are not, that they may be able to keep what they are; that is, let them become Catholics, that they may be able to keep what they are as men; that the creation of God may not perish in them, that the grace of God may come to them. For they think that they honor the Father through insult to the Son. When you say to them: You blaspheme; he answers: Why do I blaspheme? Because you say the Son is not this which the Father is. And he to me: Rather you blaspheme. Why? Because you want to make the Son equal to the Father. I want to make the Son equal to the Father, surely not a stranger? The Father rejoices, when I make the only Son equal to him; he rejoices, because he does not envy. And God, because he does not envy the only Son, therefore begot this which he himself is. You do an injury both to the Son, and to the Father himself, in whose honor you want to do insult to the Son. For surely you say the Son is not of the same substance, lest you do injury to his Father. I quickly show you that you do injury to both. How, he says? If I say to someone's son: Degenerate one, you are not like your father; degenerate, you are not what your father is. The son hears that, and is angry, and says: Then was I born degenerate? The father hears that, and is more angry. But, angry, what does he say? Then did I beget a degenerate son? Then if I am one thing, and begot another, I begot a monster. What is it, that when you want to defer to one through injury to the other, you do injury to both? You offend the Son, but you do not propitiate the Father. When out of the insult to the Son you honor the Father, you both offend the Son and the Father. From whom do you flee? To whom do you flee? Do you flee, with the Father angry at you, to the Son? What does he say to you? To whom do you flee, whom you made degenerate? Do you, with the Son offended, run to the Father? He himself too says to you: To whom do you flee, whom you said begot a degenerate? Let it suffice you; hold this, commit this to memory, write this in your faith. But that you may understand this, to God the Father and the Son, who are one, pour out prayers.
