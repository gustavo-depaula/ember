# Sermon 265/A: On the Fortieth Day, the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** The holy and solemn day of the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ has dawned today: *Let us exult and rejoice in it*[^1]. Christ descended, the underworld lay open; Christ ascended, the heavens shone bright. Christ on the wood, let the raging insult; Christ in the tomb, let the guards lie. Christ in the underworld, let those at rest be visited; Christ in heaven, let all nations believe. He, then, ought to be the author of our sermon, who is the bestower of our salvation. We speak to you of no other than him who just now was speaking to all of us from the Gospel, and, about to ascend to the Father, was saying to his disciples: *These things I have spoken to you, while I was present with you. But the Paraclete, the Spirit of truth, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things, and will remind you of all things whatever I have said. Let not your heart be troubled, nor be afraid. You heard that I said to you: I go to my Father; for the Father is greater than I*[^2].

**2.** You know, brothers, that our Lord Jesus Christ became for our sake what we are, but nonetheless remained in that form in which he is equal to the Father. For we believe that the Son of God was made a partaker of our weakness, yet not deprived of his own majesty. This, then, is our faith: God above us, and the same man among us. But because for our sake, in the form of the lowliness he assumed, he did many things here, to hide the substance of divinity, which lay hidden in him, and to show the human alone, which appeared in him; those who could not discern and understand this founded heresies. Among these are, beyond the rest, the Arians, who contend that God the Father is greater than God the Son. Let the Catholic truth answer them briefly and clearly.

**3.** For we ask of them, in what respect they say the Father is greater than the Son. If they say by magnitude, that is, by some bodily mass, as we say, for example: That mountain is greater than that one, or: That city is greater than that one; we answer them from the Gospel, that *God is Spirit*[^3], and bodily things cannot be compared with spiritual things. For one thing can be called greater or less only when in both a bodily form is defined. But God is neither extended by mass, nor distinguished by the lineaments of a body, nor enclosed by places, nor confined by straits, nor terminated by any limits: for God is great not by mass, but by power. Let the unworthy phantasms of thoughts, then, cease and rest, which burden the minds of the faithful with their imaginations; let the familiarity of the flesh too utterly depart: when we think of God, let no appearance of flesh meet our eyes.

**4.** But again they say that the Father is greater by time, that is, by age; for they assert that it can in no way be that the one who begets and the one who is born be of the same age. For they say it is necessary that he who generates exist first, from whom in turn the one who is born can exist. And from where is this, except from the thoughts of the flesh? For this they learned from the custom of human generation; nor do they consider that among men, where the son is feebler in age, there the father too is more failing in old age: and surely the son, who is younger in birth, whatever he grows and is strengthened, the father grows old and fails. If, then, they everywhere contend that the Father is older, they must of necessity confess the Son is stronger. Which, if it is surely absurd to think of God, let them at last cease to commend divine secrets by human sense.

**5.** But it is too little to convince them so, unless we can demonstrate something in the visible creation, where the one who is born is of the same age as his generator. To drive away these shadows of error, then, let us bring forward one lamp for comparison, which pours out a trembling light by the kindling of burning flax. Fire surely burns: the fire is in substance, the splendor is in appearance; and yet the splendor is born not of the splendor of the fire, but of the fire: and yet the fire never was without its splendor, although the splendor is born of the fire; but from when that small fire first took its beginning, it arose surely with its coeval splendor. The splendor, then, is coeval with the fire from which it is born, and the splendor would surely be coeternal, if the fire were eternal.

**6.** But far be it that by this most lowly comparison we should seem to have done an injury to our Lord Jesus Christ. We ought, then, to show in the Gospel where the Son himself, or in what form, said himself to be inferior to the Father, *made obedient unto death*, or where he showed himself equal to his begetter: *I and the Father are one*[^4]. For they themselves say to us on the contrary: See, the Son himself said: *The Father is greater than I*; nor do they understand that he said this set in the flesh, when he was not only less than God the Father, but, as the divine Psalm says: was *made even a little less than the angels*[^5]. If they want to hear this alone willingly, why do they not attend to what he himself said again: *I and the Father are one?* Then let them consider why it was said: *The Father is greater than I.* For, about to ascend to the Father, the disciples were saddened that he would leave them in bodily form; and he said to them: *Because I said to you: I go to the Father, sadness has filled your heart. If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I*[^6]. This is to say: therefore I withdraw from your eyes this form of a servant, in which the Father is greater than I, that, the form of a servant being removed from the eyes of the flesh, you may be able to see the Lord spiritually.

**7.** Therefore both because of the true form of a servant, which he had assumed, he spoke truly: *The Father is greater than I*, because surely God is greater than a man; and because of the true form of God, in which he remained with the Father, he spoke truly: *I and the Father are one.* He ascended, then, to the Father by that which he was a man, but remained in the Father by that which he was God, because in the flesh he came forth to us, and did not withdraw from the Father. He ascended, I say, to the Father, the *Word*, which *was made flesh*, that it might dwell among us; and he promised his presence, saying: *Behold, I am with you all the days until the consummation of the age*[^7]. According to the form of God the apostle John says of him: *He is the true God, and eternal life*[^8]. According to the form of a servant the apostle Paul says of him: *Who, since he was in the form of God, did not think it robbery to be equal to God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^9]. According to the form of God he himself speaks of himself: *I and the Father are one*[^10]; according to the form of a servant he says: *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^11]. From where is that confidence? from where is this dread? That voice surely from the property of his substance, but this from the participation of the weakness he assumed.

**8.** Understanding these things, then, prudently from the holy Scriptures, beloved, let us distinguish while we read; but, while we distinguish, lest perhaps we err, let us ask understanding from the Lord himself.
