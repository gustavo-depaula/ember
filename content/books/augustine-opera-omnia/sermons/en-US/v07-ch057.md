# Sermon 270: On the Day of Pentecost

**1.** Since we celebrate the holy solemnity of so holy a day, that today the Holy Spirit himself came, this festive and welcome solemnity admonishes us to say something about that very gift of God, the grace of God, and the abundance of his mercy toward us, that is, about the Holy Spirit himself. In the school of the Lord we speak as fellow students. For we have one master, in whom we are all one: who, lest perhaps we dare to be proud about teaching, admonished us, and said: *Do not be called Rabbi by men; for one is your master, Christ*[^1]. Under this master, then, whose chair is heaven, because we are to be instructed by his letters, attend to the few things I will say, by his gift who commands that I speak. You who know, recall; you who did not know, receive. Often it moves a devoutly curious mind, if yet human frailty and weakness be admitted to scrutinize such things. Rather, it is admitted. For not because something in the holy Scriptures is covered is it therefore closed so as to be denied, and not rather opened to one who knocks, the Lord himself saying: *Ask, and you will receive; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you*[^2]. Often, then, it moves the mind of the studious, why on the fiftieth day after the Lord's passion and resurrection the promised Holy Spirit was sent.

**2.** Here first I admonish your Charity, that it not weary you to consider somewhat why it was said by the Lord himself: *He cannot come, unless I go away*[^3]. As if, to speak according to carnal sense, the Lord Christ kept something in the heights, and, descending from there, had entrusted to the Holy Spirit what he kept, and therefore could not come to us, unless he returned who would receive what was entrusted; or as if we could not bear both, nor endure the presence of both. As if indeed one were separated from the other; or as if, when they come to us, they themselves suffered straits, and we were not rather enlarged. What, then, does it mean: *He cannot come, unless I go away? For it is expedient*, he says, *for you that I go. For if I do not go, the Paraclete will not come to you*[^4]. What this means, then, as far as we either grasp, or estimate, or by his gift perceive, or speak what we believe, let your Charity briefly receive. It seems to me that the disciples had been occupied about the human form of the Lord Christ, and, like men, were held by a human affection toward the man. But he wished them rather to have a divine affection, and so to make them spiritual from carnal: which a man does not become except by the gift of the Holy Spirit. This, then, he says: I send you a gift, by which you may be made spiritual; namely the gift of the Holy Spirit. But you cannot become spiritual, unless you cease to be carnal. But you will cease to be carnal, if the form of the flesh is taken from your eyes, that the form of God may be implanted in your hearts. For by this human form the Lord—that is, the form of a servant: *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^5]—by this form of a servant, then, the affection of Peter too was held, when he feared that the one he greatly loved would die. For he loved the Lord Jesus Christ, as a man a man; as a carnal man a carnal man, not as a spiritual man the majesty. From what do we prove this? Because when the Lord himself had asked his disciples who he was said to be by men, and they, recalling the opinions of others, had said that some said he was John, others Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets; he said to them: *But who do you say that I am?* And Peter, one for the rest, one for all: *You are*, he says: *the Christ, the Son of the living God.* This excellently, most truly: deservedly he merited to receive such an answer: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, for flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven. And I tell you*, because you told me; you told, hear; you gave a confession, receive a blessing: therefore: *And I tell you, You are Peter*: because I am the rock, *you are Peter*; for not from Peter the rock, but from the rock Peter, because not from the Christian Christ, but from Christ the Christian. *And on this rock I will build my Church*: not on Peter, which you are; but on the rock, which you confessed. And *I will build my Church*: I will build you, who in this answer bear the figure of the Church. This and the rest, for which Peter had said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*: and had heard, as you remember: *Flesh and blood did not reveal it to you*, that is, the human mind, human weakness, human ignorance, *but my Father who is in heaven*[^6]. Then the Lord Jesus began to foretell his passion, and to show how great things he was going to endure from the impious. Here Peter was terrified, and feared lest Christ the Son of the living God should perish by death. Surely Christ the Son of the living God, good of good, God of God, living of living, the fount of life and true life, had come to destroy death, not to perish from death. Yet Peter, as a man, terrified, whose affection, as I said, was human about the flesh of Christ: *Be merciful to yourself, Lord*, he says; *far be it, let this not happen.* And the Lord refutes such words with a worthy and fitting answer. As he gave worthy praise to that confession, so also a worthy rebuke to this trembling: *Get behind me, Satan*[^7]. Where is that: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona?* Distinguish the words of the one praising, and the one restraining: distinguish the causes of the confession, and of the trembling. The cause of the confession: *Flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* The cause of the trembling: *For you do not savor the things that are of God, but the things that are of man*[^8]. We would not wish, then, that it be said to such people: *For it is expedient for you that I go? If I do not go, the Paraclete will not come to you.* Unless the human form is withdrawn from your carnal gaze, you will be least able to grasp, feel, think anything divine. Let this suffice. Hence it was fitting that, after the resurrection and ascension of the Lord Jesus Christ, his promise about the Holy Spirit be fulfilled. For so the Evangelist John too had said from his own person, when Jesus, signifying the same Holy Spirit, had cried out, saying: *If anyone thirsts, let him come to me, and drink; and rivers of living water will flow from his belly.* For the Evangelist went on and said: *But this he said about the Spirit, whom those were going to receive who were going to believe in him. For the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified*[^9]. Therefore our Lord Jesus Christ was glorified by the resurrection and ascension, and sent the Holy Spirit.

**3.** But as we have learned from the holy Books, he completed forty days with his disciples after the resurrection, manifesting to them, lest they should think anything was feigned, the truth of his raised body, going in and out with them, eating and drinking. But on the fortieth day, which we celebrated ten days ago, in their sight he ascended into heaven, going as he was promised, so to come; that is, in the human form in which he was judged, in that to judge. He willed to send the Holy Spirit on a day other than the day on which he ascended; not even after two or three days, but after ten days. This question has compelled us to scrutinize and inquire into certain hidden things of numbers. Forty days have four times ten. By this number is commended, as it seems to me, a sacred meaning. For we men speak to men; and we are rightly called expounders of the Scriptures, not affirmers of our own opinions. This number forty, then, having four times ten, signifies, as it seems to me, this age, which we now pass and complete; we are carried along and worn through, by the course of times, the instability of things, by failing and succeeding, by flying rapacity, and a certain flux of things that do not stand still. This age, then, is signified by this number, because of the fourfold seasons of the world, which complete the year; the fourfold cardinal points too of the world itself, known to all, and often mentioned by sacred Scripture: *From the East and West, from the North and South*[^10]. Through these fourfold seasons, then, and through the fourfold world, the law of God is preached, as the number ten. Hence too the Decalogue is first commended. For in ten precepts the law is established: because in this number ten there seems to be a certain perfection. For up to that number the progress of the one counting goes, and from there returns from one up to ten, again to one. So the hundreds, so the thousands; so beyond, with certain tens folding together, the forest of numbers grows infinitely. And so the law being perfect in ten, and the law preached through the fourfold world, four tens make forty. But we are taught, while we are in this living of this age, to abstain from worldly desires: which the forty-day fast, known to all by the name of Lent, signifies. This the Law commanded you, this Prophecy, this the Gospel. Therefore, because this is the Law, Moses fasted forty days: because this is Prophecy, Elijah fasted forty days: because this is the Gospel, the Lord Christ fasted forty days. When, then, after forty days other ten days are completed, ten once, ten simply, not fourfold, the Holy Spirit came, that the law might be fulfilled through grace. For the law without grace is a killing letter. *For if a law had been given*, he says, *that could give life, righteousness would entirely be from the law. But Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise from faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*[^11]. Therefore: *The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life*[^12]. Not that you fulfill something other than what is commanded you through the Letter: but the letter alone makes one guilty, grace both frees from sin, and grants the letter to be fulfilled. Hence comes through grace the forgiveness of all sins, and faith which works through love. Do not, then, think the letter condemned, because it was said: *The letter kills.* For this is, the letter makes one guilty. A precept is given, you are not helped by grace: you at once find yourself not only not a doer of the law, but even guilty of transgression. *For where there is no law, neither is there transgression*[^13]. The law, then, was not blamed, when it was said: *The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life*, as if it condemned the one, and praised the other: but *The letter kills*, the letter alone without grace. Take an example. By this manner of speech it was said: *Knowledge puffs up.* What does it mean: *Knowledge puffs up?* Is knowledge condemned? If it puffs up, we will better remain unskilled. But since he added: *But love builds up*[^14]; as where he added: *But the Spirit gives life*, he gave to understand, The letter without the Spirit kills, with the Spirit gives life, and makes the letter to be fulfilled; so knowledge without love puffs up, love with knowledge builds up. Therefore the Holy Spirit was sent, that the law might be fulfilled, and that there might come to pass what the Lord himself had said: *I came not to abolish the law, but to fulfill it*[^15]. This he grants to those who believe, this he grants to the faithful, this he grants to those to whom he gives the Holy Spirit. The more capable each becomes of him, the readier he becomes to do the law.

**4.** For I say to your Love, what you too will be able to consider and easily see: love fulfills the law. The fear of punishments makes a man work, but still slavishly. For if you do good for this reason, that you fear to suffer evil, or do not do evil for this reason, that you fear to suffer evil; if someone promised you impunity, you would at once seize iniquity. It would be said to you, Be secure, you will suffer no evil, do it; and you would do it. For you were recalled by fear of punishment, not by love of righteousness. For love was not yet working from within you. See, then, how love works. Let us so love him whom we fear, that we fear him with a chaste love. For a chaste wife too fears her husband. But distinguish these fears. A chaste wife fears lest she be abandoned by her absent husband: an adulterous wife fears lest she be caught by her husband coming upon her. Love, then, fulfills the law: because *perfect love casts out fear*[^16]; namely servile fear, coming from sin. For *the fear of the Lord, chaste, remains forever and ever*[^17]. If, then, love fulfills the law, from where is this love? Recall, attend, and see, that love is the gift of the Holy Spirit. *For the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^18]. And so deservedly, when ten days were completed, by which number the perfection of the law too is commended, the Lord Jesus Christ sent the Holy Spirit: because grace grants us to fulfill the law, which he came not to abolish, but to fulfill.

**5.** But the Holy Spirit is wont in the holy Scriptures to be commended by the number seven, not ten: the law by ten, the Holy Spirit by seven. That the law is by ten, is known: that the Holy Spirit is by seven, we recall. First in that very book, at the head of the book which is entitled Genesis, the works of God are enumerated. Light is made, heaven is made, which is called the firmament between water and water; the dry land is bared, the sea is separated from the land, to the earth is given the fruitful conception of all plants; the greater and lesser luminaries are made, the sun and the moon, and the other stars; the waters bring forth their offspring, the earth its own; man is made in the image of God: God completes all his works on the sixth day; in none of the works of God, so many enumerated and completed, did sanctification sound. God said: *Let there be light; and there was light: and God saw the light that it was good*: he did not say, God sanctified the light: *Let there be a firmament, and it was made; God saw that it was good*: nor was it said that the firmament was sanctified. And so the rest, lest we delay over most manifest things, up to those things that were made on the sixth day, when, man being created in the image of God, all things are enumerated, nothing is said to be sanctified. It came to the seventh day, where no work was done, but the rest of God is intimated, and God sanctified the seventh day. In the number seven of days sanctification first sounded, sought in all the places of the Scriptures and here first found. Where the rest of God is mentioned, our rest too is intimated. For God did not labor, so as to need rest; and as though after labor he congratulated himself on a feast day, he sanctified that day, on which it is permitted to be at leisure. This is a carnal thought. Rest after all our good works was intimated to us, as the rest of God after all his good works was intimated. *For God made all things, and behold all things were very good. And God rested on the seventh day from all his works which he made*[^19]. Do you too wish to rest? First do works very good. So the observance of the Sabbath was given to the Jews carnally, like the rest, by signifying sacred signs. For a certain leisure was commanded: that leisure which it signifies, you do. For spiritual leisure is the tranquility of the heart: but the tranquility of the heart comes from the serenity of a good conscience. Therefore he truly observes the Sabbath who does not sin. For so it is commanded to those to whom the observing of the Sabbath is commanded: *You shall do no servile work*[^20]. *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^21]. The number seven, then, is dedicated to the Holy Spirit, as ten to the law. This Isaiah the prophet too intimates in this place, where he says: *The Spirit of wisdom and understanding will fill him* (count), *of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and piety, the Spirit of the fear of God*[^22]. As though descending to us, the spiritual grace begins from wisdom, ends at fear. But we, ascending, tending from the lowest to the highest, ought to begin from fear, and end at wisdom. *For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^23]. It is long, and surpasses our strength, even if not your eagerness, to recall all the testimonies about the number seven, as it pertains to the Holy Spirit. Let these, then, suffice.

**6.** Now attend to this, how, since through the grace of the Holy Spirit the law is fulfilled, both the number ten had to be recalled and commended, as we have already shown, and the number seven because of the very grace of the Holy Spirit. Christ commended by the number ten, sending the Holy Spirit after ten days, the very law which he commanded to be fulfilled. Where, then, will we find here the commendation of the number seven especially because of the Holy Spirit himself? You have in the book of Tobit that very festival, that is, Pentecost, consisting of weeks. How? Multiply the seven by itself, that is, seven by seven, which is learned in the schools: seven times seven make forty-nine. To this form of seven, by the reasoning of seven by seven: (since the Holy Spirit himself gathers us, and assembles us: hence too he gave this as the first sign of his coming, that those who received him, even individuals, spoke in all tongues. For the unity of the body of Christ is gathered from all tongues, namely through all the nations spread through the whole world. And that one then spoke in all tongues, testified that that very unity would be in all tongues. But the Apostle says: *Bearing with one another in love*, which is love: *striving to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace*[^24]. Because, then, the Holy Spirit gathers us from many into one: but he is grasped through humility, repelled through pride. For a humble heart is like water seeking a hollow place, where it may settle: but by the lifting up of pride, as by the swelling of a hill, repelled, it slips away: hence it was said: *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^25]. What is, *gives grace?* He gives the Holy Spirit. He fills the humble, because he finds them capable). Therefore, because these things are so, to the forty-nine, which are made from seven by seven, that Pentecost may come, one is added, and unity is commended.

**7.** Because the eagerness of your Charity helps our weakness before the Lord our God, receive something, as it seems to me, so much the sweeter when it is expounded, the more hidden it is if it is not expounded. Before the resurrection, the Lord commanded his disciples to cast the nets into the sea, when he chose them. They cast them, they caught an innumerable multitude of fish, so that the nets were broken, and the loaded boats sank. But he did not command on which side they should cast the nets, but only said: *Cast the nets.* For if he had commanded that they cast on the right side, he would signify only the good to be caught: if on the left, only the bad. But because they were cast at random, neither on the right nor on the left; both good and bad. This is the signification of the Church of the present time, in this age. For those servants too, sent to the invited, went out; and brought in whomever they found, good and bad, and the wedding was filled with guests reclining. Both good and bad, then, are gathered now. But why do schisms too come about, if they do not break the nets? Why is the Church often pressed by the scandals of carnal crowds making bad tumult, if the boats too are not pressed? This, then, the Lord did before the resurrection. But after the resurrection he likewise found his disciples fishing: he commanded the same nets to be cast, not anyhow and at random, because now after the resurrection. For after the resurrection his body, which is the Church, will not have bad ones. *Cast*, he says, *the nets on the right side.* The nets were cast, at his command, on the right side, and fish of a fixed number were caught. For those without number, those by whom was signified the Church which is now passed, as from that catch: *I have announced and spoken, they are multiplied beyond number*[^26]. There are understood, then, certain supernumeraries, in a way superfluous: yet they are gathered. But there on the right fish are caught, both numbered, and great. *For he who does*, he says, *and so teaches, will be called great in the kingdom of heaven*[^27]. Therefore great fish were caught, in number one hundred fifty-three. Whom does it not move, that this number is not mentioned in vain? For the Lord, signifying nothing in vain, would not either say this very thing: *Cast the nets*; or have it pertain to him that they be cast on the right side. This number too, one hundred fifty-three, signifies something: and it belonged to the Evangelist to say, as though he had looked back at that first catch, where the broken nets signified schisms; because in that Church of eternal life there will be no schisms, because no dissension; all great, because full of love: as though, then, he had looked back at what was first done to signify schisms, it belonged to him to say in this second catch: *And though they were so great, the nets were not broken.* What, therefore, the right side signified, has been said, because all good. What the greatness, has been said, because *he who does and so teaches will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.* What too that the nets were not broken, has been said, because then there are no schisms. What, then, the one hundred fifty-three? This number will surely not itself be the number of the saints. For there will not be one hundred fifty-three saints; when those alone who have not defiled themselves with women are mentioned as twelve times twelve thousand. But this number, like a tree, seems to grow up from a certain seed. But the seed of this great number is a certain smaller number, which is seventeen. Seventeen makes one hundred fifty-three. But if you count from one to seventeen, and add all the numbers: for if you do not add the numbers from one to seventeen, which you mention as wholes, they will be only seventeen: but if you so count: one, two, three; as one and two and three make six, six and four and five make fifteen; so you reach to seventeen, carrying on your fingers one hundred fifty-three. Now then recall what I mentioned and commended a little before, and see who, and what, seventeen signify. Ten, the law; seven, the Holy Spirit. Hence what do we understand, except that those will be in the Church of the eternal resurrection, where there will be no schisms, where death will not be feared, because it is after the resurrection; those, then, will be there, and will live with the Lord forever, who fulfilled the law through the grace of the Holy Spirit and the gift of God, whose feast we celebrate?
