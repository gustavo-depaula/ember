# Sermon 350: On Love

**1.** The manifold abundance of the divine Scriptures, and their most wide-ranging teaching, my brothers, he comprehends without any error, and keeps without any labor, whose heart is full of love, as the Apostle says: *But the fullness of the law is love*[^1]; and in another place: *But the end of the precept is love, from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and unfeigned faith*[^2]. But what is the end of the precept, except the fulfillment of the precept? and what is the fulfillment of the precept, except the fullness of the law? What, then, he said there, *the fullness of the law is love*, this he also said here: *the end of the precept is love*. Nor can it be doubted in any way that a man is a temple of God, in whom love dwells. For John too says: *God is love*[^3]. But the Apostles saying these things and commending to us the excellence of love, could surely belch up nothing other than what they had eaten. For the Lord himself, feeding them with the word of truth, the word of love, which is himself the living bread who came down from heaven, said: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*. And again: *By this all will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another*[^4]. For he who came to destroy through the mockery of the cross the corruption of the flesh, and to dissolve by the newness of his death the oldness of the bond of our death, by a new commandment made a new man. For it was an old thing, that man should die. That this might not always prevail in man, a new thing was done, that God should die. But because he died in the flesh, not in the divinity, through the everlasting life of the divinity he did not permit the destruction of the flesh to be everlasting. And so, as the Apostle says: *He died for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^5]. He, then, who against the oldness of death brought the newness of life, himself against old sin sets the new commandment. Wherefore whoever you are who wish to extinguish old sin, extinguish greed by the new commandment, and embrace love. For just as greed is the root of all evils[^6], so love is the root of all good things.

**2.** Love securely possesses the whole greatness and breadth of the divine words, by which we love God and neighbor. For one heavenly Teacher teaches us, and says: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind: and you shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments depend the whole Law and the Prophets*[^7]. If, then, you have no leisure to scrutinize all the holy pages, to unroll all the wrappings of the words, to penetrate all the secrets of the Scriptures; hold to love, on which all things depend: so you will hold what you have learned there; you will hold also what you have not yet learned. For if you know love, you know something on which also that depends which perhaps you do not know: and in that which you understand in the Scriptures, love lies open; in that which you do not understand, love lies hidden. He therefore holds both what lies open and what lies hidden in the divine words, who holds love in his conduct.

**3.** Wherefore, brothers, pursue love, the sweet and wholesome bond of minds, without which the rich man is poor, and with which the poor man is rich. This endures in adversities, is temperate in prosperity; in hard sufferings strong, in good works cheerful; in temptation most safe, in hospitality most open; among true brothers most glad, among false ones most patient. In Abel it is pleasing through sacrifice, in Noah secure through the flood, in Abraham's pilgrimages most faithful, in Moses most gentle amid injuries, in David's tribulations most meek. In the three children it innocently awaits the gentle fires: in the Maccabees it bravely endures the fierce fires. Chaste in Susanna toward her husband, in Anna after her husband, in Mary apart from a husband. Free in Paul to rebuke, humble in Peter to obey: human in Christians to confess, divine in Christ to forgive. But what greater or more abundant thing can I say of love, than those praises of it which the Lord sounds forth through the mouth of the Apostle, showing the more excellent way and saying: *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become a sounding brass, or a clanging cymbal. And if I have prophecy, and know all mysteries, and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing. And if I give away all my goods, and if I distribute all that is mine to the poor, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing. Love is magnanimous, love is kind. Love is not envious, does not act wrongly, is not puffed up, is not dishonored, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil, does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices together with the truth. It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Love never falls away*[^8]? How great is this? The soul of letters, the power of prophecy, the salvation of mysteries, the firm foundation of knowledge, the fruit of faith, the riches of the poor, the life of the dying. What is so magnanimous as to die for the impious? what so kind as to love enemies? It alone is not weighed down by another's happiness, because it is not envious. It alone is not lifted up by its own happiness, because it is not puffed up. It alone is not pricked by an evil conscience, because it does not act wrongly. Amid reproaches it is secure, amid hatreds it is beneficent; amid angers it is calm, amid snares innocent; amid iniquities groaning, in the truth breathing again. What is stronger than it, not to repay but to disregard injuries? What is more faithful than it, not to vanity but to eternity? For it endures all things in the present life for this reason, that it believes all things about the future life; and it bears all things that are sent here, because it hopes all things that are promised there: deservedly it never falls away. Therefore pursue love, and, thinking of it in holiness, bring forth the fruits of justice. And whatever more abundant than I have been able to say you find in its praises, let it appear in your conduct. For an old man's sermon ought to be not only weighty, but also brief.
