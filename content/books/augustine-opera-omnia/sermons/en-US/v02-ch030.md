# Sermon 42

**1** I, brothers, have small strength, but the word of God has great strength. May it prevail in your hearts. Therefore even what we say slowly you hear greatly, if you obey. As through his cloud, the Lord thundered through the prophet Isaiah. If you have sense, you were terrified. For he spoke plainly, and these things have no need of an expositor but of a doer. *What to me*, he says, *is the multitude of your sacrifices? For who required these from your hands?*[^1] God seeks us, not our things. But the sacrifice of a Christian is alms to the poor. For by this God is made propitious to sins. But unless God is made propitious to sins, who remains but a guilty man? From those offenses and sins, without which this life is not led, men are cleansed by alms. These are of two kinds, by giving out and by remitting; by giving out the good you have, by remitting the evil you suffer. These two kinds of alms the Lord, the good master, who shortened the word upon the earth that it might be fruitful and not burdensome, hear how briefly he comprised: *Remit*, he says, *and it will be remitted to you; give, and it will be given to you*[^2]. *Remit and it will be remitted to you* pertains to forgiving; *Give and it will be given to you* pertains to giving out. From that alms by which you forgive a man you lose nothing. See, he asks pardon at once; you forgave, you lost nothing. You returned home fuller in love. That other kind of alms, where we are bidden to give out to the needy, seems hard, because whatever anyone gives, that very thing which he gives he will not have.

**2** Indeed even here the Apostle makes us secure, who said: *According as each one has, not that there should be relief for others, and distress for you*[^3]. Let each one, then, measure his strength, let him not attend to treasuring up on earth. Let him give, what he gives does not perish. I do not say: This does not perish, but I say: This alone does not perish. But the other things which you do not give and which abound to you, either while you live you lose, or when you die you leave behind. Then, my brothers, whom would so great a promise not exhort? *Remit*, he says, *and it will be remitted to you; give, and it will be given to you*[^4]. When he says, *Give and it will be given to you*, attend to whom he says it, who says it. God says it to a man, the immortal says it to the mortal, so great a head of the household says it to a beggar. For he will not call back from us what we have given. We have found someone to lend to. Let us give at interest—but to God, not to a man. We give to the one who abounds, we give to the one who gave what we give. And for slight things, for frivolous things, for mortal things, for perishable things, for earthly things, he repays eternal, incorruptible things, things abiding without end. What more shall I say? He who promises, promises himself. If you love him, buy him from him. And that you may know that you give to him, hear him saying: *I was hungry and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty and you gave me drink; I was a stranger and you took me in; naked, and you clothed me; sick, you visited me; shut up, you came to me*[^5]. And they will say to him: *When did we see you set in these needs, and ministered to you?*[^6] And he: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^7]. He gives from heaven, he receives on earth. He himself gives, he himself receives. You make, as it were, a transfer-loan. Here you give, there you receive; here you give perishable things, there you receive things that will abide without end.

**3** And whenever you say to God: *Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man*—for thus we have sung—for I know with what groaning you say: *Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man*[^8]. For who in this world does not endure some evil man? When, then, you say this to God: *Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man*, just as you say it with all your heart, so with intent eyes attend first to yourself. *Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man*. Suppose God answered you: "From whom?" You will say, from Gaius, from Lucius, from some person or other whom you endure. And he answers you: "Do you say nothing to me about yourself? If I deliver you from the evil man, you are first to be delivered from yourself. You endure yourself as an evil man; do not endure yourself as an evil man." Let us see whether the other evil man finds anything to do to you. What can the evil man do to you? Do not you be evil. Let not your avarice have dominion over you, let not your concupiscence tread you down, let not your anger trample you. These inner enemies of yours, who are they? You yourself. Let them do nothing to you. Let us see what the evil neighbor, the evil patron, the evil powerful man does to you. Let us see what he does to you. Let him find you just, find you faithful, find you a Christian: what will he do to you? What the Jews did to Stephen. By doing evil they sent him to the good. Therefore when you pray that God may deliver you from the evil man, attend to yourself. Do not spare yourself. Let him deliver you from yourself. How does he deliver you from yourself? By forgiving sins, by granting merits, by giving you strength to fight against your desires, by breathing in virtue, by granting to your mind a heavenly delight by which every earthly delight may be overcome. When God furnishes these things to you, he delivers you from yourself, and secure you await, in the evils of this world that will nonetheless pass away, your Lord coming with those goods that cannot pass away. Let these things be enough for you. You see surely that, somehow, I, weak, approach, and by speaking become strong. So great is my spirit, so great my intent for your advancement. For the worker in the field, hoping for fruit, feels his labor less. You know what my fruits are—that I may be with you, and that we may all be fruits of God. Amen.
