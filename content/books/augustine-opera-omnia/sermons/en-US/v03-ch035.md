# Sermon 73/A: From the Gospel According to Matthew 13: on the Man Who Sowed Good Seed in His Field

**1.** We have heard the holy Gospel, and the Lord Christ speaking in the Gospel; from there let us speak what he himself will grant. This parable, brothers, I would perhaps labor to expound to you; but he has made an abridgment for us, because he himself who set it forth expounded it. He who reads the Gospel reads it as far as where the Lord says: *Gather first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them; but store the wheat in the barn*[^1]. But afterward: *His disciples*, as it is written, *came to him, and said to him: Explain to us the parable of the tares*[^2]. And he who is in the bosom of the Father, he himself narrated it[^3]: *He who sows the good seed*, he says, *is the Son of man*; of himself. *But the field is the world: but the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom; but the tares are the sons of the evil one. But the enemy who sows them is the devil. The harvest is the end of the age; but the reapers are the angels*[^4]. When, then, the Son of man has come, *he will send his angels, and they will gather out of his kingdom all scandals, and will cast them into the furnace of burning fire; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth. Then the just will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^5]. I have spoken the words of the Lord Christ, which were not read, but are so written. So the Lord expounded for us what he set forth. See what we choose to be in his field: see what kind the harvest finds us. For the field, which is the world, is the Church spread through the world. He who is wheat, let him persevere until the harvest; those who are tares, let them be changed into wheat. For this is the difference between men, and true ears of grain, and true tares, that the things which were in the field—what is an ear is an ear; what are tares are tares. But in the field of the Lord, that is, the Church, sometimes what was wheat is turned into tares; and sometimes what were tares are converted into wheat: and no one knows what will be tomorrow. Therefore the laborers, indignant at the householder, when they wished to go and take away the tares, were not permitted; for they wished to take them away, it was not permitted them to separate the tares. They did what they were fit for, they reserved the separations for the angels. And indeed they were unwilling to reserve for the angels the separations of the tares; but the householder, who knew all, and that the separation was to be made, commanded them to bear the tares, not to separate them: *No*, he says—when they had said: *Do you wish that we go and gather them?—no: lest perhaps, when you wish to gather the tares, you root out together also the wheat*[^6]. So, Lord, will the tares too be with us in the barn? *At the time of harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them*[^7]; bear in the field what you will not have with you in the barn.

**2.** Hear, dearest grains of Christ; hear, dearest ears of Christ; hear, dearest wheat of Christ; attend to yourselves, return to your consciences, question your faith, question your love, rouse your conscience; and if you find yourselves wheat, let it come to your mind: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^8]. But whoever, his conscience examined, finds himself among the tares, let him not fear to be changed. It is not yet commanded to reap, it is not yet the harvest; do not be today what you were yesterday, or do not be tomorrow what you are today. What does it profit you that you say you will sometime be changed? God promised pardon to you changed, he did not promise you the morrow. Such as you go out of the body, such you will come to the harvest. Someone died, he was tares; will he there become wheat? Here in the field it is brought about either from tares, wheat, or from wheat, tares; here these things can be; but elsewhere, that is, after this life, is the time of receiving what one did, not of doing what one did not do. But whoever has been as tares, and has wished to separate himself from the field of the Lord Christ, will not be wheat; for he would surely remain wheat. What does the wheat fear from the tares? *Let both grow until the harvest*[^9], the householder says: let them grow together; the reapers do not err, they know whence to make bundles, and to cast into the fire. But of wheat bundles cannot be made and cast into the fire. The bundles show the separations: there Arius has his bundle, there Eunomius has his bundle, there Photinus has his bundle, there Donatus has his bundle, there Manichaeus has his bundle, there Priscillian has his bundle. All these bundles will be cast into the fire; let the wheat be secure, pure, they will rejoice in the barn.

**3.** But where did that enemy not sow tares? What kind, what place of wheat did he find, and not scatter tares? Did he sow among the laity, and not sow among the clergy, or among the bishops? or did he sow among the married, and not sow among those professing chastity? or did he sow among married women, and not sow among consecrated virgins? or did he sow in the houses of the laity, and not sow in the congregations of monks? Everywhere he scattered, everywhere he sowed. What did he leave unmixed? But thanks be to God, since he who will deign to separate does not know how to err. For it is not hidden from your love, that in whatever lofty and exalted harvest tares are found. Even in the profession of chastity tares are found. And you say: In that place too bad men were found, and in that congregation bad men were found; but everywhere bad men were found, but the bad will not always reign with the good. Why do you wonder, that you have found bad men in a holy place? Do you not know that the first sin in paradise was disobedience, and through it the angel fell? did it defile heaven? Adam fell: did he defile paradise? One of the sons of Noah fell: did he defile the house of the just man? Judas fell: did he defile the chorus of the Apostles? But sometimes, according to human judgment, some are thought wheat, and are tares; and some are thought tares, but are truly wheat. On account of these hidden things the Apostle says: *Do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, and enlightens the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart; and then praise will be to each one from God*[^10]. The praise of man passes away; sometimes a man praises the bad, and does not know it; sometimes a man accuses a holy man, and does not know it. May God pardon those who do not know, may he come to the aid of those who labor!
