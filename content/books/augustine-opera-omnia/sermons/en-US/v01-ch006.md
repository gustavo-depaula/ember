# Sermon 5: On the Wrestling of Jacob with the Angel

**1.** This rule of discipline is especially necessary for a Christian man, that he hear the word of God while he is in this world, that he attend to him who came to save the world first through mercy and afterward, with discernment, through judgment. And therefore our Lord Jesus Christ offered himself to us as one to be imitated; so that, since we are Christians, we may either imitate him, or imitate others who have imitated him. For there are some of those who are called Christians and are not, whom the Church has partly cast out like dung, as are all heresies and all schisms, which are also compared to unfruitful branches cut off from the vine, and to the chaff which the wind snatches from the threshing floor before the winnowing. But there are others who are wicked within and are contained in the very Catholic communion, whom the good Christian must necessarily tolerate to the end, because the winnowing of this harvest and threshing floor will not be until the day of judgment. These things we have always sung to you, and in the name of Christ we suppose that they cling in your hearts. These readings that are read to you—are they now read to you for the first time, and are they not the same ones repeated daily? But just as the readings of God need to be repeated daily, lest the evils and thorns of the world spring up in your hearts and choke the seed that has been sown there, so the word of God too ought always to be repeated to you, lest, having forgotten, you say that you have not heard what we say we have said.

**2.** Many approach, and now is the time that in the name of God they should hasten to receive the grace of baptism, believing that all the sins they have previously committed are forgiven them, all of them indeed, and that they go out from it owing nothing to the Lord; just as that servant who was rendering an account to his lord—the lord found that he owed ten thousand talents, yet he departed owing nothing, not because he himself owed nothing, but because the lord was merciful, forgave everything, and released the debtor. And yet, brothers, how vehemently that same servant terrified us! Because he was unwilling to forgive his fellow servant, or to defer the payment of a hundred denarii, his lord laid back on him the ten thousand talents that he had forgiven him. Therefore those who are going to go out from baptism owing nothing and released from all sins, let them beware lest, if anyone sins against them and they are unwilling to forgive him, not only is it thenceforth not forgiven them, but also everything that had been forgiven is laid back upon them. Therefore let no one say, "Who does that, or who has done that?" By saying that to themselves, people die. Love your enemy, says the Lord. And you say, "Who does that?" Because he himself has not done it, he thinks no one could do it. The matter is in the heart. Whence do you see who does it? Do you think that the one who shouts has not forgiven? For perhaps sometimes someone shouts and orders a man to be beaten, and you think he has not forgiven. Why? When you beat your son, do you hold hatred in your heart? Therefore the matter is in the heart. God alone sees whether it has been forgiven. For sometimes someone does not beat, seems to spare with the hand, and rages in the heart and wishes evil to the man within and wants him killed. He holds an evil will against him, although he seems to render him nothing bodily. On the contrary, there are those who seem bodily, as it were, to render evil, but that bodily correction, as it were, is love. He wants the man to come to a good life, and the more he loves him, the more he wants him corrected. So too God. Does he not love us? Does he not exhort us to love our enemies, that we may, as far as we can, be like him? For he said thus: *But you, be perfect, as your Father who is in heaven; who makes his sun rise upon the good and the wicked, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^1]. How great, then, is the love in the Lord, when for sinners and the ungodly he sent Christ to be crucified, and redeemed us by the price of his blood, we who were his enemies by loving the things he made over against him who made them! When, therefore, we were doing these things, *God sent*, as the Apostle says, *his Son*[^2], and handed him over for us, the ungodly, to be killed by other ungodly people. And if so great a gift was given to those not yet faithful, what then is reserved for the faithful? Behold how God loves people. Let us attend, brothers: does he not scourge them? Does he not correct them? If he does not correct them, whence famine, whence sicknesses, whence pestilences and diseases? For all these are corrections of God. If, then, he loves and yet corrects, so you too, if you have someone in your power, although you keep the affection of love, do not nevertheless deny the scourge of correction. For if you deny it, you do not hold love; because that man dies in his sins, who, if perhaps corrected, would abandon them; and a true hatred is the more imputed to you.

**3.** Therefore let no one say, "Who can do that?" Strive to fulfill these things in your hearts. Hold fast, that you may love. Wrestle, and you will conquer. For there Christ conquers. Against what do you wrestle? Against sin you wrestle, against the words of people speaking evil: "So you do not avenge yourself? So you will remain undefended and not show him? Oh, if he had to deal with me!" Wrestle, and conquer. For if Christ had wished to command the earth, when he suffered so much from the Jews, that it should open and swallow his persecutors, could he not? If, then, he who had the power so endured them until he was lifted up on the cross, and, hanging on the cross, said, *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^3], will you, a servant redeemed by the blood of your crucified Lord, not imitate your Savior? For what need was there for him to suffer so much, who was permitted not to suffer? For he said thus: *I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it again. No one takes it from me, but I lay it down and will take it again*[^4]. And so he did. For what happened, brothers? He was hanging on the cross, as we read to the candidates. But when he saw all the Scriptures fulfilled in himself, so that they even gave him vinegar: *It is finished,* he said, *and he bowed his head and gave up the spirit*[^5], as though he had stood for this, that it might be fulfilled. When he willed, then, he laid down his life. And therefore he was God, and those men who were crucified beside him. He dies sooner, they later. And when it was ordered that the bodies be taken down from the cross on account of the sabbath, that they might be buried, they found those thieves living and broke their legs; but the Lord already dead. And yet one of them struck his side with a lance, and there flowed out blood and water. Behold your price. For what flowed from the side, but the sacrament which the faithful receive? *The Spirit, the blood of the Pasch*. The Spirit which he sent forth, and the blood and water which flowed from the side. From that very blood and water the Church is signified as born. And when did the blood and water come out from the side? When Christ was already sleeping on the cross, for Adam too received sleep in Paradise, and so Eve was brought forth from his side. Behold, then, your price. Imitate the humility and the footsteps of your Lord, and do not say, "Who does that?" There is perhaps near you one too who does not do it. But in that crowd, if you do it, it will be reckoned to you as though you find on the threshing floor one grain and chaff without end. With difficulty you find two grains joined together, but among the grains chaff is mixed in. So among those who wish to serve God, there is uproar and a multitude of wicked people surrounding them on every side, because wherever they turn, they find none but evil advisers. Be, then, a grain, and do not be anxious about the chaff. The time will come when it will be separated. Therefore we sang, *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from an unholy nation*[^6]. The Church says this, groaning among sinners. Do you think, brothers, that the Church wishes to be distinguished for this, that it may be separated from the heresies as from branches cut off? It is already separated from them. Does the Church say, *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause*, so that it may be set apart from the party of Donatus, or from the Arians, or from the Manichees? It asks to be distinguished only from those who are mixed with it, whom it must tolerate to the end. But it says this: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause*, that is, lest I be judged together with them and perish on the day of judgment. For now it has been said, *Let the weeds grow*[^7], and now the wicked are tolerated by the good, to be separated on the day of judgment.

**4.** And therefore that Jacob, who was just now read, signifies the Christian people. For he is the younger son; because the Jewish people is Esau. From Jacob indeed the Jewish nation was born, but rather in figure the Jews are understood as Esau, because the elder people was rejected, but the younger people received the primacy. When they were even wrestling in the womb, and the mother suffered distress because her inward parts were being driven about, she said, *Why is this so for me? Better for me was barrenness than that I should suffer this*[^8]. It was said to her by the Lord that two peoples were striving in her womb and two nations, and that the elder would serve the younger. What was said when they were in the womb, this was said again in the blessing of Isaac, when he blessed the younger, but thought he was blessing the elder. Isaac bore the person of the Law. The Law seems to be given to the Jews, and the kingdom itself given to the Christians. Attend, for the Law seems to promise the kingdom. To the Jews it says, *Therefore shall the kingdom be taken away from you and given to a nation doing justice*[^9]. It shall be taken from Esau and given to Jacob. Esau was born shaggy and hairy, that is, full of sins; sins clung to him. But that the other might receive the primacy, he received the goatskins on his arms, and so the father blessed him, feeling his arms and finding them shaggy. But those hairs and sins were borne by Jacob, they did not cling to Jacob. So too, then, the Church of God bears the sins of others, not its own, tolerating them to the end, just as the Lord Jesus Christ too bore the sins of others. And the father blesses the younger. And how did he bless him? A holy mystery—what kind of men they were! For the Scriptures want sharp eyes. He blessed his younger son, and he seems deceived, as though he had blessed one for another. He comes who had gone to the hunt, bringing what the father had commanded, and says, "Father, eat, as you wished." He says to Isaac, *Who are you?* And he, *I am Esau your elder son*. And he says, *So you are Esau? But who, then, is it who brought me food and I ate, and I blessed him, and he is blessed?*[^10] O to be angry at the deceiver! O to be angry at the deceitful one! Rather say: Why did he deceive me? Why did he beguile me? Let his brother take away that blessing, and let him be among the wicked. Does not this matter, done in a mystery, cry out that the elder shall serve the younger? Esau too, then, receives such a blessing. But he added, *You shall be the servant of your brother*[^11]. When the other said, *Are the blessings finished? Bless me also*, he said, *Since I have made him such, what have I to give you?* And he said, *Bless me also, father*[^12]. And he extorted it, and received a nearly similar blessing of the dew of heaven and the fertility of the earth with all abundance. And he added at once, *And you shall serve your brother. And it shall be so for you, when you shall loose his yoke from your neck*[^13]. What is the meaning of what was said, *And it shall be so for you, when you shall loose his yoke from your neck*, except that he shows that those whom Esau prefigured are such sinners that they have it in their power and in their free choice to change themselves and be joined to the brother?

**5.** Attend to the mystery. Behold, the Jew is the servant of the Christian. And this is manifest, and Jacob has filled the whole world, as you see. And that you may know that these things were said of the future, consider the history itself and see that they were not fulfilled in those two men: *The elder shall serve the younger*[^14]. For we read that Esau was much enriched and began to reign in all abundance, but that Jacob went to pasture another's sheep. And when he had begun to return and to fear his brother—it was just now read—he sends gifts, I know not how many, of cattle, and sends also a servant to say, *Behold the gifts of your brother*. He was unwilling to see him before he placated him with gifts, and afterward, the gifts received, he saw him. And when Jacob had come to him, he worshiped him from afar. How, then, *The elder shall serve the younger*, when the younger seems to worship the elder? But these things were therefore not fulfilled in the history, that they might be understood as said of the future Jacob. The younger son received the primacy, and the elder son, the people of the Jews, lost the primacy. Behold, Jacob has filled the earth, has taken hold of both nations and kingdoms. The Roman emperor, now a Christian, ordered that the Jews should not approach Jerusalem itself. And scattered throughout the world, they have become, as it were, the keepers of our books. As servants, when they go into the auditorium of their masters, carry the codices behind them and sit outside, so the elder son has become to the younger son. For sometimes they raise certain points in the Scriptures, and from the codices of the Jews something is known with certainty. Therefore they were scattered, that they might keep our books for us. The elder, then, serves the younger. For see with how great dignity the Christian people is, and in how great a falling away the people of the Jews is. When perhaps they have dared even slightly to stir against the Christians, you have heard what has befallen them in recent times. Therefore it is now true that *the elder shall serve the younger*[^15]. How, then, that blessing, *You shall have of the dew of heaven and of the fertility of the earth*? As he blessed the younger, so he blessed the elder. But to the elder it was said, *And you shall be the servant of your brother. And it shall be so, when you shall loose his yoke from your neck*[^16]. How many are there who have loosed the yoke from their neck and have become our brothers? Attend to how many Jews have believed. And now, if you find a Jew, and evangelize to him the Lord Jesus Christ, and he believes, will he not loose the yoke from his neck? And how many did this in the first times of the faith? Thousands. Indeed those who believed then, as we read, both from servants became brothers and coheirs.

**6.** Therefore the Church which says, *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from an unholy nation*[^17], does not wish to be distinguished from Esau, from whom it is already distinguished, but from wicked Christians. For this Jacob, in whom the Christian people is figured—you have heard how he wrestled with the Lord. For the Lord appeared to him, that is, an angel bearing the person of God, and he wrestled with him and wished to hold and seize him. He wrestled, prevailed, and held him. When he held him, he did not let him go unless blessed. May the Lord grant, brothers, to unfold so great a mystery. He wrestles, prevails, and wishes to be blessed by him over whom he prevailed. What, then, is the meaning that he wrestled and wishes to hold him? The Lord says in the Gospel, *The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and those who do violence seize it*[^18]. This is what we were saying a while ago: Wrestle, that you may hold Christ, that you may love your enemy. For you hold Christ here, if you have loved your enemy. And what does the Lord himself, that is, the angel in the person of the Lord, say, when he was prevailing and holding him? He touched the breadth of his thigh, and it withered, and therefore Jacob limped. It is said, "Let me go, for it is now morning." And he, "I will not let you go unless you bless me." And he blessed him. How? By changing his name: "You shall not be called Jacob, but Israel: because you have prevailed with God, you shall prevail also with men." This is the blessing. See one man: in part he is touched and withers, and in part he is blessed. The very same one man in part withered and limps, and in part is blessed that he may flourish.

**7.** But what is the meaning—as much as the Lord suggests we say, without prejudice to a better understanding—what is, *Behold, morning has now come, let me go*? This is what the Lord says after the passion to the woman who wished to hold his feet, *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^19]. What is this? For when that very reading was read, I treated of it once, how it was said, *Do not touch me, I have not yet ascended to the Father*. Why? Did no one touch him bodily, unless he had ascended to the Father? He was still here, the disciple who did not believe felt his scars. How, then, was he unwilling to be touched, except that this was said figuratively? That woman was the Church. And this is it: *Do not touch me*, do not touch me in a fleshly way, but as I am equal to the Father. But as long as you do not understand me as equal to the Father, *do not touch me*, because you touch not me, but my flesh. For Paul says, in the elevation of his progress, *And if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him so no longer*. And, *The old things have passed away, behold they have become new; but all things are from God*[^20]. What is, *And if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him so no longer*? Because when we knew him in a fleshly way, we thought him only to be a man. But truly after his grace shone upon us, we understood the Word equal to the Father. He was holding, then, and wrestling, as though wishing to embrace Jacob in his fleshly garb. But he was saying, *Let me go*, in a fleshly way, *for behold, it is now morning*, that you may be spiritually enlightened: that is, Do not think me a man. *Let me go, for it is now morning*. In the morning, in the light of truth and of wisdom through which all things were made, we understood. You will enjoy it fully when this night has passed, that is, the iniquity of this world. For then morning comes, when the Lord shall come, that he may be seen by us as he is seen by the angels. Because *now we see through a mirror in an enigma, but then face to face*[^21]. Let us hold, then, brothers, what was said, *Let me go. Behold, it is now morning*. But what did the other say? *I will not let you go unless you bless me*. Because through the flesh the Lord first blesses us. The faithful know what they receive, because they are blessed through the flesh. And they know that they would not be blessed unless that flesh, crucified, were given for the life of the world. But how is he blessed? Because he prevailed with God, because he held firmly and persevered and did not lose from his hands what Adam lost. Let us therefore, the faithful, hold what we receive, that we may deserve to be blessed.

**8.** The withered part of Jacob signifies wicked Christians, so that in Jacob himself there is both blessing and limping. He is blessed in respect of those living well, he limps in respect of those living badly. But still in one man there is both. There will be a separation and distinction afterward. This the Church desires in the psalm, saying, *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from an unholy nation*[^22]. Just so indeed, because the Gospel says, *If your foot scandalizes you, cut it off and cast it from you. For it is better for you to enter the kingdom of God having one foot, than with two feet to go into the eternal fire*[^23]. These wicked ones, then, are cut off in the end. Now the Church is lame. It sets one foot firmly, the other it has weak. Attend to the pagans, brothers. They sometimes find good Christians serving God, and they marvel and are drawn and believe. Sometimes they attend to those living badly, and they say, "Behold the Christians." But these who live badly belong to the breadth of Jacob's thigh that was touched, which withered. But the touch of the Lord's hand is the Lord's hand correcting and giving life. Therefore in part he is blessed, and in part he withers. And the Lord shows these who live badly in the Church, because thence comes what is written in the Gospel, that when the blade had grown the weeds appeared; because when people have begun to make progress, then they begin to perceive the wicked. These things are known to you; by the gift of God it comes about that they are recognized. But for now the weeds are to be tolerated until the end of the harvest, lest perhaps in rooting up the weeds the wheat too be rooted up at the same time. But the time will come when the Church shall be heard saying, *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from an unholy nation*[^24], when the Lord shall come in his glory with his holy angels, and all nations shall be gathered before him, and he shall separate them, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats, and the just shall be placed on the right, but the goats on the left. And to those it shall be said, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*; but to those, *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^25].
