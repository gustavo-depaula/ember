# Sermon 349: On Love, and on the Blind Man Who Was Given Sight

**1.** A little while ago the Apostle was speaking to us about love, when his Letter was being read: and he so commended it to us that we might understand that all other things, however great gifts of God, profit nothing without it. But where it is, it cannot be alone. Let us too, then, render to your love a sermon about love. There is one love that is divine, another that is human: of the human, one is lawful, another unlawful. Concerning these three loves, then—for it has two names among the Latins, what in Greek is called agape—I will say what the Lord grants. This, then, is my first division, what I said, that one love is human, another divine: and the human itself I divided into two, that one is lawful, another unlawful. First, then, I speak of the lawful human love, which is not reproved: then of the unlawful human love, which is condemned: third, of the divine, which leads us to the kingdom.

**2.** To convey it briefly, then, lawful is the human love by which a wife is loved; unlawful, that by which a harlot or another's wife is loved. For in the forum and the streets the more lawful love is loved than a harlot: in the house of God, in the temple of God, in the city of Christ, in the body of Christ, even the love of a harlot leads the lover to hell. Have, then, lawful love: it is human; but, as I said, it is lawful. And it is not only so lawful that it is permitted: but so lawful that, if it is lacking, it is reproved. Let it be lawful for you with human love to love your spouses, to love your children, to love your friends, to love your fellow citizens. For all these names have the bond of relationship, and a certain glue, as it were, of love. But you see that this love can belong even to the impious, that is, to pagans, Jews, heretics. For who of them does not love wife, children, brothers, neighbors, kinsfolk, friends, and so on? This, then, is human. If, then, anyone is carried away by such cruelty that he loses even the human affection of love, and does not love his children, and does not love his spouse, he is not even to be numbered among men. For he who loves his children is not to be praised; but he who does not love his children is to be condemned. For let him still see with whom he ought to have this love in common. Even wild beasts love their offspring: asps love their offspring, tigers love their offspring, lions love their offspring. For there is no beast that does not gently murmur to its offspring. For though it terrifies men, it cherishes its little ones. The lion roars in the woods, so that no one may pass: it enters the cave where it has its offspring, it lays aside all the rage of its ferocity. It puts it outside, when it itself does not enter. Therefore he who does not love his children is worse even than a lion. These things are human, and they are lawful.

**3.** Beware of unlawful love. You are members of Christ, and you are the body of Christ. Hear the Apostle, and be terrified. For he could not speak more gravely, he could not more vehemently, he could not more sharply deter Christians from the love of fornications, than where he said: *Shall I, then, taking the members of Christ, make them the members of a harlot?* But that he might say this, above he says: *Do you not know that he who clings to a harlot becomes one body with her?* And he gave a testimony from Scripture, that it is written: *They shall be two in one flesh*[^1]. For this was said by God: but of husband and wife, where it is lawful, where it is conceded, where it is honorable; not where it is base, not where it is unlawful, not where it is by every reason condemnable. But as one flesh is made in the lawful mingling of husband and wife: so one flesh is made in the unlawful mingling of harlot and lover. Since, then, one flesh is made, let that terrify you, let that make you shudder, which he added: *Shall I, then, taking the members of Christ*. Attend to the members of Christ, Christian: do not attend to the members of Christ in another, attend to the members of Christ in yourself, you who were bought by the blood of Christ. *Shall I, then, taking the members of Christ, make them the members of a harlot?* He who does not shudder at this, is an object of shudder to God.

**4.** Altogether, altogether I beseech you, my brothers: behold, let us suppose what is not the case, that God promised impunity to such people, and said: Those who do such things, I will have mercy on them, I will not condemn them. Let us make God to have said this. Even with impunity promised, does anyone take the members of Christ, and now make them the members of a harlot? He does not, if the third, divine love is there. For I mentioned three loves: I promised to speak, as the Lord would grant, about the three; about the lawful human, the unlawful human, and that excellent and divine one. Let us question the divine love, and set before it the two human loves, and say to it: Behold the lawful human love, by which a wife is loved, and daughters and other worldly relationships: behold another, unlawful, by which a harlot is loved, by which another's maidservant is loved, by which another's daughter, unsought, unpromised, is loved, by which another's wife is loved. Two loves are before you; with which of these do you wish to remain? He who chooses to remain with that lawful human love does not remain with that unlawful human love. Let no one say to himself: I have both. If you have both, by admitting to yourself the love of a harlot, you do injury, as to a matron who dwells there, to the divine love. For I think that, if you are a married man, and love a harlot, you do not send the harlot into your house, to dwell with your matron. You do not go so far as that. You seek darkness, you seek hiding-places, you do not profess your baseness. But even those who do not have wives, and are as it were more freely lovers of harlots (therefore I said: as it were, because they too are condemned, if they are now believers); I think that even a young man not yet having a wife, if he loves a harlot, does not make her dwell with his sister, does not make her dwell with his mother, lest he do injury to human modesty, lest he offend the dignity of his own blood. If, then, you do not make the harlot whom you love dwell with your mother, with your sister, lest, as I said, you offend the dignity of your own blood; do you make the love of a harlot dwell in your heart with the love of God, and offend the dignity of the blood of Christ?

**5.** Love God, you find nothing better. You love silver, because it is better than iron and bronze: you love gold more, because it is better than silver: you love precious stones more, because they surpass even the value of gold: you love, finally, this light, which everyone who fears death dreads to lose; you love, I say, this light, just as he desired it with a certain immense love, who cried out after Jesus: *Have mercy on me, Son of David*[^2]. The blind man cried out as Christ was passing by. For he feared lest he pass by and not heal him. And how much did he cry out? So that, with the crowd forbidding, he did not keep silent. He overcame the one who contradicted, he held the Savior. With the crowds clamoring against him and forbidding him to cry out, Jesus stood, called him, and said to him: *What do you wish to be done for you? Lord*, he said, *that I may see. Look up, your faith has made you whole*[^3]. Love Christ; desire the light, which is Christ. If he desired the light of the body, how much more ought you to desire the light of the heart? Let us cry out to him, not with our voices, but with our conduct. Let us live well, let us despise the world: let everything that passes be nothing to us. They will reproach us when we so live, as men worldly who love us, lovers of the earth, savoring dust, drawing nothing from heaven, snatching at free breezes with the nostrils rather than the heart: they will reproach us without doubt, and will say, if they see us despising these human, these earthly things: What is wrong with you? Why are you mad? That is the crowd contradicting, lest the blind man cry out. And there are some Christians who forbid living in a Christian way: because that crowd too was walking with Christ, and was forbidding the man crying out to Christ and desiring the light from the benefit of Christ himself. There are such Christians: but let us overcome them, let us live well; and let our very life be our voice to Christ. He will stand: because he stands.

**6.** For there too is a great mystery. He was passing by when that man was crying out: when he healed him, he stood. Let the passing-by of Christ make us intent on crying out. What is the passing-by of Christ? Whatever he endured for us temporally is his passing-by. He was born, he passed: is he still being born? He grew, he passed: is he still growing? He nursed: is he still nursing? Wearied, he slept: is he still sleeping? He ate and drank: is he still doing so? Finally, he was seized, he was bound, he was scourged, he was crowned with thorns, he was struck with slaps, smeared with spittle, hung on the wood, killed, pierced with a lance, buried, he rose again: still he passes. He ascended into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father: he has stood still. Cry out as much as you can: now he enlightens you. For in that very thing, that *the Word was with God*, he surely stood, because he was not changed. *And the Word was God*: *and the Word was made flesh*. The flesh through its passing-by did many things, and suffered: the Word stood still. By the Word itself the heart is enlightened; because by the Word itself the flesh which it took up is honored. Take away the Word, what is the flesh? It is the same as yours. But that the flesh of Christ might be honored, *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^4]. Let us cry out, then, and live well.

**7.** Love your children, love your wives, even if in a worldly way. For you ought to love according to Christ, so that you may take counsel for them according to God, and love nothing in them but Christ, and hate in your own people if they refuse to have Christ. For that is the divine love. For what will your transitory and mortal love profit them? Yet when you love even in a human way, love Christ more. I do not say that you should not love your wife; but love Christ more. I do not say that you should not love your father, I do not say that you should not love your children; but love Christ more. Hear him saying it, lest you think these words are mine: *Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me*[^5]. When you hear, *is not worthy of me*, are you not afraid? He of whom Christ says, *is not worthy of me*, is not with him: he who will not be with him, where will he be? If you do not love to be with him, fear to be without him. Why fear to be without him? Because you will be with the devil, if you are not with Christ. And where will the devil be? Hear Christ himself: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^6]. If you are not kindled by the fire of heaven, fear the fire of the hells. If you do not love to be among the angels of God, fear to be among the angels of the devil. If you do not love to be in the kingdom, fear to be in the furnace of burning, inextinguishable, everlasting fire. Let fear first conquer in you, and there will be love. Let fear be a tutor, let it not itself remain in you, but lead you to love, as to a teacher.

[^1]: 1 Cor 6:15-16
[^2]: See Mark 10:47
[^3]: See Mark 10:51-52; Luke 18:42
[^4]: John 1:14
[^5]: Matt 10:37
[^6]: Matt 25:41
