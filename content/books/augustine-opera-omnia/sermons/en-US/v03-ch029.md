# Sermon 70/A: Likewise from the Same Chapter of the Gospel, on Humility

**1.** The tongue of the Lord, the trumpet of justice and truth, standing as it were in the assembly of the human race, calls and says: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me that I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is sweet, and my burden is light*[^1]. Let him who does not labor not hear; but let him who labors hear: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened.* Let him who is not burdened not hear; but let him who is burdened hear: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened.* To what end? *And I will refresh you.* Everyone who labors and is burdened seeks refreshment, desires rest. And who does not labor in this world? Let it be told me, who does not labor either by working or by thinking? The poor man labors in works, the rich man labors in thoughts; the poor man wishes to have what he does not have, and labors; the rich man, by fearing to lose what he has, and wishing to increase what he does not have, labors more. But all carry their burdens, whatever sins, by which the proud neck is pressed; and yet under so great a mass it raises itself; and when it is pressed by sins, pride swells. Therefore the Lord says—what? *I*, he says, *will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me.* What, Lord, do we learn from you? We know that in the beginning you are the Word, and the Word with God, and the Word God; we know that all things were made through you, visible and invisible. What do we learn from you? To suspend heaven, to make firm the earth, to spread out the sea, to expand the air, to fill all the elements with suitable animals, to order the ages, to roll round the times? What do we learn from you? Or perhaps you wish us to learn the things you worked on earth? do you wish to teach these? Do we, then, learn from you to cleanse lepers, to drive out demons, to put fevers to flight, to command the sea and the waves, to raise the dead? Not these either, he says. Tell, then, what? *That I am meek and humble of heart.* Blush before God, human pride. The Word of God says it, God says it, the Only-begotten says it, the Most High says it: *Learn from me that I am meek and humble of heart.* So great a height descended to humility, and does man stretch himself out? Gather yourself, and bring yourself back, O man, to the humble Christ, lest in your stretching you burst.

**2.** Just now a psalm was sung, just now Alleluia was said: *Who is like the Lord our God, who dwells on high, and looks upon the humble?*[^2] Looking upon you, may he find you humble, lest he condemn you. He himself said it, he himself proclaimed it, he himself called the human race to this salvation: *Learn from me*, he says, not to create a creature; learn *that I am meek and humble of heart. In the beginning it was:* what loftier? *The Word was made flesh*[^3]: what humbler? He commands the world: what loftier? He hangs on the wood: what humbler? When he did these things for your sake, why are you still raised up, still swollen, an inflated bellows? God is humble, and you are proud? Perhaps, because he said: *The Lord is high, and looks upon the humble*, you say: "He does not look upon me." What more unhappy than you, if he does not look upon, but looks down upon you? Looking-upon has compassion, looking-down has contempt. But perhaps, because the Lord looks upon the humble, you think you lie hidden, because you are not humble, you are high, you are proud. You do not lie hidden here from the eyes of God. For see what he says there. *The Lord is high.* High, surely. How do you reach him? do you seek a ladder? Seek the wood of humility, and you have reached him. *The Lord is high, he looks upon the humble: but the lofty* (lest you think you lie hidden, you who are proud) *the lofty he knows from afar*[^4]. He knows them, but from afar. *Salvation is far from sinners*[^5]. The humble, what? from near. A wondrous device of the Almighty! He is high, and looks upon the humble from near; the proud are high, the high one knows them from afar. *The Lord is near to those who have crushed their heart, and the humble of spirit he will save*[^6]. So, brothers, let not pride swell in you, but rot: shudder at it, cast it away. Christ seeks a humble Christian. Christ in heaven, Christ with us, Christ in hell, not to be held back, but to loose. We have such a leader. He sits at the right hand of the Father, but he gathers us from the earth; that one thus, but this one thus: by furnishing to that one, by chastising this one; by gladdening that one, by troubling this one. Let him gather, who gathers; let him gather, lest we perish; let him gather there, where we do not perish: into that land of the living, where merits are recognized, and justice is crowned.

[^1]: Matt 11:28-30
[^2]: Ps 113:5-6 (112:5-6)
[^3]: John 1:1,14
[^4]: Ps 138:6 (137:6)
[^5]: Ps 119:155 (118:155)
[^6]: Ps 34:18 (33:19)
