# Sermon 193: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1.** When the Gospel was read, we heard the voice of the angels by which the Lord Jesus Christ, born of the Virgin's childbearing, was announced to the shepherds: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^1]. A festal voice and one of rejoicing, not for one woman, whose womb had brought forth offspring, but for the human race, for which a Virgin had borne the Savior. For it was fitting, and this was altogether seemly, that for her who had given birth, who had brought forth the Lord of heaven and earth and had remained intact after the offspring was brought forth, not little women with human ceremonies, but the angel with divine praises, should celebrate the birth-rites. Let us then say, and let us say it with as much exultation as we can, who announce him born not to shepherds of cattle, but celebrate his birthday with his sheep: let us say, I say, we too, with faithful heart and devout voice: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*. And these divine words, these praises of God, this angelic joy, examined with as much attention as we are able, let us meditate on with faith and hope and love. For just as we believe and hope and desire, so we too shall be glory to God in the highest, when, our body rising spiritual, we shall be *caught up in the clouds to meet Christ*[^2]: if only, while we are on earth, we pursue peace with good will. For in the highest is life, because there is the region of the living; and there are good days, where the Lord himself is the same, and *his years do not fail*[^3]. But whoever *wants life and loves to see good days, let him keep his tongue from evil, and let his lips not speak deceit; let him turn away from evil and do good*[^4]: and so let him be a man of good will. And *let him seek peace and pursue it*[^5]: because *on earth peace to men of good will*.

**2.** But if you say, O man, Behold, the will is present with me, but to perform the good I do not find; and you delight in the law of God according to the inner man, but you see another law in your members at war with the law of your mind, and leading you captive in the law of sin which is in your members[^6]: persist in good will, and cry out what follows: *Wretched man that I am, who will deliver me from this body of death? The grace of God, through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^7]. For he himself is peace on earth to men of good will, after the war in which *the flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh, so that you may not do the things that you would*[^8]: for *he himself is our peace, who made the two one*[^9]. Let good will, then, persist against evil desires, and persisting, let it implore the help of the grace of God, through Jesus Christ our Lord. It is opposed by the law of the carnal members, and behold, now it is even taken captive: let it implore help, let it not trust in its own strength; and, at least wearied, let it not disdain to be one who confesses. For he will be present who said to those whom he already saw believing in him: *If you continue in my word, you will truly be my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*[^10]. The truth will be present and will free us from this body of death. For on this account Truth, whose birthday we celebrate, *sprang up from the earth*[^11], that there might be peace on earth to men of good will. For who is fit to will and to be able, unless he aids us by inspiration that we may be able, who by calling granted that we should will? For everywhere his mercy goes before us, that we who were unwilling might be called, and that we might obtain the power to do what we will. Let us say to him, then: *I have sworn and resolved to keep the judgments of your righteousness*[^12]. I have resolved indeed, and because you commanded, I have promised obedience: but since I see *another law in my members at war with the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin which is in my members*[^13]; *I am humbled exceedingly, Lord, give me life according to your word*[^14]. Behold, *the will is present with me*[^15]: therefore *accept the freewill offerings of my mouth, Lord*[^16]; that there may be peace on earth to men of good will. Let us say these things, and whatever else piety, instructed by the holy readings, suggests: that we may not vainly throng the festival of the Lord born of the Virgin, begun in good will, to be perfected in fullest love; which is also *poured out in our hearts, not through ourselves, but through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^17].
