# Sermon 282: On the Heavenly Birthday of the Martyrs Perpetua and Felicity

**1.** Today we celebrate the feast of two holy martyrs. They not only distinguished themselves by outstanding virtues in their suffering, but also, through their own names, signified the reward for so great a labor of devotion, both their own and that of their other companions. Perpetua and Felicity are the names of two, but perpetual felicity is the reward of all. For all the martyrs would not bravely undergo the temporary toil of their struggle in suffering and confession unless they were to rejoice in perpetual felicity. Under the guidance of divine providence, therefore, these women were destined to be not only martyrs but also the closest of companions, as in fact they were, so that they might mark a single day of their glory and hand down to posterity a common feast to celebrate. For just as the example of their most glorious struggle urges us to imitate them, so their names bear witness that we will receive an inseparable gift. Let each hold the other; let each be bound to the other. We do not hope for either without the other. Perpetuity is of no use if there is no felicity, and felicity abandons us if it is not perpetual. Let these few words, suited to the time available, suffice concerning the names of the martyrs to whom this day is consecrated.

**2.** As for the women who bore these names, we heard when the account of their martyrdom was read, and know from the record handed down to us, that these women of such great virtues and merits were not only female but also married women. One of them was also a mother, so that an affection less able to endure suffering was added to the weakness of her sex. Thus the enemy, assailing them in every way as though they were unable to bear the harsh and cruel burdens of persecution, thought they would immediately yield to him and soon become his. But through the most watchful and courageous strength of their inner selves, they frustrated all his plots and broke his attacks.

**3.** In this company of outstanding glory there were also male martyrs; on that very day, exceedingly brave men also conquered through their suffering. Yet they did not lend their names to the day. This did not happen because the women surpassed the men in moral worth, but because feminine weakness defeated the ancient enemy with a greater miracle, and manly courage fought for perpetual felicity.
