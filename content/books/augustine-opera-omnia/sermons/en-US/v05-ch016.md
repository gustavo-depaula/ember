# Sermon 160

**1.** We have been admonished by the Apostle that *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^1]; and to that Lord we have sung, *In your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me*[^2]. This, then, is to glory in the Lord, to glory not in one's own, but in his righteousness. But this righteousness lay hidden from those who glory in their own righteousness. And this vice appeared most of all in the Jews, refusing the New Testament and remaining in the old man. In vain and unfruitfully had they read and sung in their codices, *In your righteousness rescue me. For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not submitted to the righteousness of God*[^3]. Let no one, then, glory as if in his own righteousness, even if he is righteous. For to the one glorying in his own righteousness it is said, *For what do you have that you have not received*[^4]? Therefore *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*. For what is more secure than to glory in him of whom no one at all can be put to shame? For if you glory in a man, something can be found in a man—rather, many things can be found in a man—over which the one who glories in him may be put to shame. But when you hear that one must not glory in man, surely not in yourself either: for you are not a non-man. If, then, you glory in yourself, you glory in a man; and this is more foolish and more execrable. For if you glory in some righteous man or other wise man, that one in whom you glory does not glory in himself; but if you glory in yourself, you are not wise, not righteous; but if one ought not to glory in a wise man, much less ought one to glory in a foolish one. But he who glories in himself glories in a foolish man. For by this very thing he is convicted as foolish, because he glories in himself. Therefore, *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*. Nothing is safer, nothing more secure. If you can, you have something to lay hold of; glorying in the Lord you will not be put to shame. For nothing of reproach can be found in him in whom you glory. And through this, he too who did not say, In my righteousness rescue me, but, *In your righteousness rescue me*, first said this: *In you I have hoped, O Lord; let me not be put to shame forever*[^5].

**2.** For is there anything else in which the Jews erred, or by what other vice were they made exiles from gospel grace, except that one of which the Apostle was not silent, which I mentioned a little before? *I bear witness*, he says, *to them, that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*[^6]. Where he praised, he also reproached. Whence, then, are they faulty? Because, although they have a zeal for God, it is not according to knowledge. And as though we should consult the Apostle and say: What do you mean by what you said, *Not according to knowledge*? What is this knowledge which they do not have, who nevertheless *have a zeal for God*? What knowledge do they not have—do you wish to hear? Attend to what follows: *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not submitted to the righteousness of God*[^7]. If, then, you have a zeal for God, and wish to have it according to knowledge, and to belong to the New Testament, to which the Jews could not belong for this reason, because they had a zeal for God not according to knowledge; acknowledge the righteousness of God, and do not wish to establish this very righteousness as your own, if you have any; if you live well, if you do the precepts of God, do not think it your own; for this is to wish to establish one's own righteousness. Acknowledge from whom you received it, and you have what you received. For you do not have what you have not received. *But if you have received it, why do you glory as if you had not received it*[^8]? For when you glory as if you had not received it, you glory in yourself; and where is, *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^9]? Hold the gift, but acknowledge the giver. When the Lord promised that he would give his Spirit, *If anyone thirsts*, he said, *let him come to me, and drink. He who believes in me, rivers of living water will flow from his belly*[^10]. Whence is this river in you? Recall your former dryness. For unless you had been dry, you would not have thirsted; if you had not thirsted, you would not have drunk. What is, If you had not thirsted, you would not have drunk? Unless you had found yourself empty, you would not have believed in Christ. Before he said, *Rivers of living water will flow from his belly*, he first said, *If anyone thirsts, let him come and drink*. Therefore you will have a river of living water, because you drink; you do not drink, if you do not thirst; but if you were thirsting, why did you wish to glory as if from your own river? Therefore, *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*.

**3.** *And I*, he says, *brothers, coming to you, came not in loftiness of word or wisdom, announcing to you the mystery of God*. He also says, *Did I say that I knew anything among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified*[^11]? And if he knew this alone, there is nothing he did not know. It is a great thing to know Christ crucified; but before the eyes of little ones he set as it were a wrapped-up treasure. *Christ*, he says, *crucified*. How much does this treasure have within? Then in another place, when he feared for certain people, lest by philosophy and empty deceit they be seduced from Christ, he promised the treasure of the knowledge and wisdom of God in Christ. *Beware*, he says, *lest anyone seduce you by philosophy and empty seduction, according to the elements of the world, not according to Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge*[^12]. Christ crucified, the hidden treasures of wisdom and knowledge. Do not, then, he says, be deceived by the name of wisdom. To this wrapping call yourselves; pray that this may be unwrapped for you. Foolish philosopher of this world, what you seek is nothing; what you do not seek is something. What does it profit that you thirst much, and trampling pass by the fountain? You despise the humility, because you do not understand the majesty. *For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^13]. *Jesus Christ*, he says, *crucified. I did not say that I knew anything among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified*; his humility, which the proud deride, that there may be fulfilled in them, *You have rebuked the proud; for cursed are those who turn aside from your commandments*[^14]. And what is his commandment, but that we believe in him, and love one another? In whom should we believe? In Christ crucified. What pride does not wish to hear, let wisdom hear. His commandment is that we believe in him. In whom? In Christ crucified. This is his commandment, that we believe in Christ crucified. This entirely; but that proud one, with stiff neck, swollen throat, exalted tongue, puffed cheeks, derides Christ crucified. *Cursed*, then, *are those who turn aside from your commandments*. Why do they deride, but because they see a cheap little garment wrapped around the outside, and do not see the treasure lying hidden within? He sees the flesh, sees the man, sees the cross, sees the death, and despises these things. Wait, do not pass by, do not scorn, do not insult. Wait, search; perhaps there is something within that will greatly delight you. If you should find *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man*[^15]. The eye sees the flesh; there is beneath the flesh what the eye does not see. Your ear hears the voice; there is there what the ear has not heard. There has ascended into your heart, as it were from earthly thoughts, a man crucified and dead; there is there what has not ascended into the heart of man. For accustomed thoughts ascend into our heart: *It ascended*, it says, *into the heart of Moses to visit his brothers*[^16]; this is the human condition. And when the disciples doubted about the Lord himself, and said among themselves, when they suddenly saw him risen: It is he, it is not; it is flesh, it is a spirit; he says this to them, *Why do thoughts ascend into your heart*[^17]?

**4.** Let us seek, then, if we can, not what can ascend into our heart, but where our heart may deserve to ascend. For he will deserve to be glorified in the one who reigns, who has learned to glory in the one who was crucified. Hence the Apostle himself, seeing not only where to ascend, but also by what way to ascend—for many have seen where, and have not seen by what way; they have loved the homeland of the heights, but have been ignorant of the way of humility—the Apostle, then, knowing, and thinking, and premeditating, not only where, but also by what way: *Far be it from me*, he says, *to glory, except in the cross of the Lord Jesus Christ*[^18]. He could have said, In the wisdom of our Lord Jesus Christ; and he would say the truth; he could have said, In the majesty; and he would say the truth; he could have said, In the power; and he would say the truth; but he said, *in the cross*. Where the philosopher of the world blushed, there the Apostle found the treasure; not by despising the cheap wrapping, he came to the precious thing wrapped within. *Far be it from me*, he says, *to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*. You have taken up a good burden, there is the whole of what you sought; and you have shown what great thing lay hidden there. What kind of help? *Through whom*, he says, *the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^19]. When would the world be crucified to you, unless for you were crucified the one through whom the world was made? Therefore, *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^20]. In what Lord? In Christ crucified. Where there is humility, there is majesty; where there is infirmity, there is power; where there is death, there is life. If you wish to attain to those things, do not despise these.

**5.** You heard in the Gospel about the sons of Zebedee. They sought the heights, asking that one of them sit at the right hand of so great a head of the household, the other at the left. They were indeed seeking a great loftiness, great; but because they put after it the "by what way," Christ calls them back from where they wished to go to that by which they ought to go. For to those seeking so great a loftiness, what did he answer? *Can you drink the chalice that I am going to drink*[^21]? What chalice, but of humility, but of suffering? Which he was going to drink, and, transforming our infirmity into himself, he says to the Father, *Father, if it can be, let this chalice pass from me*[^22]. Transforming into himself those very ones who refused to drink such a chalice, and sought the heights, neglecting the way of humility: *Can you drink*, he says, *the chalice that I am going to drink*? You seek Christ on high; return to the crucified. You wish to reign and to glory in the seats of Christ; first learn to say, *Far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^23]. This is the Christian teaching, the precept of humility, the commendation of humility, that we glory in nothing but the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ. For it is no great thing to glory in the wisdom of Christ; it is a great thing to glory in the cross of Christ; whence the ungodly man insults you, thence let the devout man glory; whence the proud insults, thence let the Christian glory. Do not blush at the cross of Christ; therefore you have received that very sign on your forehead, as it were on the seat of shame. Recall your forehead, that you may not dread another's tongue.

**6.** The sign of the Old Testament is circumcision in the hidden flesh, the sign of the New Testament is the cross on the free forehead. For there is concealment, here revelation; that is under a veil, this in the face. *For as long as Moses is read, a veil is placed over their heart*. Why? Because they have not passed over to Christ. *For when you have passed over to Christ, the veil will be taken away*[^24]; so that you who had circumcision in the hidden place may carry the cross on the forehead: *But we, with unveiled face beholding the glory of the Lord, are transformed into the same image*, he says, *from glory to glory, as by the Spirit of the Lord*[^25]. Do not ascribe this to yourself, do not think it yours, lest, being ignorant of the righteousness of God and wishing to establish your own, you not be subject to the righteousness of God. Pass over, then, to Christ, O you who glory in circumcision. For you wish to have glory in what you are ashamed to show. It is a sign, it is true, it was commanded by God; but it is a sign of concealment. For the New Testament was veiled in the Old; the Old Testament is revealed in the New. Therefore let the sign pass from the hidden to the manifest, and let that begin to be on the forehead which lay hidden under the garment. For that Christ was foretold in that sign, who can doubt? Hence the knife of stone: but the rock was Christ[^26]. Hence the eighth day of circumcision, and the Lord's day of resurrection. Therefore the Apostle, passing over from there, coming from there—passing over, that is, to Christ, that the veil might be taken away—knew whence to glory. *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*. For what had he said before? *For neither do those who are circumcised keep the law; but they wish you to be circumcised, that they may glory in your flesh*[^27]. What of you, Apostle? Transfer the sign to the forehead. *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*. Here I have, he says, what I was ignorant of. The New Testament came, what was concealed was revealed. Those who sat in the shadow of death[^28], light has risen for them. What was concealed was revealed to them; what lay hidden is in the open. The Rock himself came, circumcised us all in spirit, and fixed the sign of his humility on the forehead of the redeemed.

**7.** Now let glorying be in the cross of Christ; let us not be ashamed of the humility of the Most High. How long the distinction of foods, and the circumcision of the flesh? Whose god is the belly, and whose glory is in their shame[^29]. To them future things were foretold; now let them be believed as done. Let us not be ungrateful to him who came, if we waited for him to come. But whence are the Jews exiles, aliens, fugitives from this grace? *Because they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*. What knowledge? *Being ignorant*, he says, *of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own*[^30]; not holding God except in precepts, and supposing that by their own strength they could fulfill the precepts, they avoided the help. *For Christ is the end of the law*—the perfection of the law is Christ—*unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^31]. And what does Christ do? He justifies the ungodly. For by believing in him who justifies the ungodly—not the godly, but the ungodly; making godly the one he finds ungodly: *to the one*, then, *who believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned unto righteousness. For if Abraham was justified by works*, as though he himself did it, as though he himself bestowed this on himself, *he has glory, but not before God*[^32]. But *he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*; and let him say securely, *In your righteousness rescue me, and deliver me*. For he rescues and delivers those who hope in him; who do not ascribe to their own strength what they have received. *For this very thing is of wisdom, to know whose gift it is*[^33]. Who said this? He who asked God to give him continence. What righteousness, what particle of righteousness can be fulfilled without some continence? For it is a delight to sin; for if it were no delight, it would not be done. But righteousness delights less, or does not delight, or delights less than is fitting. Whence this, but from the sicknesses of the soul? Bread is loathed, and poison delights. Whence will this sickness be healed, I beseech you? Is it from ourselves and through ourselves? We who were all fit to wound ourselves, which of us is fit to cure what he did? So too in sins themselves, who does not wound himself when he wishes? But not everyone, when he wishes, heals himself. Let the soul, then, be devout, let it be faithfully Christian, let it not be ungrateful to grace. Let the physician be acknowledged: the sick man never heals himself.
