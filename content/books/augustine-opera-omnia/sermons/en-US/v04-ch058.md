# Sermon 136c

**1.** The works of our Lord Jesus Christ which he then did in bodies, he now does in hearts, although in many bodies too he does not cease at all to do them, but greater is what he does in hearts. For if it is great to see the light of heaven, how much greater is it to see the light of God! For to this the eyes of the heart are healed, to this they are opened, to this they are cleansed, that they may see the light, which is God. For *God is light*, says Scripture, *and there are no darknesses in him* [^1], and the Lord in the Gospel: *Blessed are the pure of heart, for they will see God* [^2]. We who marvel at this blind man, now seeing, let us press on, with him granting it, as much as we can, to the healing and cleansing of our hearts. Let conduct be good, and hearts are cleansed. For what does it profit to be cleansed from sins in the sacred font and at once to be defiled by most wicked conduct?

**2.** The very order of this work of the Lord by which the blind man was enlightened admonishes us to gaze on something great and necessary. For the Lord Jesus Christ could—and who is there who says: He could not?—touch his eyes without spittle and clay, and at once restore to him, or rather impart to him, sight. He could do this. What shall I say: if he had touched him with his hand? What could he not do by a word if he had commanded it? What cannot the Word do by a word? For not any word whatever, but that which *in the beginning was the Word and the Word was with God and God was the Word*. This Word, *in the beginning* God *with God, was made flesh* that it might dwell among us [^3]. The Word, then, walked clothed in flesh. And the flesh lay open, the Word lay hidden. But that this Word would come in flesh, many Prophets before, as it were his heralds, in his spirit and truth foretold beforehand. Many came before, saying: Behold he will come, and such will come in whom will be the remission of sins. Behold he will come in many ways, in many figures, in many types of sacrifices, in many veils of mysteries. They sounded nothing else than: behold he will come. After he came, at once the friend of the bridegroom at the water: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world* [^4]; who was promised to be going to come, of whom Moses wrote, of whom the Law and the Prophets are witnesses. For whose sanctification the temple was built, whose blood was figured in the victims of the priests. *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*. The Lord, then, in curing this man born blind, in whom the human race born blind was figured, in enlightening this man kept the order. He spat on the earth and made clay, and the Lord anointed his eyes with spittle. The earth signifies the Prophets. For this earth was sent before, because the Prophets, what are they except earth? Men, namely, made from earth, received the spirit of the Lord and anointed the people of God. They held prophecy, but did not yet see.

**3.** But see where he was sent, to wash his face: to the pool of Siloam. What is Siloam? Well that the Evangelist did not keep silent: *which is interpreted sent* [^5]. Who is the *sent one*, except he who constantly says: *The Father who sent me* [^6]? Who is the *sent one*, except he of whom it was said: *Behold the Lamb of God?* In him the face is washed and he who had been anointed sees, because in the Lord Christ all prophecy is fulfilled. He who does not know Christ walks anointed. And if perhaps he reads the Prophet, he is a Jew. Why do you read the Prophet? Go to Siloam that you may see him and know whose spittle you carry in your eyes. But that order which preceded in the eyes of this man, was kept also in his heart. Attend how the Jews questioned him. *What do you say of this man?* I say, he says, *that he is a prophet* [^7]. He had not yet washed the face of his heart in Siloam. His eyes indeed already lay open, but his heart was anointed, when he had washed his face. He answered as he could, as one anointed, not yet seeing. He defended his anointing, that is, of his heart, but the opening of his bodily eyes.

**4.** He said too something as one anointed, not yet seeing: *We know that God does not hear sinners* [^8]. If this were true, that Publican would have beaten his breast without cause, but let us pardon this in one still anointed, not yet seeing. For God does hear sinners too. For they are the orphans, the humble, the poor. *Judge*, he says, *for the orphan and the needy, justify the humble and the poor* [^9]. Humble and poor was he who did not dare to lift his eyes to heaven. He beat his breast, saying: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*. And he went down justified who said: *Be merciful to me a sinner* [^10]. Surely God does not hear sinners? What is an orphan, except one still a little one in faith? For "orphan" is a diminutive word: pupus, pupulus, pupillus. For when he has grown and begun to come of age, he is not a pupillus, but is now called an adult. But when he reaches twenty-five years, he is now called of legal age. What shall we say, brothers? If we consider the greatness of the holy angels, are not we men set on earth orphans? But someone says: How are we orphans, who, though little ones, yet cry out: *Our Father who are in heaven* [^11]? This because we are orphans spiritually, not carnally. For carnally an orphan is one whose father is dead; spiritually an orphan is one whose Father is hidden. Then we shall not be orphans, when we have seen our Father.

**5.** So let us find when this man, with eyes now open, yet still anointed in heart, washed his face. *The Jews, angry*, conquered and convicted, and blind being angry at the one seeing, *cast him out*. When he was cast out, then he entered there from where the Jews, set in the house of God, could not cast him out. Therefore, cast out, he found the Lord in the temple and he said to him—surely he knew by whom he had been enlightened in body, it remained that he be enlightened in heart. Now he washes the face of his heart, now he comes to Siloam, because now he understands the Only-begotten to be the sent one—*Do you*, he says, *believe in the Son of God?* And he: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?* as one anointed not yet seeing. And the Lord: *You both have seen him and he who speaks with you is the one*. To give these words is to wash the face. In short, he, with face now washed, seeing with the heart: *I believe*, he says, *Lord, and prostrate he adored him* [^12]. Turned toward the Lord . . .
