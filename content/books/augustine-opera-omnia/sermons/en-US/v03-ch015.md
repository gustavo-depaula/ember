# Sermon 62

On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 8:8: "I Am Not Worthy That You Should Enter Under My Roof," and What Follows; and Also on the Words of the Apostle, 1 Corinthians 8:10: "for If Anyone Sees Him Who Has Knowledge Reclining in an Idol's Temple," and What Follows

**1. 1.** We heard, when the Gospel was read, our faith praised in humility. For when the Lord Jesus promised that he would go to the Centurion's house, to heal his servant, he answered: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof: but only say the word, and he will be healed*[^1]. By saying himself unworthy, he showed himself worthy—not into whose walls, but into whose heart Christ should enter. Nor would he have said this with such faith and humility, unless he bore in his heart the one whom he feared to enter his house. For it was no great happiness if the Lord Jesus entered his walls, and were not in his breast. For the Master of humility, both in word and example, reclined even in the house of a certain proud Pharisee, named Simon[^2]. And while he reclined in his house, there was not in his heart a place where the Son of man might lay his head[^3].

**1. 2.** For so he called back from his discipleship a certain proud man—as is understood from the Lord's own words—who desired of his own accord to go with him. *I will follow you, Lord*, he said, *wherever you go.* And the Lord, seeing the invisible things in his heart: *The foxes*, he said, *have holes, and the birds of heaven nests: but the Son of man has nowhere to lay his head*[^4]. This is: There dwell in you ambushes like foxes, there dwells pride like the birds of heaven: but the Son of man, simple against ambushes, humble against pride, has nowhere to lay his head. And that very laying down of the head, not its raising up, is the teacher of humility. So he calls back this one who desired to go, and draws another who refused. For in the same place he said to a certain man: *Follow me.* And he: *I will follow you, Lord: but let me first go and bury my father*[^5]. He excused himself piously indeed: and therefore the more worthy that his excuse be set aside, his calling be made firm. What he wished to do was pious: but the Master taught what he ought to prefer. For he wished him to be a preacher of the living word, to make those who would live. But there were others through whom that need might be filled. *Let*, he says, *the dead bury their dead*[^6]. When unbelievers bury a corpse, the dead bury the dead. That man's body has lost its soul: their soul has lost God. For as the life of the body is the soul, so the life of the soul is God. As the body expires when it sends out the soul, so the soul expires when it loses God. God lost, the death of the soul: the soul sent out, the death of the body. The death of the body is necessary: the death of the soul is voluntary.

**1. 3.** The Lord, then, reclined in the house of a certain proud Pharisee. He was in his house, as I said; and in his breast he was not. But into this Centurion's house he did not enter, and possessed the breast. Zacchaeus indeed received the Lord both into his house and into his soul[^7]. Yet this man's faith is praised in humility. For he said: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof*[^8]. And the Lord: *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel*[^9]: according to the flesh. For this man too was now an Israelite according to the spirit. The Lord had come to carnal Israel, that is, to the Jews, there first to seek the lost sheep[^10], in which people, and from which people, he had even assumed a body: *There I have not found such faith*, he himself says. We can measure the faith of men, as men: he who beheld the inner things, he whom no one deceived, gave testimony to the man's heart, hearing the words of humility, pronouncing the sentence of healing.

**2. 4.** But whence did he presume this? *For I too*, he says, *am a man set under authority, having soldiers under me; and I say to this one, Go, and he goes; and to another, Come, and he comes; and to my servant, Do this, and he does it*[^11]. I am an authority to certain ones set under me, set under a certain authority over me. If, then, I, he says, a man under authority, have the power of commanding; what can you do, whom all authorities serve? But this man was of the Gentiles: for he was a centurion. Already the Jewish nation had a soldier of the Roman empire. There this man served as a soldier, as far as a centurion could; both under authority, and having authority; subject and obeying, ruling those subject to him. But the Lord (let your love attend to this with special and necessary care), although he was among the Jewish people, was already announcing that the Church would be throughout the whole world, into which he was going to send the Apostles: he himself unseen by the Gentiles and believed, by the Jews seen and slain. For as the Lord did not enter this man's house in body, and yet healed his faith and his very house, absent in body, present in majesty: so the same Lord was in body only among the Jewish people; among the other nations he was neither born of a virgin, nor suffered, nor walked on foot, nor endured human things, nor did divine wonders. None of these among the other nations: and yet of him was fulfilled what had been said: *A people whom I did not know served me.* How, if it did not know him? *By the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^12]. The Jewish nation knew him, and crucified him: the world heard, and believed.

**3. 5.** This absence, in a way, of his body and presence of his power in all the Gentiles he signified also in that woman who had touched the fringe of his garment, when he inquires, saying: *Who touched me?*[^13] As if absent, he inquires: as if present, he heals. *The crowds*, the disciples say, *press you, and you say: Who touched me?* For as if he walked so as to be touched by no body at all, so he said: *Who touched me?* And they: *The crowds press you.* And as though the Lord said, I seek one who touches, not one who presses. So too is his body now, that is, his Church. The faith of the few touches it, the crowd of the many presses it. For that the Church is the body of Christ, as his children, you have heard: and if you wish, you yourselves are it. The Apostle says this in many places: *For his body, which is the Church*[^14]. And again: *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^15]. If, then, we are his body, what his body then suffered in the crowd, this his Church suffers. It is pressed by the crowds: it is touched by the few. The flesh presses it, faith touches it. Lift up, then, your eyes, I beseech you, you who have whence to see. For you have what to see. Lift up the eyes of faith, touch the outermost fringe of the garment, it will suffice for salvation.

**3. 6.** See that what you heard from the Gospel as then to come is now present. *Therefore*, he says, *I say to you*: on account of the praised faith of the Centurion, as it were a foreigner in flesh, a member of the household in heart. *Therefore*, he says, *many from the east and west will come.* Not all, but *many*: yet they *from the east and west*: by these two parts the whole world is designated. *Many from the east and west will come, and will recline with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven: but the sons of the kingdom will be cast out into the outer darkness*[^16]. *But the sons of the kingdom*, namely the Jews. Whence *sons of the kingdom?* Because they received the Law, to whom the Prophets were sent, among whom were the temple and the priesthood, who celebrated the figures of all things to come. For of what things they celebrated the figures, they did not recognize the presence. *The sons*, then, *of the kingdom will go*, he says, *into the outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.* We see the Jews reprobated, we see Christians called from the east and west to a certain heavenly banquet, to recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob; where the bread is justice, where the drink is wisdom.

**4. 7.** Attend, then, brothers: for this you are, you are of this people, foretold then, now presented. You are surely of those who were called from the east and west to recline in the kingdom of heaven, not in the temple of idols. Be, then, the body of Christ, not the pressure on the body of Christ. You have the fringe of the garment, which you may touch, that you may be healed of the flow of blood, that is, of the flux of carnal pleasures. You have, I say, the fringe of the garment, which you may touch. Take the Apostles to be the garment, clinging to the sides of Christ under the weave of unity. Among these Apostles the least and last, Paul, was as it were the fringe: as he himself says: *I am the least of the Apostles*[^17]. In a garment the last and least part is the fringe. The fringe is looked upon with contempt: but it is touched for healing. *Even to this hour we both hunger, and thirst, and are naked, and are buffeted*[^18]. What so outermost, what so contemptible? Touch, if you suffer a flow of blood: power will go forth from him whose garment it is, and will heal you. The fringe was just now being set forth to be touched, when from the same Apostle it was read: *For if anyone sees him who has knowledge reclining in an idol's temple; will not his conscience, since it is weak, be built up to eating things offered to idols? And the weak one will perish in your knowledge, the brother for whom Christ died*[^19]. How do you think men can be deceived by images, which they think the Christians honor? God knows my heart, he says. But your brother does not know your heart. If you are weak, beware of a greater sickness: if you are strong, take care of your brother's weakness. Those who see these things are built up to other things, so that they desire not only to eat there, but even to sacrifice. *Behold, the weak brother perishes in your knowledge*. Hear, brother: if you despised the weak one, do you despise even a brother? Wake up. What if you sin against Christ himself? For attend to what you can in no way despise. *So*, he says, *sinning against the brothers, and striking their weak conscience, you sin against Christ*[^20]. Let those go now who despise these things, and recline in an idol's temple: will they not be pressing, not touching? And when they have reclined in the idol's temple, let them come and fill the church; not to receive salvation, but to make a pressure.

**5. 8.** But I fear, you will say, lest I offend a greater one. Fear plainly lest you offend a greater one; and you do not offend God. For you who fear lest you offend a greater one, see whether perhaps there be one greater than this one whom you fear to offend. Surely do not offend a greater one. This rule is set before you. Is it not manifest that he is least to be offended who is greater than the rest? Examine now your greater ones. First for you are your father and mother: if rightly educating, if nurturing you in Christ; they are to be heard in all things, they are to be obeyed in every command; let them command nothing against a greater one, and let them be served. Who, you say, is greater than the one who begot me? He who created you yourself. For man begets, God creates. Whence man begets, he does not know: what he is going to beget, he does not know. He who saw you in order to make you, before there was the one whom he made, is surely greater than your father. Let your fatherland be greater than your very parents; so that whatever your parents command against your fatherland, they not be heard. And whatever your fatherland commands against God, let it not be heard. For if you wish to be healed, if, after the flow of blood, if after twelve years in that disease, if after consuming all your substance on physicians, and not recovering health, you wish at last to become well, O woman, whom I address as a type of the Church: that thing your father commands, and that thing your people commands. But your Lord says to you: *Forget your people and the house of your father*[^21]. To what good? to what fruit? to what reward? *Because*, he says, *the king has desired your beauty*[^22]. He desired what he made: for, that he might make you beautiful, he loved you foul. For the unfaithful and foul one he shed his blood, he rendered her faithful and beautiful, he loved his own gifts in you. For what have you conferred on your bridegroom? What did you receive as dowry from your former father and former people? Were they not the luxuries and rags of sins? He cast off your rags, he tore your sackcloth: he had mercy, that he might adorn; he adorned, that he might love.

**6. 9.** What more, brothers? You have heard, Christians, that *sinning against the brothers, and striking their weak conscience, you sin against Christ*[^23]. Do not despise it, if you do not wish to be blotted out of the book of life[^24]. How long do we try to say to you in a brilliant and delightful way what our grief compels us somehow to say, and does not permit us to keep silent? Whoever wish to despise these things, they sin against Christ, let them see what they do. We wish the remaining pagans to be gathered: you are stones in the way; those wishing to come stumble, and turn back. For they say in their hearts: Why should we forsake the gods, whom the Christians themselves worship with us? Far be it from me, one says, that I should worship the gods of the Gentiles. I know, I understand, I believe. What do you do about the conscience of the weak one, which you strike? What do you do about the price, if you despise what was bought? See for how much it was bought: *The weak one will perish*, he says, *in your knowledge:* which you say you have, that you know an idol is nothing, and that you think of God in mind, and so recline in an idol's temple. In this knowledge the weak one perishes. And lest you despise the weak one, he added, *for whom Christ died*[^25]. Him whom you wish to despise, attend to his price, and weigh the whole world with the death of Christ. And lest you still think you sin against the weak one, and reckon the sin light and weigh it as small: *Against Christ*, he says, *you sin.* For men are wont to say: I sin against a man, but surely not against God? Deny that Christ is God. Do you dare to deny that Christ is God? Or did you learn something else, when you reclined in the idol's temple? The teaching of Christ does not admit that teaching. I ask where you learned that Christ is not God. The pagans are wont to say this. You see what evil tables do? You see that *evil conversations corrupt good morals?*[^26] You cannot speak there of the Gospel, and you hear men speaking of idols. You lose there that Christ is God: and what you drink there, you vomit in the Church. Perhaps here you dare to speak, perhaps among the crowds you dare to murmur: Was not Christ a man? was he not crucified? You learned this from the pagans, you lost your salvation, you did not touch the fringe. Touch the fringe in this too, recover your salvation. As we taught you to touch it in that which is written: *He who sees a brother reclining in an idol's temple*: touch it also concerning the divinity of Christ. The same fringe said of the Jews: *Whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all things, God blessed forever*[^27]. Behold against what true God you sin, while you recline among false gods.

**6. 10.** It is not God, he says; because it is the genius of Carthage. As though, if it were Mars or Mercury, it would be God. But attend to how it is held by them; not what it is. For I too know with you that it is a stone. If the genius is some ornament, let the citizens of Carthage live well, and they themselves will be the genius of Carthage. But if the genius is a demon, you heard there too: *What the Gentiles immolate, they immolate to demons, and not to God: I do not wish you to become partners of demons*[^28]. We know that it is not God; would that they themselves knew this too: but on account of those weak ones who do not know this, their conscience ought not to be struck. This the Apostle admonishes. For that they too hold something as a divine power, and take that statue for a divine power, the altar attests. What does an altar do there, if it is not held for a divine power? Let no one tell me, It is not a divine power, it is not God. I have already said: Would that they themselves knew this, as all of us know it. But what they hold, for what they hold it, what they do there, that altar attests. It convicts the minds of all who worship: let it not convict those who recline.

**7. 11.** But let not Christians press, if the pagans press. It is the body of Christ. Were we not saying this, that the body of Christ was pressed, and not touched? He bore those who pressed, he sought those who touched. And would, brothers, that the body of Christ be pressed by the pagans, by whom it is wont to be pressed; let not Christians press the body of Christ. Brothers, it pertains to us to speak to you, it pertains to us to speak to Christians. *For what have I to do with judging those who are outside?*[^29] the Apostle himself says. Those we address otherwise, as weak: they must be coaxed, that they may hear the truth: in you the rottenness must be cut out. If you seek whence the pagans may be conquered, whence they may be enlightened, whence they may be called to salvation: forsake their solemnities, forsake their trifles; and if they do not consent to our truth, let them be ashamed at their own fewness.

**7. 12.** If the one who is over you is good, he is your nourisher: if he is evil, he is your tempter. Both gladly receive nourishment, and in temptation be approved. Be gold. Consider this world as the goldsmith's furnace: in one narrow place there are three things, gold, chaff, fire. To these two the fire is applied, the chaff is burned, the gold is purified. Someone yielded to threats, and was led to the idol's temple: woe to me, because I bewail the chaff, I see ash. Another did not yield to threats, did not yield to torturers; led to the judge, he stood in his confession, was not bent to the idol: what does the flame do? Does it not purify the gold? *Stand in the Lord*, brothers[^30]: more powerful is he who called you. Do not fear the threats of the impious. Suffer enemies; you have those for whom you may pray; let them by no means terrify you. This is health, draw from here at this banquet: drink here, whence you may be filled, not there, whence you may go mad. Stand in the Lord. You are of silver, you will be gold. This likeness is not from us, it is from divine Scripture. You have read, you have heard: *Like gold in the furnace he proved them; and like the victim of a holocaust he received them*[^31]. Behold what you will be for the treasures of God. Be rich in God: not going to make him rich, but going to be rich from him. Let him fill you, do not admit anything else to your heart.

**8. 13.** Do we raise you to pride, or tell you to be despisers against the authorities ordained? We do not say this. You who are sick in this too, touch in this too that fringe of the garment. The Apostle himself says: *Let every soul be subject to the higher authorities: for there is no authority except from God. And those that are, are ordained by God. But he who resists authority, resists the ordinance of God*[^32]. But what, if it commands what you ought not to do? Here surely despise the authority, by fearing the authority. Notice the very grades of human affairs. If a curator commands something, is it not to be done? Yet if he commands against the proconsul, you surely do not despise the authority, but choose to serve the greater. Nor ought the lesser to be angry at this, if the greater is preferred. Again, if the proconsul himself commands one thing, and the emperor commands another, is it doubted that, the former being despised, the latter is to be served? So if the emperor commands one thing, and God another, what do you judge? Pay the tribute, be in obedience to me. Rightly, but not in an idol's temple. In an idol's temple he forbids. Who forbids? The greater authority. Pardon me: you threaten the prison, he threatens hell. Hence now your faith must be taken up as a shield, in which you can quench all the fiery darts of the enemy[^33].

**9. 14.** But the powerful one lies in wait against you, and the powerful one contrives against you: he sharpens the razor with which to shave hairs, not with which to cut off the head. What I have said, you just now heard in the Psalm: *Like a sharp razor you have wrought guile*[^34]. Why did he compare the guile of the evil powerful man to a razor? Because it is admitted only to our superfluities. As the hairs on our body seem, as it were, superfluous, and are shaved without harm to the flesh; so whatever an angry powerful man can do to you, number among your superfluities. He takes away your poverty: does he take away your riches? Your poverty, your riches, are in your heart. He could take away your superfluities, could afflict you with loss, even permitted to harm the body. Even this life, for those who think of another life; this life, I say, must be reckoned among the superfluities. For the martyrs too despised it. They did not lose life, but acquired life.

**10. 15.** Be certain, brothers, that the enemies are not admitted against the faithful, except so far as it profits for tempting and proving the faithful. Be certain, brothers, let no one say otherwise. Cast all your care upon the Lord, utterly throw your whole selves upon him. He does not withdraw himself, that you may fall. He who created us, gave us security even about our very hairs. *Amen I say to you*, he says, *the hairs of your head are all numbered*[^35]. Our hairs are numbered to God: how much more our ways, to whom our hairs are so known? See that God does not despise our least things. For if he despised them, he would not even create them. For he surely created our very hairs, and holds them numbered. But now that they exist, you say, perhaps they will perish. And here too hear his voice: *Amen I say to you, a hair of your head will not perish*[^36]. Why do you fear a man, O man set in the bosom of God? Do not fall from his bosom: whatever you suffer there will avail for salvation, not for ruin. The martyrs endured the mangling of their members, and do Christians fear the injuries of Christian times? He who does you injury now, does it fearing. He does not say openly, Come to the idol: he does not say openly, Come to my altars, feast there. And if he said it, and you were unwilling, let him complain of this, let him lay this down in his petition, in his complaint: He was unwilling to come to my altars, he was unwilling to come to the temple I venerate. Let him say this. He does not dare to say it: but he contrives other things craftily. Prepare the hairs, he sharpens the razor: he is going to take away your superfluities, to shave off whatever you are going to leave behind. Let him take away whatever will abide, if he can. What did the powerful, harmful one take away? what great thing did he take away? What a thief, what a burglar takes; that he may rage greatly, what a robber takes. If he is even permitted to kill the body itself, what does he take except what a robber takes? I honored him when I said: A robber. For any robber whatever is a man. What a fever takes, what a scorpion, what an evil mushroom. This is the whole power of those who rage, to do what a mushroom does. Men eat an evil mushroom, and die. Behold in what frailty human life is: which you are sometime going to leave behind, do not so fight for it that you yourself are left behind.

**11. 16.** Our life is Christ: attend to Christ. He came to suffer, but also to be glorified; to be despised, but also to be exalted; to die, but also to rise. The work terrifies you, see the reward. Why do you wish to come in delicacy to that thing, to which nothing leads except labor? But you fear lest you lose your silver; because you came to your silver with great labor. If you did not come without labor to silver, which you are sometime, even by dying, going to lose; do you wish to come without labor to eternal life? Let that be dearer to you, to which, after all labors, you will so come that you never lose it. If this is dear to you, to which, after all labors, you have so come that you will sometime lose it; how much more ought we to desire those perpetual things?

**11. 17.** Do not believe their words, nor fear them. They call us enemies of their idols. So may God grant, and give all things into our power, as he gave that which was broken. For this we say to your love, that you not do these things when it is not in your power to do that. It belongs to perverse men, to the furious Circumcellions, both to rage where they do not have the power, and to wish to die, hastening without cause. You heard what we read to you, all you who were lately present at the Mappalia. *When the land has been given into your power* (first he says, *into your power*, and so he said what is to be done), *their altars*, he says, *you shall destroy, their groves you shall break to pieces, and you shall shatter all their pillars*[^37]. When you have received the power, do this. Where the power is not given us, we do not do it; where it is given, we do not pass it by. Many pagans have these abominations on their estates: do we go and break them? For first we act, that we may break the idols in their heart. When they too have become Christians, either they invite us to so good a work, or they precede us. Now we must pray for them, not be angry with them. If great grief moves us, it moves against the Christians, it moves against our brothers, who wish so to enter the church that they have their body here, their heart elsewhere. The whole ought to be within. If within is what a man sees, why is without what God sees?

**12. 18.** But know, dearly beloved, that their murmurs join themselves with the heretics, with the Jews. The heretics, Jews, and pagans have made a unity against unity. Because it happened that in some places the Jews received discipline on account of their wickedness; they accuse, and suspect, or pretend, that we always seek such things against them. Because it happened that somewhere the heretics paid penalties to the laws for the impiety and fury of their violences; now they say that we seek in all things some inconvenience for them unto their ruin. Again, because it pleased that laws be passed against the pagans—rather, for the pagans, if they are wise—for just as, when a stern pedagogue comes to senseless boys playing at mud and dirtying their hands, he shakes the mud from the hand, holds out a book—so God willed, through princes subject to him, to terrify the senseless, childish hearts, that they cast off the mud from their hands, and do something useful. What is useful with the hands? *Break your bread for the hungry, and bring the needy without shelter into your house*[^38]. And yet the boys escape from the eyes of the pedagogue, and return to the mud by stealth; and when they are found, they hide their hands, lest they be seen; because, then, God willed it, they think we seek idols everywhere; which, when we find, we break in all places. Why? Are not the places in which they are before us? Or do we truly not know where these things are? And yet we do not do it: because God has not given it into our power. When does God give it into our power? When the one whose property it is will be a Christian. Just now he willed it done whose property it is. If he were unwilling to give the place itself to the Church, and only commanded that there be no idols on his property; I think it ought to be done with the highest devotion, that the Christian soul be helped by the Christians, the absent one who wishes on the land to give thanks to God, who does not wish there to be anything there in contempt of God. To this is added that he gave the very places to the Church. And were there going to be idols on the property of the Church? Brothers, behold what displeases the pagans. It is too little for them that we do not remove those from their estates, do not break them: they wish them kept even on ours. We preach against idols, we remove them from hearts: we are persecutors of idols: we profess it. Are we keepers of them? I do not do it where I cannot; I do not do it where the owner of the property complains: but where he wishes it done, and gives thanks; I will be guilty if I do not do it.
