# Sermon 248: In the Days of Easter

**1.** Today too a reading has been recited from the things that were done after the Lord's resurrection, according to the Evangelist John. Your Charity has heard with us that the Lord Jesus Christ showed himself to his disciples at the sea of Tiberias; and he who had already made them fishers of men found them still fishers of fish. Through the whole night they caught nothing: but when the Lord was seen, and at his command they cast their nets, they caught the number you heard. The Lord would never command this, unless he wished to signify something that it was good for us to know. What, then, of any great moment could pertain to Jesus Christ, whether fish were caught, or were not caught? But that fishing was our signification. Let us, then, recall with you those two fishings of the disciples, made at the command of the Lord Jesus Christ, one before the passion, the other after the resurrection. In these two fishings, then, the whole Church is figured, both what it is now, and what it will be in the resurrection of the dead. For now it has without number many, both good and bad: but after the resurrection it will have, with a fixed number, only the good.

**2.** Remember, then, the first fishing, where we may see what the Church is like in this time. The Lord Jesus found his disciples fishing, when he first called them to follow him. Then through the whole night they caught nothing. But when he was seen, they heard from him: *Cast the nets. Lord*, they said, *through the whole night we caught nothing; but see, at your word we cast the net.* They cast it, at the command of the Almighty. What else could happen, except what he willed? But yet by that very deed he condescended, as I said, to signify something to us that it is good to know. The nets were cast. The Lord had not yet suffered, had not yet risen. The nets were cast: they caught so many fish that the two boats were filled, and the very nets were torn by that multitude of fish. Then he said to them: *Come and I will make you fishers of men*[^1]. They received from him the nets of the word of God, they cast them into the world as into a deep sea: they caught as great a multitude of Christians as we behold and marvel at. But those two boats signified two peoples, of the Jews and of the gentiles, of the Synagogue and of the Church, of the circumcision and of the uncircumcision. For of those two boats, as of two walls coming from different directions, Christ is the cornerstone. But what have we heard? There the boats were pressed by the multitude. So it happens now: many Christians who live badly press the Church. It is too little that they press it: they also tear the nets. For if the nets had not been torn, schisms would not have been committed.

**3.** Let us pass, then, from this fishing which we endure, and come to that one which we ardently long for and faithfully desire. See, the Lord died, but rose: he appeared at the sea to his disciples, he commands them to cast the nets, not in just any way. Attend. For in the first fishing he did not say to them: Cast the nets on the right, or on the left. For if he said, On the left; only the bad would be signified: if he said, On the right; only the good would be figured. Therefore he did not say either On the right or On the left, because the good were to be caught mixed with the bad. But now, after the resurrection, what the Church will be like, hear, discern, rejoice, hope, grasp. *Cast*, he said, *the nets on the right side*[^2]. Now those on the right are caught: let no bad ones be feared. For you know that he said he would separate the sheep from the goats; that he would set the sheep on the right, the goats on the left: that to those on the left he would say: *Go into the eternal fire*; to those on the right he would say: *Receive the kingdom*[^3]. See from where: *Cast the nets on the right side.* They cast them, they caught: the number is fixed; no one there is over the number. But now how many over the number come to the altar, are seen among the people of God, and are not written in the book of life. There, then, the number is fixed. Strive to be of these fish too; not only by hearing and praising, but by understanding and living well. The nets are cast, then, great fish are caught. For who is there small then, when they will be equal to the angels of God? Great fish are caught, then, one hundred fifty-three. Someone will say to me, And will the saints be so few? Far be it from us to suspect that there will be so small a number of saints, even of those who will be in that kingdom, even from this Church alone. The number will be fixed: but there will be thousands of thousands from the people of Israel. Holy John in the Apocalypse says that from the people of Israel alone there will be twelve times twelve thousand, who did not defile themselves with women; for they remained virgins. But of the rest of the nations he says that there come, with white robes, so many thousands of men that no one can count them.

**4.** So this number means some sign, and by the anniversary solemnity of this sermon I ought to recall to you what you are accustomed to hear every year. One hundred fifty-three fish is a number signifying thousands of thousands of saints and faithful. But why by this number did the Lord condescend to signify so many thousands, who will be in the kingdom of heaven? Hear why. You know that the Law was given through Moses to the people of God, and that in the Law itself the chief thing recalled is the Decalogue, that is, the ten precepts of the Law. Of which one precept is about worshiping the one God; the second precept: *You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain*; the third precept about the observance of the Sabbath, which Christians observe spiritually, the Jews violate carnally. These three precepts pertain to God, the remaining seven to men: because of those two chief ones: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind; and you shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^4]. Because, then, these are two precepts, in that Decalogue three pertain to the love of God, and seven to the love of neighbor. What are the seven pertaining to man? *Honor your father and mother, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not kill, You shall not steal, You shall not bear false witness, You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, You shall not covet your neighbor's goods*[^5].

**5.** These ten precepts no one fulfills by his own strength, unless he is helped by the grace of God. If, then, no one fulfills the law by his own strength, unless God helps by his Spirit; now recall how the Holy Spirit is commended by the number seven, as the holy prophet says, that man is to be filled with the Spirit of God, of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and piety, with the Spirit of the fear of God[^6]. These seven workings commend the Holy Spirit by the number seven, who, as it were descending to us, begins from wisdom, ends at fear. But we, ascending, begin from fear, are perfected in wisdom. *For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^7]. If, then, the Spirit is needed for the law to be fulfilled, let the seven be added to the ten, it makes the number seventeen. If you count from one up to seventeen, they make one hundred fifty-three. There is no need to count everything now, count among yourselves: count thus, one and two and three and four are ten. As one and two and three and four are ten, so add the rest of the numbers up to seventeen: and you find the sacred number of the faithful and the saints who will be in heaven with the Lord.

[^1]: Luke 5:4-10
[^2]: John 21:6
[^3]: Matt 25:32-41
[^4]: Matt 22:37-40
[^5]: See Exod 20:12-17
[^6]: See Isa 11:2-3
[^7]: Ps 111:10 (110:10)
