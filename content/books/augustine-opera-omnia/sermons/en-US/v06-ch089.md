# Sermon 229/U: From the Sermons of the Octave of Easter

**1.** Let us see, then, what the Scriptures say. Light through light, the just through the Word. The firmament of heaven, the solid foundation of the Scriptures. The waters under the firmament, the earthly people; the waters above the firmament, the heavenly people. The dry land separated from the waters of the sea, souls thirsting for God separated from the multitude of the offenses of the world. The earth brings forth the herb of fodder and fruit-bearing trees, the works of mercy. The luminaries in the firmament of heaven, the preachers of the word, the Evangelists and Apostles, the spiritual gifts. Let us attend, then, to the luminaries going round the whole world, and see how the waters bring forth the creeping things of living souls[^1]. The Evangelists run about, men are evangelized. Therefore the creeping things of living souls are understood as the sacraments. Why? Because the sacraments are necessary for this, that the nations may be evangelized and men be separated from the nations, that is, that those bitter waters may bring forth and become sweet fish. For it is a great thing. No one can taste the water of the sea, but he can eat fish; they are born and nourished in bitterness. These are the sweet sacraments, which have been sent throughout the whole world. But they are called the creeping things of living souls; not yet a living soul. Why? This is somewhat dense to understand. You have just heard, when the reading was read, that Simon Magus was baptized and did not put off his evil mind[^2]; he had the form of the sacrament, he did not have the power of the sacrament. Hear what the Apostle says of the impious: *Having,* he says, *the form of piety, but denying its power*[^3]. What is the form of piety? The visible sacrament. What is the power of piety? Invisible love. Hear the power of piety: *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have love, I am become as sounding brass,* and so on: and if I have this and that, *but do not have love, I am nothing*[^4]. For the sacraments are holy and great; but man is nothing, if he does not have love. Therefore the power of the sacrament is love. The heretic does not have this love, though I find with him the form of the sacrament. And so, when a heretic comes to me, I defer to the form of the sacrament, so as not to rebaptize; but I restore the power of the sacrament, so as to instill the root of love. That, then, will be the living soul, which the earth brings forth, so that it may be a living soul, now having the power of the sacrament. This, then, is what was said: *Let the waters bring forth the creeping things of living souls*[^5], and so on.

[^1]: See Gen 1:20
[^2]: See Acts 8:13-23
[^3]: 2 Tim 3:5
[^4]: 1 Cor 13:1-2
[^5]: Gen 1:20
