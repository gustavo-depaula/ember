# Sermon 359: On What Is Written in Sirach 25:1:

"The Concord of Brothers, and the Love of Neighbors," and the Rest

On the Quarrel and Concord with the Donatists

**1.** The first reading of the divine words, from the book which is called Ecclesiasticus, commended to us three certain things excellent and most worthy of consideration: the concord of brothers, and the love of neighbors, and a man and a woman agreeing with each other[^1]. These are plainly good things, pleasant and praiseworthy in human affairs; but in divine affairs much more powerful. For who is there who does not rejoice with brothers in concord? And what is to be grieved over, in human affairs so great a thing is rare: the thing is praised by all, kept by very few. Blessed are those who embrace in themselves what they are compelled to praise even in others. No brothers fail to praise brothers in concord. And whence is it difficult for brothers to be in concord? Because they quarrel about earth, because they wish to be earth. For the sinful man heard from the beginning: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^2]. Whence let us examine and scrutinize the voice which the just man ought to hear from the contrary. For if it was rightly said to the sinner: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*; rightly it is said to the just: You are heaven, and into heaven you shall go. Or are not the just heavens, when of the Evangelists it is most plainly said: *The heavens declare the glory of God*? And indeed that it was said of them, the sequel teaches well enough. *And the firmament*, it says, *announces the works of his hands*. Whom he called heavens, the same he called the firmament. *Day to day pours forth speech, and night to night declares knowledge. There are no languages, nor words, of which their voices are not heard*[^3]. You ask whose, and you find none but the heavens'. It was said, then, of the Apostles, said of the announcers of the truth. Whence follows: *Their sound has gone forth into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. There are no languages, nor words, of which their voices are not heard*. When the Holy Spirit came upon them, and God began to dwell in the heaven which he made from earth, they spoke, with the Holy Spirit filling and granting it, in the tongues of all nations. Hence it was said: *There are no languages, nor words, of which their voices are not heard*. And because from there they were sent to the preaching of the Gospel through all nations: *Their sound has gone forth into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^4]. Whose? The heavens', to whom it is rightly said: You are heaven, and into heaven you shall go: as rightly to the sinner: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*.

**2.** If brothers, then, wish to be in concord, let them not love earth. But if they wish not to love earth, let them not be earth. Let them seek a possession which cannot be divided, and they will always be in concord. Whence is discord among brothers? whence the disturbance of piety? whence one womb, and not one mind, except while their soul is bent down, and each one looks to his own part, and spends effort on enriching and increasing his own part, and wishes to have unity in his own possession, who possesses with his brother a division? Good is this possession; whose is it? It is ours. A great possession! So it is wont to be said. Is it all yours, brother? No; I have here a co-heir: but if God wills, he will sell me his part. The flatterer answers: May God do it. What may God do? That the neighbor be oppressed, and sell his part to his neighbor. May God do it: you think well, may God fulfill it for you. *Since the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul; and he who does iniquitous things is blessed*[^5]. What is so iniquitous as to wish to grow rich by another's poverty? And yet this abounds: *He who does iniquitous things is blessed*; and perhaps he has prevailed, and perhaps he has pressed and oppressed, twisted and extorted, not just any co-heir, but perhaps a brother. It is better that I buy it, than a stranger. And that man, easily oppressed, if he is just, has consolation. Let him hear the Scripture, which just now he heard. That one labors in want, his brother is full of abundance. But he is full of earth, and empty of justice. Mark, O earth, what that poor man hears. *Do not fear, when a man has become rich, nor when the glory of his house is multiplied: because when he dies, he will not take them*[^6]. Hold, you poor man, what you will not leave behind dying, and what you may acquire living forever. Hold justice, do not repent of it. Are you saddened because on earth you are poor? Poor here was he who created the earth. The Lord your God consoles you, your Creator consoles you, your Redeemer consoles you. Your greedy brother does not console you. For our Lord deigned to be our brother. The only brother without doubt most faithful, with whom concord is to be possessed. I said he is not greedy, and perhaps I find him greedy. He is greedy: but he wishes to have us, wishes to acquire us. For so great a price he gave himself for us: nothing can be added to this price. He gave as price himself, and became our Redeemer. For he did not give himself as price in such a way that the enemy let us go and possessed him. He gave himself to death, slaying death. For by his own death he slew death, he was not slain by death; and with death slain, he freed us from death. For death was living while we were dying, it will die while we are living, when it will be said to it: *Where is, O death, your strife?*[^7]

**3.** Such a brother, then, was appealed to by a certain brother against a brother, between whom there was no concord on account of earth, and he said to him: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*. He has taken the whole, he will not give me my part, he despises me, let him at least hear you. What did it pertain to the Lord? As we humble ones think humble things, crawling on the earth, set in this life, and wishing to sadden no one, and yet very often saddening more grievously thereby, what would we say? Come, brother, render to your brother his part. The Lord did not say this. And what is more just than he? Who would find such a judge, whom he might appeal to against the greed of his brother? Did not that man rejoice that he had at last found great solace? He surely hoped also for great help, saying to so great a judge: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*. But what did he say? *O man, who set me as a divider of the inheritance among you?* The Lord repelled him, did not give what was asked, did not grant the gratuitous benefit. What was so great? What did he lose thereby? In what did he even labor in that benefit? He did not give it. Where is: *To everyone who asks you, give*[^8]? He who gave us an example of living did not do this. How shall we do it? Or how shall we give what costs us something, if we do not give a benefit where we expend nothing, where we give out nothing, where we lose nothing? The Lord did not give this, yet he did not give nothing. He denied the lesser, but bestowed what is more. He said plainly, he said: *To everyone who asks you, give*. What, if someone asks of you, I do not say what is useless to give, but what is shameful to give? What if some woman asks what the woman asked of Joseph? What if some man asks what the false elders asked of Susanna? Even here is that as it were general sentence to be followed: *To everyone who asks you, give*? Far be it. Shall we do here, then, against the precept of the Lord? Rather let us do according to the precept of the Lord, and not give to those asking evil things, nor will we act against this sentence. For it was said: *To everyone who asks you, give*. It was not said: Give everything to him who asks you. *To everyone who asks you, give*: plainly give; even if not what he asks, yet give something: he asks evil, do you give good. Joseph did this. He did not give what the shameless woman asked; and yet he gave what she ought to hear, that she might not be shameless by his example: and he himself did not fall into the pit of lust, and gave counsel of chastity. For he answered this: *Far be it from me, that I should do this to my lord, that I should defile his bed, who has entrusted to me all things in his house*[^9]. If a servant bought with money kept this faith to his lord, what ought a wife to keep to her husband? This was to admonish. I, a servant, will not do this to my lord: you, a wife, ought you to do this to your husband? Susanna too gave, and did not let them go empty, if they wished to be filled with the counsel of chastity. For not only did she not consent; but she did not keep silent why she did not consent. *If I consent*, she said, *to you, I perish to God: if I do not consent, I do not escape your hands: but it is better to fall into your hands, than to perish to God*[^10]. But what is, *It is better to fall into your hands, than to perish to God*? You have perished to God, you who seek such things. Hold this rule, then. Give when you are asked, even if not what you are asked. The Lord did this. What did that man ask? The division of the inheritance. What did the Lord give? The destruction of greed. What did he ask? what did he receive? *Tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me. Tell me, man, who set me as a divider of the inheritance among you? But I tell you*, what? *Beware of all greed*. And I tell you why. For perhaps you ask for half the inheritance for this, that you may grow rich. Hear: *A certain rich man's country yielded well*; it had great yields, that is, it was made fruitful with many fruits. *And he thought within himself, saying: What shall I do, where shall I gather my fruits?* And, thinking diligently, *I have found*, he said, *what I shall do. I will destroy the old storehouses, I will make new ones, I will fill them*. For I will make them greater than the old ones were. *And I will say to my soul: You have many goods; be satisfied, rejoice.* God said to him: *Fool*, you who seem to yourself very wise: for you know how to destroy old things, and build new ones, and you have remained in the oldness of ruin, you who ought to have destroyed the old things in yourself, that you might no longer savor earthly things: *Fool*, what did you say? to whom did you say it? You said to your soul: *Rejoice, you have many goods. This night your soul will be demanded*, to which you promised such things. *What you have promised, whose will they be?*[^11] *Do not*, then, *fear, when a man has become rich; because when he dies, he will not take them*[^12].

**4.** Behold what counsel the Lord gave to disagreeing brothers, by which they might be in concord, that they should be rid of greed, and at once be filled with truth. Let us find, then, such an inheritance. How long shall we speak of the concord of earthly brothers, which is rare, which is suspect, which is difficult? Let us speak of that concord of brothers, which ought to be, and can be, true. Let all Christians be brothers, let all the faithful be brothers, let them be born of God and from the womb of mother Church through the Holy Spirit: let them be brothers, let them too have an inheritance to be given, and not divided. Their inheritance is God himself. Whose inheritance they themselves are, he himself is in turn their inheritance. How are they themselves his inheritance? *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance*[^13]. How is he their inheritance? *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance, and of my cup*[^14]. In this inheritance concord is kept: for this inheritance there is no quarrel. Another inheritance is acquired by quarreling: this is lost by quarreling. Men, unwilling to lose this inheritance, avoid quarreling. And when they perhaps seem to quarrel, they do not quarrel. But perhaps they seem to quarrel, or are thought to quarrel, when they wish to take counsel for their brothers. See how concordant their quarreling is, how peaceful, how kind, how just, how faithful. For we seem to quarrel with the Donatists: but we do not quarrel. For he quarrels who wishes ill to his adversary; he quarrels who wishes his adversary to suffer loss, himself increase; something to leave the other, to come to himself. We are not so. You too know it, you who outside unity quarrel; you too know it, who from division have been acquired: you know that this quarrel is not such a quarrel, because it is not malevolent, because it does not tend to the loss of the adversary, but rather to his gain. For we wished those with whom we seemed to quarrel, or still seem to, to be acquired with us; not to lose them, that we may be acquired. Finally, our voice is different from that of that brother who appealed to Christ walking on earth. For we too appeal to him in this cause, sitting in heaven: and we do not say: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*; but: Tell my brother to hold the inheritance with me.

**5.** Since this we wish, and the public proceedings attest it: that we have wished this, the witnesses are not only sermons, but our letters given to them. Do you love the episcopate? Have it with us. In you we hate nothing, detest nothing, execrate nothing, anathematize nothing, except human error. Human error, we said, we detest, not divine truth: but what you have of God, we recognize; what you have wickedly your own, we correct. The sign of my Lord, the sign of my Emperor, the character of my King, I recognize in a deserter; I seek, I find, I apply, I approach, I lay hold, I lead, I correct the deserter, I do not violate the character. If anyone notices, if anyone attends, this is not to quarrel, but to love. We said that there can be in one Church, for the sake of peace, concordant brothers: for a beautiful thing is the concord of brothers. That there cannot be two bishops. We said, that both should sit in one simple basilica; the one on the chair, the other as a pilgrim; the one on the Christian chair, the other as it were a heretical colleague sitting nearby: again, the one to preside in his congregation, the other in turn in his. We said that penance was preached unto the remission of sins through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem by the Apostles[^15]. What will you answer to this Church, which is built through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem by the Apostles? We said: Let us suppose altogether that Caecilian had a bad cause. Shall one man having a bad cause, two men, five, ten, prejudge so many thousands of the faithful throughout the whole world, spread by manifold fruitfulness? We said these things. Abraham believed, and all the nations were promised to him: did Caecilian sin, and all the nations perish, so that what iniquity committed avails more than what truth promised? These things were said: they are read. Altogether against the divine examples, against the testimonies, which assert that the Church is spread throughout the whole world, of which Church in the name of the Lord we hold the unity, they could answer him nothing.

**6.** With the cause of the Church, then, safe, confirmed, and unchangeably fixed and established, as upon the foundation of the rock, which the gates of hell do not overcome[^16]: with this, then, safe, we came also to the cause of Caecilian, now secure whatever he should be found to have done. For perhaps, if as a man he were found in some fault, were we going to quarrel, that on account of the fault of one man we should judge ourselves to be condemned or rebaptized? And we said: With the cause of the Church safe, to which the sin of Caecilian does nothing prejudicial; nor does the justice of Caecilian crown the Church; nor does the fault of Caecilian condemn the Church; let us see also of what sort his cause is. We undertook it to be examined, but as that of a brother, not as that of a father, or mother. Our Father is God, our mother is the Church: Caecilian was a brother, or is a brother; if good, a good brother; if evil, an evil brother, yet a brother. If we shall have found him innocent, where then will you be, you who failed even in that human calumny? But if he shall be found guilty, if he shall be found a defendant, not even so are we conquered, because we hold the unity of the Church, which is unconquered. Let him be found altogether guilty, I anathematize the man, I do not desert the Church of Christ. This we did, we said: thereafter we will not recite his name at the altar among the bishops whom we believe faithful and innocent. This alone we did. Are you going to rebaptize the whole world on account of Caecilian? With this security established and confirmed, the cause of Caecilian began to be examined. He was found innocent, he was found to have been attacked by calumniators. Once condemned in absence, present he was thrice acquitted: condemned by a faction, acquitted by ecclesiastical truth. These things were read, these things were proved. It was asked whether they had anything to say against it. With all the evasions of their calumnies exhausted, or where they could bring forth nothing against the most evident documents, nor against the very innocence of Caecilian, a sentence was brought forth against them. And yet they themselves say: We have conquered. Let them conquer, but themselves, that Christ may possess them; let him who redeemed them conquer them.

**7.** And yet we rejoice over many. Many of them were fruitfully conquered, because they were not conquered. Human error was conquered, the man was saved. For the physician does not contend with the sick man; and if the sick man sides with the physician, the fever is conquered, and the sick man is healed. For the physician intends this, to conquer; the fever too intends this, to conquer. The sick man is set as it were in the middle. If the physician conquers, the sick man is saved: if the fever conquers, the sick man will die. In our contention, then, the physician was contending for health, for the fever the sick man was contending. Those who took note of the physician's counsel, conquered, overcame the fever. We have them healthy and rejoicing with us in the Church. They blasphemed us before, because they did not recognize us as brothers: for the fever had disturbed the mind. Yet we loved them even detesting us, and raging against us, and served the raging sick. We resisted, we contended, and as it were quarreled: and yet we loved. For all who serve such sick ones are troublesome; but they are troublesome unto health.

**8.** But we sometimes find slothful men saying: It is true, Lord, it is true; there is nothing to say. What then? Come, act. My father died there, my mother is buried there. You have named the dead and the buried. You live, there is still one with whom to speak. Your parents were Christians in the party of Donatus; their parents perhaps were themselves too Christians, their grandfathers or great-grandfathers certainly pagans. Those, then, who were first made Christians, when they buried their pagan parents, were they cold against the truth? did they follow the authority of their dead parents, and not rather prefer the living Christ to their dead parents? If, then, here is true unity, outside which you must die forever, why do you wish to follow your dead parents, dead to you and to God? What do you say? answer. You speak truth, there is nothing to say. What do you wish me to do? Some custom or other holds such men to itself. They are lethargic, they labor with the contrary disease, they are going to die by sleeping. Others are frenzied, they are troublesome. For even if the lethargic is going to die, he is not troublesome to the one serving him. The frenzied are troublesome, who have lost their mind, and, insane and furious, wander armed here and there, seeking whom they may kill, whom they may blind. For new things have been reported to us: they cut out the tongue of a certain presbyter of ours. These are frenzied. Love must be exercised, even they are to be loved. Many corrected have wept, many corrected: we know it, they came to us from the number of those very furious ones. Daily they weep over their past deeds, nor are they sated with tears, attending to the fury of those who, the drunkenness of their vanity not digested, still rage. What, then, do we do? Love compels us to serve such people. And although we are troublesome to both kinds, both by rousing the lethargic and by binding the frenzied, yet we love both.

**9.** A good thing is the *concord of brothers*; but see where: in Christ, of Christians. *And the love of neighbors.* What, if one is still not yet a brother in Christ? Because he is a man, he is a neighbor; love even him, that you may gain even him. If, then, you are in concord with your Christian brother, but love your neighbor, even one with whom there is now no concord, because he is not yet a brother in Christ, because he is not yet reborn in Christ, does not yet know the Sacraments of Christ; he is a pagan, he is a Jew; yet he is a neighbor, because he is a man: if you love even him, you have come also to another love by another gift, and so there are two in you: *The concord of brothers, and the love of neighbors*. Of all these holding concord with their brothers and loving the neighbor, the Church consists, devoted to Christ, and subject to her husband, so that there is a third, *A man and a woman agreeing with each other*[^17]. Whence we admonish your love, and exhort you in the Lord, despise present things, my brothers, which you do not carry with you by dying: beware sins, beware iniquities, beware worldly desires. For then is our fruit in us whole, and our reward with the Lord full of joys. For even if we say what must be said, even if we preach what must be preached, and absolve ourselves before the Lord in the sight of the Lord, because we have not kept silent what we fear, have not kept silent what we love, so that upon whom the sword of the Lord's vengeance comes, he may not find what to charge to the watchman: yet we do not wish our reward to be secure with you lost, but with you found. For the apostle Paul too was secure of his reward, and yet what does he say to the people? *Now we live, if you stand in the Lord*[^18]. I speak to you and to your love, according to the command of the Lord, fathers and brothers. I speak also for my brother your bishop, whose joy you ought to be, by obeying the Lord our God. Surely in the name of God this church was made for you, his work, through the beneficent, merciful, devoted contributions of the faithful brothers. This church was made for you: but you are rather the Church. It was made for you, that your bodies might enter it: but your minds ought to be that into which God may enter. You honored your bishop, that you wished to call this basilica Florentia; but his Florentia is you. For so the Apostle says: *My joy and my crown are you in the Lord*[^19]. Whatever is in the world, vanishes, passes away. This life, what is it, except what the Psalm said: *In the morning like grass it will pass away, in the morning it will flower and pass on: in the evening it will fall, it hardens and withers*[^20]? This is all flesh. Therefore Christ, therefore the new life, therefore eternal hope, therefore the consolation of immortality promised, and in the flesh of the Lord already rendered. For from us was assumed that flesh, which is now immortal, and he showed to us what in himself he fulfilled. For on our account he had flesh. For on his own account: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^21]. Seek flesh and blood: where, in the Word? Because he truly wished to suffer with us, and to redeem us, he put on the form of a servant, and he who was here came down, that he who was not absent might appear; and he who made man wished to become man, to be created of a mother who created the mother. He ascended even to the cross, he died, and showed us what we knew, to be born and to die. He, humble, fulfilled in himself these old, familiar, and known things of ours. To be born and to die we knew: to rise again and to live forever we did not know. Two old things of ours, then, the humble one assumed: two other great and new things the lofty one fulfilled. He raised up the flesh, he lifted the flesh into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father. He wished to be our head, the head cried out for the members: because even when he was here, he said: *Father, I will that where I am, there they too may be with me*[^22]. Let us hope this too of our flesh, resurrection, change, incorruption, immortality, eternal dwelling: and let us act so that we may arrive. This will be Florentia, the true Florentia.
