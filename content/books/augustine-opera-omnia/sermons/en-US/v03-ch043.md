# Sermon 78: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 17:1-8: "After Six Days Jesus Took Peter, and James, and John His Brother," and What Follows

**1.** We must inspect and treat, dearly beloved, that vision which the Lord showed on the mountain. For it is that of which he had said: *Amen I say to you, that there are some of those standing here who will not taste death, until they see the Son of man in his kingdom*[^1]. Thence began the reading which was read. *When he had said this, after six days he took three disciples, Peter, John, and James, and ascended the mountain*[^2]. Those three were certain of those of whom he had said: *There are some here who will not taste death, until they see the Son of man in his kingdom.* It is no small question. For that mountain was not the kingdom comprehended. What is a mountain to him who possesses heaven? Which we not only read, but also in a way see with the eyes of the heart. He says his kingdom, which in many places he calls the kingdom of heaven. But the kingdom of heaven is the kingdom of the saints. For *the heavens declare the glory of God*[^3]. Of which heavens it was said at once in the Psalm: *There are no speeches nor words, whose voices are not heard. Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^4]. Whose, except the heavens? Therefore of the Apostles, and of all the faithful preachers of the word of God. The heavens will reign with him who made the heavens. That this might be shown, see what was done.

**2.** The Lord Jesus himself shone like the sun; his garments became white like snow: and Moses and Elijah were speaking with him. Jesus himself indeed, he himself shone like the sun[^5], signifying himself to be the light that enlightens every man coming into this world[^6]. What this sun is to the eyes of the flesh, that he is to the eyes of the heart: and what this is to flesh, that he is to hearts. But his garments are his Church. For garments, unless held together by the one wearing them, fall. Of these garments Paul was, as it were, the last fringe. For he himself says: *I am the least of the Apostles*[^7]: and in another place: *I am the last of the Apostles*. But in a garment the fringe is the last and least. Accordingly, just as that woman who suffered a flow of blood was made whole when she touched the fringe of the Lord[^8]: so the Church which came from the Gentiles was saved by Paul preaching. What wonder if the Church is signified by white garments, when you hear the prophet Isaiah saying: *And if your sins are like scarlet, I will make them white as snow*[^9]? Moses and Elijah, that is, the Law and the Prophets, what do they avail, except when they converse with the Lord? Unless they bear testimony to the Lord, who will read the Law? who the Prophets? See how briefly the Apostle says this: *For through the Law is the knowledge of sin: but now without the Law the justice of God has been manifested*; behold the sun: *witnessed by the Law and the Prophets*[^10]; behold the splendor.

**3.** Peter sees this, and savoring human things as a man: *Lord, it is good*, he says, *for us to be here*[^11]. He was suffering weariness from the crowd, he had found the solitude of the mountain; there he had Christ the bread of the mind. Why should he depart thence to labors and griefs, having toward God holy loves, and therefore good ways? He wished it to be well with himself: whence too he added: *If you wish, let us make here three tabernacles: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah*[^12]. To these things the Lord answered nothing: but yet Peter was answered. For as he was saying these things, a bright cloud came, and overshadowed them. He sought three tabernacles: the heavenly answer showed us one to be, which the human sense desired to divide. The Word of God is Christ, the Word of God in the Law, the Word in the Prophets. Why, Peter, do you seek to divide? Rather you ought to join. You seek three: understand them also one.

**4.** So when the cloud overshadowed all, and in a way made for them one tabernacle, a voice too sounded from the cloud, saying: *This is my beloved Son*[^13]. There was Moses, there Elijah: it was not said, These are my beloved sons. For it is one thing to be the Only one, another to be the adopted. He was commended, in whom the Law and the Prophets gloried. *This is*, he says, *my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear him*[^14]. Because both in the Prophets you heard him, and in the Law you heard him. And where did you not hear him? When this was heard, they fell to the ground. Now there is shown to us, in the Church, the kingdom of God. Here the Lord, here the Law and the Prophets: but the Lord as Lord; the Law in Moses, prophecy in Elijah; but they as servants, as ministers. They as vessels: he as the fountain. Moses and the Prophets spoke and wrote: but they were filled from him when they poured forth.

**5.** But the Lord held out his hand, and roused those lying down. Then *they saw no one, except Jesus alone*[^15]. What does this mean? You heard, when the Apostle was read, that *we see now through a mirror in an enigma, but then face to face*[^16]. And tongues will be done away, when that comes which now we hope and believe[^17]. That those fell to the ground signified this, that we die: because it was said to the flesh: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^18]. But when the Lord raised them up, it signified the resurrection. After the resurrection, what is the Law to you? what prophecy to you? Therefore Elijah does not appear, Moses does not appear. There remains for you: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^19]. There remains for you, that God may be all in all[^20]. There Moses will be; but no longer the Law. We will see Elijah there too; but no longer a prophet. For the Law and the Prophets bore testimony to Christ, that he ought to suffer, and on the third day rise from the dead, and enter into his glory[^21]. Where is fulfilled that which he promised to his lovers: *He who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him*[^22]. And as if it were said: Because you will love him, what will you give him? *And I will show myself to him.* A great gift, a great promise. God does not keep for you any reward of his own: but himself. Greedy man, why does it not suffice you what Christ promises? You seem to yourself rich; and if you do not have God, what do you have? Another is poor; and if he has God, what does he not have?

**6.** Come down, Peter: you desired to rest on the mountain, come down, preach the word, press on in season, out of season, convict, exhort, rebuke, with all longanimity and teaching[^23]. Labor, sweat, suffer some torments: that what is understood in the white garments of the Lord, you may possess through the brightness and beauty of right working in love. For in the praise of love, when the Apostle was read, we heard: *It does not seek its own things*[^24]. *It does not seek its own things;* because it gives what it possesses. In another place it was said more dangerously, if you do not understand it. For commanding the faithful members of Christ according to that very love, the Apostle says: *Let no one seek what is his own, but what is another's*[^25]. For avarice, hearing this, prepares frauds, that in business, as it were seeking another's, it may circumvent someone, and seek not its own, but another's. Let avarice restrain itself, let justice come forth: let us hear, and understand. To love it was said: *Let no one seek what is his own, but what is another's.* But you, avaricious man, if you resist, and rather reduce this precept to this, that you crave another's; lose your own. But as I know you, you wish to have both your own and another's. You commit fraud, that you may have another's: suffer theft, that you may lose your own. You do not wish to seek your own, but you take away another's. But if you do this, you do not do well. Hear, O avaricious man, listen: the Apostle in another place more openly expounds for you this which he said: *Let no one seek what is his own, but what is another's.* He says of himself: *But I, not seeking what is useful to me, but what is useful to many, that they may be saved*[^26]. This Peter did not yet understand, when on the mountain he desired to live with Christ. He was keeping this for you, Peter, after death. But now he himself says: Come down to labor on earth, to serve on earth, to be despised, to be crucified on earth. Life came down, to be killed; bread came down, to hunger; the way came down, to be wearied on the journey; the fountain came down, to thirst: and do you refuse to labor? Do not seek your own. Have love, preach the truth: then you will come to eternity, where you will find security.
