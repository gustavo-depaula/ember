# Sermon 329: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1.** Through the so glorious deeds of the holy martyrs, by which the Church flourishes everywhere, with our very own eyes we prove how true is what we have sung, that *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^1]: when it is precious both in our sight, and in the sight of him for whose name it was done. But the price of these deaths is the death of one. How many deaths did one dying buy, who, if he did not die, the grain of wheat would not be multiplied? You heard his words when he was drawing near to his passion, that is, when he was drawing near to our redemption: *Unless a grain of wheat falling into the earth dies, it remains alone: but if it dies, it bears much fruit*[^2]. For he transacted a great exchange on the cross; there the purse of our price was loosed: when his side was opened by the lance of the striker, there flowed out the price of the whole world. The faithful were bought, and the martyrs: but the faith of the martyrs was proved; the blood is witness. What was spent on them, they returned, and fulfilled what holy John says: *As Christ laid down his soul for us, so we too ought to lay down our souls for the brothers*[^3]. And elsewhere it is said: *You have sat at a great table, consider diligently the things set before you, since you ought to prepare such things*[^4]. The table is great, where the delicacies are the very lord of the table. No one feeds his guests from himself: this the Lord Christ does; he himself is the inviter, he himself the food and drink. So the martyrs recognized what they were eating and drinking, that they might render such things.

**2.** But from where would they render such things, unless he gave from where they might render, who first spent? Hence too the Psalm, where we have sung what is written: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints,* what does it commend to us? There a man considered how much he received from God; he looked around at how many gifts of the grace of the Almighty who created him, who sought him lost, who gave pardon to him found, who helped him fighting with weak strength, who did not withdraw himself from him in danger, who crowned him conquering, who gave himself as the reward: he considered all these things, and cried out, and said: *What shall I render to the Lord for all the things he has rendered to me?*[^5] He did not want to be ungrateful, he wanted to repay, and what to repay he did not have. He did not say: *What shall I render to the Lord* for all the things he gave me; but, *for all the things he has rendered to me?* He did not give, but rendered. If he rendered, we had spent something. Plainly we had spent our evils, he rendered his goods: for he rendered good things for evils, while we had rendered evil things for ourselves. So he asks what to render; he suffers straits, from where to pay the debt he does not find: *What shall I render to the Lord for all the things he has rendered to me?* And as though he found what he might render: *I will take,* he says, *the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord*[^6]. What is this? Surely he was thinking to repay. Still he seeks to receive: *I will take the cup of salvation.* What is this cup? The cup of suffering, bitter and healthful: the cup which, unless the physician first drank it, the sick man would fear to touch. This is that cup: we recognize this cup in the mouth of Christ saying: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me*[^7]. For the sons of Zebedee too, through their mother, sought lofty places, that one of them might sit at the right, the other at the left: to whom he said: *Can you drink the cup, which I am to drink?*[^8] Do you seek the height? Through the valley one comes to the mountain. Do you seek the seat of brightness? First drink the cup of humility. Of that cup the martyrs said: *I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord.* Do you not fear, then, lest you fail there? No, he says. Why? Because *I will call upon the name of the Lord.* How would the martyrs conquer, unless he conquered in the martyrs, who said: *Rejoice, for I have conquered the world*[^9]? The Emperor of the heavens ruled their mind and tongue, and through them overcame the devil on earth, and crowned the martyrs in heaven. O blessed who so drank that cup! they ended their pains, and received honors. Attend, then, dearly beloved: what you cannot with your eyes, think with your mind and soul, and see that *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^10].
