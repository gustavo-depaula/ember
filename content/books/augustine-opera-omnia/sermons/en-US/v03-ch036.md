# Sermon 74: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 13:52: "Therefore Every Scribe Trained in the Kingdom of God," and What Follows

**1.** The Gospel reading admonishes us to seek and explain to your love, as far as the Lord grants, who is *the scribe trained in the kingdom of God, like a householder bringing forth from his treasure things new and old*[^1]. For in this the reading itself concluded: what are the new and old things of the trained scribe. But whom, according to the usage of our Scriptures, they called of old scribes, is well known, namely those who professed the knowledge of the Law. For such in that people were called scribes; not such as are called in the offices of judges, or in the custom of cities. For we ought not to enter the school in vain, but to know in what signification we hold the words of the Scriptures: lest, when something has sounded from the Scriptures, which is wont to be understood in another, secular use, the hearer go astray, and, thinking of what he is accustomed to, not understand what he heard. The scribes, then, were those who professed the knowledge of the Law, to whom the books of the Law pertained, whether to be guarded, or handled, or even written and understood.

**2.** Such our Lord Jesus Christ rebukes, that they have the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and neither enter themselves, nor permit others to enter[^2]; reproving, namely, the Pharisees and Scribes, the teachers of the Law of the Jews. Of whom in another place he says: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not do*[^3]. Why was it said to you: *For they say, and do not do*, except because there are certain ones of whom is shown what the Apostle says: *You who preach that one should not steal, you steal; you who say that one should not commit adultery, you commit adultery; you who abhor idols, you commit sacrilege; you who glory in the Law, by the transgression of the Law you dishonor God. For the name of God is blasphemed among the nations because of you*[^4]? Surely of these it is manifest that the Lord says: *For they say, and do not do.* Those, then, are scribes, but not trained in the kingdom of God.

**3.** Perhaps some of you may say: And how can a bad man speak good things; when it is written, the Lord himself saying: *A good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good things, and a bad man out of the bad treasure of his heart brings forth bad things*[^5]. *Hypocrites, how can you speak good things, when you are bad?*[^6] He says this: *How can you speak good things, when you are bad?* he says this: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not do.* If they say, and do not do, they are bad: if they are bad, they cannot speak good things: how do we do what we hear from them, when from them we cannot hear good things? How this is resolved, let your Holiness notice. Whatever a bad man brings forth of himself is bad; whatever a bad man brings forth out of his heart, is bad: for there is the bad treasure. But whatever a good man brings forth out of his heart, is good: for there is the good treasure. Whence, then, did those bad men bring forth good things? Because they sat in the chair of Moses. Unless he had said beforehand: *They sit in the chair of Moses*[^7]; he would never have commanded that the bad be heard. For it was one thing that they brought forth out of the bad treasure of their heart; another what, out of the chair of Moses, they sounded forth, as heralds of the judge. What a herald says will never be attributed to the herald, if he says it before the judge. It is one thing that a herald speaks in his own house, another that a herald speaks, hearing from the judge. For, whether he will or not, the herald sounds the voice of the punishment even of his friend. Likewise, whether he will or not, he sounds the voice of the acquittal even of his enemy. Give the voice from his heart: he acquits the friend, punishes the enemy. Give the voice from the seat of the judge: he punishes the friend, acquits the enemy. Give the voice of the Scribes from their heart: you will hear: *Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we shall die*[^8]. Give the voice from the chair of Moses: you will hear: *You shall not kill: You shall not commit adultery: You shall not steal: You shall not bear false witness: Honor your father and mother: You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^9]. This you do, what the chair sounds through the mouth of the Scribes: not what it sounds through the heart of the Scribes. For so, embracing both sentences of the Lord, you will neither be obedient in one and guilty in the other; but you understand that both agree, and you regard both that which is true, that *a good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good things, and a bad man out of the bad brings forth bad things;* and that, that those Scribes would not speak good things out of the bad treasure of their heart, but could speak good things out of the treasure of the chair of Moses.

**4.** Therefore those words of the Lord too will not disturb you, when he says: *Each tree is known by its fruit. Do they gather grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles?*[^10] So the Scribes and Pharisees of the Jews are thorns and thistles: and yet: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do.* Grapes, then, are gathered from thorns, and figs from thistles, as he gave you to understand according to the earlier discourse. For sometimes too in a thorny hedge of the vineyard the vines entangle themselves, and clusters hang from the bramble. Hearing the name of thorns, you are about to despise the grape. Seek the root of the thorns, and see where you find it. Follow the root of the hanging cluster, and see where you find it. So understand that one thing pertains to the heart of the Pharisee, and another to the chair of Moses.

**5.** But why are they such? Because *a veil*, he says, *is placed over their heart*[^11]. And they do not see that *the old things have passed away and all things have been made new*[^12]. Hence those are such, and whoever even now are such. Whence *old?* Because they are long since preached. Whence *new?* Because they pertain to the kingdom of God. How, then, the veil is taken away, the Apostle himself says: *But when you have passed over to the Lord, the veil will be taken away*[^13]. The Jew, then, not passing over to the Lord, does not direct the edge of his mind to the end. As at that time, in this figure, the children of Israel did not direct the edge of their eyes to the end, that is, to the face of Moses. For the shining face of Moses had the figure of the truth: a veil was interposed, because the children of Israel could not yet gaze on the splendor of his face[^14]. Which figure is done away. For so the Apostle said: *which are done away*[^15]. Why are they done away? Because when the emperor comes, the images are removed from the midst. There the image is gazed upon, where the emperor is not present: but where he is whose the image is, the image is removed. Images, then, were carried before, before our emperor, the Lord Jesus Christ, came. The images taken away, the presence of the emperor shines. So when anyone has passed over to the Lord, the veil will be taken away. For the voice of Moses sounded through the veil, and the face of Moses did not appear[^16]. So too now to the Jews the voice of Christ sounds through the voice of the old Scriptures: they hear their voice, they do not see the face of the one sounding. Do they wish, then, that the veil be taken away? Let them pass over to the Lord. For then the old things are not taken away, but stored in the treasure, that there may now be a scribe trained in the kingdom of God, bringing forth from his treasure neither new things alone, nor old things alone. For if he brings forth new things alone, or old things alone, he is not a scribe trained in the kingdom of God, bringing forth from his treasure things new and old. If he says them, and does not do them, he brings forth from the chair, not from the treasure of his heart. And we say a true thing to your Holiness; the things that are brought forth from the old are illustrated through the new. Therefore one passes over to the Lord, that the veil may be taken away.
