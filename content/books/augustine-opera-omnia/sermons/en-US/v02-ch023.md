# Sermon 35

**1** If the divine utterances are not heard carelessly, that which is written may perhaps not without reason disturb us: *Son, if you are wise, you will be wise for yourself and for your neighbors; but if you turn out evil, you alone will drink in evils*[^1]. For how can this be rightly understood? Since, just as the good life of a neighbor gladdens us, so too his perverse life saddens us. Or if this is thought to be said on account of persuasion—because the wise man is wise both for himself and for those to whom he persuades wisdom—how is it that if he turns out evil he alone will drink in evils, when of the persuasions of such men it is said: *Evil conversations corrupt good morals*[^2]? For what else does that voice of love cry: *If one member is glorified, all the members rejoice together; and if one member suffers, all the members suffer together*[^3]? How, then, is it true: *Son, if you are wise, you will be wise for yourself and for your neighbors; but if you turn out evil, you alone will drink in evils*[^4]? How shall I rejoice in the good of one in whose evil I will not be in mourning? How does the one delight me when found who could perish while I am secure? Surely if he is wise, he will be a healthy member, in whom the other members rejoice together? How, then, will the evil man alone drink in evils, when the other members suffer together likewise with a sick member?

**2** This question, accordingly, unless it be resolved, will disturb. But with the Lord's help it will be resolved, if first we hold this, defined by most certain truth, fixed and immovable: that no one can be good by another's good, nor evil by another's evil. For hence the Apostle says: *Each one of us will bear his own burden*[^5]. And elsewhere: *Therefore each one of us will render an account for himself*[^6]. And again: *But let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself alone, and not in another*[^7]. This too is said through the prophet Ezekiel: *The soul of the father is mine, and the soul of the son is mine; the soul that sins, it shall die*[^8]. He so explains that whole passage as to show that evil sons are not relieved by the good deeds of their parents, nor good sons oppressed by their evil deeds. This most truthful sentence having been first and most firmly established on account of ourselves, it remains to inspect what office we render to others, distinguishing most diligently which effect we seek for our own salvation, which affection we render to our neighbors. If you are good, you are good not by another's good, but by your own. Yet by that very good of yours by which you are good, you also rejoice together at another's good—not by borrowed goodness, but by mutual love. Likewise, if you are evil, you are evil not by another's evil, but by your own. By that same evil of yours, too, you do not love your neighbor as yourself. For neither do you love yourself. For you love iniquity, your most bitter enemy, not applied to you from without, but made by you within you for yourself. And that it may conquer you more easily, you favor it against yourself. So you are plainly convicted of hating yourself, when you love that by which you are shamefully conquered. For the divine utterance would not be deceived, in which it is said: *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^9].

**3** Hence, then, it is that the good man, by that good by which he is good, both is glad at another's good and is saddened at another's evil. And through this, using such a neighbor—since he is more truly called a neighbor who attends to you out of nearness, that is, looks upon you mercifully—using, then, such a neighbor, if you are wise, you will be wise both for yourself and for him. Not because he will be good by your good, but because by his own good he will be a lover of your good. *But if you turn out evil*, you will drink in evils not with him but alone. For he will not be worse by your evil, but will be merciful in your evil. Your malice saddens him, but his own punishment does not follow. It inflicts sadness on him, it does not share injustice with him. And so, evil, you will drink in evils not with your neighbor's good, but alone, because that sadness which the good man has about you, he has by his own good, and by your evil. That sadness indicates his love, your perdition. It condemns you, it crowns him; it depresses you, it raises him. Therefore it is also written: *Be obedient to those set over you; for they keep watch over your souls as those who will render an account, that they may do this with joy and not with sadness; for that is not expedient for you*[^10]. For you, then, it is not expedient to be weighed down by their sadness. For it is expedient for them to be saddened over your injustice. Reckon, then, your neighbors good, and be good—by your own good, indeed, not theirs, and that not given to you by yourself, but imparted from God. *For what do you have that you did not receive?*[^11] And so, if *you are wise, you will be wise for yourself and for your neighbors*, for whom it is good to be nourished by your goodness. *But if you turn out evil, you alone will drink in evils*; not also those for whom this too is good, to be saddened by your malice.
