# Sermon 185: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1.** It is called the Lord's birthday, when the Wisdom of God showed himself an infant, and the Word of God, without words, sent forth the voice of flesh. Yet that hidden divinity was both signified to the Magi by the witness of a star and announced to the shepherds by an angel's voice. This day, then, we celebrate with yearly solemnity, on which was fulfilled the prophecy that says: *truth has sprung up from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven*[^1]. Truth, which is in the bosom of the Father, sprang up from the earth, that it might be also in the bosom of a mother. Truth, by which the world is held together, sprang up from the earth, that it might be carried by a woman's hands. Truth, by which the blessedness of the angels is incorruptibly fed, sprang up from the earth, that it might be nursed at fleshly breasts. Truth, for which heaven does not suffice, sprang up from the earth, that it might be laid in a manger. For whose good did such sublimity come in such humility? For none of its own, surely; but for ours, great indeed, if we believe. Awake, O man: for you God became man. *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you*[^2]. For you, I say, God became man. You would have been dead forever, had he not been born in time. You would never have been freed from the flesh of sin, had he not taken up the likeness of sinful flesh. Everlasting misery would have possessed you, had this mercy not come to be. You would not have come back to life, had he not met your death. You would have failed, had he not come to your aid. You would have perished, had he not come.

**2.** Let us joyfully celebrate the coming of our salvation and redemption. Let us celebrate the festal day on which the great and eternal day came from the great and eternal day into this our day, so brief and temporal. He *was made for us righteousness and sanctification and redemption, so that, as it is written, Let him who boasts, boast in the Lord*[^3]. For so that we might not be like the proud Jews, who, *being ignorant of the righteousness of God and wishing to establish their own, did not submit to the righteousness of God*[^4]: therefore, when he had said, *truth has sprung up from the earth*, he at once added, *and righteousness has looked down from heaven* — lest mortal weakness should claim it for itself, lest a man should call it his own and, believing himself justified by himself, that is, made righteous by himself, should reject the righteousness of God. *Truth*, then, *has sprung up from the earth*: Christ, who said, *I am the truth*[^5], was born of a virgin. *And righteousness has looked down from heaven*: because one who believes in him who was born is justified not by himself, but by God. *Truth has sprung up from the earth*: because *the Word was made flesh*[^6]. *And righteousness has looked down from heaven*: because *every good endowment and every perfect gift is from above*[^7]. *Truth has sprung up from the earth*, flesh from Mary. *And righteousness has looked down from heaven*: because *a man can receive nothing unless it is given him from heaven*[^8].

**3.** Being justified, then, by faith, let us have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ; through whom we also have access to this grace in which we stand, and we boast in the hope of the glory of God[^9]. To these things, brothers, which you recognize along with me in a few words of the Apostle, it is a delight to add a few words of this psalm, and to find a harmony. Being justified by faith, let us have peace with God: because *righteousness and peace have kissed each other*[^10]. Through our Lord Jesus Christ: because truth has sprung up from the earth. Through whom we also have access to this grace in which we stand, and we boast in the hope of the glory of God. He did not say, "of our glory," but, of the glory of God: because righteousness did not proceed from us, but looked down from heaven. Therefore let him who boasts boast not in himself but in the Lord. For from this also, when the Lord was born of the Virgin, whose birthday we celebrate today, the proclamation of the angelic voice was made: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^11]. For whence is peace on earth, unless because truth has sprung up from the earth, that is, Christ was born of flesh? And he himself is *our peace, who made the two one*[^12]: that we might be men of good will, sweetly bound together by the bonds of unity. In this grace, then, let us rejoice, that our glory may be *the testimony of our conscience*[^13]: where we boast not in ourselves but in the Lord. For from this it is said: *My glory, and the one who lifts up my head*[^14]. For what greater grace of God could shine upon us than that, having an only-begotten Son, he should make him the son of a man, and so in turn make the son of a man the Son of God? Seek the merit, seek the cause, seek the righteousness; and see whether you find anything but grace.
