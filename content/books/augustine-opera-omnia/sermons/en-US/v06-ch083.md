# Sermon 229/N: A Tractate on the Saturday of the Octave of Holy Easter

**1.** Behold, the Lord, appearing again to the disciples after the resurrection, questions the apostle Peter; and he compels him to confess love three times, who three times had denied through fear. Christ rose in the flesh, and Peter in the spirit; for when Christ died by suffering, Peter had died by denying. The Lord Christ is raised from the dead; he raised up Peter from his love. He questioned the love of the one confessing, and entrusted to him his sheep. For what could Peter offer Christ, in that he loved Christ? If Christ love you, it profits you, not Christ; and if you love Christ, it profits you, not Christ. Yet the Lord Christ, willing to show where men ought to show that they love Christ, opened it clearly enough by entrusting it in his sheep. *Do you love me?* I love. *Feed my sheep*[^1]. This once, this a second time, this a third time. Nothing else did Peter answer, but that he loved him; nothing else did the Lord ask, but whether he loved him; nothing else did he entrust to the one answering, but his sheep. Let us love, and we love Christ. For Christ, always God, was born a man in time. As man from a human being, he appeared as a man to men; he did many wonders, as God in a man. He suffered many evils, as a man from men; he rose after death, as God in a man. He dwelt on earth forty days, as a man with men; before their eyes he ascended into heaven, as God in a man, he sits at the right hand of the Father. All this we believe, we do not see; and we are bidden to love the Lord Christ, whom we do not see; and we all cry out and say: I love Christ. *If you do not love the brother whom you see, how can you love God whom you do not see?*[^2] By loving the sheep, show that you have the love of the shepherd; for the sheep too are members of the shepherd. That the sheep might be his members, he deigned to be a sheep; that the sheep might be his members, *he was led like a sheep to the slaughter*[^3]; that the sheep might be his members, of him it is said: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^4]. But great is the strength in this Lamb. Do you wish to know how great a strength appeared in this Lamb? The Lamb was crucified, and the lion was conquered. See and consider with what power the Lord Christ governs the world, who by his death conquered the devil.

**2.** Let us love him, then; let nothing be dearer to us than he. Do you think, then, that the Lord does not question us? Did Peter alone deserve to be questioned, and not we? When that reading is read, each Christian is questioned in his heart. When, then, you hear the Lord saying: *Peter, do you love me?* consider it a mirror, and attend to yourself there. For what else did Peter himself bear but the figure of the Church? When the Lord, then, questioned Peter, he questioned us, he questioned the Church. For, that you may know that Peter bore the figure of the Church, recall that place in the Gospel: *You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church; and the gates of hell will not conquer it; to you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven*[^5]. One man receives them. For what the keys of the kingdom of heaven are, he himself explained: *Whatever you bind on earth will be bound also in heaven; and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed also in heaven*[^6]. If it was said to Peter alone, Peter alone did this; he died, and departed; who, then, binds, who looses? I dare to say it: we too have these keys. And what shall I say? That we bind, that we loose? You bind too, and you loose too. For he who is bound is separated from your fellowship; and when he is separated from your fellowship, he is bound by you; and when he is reconciled, he is loosed by you, because God too is asked by you on his behalf.

**3.** For we all love Christ, we are his members; and when he entrusts the sheep to the shepherds, the whole number of shepherds is brought back to the body of one shepherd. For, that you may know that the whole number of shepherds is brought back to the one body of the one shepherd: certainly Peter is a shepherd, plainly a shepherd; Paul is a shepherd, so indeed a shepherd; John is a shepherd, James a shepherd, Andrew a shepherd, and the rest of the Apostles shepherds. All the holy bishops are certainly shepherds, so plainly. And how is it true: *And there will be one flock and one shepherd*[^7]? Further, if it is true: *There will be one flock and one shepherd,* the whole countless number of shepherds is brought back to the body of one shepherd. But there you are too, you are his members. He was trampling those very members, when the Head cried out for his members—Saul, that former persecutor, afterward a preacher, breathing slaughter, deferring faith. By one voice his whole onslaught was laid low. By what voice? *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^8] Could Saul throw even a stone up to heaven, where Jesus sits? Suppose Saul had been in the crowd, when Jesus hung on the wood. Suppose Saul too had said with the crowd: *Crucify, crucify*[^9]; and had been among those who wagged their head in mockery, and said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*[^10]. What would he do to one sitting in heaven? What harm would the word do? What harm the shout? What harm the wood? What harm the lance? Nothing could now be done to him, and yet he cried out: *You persecute me.* When he cried out: *You persecute me,* he showed that we are his members. By the love of Christ, then, whom we love in you; by the love of Christ, whom you too love in us, amid temptations, amid labors, amid sweats, amid anxieties, amid miseries, amid groans, he will lead us where there is no labor, no misery, no groan, no sighing, no trouble, where no one is born, no one dies, no one fears the wrath of the powerful, while clinging to the face of the Almighty.

[^2]: 1 John 4:20
[^3]: Isa 53:7
[^4]: John 1:29
[^5]: Matt 16:18-19
[^6]: Matt 18:18
[^7]: John 10:16
[^8]: Acts 9:4
[^9]: Luke 23:21
[^10]: See Matt 27:40
