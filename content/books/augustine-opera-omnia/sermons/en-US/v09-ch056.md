# Sermon 335/K: On the Anniversary of the Burial of a Bishop

**1.** This solemnity, my brothers, is in honor of God on account of a servant of God. For a servant, when he is rightly honored, is honored in the name of his Lord. So the blessed . . . servant of God, today laid down the burden of his flesh and committed to the earth his flesh that will rise again. Earth was rendered to earth, the spirit to the Spirit of God. But the laying down of the body is to each man such as he was in the body. For death is a certain middle thing, which in itself is neither good nor bad; for when the last day of this life comes, it distinguishes and separates two things that had been joined, the invisible soul from the visible flesh, the perceiving soul from the flesh in which the soul perceives, because flesh without the soul perceives nothing. So these two joined things, very unlike one another, soul and flesh, separated make death. Accordingly death, which disjoins and separates these two, seems in itself neither good nor bad, but to the good it is good and to the bad it is bad.

**2.** You heard the Apostle saying: *What I should choose I do not know. But I am pressed by two, having the desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ: for that is much the best; but to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^1]. One thing is good, another is necessary. "Good" has the name of goodness, "necessary" has the name of necessity. If all were learned, what need was there for the apostle Paul to be held longer in this life? But because many were to be built up, the architect was held, who knew most skillfully to lay the foundation Christ in the hearts of believers. So too the blessed . . . dispensed the word and sacrament of God as long as the Lord willed. But when it pleased the householder to call his servant out of the house of clay and transfer him to heaven, the clay house itself too was committed to the earth, and that clay house awaits the resurrection when it will see the Creator. *For the day will come,* as the Lord says, *when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice, and those who did well will come forth to the resurrection of life, but those who did evil to the resurrection of judgment*[^2]. Life is to be loved, judgment to be feared. Here choose what you love, avoid what you fear; for no one avoids the judgment by fearing it, but by living well. For what does it profit to fear and to live badly? If you live badly and fear, you will come to what you love, but do you escape what you fear?

**3.** Here, then, is the contest, this life is a theater to God watching: here is the battle, here the conflict with all the vices and especially with the prince of the vices, as with Goliath. For the devil challenges the soul as it were to single combat. He is conquered when one stands, but in the name of the Lord, not in the strength of the warrior. Whatever evil and unlawful thing is suggested to your heart, whatever malignant desire has arisen against your mind from your flesh, these are the darts of that enemy, who challenges you to single combat. Remember that you are fighting. The enemy is invisible, but invisible too is your protector. You do not see with whom you contend, but you believe in him by whom you are protected. And if you have the eyes of faith, you see him too: for every faithful one sees with the eyes of faith the adversary daily challenging. But from where do you fight? From where, do you think? Behold, as David did. From the river he chose five smooth stones. The river is the passing of temporal things: in this passing of temporal things, the human soul received the law from God. And since the law is first contained in the five books of Moses, he chose five stones from the river. But they were called smooth, because he who used them was smooth, because he was gentle, because he was meek, because he was submissive: *For my yoke is smooth, and my burden is light*[^3]. But what does the law profit if there is no grace?

**4.** Therefore he put those five stones into a pail of milk, because milk signifies grace, because it is given freely. The woman receives food: the things necessary for the support of the body pass into the juice of the flesh, the rest, superfluous, are discharged by their own ways, the juice of milk runs to the breasts that it may freely feed the hungry little ones. For the mother has full breasts and seeks lips into which to pour, and the milk, if no one sucks it, burdens the mother. So the holy ones of God have their breasts full of the grace of God: they seek into whom to pour it. And the Lord himself, to nurse us as it were, see what he did. His wisdom, which none of us could comprehend with the mind as it were solid food, but the angels are fit to receive it, for the angels live on that very food: but men cannot use such food, because they are weak, what then did God do, but what a godly mother? Because a little infant cannot take solid food, the mother transfers the solidity of the food into flesh, and as it were makes it flesh that it may be fit for the infant. So God made the Word flesh that it might be fit for us little ones. Behold solid food: *In the beginning was the Word and the Word was God*[^4]. This solidity the angels eat: they feed and are refreshed, and there is no failing of that on which they feed. Who of us could approach, with the weakness of this mind and flesh, to feed there on what was said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing. What was made, in him is life: And the life was the light of men, and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*[^5]? To this solidity what human weakness can approach? What ease of ours can take it in? But do not be frightened, O throng of little ones! *The Word became flesh, and dwelt among us*[^6].

**5.** Therefore, to signify this grace, the servant of God put five stones into the pail in which he was accustomed to receive the milk when he milked, and he went forth to the fight armed with grace, and the more unconquered the more faithful. That man *cursed by his gods*[^7], this man did not return a curse for a curse, but that man spoke, this man presumed. Worthy were those by whom he might be cursed, worthy is our God by whom one might presume. With one stone he laid low so monstrous and terrible an enemy. He received it on the forehead, and fell, where he did not have the sign of grace. David ran, stood over the prostrate but not yet utterly extinguished—he lay but still somehow lived—and with that man's own sword he slew the remnants of life. And this signifies something. My brothers, by the first dispensation of our Lord Jesus Christ, as of that spiritual David who had come from the family of David, our enemy received it on the forehead, and was laid low. There lay all the superstition of the gentiles, which could not afterward raise itself against the Church of God, because even when it raised itself, then it was acting, but martyrdom was being crowned. Then, the Church advancing, since that Goliath was carrying a great broadsword or blade, that is, a most monstrous sword, the eloquence of this world which subjected many minds to itself, many servants of God learned even that eloquence, that Goliath might be killed by his own sword. How eloquent was holy Cyprian, how gleaming his blade appeared in his letters. It is the sword of Goliath, but wrested from him already lying, that the enemy might be slain. With that very eloquence we daily fight with Goliath, and would that it might befall us, with him slain, to triumph. But daily from his remnants he fights in the hearts of men: let us in the name of the Lord overcome him.

**6.** Let no one, my brothers, absolutely no one contend with any vice in his heart and presume on himself. Do not be negligent in fighting, but neither proud in presuming. Whatever it is that moves you, whether from ignorance or from desire, come to the fight, do not be sluggish, but call upon the spectator that he may help the laborer. So you conquer. Otherwise you do not conquer because it is not you who conquer. For did David conquer? Attend to his words and see that he himself did not conquer. For he says: *The battle is God's*[^8]. What is it to say: *This battle is God's,* but: God fights through me? Of myself he uses as it were his vessel; he himself lays low the enemy, he himself frees the people, he himself gives the glory, not to us but to his name. So by fighting thus and confessing him, we securely end this life, and, the contest finished, we rest in the bosom of holy rest, where the blessed . . . rests, surely after grave contests, surely after marvelous battles. For a man fights, sometimes with no other man seeing. For no other man sees in your heart what thoughts you suffer, under what suggestions you are in danger, by what desires you are goaded. Some things flatter, some terrify: it is to be feared lest you be caught by those flattering, lest you be broken by those terrifying. In this contest what remains but to say: *In the name of the Lord my God I will overcome him*[^9]; in this contest what remains to say but: *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory*[^10]? If you hold these things, having the law in the pail of milk, you will be unconquered. Easily will be laid low whatever struggles against you, that he who appointed you the contest may watch you contending and help you laboring and crown you conquering.
