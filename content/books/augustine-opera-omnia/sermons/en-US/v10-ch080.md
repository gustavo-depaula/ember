# Sermon 393: On Penitents

**1.** Penitents, penitents, penitents (if indeed you are penitents, and not mockers), change your life, be reconciled to God. For you too are feeding with a chain. With what chain, you say? *What you bind on earth shall be bound also in heaven*[^1]. You hear of a binding, and do you think to play a fraud on God? You do penance, you bend the knee, and you laugh, and mock the patience of God? If you are a penitent, let it grieve you: if it does not grieve you, you are not a penitent. If, then, it grieves you, why do you do what you did badly? If it grieves you to have done it, do not do it. If you still do it, surely you are not a penitent. Indeed, dearest ones, men fall sick, and they send to the church, or are carried to the church, and they are baptized, and renewed, and from here they will be happy. But that is not the case of penance. He who has not yet received Baptism has not yet violated the Sacrament: but he who has violated the Sacrament by living badly and ruinously, and for that reason has been removed from the altar, lest he eat and drink judgment to himself; let him change his life, correct himself, and be reconciled while he lives, while he is well. Does he too then wait to be reconciled, when he begins to die? We have known by experience that many have breathed their last while waiting to be reconciled. Then I also say, in the sight of God, to your fear, my fear. But he who does not fear despises me who fear, but to his own harm. Hear, then. I am certain that a baptized man, if he has led a life—I do not dare to say without sin, for who is without sin?—but a life without crime, and has had such sins as are daily forgiven to the one praying, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^2]; when he has finished his day, he does not finish his life, but passes from life to life, from a toilsome to a quiet, from a wretched to a blessed: whether such a one runs of his own will to Baptism, or, set in peril, is baptized and departs from this life, he goes to the Lord, he goes to rest. But the baptized man who is a deserter and violator of so great a Sacrament, if he does penance from his whole heart, if he does penance where God sees—who saw the heart of David, when, rebuked by the prophet, and rebuked gravely, after the terrible threats of God he cried out, saying: *I have sinned*; and at once heard: *The Lord has taken away your sin*[^3]. So much do three syllables avail. There are three syllables: *I have sinned*; but in these three syllables the flame of the sacrifice of the heart ascended to heaven. Therefore he who has truly done penance, and has been loosed from the bond by which he was bound and separated from the body of Christ, and has lived well after his penance, as he ought to have lived before his penance: after reconciliation, whenever he dies, he goes to God, he goes to rest, he will not be deprived of the kingdom of God, he will be separated from the people of the devil. But if anyone, set in the last necessity of his sickness, wishes to receive penance, and receives it, and is at once reconciled, and departs hence; I confess to you, we do not deny him what he asks, but we do not presume that he departs hence well. I do not presume: I do not deceive you, I do not presume. The faithful man living well departs hence secure. The man baptized at the hour departs hence secure. The man doing penance, and reconciled while he is well, and afterward living well, departs hence secure. The man doing penance at the last and reconciled—if he departs hence secure, I am not secure. Where I am secure, I am secure, and I give security: where I am not secure, I can give penance, I cannot give security. (But let someone say: Good priest, you say that you do not know and can give us no security whether he is saved, and deserves to come to Christ, to whom penance is given as he dies, who while he lived, while he was well, was impenitent; instruct us, then, I pray, how we ought to live well after penance. I say: abstain from drunkenness, from concupiscence, from theft, and from evil-speaking, from immoderate laughter, from idle words, for which men will render an account on the day of judgment. Behold how light the things I have said. Yet all are grave and pestilent. And I say another thing: not only after penance ought a man to keep himself from these vices, but also before penance, while he is well; because if he waits until the last of his life, he does not know whether he will be able to receive that very penance and confess his sins to God and to the priest. Behold why I said that one must live well both before penance, and better after penance.) Attend to what I say: I ought to expound it more plainly, lest anyone understand me badly. Do I say: He will be condemned? I do not say it. But neither do I say: He will be freed. And what do you say to me? I do not know: I do not presume, I do not promise; I do not know. Do you wish to free yourself from doubt? Do you wish to escape what is uncertain? Do penance while you are well. For if you do true penance while you are well, and the last day finds you, run to be reconciled; if you do so, you are secure. Why are you secure? Because you did penance at the time when you could also have sinned. But if you wish to do that very penance then, when you can no longer sin; the sins have left you, not you them. But whence do you know, you say, whether perhaps God may forgive me? You say true. Whence, I do not know. That I know, this I do not know. For this reason I give you penance, because I do not know. For if I knew it would profit you nothing, I would not give it to you. Likewise, if I knew it would profit you, I would not admonish you, I would not frighten you. There are two things: either you are pardoned, or you are not pardoned: which of these will befall you, I do not know. Therefore let go the uncertain, hold the certain.

[^1]: Matt 16:19
[^2]: Matt 6:12
[^3]: 2 Sam 12:13
