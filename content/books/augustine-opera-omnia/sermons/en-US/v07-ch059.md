# Sermon 272: On the Day of Pentecost, to the Infants, on the Sacrament.

**1.** This which you see on the altar of God, you saw also the past night: but what it was, what it meant, what great matter's sacred sign it contained, you had not yet heard. What you see, then, is bread and the cup; which your eyes too report to you: but what your faith asks to be instructed about, the bread is the body of Christ, the cup the blood of Christ. This indeed has been said briefly, which perhaps suffices for faith: but faith desires instruction. For the prophet says: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^1]. For you can now say to me: You commanded that we believe, expound that we may understand. For such a thought can arise in anyone's mind: Our Lord Jesus Christ, we know from where he took flesh; from the Virgin Mary. He was suckled as an infant, was nourished, grew, was brought to a youthful age, suffered persecution from the Jews, was hung on the wood, was killed on the wood, was taken down from the wood, was buried, rose on the third day, on the day he willed ascended into heaven; thither he lifted his body; from there he is to come to judge the living and the dead; there he now is, sitting at the right hand of the Father: how is the bread his body? and the cup, or what the cup holds, how is it his blood? These things, brothers, are therefore called sacred signs, because in them one thing is seen, another understood. What is seen has a bodily appearance, what is understood has a spiritual fruit. If, then, you wish to understand the body of Christ, hear the Apostle saying to the faithful: *But you are the body of Christ, and members*[^2]. If, then, you are the body of Christ and members, your mystery is set on the Lord's table: you receive your mystery. To that which you are, you answer Amen, and by answering you subscribe to it. For you hear, The body of Christ; and you answer, Amen. Be a member of the body of Christ, that the Amen may be true. Why, then, in bread? Let us bring nothing here of our own, let us hear the Apostle himself again and again, who, when he spoke of this sacred sign, said: *One bread, one body, we many are*[^3]: understand and rejoice; unity, truth, piety, love. *One bread*: who is this one bread? *One body, we many.* Recall that bread is not made from one grain, but from many. When you were exorcised, you were as it were ground. When you were baptized, you were as it were moistened. When you received the fire of the Holy Spirit, you were as it were baked. Be what you see, and receive what you are. This the Apostle said about the bread. Now what we should understand about the cup, even though not said, he sufficiently shows. For as, that the visible appearance of bread may exist, many grains are moistened into one, as though that came to pass which the holy Scripture says of the faithful: *They had one soul, and one heart toward God*[^4]: so too with the wine. Brothers, recall from what wine is made. Many grains hang on the cluster, but the liquor of the grains is mingled into unity. So too the Lord Christ signified us, willed us to belong to him, consecrated on his table the sacred sign of our peace and unity. He who receives the sacred sign of unity, and does not keep the bond of peace, receives not a sacred sign for himself, but a testimony against himself. Turned to the Lord God the Father almighty, let us with a pure heart give him, as far as our littleness can, the greatest and truest thanks; praying with our whole soul his singular gentleness, that he may condescend to hear our prayers in his good pleasure; that he may by his power also expel the enemy from our acts and thoughts, multiply our faith, govern our mind, grant us spiritual thoughts, and lead us to his blessedness: through Jesus Christ his Son. Amen.

[^1]: Isa 7:9 (Old Latin)
[^2]: 1 Cor 12:27
[^3]: 1 Cor 10:17
[^4]: Acts 4:32
