# Sermon 295: On the Birthday of the Apostles Peter and Paul

**1. 1.** The suffering of the most blessed Apostles Peter and Paul consecrated this day for us. We are not speaking of obscure martyrs. *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*. These martyrs saw what they preached, following equity, confessing truth, and dying for truth. Blessed Peter, first of the Apostles, a fervent lover of Christ, deserved to hear: *And I tell you that you are Peter.* For he himself had said: *You are the Christ, the living God's Son.* Christ told him: *And I tell you that you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church*. Upon this rock I will build the faith you confess. Upon what you said, *You are the Christ, the living God's Son,* I will build my Church. For you are Peter. Peter comes from 'rock,' not 'rock' from Peter. Peter comes from 'rock' as Christian comes from Christ. Do you want to know from what rock Peter is named? Hear Paul: *I do not want* *you to be unaware, brothers,* Christ's Apostle says, *I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea; all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and sea; all ate the same spiritual food and drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual rock following them, and the rock was Christ*. See where Peter's name comes from.

**2. 2.** Before his Passion, as you know, the Lord Jesus chose his disciples, whom he called Apostles. Among them, almost everywhere Peter alone deserved to bear the person of the whole Church. Because of that person of the whole Church that he alone bore, he deserved to hear: *I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven*. For not one man but the Church's unity received these keys. Peter's excellence is therefore proclaimed from this: he represented the Church's universality and unity when he was told, *I entrust to you* what was entrusted to all. To know that the Church received the keys of the kingdom of heaven, hear in another passage what the Lord says to all his Apostles: *Receive the Holy Spirit.* And immediately: *If you forgive anyone's sins, they will be forgiven; if you retain anyone's, they will be retained*. This concerns the keys of which it was said: *Whatever you release on earth will also be released in heaven*; *and whatever you bind on earth will also be bound in heaven.* But he said this to Peter. To know that Peter then bore the person of the universal Church, hear what is said to that Church, to all the holy faithful: *If your brother sins against you, correct him between yourself and him alone. If he does not hear you, take one or two with you, for it is written: Every word will stand on the testimony of two or three witnesses. If he does not hear them either, refer it to the Church; if he does not hear the Church either, regard him as a gentile and tax collector. I tell you truly: whatever you bind on earth will also be bound in heaven, and whatever you release on earth will also be released in heaven*. The dove binds, the dove releases; the building upon the rock binds and releases.

**3. 2.** Let the bound fear; let the released fear. Let those released fear being bound; let those bound pray to be released. *Each person is constrained by the cords of his sins*, and outside this Church nothing is released. The man dead four days is told: *Lazarus, come outside!* He came from the tomb with hands and feet bound in wrappings. The Lord awakens him so that the dead man comes from the tomb, as he touches the heart so that confession of sin comes outside. But this is little; he is still bound. Therefore, after Lazarus came from the tomb, the Lord told his disciples, to whom he had said, *Whatever you release on earth will also be released in heaven*: *Release,* he said, *him and let him go*. He awakened him himself; through his disciples he released him.

**3. 3.** Therefore, the Church's strength was especially commended in Peter, because he followed the Lord going to his Passion; a certain weakness was also marked, because when questioned by a maidservant he denied the Lord. See, that lover suddenly a denier! The one who presumed on himself discovered himself. For as you know, he had said: *Lord, I will be with you unto death; if I must die, I will lay down my life for you.* The Lord told the presumptuous man: *Will you lay down your life for me? I tell you truly: before the rooster crows, you will deny me three times*. What the physician foretold happened; what the sick man presumed could not happen. But then what? Immediately the Lord looked at him. So it is written, so the Gospel speaks: *The Lord looked at him, and he went outside and wept bitterly*. *He went outside:* that is, to confess. *He wept bitterly,* the one who knew how to love. Sweetness followed in love, where bitterness had preceded in sorrow.

**4. 4.** Rightly, after the Resurrection, the Lord also entrusted his sheep to Peter himself to be fed. For Peter was not the only disciple who deserved to feed the Lord's sheep; but when Christ speaks to one, unity is commended, and to Peter first because Peter is first among the Apostles. *Simon, son of John,* the Lord says, *do you love me?* He answered: *I love you.* Questioned again, he answered again. Questioned a third time, as though not believed, he becomes sad. But how could the one who saw his heart not believe him? Finally, after that sadness, he answered: *Lord, you know everything; you know I love you.* For you who know everything are not ignorant of this alone. Do not be sad, Apostle. Answer once, twice, a third time. Let confession conquer three times in love, because presumption was conquered three times in fear. What you bound three times must be released three times. Release through love what you bound through fear. Yet once, twice, and a third time the Lord entrusted his sheep to Peter.

**5. 5.** Pay attention, my brothers: *Feed,* he says, *my little sheep, feed my lambs*. *Feed my sheep:* did he say yours? Good slave, feed the Lord's sheep, bearing the Lord's mark. For *was* *Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name* of Peter and *Paul?* Therefore, feed his sheep, washed in his baptism, marked with his name, redeemed by his blood. *Feed,* he says, *my sheep.* For heretics, wicked and runaway slaves, dividing among themselves what they did not buy and making private possessions from stolen things as though their own, seem to themselves to feed their own sheep. What else, I ask you, does this mean: 'Unless I baptize you, you will be unclean; unless you have my baptism, you will not be washed'? Have you not heard: *Cursed is everyone who places his hope in a human being*? Therefore, beloved, those Peter baptized are Christ's sheep, and those Judas baptized are Christ's sheep. See what the bridegroom says to his beloved in the Song of Songs, when the bride told him: *Tell me, you whom my soul has loved, where you feed, where you rest at noon, lest I become like one veiled beside your companions' flocks. Tell,* she says, *me where you feed and rest at noon,* in truth's splendor, in love's fervor. Why are you afraid, beloved? What do you fear? *Lest I become,* she says, *like one veiled,* that is, obscure, as though not the Church, because the Church is not veiled. For *a city* *set upon a mountain cannot be hidden*. *And* wandering, *I may stumble* not upon your flock, but *upon your companions' flocks.* For heretics are called companions. *They went out from us*; before going out, they approached the same table with us. What answer, then, is given her? *Unless you know yourself*—the bridegroom speaks, answering her question—*unless you know yourself, beautiful among women.* O truthful one among heresies, *unless you know yourself,* because so much was foretold about you: *In your seed all nations will be blessed*; the God of gods, the Lord, spoke and called the earth from sunrise to sunset; *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance and earth's ends as your possession*; *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*. These testimonies were foretold about you. Therefore, *unless* *you know yourself, go out yourself.* For I do not cast you out, so that those remaining may say of you: *They went out from us. Go out yourself in the tracks of flocks,* not of the flock of which it was said: *There will be one flock and one shepherd*. *Go out yourself in the tracks of flocks and feed your kids*, not, as Peter, *my sheep.* For these sheep entrusted to him, Peter deserved to be crowned with martyrdom, which deserved to be celebrated throughout the world in today's solemnity.

**6. 6.** Let Paul too come from Saul, a lamb from a wolf: first enemy, then Apostle; first persecutor, then preacher. Let him come and receive letters from the chief priests to bring Christians bound for punishment wherever he finds them. Let him receive them, receive them; set out, proceed, pant for slaughter, thirst for blood: *The one dwelling in heaven will laugh at him*. For he went, as it is written, *breathing slaughter,* and approached Damascus. Then the Lord from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* I am here; I am there: here the head, there the body. Therefore, let us not marvel, brothers; we belong to Christ's body. *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad.* You harm yourself, for my Church grows through persecutions. But trembling and frightened, he said: *Lord, who are you?* And he: *I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute.* Immediately changed, he awaits a command; he lays aside envy and prepares obedience. He is told what to do. Before Paul is baptized, the Lord speaks to Ananias: *Go to that street, to that man named Saul, and baptize him, because he is a chosen vessel for me.* A vessel should carry something; it should not be empty. The vessel must be filled—with what, except grace? But Ananias answered our Lord Jesus Christ: *Lord, I have heard that this man has done many evils to your saints. Now he carries letters from the chief priests to bring bound wherever he finds men of this way*. And the Lord told him: *I will show him what he must suffer for my name*. Ananias trembled at hearing Saul's name; the weak sheep trembled at the wolf's reputation, even beneath the shepherd's hand.

**7. 7.** See, the Lord showed him what he must suffer for his name. Afterward he trained him through toil: in chains, beatings, prisons, and shipwrecks. He himself arranged his suffering and brought him to this day. One day of suffering for two Apostles; and those two were one. Although they suffered on different days, they were one. Peter went before; Paul followed. First Saul, then Paul, because first proud, then humble. Saul was named from Saul, holy David's persecutor. The persecutor was cast down; the preacher raised up. He changed pride's name for humility. For Paul means small. Consider Your Charity's own words: do we not say daily, 'I will see you after a little while; a little later I will do this or that'? What, then, is Paul? Ask him himself. *I am,* he says, *the least of the Apostles*.

**8. 8.** We celebrate a feast day consecrated for us by the Apostles' blood. Let us love their faith, life, labors, sufferings, confessions, and preaching. For we advance by loving, not by celebrating these things for fleshly enjoyment. What do the martyrs seek from us? They lack something if they still seek human praises. If they still seek human praises, they have not yet conquered. But if they have conquered, they seek nothing from us for themselves, but seek it for our own sake. Therefore, let our way be directed before the Lord. It was narrow, thorny, and hard; through the passage of so many such people it has become smooth. The Lord himself passed first; the fearless Apostles passed, then martyrs, boys, women, and girls. But who was in them? The one who said: *Without me you can do nothing*.
