# Sermon 379

**1.** Saint John the Baptist, whose birthday is celebrated today, was so great among men that the Lord Christ bore him this testimony, saying: *Among those born of women none has arisen greater than John the Baptist*[^1]. We heard, when the holy Gospel was read, how marvelously he was conceived out of despair, with how great a testimony of the Holy Spirit he was begotten. His father, because he did not believe, was struck dumb. So, announced by an angel and not believed, he took away the voice from the father; born, he loosed his tongue. To unfold the great and deep mystery of so great a man, indeed even to think of it worthily, is difficult. Yet according to our strength, as much as God grants, for the festival of this day—since this above all you owe and expect to hear—receive, I say, the mystery of this matter.

**2.** Before the Lord Christ, John was sent ahead. The prophets too were sent ahead, in the ages above, who did not fail to preach Christ and to foretell that he would come. For so great a judge was to come that many heralds had to go before. From the very beginning of the human race Christ did not cease to be prophesied and announced as the one to come: all which prophecies we cannot, of course, recount; but those who are diligent toward the divine Scriptures both know what I pass over and recognize what I say. Last of all John was born, a man—but a man than whom none whatever was greater, and a man to whom no man whatever could be compared. But the Lord Christ was to come, not only man but also God; because, God in flesh, surely both God and man: God always, man for a time; God before times, man in time; God before the ages, man at the end of the age; God, through whom man was created, who made man, for the sake of man deigned to become what he made. This, then, is Christ. But John, only a man. To the coming Lord Jesus Christ, then, who was more than man, lest he be thought only a man, John had to bear the greater testimony.

**3.** I have spoken briefly. If the more intelligent have prevailed, still the slower are not to be abandoned. This very thing I have said, let it again, with the Lord's help, be more plainly explained. Christ, I say, the Lord, not only God, not only man, but both God and man: God who made us, man who remade us. But John, only a man. But how great a man? Hear, concerning him, God and man. O Lord, who is John? How great is John? *Among those born of women*, he says, *none has arisen greater than John the Baptist*[^2]. O John, O great man, than whom among those born of women none greater has arisen, do you too tell me: Who is this? Tell me, O great man: Who is this one who is thought to be only a man? Who he is, hear: *Whose sandals*, he says, *I am not worthy to carry*[^3]. Who he is, hear; I speak John's words about the Lord Christ: *He who has the bride*, he says, *is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom*—speaking of himself—*stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice*[^4]. Again, another testimony of John himself: *We*, he says, *have all received of his fullness*[^5].

**4.** Understand, my brothers, recall the saving mystery, hunger for the word of God, grasp what we preach, that we may rejoice together over the truth. Who and how great Christ the Lord is, who for our sake became so very small, ask of John the Evangelist. Let him speak of the Lord Christ: *In the beginning was the Word*[^6]. What does Moses say? *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^7]. What does John say? *In the beginning*—not, God made the Word, but, *the Word was*. The Word was before heaven and earth were made in the beginning; the Word was, that this might be made; but the Word of God, lest, by the habit of human words, that through which it was made should be despised. Hear John: *The Word was with God, and the Word was God*. What sort of God, how great a God? *All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing. What was made in him was life, and the life was the light of men. And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*[^8].

**5.** What, then, is meant by: *The darkness did not comprehend it*? Be light, that you may comprehend. Let faith make you light, that the vision may fill you. *As long as we are in the body, we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord*[^9]; if we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord, we are on pilgrimage away from the light. What then? Shall we remain in darkness? Far from it. *Draw near to him, and be enlightened*[^10]. Then, because John the Evangelist said, *The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*, lest the darkness should remain, what does he say afterward? *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John*. God could not be seen: a lamp was lit: *There was a man whose name was John*. So great was the light in Christ, which bleary hearts could not grasp: comfort was made for them out of a lamp, so that the lamp might bear testimony to the day. *For there was a man sent from God, whose name was John. He came for a testimony, to bear witness to the light*. Why? Because *the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*. So John came, a man blended with darkness by the mortality of his weakness. Let him come and say of Christ what man does not grasp: to bear witness to the light, for this he came. And it follows: *He was not the light*. Who? John. *He was not the light, but came to bear witness to the light*. Of what light? It follows: *It was the true light*. True. What sort of true light? *Which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^11]—therefore John too. If it enlightens every man, then John too, than whom none greater has arisen among those born of women. If it enlightens every man, John spoke truly: *We have all received of his fullness*[^12].

**6.** Was John, then, not the light? What can we say against the Gospel? Another John, himself truthful too, testifies and says: *He was not the light*[^13]. The Apostles were called light; and was he not light than whom none greater arose among those born of women? But whence do we prove that the Apostles were called light? Hear the Lord Christ speaking to the Apostles: *You are the light of the world*[^14]. And John was light: I do not dare to deny to John what was given to the Apostles, nor would I deny it to the Lord Jesus himself, who deigned to bear him such testimony, that *among those born of women none greater arose than John the Baptist*[^15]. Let him, then, who promised enlightenment resolve the question—let him plainly resolve it. Hear. *He was not the light* was said in such a way that you should not believe yourself enlightened by him. In himself, as regards himself, because he had been enlightened, he was light. For enlightening you, he was not the light. Finally, to teach you how he was not the light, he at once added in comparison with whom he was not: *It was the true light*. He added *true*. What do you mean by saying *true*? *Which enlightens every man*; for a light that is enlightened is light by participation in the light, not by its own power. The true light, which enlightens, is not extinguished; a lamp can be both lit and extinguished. How were the Apostles called light? As a lamp. Whence is this proved? Lest I seem insulting, hear shortly after the Lord himself: *You are the light of the world*, and it follows: *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel*[^16]; therefore you are light as a lamp is. And John? Let the Lord himself be heard; he said of John himself: *He was a burning and shining lamp*[^17]. The Apostles a lamp, John a lamp. Rightly did he recognize himself a lamp and subject himself to the day: *I am not worthy*, he says, *to loose the strap of his sandal*[^18]. He did well not to set himself on high, lest he be extinguished by the wind of pride.

**7.** We say, then, that through John, and through man, one must believe in Christ, and that hope is not to be placed in man but in Christ. Behold, you have a great man, than whom none greater arose. He is John, but see where he sends you. John, the friend of the bridegroom, is zealous for the bridegroom, not for himself, like the blessed apostle Paul. He too, the friend of the bridegroom—how unwilling he was that hope be placed in himself! Carnal men in that time divided themselves, and some said, *I am Paul's*, others, *I am Apollos'*, others, *I am Cephas'*, others, *I am Christ's*[^19]. The triple cry is the cry of chaff: *I am Cephas'*, that is, Peter's, is still the cry of chaff. *I am Christ's*: now it is wheat, there is the lump which, when the threshing floor is winnowed, will appear at the end and will fill the granary of eternal life. So then the apostle Paul, because he was zealous for the bridegroom, not for himself, rebukes them and repels them from himself. Does he act for himself? For what was it to him? He says: *Has Christ been divided? Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^20] "Surely you have honored me, surely you wished to be mine. Do not be mine, lest we perish together; but let us all be his, that we may remain in the winnowing fan: *Was Paul crucified for you?*" I am, he says, the friend of the bridegroom: I am zealous for the bridegroom, not for myself. Consider, my brothers, and see what I say. If someone goes on a journey and entrusts his bride to a friend, and, while he is away on the journey, a wanton woman fixes her eye on the friend who is the guardian, will he not shudder at another's lust, lest he lose his own faithfulness? So too John—see where he sent: *I indeed*, he says, *baptize you in water. But he who comes after me is greater than I, whose sandal strap I am not worthy to loose*[^21]. *He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit*[^22]. Come through me, but do not remain in me. Pass over to him who created both you and me, because he gives life: *We have all*, he says, *received of his fullness*[^23]. Whence we, thence you also; let us drink together, lest we perish through pride. John, then, sent to Christ. However righteous they may be, by however great grace they stand out, with however great wisdom they shine, by however great merits they are exalted, they are mountains. Attend to the psalm: *I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help shall come to me*[^24]. Because *there was a man sent from God, whose name was John; he came to bear witness to the light*[^25]. You lifted up your eyes to the mountain John, whence help may come to you, because he bears witness to the light. Follow the psalm, do not remain on the mountain: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^26]. He is Christ: *All things were made through him*[^27]. He himself is the fashioner of the world: for he is the Word of the Father, and the Father made all things through the Word. Do not despise the lowly garment, because it is medicine for the sick. Or did he grow cheap because he lay hidden? For would you bear it if he lay open? Thank him, because, clothed in man, he blended himself with our weakness, that he might make us sharers in his divinity. Hold fast faith, that you may come to vision. Walk the way, that you may be led to the homeland. He abides there where we shall go, he came by the way by which we return; but he came in such a way that he did not depart from there, he ascended into heaven in such a way that he did not abandon us.
