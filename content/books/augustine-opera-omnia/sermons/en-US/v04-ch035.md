# Sermon 119

**1.** That our Lord Jesus Christ was made man by seeking lost man, our preaching has never kept silent, and your faith has always held; but that this Lord of ours, who was made man for our sake, was always God with the Father, and will always be, rather always is; because where time does not pass, there is no: He was, and: He will be. For what is said: He was, now is not; what is said: He will be, is not yet; but always he is, because he truly is, that is, he is unchangeable. Just now the gospel passage admonished us, a great and divine secret. For this beginning of the Gospel holy John belched forth, because he drank from the breast of the Lord. For recall, and it was read to you very recently, how that same holy John the Evangelist reclined on the bosom of the Lord. Wishing to expound this openly, he said: *Upon the breast of the Lord* [^1]; that we might understand what he meant by, on the bosom of the Lord. For he who reclined upon the breast of the Lord, what do we think he drank? Let us not think, but let us drink; for now we too have heard what we may drink.

**2.** *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^2]. O what preaching! O the belching forth of the fat of the Lord's breast! *In the beginning was the Word*. Why do you seek what was before? *In the beginning was the Word*. If the Word had been made (for he is not made through whom all things were made); if the Word had been made, Scripture would say: In the beginning God made the Word; as it said in Genesis: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth* [^3]. Therefore in the beginning God did not make the Word, because *in the beginning was the Word*. This Word that was in the beginning, where was it? Follow: *And the Word was with God*. But we are wont, hearing human words daily, to hold cheap this name of Word. Here do not hold cheap the name of the Word: *God was the Word. This*, that is, the Word, *was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him; and without him nothing was made* [^4].

**3.** Stretch out your hearts, help the poverty of our sermon. What I am able to say, hear; what I am not able to, think. Who comprehends the abiding Word? All our words sound and pass away. Who comprehends the abiding Word, except him who abides in it? Do you want to comprehend the abiding Word? Do not follow the river of the flesh. For this flesh is a river; for it does not abide. As from a certain hidden fount of nature, men are born, men live, men die; and we do not know whence they come, nor know whither they go. The water lies hidden, until it comes forth from the fount; it runs and appears in the river; but again it lies hidden in the sea. Let us despise this river, flowing, running, ceasing, let us despise it. *All flesh is grass, and all the honor of the flesh like the flower of grass. The grass withered, the flower fell*. Do you want to abide? *But the Word of the Lord abides forever* [^5].

**4.** But that he might help us: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us* [^6]. What is: *The Word was made flesh?* The gold was made grass. The grass was made to be kindled; the grass was kindled, but the gold remained; and in the grass it did not perish, and it changed the grass. How did it change it? It raised it up, gave it life, lifted it to heaven, placed it at the Father's right hand. But that it might be said: *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*, let us recall a little what went before. *He came to his own, and his own did not receive him. But as many as received him, he gave them power to become sons of God*. *To become*, because they were not; but he was in the beginning. He gave, then, *them power to become sons of God, to those believing in his name; who were born not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God* [^7]. Behold they are, in whatever age of the flesh they may be; you see the infants; see and rejoice. Behold they are: but they were born of God. The womb of the mother, the water of Baptism.

**5.** Let no one with a poor little soul feel, and turn over within himself most beggarly thoughts, and say to himself: How was it *in the beginning the Word, and the Word with God, and the Word was God; all things were made through him*; and behold *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us?* Hear from what it was made. Surely to those believing in his name he gave power to become sons of God. Nor let those themselves to whom he gave power to become sons of God think it impossible to become sons of God. *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*. Do not think it too much for you, to become sons of God; for your sake the Son of man was made, who was the Son of God. If he himself was made, that he might be less who was more; can he not make from that by which we were less, that we may be able to be something more? He came down to us, and shall we not ascend to him? He took on for us our death, and will he not give us his life? He suffered on your account your evils, and will he not give you his goods?

**6.** But how, he says, could it come about that the Word of God, by which the world is governed, through which all things both were created and are created, confined himself in the flesh of a Virgin; left the world, and forsook the angels, and was shut up in the womb of one woman? You do not know how to think divine things. The Word of God (I speak to you, O man, of the omnipotence of the Word of God I speak to you) was able to do absolutely the whole, because the Word of God is both omnipotent, and could both remain with the Father, and come to us; and proceed in the flesh to us, and lie hidden with him. For not, if he had not been born of flesh, would he not be. He was before his flesh; he himself created his mother. He chose her in whom he might be conceived, he created her of whom he might be created. Why do you wonder? I speak to you of God: *God was the Word* [^8].

**7.** I am dealing with something of the Word, and the human word perhaps can do something similar; though far unequal, far distinct, in no particle to be compared, yet to be conveyed to you by some likeness. Behold, the word I speak to you, I first had in my heart; it came forth to you, nor did it depart from me; it began to be in you, what was not in you; it remained with me, when it went out to you. Just as, then, my word was put forth to your sense, nor did it depart from my heart; so that Word was put forth to our sense, nor did it depart from its Father. My word was with me, and went forth into voice: the Word of God was with the Father, and went forth into flesh. But can I do with my voice, what he could with his flesh? For I cannot hold my flying voice; he not only held his flesh, that it might be born, live, act; but even raised it up dead, and lifted to the Father a certain vehicle in which he came forth to us. You may call the flesh of Christ a garment, you may call it a vehicle, and, in the way he himself perhaps deigned to signify it, you may call it his mount; because on that mount he lifted up the one who had been wounded by robbers [^9]; in short, what he himself said more openly, you may call it a temple [^10]; this temple now knows death, sits at the right hand of the Father; in that temple he is to come to judge the living and the dead. What he admonished us by precept, he showed by example. What he showed in his flesh, this you ought to hope for in your flesh. This is faith, hold what you do not yet see. There is need that you remain, by believing, in that which you do not see; lest, when you see, you blush.
