# Sermon 362: On the Resurrection of the Dead

**1. 1.** Keeping in memory our promise, we have had suitable readings recited too from the Gospel and the Apostle. For whoever of you were present at the previous sermon recall with us that the question proposed about the resurrection was distributed by us into a twofold disputation: that first we should discourse, on account of those who doubt, or even who deny, whether there will be a resurrection of the dead; but afterward, as much as we can, we should inquire according to the Scriptures, what the future life of the just will be like in the resurrection. In the first part, then, where we treated that the dead rise, we lingered so long, as you deign to remember, that time was lacking for the second question to be treated, and so we were compelled to defer it to this day. This debt of ours, then, your attention demands, and we recognize the time for rendering it.

**2. 1.** Together, then, with the pious attention of the heart, let us beseech the Lord, that both we may opportunely pay the debt, and you may wholesomely receive it. For this is, it must be confessed, a greater question: but stronger than all difficult questions is love, which we all ought to serve, that God who commanded this may turn all our difficulties into ease and joy.

**2. 2.** You remember that it was answered by us on that day to certain ones saying, as the Apostle rebukes them: *Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we die*: subjoining and saying: *Evil conversations corrupt good morals*; and so concluding: *Be sober, you just, and do not sin: for some have ignorance of God; I speak to your reverence*[^1]. These apostolic words we have all heard, and committed to heart: and whoever has heard and committed to heart, let the works show it. For he who hears is as a field receiving the seed of the sower: but he who commits to heart is like one breaking the clod, and covering what was sown: but he who works according to that which he heard and committed to heart, he it is who rises into the harvest, and bears fruit with patience, one thirtyfold, another sixtyfold, another a hundredfold[^2]. For this one not fire as for chaff, but granaries as for wheat are prepared.

**3. 2.** In those hidden granaries, then, in the resurrection of the dead, is that perpetual blessedness, even those secret places of the just, to which Scripture commends that they are to be received.

**3. 3.** By the name of vessels too he mentioned it in another place, when the Lord Jesus Christ said that the kingdom of heaven is like a dragnet, that is, nets; for certain nets are called a dragnet. *The kingdom of heaven is like*, then, he says, *a dragnet cast into the sea, and gathering of every kind: which, when it was full, drawing it out, and sitting by the shore, they chose the good into vessels, but the bad they cast out*[^3]. Our Lord wished to signify that now the word of God is so cast over peoples and over nations, as a dragnet is thrown into the sea. But it gathers now, by the Christian sacraments, both good and bad: but not all whom the dragnet takes up are also stored in the little vessels. For the little vessels are the seats of the saints, and the great secret places of the blessed life, to which not all can come who are called Christians, but those who are so called that they may be such. But surely both good and bad fish swim within the dragnet, and the good tolerate the bad, until at the end they are separated. It was said too in a certain place: *You will hide them in the hiding of your face*[^4]; for he was speaking of the saints. *You will hide them*, he says, *in the hiding of your face*: that is, where the eyes of men cannot follow, nor the thoughts of mortals; signifying certain secret things too hidden, too concealed, he said, in the hiding of the face of God. Is it to be thought carnally that God has a certain great face, and that in his face there is some bodily receptacle, where the saints are to be hidden? These things, you see, brothers, how carnal they are, and to be spurned from the heart of all the faithful. What ought the hiding of the face of God to be understood as, then, except that which is known only to the face of God? When, then, granaries are spoken of, that secret places may be signified, and in another place little vessels are spoken of; neither are they the granaries which we know, nor little vessels. For if there were some one such thing, the other would not be spoken of. But because through likenesses known to men unknown things are conveyed, as far as they can be, take both names for this purpose, that you may understand a secret thing, both by the name of granary and by that of little vessels. But if you ask what kind of secret, hear the prophet saying: *You will hide them in the hiding of your face*.

**4. 4.** Since these things are so, brothers, we are still on pilgrimage in this life, we still sigh by faith for that I-know-not-what homeland. And why I-know-not-what, from where we are citizens, except because, on pilgrimage in a far country, we have forgotten it, so that we can call our homeland I-know-not-what? This forgetfulness the Lord Christ drives out of the heart, the king of that homeland, coming to the pilgrims; and by the assumption of flesh his divinity becomes for us the way, that through the man Christ we may go, and in God Christ we may remain. What then, brothers? That secret which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man[^5], with what eloquence are we going to unfold it to you, or with what eye are we going to see it? We can sometimes know something which yet we cannot speak: but what we do not know, we sometimes cannot speak. Since, then, it can happen that even if I knew those things, I could not speak them to you; how much more difficult will my speech be, when even I, brothers, walk with you by faith, not yet by sight? But is this I, or even the Apostle himself? For he consoles our ignorance, and builds up faith, saying: *Brothers, I do not consider myself to have laid hold of it. But one thing, forgetting the things that are behind, stretched out toward the things that are ahead, I follow with intent toward the prize of the heavenly calling*[^6]; whence he shows himself to be on the way. And in another place: *As long as we are*, he says, *in the body, we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord: for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^7]. And again: *For by hope*, he says, *we are saved. But hope which is seen is not hope: for what one sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^8].

**5. 5.** So, then, brothers, hear from me a voice which is in the Psalms, pious, humble, gentle, not exalted, not turbulent, not headlong, not rash. For the Psalm says in a certain place: *I believed, therefore I spoke*[^9]. And the Apostle inserted this testimony, and subjoined: *And we believe, therefore we also speak*[^10]. Do you wish, then, me to say those things which I know? I do not deceive you, hear what I have believed. Let it not grow cheap to you, because you hear what I have believed: for you hear a truthful confession. But if I said: Hear what I know; you would hear a rash presumption. If, then, brothers, all of us, and, as we believe by the writings of the saints, all also who lived in the flesh before us, and through whom the Spirit of God speaking distributed to men as much as would suffice to be signified to those on pilgrimage, all speak what we believe: but the Lord himself, the things which he knew. What then, if only the Lord could know about the life that is to come forever what he was saying; but the others following the Lord, for the reason that they believed? We find our Lord Jesus Christ himself, knowing what he was saying, yet not saying it. For he says in a certain place to his disciples: *I have yet many things to say to you; but you cannot bear them now*[^11]. He, on account of their weakness, not on account of his own difficulty, deferred to say what he knew. But we, on account of the common weakness of all of us, do not try to speak worthily enough what we know, but unfold, as we can, what we worthily believe; and do you grasp as you can. And if perhaps any of you can grasp more than I can say, let him not attend to the meager stream, but run to the most rich fountain: since with him is the fountain of life, in whose light we shall see light[^12].

**6. 6.** Since, then, we have disputed that there is a resurrection, so we believe, so we ought to believe, so we speak, because so we have believed, if we are Christians, beholding the power of the arm of the Lord laying low everywhere the pride of the nations, and building up this faith so widely throughout the world, as was promised long before it came to pass: beholding these things, we are built up to believe those things which we do not yet see, that we may receive that very sight as the reward of faith. Since, then, it is manifest to our faith that there will be a resurrection of the dead, and so manifest that whoever doubts about it would most shamelessly call himself a Christian; it is asked what sort of bodies the saints will have, and what their life will be. For it has seemed to many that the resurrection indeed takes place, but through souls alone.

**7. 7.** But because the bodies too rise, there is no need to discourse long after the previous sermon. But a question of this kind is objected: If the bodies are to be, what kind are they to be? Such as they now are, or of another kind? If of another kind, what is that kind? If such, then for the same works? Because the Lord does not prescribe for the same, because the Apostle does not teach for such. For not for the same life, not for the same mortal and corruptible and perishing and passing deeds, not for carnal joys, not for carnal consolations. If, then, not for the same, neither such. If not such, how then will the flesh rise? But we have the resurrection of the flesh in the rule of faith, and confessing it we are baptized. And whatever we confess there, we confess out of truth and in truth, in which we live and move and are[^13]. For by temporal deeds and certain passing and transient acts we are instructed unto eternal life. All the things which were done, that we might hear something wholesome, that miracles might be done, that our Lord might be born, hunger and thirst, be seized, be afflicted with insults, be beaten, be crucified, die, be buried, rise again, ascend into heaven, all have passed away; and when they are preached, certain temporal and transitory actions of our faith are preached. Does it follow that because these themselves pass away, what is built up through them likewise passes away? For let your holiness attend, that you may see this through a likeness. An architect builds, by means of passing machines, a house that abides. For in this so great and ample building which we see, when it was being constructed, there were machines, which now are not here; because what was being built by them now stands complete. So, then, brothers, something was being built in the Christian faith, and certain temporal machines were completed. For that our Lord Jesus Christ rose again is past; for he does not still rise: and that he ascended into heaven is past; for he does not still ascend. But that which is in that life, where now he does not die, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^14]; that there lives forever even that very human nature in him, which he deigned to assume, and in which to be born, and in which to die and be buried, this was built, this always abides. But the machines by which it was built have passed away. For Christ is not always conceived in the virginal womb, or always born of the virgin Mary, or always seized, or always judged, scourged, crucified, buried. All these things are reckoned as machines, that by these machines might be built that which abides forever. But this resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ is placed in heaven.

**8. 8.** Let your love attend to a wonderful building. For those earthly buildings press the earth with their weight, and the whole bent of the weights in this structure leans by its greatness toward the earth, and unless it is held in, it sinks downward, whither the weight draws it. Because, then, it is built on the earth, on the earth the foundation is laid first; that the builder may build securely upon the foundation. Therefore he places at the bottom most firm masses, that they may suitably be able to carry what is set upon them, and in proportion to the greatness of the building the greatness of the foundation is prepared: on the earth, however, as I said, because even what is built above is surely placed on the earth. That our pilgrim Jerusalem is built in heaven. Therefore Christ the foundation went before into heaven. For there is our foundation and the head of the Church: for he is called both foundation and head, and truly it is so. Because the head of the building is the foundation: for the head is not that which is finished, but from where it begins upward. The summits of earthly buildings are raised on high, yet they set the head in the solidity of the earth. So too the head of the Church went before into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father. As men work, so as to draw something for setting up the foundation which they may establish at the bottom, on account of the security of the mass to come in the constructions of the future fabric: so through all those things which befell in Christ, to be born, to grow, to be seized, to suffer insult, to be scourged, to be crucified, to be slain, to die, to be buried, as it were the mass was drawn to the heavenly foundation.

**8. 9.** With our foundation, then, placed on high, let us be built up in it. Hear the Apostle: *No one*, he says, *can lay another foundation, except that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^15].

**9. 9.** But what follows? *But let each one see what he builds upon the foundation, gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, stubble*[^16]. In heaven indeed Christ is, but also in the heart of believers. If Christ holds the first place, the foundation is rightly laid. Therefore let him who builds build securely, if according to the dignity of the foundation he builds gold, silver, precious stones. But if not according to the dignity of the foundation he builds wood, hay, stubble; let him at least keep the foundation, and on account of those dry and fragile things which he has built up, let him prepare himself for fire. But if it is the foundation, that is, if Christ has obtained the first place in the heart, but worldly things are so loved that they are not preferred to Christ, but the Lord Christ to them, so that he is in the building of the heart holding the foundation, that is, the first place: *he will suffer*, he says, *loss; but he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire*[^17]. It is not now the time to exhort you to build gold, silver, precious stones rather than wood, hay, stubble, upon so great and strong a foundation: but yet take this thing briefly said as though said at length and in many words. For we know, brothers, that whoever of you, on account of those things which he now loves, were cast into prison by the threats of some judge, so as to suffer only smoke, would prefer to lose all those things, than to suffer that place. But somehow when fire is named, to come on the day of judgment, all despise it, and fearing the flames of the hearth, they count the flame of hell as nothing. What is this hardness? what is so great a perversity of heart? If men at least so feared what the Apostle says, *through fire*, as each one fears lest he burn alive, which befalls him in one moment, until the sense, leaving the members, makes all those flames superfluous; yet he would fear, and would not do something which is rightly forbidden, lest he come to that torment of one moment.

**10. 10.** But, as I said, brothers, there is now no space for discoursing about this matter: this I say, that we ought to hope in the resurrection of the dead for that which was expressed in our head, which was expressed in the body of our Lord Jesus Christ. Whoever hopes for another thing, now does not build upon the foundation, not only gold, silver, and precious stones, but not even the stubble itself. For he places the whole outside, because he does not place it in Christ. Our Lord, then, rose again in that body in which he was buried. The resurrection is promised to Christians. Let us hope for such a resurrection as preceded in our Lord the faith of us all. For he went before for this, that our faith might be built up upon it. What then? How shall we not be such as we now are? For the flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ rose again, but it ascended into heaven. On earth it kept human offices, that it might persuade that this had risen which had been buried. But is there even in heaven such food? For we read that even the angels carried out human offices on earth. They came to Abraham, and ate; and with Tobit there was an angel, and he ate. What do we say, that that eating was a phantom, and was not true? Is it not manifest that Abraham killed a calf, made loaves, and set them on the table; that he ministered to the angels, and they ate[^18]? All these things were most manifestly done and most manifestly expressed.

**11. 11.** What, then, does the angel say in the book of Tobit? *You saw me eat, but you saw with your sight*[^19]. Was it because he was not eating, but seemed to eat? Rather indeed he was eating. What is, then, *you saw with your sight*? Let your holiness attend to what I say: attend to the prayer more than to me; that you may understand what we say, that we too may so say as it behooves you to hear and understand what you hear. Our body, as long as it is corruptible and mortal, suffers the need of refreshment, whence too hunger arises: therefore we hunger and thirst; and if we put off our hunger and thirst longer than the body can sustain, it is led to a wasting leanness, and to a certain diseased emaciation, the strength departing and not coming again: and if it goes further, death too will follow. For there always departs something from our body as it were by a certain river of departing, but we do not feel the strength departing from there, because by refreshment we take in fresh strength succeeding. For what comes abundantly departs little by little: therefore in a short time we refresh, but over a longer time the strength deserts us, which was received when we refreshed ourselves. Just as oil in a lamp, which is put in over a short time, is consumed over a longer time little by little. But when it is nearly consumed, now the languor of that little flame, as it were the hunger of the lamp, admonishes us, and at once we come to its aid, that that appearance may be restored, and the light may abide in the lamp, refreshed with its food when we add oil. So our strength, which we receive by eating, goes and deserts us by a perpetual departing, but little by little. For this very thing now goes on in us, and in all our actions, in all our rest too there does not cease to go what was received: and if it is altogether consumed, so a man both dies as a lamp is extinguished. But that he may not die, that is, that he may not be extinguished, not because he dies in mind, but that this bodily life of ours may not be extinguished, and a certain as it were keeping-watch in this body may succeed, we run and supply what has departed, and we are said to refresh. He who says he refreshes, what does he refresh, if nothing fails? Through this need, then, and corruption we are all going to die too, because such is this body, that death is owed and reserved to it. For this mortality the skins signify, with which Adam and Eve were clothed, and sent out of paradise[^20]. For the skins indicate death, which are wont to be drawn from dead beasts. Since, then, we carry this failing weakness, to which, even if food is never lacking, but ever and again repairs the strength, yet it does not bring it about that death is not to come (for that whole state of the body, through the succeeding ages, even if one lives here longer, comes at some time to the limit of old age, and beyond which to be extended it will not find except death. For even the lamp itself, even if you ever and again always put in oil, cannot always burn; because, that it may not be extinguished by other accidents, the wick itself fails, and is consumed by a certain as it were old age); as long, then, as we bear such bodies, from defect we are in need, from need we hunger, from hunger we eat. But the angel does not eat from need. For it is one thing to do something by power, another by necessity. Man eats, lest he die: the angel eats, that he may suit himself to mortals. For if the angel does not fear death, he is not refreshed from defect: if he is not refreshed from defect, he does not eat from need. But those who saw the angel eat thought him as it were hungering. This is what he says: *You saw with your sight.* For he did not say: You saw me eat, but I did not eat: *You saw*, he says, *me eat, but you saw with your sight*; that is, I was eating that I might suit myself to you, not because I was suffering any hunger or need, by whose compulsion you are accustomed to eat, and therefore those whom you see eating, you suspect that this is done from need, who measure by your own custom what you see: this is, *you saw with your sight*.

**12. 12.** What then, my brothers? *We know*, as the Apostle says, *that Christ rising from the dead now dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him. For in that he died to sin, he died once: but in that he lives, he lives to God*[^21]. If, then, he now dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him; let us so hope to rise again, that we may always be in that state into which by rising we shall be changed. But of eating and drinking, even if there will be the power, there will not be the necessity. But there was then a reason why the Lord did this, because there were still in the flesh those to whom he wished to suit himself, to whom he also wished to show his scars. For he who made the eyes of the blind man, which he had not received in his mother's womb, could not rise again without scars. He who, if he wished to change the mortal need of his own flesh before death, so that it had no want of necessity, could surely do so: for he had it in his hand, because he was God in the flesh, and the almighty Son, as the Father is almighty. For even his own flesh he changed before death into what he wished. For when he was on the mountain with the disciples, his face shone like the sun[^22]. But this he did by power, wishing to show that he could change even his own flesh from all need, so as not to die if he did not wish. *I have power*, he says, *to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again. No one takes it from me*[^23]. Great is this power, by which he could even not die: but greater the mercy, by which he wished to die. For he did by mercy what he could even not do by power, that he might establish for us the foundation of the resurrection: that what he bore mortal for our sake, he might both die, because we are to die; and rise again to immortality, that we may hope for immortality. And therefore before death it is written not only that he ate and drank, but also that he hungered and thirsted[^24]; after the resurrection only that he both ate and drank, but not that he hungered and thirsted; because in a body no longer to die there was not that need of corruption, that there should be a necessity of refreshment, but there was the power of eating. It was done for the sake of suiting himself, not that the want of the flesh might be relieved, but that the truth of the body might be persuaded.

**13. 13.** Against this so great evidence, some make a question for us from the Apostle: against this disputation, see what they object. The flesh, they say, will not rise: for if it rises, it will possess the kingdom of God; but the Apostle plainly says: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*[^25]. You heard, when the Apostle was being read. We say that the flesh rises, and the Apostle cries out: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God.* Do we, then, preach against the Apostle, or did he preach against the Gospel? The Gospel attests with a divine voice: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^26]. If it was made flesh, it was made true flesh. For if not true flesh, neither flesh. As true was the flesh of Mary, so true the flesh of Christ, which was assumed from there. This true flesh was seized, scourged, buffeted, hung; this true flesh died, this true flesh was buried; this true flesh rose again even from death. The scars render testimony: the eyes of the disciples see, and still the wonder wavers; the hand handles, lest the mind doubt. Against so great evidence, brothers, by which our Lord Jesus Christ wished to persuade his disciples who were going to preach it throughout the whole world; against this evidence does the Apostle seem to fight, saying: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*?

**13. 14.** We could solve this question thus, and resist vain slanderers: nevertheless it will be solved both in this way, as can be quickly answered, and we will consider more diligently what the Apostle says, whence it was said. I say, then, how we can most easily answer. What does the Gospel hold? That Christ rose again in that body in which he was buried: because he was seen, because he was handled, because he said to the disciples who thought it was a spirit: *Touch, and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^27]. What does the Apostle say against this? *Flesh and blood*, he says, *will not possess the kingdom of God.* I embrace both, nor do I say these things conflict, lest I myself fight against the goad. How, then, do I embrace both? Quickly, as I said, I could answer thus: The Apostle says: *Flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God as an inheritance*[^28]. He said rightly; for it is not for the flesh to possess, but to be possessed. For your body does not possess anything, but your soul possesses through the body, which possesses even the body itself. If, then, the flesh so rises that it may be had, not have; that it may be possessed, not possess: what wonder if flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God, because it will surely be possessed? For the flesh possesses those who are not the kingdom of God, but the kingdom of the devil, and therefore are subjected to the pleasures of the flesh. Whence too that paralytic was carried on a pallet: but when healed the Lord said: *Take up your pallet, and go to your house*[^29]. So, then, with the paralysis healed he holds his flesh, and leads it where he wishes: he is not, where he does not wish, dragged by the flesh, and he carries the body rather than is carried by the body. It is manifest that in that resurrection the flesh will not have the pull of enticements, so as to lead the soul by certain titillations and coaxings, where the soul does not wish, and very often it is overcome, saying: *I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members.* Still the paralytic is carried on a pallet, he does not yet carry: let him cry out, then: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?* Let it be answered: *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^30]. When, then, we have risen again, the flesh will not carry us, but we will carry it: if we carry it, we will possess it: if we possess it, we will not be possessed by it; because, freed from the devil, we are the kingdom of God: and so flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God. Therefore let those slanderers be silent, who truly are flesh and blood, and can think nothing except carnally. Because even of those persevering in the same prudence of the flesh, whence they are deservedly called flesh and blood, it could rightly be said: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God.*

**14. 14.** Let this question be solved in this way too: that such men, who are called flesh and blood (for of such the Apostle too says: *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood*[^31]), if they do not turn themselves to the spiritual life, and by the spirit put to death the deeds of the flesh, will not be able to possess the kingdom of God.

**14. 15.** Nevertheless, what does the Apostle say, someone will ask? For that is the truer sense which is opened by the context of the reading. And so let us rather hear him himself, and from the whole connection of Scripture which is around it, let us see what in that place he wished to be understood. For he says thus: *The first man of the earth, earthly; the second man of heaven. As is the earthly one, such also the earthly; and as is the heavenly one, such also the heavenly. As we have borne the image of the earthly one, let us bear also the image of him who is of heaven. But this I say, brothers, that flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God; nor will corruption possess incorruption*[^32]. Let us see them one by one. *The first man*, he says, *of the earth, earthly; the second man of heaven. As is the earthly one, such also the earthly*, that is, all going to die: *and as is the heavenly one, such also the heavenly*, that is, all going to rise again. For now the heavenly man has risen, and ascended into heaven: to whom by faith we are now incorporated, that he himself may be our head; but the members in their own order follow their head, and what was demonstrated beforehand in the head may be demonstrated in its own time in the members: but now let us bear this by faith, that we may come to the very reality and sight in its own time. For so he says in another place: *But if you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth*[^33]. As, then, when in ourselves we have not yet risen, as Christ in body, yet by faith we are said to have risen with Christ: so he bids us to bear meanwhile by faith the image of the heavenly man, that is, of him who is now in the heavens.

**14. 16.** But if anyone asks why he did not call the second man "in heaven," but "of heaven," since the Lord himself too received his body from the earth, because surely Mary was descended from Adam and Eve; let him understand that the earthly man is so called according to earthly concupiscence: and since that affection is earthly by which through the intercourse of male and female men are born, drawing even from their parents original sin; but the body of the Lord was created from the virginal womb by no such affection, although Christ assumed flesh from the earth, which is understood that the Holy Spirit signifies, saying: *Truth has arisen from the earth*[^34]; yet not earthly, but a heavenly man, and "of heaven," he is called. For if he granted this to his faithful by grace, so that the Apostle rightly says: *For our citizenship is in the heavens*[^35]; how much more is he himself a heavenly man, and to be called "of heaven," in whom there was never any sin? For on account of sin it was said to man: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^36]. Of heaven, then, that heavenly man is most rightly called, whose citizenship never departed from heaven: although the Son of God, made also the son of man, assumed a body from the earth, that is, the form of a servant. For none ascended, except he who descended. Because even if the rest, to whomever he has granted it, ascend, or rather are lifted up into heaven by his grace, even so he himself ascends, because they become his body; and according to this one ascends: since the Apostle expounds a great mystery in Christ and the Church, by which it is written: *And they shall be two in one flesh*[^37]. Whence too it is said: *Therefore now not two, but one flesh.* Wherefore *no one ascends into heaven, except he who descended from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven*[^38]. For on this account he added, *who is in heaven*, lest anyone should think that his citizenship had departed from heaven, when through an earthly body he appeared on earth to men. Therefore *as we have borne the image of the earthly one, let us bear also the image of him who is of heaven*, meanwhile by faith, through which we have also risen with him: that we may both have the heart on high, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God; and therefore seek and savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth.

**15. 17.** But because he was dealing with the resurrection of the body: for so he had set it forth: *But someone will say: How do the dead rise? With what body do they come?*[^39] and therefore he had said: *The first man of the earth, earthly, the second man, of heaven. As is the earthly one, such also the earthly; and as is the heavenly one, such also the heavenly*; that we might hope this to be in our body, which preceded in the body of Christ; and although we have not yet in reality received this, let us meanwhile hold it by faith. Therefore he had subjoined: *As we have borne the image of the earthly one, let us bear also the image of him who is of heaven.* Lest we should believe that we are going to rise to such things as, according to the first man, we were corruptibly doing, he at once subjoined: *But this I say, brothers, that flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God as an inheritance*[^40]. And wishing to show what he meant by flesh and blood, that he signifies by the name of flesh and blood not the appearance itself of the body, but corruption, which corruption will not then be. For a body without corruption is not properly called flesh and blood, but a body. For if it is flesh, it is corruptible and mortal: but if it now does not die, it is now not corruptible; and therefore, without corruption, with the appearance remaining, it is now called not flesh, but a body: and if it is called flesh, it is now not properly called so, but on account of a certain likeness of appearance. As we can perhaps, on account of the same likeness, call even the flesh of angels flesh, when they appeared to men as men do; when they were a body, not flesh: because the need of corruption was not in them. Because, then, we can according to likeness call even a body that is now not corrupted flesh, the anxious Apostle, following, wished to expound what he meant by flesh and blood: that he said this according to corruption, not according to appearance: and he at once subjoined: *Nor will corruption possess incorruption as an inheritance*[^41]: as though he said: What I said: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*; this I said, because *corruption will not possess incorruption*.

**15. 18.** And lest anyone should say: If, then, incorruption cannot be possessed by corruption, how will our body be there? Hear what follows. As though it were said to the Apostle: What is it, then, that you say? have we believed in the resurrection of the flesh in vain? If flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God, have we believed in vain that our Lord rose from the dead in the body in which he was born and crucified, that in it he ascended into heaven before the eyes of his disciples, from which heaven he cried out to you: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* This occurred to the holy and blessed apostle Paul, and travailing with pious love over his children begotten in Christ through the Gospel[^42], with whom he still travailed until Christ was reformed in them[^43], that is, until they bore by faith the image of him who is of heaven. For he did not want them to remain in that ruin, so as to think that they were going to do such things in the kingdom of God, in that eternal life, as they did in this life, in the pleasures of eating and drinking, of marrying, of taking wives, and of begetting them carnally: for these works the corruption of the flesh has, not the appearance itself of the flesh. Now, then, because we are not going to rise to such things, the Lord prescribed, as I had long ago said, in the gospel reading which was just now recited. For the Jews believed indeed in the resurrection of the flesh, but thought it would be such that the life would be in the resurrection such as they led in this age; and so, thinking carnally, they could not answer the Sadducees who set forth a question about the resurrection, whose wife the woman would be, whom seven brothers succeeding one another had had, since each one wished to raise up seed for his dead brother from his wife. For the Sadducees were a certain sect of the Jews, which did not believe the resurrection. The Jews, then, when the Sadducees set forth this question, wavering and hesitating could not answer, because they thought that the kingdom of God could be possessed by flesh and blood, that is, incorruption by corruption. The Truth came, is questioned by the deceived and deceiving Sadducees, that question is set forth to the Lord. And the Lord, who knew what he said, and wished us to believe what we did not know, answers by the authority of his majesty what we may believe. But the Apostle expounded as much as was given to him: that, as much as we can, we may understand. What, then, does the Lord say to the Sadducees? *You err*, he says, *not knowing the Scriptures, nor the power of God. For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor take wives; for neither do they begin to die, but they will be equal to the angels of God.* Great is the power of God. Why do they not marry, nor take wives? Because they will not begin to die. For there is a successor, where there is a predecessor. There is, then, no such corruption there. And that Lord ran through the ages from infancy to youth, because he still bore the substance of mortal flesh: after he rose again in the age in which he was buried, do we believe that he grows old in heaven? Therefore *they will be*, he says, *equal to the angels of God.* He took away from the midst the suspicion of the Jews, he refuted the calumnies of the Sadducees: because the Jews believed indeed that the dead would rise, but for what works they would rise, they thought carnally. *They will be equal*, he says, *to the angels of God.* You have heard about the power of God, hear also about the Scriptures. *About the resurrection*, he says, *have you not read, how the Lord spoke to Moses from the bush, saying: I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? He is not the God of the dead, but of the living*[^44].

**16. 19.** That we rise, then, has been said: and that we rise to the life of the angels, we have heard from the Lord: but in what appearance we rise, he himself showed in his resurrection. But that the appearance itself will not have corruption, the Apostle says: *But this I say, brothers, that flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God as an inheritance; nor will corruption possess incorruption as an inheritance*; that he might show that by the name of flesh and blood he wished to understand the corruption of the mortal and animal body. Then he himself now solves the question, which the anxious hearers could ask of him: because he himself is more anxious about the understanding of his children, than the children are about the words of their parents. He subjoins, then, and says: *Behold, I tell you a mystery.* Let your thought rest, O man, whoever you are. For you had begun to think from the words of the Apostle that human flesh does not rise, when you heard: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*: but lend your ear to the following words, and correct the presumption of your thought. *Behold*, he says, *I tell you a mystery: we shall all indeed rise, but we shall not all be changed.* What is this? Change indeed is either for the worse, or for the better. If, then, change is set down, so that we do not yet see what it will be like, whether into something better, or into something worse: let him therefore follow, and let him himself expound; what is it for us to suspect? And perhaps the apostolic authority does not permit you to slip into error by human suspicion in your conjectures, and expounds this clearly, what kind of change he wishes to be understood. What then? When he had said: *We shall all rise, yet we shall not all be changed*: I see that all are going to rise, both the good and the evil; but let us see who will be changed; and from this let us understand the change, whether it is going to be for the better or for the worse. For if this change belongs to the evil, it will be for the worse; but if to the good, it will be for the better. *In an atom*, he says, *in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will rise incorrupt, and we shall be changed*[^45]. Now, then, this change will be for the better, when he says: *And we shall be changed*; but it is not yet expressed as much as it ought to be expressed, to what point our body is changed for the better. What is that "better" itself, it is not yet said. For even when one is changed from infancy to adolescence, he can be said to be changed for the better, where, even if less weak, yet it is still weak and mortal.

**17. 20.** Therefore let us consider each thing more diligently. *In an atom*, he says. It seems difficult to men that the dead should rise: but it is wonderful how the Apostle has taken away all doubtful difficulties from the hearts of the faithful. You say: The dead do not rise: I say not only that the dead rise, but with as great speed as you were not conceived and born. For how great a delay there is that a man be formed in the womb, be perfected, be born, be strengthened by the succession of ages! Is he perhaps going to rise again thus? No: but *in an atom*, he says. Many do not know what an atom is. It is called atom from tomē, which is "section": atomon in Greek is what cannot be cut. But "atom" is said of body, it is said of time. It is said of body, if something can be found which indeed is held to be unable to be divided, some little body so minute, that it no longer has where it can be cut. But an atom in time is a short moment, which now has no place where it may be divided. For example, that even slower hearts may grasp what I say: it is a stone; divide it into parts, and divide the parts themselves into pebbles, the pebbles indeed into grains, such as are of sand, and again divide the grains of sand themselves into the most minute dust, until, if you can, you come to some minute thing, of such a kind as can now not be divided. This is the atom in bodies. But in time it is thus understood. A year, for example, is divided into months, the months are divided into days, the days can still be divided into hours, the hours still into certain rather extended parts of hours, which admit divisions, until you come to so great a point of time, and a certain drop of a moment, that it cannot be extended through any little delay, and therefore now cannot be divided: this is the atom of time. You said, then, that the dead do not rise: not only do they rise, but they rise with such great speed, that in an atom of time will be the resurrection of all the dead.

**18. 20.** And expounding to you the speed of the atom, when he had said: *in an atom*, he at once added how much action and movement can take place in an atom of time: *In the twinkling of an eye*, he says. For he knew he had spoken obscurely: *in an atom*, and he wished to say more plainly what would be more easily understood. What is the twinkling of an eye? Not that by which we close or open the eye with the eyelids: but he calls the twinkling of the eye the emission of rays to discern something. For as soon as you cast your gaze, the emitted ray reaches the sky, where we behold the sun and moon and stars and constellations, separated from the earth by so immense an interval. But by the last trumpet he means the last sign. *For the trumpet*, he says, *will sound, and the dead will rise incorrupt, and we shall be changed*[^46]. By "we" he surely means the faithful, and those who rise first to eternal life. Therefore that change, because it is of the pious and holy, will be for the better, not for the worse.

**18. 21.** But what is this change? what is that which he says: *We shall be changed*? Is the appearance which now is lost, or only the corruption, on account of what was said: *Flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God; nor will corruption possess incorruption as an inheritance*? Which, lest it should move the hearer to despair of the resurrection of the flesh, he subjoined: *Behold, I tell you a mystery: we shall all rise, but we shall not all be changed.* And lest we should think the change would be for the worse: *and we*, he says, *shall be changed.* It remains, then, for him to say what kind of change there will be. *For this corruptible*, he says, *must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality.* If this corruptible will put on incorruption, and this mortal will put on immortality; now the flesh will not be corruptible. If, then, the flesh will not be corruptible, the name of corruption will cease in flesh and blood, the proper name too of flesh and blood will cease; because these are names of mortality. And if it is so, both the flesh rises, and because it is changed and made incorrupt, *flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God.* But if anyone wishes to understand that change in those whom that day will then find still living: so that those who were already dead may rise, but those who still live may be changed; so that the Apostle is believed to have taken on their person, when he says: *And we shall be changed*; yet the same reasoning will follow, because that incorruption will surely pertain to all, *when this corruptible will put on incorruption, and this mortal will put on immortality. Then will come about the saying which is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where is, O death, your strife? where is, O death, your sting?*[^47] But the body which is now not mortal is not properly called flesh and blood, which are earthly bodies: but it is called a body, which now can be called heavenly. As the same Apostle, when he spoke of the difference of fleshes: *Not all*, he says, *flesh is the same flesh. One indeed is of men, another of beasts, another of fish, another of birds, another of serpents. And there are*, he says, *heavenly bodies, and earthly bodies*[^48]. But in no way would he say heavenly fleshes; although fleshes can be called bodies, but earthly. For all flesh is a body: but not every body is flesh; not only because a heavenly body is not called flesh, but even certain earthly ones themselves, such as wood and stones, and whatever is of the kind. Even so, then, flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God: because the flesh, rising again, will be changed into such a body, in which now there will not be mortal corruption, and therefore not the name of flesh and blood either.

**19. 22.** But attend, brothers, we ask you; it is not a thing to be lightly esteemed, it is about our faith: against which we must beware not so much from the pagans, as from certain perverse men, who wish to be called and seem Christians. For there were not lacking even under the Apostles those who said that the resurrection had already taken place, and perverted the faith of some, of whom the Apostle says: *Who about the truth*, he says, *have erred, saying that the resurrection has already taken place; and they have subverted the faith of some*[^49]. But it is not without point that he did not say: They have strayed from the truth; but: *about the truth*, yet they did not hold the truth. Death, then, is taken away, and in a certain way will not be: as the Apostle says: *The mortal will be swallowed up by life*[^50]. For so it was said of the Lord too, that he swallowed up death[^51]. For death does not as it were withdraw having some substance of its own: but in the very body where it was, it will cease to be, so that you see the appearance, hold the appearance, seek corruption and mortality, and do not find it. Has corruption, then, withdrawn somewhere? No: but there it was destroyed, there it was swallowed up. Therefore, when he had said: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality: then will come about*, he says, *the saying which is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where is, O death, your strife? where is, O death, your sting?* And he did not say: Death has withdrawn into victory; but: *Death is swallowed up in victory.* How, then, did those err about the truth? Because they said that one resurrection is true, but denied the other.

**20. 23.** For there is a resurrection according to faith, in which everyone who believes rises in spirit. For he will rise well in body, who first rose in spirit. For those who have not first risen in spirit by faith, will not rise to that change in body, where all corruption will be taken up and swallowed up, but to that penal wholeness. For even the bodies of the impious will be whole, nothing of them will appear diminished; but the wholeness of the body will be for punishment, and a certain, so to speak, a certain firmness of the body, a corruptible firmness: because where there can be pain, it cannot be said that there is no corruption; although that weakness will not fail in the pains, lest the pain itself die. For it is not incongruously believed that corruption itself is signified prophetically by the name of worm, and the pain itself by the name of fire. But because the firmness will be so great, that it neither yields to the pains unto death, nor is changed into incorruption, in which there is no pain; therefore it is written thus: *Their worm will not die, and their fire will not be extinguished*[^52]. But the change which will not have corruption will be of the saints. It will be of those, then, who now have the resurrection of the spirit by faith; of which resurrection the Apostle says: *But if you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God: savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.* As we die according to the spirit, and rise according to the spirit: so afterward we die according to the flesh, and rise according to the flesh. According to the spirit there is death, not to believe the vain things which one used to believe, not to do the evil things which one used to do. According to the spirit there is resurrection, to believe the wholesome things which one did not believe, and to do the good things which one did not do. He who thought earthly idols and figments to be gods, came to know one God, believed in him, died to idolatry, rose again in the Christian faith. He was a drunkard, he is sober; he died to drunkenness, he rose again in sobriety. So when one withdraws from all evil works, a certain death takes place in the soul, and in his good works it rises again. *Put to death*, says the Apostle, *your members which are upon the earth, uncleanness, disorder, evil concupiscence, and greed*, he says, *which is the service of idols*[^53]. With these members, then, put to death, we rise again in the good things which are contrary to them; in holiness, in tranquility, in love, in almsgiving. But as death according to the spirit precedes the resurrection which is according to the spirit: so death according to the flesh will precede the resurrection which is to come according to the flesh.

**21. 24.** Let us, then, know both, the spiritual and the bodily. To the spiritual belongs what was said: *Rise, you who sleep; and rise from the dead*[^54]; and that: *To those who sat in the shadow of death, light has arisen for them*[^55]; and that which I mentioned a little before: *If you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above*[^56]. But to the bodily resurrection belongs what the Apostle now says, who had set forth such a question for himself: *But someone says, how do the dead rise? with what body will they come?* He was dealing, then, with the resurrection of the body, in which the Lord went before his Church; of this, then, he says: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality*[^57]. On account of that which he had said: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God.* But we have in another place the most plain testimony of the apostle Paul himself about the resurrection according to the spirit, and about the resurrection according to the flesh. For a mortal body, which is animated or was animated, is called flesh. So, then, the Apostle speaks: *But if Christ is in you, the body indeed is dead on account of sin, but the spirit is life on account of justice.* Behold, now the resurrection of the spirit through justice is understood to have taken place: see whether the resurrection of the body too is to be hoped for. For he did not wish to call the mortal body mortal, but dead; and yet he opens what he meant by this in what follows. He follows, then, and says: *If, then, the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you; he who raised our Lord Jesus will give life also to your mortal bodies through his Spirit dwelling in you*[^58]. Wherefore those *erred about the truth*, they denied one resurrection. For if they denied it altogether, they would stray from the truth, not *about the truth*. But because they *erred about the truth*, they confessed one, which takes place according to the spirit; but denied the other, which is hoped for through the resurrection of the flesh, *saying that the resurrection has already taken place*: which unless they said in such a way as to forbid that which is to be in the body to be believed and hoped for, it would not be said of them: *They subvert the faith of some*.

**22. 25.** But hear now the most manifest testimony of the Lord himself in the Gospel according to John, in one place declaring both resurrections, both that which now takes place according to the spirit, and that which afterward will be according to the flesh, in such a way that he cannot at all doubt who in any way calls himself a Christian, and is subject to gospel authority; so that no access may remain to the slanderers, and to those wishing, as it were through the Christian faith, to overthrow Christians, by inserting their poisons, that they may kill the souls of the weak. But hear from the very book. For on this account I perform the office not only of disputer, but also of reader, that this our sermon may be supported by the authority of the holy Scriptures, not built upon sand by human suspicions, if perhaps something does not occur to me from memory. Hear, then, the Gospel according to John. The Lord speaks: *Amen, amen I say to you, that he who hears my word and believes him who sent me, has eternal life; and does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life. Amen, amen I say to you, that the hour comes, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who hear will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself; and he gave him the power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man. Do not wonder at this, for the hour comes, in which all who are in the tomb will hear his voice: and those will come forth who did good, into the resurrection of life; but those who did evil, into the resurrection of judgment*[^59]. I think indeed that many understand from the Lord himself in this place both resurrections, both according to the spirit through faith, and according to the flesh, distinctly and openly declared through that most well-known trumpet. Yet let us diligently consider these same words, that it may be manifest to all who hear. *Amen, amen I say to you, that he who hears my word and believes him who sent me, has eternal life; and does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life.* The resurrection according to the spirit is that which now takes place through faith. But lest it should seem set forth as though still far in the future; although he did not say: He will pass from death to life; but: *has passed from death to life*: yet lest he should seem to have used a word of past time figuratively, as is: *They pierced my hands and feet*[^60], which was foretold as still future; he follows, and explains this very thing more plainly: *Amen, amen I say to you*, he says, *that the hour comes and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who hear will live.* What he said above: *He has passed from death to life*; this he now says: *they will live.* But lest that which he said: *The hour comes*, should be hoped for through the end of the age, when also the resurrection of bodies will be; he added: *and now is.* For he did not say only: *The hour comes*; but: *The hour comes, and now is.* But those who hear this voice will live; namely with the life which he signified above, saying: *He has passed from death to life.* Here, then, he signified those who do not pertain to the punishment of judgment; because they anticipate the judgment by their faith, and pass from death to life.

**23. 26.** It remains, then, for him to show the future judgment between the good and the evil: because here he mentioned only the good as pertaining to the present resurrection, which is according to the spirit. He follows, then, and says: *And he gave him the power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man.* He conveyed according to what he received the power of judgment: *because he is*, he says, *the Son of man.* For according to that which he is the Son of God, he has eternal power with the Father. Now how the judgment will be, he consequently expounds: *Do not*, he says, *wonder at this, for the hour comes, in which all who are in the tombs will hear his voice; and those will come forth who did good, into the resurrection of life; but those who did evil, into the resurrection of judgment.* Above, when he had said: *The hour comes*; he added: *and now is*: lest that hour should be thought to be the one foretold, in which at the end of the age there will be the resurrection of bodies. Here, then, because he wished that very one to be understood, when he had said: *The hour comes*; he did not add: *and now is.* Likewise above he said that the dead hear the voice of the Son of God, but made no mention of tombs; that we might distinguish the dead through error of the mind, who now rise through faith, from those dead whose corpses are in the tombs, going to rise at the last day of the age. Here, then, that that resurrection of bodies might be hoped for at the end: *All*, he says, *who are in the tombs, will hear his voice, and will come forth.* Likewise above: *They will hear*, he says, *the voice of the Son of God; and those who hear will live.* What need was there to add: *Those who hear*, when it could be said thus: They will hear the voice of the Son of God, and will live; except because he was speaking of those who are dead by error of mind, many of whom hear and do not hear, that is, do not obey, do not believe? But those who so hear, as he wished to be heard, when he said: *He who has ears to hear, let him hear*[^61], they themselves will live. Many, then, will hear, but *those who hear, will live*, that is, those who believe. For those who hear so as not to believe, will not live: whence it appears of what death, and of what life he spoke in that place: namely, of the death which pertains to the evil alone, by the very fact that they are evil; and of the life which pertains to the good alone, by the very fact that they become good.

**24. 26.** But here, where he declares that they will rise according to their bodies, he does not say: They will hear his voice, and those who hear will come forth. For all will hear the last trumpet, and will come forth, because we shall all rise. But because not all will be changed, he follows and says: *Those who did good, into the resurrection of life; but those who did evil, into the resurrection of judgment*[^62]. So above, where one revives through faith according to the spirit, all revive to the same lot; so that their life is not distributed into blessedness and misery, but all pertain to the good part. And therefore, when he had said: *Those who hear will live*; he did not add: Those who did good, into eternal life; but those who did evil, into eternal punishment. For this very thing which was said, *they will live*, he wished to be taken only in a good sense; as also above: *He has passed*, he says, *from death to life*; nor did he say to what life; because to revive from death through faith cannot be an evil life. But here he did not first say: They will hear his voice and will live; for that very word, *they will live*, he wished to be understood in a good sense throughout that whole reading; but he said: *they will hear, and will come forth*, by which word he signified the bodily movement of bodies from the places of their burials. But because to come forth from the tombs will not be for all unto good: *Those who did good*, he says, *into the resurrection of life*; here too he wished life to be understood only in a good sense; *but those who did evil*, he says, *into the resurrection of judgment*, putting judgment, namely, for punishment.

**25. 27.** Now, then, brothers, let no one seek with perverse subtlety what kind of figure the bodies will have in the resurrection of the dead, what stature, what movement, what manner of walking. It is enough for you that your flesh rises in that appearance in which the Lord appeared, namely in the appearance of a man. But do not, on account of the appearance, fear corruption: for if you do not fear corruption, you will not fear that sentence either: *Flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*; nor will you fall into the snare of the Sadducees, which you would for this reason be unable to avoid, because you think that men rise for this, to take wives, and beget children, and do the works of mortal life. If you ask what the life will be like, what man could explain this? It will be the life of the angels. Whoever can show you the life of the angels, will show you their life; because they are going to be equal to the angels. But if the life of the angels is hidden, let no one seek further; lest by error he not come to that which he seeks, but to what he himself has imagined for himself. For he seeks too hastily, and seeks in haste. You, walk on the way; you will come to the homeland, if you do not abandon the way. Hold Christ, then, brothers, hold faith, hold the way: it itself will lead you to that which you cannot now see. For in that head appeared what is to be hoped in the members; in that foundation was demonstrated what may be built up in our faith, that afterward it may be perfected in sight: lest perhaps, when you think you discern, something may appear to you through a false image as if it were what it is not, and, with the way abandoned, you stray into error, and to the homeland whither the way leads, that is, to the sight whither faith leads, you do not come.

**26. 28.** You will say: How do the angels live? It is enough for you to know this, that they do not live corruptibly. For it can more easily be told you what will not be there, than what will be there. For I too, my brothers, can briefly run through for you certain things which will not be there: and this I can do for this reason, because we have experienced these things, and we know those things which will not be there. But what will be there we have not yet experienced. *For we walk by faith, not yet by sight: as long as we are in the body, we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord*[^63]. What, then, will not be there? To take a wife, that children may be propagated; because there is no death there: there will not be growing there, because not growing old: there will not be refreshment, because not failing: there will not be business there, because no need: nor those very praiseworthy works of innocent men, which the want and necessity of this life compels them to do. For I do not say only that the works of robbers or moneylenders will not be there, but those very things which innocent men do on account of the need of relieving human necessities, will not be there.

**27. 28.** It will be a perpetual sabbath, which is celebrated by the Jews temporally, but by us understood eternally. There will be ineffable rest, which cannot be unfolded; but, as I said, it is somehow unfolded, when the things which will not be there are spoken. To that rest we tend, to it we are spiritually reborn. For as we are carnally born to labors, so we are spiritually reborn to rest: with him crying out: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you*[^64]. Here he feeds, there he perfects: here he promises, there he renders: here he signifies, there he expresses. But when in that blessedness we shall be perfect and saved both in spirit and in body, these affairs will not be; nor will those be there which here are praised in the good works of Christians. For who is not praised as a Christian for holding out bread to the hungry and drink to the thirsty, clothing the naked, receiving the pilgrim, making peace among the quarrelsome, visiting the sick, burying the dead, consoling the mourner? Great works, full of mercy, full of praise and grace. But not even these themselves will be there: because the need of misery procured the works of mercy. Whom will you feed, where no one hungers? To whom will you give drink, where no one thirsts? Or are you going to clothe the naked, where all are clothed with immortality itself? You heard a little before of the tunics of the saints, the Apostle speaking: *This corruptible must put on incorruption*[^65]. Where "put on" sounds, it indicates a garment. This garment Adam lost, so that he received skins. Or are you going to receive the pilgrim, where all live in their own homeland? Will you visit the sick, where all will thrive with the same firmness of incorruption? Are you going to bury the dead, where they always live? Are you going to reconcile the quarreling, where all things are in peace? Are you going to console the sad, where all will rejoice forever? Since, then, the miseries will cease together, these works of mercy will cease.

**28. 29.** What, then, will be done there? Have I not already said that it is easier for me to say what will not be there, than what will be there? This I know, brothers, that we shall not be sleeping in idleness, because sleep too has been given as a relief for the failing of the soul. For the fragile body would not bear the perpetual intention agitating the mortal senses, unless, with the senses lulled to sleep, the fragility itself were repaired to bear the same agitation; and as renewal will come from death, so now from sleep comes waking. There, then, it will not be. For where there is no death, there will not be even an image of death. Nor yet let the fear of weariness creep up on anyone, when it is said to him that he will always keep watch, and will not do anything. I can say, and indeed how it will be I cannot say, because I cannot yet even see it: yet I say something not shamelessly, because I say it from the Scriptures, what our action will be there. Our whole action will be Amen and Alleluia. What do you say, brothers? I see that you have heard and rejoiced. But do not again be saddened by a carnal thought, because if perhaps one of you should stand and say daily: Amen and Alleluia, he would wither with weariness, and in those very words would doze off, and would now wish to be silent: and therefore think the life to be despicable to himself, and not desirable, saying to yourselves: We are always going to say Amen and Alleluia, who will endure? I will say, then, if I can, as much as I can. We will not say Amen and Alleluia with passing sounds, but with the affection of the mind. For what is Amen? what is Alleluia? Amen is "it is true": Alleluia is "praise God." Because, then, God is unchangeable truth, without defect, without advancement, without loss, without increase, perpetual without any inclination to falsehood, and stable, and ever abiding incorruptible; but these things which we do in the creature and in this life are as it were figures of things through the significations of bodies, and certain things in which we walk by faith; when we shall see face to face what we now see through a mirror in an enigma[^66], then with a far other and ineffably other affection we will say: It is true; and when we say this, we will surely say Amen, but with an insatiable satiety. For because nothing will be lacking, therefore satiety: but because that which will not be lacking will always delight, therefore there will be a certain, if it can be said, insatiable satiety. As insatiably, then, as you will be satisfied with truth, with so insatiable a truth will you say: Amen. Now what it is no one can say: *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^67]? Since, then, without any disgust and with perpetual delight we shall see the truth, and behold it with most certain evidence, kindled with love of the truth itself and clinging to it with a sweet and chaste embrace, and that an incorporeal one, with such a voice too we will praise him, and will say: Alleluia. For exulting, with most ardent love toward one another and toward God, to a like praise, all the citizens of that city will say: Alleluia, because they will say: Amen.

**29. 30.** This life of the saints, then, will so fill even their bodies, changed into a heavenly and angelic state, and will immortally invigorate them, that no corruption of need will turn or call them away from that most blessed contemplation and praise of the truth. So for them the truth itself will be food: but the rest itself as it were a reclining. For what was said, that reclining they will feast, as the Lord says: *That many will come from the east and the west, and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of my Father*[^68]: this was signified, that with great rest they will be fed with the food of truth. For such food refreshes, and does not fail; it fills and is whole: you are consumed, it is not consumed. Such is that food, not like this, which fails that it may refresh, and lest it end the life of him who receives it, it itself is ended. That reclining, then, will be everlasting rest: those feasts, unchangeable truth will be: that feasting, eternal life will be, that is, knowledge itself. Because *this is*, he says, *eternal life, that they may know you the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^69].

**30. 31.** For that that life will abide in the contemplation of truth not only ineffably, but also delightfully, Scripture attests in many places, all of which we cannot mention. Hence is that: *He who loves me keeps my commandments; and I will love him; and I will show myself to him*[^70]. As though the fruit and reward were sought from him, because his commandments are kept: *I will show*, he says, *myself to him*; setting perfect blessedness in his being known as he is. Hence is that too: *Most beloved, we are the sons of God, but it has not yet appeared what we shall be. We know that when he appears, we shall be like him; because we shall see him as he is*[^71]. Therefore the apostle Paul too: *Then*, he says, *face to face*[^72]: because he also said in another place: *We are transformed into the same image from glory to glory, as by the Spirit of the Lord*[^73]. And in the Psalms he says: *Be at leisure, and see that I am the Lord*[^74]. Then, then, we shall best see, when we shall most be at leisure. But when shall we most be at leisure, except when the laborious times have passed, the times of needs, by which we are now bound, as long as the earth brings forth for sinful man thorns and thistles, that in the sweat of his face he may eat his bread? With the times of the earthly man, then, in every part finished, and the day of the heavenly man in every part perfected, we shall most see; because we shall most be at leisure. For with corruption and need ended in the resurrection of the faithful, there will be no reason for laboring. For as though it were said: Recline and eat; so it was said: *Be at leisure and see.* We shall, then, be at leisure and shall see God as he is; and seeing him we shall praise God. And this will be the life of the saints, this the action of those at rest, that without failing we shall praise. We shall not praise for one day: but as that day has no limit of time, so our praise will have no limit of ceasing; and therefore unto ages of ages we shall praise. Hear too the Scripture saying to God what we desire: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; unto ages of ages they will praise you*[^75]. Turned to the Lord, let us beseech him for ourselves and for all his people standing with us in the courts of his house: which may he deign to guard and protect; through Jesus Christ his Son our Lord, who lives and reigns with him unto ages of ages. Amen.
