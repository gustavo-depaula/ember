# Sermon 314: On the Feast of Stephen the Martyr

**1.** Yesterday we celebrated the birthday of the Lord; today we celebrate the birthday of the servant: but we celebrated the birthday of the Lord, on which he deigned to be born; we celebrate the birthday of the servant, on which he was crowned. We celebrated the birthday of the Lord, on which he received the garment of our flesh: we celebrate the birthday of the servant, on which he cast off the garment of his flesh. We celebrated the birthday of the Lord, on which he was made like us: we celebrate the birthday of the servant, on which he was made near to Christ. For as Christ by being born was joined to Stephen, so Stephen by dying was joined to Christ. But the Church frequents the day both of the nativity and of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ with the homage of a twofold devotion, since each is a medicine. For he was born, that we might be reborn: he died, that we might live forever. But the martyrs came to evil contests by being born, dragging original sin: but by dying they passed to most certain good things, ending all sin. For if the rewards of future blessedness did not console those set in persecution, when would they sustain those torments from various sufferings? If blessed Stephen, set under a shower of stones, had not thought of future rewards, how would he have borne that hail? But he carried in his mind the precept of him whose presence he beheld in heaven; and, suspended toward him by a most ardent love, he desired to leave the flesh as quickly as possible, and to fly to him: nor did he now fear death, because he saw living Christ, whom he knew to have been killed for him; and through this he too hastened to die for him, that he might live with him. For what the most blessed Martyr saw, set in that struggle, you recall without doubt his words, which you are accustomed to hear from the book of the Acts of the Apostles. *Behold,* he says, *I see the heavens opened, and Christ standing at the right hand of God*[^1]. He saw Jesus standing: therefore he stood, and did not fall; because Jesus, standing above and watching from above his soldier contending below, supplied unconquered strength to him, that he should not fall. *Behold,* he says, *I see the heavens opened.* Blessed man, to whom the heavens lay open. But who opened heaven? He of whom it is said in the Apocalypse: *Who opens, and no one shuts; shuts, and no one opens*[^2]. When Adam was cast out of paradise, after that first and unspeakable sin, heaven was closed against the human race: after the passion of Christ the thief entered first, afterward Stephen saw it opened. Why do we marvel? That he faithfully saw, faithfully made known, and forcibly entered?

**2.** Come, brothers, let us follow him; for if we follow Stephen, we will be crowned. But he is especially to be followed and imitated by us in the love of enemies. For you know that, surrounded by a crowded gathering of enemies, when he was being battered by frequent blows of stones from this side and that, placid and intrepid, mild and gentle among the stones by which he was being killed, gazing on him for whom he was being killed, he did not say: Lord, judge my death; but, *receive my spirit*[^3]. He did not say: Lord Jesus, avenge your servant, whom you see condemned to this punishment of death; but, *do not lay this sin to their charge*[^4]. So the most blessed Martyr, persisting in the testimony of the truth, and burning with the spirit of love, as you know, came to a most glorious end; and he who, having been called, persevered to the end, obtained in the end what he was called, and Stephen, by the glory of his name, was led to the crown. So when blessed Stephen first shed his blood for Christ, the crown, as it were, came forth from heaven; that those who followed should receive it as a reward, who imitated the courage of the one going before in the battle. Afterward frequent martyrdoms filled the earth. Whoever afterward shed their blood for the confession of Christ, placed that crown on their head, and kept it whole for those who would follow. And now, brothers, it hangs from heaven: whoever longs for it will fly swiftly to it. And to exhort Your Holiness briefly and clearly, there is no need of many words: let whoever desires the crown follow Stephen. Turned to the Lord, etc.

[^1]: Acts 7:56
[^2]: Rev 3:7
[^3]: Acts 7:59
[^4]: Acts 7:60
