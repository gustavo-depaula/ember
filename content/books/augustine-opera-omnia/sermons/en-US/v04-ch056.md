# Sermon 136a

**1.** The Lord Jesus *came into this world to save sinners* [^1]. He found, then, a man blind from birth. For who of men is not born blind? In mind I say, not in body. But, that he may see, he is anointed with spittle and clay; but not with any spittle, not of anyone, but of Christ. The spittle of Christ is prophecy, the clay is men. Recall from what man was made. When, then, men prophesied, there was spittle in the clay. What shall I say of the ancient prophets? The Apostle himself says: *We have this treasure in earthen vessels* [^2]. This treasure, behold you have spittle: from it first this man was anointed; and everyone who is born blind is anointed from this, and sent to the pool of Siloam. For could Christ not open his eyes from his own spittle? In short, he could even without spittle and without clay command that he should see, and it would be done. He could: but the doings of miracles are, as it were, the words of sacraments. Therefore he is sent to the pool of Siloam. Why these delays? We acknowledge the power. What you will you do, Christ; let this blind man now see. No, he says; let him go first to the pool of Siloam, and wash his face. Thanks to the holy Gospel, it interprets for us the pool of Siloam. *Siloam*, it says, *which is interpreted sent* [^3]. Who is this sent one? Acknowledge the sent one; he cries: *The Father sent me* [^4]. Therefore he sent him to himself: he sent the one believing to baptism. He washed his face, and saw; his sins were blotted out, and the light shone forth. But now in that, questioned, and so harried by the Jews, he answered thus, he was already anointed in heart. But when he washed his face from Siloam, the reading itself testifies. He was, then, still anointed, did not yet see, when he said: *We know that God does not hear sinners* [^5].

**2.** What hope is there for men, if God does not hear sinners? Did not two go up into the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a Publican? Did not the Pharisee say: *I give you thanks, that I am not like the rest of men, the unjust, plunderers, like this Publican too* [^6]? He asked nothing, he had gone up as it were satiated, and was belching forth his fullness. He did not say: Help me; he did not say: Have mercy on me, *because my father and my mother forsook me*; he did not say: *Be my helper, do not forsake me* [^7]. But the Publican stood afar off. A wondrous thing: in the temple he stood afar off, and he himself drew near to the God of the temple. He stood afar off, then, *nor dared to lift his eyes to heaven: but he beat his breast, saying: Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*. We have heard the dispute: let Christ speak the sentence. Behold he says it, let us hear: *Amen*, he says, *I say to you, that the Publican went down from the temple justified, more than that Pharisee* [^8]. Surely God does not hear sinners. When he beat his breast, he punished his sins; when he punished his sins, he drew near to God the judge. For God hates sins: if you too hate them, you begin to be joined to God, so that you say to him: *Turn your face from my sins*. Turn your face: but from what? From my sins; *do not turn your face from me* [^9]. But what is: *your face from my sins?* Do not see them, do not acknowledge them, that you may pardon me. Therefore there is hope even for the sinner; let him ask God, let him not despair, let him beat his breast, let him take vengeance on himself by repenting, lest he take vengeance by judging. Humbled, he draws near to the Most High.

**3.** But why the Lord said: *The Publican went down justified, more than that Pharisee*, he at once added the reason, he did not defraud you. For he said, as though we said to him, why this? *Because everyone who*, he says, *exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted* [^10]. You have heard why: if you have heard and understood, do what you have heard, humble yourself, ask God, tell the Lord your God that you are a sinner; what he sees, even if you do not say it. You say perhaps: If he sees it before I say it, what need is there for me to say it? O man, you have forgotten: *It is good to confess to the Lord*? you have forgotten: *Confess to the Lord, for he is good* [^11]? Even if you do not confess to a man as judge, because he is evil, confess to the Lord, for he is good; confess, groan, repent, beat your breast. Such a spectacle delights God, where he sees his sinner avenging his own sin. You acknowledge it, and he pardons; you punish, and he spares. But that he may spare you, you ought not to spare your sins. Answer: Let him not spare it, let him blot out my iniquity.

**4.** After many things, that man, seeing from blind, was cast out of the synagogue of the Jews; they were angry at him, they cast him out of their synagogue. Behold what his parents feared; the Evangelist expounded this to us, he did not keep silent about it: *For his parents feared*, he says, *lest they confess Christ, and be taken out of the synagogue; and therefore they said: He is of age, ask him* [^12]. They feared, then, to be cast out of the synagogue: he did not fear, and was cast out; his parents remained there. Christ remains as receiver, that this man may say: *Because my father and my mother forsook me*. And what did he add? *But the Lord took me up* [^13]. Come, Christ, take him up; they cast him out, you receive; take up, you the sent one, the one not admitted. Behold he takes him up: he shows himself to the eyes which he himself deigned to open. *Do you*, he says, *believe in the Son of God?* And he, still anointed: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?* And the Lord: *You both have seen him, and he who speaks with you is the one* [^14]. He washed his face. Now then, seeing with his heart, he adored his Savior. This Christ Jesus does, as it were, to the human race born blind, still anointed bodily, on account of the miracle; but he did a miracle, that he might commend faith; he commended, from that miracle of the opened eyes of the blind man born, that faith by which daily he opens the eyes of the human race and of that very blind man born.
