# Sermon 366: On Psalm 23 (22):1: "the Lord Guides Me, and Nothing Will Be Lacking to Me"

**1.** A psalm, dearest ones, you who are hastening to the Baptism of Christ, in the name of the Lord we hand over to you to be committed to memory; whose mystery it is necessary that we expound, with divine grace enlightening. For this psalm specially contains the reformation of the fall of the human race, and the discipline of the holy Church, and at the same time the sacraments. Let the ears of your heart, then, lie open in silence to hear, and let the furrows of your whole attention, prepared, find the seed of the word: that what now the thirsty earth receives, in the accepted time, made drunk with the blood of Christ, germinating in a tall stalk, may bring forth abundant fruits.

**2.** *The Lord guides me, and nothing will be lacking to me*[^1]. A great beginning, dearest ones, of one confessing. For he confirmed the trust of his defense, he strengthened the infinite substance of his riches. *The Lord guides me*, he confirmed the trust of his defense. *And nothing will be lacking to me*, he strengthened the infinite substance of his riches. But let us seek whose, of what greatness, and of what kind this confession is. It is his, his, dearest ones, who, going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, fell among robbers[^2]. Who, despoiled of the dignity of his first origin, and laid low on the ground by the dart of death, lay without strength and naked. Who, with the trumpet of the Law and the Prophets sounding, while he tries to rise by his own strength, drawn back by the pain of the wound, fell more grievously into the fall where he lay. Because the Law, as the Apostle says, *brought nothing to perfection*[^3]. Whom that Samaritan of ours, namely Christ, who was called a Samaritan by the Jews, which is interpreted Guardian, having mercy as he passed by the same way, that is, when in the flesh he had come to die for us sinners as a just man, lifting him from the earth, set on his own beast; and carrying the one who strayed like a sheep on his own shoulders, called him back to paradise, from where he had fallen, to the number of a hundred, that is, to the perfect number[^4]. For he, as the prophet says, *bore our sins, and grieved for us*[^5]. Now say, O man, say, sitting on the beast of mercy, and on the shoulders of the Lord's love, you who, recognized, recognize your author and Lord: *The Lord guides me.* Which surely, unless raised by the Lord, lying you could not say. He himself, then, guides you, who carries you. For when you say: *The Lord guides me*; there is nothing of your own, whence you may trust in yourself. And so beware lest you be exalted by your merits. For there were none, when the Lord came to raise you. Finally he found you naked, not clothed; wounded, not whole; lying, not standing: he found you straying, not returning. Beware boasting, beware; because he who, having mercy, lifted you half-dead from the earth, carries the humble, casts down the one exalting himself. For when you say, fearing, and walking in innocence: *The Lord guides me*; you will trustingly add, *and nothing will be lacking to me.* Since nothing is lacking to those who fear him[^6]; and the Lord will not deprive of good things those walking in innocence[^7].

**3.** For that you may know that nothing will be lacking to you, add what follows: *In a place of pasture there he has set me; upon the water of refreshment he has brought me up*[^8]. Recognize, O man, what you were, where you were, under whom you were. You were a straying sheep, in a pathless and waterless place, you were fed on thorns and thistles: set under a hireling, with the wolf coming, you were not secure. But now, sought again by the true shepherd, carried by his piety on his shoulders, you have been led back to the sheepfold, that is, to the house of God, namely the Church, where your shepherd Christ, and the sheep gathered together, dwell. This shepherd is not like the hireling, under whom, wretched, you labored, under whom you feared the wolf. But do you wish to know how great care this good shepherd has of you? He laid down his soul for you. For he himself says in the Gospel: *The good shepherd lays down his soul for his sheep*[^9]. This he did. For when the wolf lay in wait for you, he offered himself to be slain for you. Now, then, you remain secure in the sheepfold. Nor do you need anyone else, who may shut and open the door of your fold: because Christ is for you both shepherd and door; he himself both pasture and provider. *I am*, he says, *the door of the sheep. Through me if anyone enters, he will be saved; and he will go out and come in, and will find pasture*[^10]. The pastures, then, which this good shepherd prepared for you, in which he set you to be satisfied, are not a variety of green herbs, of which some are of sweet juice, some most bitter, which by the succession of times sometimes are, sometimes are not. Your pastures are the words of God and his sweet commandments, sown. Of these pastures he had tasted who said to God: *How sweet to my jaws are your words, above honey and the honeycomb to my mouth*[^11]! Of these pastures this same one cries out to the sheep of the Lord, and says: *Taste and see how sweet the Lord is*[^12]. Read, then, the decalogue of the Old Testament: *You shall not kill, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false witness*[^13], and the rest. Read the praise of the precepts of the New Testament: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the land as an inheritance*[^14]: and the rest that follow, or are similar, and many things sown through the Prophets and Apostles. Of these pastures surely the shepherd cries to the sheep: *Work for the food which does not perish*[^15]. But for this reason it does not perish, because the word of the Lord abides forever. The word of the Lord is your food, and not only food, but also drink. Hear him saying through the prophet to the old people: *Those who eat me will still hunger; and those who drink me will still thirst*[^16]. Likewise through himself: *My flesh*, he says, *is truly food, and my blood is truly drink*[^17]. But these pastures are not far from the water of refreshment. The Church, the catholic Church of God, is one place for these; where the commandments of life are your pastures, and the fountain of water springing up to eternal life, with whose streams you will be refreshed, when you are baptized to be restored in Christ. Unless, then, your pastures are watered by this water, you cannot be brought up: because the commandments of God cannot germinate without the Baptism of Christ, nor be eaten to the satisfaction of the soul.

**4.** When, then, through the water of refreshment of Christ you begin to be fit to be satisfied with the savor of the sweet pastures; then you will know, and will cry out rejoicing, and saying: *He has converted my soul, he has led me along the paths of justice for his name's sake*[^18]. The devil through sin overturned your soul, and turned it away from God; which God the Father through Christ, not by your merits, but for his own name's sake, converted to himself. Now, then, enlightened, now converted, now believing, now satisfied with the divine pastures through the water of refreshment, you will say: *He has converted my soul.* Good is the glory of your confession, if there is not a change of your conscience. For then truly and with unchangeable conscience you will say this, when you say it not on account of your own merit, but for his name's sake. But what are the paths of justice, along which he has led you? Hear your leader. *How wide*, he says, *and spacious is the way which leads to perdition; but narrow and strait is the way which leads to life*[^19]. For every path is short and a shortcut. Not, then, through the delights of the world, through the abundance of gold and silver, conspicuous too with precious stones and garments, endowed with every honor, with nobility, swollen with all the wisdom of the philosophers, has God ordained you to go to the kingdom of heaven: because all these things, and things like them, which they use badly, which it is given to use well, become for them a wide and spacious way, that is, only in the hope which is seen; and it leads them, when they have been deprived of the present life, not to the hope in which they had no hope, but to perdition. For they slept their sleep, and all the men of riches found nothing in their hands[^20]. He has disposed you to go along the paths of justice, that is, through mercy and truth. For all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth[^21]. With the wide and spacious way despised, he wishes you to walk the narrow and short paths, that is, through hunger and thirst, through nakedness, through fasting, through ignobility, through poverty, through patience, through contempt of all present things, but having the promised hope. But do you wish evidently to know the shortcut of the paths, along which you are bidden to walk? *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul; and your neighbor as yourself. For on these two commandments depend the whole Law and the Prophets*[^22]. That, then, you may quickly arrive, if you are hastening to the kingdom of heaven, walk in these two paths, which are the head of all, and make one way; that, when you have arrived, you may rejoice to have completed them all diligently and without weariness.

**5.** Hold, then, these paths, in these let your steps remain, amid the snares of the raging devil, that you may securely sing to God, and say: *For even if I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will not fear evils; for you are with me*[^23]. The shadow of death is the way of sin, in which the rascal and pirate devil spreads the nets of deception for those walking rightly. It is a shadow, because nothing is common to light with darkness. The works of this shadow the Apostle teaches must be cast off, saying: *Let us cast off the works of darkness, and put on the arms of light; as in the day let us walk honorably, not in carousings and drunkenness, not in beds and shameless deeds, not in contention and rivalry*[^24]. As long, then, as you remain in the present life, in the midst of vices, of worldly afflictions, which are the shadow of death, you walk. Let Christ shine in your heart, who enlightens the lamp of our mind through the love of God and neighbor: and you will not fear evils, since he himself is with you. *I will not*, he says through the prophet, *desert you, nor abandon you*[^25]. Likewise in the Gospel: *Behold, I will be with you all the days even to the consummation of the age*[^26]. Your guardian is fit. The Lord your God is with you. Beware lest by boasting you desert your companion, and remain deserted in the shadow of death.

**6.** When, therefore, you recognize that you are assailed by the snares of the enemy and terrified in the shadow, take hold of the rod of discipline, and lean trustingly on the staff of mercy, that, when Christ the sun of justice has shone to your aid, you may truly say: *Your rod and your staff, they have consoled me*[^27]. For the rod governs the proud, according to what is said in the second psalm of Christ: *You will rule them with a rod of iron, and like a potter's vessel you will shatter them*[^28]. But the staff sustains the weak and the tired. Remember, then, the rod of correction and discipline, that when you are filled with the good things of the gifts of God, you may not be exalted by pride, and murmur against him: because like a potter's vessel, angry, he will shatter you in it. Remember too the help of the staff, and do not trust in your own strength, nor say: I am holy, I cannot totter. Our weakness has many falls, nor is it governed by the holiness of its own works, while still placed on the earth giving thorns and thistles, nor does it have the glory of purity. As long, then, until it returns to the earth from which it was taken, unless it is governed by the staff of divine grace, it cannot stand. Whether, then, you prosper in God, or are troubled by the storm of temptations, cast yourself wholly on the staff of God's mercy, that when upon it you recline to be fed with spiritual gifts, delighted by the taste of its sweetness, you may fittingly say: *You have prepared in my sight a table against those who trouble me. You have anointed my head in oil, and your inebriating cup, how excellent it is!*[^29] This, supported throughout the world by the staff of grace, the whole Church sings. This against heretics, Jews and Gentiles, who trouble her by mocking, she preaches, glorying not in herself, but in the Lord. For the table of joy is the passion of Christ, who offered himself for us on the table of the cross as a sacrifice to God the Father, granting to his catholic Church a vital banquet, satisfying us namely with his body, and inebriating us with his blood. Fed and made alive by this table, against those who trouble her, the Church exults, having the hope of eternal life through her life the Lord Christ, who anointed her abundantly with the oil of gladness through the Holy Spirit. On account of this table the Apostle rebuked the Corinthians reclining in the idol-temple, saying: *You cannot share the table of the Lord and the table of demons: you cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of demons*[^30].

**7.** When, then, the divine grace, to which you are hastening, has carried you in, you will recognize the table of the spiritual banquet, so that, the truth recognized, each one of you, exulting, and giving thanks to God, may now fittingly and trustingly say: *And your mercy will follow me all the days of my life*[^31]. A great consolation of glory accompanies you. On account, namely, of the misery of your weakness, the mercy of God follows you. But first, that it may show you the way of eternal life, it goes before you, that is, precedes you, according to what he says in another psalm: *My God, his mercy will go before me*[^32]. His mercy, then, goes before you, that is, leads you into the way when you do not know the way, calls you to God when made far from God; draws the slave of sin, that it may make him free, that by walking in the way all the days of your life you may not stray. It also follows you, guarding your back, lest the serpent the devil, lying in wait for your heel, with whom you have enmity, supplant you. For a robber either kills by meeting you, or rising up behind your back. On this account the mercy of God both precedes and follows you, that in the middle you may walk safe and secure all the days of your life. Have, then, hope and glory, not in yourself, but in the preceding and following mercy of God: by which you, a sinner, were prevented, that you might be saved; you were not found just, that you might glory in having pleased.

**8.** But see whither he leads you, if you do not desert the one leading. Not into the field of worldly misery, that among thorns and thistles you should seek bread in labor and sweat; not to the dangers of the sea, that for the sake of trade you should chase uncertain gains in a fragile ship, where with the intention of gain many have sunk. He leads you to the house of God, not as a guest for a time, that you should migrate from it; but as a dweller, that you may remain in it. For it follows: *And that I may dwell in the house of the Lord for length of days*[^33]. This house of the Lord is paradise. Length of days is eternal life. There you will not hunger, not thirst, nor will you labor in the heat of sun and moon, you will not feel cold and the storms of winter. Sadness and mourning are not in it. In the fellowship always of the saints you will be blessed. You will rejoice with them, and exult, living and praising God unto ages of ages. For he says in another psalm: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; unto ages of ages they will praise you*[^34]. This is the hope of your faith, dearest ones. You have come to believe in the Lord; hasten and give effort to lay hold by good conduct of what you have believed. For you are not made Christians on account of the present life, but on account of the future life, which the Lord Christ himself bestows on you believing and persevering in him: who lives and reigns with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Spirit, unto ages of ages. Amen.
