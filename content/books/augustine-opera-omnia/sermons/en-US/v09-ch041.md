# Sermon 331: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1. 1.** By that evangelical trumpet, when the Lord says: *He who loves his soul will lose it; and he who loses it for my sake will find it*[^1], the martyrs were kindled to battle; and they conquered, because they presumed not on themselves, but on the Lord. *He who loves his soul will lose it.* In two ways can what was said be understood: *He who loves his soul will lose it.* If you love it, you lose it. And in another way: Do not love it, lest you lose it. The former way has this sense: If you love it, lose it. If, then, you love it, if you love it, lose it. Sow it here, and you will reap it in heaven. If the farmer does not lose the wheat in the seed, he does not love it in the harvest. The other way is thus: Do not love it, lest you lose it. Those seem to themselves to love their souls, who fear to die. If the martyrs had so loved their souls, without doubt they would have lost them. For what would it profit to hold the soul in this life, and lose it in the future? what would it profit to hold the soul on earth, and lose it in heaven? And what is it to hold it? how long to hold it? What you hold, perishes from you: if you lose it, you find it in yourself. Behold, the martyrs held their souls. And how would they be martyrs, if they had always held them? But behold, if they had held them, would they have lived until this day? If by denying Christ they had held their souls in this life, would they not long since have passed from this life, and lost their souls altogether?

**2. 1.** But because they did not deny Christ, they passed from this world to the Father. They sought Christ, by confessing; they held him, by dying. So at a great gain they lost their souls; losing hay: meriting a crown: meriting a crown, I say, and holding life without end.

**2. 2.** So it comes to pass, indeed it has come to pass in them, what the Lord added next: *And he who loses his soul for my sake will find it*[^2]. *He who loses it,* he says, *for my sake.* The whole cause is there. *He who loses it,* not in any way whatever, not for any cause whatever, but *for my sake.* For the martyrs had already said in prophecy: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long*[^3]. Therefore it is not the punishment that makes the martyr, but the cause.

**3. 2.** When the Lord suffered, the cause distinguished the three crosses. He was crucified between two thieves: on this side and that the criminals fixed, in the middle he himself. And as though that wood were a tribunal, he condemned the one insulting, he crowned the one confessing. What will he do as judge, if he could do this when judged? So now he was distinguishing the crosses. For, if the punishment were questioned, Christ was like the thieves. But if anyone questions the cross, why Christ was crucified; he answers us, For your sake. So let the martyrs too say: And we died for your sake. He for our sake, we for his sake. But he for our sake, that he might confer a benefit on us: we for his sake, not to impart a benefit to him. Accordingly, in both ways counsel was taken for us: both what flows from him comes to us, and what is done for his sake returns to us. For he is the one of whom the soul says, which rejoices in the Lord: *I said to the Lord, You are my God; for you have no need of my goods*[^4]. For what is, *my goods,* but those given by you? And how does he need any good, from whom is given every good?

**4. 3.** He gave us nature, that we might exist: he gave a soul, that we might live: he gave a mind, that we might understand: he gave nourishment, that we might sustain our mortal life: he gave light from heaven, fountains from earth. But all these are gifts common to good and bad. If he gave these also to the bad, does he then keep nothing proper for the good? He keeps it plainly. And what is that which he keeps for the good? *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^5]. For what ascended into the heart of man was below the heart of man: therefore it ascended into the heart, because above it is the heart by which it ascends. What he keeps for the good, the heart ascends there. Not what ascends into your heart, but to where your heart ascends, this God keeps for you. Do not, when you hear, Lift up your heart, hear it as deaf. So what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man; the eye has not seen, because it is no color; the ear has not heard, because it is no sound; nor has it ascended into the heart, because it is no earthly thought. So understand: *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love him*[^6].

**5. 4.** Still you will perhaps ask me what this is. Ask him, who has begun to dwell in you. Yet I too say what I feel about it. For you ask this, what God keeps as proper for the good, if he lavishes so much on both bad and good. And when I said: *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man,* there are those who say, Do you suppose what it is? Behold what it is that God keeps for the good alone, whom yet he himself has made good: behold what it is. Briefly our reward was defined through the prophet: *I will be their God, and they will be my people*[^7]. *I will be their God:* he promised himself to us as a reward. Seek another, if you find a better. If I said, He promised gold; you would rejoice: he promised himself, and are you sad? If a rich man does not have God, what does he have? Do not seek anything from God, except God. Love freely, desire him alone from him. Do not fear poverty: he gives himself to us, and suffices us. Let him give himself to us, and suffice us. Hear the apostle Philip in the Gospel: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^8].

**6. 5.** Why, then, do you marvel, brothers, if the martyrs, lovers of God, bore so much, to acquire God? See how much the lovers of gold suffer. They commit themselves to sailing through the harshness of winter: so they boil with avarice, that they dread no cold; they are tossed by the winds, suspended and plunged by the waves; by unspeakable perils they are driven even to death. Let them too say to gold: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long*[^9]. Let the martyrs say to Christ: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long.* The voice indeed is similar: but the cause is very dissimilar. Behold both said, these to Christ, those to gold: *For your sake we are put to death all the day long.* Let Christ answer his martyrs, If you will die for me, you will find both yourselves and me. But let gold answer the greedy, If you are shipwrecked for me, you will lose both yourselves and me. So loving and imitating; not loving in vain, but loving and imitating, let us celebrate the days of the martyrs, and mitigate these our heats with the refreshment of joys. For with them we will reign without end, if we love them faithfully and not vainly.
