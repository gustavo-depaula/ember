# Sermon 335/E: On the Martyrs

**1.** The Lord wanted us to celebrate with you the solemn day of the blessed martyrs. From this, then, let us speak something, which the Lord may grant, who wanted the ark, in which he figured the Church, to be built of squared timbers. For a square, wherever in the dust, you find standing. A marvelous thing and as it were impossible but yet attend and you will find it; a square can be thrown down, it cannot fall. The martyrs were thrown down on the earth of humility but they did not fall, because in heaven they are crowned. There was no martyr who was not square in truth.

**2.** The glory of the martyrs in their solemnities can be noted by men, but how great it is in the sight of God cannot be seen. *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^1]. How precious, and indeed in the sight of the Lord! For when they were killed in the sight of men, they were cheap. How would the blood of the martyrs have been shed so abundantly unless it had been cheap to those shedding it? Those who killed the martyrs did not know that he was sowing. For from those few falling into the earth this crop rose up. So precious, even then when it seemed cheap to human eyes, in the sight of the Lord was the death of his saints, and how precious! What was the price of the death, but the death of the holy of holies? What is the holy of holies? It is known, there is no need for us to say it. Why then do we marvel if the death of the saints is precious, for whom the holy of holies died? He himself was that first grain of which this is. Of himself he spoke in the Gospel: *A grain of wheat,* he says, *unless it falls into the earth remains alone. But if it falls and dies it bears much fruit*[^2]. Christ was sowing and the Church was sprouting. And the grain fell and the grain rose and the grain ascended into heaven where there is a multitude of grains. Ask the Psalm; the grain that fell, where is it? *Be exalted above the heavens, O God*[^3]. The crop, where is it? Why, before I spoke, did you cry out, except because you too belong to that crop? Yet I will say also what you know. For it delights me to say what I ought, that from this I may merit to have what I believe. Where is that grain which fell? *Be exalted above the heavens, O God.* Where is his crop? *And over all the earth your glory*[^4].

**3.** But perhaps, dearly beloved, you may have noticed in the holy readings which were recited a little before a certain not contemptible question. For in the book of holy John which is called the Apocalypse, when the glory of the martyrs was read, it was said of them: *And in their mouth no lie was found and they are blameless*[^5]. Again the psalm which was sung and which we heard, suiting the martyrs: *Every man is a liar*[^6]. The martyrs are praised and it is said: *In their mouth no lie was found.* And again from the voice of a martyr it is said: *I said in my ecstasy: every man is a liar.* If *every man is a liar,* then he who said this is a liar, but because he who said this is not a liar, then *every man is a liar.* And yet he himself who said this, because he was a man, was he not a liar? How did he say truly: *Every man is a liar?* If I found one man truthful, I would refute the sentence saying *every man is a liar.* So many thousands were found of those who were read from the Apocalypse, in whose mouth no lie was found, and it is read to me: *Every man is a liar.* Or perhaps we should sympathize with this man who said it, since he said it troubled. For he says: *I said in my ecstasy.* "Ecstasy" is understood as fear. For many manuscripts, where it is written: *I said in my ecstasy,* have: *I said in my fear.* Some have: *in the excess of my mind.* Since, then, some put "excess of mind," others put "fear," he who put the very Greek word thought it a saving of effort, lest the rashness of translation should weaken the force of the expression. Perhaps, then, since fear is a terror moving the mind from its place, therefore he could not absurdly translate it who said "fear," so that it agreed with him who said: *in the excess of my mind.* Perhaps, then, so that, set in trial and prepared to deny, he may have said in his fear: *Every man is a liar,* that is: What need is there that by speaking the truth I should die? But if I deny Christ and say what is false, I will remain a liar: *Every man is a liar.* Let him not believe his fear, in his fear perhaps he is deceived. And when he was trembling he received strength to say: *O Lord, I am your servant and the son of your handmaid: you have broken my bonds*[^7]. My great bond was the love of living, and in it the cause of dying. For many by loving to live have died forever. And again, many martyrs by despising life with an end have obtained life without end. As he who loves money, often by loving money neglects money, that by neglecting money he may gain more money. Whence the well-known saying of a certain known man: "To despise money at the right place is sometimes the greatest gain." And this the moneylenders do. Money, by giving, they acquire, as though they sow a little to reap much. So too the martyrs, by loving life despised life. By fearing death, they died; by wanting to live, they refused to live. So many bonds hold the soul: love of riches, love of powers, love of the affection of wife, parents, children, brothers, love of the homeland, love of one's land, love of this light, love of any kind of life only because it is life. By these bonds, then, while it is held back, it came to the trial, that if it confesses Christ all would be broken. Terrified and bound by so many tethers, fearing to be loosed, he said in his fear: *Every man is a liar.* Let us pardon him fearing, let us favor him rejoicing: *You have broken my bonds.* Love made truthful him whom fear had made a liar. See Peter saying: *With you even to death*[^8]. See him promising in presumption, denying in fear; troubled by the questioning of one maid he said: *I do not know, I am not acquainted*[^9]. Do you think he did not at once say: *Every man is a liar?* But he wept bitterly and with most abundant tears washed away the filth of fear. And the Lord rose again and strengthened him by the example of his resurrection. He saw living him whom he had grieved over dying. Now Christ no longer found him fearing and denying. For why did he deny, except that he feared to die, except that he despaired of the resurrection? The Lord rose, showed himself to his eyes, strengthened him in love by asking him three times about love, who had three times denied him in fear, poured in the Holy Spirit. The short life grew cheap, the infinite was loved, and Peter suffered. Fear perished, love conquered. When he was questioned: *Every man is a liar.* When he suffered: *You have broken my bonds.*

**4.** We can say this, and on account of the fear of the speaker not reckon true the sentence by which it was said: *Every man is a liar.* But the apostle Paul corrects this sense, who set this testimony from the Scriptures and said: *God alone is truthful, but every man is a liar as it is written*[^10]. So *every man is a liar.* Understand it as the voice of truth, not of fear, for if you understand it as the voice of fear, not of truth, you charge the Apostle with falsehood. It is altogether true: *Every man is a liar.* And it is true what was said of so many thousands of men: *In their mouth no lie was found.* When the martyrs suffered, they confessed Christ, they did not deny that they were Christians. That they had held an assembly against the command of the kings, then unbelievers, they professed without doubt and trembling. On account of this no lie was found in their mouth. And if perhaps before they had any lies, on account of which it is true: *Every man is a liar,* they washed those away by that truth, they covered them by that love, because *love covers a multitude of sins*[^11].

**5.** Still let the more diligent searcher of the Scriptures question me and say to me: What then, when they were truthful, were they not men? In confession, in passion, when they were truthful, surely then were they not men? But if they were men, and were truthful, how is it true: *Every man is a liar?* I say plainly, I say without injury to the martyrs, indeed I say with the glory of the martyrs, that *he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^12]. Plainly insofar as they were men, they were liars. How then did they speak truly? Because the Lord had said to them: *For it is not you who speak*[^13]. Have you not heard today's reading too from the holy Gospel: *Do not premeditate what you may say: for I will give you a mouth and wisdom*[^14]. *I will give you,* for *every man is a liar.* So every man of his own a liar, of God truthful. In himself a liar, in God truthful. *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^15]. Attend to Peter himself: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^16]. A truthful man, but surely not because a man? Not of his own, because *every man is a liar.* But from where? Hear the Lord himself: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood,* that is, man, because *every man is a liar, did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^17]. So of your own a liar, of mine truthful. *For all things that the Father has are mine*[^18]. Finally a little after, do you want to hear Peter among all men, because he too is a man, do you want to hear Peter a man and therefore a liar? At once the Lord began to foretell his future passion and death. Where is Peter? Where the man? *Far be it from you, Lord, this shall not happen*[^19]. A man, a lying man, as a man of his own. And do you want to know that of his own? What did the Lord at once say? *Get behind me, Satan, you are a stumbling block to me*[^20]. Why, except that he lied, except that, deceitful, he spoke his own? But from where does he lie? *For you do not savor the things that are of God, but the things that are of men*[^21].

**6.** So all good things are to us from the highest good. All good things are to us from the perpetual fountain of goodness. Unless perhaps we have goodness from God and patience of our own. For thus the reading of the Gospel closed: *And in your patience you will possess your souls*[^22]. So someone will say: Our truth is not ours, since *every man is a liar,* and the one, who is of God, is truthful, when truthful. But our patience is ours. The Lord himself says: *In your patience you will possess your souls.* Do not be exalted, lest you lose what you have received. For it is your patience for this reason, because you have it, if indeed you have it. It is yours because you have it, not because you have it from yourself. *For what do you have that you have not received?*[^23] As we say: Give us this day our daily bread, and you say "our" and you say "give us." So what you call "our," is not from you so as to be yours. For if God did not make ours what he gave, he surely would not give it; what he gives you, by receiving becomes yours. But let us prove this of patience, lest perhaps the bread be so, but the patience be not so. Who said to us: *In your patience you will possess your souls,* who but Christ, but God? To whom we say: *Shall not my soul be subject to God, for from him is my patience?*[^24] He himself says to us: *In your patience you will possess your souls.* We say to him: *For you are my patience*[^25]. Whence the weak may say to virtue, if he wants to have virtue: *I will love you, O Lord, my strength*[^26]. Let us pray that we may have what we do not yet have. Let us not be ungrateful for the things we have. *For we have not received the spirit of this world, but the Spirit which is from God, that we may know the things that have been given us by God*[^27].
