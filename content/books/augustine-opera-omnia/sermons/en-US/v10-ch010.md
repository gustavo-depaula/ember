# Sermon 346/B: On the Pilgrimage of This Life

**1.** That this life of ours, dearest brothers, is a certain pilgrimage away from the homeland of the saints, the heavenly Jerusalem, the apostle Paul most plainly teaches, saying: *As long as we are in the body, we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord*[^1]. And because every pilgrim surely has a homeland—for no one is a pilgrim without a homeland—we ought to know what our homeland is, to which, neglecting all the allurements and delights of this life, we must hasten to that toward which we are tending, and where alone it is allowed us to rest. For God did not wish us to have true rest anywhere except in that homeland: for if he gave rest here too, it would not delight us to return. Calling this homeland, then, Jerusalem—not that earthly one, which is in slavery with her children[^2], as the same Apostle admonishes: for that one was given on earth, for the sake of the shadow of a certain signification, to carnal men, who, though worshiping one God, yet desired earthly happiness from him. But there is another Jerusalem, which he says is in the heavens: for Jerusalem *which is above is free, which is the mother of us all*[^3]. He calls her mother, as a metropolis: for metropolis too is interpreted as mother-city. Therefore we must hasten to her, and recognize that we are pilgrims, and are on the way.

**2.** Every man who does not yet believe in Christ is not even on the way: for he wanders; and he himself seeks a homeland, but does not know by what way, does not know where. What is it that I say, he seeks a homeland? Every soul seeks rest, and seeks blessedness; no one, asked whether he wishes to be blessed, answers with any doubt that he wishes it: every man cries out that he wishes to be blessed; but by what way one goes to blessedness, and where that blessedness is found, this men do not know: therefore they wander. For no one wanders who goes nowhere: by going, and not knowing by what way one goes, all wandering arises. The Lord recalls us to the way; and when we are made faithful, believing in Christ, we are not yet in the homeland, but yet we have already begun to walk on the way. As, then, we exhort and admonish all our dearest ones—if indeed we remember that we are Christians—those who wander in empty superstitions and heresies, that they may come to the way, and walk on the way; so too those who are already on the way ought to exhort one another. For no one arrives, except one who is on the way: but not everyone who is on the way arrives. Those, then, are held in greater danger who do not yet hold the way; but those who are already on the way ought not themselves to be secure yet: lest, held back by the delights of the way itself, they be not snatched with so great a love toward that homeland where alone is rest. For our steps on this way are the love of God and of neighbor. Whoever loves, runs: and the more closely each one loves, the more eagerly he runs; but the less each one loves, the more sluggishly he moves on the way. But if he does not love, he has altogether remained on the way; but if he desires the world, he has looked back from the way, he does not have his face toward the homeland. What does it profit, that he is on the way, but does not go, but returns? that is, what does it profit, that he is a catholic Christian—for this is to be on the way—and by loving the world walks on the way, but has looked back? for from where he began, there he returns. But if through some snares of the enemy tempting and robbing on this journey he is led aside from the catholic Church, either to a heresy, or to some rites of the pagans, or to whatever other superstitions and devices of the devil, he has now also lost the way, he returns to wandering.

**3.** And so, brothers, let us run on this way, because we are catholic Christians, which is the one Church of God, as it was foretold in the holy Scriptures. For God did not wish her to be hidden, that no one might excuse himself: she was foretold as going to be throughout the whole world, she has been displayed to the whole world. Nor ought the innumerable heresies and schisms to move us: they would trouble us more if they did not exist, because these too were foretold. All, whether those who remain in the catholic Church, or those who are outside the catholic Church, bear witness to the Gospel. What is it that I say? They give testimony that all things are true which were said in the Gospel. For how was the Church of God foretold as going to be among the nations? One, established on the rock, which the gates of hell shall not overcome. The gate of hell is the beginning of sin: *For the wages of sin is death*[^4], and death surely pertains to hell. But what is the beginning of sin? Let Scripture be questioned. *The beginning*, it says, *of all sin is pride*[^5]; and if pride is the beginning of sin, pride is the gate of hell. Now, then, consider what thing has begotten all heresies: you will find no other mother than pride. For when men give much to themselves, and call themselves holy, and wish to draw crowds to themselves, and to tear them away from Christ, they have made heresies—useful heresies and useful schisms—only through pride. But because by all those heresies and schisms, that is, by the children of pride, the catholic Church is not overcome, therefore it was foretold: *And the gates of hell shall not overcome her*[^6].

**4.** And so, brothers, as I had begun to say, we are on the way: let us run by love and charity, forgetting temporal things. This way seeks the strong, and does not want the slothful. The robberies of temptations abound: the devil lies in wait in every throat[^7], everywhere he tries to enter and to possess; and whomever he possesses, he calls back from the way, or delays him: he calls him back, and makes him not advance, or even go astray, so that he may be ensnared in errors and in the heresies of schism, and be led aside to some kinds of superstitions. For he tempts either through fear or through greed; but first through greed, through some promises and pledges, or through the seductions of delights. When he has found that a man has despised these, and has as it were closed against him the door of greed, he begins to tempt through the door of fear; so that, if you now wished to acquire nothing in this world, and for that reason had closed the door of greed, you have not yet closed the door of fear, if you fear to lose what you had acquired. Be strong, then, in faith[^8]: let no one seduce you to fraud through any promise, let no one drive you into fraud through any threat. Whatever the world promises you, the kingdom of heaven is greater; whatever the world threatens you with, hell is greater. Therefore, if you wish to escape all fears, fear the everlasting punishments which God threatens. Do you wish to trample all the greeds of concupiscence? Desire eternal life, which God promises. So you close this door to the devil, you open this one to Christ.
