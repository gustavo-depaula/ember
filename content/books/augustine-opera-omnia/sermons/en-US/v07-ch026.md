# Sermon 252/A: In the Days of Easter, a Sermon of Saint Augustine on the Gospel, John 21

**1.** According to the Evangelist John many things are written, how the Lord Jesus appeared to his disciples after the resurrection. And he appeared to them also as they were fishing. This fishing of the disciples took its beginning at the sea of Tiberias, where the Lord wished to show himself to them; which contains a great sacred meaning, already known to you, but now to be briefly recalled by us.

**2.** Recall that fishing, when he called fishers of fish, and made them fishers of men, in his first preaching, far from the passion. For then, as you ought to remember, he came to the disciples, not yet disciples; for then they followed him, leaving their nets. And he found that they had caught nothing the whole night, and said to them: *Cast your nets into the sea.* And they cast them, and caught so many fish that two boats were filled, were pressed, and almost sank. Then by the multitude of fish the nets were broken. Then he said to them: *Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men.* And he did, and they cast the nets of the apostolic word through the sea of the world, and caught many fish. If you wish to consider the number of fish, the innumerable number, consider the multitudes of Christians: these are caught in the holy nets, caught for life, not for death. But yet from those very many that were caught, many schisms too were made, because the nets were broken. The two boats too, that is, the two little boats that were then filled, signified those coming from the circumcision and the uncircumcision, that is, the Church gathered from the Jews and the gentiles. Hence Christ is called the cornerstone; because in the corner the walls coming from different directions, as it were, kiss one another. Therefore those boats were filled, pressed almost and sunk. This signified Christians living badly, and pressing the Church with bad conduct. But yet the boats were not sunk; for the Church tolerates those living badly: it can be pressed, it cannot be sunk. But now after the resurrection Christ signified the Church such as it will be after our resurrection: for then it will have only the good, no bad one mixed in, a happy Church.

**3.** What, then, did he say then to the disciples after the resurrection? *Cast the nets on the right side.* In that earlier fishing he had not said, on the right side, lest he signify the good only; nor did he say, on the left, lest he signify the bad only; but the nets were cast at random, because it was going to have good and bad. So the Lord Christ himself narrated by a certain likeness. For he says: *The kingdom of heaven is like a net cast into the sea, in which fish of every kind were gathered. When they have brought it to the shore, they sit, and choose the good and put them in their vessels; but the bad they cast outside.* He proposed, and expounded. *So it will be also at the consummation of the age*, he says: *the angels come, and gather the bad from the midst of the just, and cast them into the furnace of burning fire; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^1]. Let the good fish, then, swim with the bad, let the bad swim with the good; let them swim among the nets, and not break the nets; for those who break the nets are bad! but those who remain within the nets are both good and bad, but only for now.

**4.** But then what did he say? *Cast the net on the right side.* What does it mean, on the right side? You are going to catch on the right side those who will stand on the right. For he is going to say to those who are on the right: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^2]. These he signified, when he commanded that the nets be cast on the right side. They were cast: they caught many and great fish, and he did not pass over the number in silence. In that first fishing, which signifies the good and the bad, he did not give the number: for there were supernumeraries there. Who are the supernumeraries? Those who do not belong to the number of the saints; from whom come those who break the nets, make schisms; from whom come those who renounce the world by words and not by deeds, who receive the sacred sign of the new man, and persevere in the old man. These, then, were going to be there. Therefore it was not said: on the right side; therefore the number was passed over in silence. They themselves are the supernumeraries, of whom the Psalm says: *I have announced and spoken, they are multiplied beyond number*[^3]. There was the holy number there, there were also many beyond the number: but here no one is beyond the number.

**5.** And how many were they? *One hundred fifty-three.* Is this the whole number of the saints? Far be it that even this church alone in which we now speak should have so few. What then? We who do not know ought to come to know, and we who know to remember: let the suggestion do the one, the reminder the other, lest forgetfulness creep in. *One hundred fifty-three*, he says. It belongs to the Evangelist to say this. *Though they were so great*, he says, *the net was not torn*[^4]; as one who recalled that in the first fishing those nets were broken. Now what? And though they were so great, he says, the net was not torn. Who there now fears schisms, where the bosom of unity and the germ of mother Church cannot be torn? For no friend is separated from her, no enemy is joined to her: for all who cling to her will be chosen, will be perfect. There will be thousands of thousands, and more than thousands of thousands, and yet they will be reckoned by this number.

**6.** This number rises from seventeen: whoever wishes to count from one up to seventeen, and add them all, will find it. He set down one: let him add two also, that they become three; let him add three also, that they become six; let him add four also, that they become ten, and so on let them arrive at the number seventeen, and he will find one hundred fifty-three. It remains that I be asked what seventeen means. If we find the reason of this lesser number, that is seventeen, the sacred meaning of the greater, that is one hundred fifty-three, will be clear. Here in seventeen is the root; there the tree. So what does seventeen mean? Ten signify the law: for the ten precepts of the law are written on two tablets of stone by the finger of God, as the law says, as the holy Books testify. For the law is marked by the number ten. But who fulfills the law without help? Absolutely no one. *For if a law had been given that could give life, righteousness would entirely be from the law. But Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise from faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*[^5]. The law was given. Not to say many things, among the other precepts it has, *You shall not covet your neighbor's goods.* Do not covet: do not pass before another's farm, and sigh, because it is good. Do not covet your neighbor's goods. *The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof*[^6]: what have you not acquired, if you have held God? Do not, then, covet your neighbor's goods. The law was heard, and from fear of punishment certain men held themselves from the bad deed; yet they did not hold themselves from the bad delight. Therefore, Lord, give help: *For he will give a blessing, who gave the law*[^7]. But as it was said here: He who gave the law will give a blessing, that is, the help of the Holy Spirit, that the law may be fulfilled, so it was said of the wisdom of God, *she carries the law and mercy on her tongue*[^8]. If she carried the law alone for herself, who would endure? The deeds of the law would be demanded, and all would be found guilty. Mercy was added, which helps you to do, forgives what you do not do. This mercy is from the Holy Spirit: but the Holy Spirit is commended in the Scriptures by the number seven. From one place I recall it. Isaiah too says: *The Holy Spirit will rest upon him.* And he enumerates: *The Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and fortitude, the Spirit of knowledge and piety, the Spirit of the fear of God*[^9]. This Spirit is added, and seven and ten come to be. When the seven have been added to the ten, they make the saints, not believing from the law, but presuming on the help of God; so that it is said to one's Lord: *Be my helper, do not forsake me, nor despise me, O God of my salvation. For my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the Lord has taken me up*[^10]. And these have learned to say with you: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^11]. Mercy, then, was added to the law. Let us not fear, we who are in seventeen; for if we are in seventeen, we will arrive at one hundred fifty-three; and if we arrive at one hundred fifty-three, we will be on the right; if we are on the right, we will receive the kingdom.
