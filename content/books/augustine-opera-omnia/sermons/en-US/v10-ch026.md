# Sermon 352/A

**1.** The Lord Jesus, the founder of the holy Gospel, as it were by a law of mercy wishing no one to perish[^1], since indeed he came to save what had perished[^2], as it were in an assembly of the human race cried out, as we have heard: *Because the times are fulfilled and the kingdom of God has drawn near, repent and believe in the Gospel*[^3]. This voice the founder of the world uttered, and the world heard. Let that be done which he said who came to come to our aid, lest he come to judge the despisers. Let it be done, while the voice of mercy sounds, before the tribunal of justice comes. He of whom it was said in the psalm, *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^4], himself kept the order of his help. First he came to dispense mercy, going to exercise judgment. For not judgment, but *mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*. What greater mercy can be desired, sought, demanded, than that God did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all, that with him he might give us all things[^5]? What greater mercy, than to come to die not for the just, but for the impious[^6]? What greater mercy, than to come and not to condemn those worthy to be condemned, but to free those unworthy to be freed?

**2.** But let this mercy not be despised, my brothers. *Merciful and compassionate is the Lord.* Add still: *long-suffering.* Add still: *and greatly merciful.* Fear what follows: *and truthful*[^7]. Far be it from us that we despise the merciful one, so as to experience the truthful one! What need is there, then, that we cry out? Let us rather hear the one crying out: *Because the times are fulfilled*. For *the fullness of times came*, as the Apostle says, and in the fullness of times *God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, and that we might receive the adoption of sons*[^8]. Commending this fullness of times, the Lord said: *The times are fulfilled*; he came, awaited by few, found by many. Therefore *the times are fulfilled: repent.* How long ago is it that this is cried out, and would that at some time it were heard: *Because the times are fulfilled and the kingdom of God has drawn near, repent and believe in the Gospel*! Renounce and convert. This is *repent and believe in the Gospel*: renounce dead works, believe in the living God. What does it profit to believe without good works? It was not the merit of good works that brought you to faith, but faith begins, that good works may follow[^9]. This is said to the holy Church in the Song of Songs: *Come, my bride, you will come, and you will pass through from the beginning of faith*[^10]. But who believes God, unless he has renounced the devil? Therefore, that you may renounce, *repent; believe*, that you may be saved.

**3.** This was said to the Jews, this was said to idolatrous gentiles. Is it still to be said to Christians? Is it still to be said to Christians daily reading that *the times are fulfilled*, is it still to be said to Christians that *the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*, is it still to be said to Christians: *Repent and believe in the Gospel*? Plainly, he to whom I say, Believe in the Gospel, is not yet a Christian. No one, as I suppose, hears me in this multitude who does not yet believe in the Gospel. Many hear me who are not yet made faithful through baptism, and are still catechumens: not yet born, but already conceived. But when or how would they be conceived in the womb of mother Church, unless they were signed with some sacrament of faith? Many also hear me who are baptized and, neglecting in themselves the grace of God[^11], love to sin. And to these what shall I say: *The times are fulfilled?* They answer: "We know it." *The kingdom of God has drawn near?* They answer: "Long ago we heard, long ago we believed." What, then, shall I say? Since I am speaking to men who love to sin, despising in themselves the grace of the Redeemer, what shall I say? *Repent.* I will plainly say it, even if they do not permit me to say: *The times are fulfilled*, since they know it; *The kingdom of heaven has drawn near*, because this too they know; that very last thing which was set down, *Believe in the Gospel*, they do not permit me to say to them, because they have already believed; let them hear, then: *Repent*, because they have not yet done it. From here, then, let us speak to the catechumens and to the negligent faithful. Let the diligent hear, since they too cannot be lacking in the members of Christ. Let them not think me wordy and superfluous, if they have love: it is a common business; they wish me to speak also to those whom they desire to be corrected along with them.

**4.** Let them, then, hear me saying not, *The times are fulfilled, the kingdom of heaven has drawn near, believe in the Gospel*, but saying, *Repent.* The catechumen answers me: "Why do you say to us: *Repent?* Let me first be faithful, and perhaps I will live well, and I will not be a penitent." I answer such people: That you may be faithful, *repent.* For those coming to baptism come through penance. For unless by repenting they condemn the old life, they will not as candidates come to the new life. That I might say this, I have not dared beyond measure, nor usurped to myself anything unaccustomed to the Scripture of God. Let the catechumens hear the Acts of the Apostles. When the apostles, filled with the Holy Spirit, spoke to the crowds, because they were speaking in all tongues, the crowds, astonished and made more attentive, heard the word of God from the apostles[^12]. All, because they were men of diverse tongues, *were pierced*, and because they were Jews, they repented of having crucified Christ, and, troubled about their own salvation and fearing certain damnation for so great a crime, said to the apostles: *What shall we do, brothers?* And the apostles: *Do penance, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ*[^13]. You have received how those not yet baptized were ordered to come to baptism through penance. This, then, we too will say to the catechumens. For we have mentioned the testimony of the Scriptures, lest they should think we say to them shamelessly that, before they were faithful, we wished them to be penitents.

**5.** But now let those who are faithful and live badly not think that I say to them shamelessly: *Repent.* Now, then, I will speak to both: Change your life, lest you lose life. Condemn past sins, fear future evils and hope for good things. The evil man first does not contradict himself in hoping for good things, who is not good. You hope for good: be what you hope. First hear the one commanding, and then demand of the one promising. God has become your debtor not by borrowing, but by promising. *For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor, or who has first given to him and it shall be repaid him?*[^14] What has he received from you, who gave you everything? Therefore he deigned to become a debtor, because he promised, and so to become a debtor that he even made a bond. This bond is the Gospel, it is the Scripture, in which God made himself your debtor not, as I said, by borrowing, but by promising. Therefore hear: *Repent, believe in the Gospel.* Hear the command, and then demand the bond. "Tomorrow," he says. For he found nothing to say to me, set before his own face and now displeasing to his own eyes. How much more, then, to the purer eyes of God! When, then, he had displeased himself in his evil ways, "Tomorrow," he says, "I will amend; do not press me, let today too pass. Why do you hurry? Tomorrow I will amend. Why do you press me today?" Because the day does not stand by your decision. All dispute with them is about delay. For they say: "Let today pass as yesterday did. Tomorrow I will be corrected, changed, amended, I will fulfill what is commanded: I will repent," that is: the whole thing is about delay. Tomorrow is not far off. But see, lest, just as you wished today to be like yesterday, so you wish tomorrow to be like today. Although I could say: Why not today, but tomorrow, you who do not know what will happen to you tomorrow? But behold, let us bear with it. Do not make tomorrow like today. "For what," he says, "if I do? Do I not hold it written: *On whatever day the unjust and impious man is converted, I will forget all his injustices*[^15]? It was said, and divinely said." I admit: it was divinely written. How well you remembered the bond of God! What is owed to you, you hold; what you owe, you forget.

**6.** Impious men hear these things, not knowing the law, banished from the grace of God, unworthy of the mercy of God, because it was said in our Scripture: *On whatever day, if the unjust man is converted, I will forget all his iniquities*[^16], and they say to us: "You make men sin, who promise them such great impunity if they are suddenly converted." For do we dare not to promise what God has promised? "Not good," they say, "is the law which makes sinners abound." I would like to know whether he who speaks does not judge himself a sinner. And he is angry with God who waits, whom God waits for. What if God, who by his patience leads to penance, did not promise this[^17]? If he did not promise this, would not more become sinners by despairing? Let your love attend a little. Who is found not a sinner? The flesh weak, the age feeble, the sense narrow, the mind fearful, nature ignorant of things—into how great sins does it cast the incautious and negligent headlong? How few can appear and exist who are not convicted of being sinners? Take away that mercy, take away that promised pardon, take away in this most turbulent sea of iniquities the harbor of indulgence, let there be no place to which the tossing may withdraw: do they not by despair add sins to sins, and say to themselves: "Now I am a sinner, there is no mercy upon sinners, condemnation is necessarily owed? Why do I not do what I wish, why do I not satisfy my lusts, why do I not at least now do whatever delights me, doomed to lose this life, going to receive none afterward?" So are desperate gladiators, whence too a proverb arose: Back away from the wounded man. For the wounded man seeks where he may die, and does not doubt to do evil, because he hopes for no pardon. He lets the reins loose to his desires, he is carried where he can, he desires whatever pleases him to be lawful for him: he perishes, then, by despairing. But do not you perish by hoping either. Two voices, both contrary, both dangerous, through which men perish, but yet God despised neither voice: he cared for both this and that. What are those two voices? "Why should I not sin, why should I not do whatever I wish? When I convert, all is forgiven me." Such perish by hoping. The other voice, on the other side contrary indeed, but equally dangerous. "Why should I not do whatever I wish, to whom no mercy is owed, to whom no pardon will be given? I will do whatever I wish." They perish by despairing.

**7.** What shall I accuse first: perverse hope or unfaithful despair? Why do you perish by hoping, why do you perish by despairing? Let those hear a few things who wish to perish by hoping: *Return, transgressors, to the heart*[^18]. Hear God saying, because you say "Tomorrow, tomorrow," *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord*[^19]. To you I speak, who prepare to be converted to the Lord, but defer it; to you I speak, for whom I fear lest hope destroy you: hear not me, but him himself in whom you hope and by hoping too much you perish: *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day. For suddenly his wrath will come, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^20]. Where is what you were saying: "I defer to tomorrow, and tomorrow to another tomorrow, since God promised me indulgence, saying: *On the day the sinner is converted*; for he did not say: If today he is converted, but: *On whatever day he is converted*"? God answers you: "I promised you indulgence, I did not promise you tomorrow." Or do you perhaps wish to join and couple together two promises, what God promised and what the astrologer promised? For God promised indulgence, the astrologer perhaps promised you tomorrow. Watch out, wretch: the astrologer is deceived, and God condemns you. How many, suddenly snatched away, were permitted neither to make satisfaction to God nor to accuse the astrologers.

**8.** Let him too hear, who had perished by despairing, what he was saying: "I have now committed such great evils that I hope for no indulgence. It remains to fulfill my lusts, to satisfy my delights, that I may at least have here the rest which I am not going to have there." Hear you too the voice of God, not your own: "Despairing one, return to hope." Hear Paul saying: *Therefore I obtained mercy—I who persecuted the Church of God—that in me Christ Jesus might show all his long-suffering, for the instruction of those who will believe in him unto eternal life*[^21]. You have, then, a pattern: do not despair, hear the bond which a little before the one falsely hoping recited, and from it let the despairing one be corrected. What were you hearing? Whence did he flatter himself, because he put it off from day to day? "God, God to me: *On whatever day the unjust and impious man is converted, I will forget all his iniquities*[^22]." Read, and return. You were sitting on the bank, or carrying the noose by despairing, you desired to bring death upon yourself, perhaps, having nothing to live on, you wished to die: return, you have something to live on: behold the bread of God. *I do not wish the death of the impious, so much as that he turn back and live*[^23].

**9.** Therefore, brothers, there is no reason to complain about God, there is no place where he should displease the perverse, although he cannot displease any but the perverse. The straight does not please the perverse, the distorted cannot be fitted to the straight. For there is nothing to be said. You were saying: "Behold, Christians give impunity: they make sin abound." I have given the reason, that men would perish more by despairing, if no harbor of indulgence were set forth to the tossing one. Both, then, God did not abandon, both those hoping badly and those despairing badly. On account of those despairing badly he gave the harbor of indulgence, on account of those hoping badly he made the day of death uncertain. There is nothing to be said, but there is something to be done. Let them say to me, then: "What shall we do?", those who have been moved by the word of truth; let them say it not with their voices, but with their hearts: "What, then, shall we do?" What shall I answer, except what we have heard: *Repent*? What more shall I say? *Repent.* You are a catechumen: repent and you will be renewed; you are baptized badly, not badly baptized: repent and you will be healed. I do not find what to say to this, you know what to do. Turned to the Lord . . .
