# Sermon 373: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1.** The solemnity which we celebrate today received the Greek name Epiphany on account of the manifestation of the Lord. For on this present day, with the magi adoring him as it were the firstfruits of the Gentiles, he is commended as manifested, who a few days ago is recorded also as born. That cornerstone, which joined as it were two walls, namely of the circumcision and the uncircumcision, that is, of the Jews and the Gentiles, coming from diverse directions, in its own unity, *and became our peace, who made both one*[^1]; that he might be announced to the shepherds of the Jews, angels came from heaven[^2]; and that he might be adored by the magi of the Gentiles, a star shone from heaven[^3]. Whether, then, through angels, or through a star, the heavens declared the glory of God: that in the grace of him who was born, the apostles too might declare it, carrying the Lord like the heavens, and their sound might go out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world[^4]. Which things, since they have come to us too, we believe, therefore we also speak[^5].

**2.** Many things, then, are to be considered, brothers, in this gospel reading which we have heard. The magi come from the east, they seek the king of the Jews, whom so many kings of the Jews never sought before. But they seek, not someone of manly age, or aged, conspicuous to human eyes, on a lofty throne, powerful with armies, terrifying with arms, gleaming with purple, refulgent with a diadem [or exulting for himself on the cross, on which he might redeem all the martyrs confessing him, or rising from the dead, or ascending into the heavens]; but one newly born, lying in a cradle, gaping at the breasts, with no adornment of body, with no strength of members, with no resources of parents, surpassing neither by his age, nor by the power of his own. And they seek the king of the Jews from the king of the Jews; from Herod [a man], Christ [God and man; from an earthly king, a man, the king of heaven who had founded man]: from a great one a little one, from a famous one one hidden, from a lofty one a humble one, from a speaking one an infant, from a wealthy one a poor one, from a strong one a weak one; and yet [though from Herod the persecutor, the Lord ruling over himself and others], from a despiser one to be adored: surely in whom no royal pomp was seen, but true majesty was adored.

**3.** Finally Herod fears: the magi desire. They long to find a king, he fears to end his reign. Both at last seek: they, one through whom they may live; he, one whom he wishes to kill: he, against whom he may commit a great sin; they, who may forgive them all their sins. For Herod kills many little ones, while he wishes to come to the death of one. And when he has accomplished a most cruel and most bloody slaughter in so many innocents[^6]; he first slew himself by so great an iniquity. Meanwhile our king [Christ], the Word [of God] an infant [God], while the magi adored him, while the little ones died for him, whether he lay, or nursed; and not yet speaking, he was finding believers; and not yet suffering, he was even making martyrs. O blessed little ones, just now born, never tempted, not yet having struggled, already crowned! Let him doubt of your crown in the passion for Christ, who thinks that the Baptism of Christ does not profit even the little ones. You had not indeed the age, in which you could believe in Christ who was to suffer: but you had the flesh, in which for Christ who was to suffer you might sustain suffering. In no way would the grace of the infant Savior desert these infants, who had come to seek what had perished, not only by being born in the flesh, but also by hanging on the cross [by descending to the lower regions, and ascending into the heavens, and sitting at the right hand of the Father]. For he who, being born, could have angels as preachers, the heavens as narrators, the magi as adorers, could also grant to them that they should not die here for him, if he knew that they would perish by that death, and not rather live by a greater happiness. Far be it, far be it, that [Christ], coming to free men, should have done nothing about the reward of those who were killed for him, who, hanging on the wood, prayed for those by whom he was being killed[^7].

**4.** What shall I say of the unhappiness of the Jews, who, with the magi seeking Christ, even brought forth the prophecy as an indicator, designated the city of Bethlehem [which they themselves did not find]? Like the builders of Noah's ark, they furnished others a place where they might escape, and they themselves perished in the flood[^8]: like milestones, they showed the way, and could not walk [because, stupid, they remained on the way]. It was asked of them where Christ would be born; they answered: *In Bethlehem of Judah. For so it is written through the prophet: And you Bethlehem, land of Judah, are not the least among the leaders of Judah. For from you shall come forth a king, who is to rule my people Israel*[^9]. The inquirers heard and went away; the teachers spoke and remained: and separated by contrary affections, the former became adorers, the latter persecutors. Now too the Jews do not cease to display to us something similar. For some of the pagans, that they may know Christ was foretold beforehand, when we bring forth to them clear testimonies from the Scriptures, suspecting lest perhaps these were fabricated by the Christians, prefer to believe the books of the Jews: and just as the magi did then, they leave these people to read on emptily, while they themselves go on to adore faithfully.

**5.** Let us therefore celebrate, rejoicing in the Lord, this festal day of the Lord—not only the day on which he was born of the Jews, but also the day on which he was made manifest to the Gentiles. It is a delight to gaze upon all these things, to bring them together into one view, and to behold them with the mind in a spiritual spectacle. Christ is born: a virgin conceives, a virgin gives birth, a virgin nurses; fruitfulness is present, yet integrity is not absent. Angels announce, shepherds glorify, the heavens declare, the Magi long for him, kings dread him, the Jews point him out, the Gentiles adore him: the raging are frustrated, infants are crowned, believers are filled with wonder. What is this lofty lowliness, this strength in weakness, this greatness in a little child? Surely that Word made all these things, the Word through whom all things were made. The Word who was far from us *was made flesh, that he might dwell among us*[^10]. Let us therefore acknowledge, within time, the one through whom times were made; and as we celebrate his festivals in time, let us long for the rewards that are eternal.
