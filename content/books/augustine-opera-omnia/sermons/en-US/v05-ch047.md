# Sermon 182

**1. 1.** When the apostle John was being read, we heard the Holy Spirit speaking through him and saying, *Beloved, do not believe every spirit; but test the spirits, whether they are from God*. I repeat, because it is necessary that I repeat, and that I vehemently impress this upon your minds, as far as the Lord helps. *Beloved, do not believe every spirit; but test the spirits, whether they are from God. For many false prophets have gone out into this world*[^1]. The Holy Spirit commanded that we not believe every spirit: and he stated the cause why he commanded this. What is that cause? *Because many*, he says, *false prophets have gone out into this world*. Whoever, then, despises these precepts, and thinks that every spirit should be believed, must necessarily run into false prophets, and, what is worse, blaspheme the true Prophets.

**2. 2.** Here now a man made cautious by this precept is going to say to me: I have heard, I hold it, I desire to obey, because I too do not wish to run into false prophets. For who would wish this, to be deceived by liars? For a false prophet is a lying prophet. Give me a religious man, he does not wish to deceive; give me an ungodly, sacrilegious man, he wishes to deceive, he does not wish to be deceived. Since, then, the good do not wish to deceive, and neither the good nor the evil wish to be deceived, who is there who would wish to run into a false prophet? I say the words of one consulting me: but surely no one runs into a false prophet but unwillingly. I have heard the precept of John—rather, of the Lord through John: *Do not believe every spirit*. Behold, I accept it, so I wish. He adds and says: *But test the spirits, whether they are from God*. Whence do I test them? I would wish to test, if I could not err. Surely, unless I test the spirits that are from God, I will necessarily run into spirits that are not from God, and from this I will be seduced by false prophets. What shall I do? How shall I observe it? Oh, if holy John, just as he said to us: *Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are from God*, would deign to say how the spirits that are from God are to be tested! Do not be anxious, and hear this. *Hence is the Spirit of God known*: this surely you were waiting to hear, that you might test the spirits that are from God. *Hence is the Spirit of God known*: John said it, not I; this follows in the reading I treat. For when he made us anxious and cautious for this, that we not believe every spirit, but test the spirits that are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into this world; at once he saw what we desired, he met our expectation, he cast an eye on our silent thought. Thanks be to God, that he deigned to say this too through him. *Hence is the Spirit of God known*. Come, hear; hear, understand, distinguish; cleave to the truth, resist falsehood. *Hence is the Spirit of God known*. Whence, I ask you? This is what I desired to hear. *Every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is from God: and every spirit that does not confess Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is not from God*[^2]. Meanwhile, then, beloved, repel from your ears every disputant, preacher, writer, whisperer, who denies that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. Therefore repel the Manicheans from your homes, from your ears, from your hearts. For the Manicheans most openly deny that Christ came in the flesh. Their spirit, then, is not from God.

**2. 3.** Here I see whence the wolf wishes to creep in; I recognize it, and, as far as I am able, I show that it must be avoided. Hence, in what I said, or rather mentioned as said by the Apostle—that *every spirit that denies Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is not from God*—the Manichean lies in wait in this word, and says to me: Behold, the spirit that denies Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is not from God; whence, then, is it? If it is not from God, he says, whence is it? For can it be, except from elsewhere? If, then, he says, it is not from God, and is from elsewhere, you see there are two natures. We have found the wolf: let us stretch the wholesome nets, let us hunt, let us capture, let us slay the captured. Let us plainly slay it; let the error die, let the man live. Behold, in what I said: Let us capture, let us slay; let the error die, let the man live; there the question is resolved. But recall what I proposed, lest, having forgotten the question, you do not understand the resolution. *Every spirit that does not confess Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is not from God*.

**3. 3.** And the Manichean at once: And whence is it? If it is not from God, it is from elsewhere. If it is from elsewhere, I have taught that there are two natures. Hold this question, and recall your minds to those words of mine, where I said: Let us capture, let us slay; let the error die, let the man live. The error is not from God, the man is from God. Return to the question: *Every spirit that does not confess Jesus to have come in the flesh is not from God*. I too say: *All things were made through him*[^3]. *Let every spirit praise the Lord*[^4]. But if not every spirit is from God, how does a spirit that is not from God praise the Lord? Surely let every spirit praise the Lord. I see both, I understand the languishing one; let the vice be healed, let the nature be freed. The vice is not the nature, but is hostile to the nature. Heal whence you languish, there remains whence you praise. Medicine pursues the vices, not the nature. *Every spirit that does not confess Jesus Christ to have come in the flesh is not from God*. In so far as he does not confess Christ to have come in the flesh, in so far he is not from God; because that error which does not confess Christ to have come in the flesh is not from God. Brothers, what is it that we are reborn? If we were born well, what is it that we are reborn? The nature which had been corrupted is repaired; the nature which had fallen is raised up; the nature which lay deformed is reformed by grace. For only the Creator—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; a threefold unity, one trinity; that nature alone is unchangeable, immutable, subject neither to defect nor to progress, neither falls so as to be less, nor transcends so as to be more; perfect, eternal, in every way unchangeable, that nature alone. But the creation is good, yet very unequal to the Creator. You wish to cleave to the devil the deserter, if you strive to equate the founded to the Founder.

**4. 4.** Let the soul recognize its condition: it is not God. When the soul thinks itself God, it offends God: it does not find a savior, but finds a condemner. For God, when he condemns evil souls, does not condemn himself: but if the soul is what God is, it condemns itself. Let us give honor to our God, brothers, to whom we cry, *Free us from evil*. And if it whisper to you, that in prayer you may find a temptation, and say to you: What is it that you cried, *Free us from evil*? Surely it is not evil? Answer it: I am evil; and if he frees me from evil, I shall be from evil good: let him free me from myself, that I not run into you. Say this to the Manichean: If God frees me from myself, I shall not run into you; because if God frees me from my evil self, I shall be good; if I am good, I shall be wise; if I am wise, I shall not err; if I do not err, I cannot be deceived by you. Let God, then, free me from myself, and I do not run into you. For it is my vice, that I err and believe you: since my soul has been filled with illusions[^5]. I am not light to myself: for if I were, I would never have erred. Therefore I am not a part of God, because the substance of God, the nature of God, cannot err: but I err; for you yourself too confess it, you call yourself wise, you try to free me from error. Whence, then, do I err, if I am the nature of God? Blush, give honor to God. I say that you still greatly err: but, as you yourself confess, you had erred. Had the nature of God, then, erred? Had the nature of God gone into uncleanness? Did the nature of God commit adulteries? Did the nature of God do unlawful debaucheries? Did the nature of God, blind, not know where it was going? Was the nature of God overwhelmed with misdeeds and shameful acts? Blush, give honor to God.

**5. 5.** You cannot be light to yourself; you cannot, you cannot. *It was the true light*. In comparison with John it was said: *It was the true light*. Was not John too a lamp? *He was a lamp burning and shining*[^6], the Lord said. Is a lamp not light? But it was *the true light*. A lamp can both be kindled and be extinguished: the true light can kindle, it cannot be extinguished. *It was*, then, *the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^7]. We are to be enlightened, we are not the light. Wake up, cry out with me: *The Lord is my illumination*[^8]. What is it, then, that you say? Are there, then, no evils? There are evils, but they are changed; and they themselves will be goods: because those very evils are evils by vice, not by nature. What is, *Free us from evil*? Could we not, and can we not say these words: Free us from darkness? From what darkness? From ourselves, if there are in us any remnants of darkness, until we become wholly light, having nothing in us that resists love, that fights against truth, that lies under infirmity, that fails by the condition of mortality. Then see what the whole will be, when it will be: *This corruptible* will be clothed *with incorruption, and this mortal* will be clothed *with immortality. Then shall come to pass the saying that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your struggle? Where, O death, is your sting? But the sting of death is sin*[^9]. Where is the evil?

**6. 6.** Now the evils of men, what are they? Error and infirmity. Either you do not know what you do, and by erring you fall; or you know what ought to be done, and you are overcome by infirmity. Therefore every evil of man is error and infirmity. Against error cry out: *The Lord is my illumination*. Against infirmity add: *And my salvation*[^10]. Believe, be good; you are evil, you will be good. Do not divide. The nature in you is to be healed, not separated. Do you wish to know what you are? Darkness. Why darkness? O man who say: God is corrupted, can anything be deeper than these darknesses? Believe, acknowledge that Christ came in the flesh, that he received what he was not, did not lose what he was; that he changed man into himself, was not changed into man. Acknowledge, and you yourself too from evil will be good, from darkness will be light. Or do I lie, and have nothing whence to prove it? You accept the Apostle, if you do not pretend to accept him; you read the Apostle, and you are deceived, and you deceive. Whence are you deceived? By erring to your harm by your very self. But if you believe, and shake off the error, you will hear from the Apostle: *For you were once darkness; but now you are light*. But he added: *light*; but where? *In the Lord*[^11]. Therefore darkness in you, light in the Lord. Because you cannot shine to yourself, by approaching you are enlightened, by withdrawing you are darkened; because you yourself are not light to yourself, you are enlightened from elsewhere. *Approach him, and be enlightened*[^12].

**7. 7.** I know, dearly beloved, that I have lingered much on one matter in this reading of holy John, and I see that you are not to be wearied further, nor to be filled beyond your capacity; and our infirmity too must be considered. For these words of holy John still have their great hidden places. Meanwhile, repel those who deny that Christ came in the flesh. For it is agreed that they are not from God. In so far as they err, in so far as they sin, in so far as they blaspheme, they are not from God: let them be healed, and they will be from God; for by nature too they were from God. As much as I have disputed about this, attend to the Scriptures. Do not believe those who deny that Christ came in the flesh. But surely you will say to me: Then he who says that Christ came in the flesh is from God? Let us hear the Donatists, because they confess that Christ came in the flesh; let us hear the Arians, because they confess that Christ came in the flesh; let us hear the Eunomians, because they confess that Christ came in the flesh; let us hear the Photinians, because they confess that Christ came in the flesh. For if all spirits that confess Christ to have come in the flesh are from God, how many lying, deceitful, mad heresies there are, which yet confess that Christ came in the flesh. What, then, shall we say? How shall we resolve this question? However it is to be resolved, it cannot be resolved today. Hold me a debtor: but pray to God for me and for you, as helper. Let us turn to the Lord, etc.
