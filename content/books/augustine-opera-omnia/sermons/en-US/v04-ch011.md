# Sermon 103

**1.** The words of our Lord Jesus Christ, which have just been recited from the Gospel, admonish us that there is some one thing toward which we tend, while we labor in the multitude of this world. But we still tend toward it as pilgrims, not yet abiding; still on the way, not yet in the homeland; still by desiring, not yet by enjoying. Yet let us tend toward it, and without sloth and without intermission let us tend, that we may at length be able to arrive. Martha and Mary were two sisters, both sisters not only in flesh but also in religion; both clung to the Lord, both with one accord served the Lord present in the flesh. Martha received him, as pilgrims are wont to be received. But still it was a handmaid who received the Lord, a sick woman the Savior, a creature the Creator. She received him to be fed in the flesh, while she was to be fed in the Spirit. For the Lord willed to take the form of a servant, and, having taken the form of a servant, to be fed in it by servants, by condescension, not by condition. For this too was condescension, to offer himself to be fed. He had flesh, in which indeed he would hunger and thirst: but do you not know that in the desert the angels ministered to him when he was hungry [^1]? Therefore, that he willed to be fed, he bestowed upon the one feeding. And what wonder, if he also bestowed it on the widow through holy Elijah, whom he first fed by the ministry of a raven [^2]? Had he failed in feeding him, when he sent him to the widow? By no means; rather, by the service shown to his servant, he was arranging to bless the devout widow. So then the Lord was received as a guest, who came into his own, and his own did not receive him: but as many as received him, he gave them power to become sons of God [^3]; adopting servants, and making them brothers; redeeming captives, and making them coheirs. Yet let none of you perhaps say: O blessed are they who deserved to receive Christ into their own house! Do not grieve, do not murmur that you were born in times when you no longer see the Lord in the flesh: he has not taken that condescension from you. *When you did it to one of the least of mine*, he says, *you did it to me* [^4].

**2.** These few things about the Lord being fed in the flesh, but feeding in the spirit, we have said as the time allowed: let us come to the matter which I proposed concerning unity. Martha, arranging and preparing to feed the Lord, was occupied about much serving: Mary her sister chose rather to be fed by the Lord. She in a way deserted her sister laboring about much serving, and sat herself at the Lord's feet, and, being at leisure, was hearing his word. A most faithful ear had heard: *Be still, and see that I am the Lord* [^5]. The one was troubled, the other was feasting: the one was arranging many things, the other was gazing on one. Both offices were good: but still, which is the better, what shall we say? We have one whom to ask, let us hear together. Which is better, we have already heard when it was read, and, as I recall it, let us hear again. Martha appeals to her guest, lays before the judge the petition of devout complaints, that her sister deserted her, and neglected to help her laboring so in the serving. While Mary did not answer, but was nevertheless present, the Lord judges. Mary preferred, as one at leisure, to commit her cause to the judge, and was unwilling to labor in answering. For if she prepared a speech for answering, she would relax the intentness of hearing. So the Lord answered, who did not labor in word, because he was the Word. What, then, did he say? *Martha, Martha*—the repetition of the name is a sign of love, or perhaps of stirring intentness: that she might hear more attentively, she was called twice: *Martha, Martha*, hear: *You are occupied about many things, but one thing is needful* [^6]: that is, one thing is necessary. Not one work as a single work: but it is a work, it is expedient, it is necessary; this one thing Mary had chosen.

**3.** Think on the one, my brothers, and see, in the very multitude, whether anything delights but the one. Behold, by God's favor how many you are: who could bear you, unless you had a taste for the one? Whence in the many this quiet? Give one, and there is a people: take away the one, and there is a mob. For what is a mob, but a multitude disturbed? But hear the Apostle: *I beseech you, brothers*. He was speaking to a multitude: but he wanted them all to make one. *I beseech you, brothers, that you all say the same thing, and that there be no schisms among you; but that you be perfect in the same mind and in the same knowledge* [^7]. And in another place: *Of one mind, thinking one thing, nothing through contention, nor through vainglory* [^8]. And the Lord to the Father concerning his own: *That they may be one, as we also are one* [^9]. And in the Acts of the Apostles: *And of the multitude of believers the soul was one and the heart one* [^10]. Therefore *magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together* [^11]. Because one thing is necessary, that one heavenly thing, the one where the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one. See that unity is commended to us. Certainly our God is Trinity. The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Father, the Holy Spirit is neither the Father nor the Son, but the Spirit of both: and yet these three are not three gods, not three almighties, but one almighty God, the Trinity itself one God: because one thing is necessary. To this one thing he does not lead us, unless we, being many, have one heart.

**4.** Good are the services about the poor, and especially the due services about the holy servants of God, the religious offices. For they are repaid, not given, as the Apostle says: *If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal things* [^12]? They are good, we exhort you to them, and in the word of the Lord we build you up; do not be slothful in receiving the saints. Once, not knowing it, by receiving those whom they did not know, some received angels [^13]. These things are good: yet better is what Mary chose. For that has occupation out of necessity: but this has sweetness out of love. A man wants to be of service when he serves; and sometimes he cannot: what is lacking is sought, what is at hand is prepared; the mind is stretched. For if Martha sufficed for those things, she would not ask her sister's help. They are many, they are diverse, because they are carnal, because they are temporal: even if they are good, they are passing. But what does the Lord say to Martha? *Mary has chosen the better part* [^14]. Not you the bad, but she the better. Hear why the better: *Which will not be taken from her*. From you the burden of necessity will one day be taken: eternal is the sweetness of truth. What she chose will not be taken from her. It is not taken away, but rather increased. In this life it is increased, in the other life it will be perfected, never taken away.

**5.** But you, Martha—be it said with your leave—blessed in good service, for this labor of yours you seek a reward, rest. Now you are occupied about much serving, you want to feed mortal bodies, even those of the saints: but when you come to that homeland, will you find a pilgrim to receive into hospitality? will you find one hungry, for whom to break bread? one thirsty, to whom to hold out drink? a sick man, whom to visit? a quarreler, whom to reconcile? a dead man, whom to bury? All these will not be there: but what will be there? What Mary chose: there we shall be fed, we shall not feed. Therefore that will be there full and perfect, which here Mary chose: from that rich table, from the word of the Lord, she was gathering crumbs. For do you want to know what will be there? The Lord himself says of his servants: *Amen I say to you, that he will make them recline, and will pass by, and will minister to them* [^15]. What is *to recline*, except to be at leisure? What is *to recline*, except to rest? What is: *He will pass by, and will minister to them?* First he passes by, and so ministers. But where? At that heavenly banquet, of which he says: *Amen I say to you, many will come from east and west, and will recline with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven* [^16]. There the Lord feeds, but first he passes over from here. For, that you may know, "Pasch" is interpreted "Passover." The Lord came, did divine things, suffered human things. Is he still spat upon? is he still struck? is he still crowned with thorns? is he still scourged? is he still crucified? is he still wounded with a lance? He has passed over. Indeed the Gospel too speaks thus, when he kept the Pasch with his disciples. What does the Gospel say? *But when the hour had come, that Jesus should pass over from this world to the Father* [^17]. So he passed over, that he might feed: let us follow, that we may be fed.
