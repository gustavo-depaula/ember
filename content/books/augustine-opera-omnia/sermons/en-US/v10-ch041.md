# Sermon 360/C

**1.** For nothing is sweeter than the zeal of brothers, but nothing is more dangerous than the dissension of peoples. By the sound of the tongue of our mouth, flesh (caro) and love (caritas) seem neighbors: they are neighbors in the sound of the tongue. For what sounds so neighboring as caro and caritas? Yet they are very far apart from each other, even in this time. How far apart these two which sound alike are, appears to you well enough, since indeed, where love is, the heart is widened, the flesh is straitened. But because our love itself too labors in the flesh, and the spacious fields of the divinity have not yet received you, as long as the bond of weakness still holds, nevertheless, dearest ones, consider how ample have been made the spaces of these buildings! Do you think those set far off hear with more difficulty? The path of our voice is your stillness. Behold, how quickly the still hear, even what is not said with so loud a voice! Help yourselves, then, and, as it is written, *bear one another's burdens*[^1], that you may together grasp what is given to all.

**2.** After long and lasting desire we see your love bodily present; in spirit neither you ever departed from us, nor we from you[^2]. When we have "hearts on high," there you dwell with us, where no one presses upon another. Yet we ask pardon, brothers, if perhaps to some of you we seem to have come to you later than both we and you would wish. Great necessities held us in the summer time, and those necessities surely were not hidden from you. For we were helped too by your prayers, that what had been necessities might become pleasures. In the city of Hippo where I serve my sons, your brothers, we long travailed, at last we have seen unity. Again and again help us with your vows and prayers, that *the Lord may confirm what he has worked in us*[^3]. But now, although we were still doing these things there, even in the region of Hippo peoples have now begun to be converted, by whom unity is held the more slowly the more difficultly rusticity understands. Now, then, we have been not led but snatched by your desire, that, ourselves desired, we may come again to those people. Receive, therefore, the petition for pardon for the delay. For then my venerable brother and colleague Maximinus too was converted to the catholic Church. Then, therefore, his newness in the catholic Church, through which he crushed the hard kingdoms of oldness, could surely not be abandoned by me nor was it fitting, nor was it fitting for him to withdraw from there at once. When, therefore, the Lord judged it opportune, he permitted us both to come. I think, then, that you easily pardon my delay, because I came with him afterward, on whose account I did not come first.

**3.** Now, then, dearest ones, receive what now presses. We heard, absent, of your fervors, you heard, absent, of our fervors. You love unity, you cherish peace, you guard peace, you hunger for peace. We approve and rejoice at the healing of your palate, by which you taste *how sweet the Lord is*[^4]. For good bread is good to the healthy. But the sick man can praise bread, though good, by sight, but cannot eat it when offered. For what is our bread, except he who said: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^5]? Is it perhaps bread and is not peace? Let us prove that it is peace too. For we proved that it is bread by his most manifest testimony: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven.* Let the Apostle too say: *For he himself is our peace*[^6]. He, then, who says: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*, of him it is said: *He himself is our peace.* We have, then, the bread as peace, but if we are healthy, let us eat it.

**4.** You have heard the bread itself speaking, just now from the Gospel. The disciples were seeking the first and high place, and there was a contention of glory among the sons of love. They were seeking *who was the greater among them*[^7]. Weakness sought the greater place, which surely love obtains. They did not yet know by what way they went, even if they understood whither they went. Through humility one comes to loftiness. The way is Christ[^8]. He is the bread, he is the peace, he himself is also the way. Ask of him whither you wish to go, he answers: "To me." Ask by what way you wish to go, he answered: "Through me." And he remained as that whither we go, and he came as that by which we go. Therefore, dearest ones, sons of peace[^9], sons of light[^10], sons of love, offshoots of the catholic Church, if we are firm, let us serve the weak; if we are healthy, let us serve the sick. Our Lord served. You attend to the fact that the Lord serves; the servant is sick, whom the Lord serves. Behold, let us praise our bread, with as much strength as we can. *Behold how good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell in unity*[^11]. Be delighted, you just, in the Lord[^12].

**5.** Plainly *it is good for brothers to dwell in unity.* All concede that it is *good*, not all grasp that it is *pleasant*. Ask anyone, even if he is still a heretic or now displays his face and covers his mind; ask, question the one who disdains, who refuses, who repels the hand of the one serving and wishing to feed his sickness; yet hold him, ask of him: "Is unity good?" If he can, let him say: "It is evil." Plainly I do not spare him, I ask: Is unity good? He answers: "Good." Willing or unwilling, he answers this: "Unity is good." Or are you silent? Even if you are silent, surely you are silent for this reason, because you cannot say: "Not good." Iniquity does not permit you to say it is good, but truth does not allow you to deny it is good. Yet I press to wring out the word, I will not desist, I will not withdraw, you will not be rid of me unless you say something. I have found your ears at last at some time; if I do not hold you as loving, I hold you at least as fearing. Say, answer me. What I ask is easy, what I question is brief. Is unity good? What will it do? In no way is it going to say: "It is not good." Therefore, even just to be rid of me, it is going to say: "Good." And I answer: What you praise, if it is a possession, hold it with me; if it is a garment, be clothed with me; if it is bread, eat with me. "It is good," he says, "I do not deny it, but because I am compelled to it, therefore I am unwilling for it." Therefore it is good, but because you are compelled to the good, therefore you are unwilling for the good: as though indeed I would be troublesome in compelling, if you were eager in asking. If it is good and you are unwilling, therefore I compel. For what you confess to be good, you are unwilling for not by truth, but by weakness. I serve the weak: you are sick, I am your minister. I offer food, receive the food which you praise. Do you perhaps, as the sick are wont to refuse food set before them, complain that it is badly cooked? You will be able to say none of these things to the food which I offer. Christ is the bread, Christ is the peace. This food was formed in the womb of virginity, cooked by the fire of the passion. Take, brother; receive, brother, receive something lest you die. You surely praise unity. Your weakness is against me, not your judgment. I offer food, not only that which confirms the sick, but also that which sustains the sick. I am troublesome when I press it in, but impious if I should withdraw it. "Behold," he says, "I take it."

**6.** What sort of sick do we endure, brothers? "Behold," he says, "I take it." Some have come, yielding to the troubles of those serving them, to the anxiety, though importunate, yet of parents showing motherly love. Why do I say of parents, brothers? I do not mean myself, nor any man. Our parents, feeding the healthy, refreshing the sick, are God the Father and mother Church. This pious mother, then, both giving birth to her conceived sons and travailing with the imperiled, did not spurn the sickness of her own; though troublesome, though importunate, she approached those lying down; she pressed food upon those refusing. They hate the one refreshing them, they fear more to experience the one lamenting them. She refreshes the sick man, she laments the dead. Let her be troublesome in this . . .
