# Sermon 299/D: On the Birthday of the Holy Martyrs of Scilli

**1.** The holy martyrs, the witnesses of God, lest by living they should die, chose rather to live by dying; lest by fearing death they should deny life, by loving life they despised life. To make them deny Christ, the enemy promised life, but not such as Christ promises. So, believing what the Savior promised, they laughed at what the persecutor threatened. Brethren, when we celebrate the feasts of the martyrs, let us recognize that examples are set before us, which we may attain by imitating. For we do not add to the glory of the martyrs by holding this gathering. Their crown is known to the peoples of the angels. As for us, when their sufferings were read, we were able to hear them. But what they received: *Eye has not seen, nor ear heard*[^1]. Of the good things of this world, some are superfluous, others necessary. Attend a little while we speak of this, and let us distinguish, if we can, which are the superfluous goods of this world and which the necessary; so that you may see that Christ is not to be denied either for the superfluous or for the necessary. Who can enumerate the superfluous things of this world? If we wished to recount them, we would make great delays. So let us name the necessary things; whatever others there are, these will be superfluous. Two things are necessary in this world: health and a friend; these are what we should value greatly, what we should not despise. Health and a friend are natural goods. God made man, that he might be and live: that is health; but, lest he be alone, friendship was sought. So friendship begins from spouse and children, and proceeds even to strangers. But if we consider that we have had one father and one mother, who will be a stranger? Every man is neighbor to every man. Ask nature. Is he unknown? he is a man. Is he an enemy? he is a man. Is he a foe? he is a man. Is he a friend? let him remain a friend. Is he an enemy? let him become a friend. How wisdom became our neighbor.

**2.** To these two things necessary in this world, health and a friend, wisdom came as a stranger. It found all foolish, wandering, cultivating superfluous things, loving temporal things, ignorant of eternal things. This wisdom was no friend to fools. So, since it was no friend to fools, and was far from fools, it took up our neighbor, and became our neighbor. This is the mystery of Christ. What is so far apart as folly from wisdom? What is so near as man and man? What is so far off, I say, as wisdom from folly? So wisdom took up man, and became neighbor to man through that which was near. And behold, since wisdom itself said to man: *Behold, piety is wisdom*[^2]—and it belongs to man's wisdom to worship God, for this is piety—two commandments were given to us: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*. The other: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^3]. And the one who heard this said: *And who is my neighbor?*[^4] He thought the Lord would say: Your father and your mother, your spouse, your children, your brothers, your sisters. He did not answer this, but, wishing to commend every man as neighbor to every man, he set out a narrative. *A certain man*, he says. Who? A certain one, yet a man. *A certain man*. So what man? A certain one, but yet a man. *Was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers*[^5]. These are called robbers, who also pursue us. Wounded, stripped, left half dead on the road, he was despised by those passing by, by a priest, by a Levite; but by a Samaritan passing by he was noticed. He went up to him: his wounds inspected, he was lifted onto a beast, brought to an inn; care was ordered to be given him, and expense was laid out. He who had asked is asked who was neighbor to this half-dead man. Because two had despised him, and they neighbors had despised, a stranger came near. For this man of Jerusalem had priests and Levites as neighbors, Samaritans as foreigners. The neighbors passed by, and the stranger became the neighbor. Who then was neighbor to this man? Say it, you who had asked, saying: *Who is my neighbor?* Now answer what is true. Pride had asked: let nature speak. So what does he say? *I think, the one who showed him mercy*[^6]. And the Lord to him: *Go, and you do likewise*[^7].

**3.** Let us return to the matter. Now we see three things: health, a friend, wisdom. But health and a friend are also of this world; wisdom is from elsewhere. For health's sake, food and clothing, and, if ill health befalls, medicine. Speaking to the healthy as a healthy man, the apostle says: *But godliness with contentment is great gain. We brought*, he says, *nothing into this world, but neither can we take anything away. Having food and clothing, let us be content with these*[^8]. These are necessary for health's sake: but what is for the superfluous things? *For those who wish*, he says, *to become rich*—for the sake of superfluous things, of course—*fall into temptation and a snare and many foolish and harmful desires, which plunge a man into ruin and destruction*[^9]. Where then is health? So for health's sake, *having food and clothing, let us be content with these*. For a friend's sake, what? What more could be said to you than, you shall love your neighbor as yourself? So as health is for you, let health be for your friend too. For your friend's clothing: *Whoever has two tunics, let him share with one who has none*. For your friend's food: *and whoever has provisions, let him do likewise*[^10]. You are fed, you feed; you are clothed, you clothe. These things are of this world; but from elsewhere is what wisdom is: you learn, and you teach.

**4.** Now set before your eyes the contest of the martyrs. The enemy comes, he compels them to deny Christ. But let us bring him in still flattering, not yet raging. He promises riches and honors. These are superfluous things: those who are tempted by such gifts to deny Christ have not yet come to the contest, have not yet found the struggle, have not yet provoked the most ancient foe to true battle. But the man of faith, to whom such things were promised, despised them, and said: Shall I deny Christ for riches? Shall I deny riches for riches? Shall I deny a treasure for gold? Surely he is the one *who for our sake became poor, though he was rich, so that by his poverty we might be enriched*[^11]. Surely he is the one of whom the apostle likewise says: *in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge*[^12]. You attend to what you promise, because you cannot see what you try to take away. I see by faith what you wish to take from me: you see with the eyes of flesh what you wish to give; better are the things the eye of the heart beholds than what the eye of flesh sees. *For the things that are seen are temporal; but the things that are not seen are eternal*[^13]. So I despise your gifts, the faithful soul says, because they are temporal, they are superfluous, they are fleeting, they are flighty, they are full of perils, full of temptations. No one has these when he wishes, and loses them even when he does not wish. The promiser is despised, another comes near, that is, the persecutor; the flatterer is despised, he begins to be a raging foe; the snake is despised, he is turned into a lion. Do you not wish, he says, to receive ampler riches from me? If you do not deny Christ, I will take away those you have. You are still raging against my superfluous things. *Like a sharp razor you have wrought deceit*[^14]. You shave the hairs, you do not cut the skin: take these from me too. Indeed, since you saw that from them I gave to the poor, I received guests, I did what Paul had advised: *Charge*, he says, *the rich of this age, charge them not to be proud, nor to set their hope on the uncertainty of riches, but on the living God, who gives us all things abundantly to enjoy. Let them do good, let them be rich in good works, let them give readily, let them share, let them lay up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^15]. Taking these from me, I shall not do those works: shall I be less in God's sight because I wish and cannot? Or am I so deaf to the voice of the angels: *Peace on earth to men of good will?*[^16] Take then my superfluous things. *We brought nothing into this world, but neither can we take anything away. Having food and clothing, let us be content with these*[^17].

**5.** But the persecutor says: I take away your food, I take away your clothing. We have come to the struggle, the enemy raged more fiercely; the superfluous things are passed, we have come to the necessary. *Depart not from me, for tribulation is near*[^18]. Nothing is so near to one's soul as one's own flesh; in the flesh you feel hunger and thirst and heat. There I wish to see you, good martyr, witness of God! See, he says, see! *Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?*[^19] What is it that you threaten? I take away your food, I take away your clothing. *Tribulation? Or distress? Or famine? Or nakedness?*[^20] Let him threaten from the other side. I take away your friend, I kill those most dear to you before your eyes, I will slaughter your wife and children. You kill, you kill? Let them not deny, and you do not kill. What is this? you do not frighten me about myself, you frighten me about my own? If they do not deny, you do not kill mine; if they deny, you kill strangers. Let the persecutor add still more, and rage, and say: If you do not care about your own, I will take even you from this light. From this light: is it then from the eternal light? From what light will you take me? which I share with you too. It is not great, since you also see it. For this light I will not deny the light. *It was the true light*[^21]. I know to whom I say: *For with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light*[^22]. Take away my life, take away the light; I shall have life, I shall have light. I shall have life where I shall not suffer you as a slayer; I shall have light which neither you—not to mention—nor any night can take from me. The martyr has conquered. Or is there anything else where we should look for a greater contest? No. He threatens death, he rages against health, he tears with hooks, he tortures with torments, he burns with flames, he sets beasts upon him: and here he is conquered. Why is he conquered? Because *in all these things we more than conquer through him who loved us*[^23].

**6.** So, my brethren, let Christ not be denied for the superfluous, let him not be denied for the necessary: no one is more necessary than he. I called health and a friend necessary. You sin for health's sake, and you deny Christ; by loving health you will not have health. You sin for your friend's sake, and, lest you offend him, you deny Christ. Alas for me, wretched! Sometimes he is denied by being ashamed. The persecutor does not rage, the despoiler does not strip, the torturer does not press; only, lest you displease your friend, you deny your Lord. I see what your friend has taken from you: show what he will give. What will he give? The very friendship through which you sin, by which you are entangled, by which you are made an enemy of God. He would not be your friend, if you were a friend to yourself; but, since you yourself are your own enemy, you suppose your enemy a friend to you. But whence are you your own enemy? Because you love iniquity: *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^24]. But Christ is not denied to please an impious and perverse friend; he is not denied, but he is reviled by the impious, accused by the impious, and by the faithful one, through shame, he is not defended, he is deserted, passed over in silence, not preached. The tongue of the blasphemer rages, and there is none to praise. How many evils are committed as if for necessary things, for food, for clothing, for health, for a friend; and all those things that are sought after rather perish. But if you despise present things, God will give you eternal things. Despise health, you will have immortality; despise death, you will have life; despise honor, you will have a crown; despise a man as friend, you will have God as friend. But there, where you will have God as friend, you will not be without a neighbor friend; there friends will be with you, whose deeds and confessions were read a little while ago.

**7.** We have heard men acting bravely, confessing manfully; we have heard women too holding to Christ not as women, forgetting their sex. There, with these, will be that friendship where there will be no concupiscence of the flesh, and there will be only wisdom with friends to enjoy. Behold what we lose, if we love these things here and deny Christ! A neighbor there does not frighten us, if he dies; there will be no mourning there, where there will be eternal life; nor is even this necessary: *Having food and clothing, let us be content with these*[^25]. Our clothing will be immortality, this food will be love, life will be eternal; nor will we do there the good works that are called such from these things; we will not reach them, if we do not do them here. You will not be told: *Break your bread for the hungry*[^26], where there will be no hunger; you will not be told: Receive as a guest, where you will find no stranger; you will not be told: Set free the oppressed, where there will be no enemy; you will not be told: Reconcile the quarrelsome, where there will be eternal peace. See, my brethren, how it is to be endured here by seeking; there we will have it, where we cannot perish. Do you seek health? Despise it, and you will have it. You deny Christ, fearing to offend the friendships of men: confess Christ, and the city of the angels will be a friend to you, the city of the patriarchs, the city of the prophets, the city of the apostles, the city of all the martyrs, the city of all the good faithful. Christ himself founded it forever[^27].
