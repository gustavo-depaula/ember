# Sermon 335/G: On the Martyrs

**1.** And so our martyrs loved God much. Because they had perfect love in them, therefore they did not fear the savagery of the persecutor. So the perfect love of the martyrs made them fear nothing. On every side the persecutors raged. On every side they suffered the storms and tempests of this world. They were strong in God and in Christ. Their own people weeping persecuted them more than their enemies raging. On this side the enemy terrified. On this side the wife lamented: "To whom do you leave us? Consent, live with us, God forgives! To whom do you leave our loneliness?" These things that man threatens: "Unless you do it I will torture, kill, burn." Between the threats of the persecutor and the tears of the wife, he stood, neither broken on this side, nor bent on that, fixed in God, upright in Christ, laughing at the threats of the persecutor—for the persecutor could not threaten eternal punishments—despising even the tears of the wife, because the marriage of the soul with Christ is greater. Hoping for the heavenly he spurned the earthly. Seeing the future he was not terrified by the present, and, love being fulfilled, as it is written: *it casts out fear*[^1]. For there was nothing that a man perfect in love should fear. But we find it said elsewhere: *The fear of the Lord, chaste, abiding forever and ever*[^2]. Here it is said: *Perfect love casts out fear,* there because the fear of the Lord abides forever and ever. So there are two fears: one which love casts out, the other abiding forever and ever. So there is an earthly fear, there is also a chaste one, nor could it be better called than chaste.

**2.** So, as I had begun to say, why do you boast of your bad punishment, O schismatic, which has no good cause? So let us honor the martyrs within in the Catholic Church, having grace not audacity, godliness not rashness, constancy not obstinacy, gathering not scattering. Accordingly hear the martyr praying: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause*[^3]. He did not say: my punishment, but: my cause. For he attended to the martyr of the martyrs, the head of the martyrs, the lord of the martyrs, the example of the martyrs, the spectator of the martyrs, the helper of the martyrs, the crowner of the martyrs. He attended to him not magnifying the punishment, but distinguishing the cause. For he himself said: *Blessed are those who suffer persecution*[^4]. Still it is confused. The adulterer suffers persecution for the sake of lust, the murderer for the sake of cruelty, the thief for the sake of fraud, the rest of the wicked suffer persecution for their crimes. What you say let me hear. Blessed are those who suffer persecution. You have recalled the punishment. Distinguish the cause. Hear, he says, I distinguish. Follow: those who suffer persecution, for the sake of righteousness. Why do you exaggerate the punishment to me? O false witness, prove the righteousness. So it is not the punishment that makes the martyrs, but the cause. Do not exaggerate your punishment, prove first your righteousness, which you are not going to do but you will indeed prove by the cutting off of your iniquity. If you want, brothers, to imitate the true martyrs, choose for yourselves the cause, that you may say to God: *Judge me, O Lord, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^5]. Distinguish not my punishment, for the nation that is not holy has this too, but my cause which none has but the holy nation. So choose for yourselves the cause, a good and just cause, since *blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of righteousness, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^6].

[^1]: 1 John 4:18
[^2]: Ps 19:9 (18:10)
[^3]: Ps 43:1 (42:1)
[^4]: Matt 5:10
[^5]: Ps 43:1 (42:1)
[^6]: Matt 5:10
