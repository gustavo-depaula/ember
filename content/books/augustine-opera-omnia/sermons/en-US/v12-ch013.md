# Sermon 319B

**1.** Yesterday we celebrated the birthday on which the king of martyrs was born into the world; today we celebrate the birthday on which the first of the martyrs departed from the world. For it was fitting that first the immortal should take flesh for mortals, and thus a mortal should despise death for the immortal. The Lord was born to die for the servant, so that the servant should not fear to die for the Lord. Christ was born on earth so that Stephen might be born in heaven. The Lord entered the world so that Stephen might enter heaven. The exalted descended to lowliness, and the lowly ascended to the heights. God's Son became the Son of man to make the sons of men sons of God. Christ worked miracles; Stephen did too. But Christ worked them without Stephen: could Stephen work them without Christ? Stephen lived from Christ as a branch lives from the vine. Do you marvel at the fruit hanging from the branch? See the branch remaining in the vine.[^1]

**2.** Come, holy Stephen, say something to the Jews, so that you may begin to be stoned and may also be crowned. Hurl words and receive stones. Say something about uncircumcised hearts, and you will die by stones. Say something about him whose name they refuse to hear. *Let them be ashamed* and rage; *let deceitful lips be struck dumb*. Tell them where you see alive the one they mocked when crucified. Tell them where you see him, and you will not live here, but will live better with him. Tell them that what they did amounts to nothing: the one they killed lives. Tell them that you see standing at the Father's right hand the one they mocked hanging on the Cross. And he says: *See, I see the heavens opened and the Son of man standing at God's right hand*. *But they cried out and stopped their ears*. They closed their ears and ran for stones, *and all rushed upon him*, and *they stoned Stephen as he called upon God and said*: “*My soul has clung closely after you*, because my flesh is being stoned for you. *Receive my spirit* and fulfill your promise to me.”[^2]

**3.** Christ prayed for those who crucified him; Stephen too prayed for those who stoned him. Christ the Lord prayed fastened to the wood; Stephen, standing, commended his spirit to the Lord, but knelt to pray concerning their offense. As a friend he asked the Lord on behalf of enemies, saying: “Certainly I am suffering, I am being stoned; they rage against me, roar against me. But *do not charge this to them as sin*, because I first heard from you what I am saying to you. I, your servant, suffer; there is a great difference between me and you. You are Lord, I a servant; you the Word, I a hearer of the Word; you the teacher, I a disciple; you the creator, I created; you God, I man. There is a great difference between the sin of those stoning me and the sin of those who crucified you. So when you said, *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*, you asked pardon for a great sin and taught me to ask it for a very small one.” *Lord*, he says, *do not charge this sin to them*, as though saying, “Let me suffer in the flesh; let them not perish in their minds.” They threw stones; holy Stephen poured out prayers. Great devotion, brothers, great mercy! Indeed, the very things he ate at his Lord's table he prepared at his own banquet. It is written in the law: *You have sat at a great table; know that you must prepare such things*. Thus in prayer he presented the same things he had eaten at his Lord's table. Christ the Lord said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*; holy Stephen said: *Do not charge this sin to them. Having said this, he fell asleep*. O sleep of peace! What could be more tranquil, more restful than that sleep? How was he going to his friends, who so loved his enemies?[^3]

**4.** So that Your Holiness may know how much the prayer of the holy martyr Stephen accomplished, return with us to that young persecutor named Saul, as we heard when the book of the Acts of the Apostles was read. While holy Stephen was being stoned, Saul guarded the clothes of all those throwing stones, so that he seemed to be stoning Stephen through everyone's hands. As he raged, on the road *a light from heaven suddenly shone around him, and falling to the ground he heard a loud voice from heaven saying to him: Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad. And he said:* ‘*Who are you, Lord?*’ *And the Lord said to him:* ‘*I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute*. Why do you raise yourself against me to your harm, instead of humbling yourself for your good? What have I to do with you? For the evils you committed against me, I should indeed have destroyed you long ago, but my Stephen prayed for you. *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*? And because you raged in vain against my name, I am making you my servant.’[^4]

**5.** O Saul, ravenous wolf, you have eaten: wait a little, and you will digest. I will say it plainly: the son of perdition was struck down, and a chosen vessel was raised up. For if holy Stephen had not prayed in this way, the Church would not have Paul today. Paul was raised up because holy Stephen, bowed to the earth, was heard. Thus what Saul did, Paul will suffer; what the persecutor did, the preacher will suffer; what the wolf did, the lamb will suffer. This is how vengeance should be taken on such a man. Let him learn to suffer what he used to do; let him too feel what he inflicted on others. Ananias is told: *Go to him and mark him with my mark, for he will suffer much for my name*. Ananias came, baptized Saul, and made him Paul; baptized the wolf and made him a lamb. We began to have as a preacher the one holy Stephen had as a stone thrower. Holy Stephen was heard in such a way that through his prayers the sin Saul had committed was wiped away. Let us, then, commend ourselves to his prayers. For he who was then heard so well on behalf of those stoning him will much more be heard now on behalf of those honoring him.[^5]
