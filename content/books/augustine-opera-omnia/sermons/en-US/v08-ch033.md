# Sermon 298: Discourse on the Birthday of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul

**1. 1.** We ought indeed to have celebrated the day of such great martyrs, that is, the holy apostles Peter and Paul, with a larger gathering. For if we celebrate the birthdays of the lambs with very large gatherings, how much more ought we to celebrate those of the rams? For of the faithful whom the Apostles gained through their preaching it is written, *Bring to the Lord the offspring of rams*. Through the straits of suffering, through a road full of thorns, through the tribulations of persecution, the faithful had the Apostles as leaders so that they might pass through afterward. Blessed Peter, the first of the Apostles, and blessed Paul, the last of the Apostles, who duly worshiped him who said, *I am the first, and I am the last*, met on a single day of suffering, the first and the last together. Peter ordained Saint Stephen. When the martyr Stephen was ordained a deacon, the apostle Peter ordained him along with the other Apostles. Peter ordained him; Paul persecuted him. But let us not seek Paul's first deeds; let us rejoice in the last deeds of the last Apostle. For if we seek the earlier deeds, even Peter's will not be very pleasing. We have said that Paul persecuted Stephen. Let us look at Peter denying the Lord. Peter washed away his denial of the Lord with tears; Paul atoned for his persecution of Stephen with blindness. Peter wept before the scourge; Paul also suffered the scourge. Both were good, holy, utterly devoted; their letters are read daily to the peoples. And to what peoples? And to how many peoples? Attend to the Psalm: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world.* We too provide the proof: these words have reached us too, awakened us from sleep, and turned us from the madness of unbelief to the health of faith.

**2. 2.** I say these things, dearly beloved, happy indeed today because of so great a festival, but a little sad because I do not see as many people gathered as ought to have gathered for the anniversary of the Apostles' suffering. If it were unknown to us, we would not be held accountable; but if no one is unaware of it, what is this great laziness? Do you not love Peter and Paul? Through you I am speaking to those who are not here. For I thank you, because at least you have come. Can the heart of any Christian fail to love Peter and Paul? If it is still cold, let him read and love; if he does not yet love, let him receive the arrow of the word in his heart. For it was of these Apostles that it was said, *Your arrows are sharp, most powerful.* Through these arrows what follows was accomplished: *Peoples will fall beneath you.* Such wounds are good. The wound of love brings health. Christ's bride sings in the *Song of Songs*: *I am wounded with love*. When is this wound healed? When our desire is satisfied with good things. It is called a wound as long as we desire and do not yet possess. For love is such that pain is there as well. When we arrive, when we possess, then pain passes away; love does not fail.

**2. 3.** You have heard the words in Paul's letter to his disciple, blessed Timothy: *For I am already being offered in sacrifice*. He saw his suffering approaching; he saw it, but did not fear it. Why did he not fear? Because he had already said, *Having a desire to depart and be with Christ. For I,* he says, *am already being offered in sacrifice.* No one says with such exultation that he is going to dine and enjoy a great banquet as Paul says that he is going to suffer. *For I am already being offered in sacrifice.* What does *being offered in sacrifice* mean? I will be a sacrifice. A sacrifice to whom? To God, because *the death of his saints is precious in the Lord's sight*. *I, he says, am being offered in sacrifice.* I am confident; I have a priest above to offer me to God. I have as my priest the very one who was first a victim for me. Already *I am being offered in sacrifice, and the time of my release is at hand*. He meant release from the body.

**3. 3.** For there is a certain sweet bond of the body, and a person is bound and does not want to be released. Yet the one who said, *Having a desire to depart and be with Christ*, rejoiced that these bonds would at last be loosened: the bonds of bodily members would be loosened, and the clothing and adornments of eternal virtues received. Confidently he laid aside the flesh, about to receive a crown. A happy change, a holy migration, what a blessed dwelling! Faith sees it, though the eye does not yet, because *neither has eye seen, nor ear heard, nor has there entered the human heart what God has prepared for those who love him.* Where do we suppose these saints are? Where it is well. Why ask more? You do not know the place, but consider their merit. Wherever they are, they are with God. *The souls of the righteous are in God's hand, and torment will not touch them*. But they passed through torments to a place without torment; through straits they reached a place of spaciousness. Let no one who desires such a homeland fear a toilsome road. *The time,* he says, *of my release is at hand. I have fought the good fight, completed the course, kept the faith; henceforth there remains for me the crown of righteousness*. Rightly you hurry, rightly you rejoice that you are to be offered in sacrifice, for *there remains* for you *the crown of righteousness.* The bitterness of suffering still lies ahead, but the thought of the one about to suffer passes beyond it and considers what lies farther on: not the road traveled, but the destination. And because the destination is contemplated with great love, the road is trodden with great courage.

**4. 4.** But after saying, *There remains for me the crown of righteousness,* he added, *which the Lord, the just judge, will repay me on that day*. Being just, he will repay it; earlier he did not repay. For if, O Paul, formerly Saul, when you were persecuting Christ's saints and guarding the clothing of those who stoned Stephen, the Lord had exercised just judgment toward you, where would you be? What place in the depths of hell could have been found for such a crime of yours? But he did not repay you then, so that he might repay you now. For we read your words about your former deeds in your letter, and through you we know them. You said, *For I am the last of the Apostles, unworthy to be called an apostle*. You are unworthy, and he made you one. He did not, then, repay what was owed to you, because he bestowed honor on an unworthy man to whom punishment was owed. *I am not,* he says, *worthy to be called an apostle.* Why? *Because I persecuted God's Church*. If you persecuted God's Church, how, then, are you an Apostle? *But by God's grace I am what I am*. Previously grace, now a debt; previously grace was given, now a debt is repaid. *By God's grace I am,* he says, *what I am.* I am nothing. Whatever I am, *I am by God's grace.* Whatever I am—but now I am an Apostle; for what I was, I was myself. *By God's grace I am what I am, and his grace in me was not empty; rather, I labored more than all of them*. What is this, apostle Paul? You seem to have exalted yourself; this seems to have been said with a little lifting of the neck: *More* *than all of them I labored.* Acknowledge it, then. I acknowledge it, he says: *Yet not I, but God's grace with me*. He had not forgotten; rather, the last Apostle was saving for last what would make us rejoice: *Yet not I, but God's grace with me.*

**5. 5.** Then there was no repayment; what about now? *I have completed the course, kept the faith; henceforth there remains for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will repay me on that day*. You fought the good fight. But who made you conquer? I read your own words to you, and you say, *I thank God, who gives us victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*. What good would fighting do if you could not conquer? So you have this: you indeed fought, but Christ gave the victory. Follow the next point: *I have completed the course.* Who accomplished this in you as well? Did you not say, *It belongs neither to the one who wills nor to the one who runs, but to God who shows mercy*? Say what follows: *I have kept the faith*. And where did this come from for you? Hear your own words: *Mercy,* he says, *I obtained, so that I might be faithful*. Therefore you kept the faith through God's mercy, not through your own strength. *Henceforth,* then, *there remains* for you *the crown of righteousness, which he will repay* you, *the Lord, the just judge, on that day*. For he will repay merits; that is why he is a just judge. But here too do not lift your neck, because your merits are his gifts. What I have said to him, I learned from him; and surely you have learned it with me in this school. We preside from a higher place for the sake of proclamation, but in one school we have a common Teacher in heaven.
