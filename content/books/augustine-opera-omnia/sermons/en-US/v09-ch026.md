# Sermon 317: On Stephen the Martyr

**1. 1.** The martyr Stephen, blessed and the first after the Apostles ordained a deacon by the Apostles, crowned before the Apostles; he illumined those lands by his suffering, he visited these by his death. But he would not, though dead, visit, unless even dead he lived. A scanty dust gathered so great a people: the ash lies hidden, the benefits lie open. Consider, dearly beloved, what God keeps for us in the region of the living, who bestows so much from the dust of the dead. The flesh of holy Stephen is spread abroad through individual places: but the merit of his faith is commended. Let us so expect to obtain temporal benefits, that by imitating him we may merit to receive the eternal. What the blessed Martyr set before us to imitate in his passion, to attend to this, to believe this, to fulfill this, is truly to celebrate the solemnities of the Martyr. Our Lord, among the great and healthful, divine and most lofty precepts which he gave to his disciples, this seems hard to men, that he commanded them to love their enemies. A hard precept, but a great reward. Finally, when he admonished this, see what he said: *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute you*[^1]. You have heard the work, expect the reward; and see what he adds: *That you may be,* he says, *sons of your Father who is in heaven, who makes his sun to rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^2]. This we see, this we cannot deny. Surely it was not said to the clouds: Rain upon the fields of my worshipers, and withdraw from the fields of my blasphemers? Surely it was not said to the sun: Let those who worship me see you, let those who curse me not see you? Benefits from heaven, benefits from earth: the fountains gush, the fields grow fat, the trees are laden with fruits. The good have these things, the bad have them too; the grateful have them, the ungrateful have them. He who bestows so great things on the good and the bad, do we think keeps nothing for the good? This he gives to the good and the bad, which he gave also to the stoners of Stephen: but this he keeps for the good, which he gave to Stephen.

**2. 2.** Especially, then, brothers, by the example of this Martyr, let us learn to love our enemies. The example is set before us from God the Father, *who makes his sun to rise upon the good and the evil*[^3]. The Son of God too said this, after the receiving of his flesh, through the mouth of his flesh, which he took up by loving his enemies. For he who came into the world as a lover of his enemies, found absolutely all his enemies, found no friend. For his enemies he shed his blood: but by his blood he converted his enemies. The sins of his enemies he blotted out by his blood: by blotting out their sins, he made friends out of enemies. Of these friends was Stephen too: indeed he is and will be. Yet the Lord himself first showed on the cross what he admonished. For with the Jews everywhere raging, growing angry, mocking, insulting, crucifying, he said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^4]. For blindness crucifies me. Blindness was crucifying him: and crucified, he made for them an eye-salve from his blood.

**2. 3.** But men lazy at the precept, greedy for the reward, who do not love their enemies, but desire to avenge themselves on them, nor attend to the Lord, who, if he wanted to avenge himself on his enemies, there would not remain one to praise him; when they hear this passage of the Gospel, in which the Lord on the cross said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do;* they say to themselves, He could do this, as the Son of God, as the only-begotten of the Father. For the flesh hung, but God lay hidden within. But what are we, to do these things? Did he who commanded it deceive us? Far be it: he did not deceive. If you think it too much for you to imitate your Lord, attend to Stephen your fellow servant. The Lord Christ, the only Son of God: surely Stephen was not this? The Lord Christ, born of an uncorrupted virgin: surely Stephen was not this? The Lord Christ came, not in the flesh of sin, but in the likeness of the flesh of sin: surely Stephen was not this? He was born as you are; he was born from where you too were; he was reborn from him from whom you too were; he was redeemed at the price at which you too were; he is worth as much as you are worth. One instrument was made for us. The Gospel is the instrument, where we were all bought: where you are, there he is. Because we are servants, it is an instrument: because we are sons, it is a testament. Attend to him, attend to your fellow servant.

**3. 4.** Is it too much for you, because you have weak eyes, to gaze at the sun? Look at the lamp. For the Lord said to his disciples: *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house*[^5]. The house is the world: the lampstand is the cross of Christ: the lamp shining on the lampstand is Christ hanging on the cross. On that very lampstand there shone also that man, who before kept the garments of those stoning, from Saul Paul, from a wolf a lamb, both small and great; a snatcher of lambs, and a shepherd of lambs: on that very lampstand he shone, when he said: *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^6]. *So let your light shine,* he says, *before men*[^7]. Behold the light of Stephen shines, this lamp shines: let us attend to it. Let no one say, It is too much for me: he was a man, you are a man. But he did not receive from himself. Did he receive, and close it against you? The fountain is common: drink from where he drank. By the benefit of God he received: he who gave abounds; and you too ask, and receive.

**4. 5.** The Lord bitterly and sharply reproved the Jews, but by loving: *Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites*[^8]. When he said these things, who would not say that he hated them? He came to the cross, and said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^9]. So too Stephen in his sermon first reproved: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears.* These are the words of holy Stephen, when he addressed the Jews: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears; you have always resisted the Holy Spirit, as your fathers also did. Which of the prophets did your fathers not kill?*[^10] By saying these things, he seems to hate, seems to rage. The tongue cries out, the heart loves. We have heard the crying tongue, let us prove the loving mind. For when they had run to the stones, hard men to a hard man, they hurled at him their own equals. He was stoned with stones, who was dying for the Rock; as the Apostle says: *But the Rock was Christ*[^11]. And though there was such constancy in teaching, see what patience there was in death. For these were shaking his body with the blows of stones, and he was praying for his enemies: the outer man was being battered, and the inner was supplicating. But the Lord who had girded him, who had tested him, who had set his mark upon him, not on the hand, but on the forehead, watched his soldier from above, about to help the one contending, to crown the one conquering. Finally he showed himself to him. *For behold,* he says, *I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing at the right hand of God*[^12]. He alone saw, because he appeared to him alone. And what does he say for himself? *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit*[^13]. For himself praying he stood, for them he bends the knee: for himself erect, for them bowed; for himself lofty, for them humble: he bent the knee, and said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^14]. And having said this, he fell asleep. O sleep of peace! He who slept among the stones of his enemies, how will he wake in his own ashes? He slept secure, quiet in peace; because he commended his spirit to the Lord.

**5. 5.** So Stephen loved his enemies. For he who, praying for himself, stood, for them bent the knee. Surely he fulfilled what is written. He was a true imitator of the Lord's passion and an approved perfect disciple of Christ, who fulfilled in his own passion what he had heard from the master. For the Lord, when he hung on the cross, said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^15]; and blessed Stephen, when he was already nearly overwhelmed by stones, said thus: *Lord Jesus, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^16]. O apostolic man, now from disciple a master. For it behooved the first martyr to follow the teaching of Christ the Master. He prays for the impious, prays for the blasphemers, for those stoning him. Because, then, it was difficult to be heard for such men, weakness drew near, that love might be strengthened. He bent the knees, he wrung it out. Do you think he was not heard, when he said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge?* He was heard. For many of them believed. But I do not send you far off. That Saul, who stoned with the hands of all, who kept the garments of those stoning, Stephen was heard for him. Afterward he was raging; having received writings against the Christians he went on raging; he thirsted for blood, panted for slaughter. And the Lord, who had heard Stephen for him: *Saul, Saul,* he says, *why do you persecute me?*[^17] He whom you killed prayed for you. And I choose you, that you may confess me and die for me.

**6. 5.** So let us celebrate the birthday of holy Stephen and honor it with due veneration. We have celebrated the birthday of the Lord; let us celebrate that of the servant too. We have kept the nativity of the Savior; let us keep the birthday of the martyr too. The uncorrupted virgin Mary bore our Lord, and holy mother Church brought forth the glorious Stephen to the palm of martyrdom.
