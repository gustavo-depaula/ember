# Sermon 28

**1** Of all the divine utterances, let us rather, with the Lord's help, discourse on this, which we heard last: *Let the heart of those who seek the Lord rejoice*[^1]. For opportunely we are also fasting in our bellies. Our heart will rejoice, if we hunger in our minds. When some pleasant things are set on our table, the throats of those who seek food rejoice. When things variously colored and pleasingly painted are set before our sight, the eyes of those who seek to see something luminous rejoice; the ears of those who seek song rejoice; the smell of those who seek a pleasant odor rejoices. *Let the heart of those who seek the Lord rejoice*.

**2** Without doubt, the several things that are presented to our several senses delight the several senses. For neither does sound delight the sight, nor color the hearing. But to our heart the Lord is both light and voice and odor and food. And therefore he is all things, because he is none of these. &lt;And therefore he is none&gt; of these, because he is the creator of all these. He is light to our heart, to whom we say: *In your light we shall see light*[^2]. He is sound to our heart, to whom we say: *To my hearing you will give exultation and gladness*[^3]. He is odor to our heart, of whom it is said: *We are the good odor of Christ*[^4]. But if you seek food, because you have fasted: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice*[^5]. And of the Lord Jesus Christ himself it is said that he became for us *justice and wisdom*[^6]. See, the banquet is prepared. Christ is justice; he is nowhere lacking; he is not prepared for us by cooks, nor is he brought by merchants from regions overseas like foreign fruits. He is a food that everyone perceives who has the healthy throat of the inner man. He is a food who, commending himself, says: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^7]. He is a food that restores and does not fail; a food that, when taken, is not consumed; a food that satisfies the hungry and remains whole. When you depart from here to your tables, you will eat nothing of the sort. Because, then, you have gathered for this banquet, eat well. But when you go away, digest well. For he eats well and digests badly who hears the word of God and does not do it. For he does not draw out the useful juice, but, raw, belches up disgust from indigestion.

**3** And do not marvel that our hearts so feast that they are both restored and do not diminish that from which they are restored. To our bodily eyes God has given such a food. For this light is food for the eyes; by it our eyes are fed, and if anyone is long in darkness, they fail as though by fasting. For men have lost their eyes by sitting in darkness, though nothing rushed into the eyes, nor did anyone strike them, nor did any foreign moisture flow in, nor dust, nor smoke. A man is brought out after darkness, and does not see what he used to see. His eyes have died of hunger; by not taking their food—that is, light—they failed. See, then, what I proposed, what sort of food is the food of our eyes. This light is seen by all, it feeds the eyes of all. And the sight of the one who sees is restored, and the light remains whole. If two see, it remains as great; if more see, it remains the same. Let the rich man see, let the poor man see, it is equal to all. No one sets boundaries in it. The want of the poor is filled, the avarice of the rich is at a loss. For does he who has more see more? Or, by producing gold, does he forestall the poor man, and buy for himself what he may see, so that the other may not have it? If, then, such is the food for our eyes, what is God himself to our minds?

**4** A certain food of the ears too is sound. And what is it like? For from these sensible things of the body let us conjecture the intelligible things of the mind. See, I speak to your Charity. Ears are present, minds are present. I have named two things: ears and minds. And in what I speak there are two things: sound and meaning. They are carried together, they reach the ear together. The sound remains in the ear, the meaning descends into the heart. But about the sound itself let us first observe how much more excellently we ought to love the meaning. The sound is, as it were, body; the meaning is, as it were, soul. But the sound, as soon as it has struck the air and touched the ear, passes, and is not recalled, and no longer sounds. For the syllables so succeed one another, going before and following, that the second does not sound unless the first has passed. Yet, transitory though it is, it has a great miracle. For see, if I set out loaves before you while hungry, the whole would not reach each one. You would divide among yourselves what I had set out, and the more of you there were, the less you would have. But now I utter a sermon. You do not divide the words and syllables among yourselves, nor cut up my sermon, that one should take this part, another that, and so, piecemeal and bit by bit, what I say should reach each one. But one hears the whole, two hear the whole, more hear the whole, and however many come, they hear the whole. And it suffices for all and is whole for each. Your ear is prepared for hearing, and the neighboring ear of another does not defraud it. If this happens with a sounding word, what happens with the almighty Word? For just as this voice of ours is whole to the ears of all who hear, each one, and is whole with each one, and there are not as many voices of mine as there are ears of yours, but one voice fills many ears, not divided but whole to all, so think of the Word of God: wholly in the heavens, wholly on earth, wholly in the angels, wholly with the Father, wholly with the Virgin, wholly in eternity, wholly in the flesh, wholly in the underworld when he visited it, wholly in paradise to which he transferred the thief. These things I have said about sound.

**5** What if I say something about meaning? And how much less is it than the Word of God? For see, I utter a sound. But when I have uttered it, I do not recall it. But if I want to be heard, I utter another sound, and when that has passed, I utter another, or silence follows. But the meaning I both utter to you and keep with me. And you find what you heard, and I do not lose what I said. See how true these things are, *and let the heart of those who seek the Lord rejoice*[^8]. For the Lord is that principal truth itself. The meaning, then, remaining in my heart, migrates to yours, and does not desert mine. Yet when the meaning is within my heart, and I want it to be also within your heart, I seek by what way it may pass over to you, as a kind of vehicle—the sound. And I take up the sound, and as it were place the meaning upon it, and utter it, and bring it forth, and teach, and do not lose it. If my meaning could do this with my voice, could the Word of God not do it with his flesh? For see, the Word of God, God with God, the Wisdom of God remaining unchangeably with the Father, that he might come forth to us, sought the flesh as a kind of sound, inserted himself into it, and came forth to us, and did not withdraw from the Father. Understand, savor what you have heard. Think how great and of what sort it is, and have higher thoughts of God. He surpasses all light, surpasses all sound, surpasses all meaning. He is to be longed for, and we are to gape for him with love, that *the heart of those who seek the Lord may rejoice*[^9].
