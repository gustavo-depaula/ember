# Sermon 125

**1.** Not unfamiliar things are repeated to your ears or your hearts; yet they restore the affection of the hearer, and in a way the things recalled renew us; nor is it irksome to hear what is known, because the things that are the Lord's are always sweet. So is the exposition of the divine Scriptures, as are the divine Scriptures themselves; even if they are known, they are yet read for remembrance. So too their exposition, even if it is known, is yet to be repeated, that those who have forgotten may be reminded, or those who perhaps have not heard may hear; and that those who hold what they have been accustomed to hear, by repetition it may come about that they cannot forget. For we recall that we have already spoken to Your Charity about this passage of the Gospel. Nor is it yet irksome to recall the same things to you, just as it was not irksome to repeat the same reading to you. The apostle Paul says in a certain Letter: *To write the same things to you, is indeed not irksome to me, but for you it is necessary* [^1]. So we too to say the same things to you, for us is not irksome, but for you safe.

**2.** The five porticoes, in which the sick lay, signify the law, which was first given to the Jews and the people of Israel through the servant of God, Moses. For Moses himself, the minister of the law, made five books. On account of the number of books, then, which he wrote, the five porticoes figured the law. But because the law was not given to heal the sick, but to betray and show; for so the Apostle says: *For if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would altogether be from the law; but Scripture shut up all things under sin, that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe* [^2]; therefore in those porticoes the sick lay, they were not healed. For what does he say? *If a law had been given which could give life*. Therefore those porticoes, which figured the law, could not heal the sick. Someone says to me: Why then was it given? The apostle Paul himself expounded it: *Scripture*, he says, *shut up all things under sin, that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*. For those who were sick thought themselves healthy. They received the law, which they could not fulfill; they learned in what disease they were, and implored the hands of the physician; they wanted to be healed, because they recognized that they labored; which they would not recognize, unless they could not fulfill the law given. For man seemed innocent to himself, and from that very pride of false innocence became more insane. To tame pride, then, and to lay it bare, the law was given; not to free the sick, but to convict the proud. So let Your Charity attend: for this the law was given, to betray diseases, not to take them away. Therefore those sick, who could be sick more secretly in their own houses, if those five porticoes were not there, were betrayed to the eyes of all in those porticoes, but by the porticoes were not healed. So the law was useful for betraying sins, because man, made more abundantly guilty by transgression of the law, might, his pride tamed, implore the help of the one having mercy. Attend to the Apostle: *The law crept in, that the offense might abound; but where the offense abounded, grace too superabounded* [^3]. What is: *the law crept in, that the offense might abound?* As he says in another place: *For where there is no law, neither is there transgression* [^4]. A man can be called a sinner before the law, a transgressor he cannot. But when, the law received, he has sinned, he is found not only a sinner, but also a transgressor. Since, then, transgression is joined to sin, therefore the offense abounded. But the offense abounding, human pride learns at last to be subjected, and to confess to God, and to say: *I am weak* [^5]. To say too those words of the Psalm, which only a humbled soul says: *I said: Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you* [^6]. Let the weak soul say this, at least convicted by transgression; and not healed, but shown by the law. Hear Paul himself too showing you that the law is both good, and yet that, except by the grace of Christ, it does not free from sin. For the law can forbid, and command: to bring medicine, that what does not allow a man to fulfill the law may be healed, it cannot, but grace does that. For the Apostle says: *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man*. That is, now I see that what the law reproves is evil; and what the law commands is good. *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man. I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind, and making me captive under the law of sin* [^7]. This, from the penalty of sin, from the propagation of death, from the condemnation of Adam, fights against the law of the mind, and makes captive to the law of sin, which is in the members. This man was convicted; he received the law that he might be convicted; see what it profited him that he was convicted. Hear the following words: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord* [^8].

**3.** Attend, then. Those porticoes signified the law, carrying the sick, not healing them; betraying, not curing. But who cured the sick? He who went down into the pool. But when did the sick man go down into the pool? When the angel gave a sign by the moving of the water. For that pool was so sanctified, that an angel came, and moved the water. Men saw the water; but from the moving of the troubled water they understood the presence of the angel. If anyone then went down, he was cured. Why, then, was that sick man not cured? Let us consider his words: *I have no man*, he says, *to put me into the pool when the water has been moved; for while I come, another goes down* [^9]. So you cannot afterward go down, if another goes down before you? It is signified here, that only one was cured at the moving of the water. Whoever went down first, he alone was cured; but whoever went down afterward, was not healed at that moving of the water; but waited until it was moved again. What, then, does this sacrament mean? For it is not without cause. Let Your Charity attend. Waters are set in the Apocalypse as a figure of peoples. For when he had seen many waters, John asked in the Apocalypse what it was, and it was said to him that they are peoples [^10]. So that water signified the people of the Jews. For just as that people was held in the law by the five books of Moses, so that water was surrounded by five porticoes. When was the water troubled? When the people of the Jews was troubled. And when was the people of the Jews troubled, except when the Lord Jesus Christ came? The passion of the Lord, the troubling of the water. For the Jews were troubled, when the Lord suffered. Behold, to that very troubling pertains what was just now read. The Jews wanted to kill him, not only because he did these things on the Sabbath, but because he called himself the Son of God, making himself equal to God. For Christ called himself the Son of God in one way, in another it was said to men: *I said: you are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High* [^11]. For if he so made himself the Son of God, as any man can be called a son of God (for by the grace of God men are called sons of God), the Jews would not be angry. But because they understood him to call himself the Son of God in another way, according to which it is: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^12]; and according to what the Apostle says: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God* [^13]; they saw a man, and were angry, because he made himself equal to God. But he knew himself equal, but where they did not see. For what they saw, they wanted to crucify; what they did not see, they were judged by. What did the Jews see? What the Apostles too saw, when Philip said: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*. But what did the Jews not see? What not even the Apostles themselves, when the Lord answered: *So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me? He who sees me, sees also the Father* [^14]. So because the Jews could not see this in him, they held him as a proud and impious man, making himself equal to God. There was a troubling, the water was troubled, the angel had come. For the Lord too was called *the Angel of great counsel* [^15], because the messenger of the Father's will. For "Angel" in Greek is in Latin "Messenger." And you have the Lord saying that he announces to us the kingdom of heaven. So that Angel of great counsel had come, but the Lord of all angels. And for this reason an Angel, because he took on flesh: but the Lord of angels, from this, that *all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made* [^16]. For if *all things*, then the angels too. And therefore he himself was not made, because *all things were made through him*. But what was made, was not made without the Word working. But the flesh, which was made the mother of Christ, could not be born, except by the Word, which was afterward born of her, being created.

**4.** The Jews, then, were troubled. What is this? Why does he do these things on the Sabbath? And most of all at those very words of the Lord: *My Father works until now, and I work* [^17]. It troubled them what they understood carnally, that on the seventh day God rested from all his works [^18]. For it is written in Genesis, and most excellently written, and there is a reason why it is written. But they, thinking that God, as it were wearied, rested after all things on the seventh day, and therefore blessed it, because in it he was refreshed from weariness, did not understand, fools, that he who made all things by a word could not be wearied. Let them read, and tell me how God could be wearied, saying: *Let it be made; and it was made*. Today if a man should so do, as God did, who is wearied? *He said: Let there be light; and there was light*. Likewise: *Let there be a firmament; and it was made* [^19]; or if he said, and it was not made, he was wearied. In another place briefly: *He himself said, and they were made; he himself commanded, and they were created* [^20]. He who so makes, then, how does he labor? But if he does not labor, how does he rest? But in that Sabbath, where God is said to rest from all his works, in the rest of God our rest is signified; because there will be a Sabbath of this world, when the six ages have passed. As it were, the six days of the age pass. One day passed from Adam to Noah; another from the flood to Abraham; the third passed from Abraham to David; the fourth passed from David to the migration to Babylon; the fifth from the migration to Babylon to the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. Now the sixth day is enacted. In the sixth age, in the sixth day we are. Therefore let us be reformed to the image of God, because on the sixth day man was made to the image of God [^21]. What formation did there, this reformation does in us; and what creation did there, this re-creation does in us. After this day in which we now are, after this age, there is to come the rest which is promised to the saints, which was prefigured in those days. Because indeed, after all that he made in the world, he made nothing new afterward in creation. Created things themselves will be turned and changed. For from the time created things were established, nothing more has been added. But yet, unless he who made it governed the world, what was made would fall; he can only administer what he made. Because, then, nothing has been added to creation, he is said to have rested from all his works; but because he does not cease to govern what he made, rightly the Lord said: *My Father works until now*. Let Your Charity attend. He completed, he is said to have rested; for he completed the works, and added nothing. He governs what he made: so he does not cease. But with as great ease as he made, with as great ease he governs. For do not think, brothers, that he did not labor when he founded, and labors because he rules; as in a ship those who build the ship labor, those who steer labor; for they are men. For he, with as great ease as he said, and they were made, with as great ease and judgment governs all things through the Word.

**5.** Not because human affairs seem perverse, should it seem to us that there is no governance of human affairs. For all men are ordered in their places: but to each one it seems that they have no order. Only see what you want to be: for however you want to be, the Maker knows where to place you. Attend to the painter. Various colors are set before him, and he knows where to place each color. Surely the sinner wanted to be a black color; does the order of the Maker therefore not know where to place him? How much does he order from the black color? How many ornaments does the painter make? From it he makes hair, makes a beard, makes eyebrows; he makes the forehead only from white. See what you want to be: do not be concerned where he orders you who does not know how to err, he knows where to place you. For so through these laws of the world we see this come about. Someone wanted to be a housebreaker: the law of the judge knows that he acted against the law; the law of the judge knows where to place him: it orders him most excellently. He indeed lived badly: but the law did not order him badly. From a housebreaker he will be a mine-worker: from the work of the mine-worker how many works are built? The penalty of that condemned man is the ornaments of the city. So then God knows where to place you. Do not think that you disturb the counsel of God, if you want to be perverse. He who knew how to create you, does he not know how to order you? It is good there that you strive toward it, that you may be placed in a good place. What was said of Judas by the Apostle? *He went to his own place* [^22]. With the divine providence surely working, because by his evil will he wanted to be evil, but God did not make the evil by ordering it. But because he himself, evil, wanted to be a sinner, he did what he willed, but suffered what he did not will. In that he did what he willed, his sin is detected; in that he suffered what he did not will, the order of God is praised.

**6.** Why did I say this? That you may understand, brothers, that it was most excellently said by the Lord Jesus Christ: *My Father works until now*. Because he does not forsake the creation which he made. And he said: *As he works, I too work* [^23]. There already he signified himself equal to God. *My Father*, he says, *works until now, and I work*. That carnal sense about the Sabbath was troubled. For they thought the Lord, wearied, had rested, so as to do nothing. They hear: *My Father works until now*, they are troubled. *And I work*: he made himself equal to God, they are troubled. But do not now take fright. The water is troubled, the sick man is to be healed. What is this? Therefore they are troubled, that the Lord may suffer. The Lord suffers, the precious blood is poured out, the sinner is redeemed, grace is granted to the one sinning, and saying: *Wretched man that I am, who has freed me from the body of this death? The grace of God through our Lord Jesus Christ* [^24]. But how is he cured? If he goes down. For that pool was such that one went down into it, not up to it. For there could be such pools too, so constructed that one went up to them. But why was that one made such, that one went down into it? Because the passion of the Lord seeks the humble. Let the humble go down, let him not be proud, if he wants to be healed. But why one? Because the Church is one through the whole world, unity is saved. Where, then, one is saved, unity is signified. By "one" understand unity. From unity, then, do not depart, if you do not want to be exempt from this salvation.

**7.** What, then, does it mean that he was sick for thirty-eight years? I know, brothers, that I have already said these things; but even those who read forget, how much more those who rarely hear? Let Your Charity attend a little. In the number forty the completion of justice is figured. The completion of justice, because here we live in labor, in toils, in continence, in fastings, in vigils, in tribulations; this very thing is the exercise of justice, to bear this time, and to fast in a way from this world; not from the food of the flesh, which we do rarely; but from the love of the world, which we ought always to do. He, then, fulfills the law who abstains from this world. For one cannot love what is eternal, unless he ceases to love what is temporal. Attend to the love of man: think of it as the hand of the soul. If it holds something, it cannot hold something else. But that it may be able to hold what is given, let it let go what it holds. This I say, see that I say it openly: He who loves the world cannot love God; he has his hand occupied. God says to him: Hold what I give. He does not want to let go what he held: he cannot receive what is offered. Did I say: Let someone not possess? If he can, if perfection requires this of him, let him not possess. If he cannot, hindered by some necessity, let him possess, let him not be possessed; let him hold, not be held; let him be the master of his property, not the servant; as the Apostle says: *For the rest, brothers, the time is short; it remains that those who have wives be as though not having them; and those who buy, as though not possessing; and those who rejoice, as though not rejoicing; and those who weep, as though not weeping; and those who use this world, as though not using it: for the figure of this world passes away. I want you to be without anxiety* [^25]. What is: Do not love what you possess in this world? Let it not hold your hand, by which God is to be held. Let your love not be occupied, by which you can tend to God, and cling to him who created you.

**8.** You say, and answer me: God too knows that I possess innocently. Temptation proves it. What you possess is troubled for you, and you blaspheme. Just now we were suffering such things. What you possess is troubled, and you are not found the one you were, and you show that one thing is in your voice now, and another the day before in your voice. And would that you would at least defend even your own with an outcry, and not try with boldness to usurp another's; and, what is worse, lest you be reproved, you say that what is another's is yours. But what need is there? This I admonish, this I say, brothers, and I admonish fraternally; God commands, and I admonish, because I am admonished. He frightens me, who does not allow me to be silent. From me he demands what he gave. For he gave it to be doled out, not to be kept. But if I keep it, and hide it, he says to me: *Wicked and slothful servant, why did you not give the money to the moneychangers, that I, coming, might demand it with interest* [^26]? And what will it profit me, that I lost nothing of what I received? It is too little for my Lord, he is greedy: but the greed of God is our salvation. He is greedy, he seeks his own coins, he gathers his image. *You should give*, he says, *the money to the moneychangers, and I, coming, would demand it with interest*. And if perhaps forgetfulness made me not admonish you; the temptations and tribulations which we suffer would admonish you. Surely you have heard the word of God. Blessed be the Lord and his glory. For you have gathered, and you hang on the word of the dispenser of God. Do not attend to our flesh, through which it is presented to you; because the hungry do not attend to the cheapness of the little vessel, but to the dearness of the food. God proves you. You have gathered, praise the word of God; temptation will prove how you hear: you will have affairs, in which it will be shown what kind you are. For even one who today shouts insults was yesterday gladly hearing. Therefore I forewarn, therefore I say, therefore I am not silent, my brothers, because the time of questioning will come. *For the Lord questions the just and the impious*. Surely you sang this, surely we sang it together: *The Lord questions the just and the impious*. And what follows? *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul* [^27]. And in another place: *In the thoughts of the impious there will be questioning* [^28]. Not where I question you, there God questions. I question your tongue, God questions your thought. But he knows how you hear, and knows how to demand, who commands that I give. He willed me to be a doler-out, the exacting he kept for himself. To admonish, to teach, to rebuke is ours; but to save and to crown, or to condemn and send into hell, is not ours. But the judge will hand over to the officer, the officer into prison. *Amen I say to you, you will not come out from there, until you pay the last farthing* [^29].

**9.** Let us return, then, to the matter. The completion of justice is shown by the number forty. What is it to fulfill the number forty? To restrain oneself from the love of this world. Continence from temporal things, lest they be ruinously loved, is, as it were, to fast from this world. Therefore the Lord fasted forty days [^30], and Moses [^31], and Elijah [^32]. He, then, who gave servants to be able to fast forty days, could he not fast eighty or a hundred? Why did he not want more than he gave servants, except because in that very number forty is the mystery of fasting, to restrain oneself from this world? What is it to say this? What the Apostle says: *The world has been crucified to me, and I to the world* [^33]. He, then, fulfills the number forty. And what does the Lord show? That Moses did this, Elijah did this, Christ did this, the law too, the Prophets too, the Gospel too teaches this; lest you think there is one thing in the law, another in the Prophets, another in the Gospel. All the Scriptures teach you nothing else, but continence from the love of the world, that your love may run toward God. It is figured that the law teaches this, that Moses fasted forty days. It is figured that the Prophets teach this, that Elijah fasted forty days. It is figured that the Gospel teaches this, that the Lord fasted forty days. Therefore on the mountain those three appeared, the Lord in the middle, Moses and Elijah at the sides. Why? Because the Gospel itself has testimony from the law and the Prophets [^34]. But why is the completion of justice in the number forty? In the Psalter it was said: *God, I will sing you a new song; on a ten-stringed psaltery I will play to you* [^35]. Which signifies the ten precepts of the law, which the Lord came not to abolish, but to fulfill. But that law itself, through the whole world, is established to have four corners: the East, the West, the South and the North, as Scripture says. Whence too the vessel, which carried all kinds of animals figured, which was shown to Peter, when it was said: *Slay and eat* [^36], that it might be shown that the nations would believe and enter into the body of the Church, just as what we eat enters into our body, was let down from heaven by four corners (these are the four parts of the world) showed that the whole world would believe. So in the number forty is continence from the world. This is the fullness of the law: but the fullness of the law is love. Therefore before Easter we fast forty days. For it is a sign, before Easter, of this laborious life of ours, where in labors and toils, and continence, we fulfill the law. But after Easter, that is, we celebrate the days of the Lord's resurrection, signifying our resurrection. Therefore fifty days are celebrated: because the wage of a denarius is added to the forty, and it becomes fifty. How is the denarius a wage? Have you not read that those who were hired for the vineyard, whether those at the first, or those at the sixth, or those at the last hour, could receive only a denarius [^37]? When the wage of our justice has been added to it, we shall be in the fifty. Then it will be free for us only to praise God. Therefore through those days we say *Alleluia*. For *Alleluia* is the praise of God. In this fragility of mortality, in this forty here, as it were before the resurrection, let us groan in prayers, that then we may praise. Now is the time of desiring, then will be the time of embracing and enjoying. Let us not fail in the time of the forty, that we may rejoice in the time of the fifty.

**10.** But who is it who fulfills the law, except him who has love? Ask the Apostle: *The fullness of the law is love* [^38]. *For the whole law is fulfilled in one saying, in that which is written: Love your neighbor as yourself* [^39]. But the precept of love is twofold: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind. This precept is great. The other is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself*. They are the Lord's words in the Gospel: *On these two precepts the whole law hangs and the Prophets* [^40]. Without twofold love the law cannot be fulfilled. As long as the law is not fulfilled, it is weakness. Therefore he who was sick for thirty-eight years had two less. What is: He had two less? He did not fulfill those two precepts. What does it profit that the rest are fulfilled, if those are not fulfilled? Do you have thirty-eight? If you do not have those two, the rest will profit you nothing. You have two less, without which the rest are worth nothing, if you do not have the two precepts which lead to salvation. *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have love, I have become sounding bronze, or a tinkling cymbal. And if I know all sacraments and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to move mountains; but do not have love, I am nothing. And if I distribute all my substance, and if I hand over my body to be burned; but do not have love, it profits me nothing* [^41]. They are the Apostle's words. All those things, then, which he said, are, as it were, thirty-eight years: but because love was not there, it was weakness. Who, then, will heal from this weakness, except him who came to give love? *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another* [^42]. And because he came to give love, and love perfects the law; rightly he said: *I did not come to abolish the law, but to fulfill it* [^43]. He healed the sick man, and told him to carry his pallet, and go into his house. This he said also to the paralytic whom he healed [^44]. What is it: to carry our pallet? The pleasure of our flesh. Where we lie sick, it is, as it were, our bed. But those who have been healed restrain and carry it, are not held by that very flesh. So, healthy, restrain the fragility of your flesh, that by the sign of the forty-day fast from this world, you may fulfill the number forty, who healed that sick man, who came not to abolish the law, but to fulfill it.

**11.** Having heard this, direct your heart to God. Do not deceive yourselves. Question yourselves then, when it is well in the world; question yourselves then, whether you love this world, or whether you do not love it; learn to let go, before you are let go. What is it: to let go? Not to love in mind. While what you are to lose is still with you, either living or dying you let it go, it cannot be with you always; while, then, it is still with you, loose the love; be ready in the will of God, to hang upon God. Hold yourself to him whom you do not lose unwilling; that if it happen that you lose these temporal things, you may say: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it was done; blessed be the name of the Lord* [^45]. But if it happen, and God wills this, that those things you have be with you to the end; freed from this life, you receive the denarius of the fifty, and there comes to be in you the completion of blessedness, when you sing *Alleluia*. Having these things which I have recalled in memory, may they avail that you do not love the world. Its friendship is evil, it is deceitful, it makes one an enemy of God. Quickly in one temptation a man offends God, and becomes an enemy. Rather, he does not then become an enemy, but then is betrayed to have been an enemy. For when he loved and praised, he was an enemy; but neither did he himself know it, nor others. Temptation comes, the vein is touched, and the fever is betrayed. And so, brothers, the love of the world, and the friendship of the world make men enemies of God. And it itself does not display what it promised, it is a liar, and deceives. Therefore men do not cease to hope in this world, and who comes to all that he hopes for? But to whatever he comes, at once that grows cheap to him to which he comes. They begin to desire other things, other things are hoped for as dear; which when they come, whatever comes to you, grows cheap. Hold, then, to God, because he never grows cheap, because nothing is more beautiful. For these things grow cheap for this reason, because they cannot stand, because they are not what he is. For to you, O soul, nothing suffices except him who created you. Whatever else you grasp is wretched; because he alone can suffice you who made you to his likeness. From that very voice it was said: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us* [^46]. There alone can there be security, and where there can be security, in a way there will be an insatiable satiety. For you will neither be sated, so as to want to depart; nor will anything be lacking, so that you suffer, as it were, want.
