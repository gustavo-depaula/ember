# Sermon 69: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 11:28-30: "Come to Me, All You Who Labor and Are Burdened," and What Follows

**1. 1.** We heard in the Gospel that the Lord, gladdened in spirit, said to God the Father: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones. Yes, Father, for so it was pleasing before you. All things have been handed over to me by my Father: and no one knows the Son, except the Father; nor does anyone know the Father, except the Son, and he to whom the Son wishes to reveal him*[^1]. In crying out we labor, in hearing you labor. Let us hear, then, him who follows, and says: *Come to me, all you who labor*[^2]. For why do we all labor, except because we are mortal men, frail, weak, carrying vessels of clay, which make straits for one another? But if the vessels of flesh are straitened, let the spaces of love be enlarged. What, then, does he say: *Come to me, all you who labor*, except that you may not labor? Finally, his promise is at hand: since he called those who labor, lest perhaps they ask with what reward they were called: *And I*, he says, *will refresh you*[^3].

**1. 2.** *Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me*[^4]: not to fashion a world, not to create all things visible and invisible, not to do miracles in the very world, and raise the dead; but *that I am meek and humble of heart*[^5]. Do you wish to be great? begin from the least. You think of constructing a great building of loftiness; first think of the foundation of humility. And however great a mass of building one wishes and intends to set on top, the greater the building will be, the deeper he digs the foundation. And the building, indeed, when it is constructed, rises into the heights: but he who digs the foundation is pressed down to the depths. So the building too is humbled before its loftiness, and the summit is raised after the humbling.

**2. 3.** What is the summit of the building to be constructed, which we are striving at? To what is the peak of the edifice to reach? I say it quickly, even to the sight of God. You see how lofty it is, how great a thing it is, to behold God. He who desires it understands both what I say and what he hears. The sight of God is promised to us, of the true God, the most high God. For this is the good, for the one seeing to see the seer. For those who worship false gods easily see them: but they see those, who have eyes and do not see. But to us is promised the vision of the living and seeing God, that we may crave to see that God of whom Scripture says: *He who planted the ear, does he not hear? he who formed the eye, does he not consider?*[^6] Does he then not hear, who made for you whereby you hear? and does he not see, who created whereby you see? Well in the same Psalm he speaks beforehand and says: *Understand, then, you who are foolish among the people; and you fools, at some time be wise*[^7]. For many do evil, while they think they are not seen by God. And it is hard indeed that they believe he cannot see: but they think he is unwilling. Few are found of such impiety that there is fulfilled in them what is written: *The fool said in his heart, There is no God*[^8]. That madness is of few. For as great godliness is of few; so great impiety is no less of few. But what I say, the crowd says: Behold, does God now think of that, to know what I do in my house, and does God care what I wish to do in my bed? Who says it? *Understand, you who are foolish among the people; and you fools, at some time be wise.* You, being a man, since you labor if you know all the affairs of your house and all the words, all the deeds of your servants pertain to you, do you think that God too so labors to attend to you, who did not labor to create you? Did he not direct his eye to you, who made yours? You were not, and he created you that you might be: does he not care for you, now that you are, who calls the things that are not, as though they were[^9]? Do not, then, promise yourself this. Whether you wish or not, he sees you; and from his eyes there is nowhere you may hide yourself. For if you ascend into heaven, there he is; if you descend into hell, he is present[^10]. You labor, unwilling to withdraw from evil deeds, and wishing not to be seen by God. A great labor. Do you wish to do evil daily, and suppose you are not seen? Hear the Scripture saying: *He who planted the ear, does he not hear? he who formed the eye, does he not consider?* Where do you hide your evil deeds from the eyes of God? If you do not wish to withdraw from them, you labor much.

**3. 4.** Hear him saying: *Come to me, all you who labor*[^11]. You do not end the labor by fleeing. Do you choose to flee from him, not to him? Find whither, and flee. But if for this reason you cannot flee from him, because he is everywhere present; flee near at hand to God, who is present where you stand. Flee. Behold, by fleeing you have passed beyond the heavens, there he is: you have descended to hell, there he is: whatever solitudes of the lands you choose, there is he who said: *I fill heaven and earth*[^12]. So if he himself fills heaven and earth, and there is nowhere to flee from him; do not labor: flee to the present one, lest you feel the one coming. Presume that you will see, by living well, him by whom you are seen even when living badly. For by living badly you can be seen, you cannot see: but by living well you both are seen, and see. For how much more familiarly will he see you, who crowns the worthy one, who mercifully saw to call the unworthy one? Nathanael said to the Lord, whom he did not yet know: *Whence do you know me?* The Lord said to him: *When you were under the fig tree, I saw you*[^13]. Christ sees you in your shadow: will he not see you in his light? For what is: *When you were under the fig tree, I saw you?* What does this mean? what does it signify? Recall the original sin of Adam, in which we all die. When he first sinned, he made for himself loincloths of fig leaves, signifying in those leaves the itch of lust, to which by sinning he came[^14]. Thence we are born, so we are born, in the flesh of sin we are born, which only the likeness of the flesh of sin heals. Therefore God sent his Son in the likeness of the flesh of sin[^15]. Thence he came, but not so. For the virgin conceived him not by lust, but by faith. He came into the virgin, who was before the virgin. Her whom he created he chose, her whom he would choose he created. He brought to the virgin fecundity, he did not take away integrity. He, then, who came without the itch of the fig leaves to you, when you were under the fig tree, saw you. Prepare yourself to see sublimely the one by whom you have been seen mercifully. But because it is a great summit, think of the foundation. By what, you say, foundation? Learn from him, that he is meek and humble of heart. Dig this foundation of humility in yourself, and you will reach the summit of love. Turned to the Lord, etc.
