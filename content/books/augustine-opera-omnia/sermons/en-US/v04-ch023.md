# Sermon 112

**1.** Holy readings have been set before us, both for us to hear, and from which to bring forth, with the Lord's help, something of a sermon. In the apostolic reading thanks are given to God for the faith of the nations, surely for this reason, because he himself made it. In the psalm we said: *God of hosts, turn us, and show your face, and we shall be saved* [^1]. In the Gospel we have been called to a supper. Rather, others called, we not called but led; not only led but even compelled. For we heard thus, that *A certain man made a great supper* [^2]. Who is this man, except the *Mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus* [^3]? He had sent that the invited might come; because the hour had now come, that they should come. Who are the invited, except those called through the Prophets sent before? How long is it, from which Prophets are sent and invite to Christ's supper! And they were sent to the people of Israel. Often they were sent, often they called, that men might come to the hour of the supper. But those, while they accepted the inviters, refused the supper. What is: They accepted the inviters, refused the supper? They read the Prophets, and killed Christ [^4]. But when they killed Christ, then, not knowing it, they prepared the supper for us. The supper now prepared, Christ slain, after Christ's resurrection the supper of the Lord which the faithful know was commended, and confirmed by his hands and mouth, the Apostles were sent, to whom the Prophets had been sent before. Come to the supper—for so it had been appointed, that Christ should be slain—the Apostles said: Come to the supper.

**2.** They made excuses, who refused to come. How did they make excuses, brothers? There were three excuses. One said: *I have bought a farm, I go to see it: hold me excused* [^5]. Another said: *I have bought five yoke of oxen, I want to prove them: hold me excused* [^6]. The third said: *I have taken a wife: hold me excused, I cannot come* [^7]. Do we suppose these are not the excuses that hinder all men who refuse to come to this supper? Let us seek them, let us examine them, let us find them: but that we may beware of them. In the bought farm, dominion is noted. So pride is chastised. For to have a farm, to hold it, to possess it, to subject men to oneself in it, to lord it, delights. An evil vice, the first vice; for the first man wanted to lord it, who refused to have a Lord. What is "to lord it," except to rejoice in one's own power? There is a greater power: let us be subject to him, that we may be able to be safe. *I have bought a farm: hold me excused*. Pride, invited, refused to come.

**3.** Another said: *I have bought five yoke of oxen*. Would not "I have bought oxen" suffice? There is surely something which by its obscurity provokes us to seek and understand, and, because it is shut, exhorts us to knock. "Five yoke of oxen"; the senses of this flesh. Five are counted the senses of this flesh, which is known to all; and those who perhaps have not noticed, when reminded, without doubt recognize it. The senses of this flesh are found to be five; sight in the eyes, hearing in the ears, smell in the nostrils, taste in the gullet, touch in all the members. White and black and colored in whatever way, bright and dark, we perceive by seeing; harsh and tuneful, we perceive by hearing; sweet-smelling and foul-smelling, we perceive by smelling; sweet and bitter, we perceive by tasting; hard and soft, smooth and rough, hot and cold, heavy and light, we perceive by touching. They are five, and they are yokes. But that they are yokes, in the first three senses easily appears; there are two eyes, two ears, twin nostrils: behold three yokes. But in the gullet, that is, the sense of tasting, a certain doubling is found; because nothing tastes by tasting, unless it is touched by the tongue and palate. The pleasure of the flesh, which pertains to touch, is more hiddenly doubled; for it is outside and inside: so it too is twofold. Why are they called yokes of oxen? Because through these senses of the flesh earthly things are sought, and oxen turn the earth. But there are men removed from faith, given to earthly things, occupied with carnal things; they refuse to believe anything, except what they perceive by this fivefold sense of the body. In its senses they set for themselves the rules of all truth. I, he says, do not believe, except what I see; behold what I have known, behold what I know. It is white, it is black, it is round, it is square, it is colored thus or thus; I have known it, I know it, I hold it; nature itself teaches me. I am not compelled to believe what cannot be shown me. It is a voice: I perceive that it is a voice; it sings well, it sings badly, it is sweet, it is harsh; I have known it, I know it, it has reached me. It smells good, it smells bad: I perceive it, I know it. This is sweet, this bitter; this salty, this tasteless; what more you may tell me, I do not know. By touching I have known what is hard, what is soft, what is smooth, what is rough, what is warm, what is cold; what more will you show me?

**4.** By such a hindrance our apostle Thomas was held, who about the Lord Christ, that is, about Christ's resurrection, would not believe even by the eyes alone. *Unless I put*, he said, *my fingers in the place of the nails and wounds, in his side, I will not believe* [^8]. And the Lord, who could rise again without any trace of a wound, kept the scars, that they might be touched by the doubter, and the wounds of the heart be healed. Yet, about to call to the supper, against the excuses of the five yoke of oxen: *Blessed*, he says, *are they who do not see, and will believe* [^9]. We, my brothers, called to the supper, were not hindered by these five yokes. For we did not desire to see the face of the Lord's flesh in this time, nor did we long to admit to our ears the voice proceeding from the mouth of that flesh; we sought no temporal odor in him; a certain woman poured over him most precious ointment, that house was filled with the fragrance; but we were not there; behold, we did not smell it, and we believed. He gave the disciples the supper consecrated with his own hands; but we did not recline at that banquet, and yet that very supper we eat daily by faith. And do not think it a great thing to have been present at that supper, which he gave with his own hands, without faith. Faith afterward proved better than faithlessness then. Paul, who believed, was not there; Judas, who betrayed, was there. How many even now at this very supper, although they did not then see that table, nor look with their eyes on what the Lord carried in his hands, nor taste it with their gullet, yet, because it is the same that is now prepared, how many even now at this very supper eat and drink judgment to themselves!

**5.** But from where did an occasion, as it were, arise for the Lord to speak of this supper? One of those reclining had said—for he was at the banquet to which he had been invited: *Blessed is he who eats bread in the kingdom of God* [^10]. As though this man sighed for something far off, and the bread itself was reclining before him. For what is the bread from the kingdom of God, except he who says: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven* [^11]? Do not prepare the gullet, but the heart. From there this supper was commended; behold, we have believed in Christ, when by faith we receive. In receiving, we know what we should think. We receive a little, and in the heart we are fattened. Therefore not what is seen, but what is believed, feeds. So we did not seek even that last sense—we did not say: If they believed who saw the Lord himself rising (if what is said is true) with their eyes, touched him with their hands; we did not touch him: why do we believe? If we thought such things, we would be hindered from the supper by those five yoke of oxen. And that you may know, brothers, that not the delight of these five senses, which soothes and brings in pleasure, but a certain curiosity was noted, he did not say: *I have bought five yoke of oxen*, to feed them, but: *To prove them*. He who wants to prove them, through the yokes of oxen does not want to doubt, just as holy Thomas through those very yokes refused to doubt. Let me see, let me touch, let me put my fingers. Behold, he says, *put your fingers into my side, and do not be unbelieving* [^12]. I was slain for you: through the place you want to touch, I poured out blood, that I might redeem you; and do you still doubt about me, unless you touch me? Behold, this too I grant, behold, this too I display: touch, and believe. I have found the place of the wound: heal the wound of doubt.

**6.** The third said: *I have taken a wife*. This is the pleasure of the flesh; how many it hinders! Would that outside, and not within. There are men who say: It is not well with a man, unless the delights of the flesh are present. These are they whom the Apostle notes, saying: *Let us eat and drink; for tomorrow we die* [^13]. Such things that proud rich man said at his banquets: *Let us eat and drink: for tomorrow we die*. Who has risen from there to here? Who has told us what is done there? We take with us this, that in this time it is well with us. He who says this has taken a wife: he embraces the flesh, he delights in the pleasures of the flesh, he excuses himself from the supper; let him beware, lest he die of an inner hunger. Attend to John, the holy apostle, the evangelist: *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world* [^14]. O you who come to the Lord's supper: *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world*. He did not say: Do not have, but: *Do not love*. You had, you possessed, you loved: you clung. The love of earthly things is the birdlime of spiritual wings. Behold, you desired: you clung. Who will give you wings like a dove's [^15]? When will you fly, where you may truly rest, when here, where you have clung badly, you wanted perversely to rest? *Do not love the world*; it is the divine trumpet. The assembly of this trumpet is the whole world. To the whole world it is said: *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. Whoever loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him: for all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age* [^16]. From the bottom he began: where the Gospel ended, from there he began; and from where the Gospel began, there he set his end. *The desire of the flesh: I have taken a wife. The desire of the eyes: I have bought five yoke of oxen. The ambition of the age: I have bought a farm* [^17].

**7.** But for this reason these senses were commended as a whole by the eyes alone, because the eyes have the primacy among the five senses. Therefore, although sight properly pertains to the eyes, we are accustomed to call "seeing" through all five senses. How? First, what pertains to the eyes themselves, you say: See how white it is, attend and see how white it is; this pertains to the eyes. Hear and see how tuneful it is; can you say conversely: Hear and see how white it is? This which is said, "see," runs through all the senses; but the property of the other senses does not run through itself. Attend and see how white it is; hear and see how tuneful it is; smell and see how sweet it is; taste and see how sweet it is; touch and see how soft it is. Surely, since there are five senses, we would rather say thus: Hear and perceive how tuneful it is, or: Smell and perceive how sweet it is; taste and perceive how sweet it is; touch and perceive how warm it is; feel and perceive how smooth it is; feel and perceive how soft it is. None of these is wont to be said. For even the Lord himself, when after the resurrection he had appeared to his disciples, and they, seeing him, still wavered in faith, thinking they saw a spirit: *Why*, he said, *do you doubt, and why do thoughts rise in your heart? See my hands and my feet* [^18]. And it is too little, *See: Touch*, he said, *and feel and see* [^19]. Attend and see: *feel and see*. With the eyes alone see; with all the senses see. Because he was seeking the inner sense of faith, he applied himself also to the outer senses. We have received nothing from the Lord by these outer senses; by hearing we heard, by the heart we believed [^20]; and that very hearing not from his mouth, but from the mouth of his preachers, from the mouth of those who were already supping and inviting us by belching forth.

**8.** Let us, then, take away from our midst vain and evil excuses: let us come to the supper, by which we may be fattened within. Let not the haughtiness of pride hinder us, let not unlawful curiosity lift us up or hold us and turn us from God; let not the pleasure of the flesh hinder us from the will of the heart. Let us come, and be fattened. And who came, except beggars, the weak, the lame, the blind? But those did not come, the healthy rich, as though walking well and seeing keenly, that is, presuming much on themselves, and therefore the more despairing the more proud. Let the beggars come: because he invites, who for our sake became poor, when he was rich, that by his poverty we beggars might be enriched [^21]. Let the weak come: because *the healthy have no need of a physician, but the sick* [^22]. Let the lame come, who say to him: *Direct my steps according to your word* [^23]. Let the blind come, who say to him: *Enlighten my eyes, lest I ever sleep in death* [^24]. Such came at the hour, those first invited being rejected by their excuse. They came at the hour, they entered from the streets and lanes of the city. And the servant who had been sent answered: *Lord, what you commanded has been done, and there is still room. Go out*, he says, *into the ways and hedges; and whomever you find, compel them to enter* [^25]. Those you find, do not wait for them to consent: *compel them to enter*. A great supper, a great house I have prepared; I will not allow a place to be empty there. The nations came from the streets and lanes; let heretics and schismatics come from the ways and hedges. *Compel them to enter*. Here they find peace, for those who build hedges seek divisions. Let them be drawn from the hedges, let them be torn from the thorns. They have stuck in the hedges, and they refuse to be compelled: Let us enter, they say, by our own will. The Lord did not command this: *Compel them*, he says, *to enter. Outside* let necessity be found, within will shall be born.
