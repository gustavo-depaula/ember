# Sermon 77/C: From the Sermon on What Is Written: "If Anyone Wishes to Come After Me, Let Him Deny Himself" [Fragment]

**1.** When a man sees someone living badly, and perhaps furnishing something to the Church, and does not rebuke him, he flees in mind. What is "to flee in mind"? To fear. Fear is an inner flight. Why does he fear? Because he is a hireling. Lest perhaps the other take it ill that he is rebuked, and not give what he is wont to give. He sees the wolf coming, that is, the devil breaking the necks of the one living badly; and he flees in mind, abstains from useful rebuke, full of fear. But he who is a shepherd, and has care for the sheep, does not abandon him[^1], and does what the Apostle says: *Rebuke the unruly, console the fainthearted*, and the rest[^2]. Let no one, then, think a shepherd godly, or one who calls himself a shepherd, because he does not render evil for evil, when he rather renders evil for good. For the other, even if he is a wicked sinner, gives of his goods to the Church; but this one renders evil for good to him, from whom he withdraws rebuke. But since all this must be done out of love, but sometimes men think their rebukers their enemies, therefore, when he had said: *Rebuke the unruly*, he added: *Console the fainthearted.* For perhaps from rebuke he begins to faint, and is disturbed: then one must console him. *Receive the weak*[^3], lest through weakness they fall. If weakness has made him falter, let love receive him in its bosom. And when he had said these things, he added at the last: *See that no one render to anyone evil for evil*[^4]. So rebuke is not an evil, if it is done. But the good sheep, when it is rebuked by its overseer, what does it say? *The just man shall correct me in mercy*[^5].

[^1]: See John 10:12-13
[^2]: 1 Thess 5:14
[^3]: See Rom 14:1
[^4]: 1 Thess 5:15
[^5]: Ps 141:5 (140:5)
