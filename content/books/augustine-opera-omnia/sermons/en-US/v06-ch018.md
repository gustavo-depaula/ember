# Sermon 198: Augmented

**1.** We admonish your Charity, since we see that you have, as it were, assembled solemnly and gathered more frequently than usual at this hour and at this place, that again and again you may remember what you were just now singing, lest the tongue be clamoring while the heart is mute; but that what you sounded with your voice to one another's ears, you may cry with feeling to the ears of God. And this you sang: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles, that we may confess your holy name*[^1]. And now, if the solemnity of the Gentiles, which takes place on this day in worldly and fleshly joy, in the din of the most empty and most base songs, in the celebration of that false festival — if the things that the Gentiles do today do not delight you, you are gathered from among the Gentiles.

**2.** You certainly were singing, and the sound of the divine song is still fresh in your ears: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles*. Who can be gathered from among the Gentiles, except when he is made safe? Those, then, who mingle with the Gentiles are not safe. But those are saved who are gathered from among the Gentiles; they are saved by the salvation of faith, spiritual salvation, the salvation of the promises of God, the salvation of good hope, the salvation of most sincere love. He, then, who believes and hopes and loves, is not at once now to be called safe. For it matters what he believes, what he hopes, what he loves. For no one lives in any sort of life without these three affections of the soul: believing, hoping, loving. If you do not believe what the Gentiles believe, do not hope what the Gentiles hope, do not love what the Gentiles love, you are gathered from among the Gentiles. Nor let the bodily commingling frighten you in so great a separation of mind. For what is so separated as that they should believe demons to be gods, while you believe God who is the true God? that they should hope for the empty things of the world, while you hope for eternal life in Christ? that they should love the world, while you love the maker of the world? He, then, who believes one thing, hopes another, loves another, let him prove it by his life, show it by his deeds. Are you going to keep today the celebration of New Year's gifts with a pagan, to play at dice with a pagan, to make yourself drunk with a pagan: how do you believe one thing, hope another, love another? how do you sing with unblemished brow: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles?* For you are set apart from the Gentiles, mingled in body with the Gentiles, by an unlike life. And how great that separation is, see, if only you do it, if only you prove it. For now the Lord our God Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who for our sake was made man, gave a price for us. And so, if he has already given the price, he gave it for this, that he might redeem, that he might gather us from among the Gentiles. But if you mingle with the Gentiles, you are unwilling to follow him who redeemed you. And you mingle with them in life, in deeds, in heart, by hoping for such things, by believing such things, by loving such things. You are ungrateful to your Redeemer, and do not acknowledge your price, the blood of the spotless Lamb. That you may, then, follow your Redeemer, who redeemed you with his blood, do not mingle with the Gentiles in the likeness of manners and deeds. They give New Year's gifts, you give alms. For we do not say to you, brothers, "They give, you do not give"; rather, give more than they give, but as those who believe one thing, hope another, love another; for we do not say to you, "They believe, you do not believe; they hope, you do not hope; they love, you do not love"; but we say to you: "They believe that, you believe this; they hope that, you hope this; they love that, you love this; they give that or give to those, you give this or give to these." They give New Year's gifts, then; you give alms. They commit themselves to the chances of earthly things, you commit yourselves to the words of the divine Scriptures. They run to the theater, you to the church. They get drunk, you fast. If you do these things, you have truly sung: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles*. Now those who willingly heard what I said stand surely with those who did not willingly hear; and yet the former are now gathered from among the Gentiles, while the latter are mingled with the Gentiles.

**3.** I now speak to Christians: if you believe what the Gentiles believe, if you hope what the Gentiles hope, if you love what the Gentiles love, live as the Gentiles live; but if you believe one thing, hope another, love another, live otherwise, and prove your differing faith and hope and love by differing manners. What is it that the Gentiles believe? As I have already said, they call gods those whom the apostle Paul shows us to be otherwise: *For the things that the Gentiles sacrifice*, he says, *they sacrifice to demons and not to God. I do not want you to become partners with demons*[^2]. Therefore their manners delight their gods. But he who said: *I do not want you to become partners with demons*, willed that we should be separated, in life and manners, from those who serve demons. For those demons take delight in songs of vanity, take delight in trifling din and in the various base things of the theaters, the madness of the circus, the cruelty of the amphitheater, the heated contests of those who, for pestilent men, take up both quarrels and disputes even to enmities, for a mime, for an actor, for a charioteer, for a hunter. Doing these things, they place, as it were, incense to demons out of their hearts. For the seducing spirits rejoice in the seduced, and they are fed on the evil manners and the base and infamous life of those whom they have seduced and deceived. But now, if you say to a man, "You love a charioteer," he answers, "Of course I love him," because, even if he denies it, he is caught favoring him, shouting for him, brawling for him. And if you add, "You love him greatly," he will answer, "Greatly." If you say to him, "May you be such a one, and your sons," at once, if he appears respectable, he will answer, vexed, "Why have you done me an injury?" "Do I do you an injury when I say, May you be such a one, or do you, who so love such a one as you dread to be?" You, on the contrary, love the martyrs. So, just as you say to a pagan, "You love that charioteer," and he says, "I love him," and does not blush, so say to a Christian, "You love Cyprian," he answers, "I love him"; say to him, "May you be such a one," he answers, "Would that God might grant this!" How sincere that love is, how chaste, how secure, especially of one already crowned! For of one still struggling, love is anxious; yet all are loved in him who has already conquered, who now sits at the right hand of the Father[^3], who now from above, as the president of that contest which Paul recalls[^4], not only watches those who struggle, but also helps those who endure.

**4.** And so many will struggle today in their heart with the word they have heard. For we said: Do not give New Year's gifts, give to the poor. It is too little to give only as much, give even more. You do not want more? then give as much. But you say to me: "When I give New Year's gifts, I too receive." What then? When you give to a poor man, will you receive nothing? Surely you do not believe this, which the Gentiles believe; surely you do not hope this, which the Gentiles hope; surely you do not love this, which the Gentiles love. Behold, if you say that you receive nothing when you give to a poor man, you have become equal to the Gentiles, in vain you have sung: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles*. You have forgotten what will be said to those who have given: *Come, you blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^5], and what will be said to those who have not believed: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^6]. If he so gave the kingdom to these as to give nothing to those but only to leave them, you ought to love what he gives and to be unwilling to be cheated of so vast and ineffable a good. But this is not the only thing, that he sends these into the kingdom but not those; rather, he also says: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*. Let fear and love compel you. If you love less what he promises, fear what he threatens. For you begin from fear, you are perfected in love. By fearing hell you do what is commanded, but as long as you do it by fearing, you do it as a slave; but when you do it loving, you now do it free. Be a good slave, that you may merit to be free. Begin to fear the one you should love, whom, when you have loved him, you will not fear. For it is written: *Fear is not in love, but perfect love casts out fear*[^7]. The apostle John says this. But if *perfect love casts out fear*, let fear first possess the breast: there love will be born, and so, as love grows, fear will decrease; as the one increases, the other will be diminished; when the one is perfected, the other is shut out. For *fear is not in love, but perfect love casts out fear*. You do it if you love; if you do not yet love, do it if you fear; but if you neither fear nor love, in vain you will sing: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles*. For you are still Gentiles, yoked together with unbelievers[^8]. What your Lord did for you, do you for the pagans, because, when the Lord did it for you, you were not yet a Christian. Let those who believe one thing, hope another, love another, hear, whether they will or not: let us say what we know; let them do what they will, only let them know that they do not for nothing whatever they do. For there is a reward not only for good deeds, but also for evil. The reward of evil deeds is called punishment, of good deeds, a crown.

**5.** What, then, did your Lord do for you, before you were a Christian? He suffered for you. And what does he say in the psalms? *But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself in sackcloth, and humbled my soul in fasting*[^9]. If you understand it figuratively, the Lord, as it were, fasted, by not receiving the ungodly into his body. And by that very fasting he hungered, when, finding no fruit on that tree, he cursed it, and it withered[^10]. What is it that he says: *I clothed myself in sackcloth?* As though, with his power hidden within the weakness of the flesh, I set that same mortal flesh before the eyes of my persecutors. For in that flesh, which he had taken up from the old age of our mortality without any sin of his own, he bore our sins[^11]. And sackcloth surely pertains to sins, on account of the haircloth of the goats' hair that will be set on the left[^12], unless they first pass over into the lamb. In that form of a servant, then, sinning in nothing, and therefore owing nothing to death, he nonetheless rendered for us what he did not owe, that he might free us from our debt. In that sackcloth he bore our price. For from "sack" (saccus) the diminutive "little purse" (saccellus) is also derived. But our great price he bore in the sackcloth with which he clothed himself, setting it before his persecutors, by whose ungodliness he humbled his soul in fasting. Finally, when he hung on the cross, the sackcloth was torn open by the lance, and the price of the whole world flowed out[^13]. Your Lord, then, suffered for you before you were a Christian, that he might buy you and you might become a Christian.

**6.** But what does Scripture say? *As he laid down his soul for us, so we ought to lay down our soul for the brothers*[^14]. If we cannot yet suffer for the Gentiles, we can at least fast for the Gentiles. How far you are from the imitation of your Lord! and when you have fasted, how far you still are from it! And it is good to draw near. But how is it that you shrink from that very perfection, whose step you fear, a step so near, so lowly! That step is near, on the ground — I do not know if it should even be called a step — which you are unwilling to ascend. For what great thing is it to fast at this season, to dine late on so short a day? It is no great thing, it is not laborious at all. The care of a single business often forces one to do what you are unwilling to render to God out of devotion. Because the Church of God wills it, you do not want to fast. If you were playing at dice, you would fast, and you would fast so as not to get up conquered. To win, fearing lest you should be beaten of money by a man, you would fast, and you do not fast, not fearing lest in your heart you should be beaten by the devil. For nothing is easier than fasting on such short days. But on the Kalends of January you do not want to fast. Prove yourselves, that you may rejoice over yourselves, that we may rejoice over you. How small a temptation! Yet it proves the Christian breast.

**7.** You hear the sermon now: you are held in suspense, you are quickened by the word of God, and the exhortation itself grows warm in the heart. We kindle something in your minds: we see it, we recognize it; but let that flame take hold of the timber. For from this place, a little while after the sermon is finished, you are going out to the storms and to the cold of the world. You know that this world seems to seethe in a perverse solemnity: the winds are cold, where it is to be feared lest Christian hearts freeze. And so, brothers, as I said, let the divine word take hold of the strength of your heart. And if it takes hold, those storms which you will suffer outside a little while hence will rather kindle a greater flame — they will not put it out — if it is no small spark that can be extinguished. If your heart now burns like tow, when you shout, when you love, when you rejoice in the word of God, you are going out a little while hence, and it will be extinguished by the breath of one mouth, when it is said to a man: "Are you really going to fast today?" It will be extinguished at once, because it had begun to burn like tow. But if it so burns as coals of fire, however many contrary blasts of perverse persuasion arise, they will increase the fire and more vehemently kindle it. But those burn like coals who have come back to life. For coals, when they burn, as it were come back to life from the dead. Many have even this word in custom; when they send to kindle a fire, they say, "Bring live ones," meaning those that are burning. By which surely they signify the extinguished ones to be, as it were, dead. If, then, you have come back to life by the fervor of the love of Christ, let his desire so burn in your hearts that no blast of those dissuading may extinguish it. For I know what you are going to suffer when you go out from here, and thanks be to God that you have assembled. For that you assemble more frequently on these days does not displease us, but rather even pleases us. For you have found the way, because those whose manners are unlike yours hasten to other things and occupy themselves in various empty pleasures, and so they grant you leisure and free time, that there may be done in you what was said: *Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the Gentiles*. Therefore you are now gathered; and even if you go out and are mingled with them in bodily association, yet, if you do not consent with them in evil and trifling things, you will remain gathered from among the Gentiles, wherever you may be in body. And would that you might suffer wicked dissuaders only in the streets, and not perhaps even in your homes! The father wants to fast, the son does not; or the son wants to, the father does not; or the husband wants to, the woman does not; or she wants to and he does not. He, then, who does not want to, and for this reason does not want to because he thinks this day a festival, is a contrary blast: but let that other so burn that not only can he himself not be extinguished, but the other can be kindled by him.

**8.** My brothers, if you understand well what you hear, I do not doubt that you grieve for those who are still held by that madness. You grieve for them, because you too perhaps were once held by madness of this kind; and now your heart is healthy, which you have compared to their madness, and, pitying them, you grieve, but you do not grieve with despair. For if it could be brought about in you that what you loved yesterday you do not love today, it can be brought about in him too. But if it can be brought about in him too, as long as he is such and you grieve for him, pray for him. And that your prayer may be heard, fast for him and give alms, and in this wear out the day for him whom you love — let him, not loving himself, wear himself out in contrary things: for *he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^15] — that at some time he too may hate iniquity, loving his own soul, and within may grieve with you for others, and with you pray and fast for them. For, brothers, although the other days also hold them in trifles, yet especially on their solemnities a greater license of trifles rouses them to a greater love of the world and to the ruin of pleasures. For when one does it in his house, he is less mad amid colder neighborhoods; but when all do it, they kindle one another. But I have already told you: their ardor is your cold. All love is ardor. But it matters what is loved. When the heart burns for the world, it is cold toward God. Therefore let it grow cold toward the world, and burn for God. When, then, they do these things more ardently and more frequently, just as they rouse themselves to wickedness against themselves, so let them rouse you to mercy on their behalf. They are indeed always to be grieved for, as long as they are pagans, as long as they pursue vanities, as long as they are given over to demons. As long as they wish to adore what they have made, forgetting by whom they were made, they are always to be lamented. But through their solemnities they rouse a new grief. When you see them flowing apart through various trifles, through the pleasures of luxury, through immoderate drunkenness, through dice and manifold madnesses, let their new commotion make for you a new grief, if you are a Christian, if you have mercy; because, even when you were not what you are, the Church had mercy on you, which was then still among a few. But now that she is grown and copiously and widely spread abroad in the name of Christ, are not those rather to be grieved for who are still heavy of heart and love vanity and seek a lie[^16]? To those, surely, who, now separated from the human race, by I know not what most stubborn obstinacy remain a few, a greater grief is owed, because a greater disease makes them vanish away and not be healed by the authority of so great a medicine.

**9.** And would that we lamented only pagans! We would soon lament almost no one. Let Christians not go to the theaters: the pagans flee from there, if not for love of the truth, at least for shame at their fewness. A greater sacrifice of prayers, surely, must be offered by us, in a sharper grief of heart, that those who are called Christians may merit to be corrected. It is not, then, shameless, brothers — rather it is a very opportune fast — when it is made for those who labor because they wish to, who, if they did not wish to, would not labor. For they are pressed by their own sins and congratulate themselves on their most filthy pleasures, and do not hear the voice of him who says: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you*[^17]. They are unwilling to be refreshed in the sweetness of Christ, and think themselves refreshed in the satiety of lust. That is not refreshment, but ruin. Nor should you celebrate the very festal days of the Christians as drunkards do, although fasts ought to be relaxed on account of the sacrament of joy. Yet it is one thing to signify joy by the relaxation of a fast, another to repel righteousness by a burdened heart. The Lord says, the Truth says: *Let not your hearts be weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness*[^18]. It is foolish and irreligious to wish to please the martyrs by that very thing which they, had they not despised it, would never have come to the glory of a martyr.

**10.** But perhaps you will not suffer pagans of this kind. For some of the pagans themselves despise those given over to the wickedness of pleasures and drunkenness, and say: "Just as you have bad Christians, so we have bad pagans. For the good pagans, see what they are." Then they name, as it were, the wise men and philosophers of the world, as endowed with surpassing learning. But let them not frighten you. Greatness is one thing, swelling is another. For what swells also appears, as it were, great, but it is not sound. Hear the Apostle saying: *Beware lest anyone lead you astray through philosophy and empty seduction, according to the tradition of men, according to the elements of this world and not according to Christ; for in him dwells all the fullness of divinity bodily, and you are filled in him, who is the head of all principality and power*[^19]. For, that they may interpret their idols learnedly and as it were prudently, they flee to the *elements of the world*. You reprove someone for worshiping idols, and it is manifest that he adores an idol: he is convicted by the thing itself, and there he has his affection — namely, in that idol — there he places his being heard; but a, as it were, more skilled and more learned pagan, what does he say to you? "Unskilled pagans do this, that they adore an idol as an idol, just as your people do too who adore the columns in the church."

**11.** Therefore I say, brothers: do not do that by which the pagans may insult us; so enter the church that you give no occasion to the pagans why they should be unwilling to enter the church. For the church is entered for the sake of the brotherly assembly. For the temple of your prayer is your heart. Cleanse the place where you pray, and you will be heard. For if you cleanse the visible place where you make your prayer, not on account of the eyes of God — for he sees all things just as he created all things — but lest your own eyes be offended and turn away your attention, how much more ought you to cleanse the place where, and that with which, you call upon God? Have your inner chamber, into which you may enter and shut the door, as the Lord himself says[^20], that is, the sense of your body, barred against bodily enticements; but the brotherly assembly makes for ardor in praying and praising God. As, if some weight were being moved, and what is called a work-chant were sung, would not the very multitude of those singing together and working together, harmonious in one voice and most concordant in one effort, rouse even your feeble strength, so that you too would want to hold the rope and rejoice in the fellowship of that work? The gatherings of the brothers, then, sharpen love. And every man prays within, when he prays well, as the Lord answered the Samaritan woman: *The hour will come*, he says, *and now is, when you will adore the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem*[^21]. And a little after: *The hour will come*, he says, *and now is, when the true worshipers will adore the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father seeks such to adore him. God is a spirit, and those who adore him must adore him in spirit and in truth*[^22]. If, then, God is to be entreated *in spirit*, let a dwelling be made: the dweller will be present. *We come to him*, he says, I and my Father, *and we will make our dwelling with him*[^23]. Come, then, to the church, that the fervor of praying may be roused, that the devotion of a peaceful assembly may make the merit of being heard before God; not because God dwells in an earthly place, where he may hear you. Do not think that within walls of this kind God hears only: in prison he heard the martyrs. *What house*, he says, *will you build for me, or what is the place of my rest? Has not my hand made these things?*[^24] And yet he said that he has a place in the pious heart of a man. For he says: *Upon whom does my Spirit rest? Upon the humble and the quiet and him who trembles at my words*[^25].

**12.** Why have we said this? Lest, when we insult the pagans, we do that by which the pagans may insult us. Come to the places of the martyrs in such a way that out of the places there may be a pious commemoration in your hearts, and out of the honor of the martyrs there may arise an affection toward God, who did not desert the martyrs in tribulation, who helped them as they fought, who crowned them as they conquered. So you make yourselves worthy ones for whom the martyrs may pray. Otherwise a good servant is greatly indignant if, the Lord being despised, he himself should be adored — a good servant, who was even made a son out of a servant[^26]. For one thing is a servant still, who is also to be a son out of a servant, and another a son already. One thing a servant by fear, another a son by love. A great house has all: hired men and servants and sons. The hired men are those who seek worldly advantages in the Church, of whom the Apostle says that they announce the Gospel not chastely[^27], and yet he permits it, saying: *Whether in pretense or in truth, let Christ be announced*[^28]. But the servants are those who out of fear do what is commanded by the Lord. They belong to the house indeed, and are more inward in the great house than the hired men; out of these servants sons are made, when they have begun to serve out of love. So, then, as I had begun to say, the great house has all. What do we think the martyrs are, my brothers? Far be it that we should count them among the hired men, but neither among those who are not yet sons! For they loved Christ, and for love of him despised not only all the pleasures of the world, but also all torments, made drunk with the cup of his Spirit, of which it is said: *Your cup that makes drunk, how excellent it is!*[^29] A good servant, then, as I said, who is now even called a son, does not want himself to be adored, but his Lord. Attend, brothers, and recall what you frequent daily, what the truth teaches in the Church. The faithful know in what order the martyrs are commemorated in the sacraments, when our vows and prayers are directed to God; the faithful know it, and let the catechumens hasten to know it. For who repels them? To whom is what is closed for one who defers not open to one who wills?

**13.** But let us attend, brothers, to what the good servants did while still set in this body, when they were honored by men and began to be worshiped as gods of the nations: Paul and Barnabas, because they worked miracles in Christ, because they had exceeded the human measure, the pagans according to their custom called Barnabas Jupiter, but Paul Mercury, because he was readier in speaking, and they had begun to offer victims to them; which honor they so shuddered at that they tore their garments and taught them, as much as they could, who was the one to be adored, by whose power they did these things[^30]. Attend to these things, brothers, that you may be strong and fortified against even the more elegant pagans. Why do I say more elegant, who are rather in greater peril? For the more learned they seem to themselves, the more unteachable they become. For they blush to learn, lest they confess that they are ignorant, and there is not in them that pious humility which alone the humble God came to teach. Or perhaps someone will say: "Paul and Barnabas refused the honor of the sacrifices that was offered to them for this reason, that those made the one Jupiter, the other Mercury, whom the apostles, by the Christian religion, detested as false gods, and so they were indignant at being compared to demons or idols, not because they wished God to be adored rather than themselves." They would, then, have said this to those who wished to sacrifice: "You do us an injury, you compare us to demons, than whom we are better"; rather, they tore their garments, grieving, and humbly admonished that these things ought to be done neither to them nor to anyone, but that men ought to be turned from these vanities to the one God, saying: *Men, what are you doing? We are men like you, announcing to you that you should return from these vanities to him who made heaven and earth and sea and all that is in them*[^31]. Saying these and such things, they scarcely persuaded them not to sacrifice to them. But that you may more fully show the ungodly that they refused these sacrifices not because they thought an injury was done them, but that God alone might be honored, to whose worship they labored most that all might be converted, attend to Peter: when men marveled at his deed — rather, not his, but God's — when that lame man of forty years, who sat at the Beautiful Gate, rose at the word of God and walked, he did not want glory to be given to himself, but to Christ, and said: *Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this, or why do you gaze at us, as though by our own power or authority we had made this man walk? The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified his Son, whom you handed over to judgment*[^32].

**14.** But if it is too little that men did this, that they wished glory to be given not to themselves but to God, look at the angels. In the Apocalypse a holy angel — for there are also perverse angels, for whom and for the devil their prince the eternal fire is prepared — an angel, then, not fallen by pride, but remaining in holy humility under God, not raising himself up in elation against God nor saying: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^33], but remaining in the south, where Christ feeds and where he lies down... For it is written: *Where you feed, where you lie down at noon*[^34]. For to the southern region the northern is contrary: therefore it signifies the cold and the dark. But the south signifies the enlightened and the fervent. Those, then, who are good, as it were at noon, are fervent and shine; but those who are evil, as it were in the north, cold, are darkened by a dark gloom. With those at noon God feeds and lies down, whence it is said: *Where you feed, where you lie down at noon*; not with those of whose party the devil says: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*. For he could be adored only by such as these, that they might think him a god. That angel, then, remaining in the south, fervent with holiness and bright with wisdom, was showing certain things in the Apocalypse to the apostle John. But he, disturbed, fell at the feet of the angel[^35].

**15.** See, brothers, before all things, to which angels humble men are like, and to which the proud are like. For those who make schisms and heresies wish their own name to be named and the name of Christ to be darkened, and they too choose a seat in the north. For men would not follow them, the Church deserted, as though they were Christ, unless they were darkened in understanding and estranged from the fervor of love[^36]. But to which are those like who, holding to humility, choose *to be cast away in the house of the Lord rather than to dwell in the tents of sinners*[^37]? What did Simon want, but to be praised in miracles, to be lifted up in pride? For that very thing compelled him to think the gift of the Holy Spirit something to be bought with money[^38]. Contrary to this pride, the Apostle, in humility, by remaining in the south, fervent in spirit[^39], shining with prudence, says: *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the growth*[^40]. And again: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^41] How he refuses to be worshiped in place of Christ, how zealous he is for the bridegroom, and is unwilling to display himself as a bridegroom to a fornicating soul! Such, then, were those holy ones, according to their measure — for they will be perfect as the angels after the resurrection of the body — but yet, according to their measure, just as the angels also, they refused the honor that men paid them as though it were properly theirs, while they wished him to be worshiped and all hearts to be turned to him, in whom they too gloried[^42], truly good and faithful servants, sons, such also the holy angels. For even though still men set in the flesh and placed in such great temptations, they refused the honor that was conferred on them toward pride, that the one might be honored in all who alone is honored without peril to the one honoring. For that he might be honored, where do they place themselves? how do they cast themselves down? Does it not seem great to plant and to water? But *neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters*. He says he is not anything toward the salvation of those whom he desired to build up in Christ. If, then, men still living in the flesh do these things, how much more the martyrs and the angels! For the more holily they live, the more they love the glory of God and have their hope in him alone[^43]. For we too shall be as the angels, but after the resurrection. So the Lord said: *They will be equal to the angels of God*[^44].

**16.** Return, then, with me to what I had begun to relate, and be instructed wholesomely as to why you give your leisure to God. That angel, then, was showing in the Apocalypse wonderful and mystical visions to the apostle John, the servant of Christ, the son of mother Church and made among the sons of God. But he, disturbed in a certain vision, fell, as I had begun to say, at the feet of the angel. But the angel did not accept the honor offered to him by the man, which was owed to God, and said to him: "Rise, what are you doing? *Adore God. For I too am your fellow servant, and of your brothers*"[^45]. Why have we said this — contemplate the sum. We had begun to deal with the pagans as though more learned — for those are less learned about whom these are unwilling to be measured — since they say to us: "You too have adorers of columns and sometimes even of pictures." And would that we did not, and may the Lord grant that we do not! But yet this the Church does not teach you. For which of their priests ever ascended, and from a higher place commanded the people not to adore idols, as we in Christ publicly preach that neither columns nor stones of buildings in holy places nor even pictures should be adored? Rather, their very priests, turned toward the idols, offered victims for the peoples, and still desire to offer them.

**17.** "We," they say, "do not worship images, but what is signified through the image." I ask what the images signify, I ask what the image of the sun signifies: surely nothing other than the sun? For the interpretations of other images perhaps have hidden meanings. Meanwhile let us pass these by and set them aside to be taken up a little later. The image of the sun certainly signifies nothing but the sun, and that of the moon the moon, and that of Tellus the earth. If, then, they do not adore that which they see in the image, but that which the image signifies, why, having before their eyes those very most well-known things which are signified by those images, do they adore the images for them? For if what is signified were not seen, the sign might rightly be adored in place of the thing signified. But since they see the sun, which is signified by the image of the sun, why do they put their back to him who is signified, and their face to the sign by which he is signified? For if they did not make images of the things that are seen, and made images only of those that are not seen, they could deceive the less prudent and say: "Behold, we worship the sun. But because we see him, we have set up no image for him. Likewise the moon and the stars, as we see them, so we worship; we have made or have no images of them. For it is foolish to set a visible sign in a closed temple of that thing which can be seen and adored in the open sky. But when we worship the mind, or genius, or soul, or virtue, or righteousness — these now are invisible — we set up visible images for them, that, when these are seen and adored, we may think reverently of those invisible things." But now they are most plainly convicted of being given over to the images themselves, not to the things signified through the images, since they have made images even of visible things set before the eyes of all. The sun has an image, which, if they adored it in the sky, they would be justly reproved with the truest piety and religion. Furthermore, they have come to such madness that they put their back to the sun and their face to its image. How will so perverse a one hear you, when you leave him and turn to his false and most deceptive image made by the hand of a man? As if you should come to the house of a head of household to ask something, and he stands in his courtyard, but you turn your back to him and your face to a tablet where he is painted, and as if you pour out the prayers of your desire not only not to the man but to the picture, indeed even in the presence of the man himself, whose image that picture is, would he not order you, mocked or counted as mad, to be driven from his house? Nothing would be more like this folly than what these men do, even if the sun were to be adored. But because these things are not to be adored, we will say so in the name of the Lord, if he gives space and opportunity. But now we say these things, as to how these men are caught in their works. You made a temple of the moon, you set up an image for her; the moon herself rises daily, scarcely for two or three days in a month can she not be seen: adore her, if she is to be adored, if you have now come to this disgrace, that you wish to adore the visible creature as God, than which the invisible creature is surely superior, which yet not even itself, but the Creator alone, is to be adored as God. But behold, before your eyes are the very things you worship, whether the sun or the moon or the stars or the earth: why do you seek their images, when they themselves, present, are to be adored by you, unless because men are given over to the things they have made with their hands, forgetting him by whose hands they were made?

**18.** But now let us see how they interpret the images. "I do not," he says, "call the statue of Neptune Neptune, but Neptune is some other thing that is signified through this image." What is Neptune? "The sea," he says. This is the whole meaning, that Neptune is the sea. But the Christian neither worships that statue which you call Neptune, nor the sea which you interpret as Neptune. And what does he worship? Namely him *who made heaven and earth and the sea and all that is in them*[^46]. But what do you say? "That statue signifies the sea." And this is said as it were in part, in secret, in the ear, that it may be thought a great mystery, that that statue signifies the sea, so that, namely, if anyone adores that statue, he may seem to himself to adore the sea. Only the inlanders, then, who do not see the sea itself, ought to have images of Neptune, so that those who do not have the thing might have a sign in place of the thing. But now they have made a shrine of Neptune right beside the sea, and, putting their backs to the sea, they adore Neptune. Nor do the very waves crashing at their backs turn their adorer toward themselves; and, what is more foolish, the one who prays wants the sea to be silent and the statue to hear, because, of course, the sea has its hearing not in its salt waves, but in the foreign bronze ears.

**19.** So too the image of Tellus. "It is not," he says, "this that you see that is worshiped, but this signifies the earth." This, even if he did not say it, would be apparent. In vain he wishes to bring forth as though from a mystery what he proclaims in the name. For Tellus is by another word the earth. Yet they call the same one mother earth, for whom they have made a temple and an image. Behold, I understand, as in the case of the sun, as in the case of the moon: there is no need for him to expound it; for these images are called by the same name as the very things of which they are images, which we surely see and which appear most well-known. Or is it perhaps that what they add, "and mother," this must be learned? "She is," they say, "the mother of all that are born." And let her be the mother of all that are born: am I therefore to adore the earth, and not him who said: *Let the earth bring forth*[^47] whatever it brought forth? For without his word it would not only be barren, but would not exist at all. What, then, does this man, as it were an expounder of mysteries, wishing to turn us from the Creator and to turn us to the creature, expound to me as something great, that I should worship the earth? Clearly this is rightly said to farmers, that, if they do not wish to suffer hunger, they should cultivate the earth, not by adoring, but by plowing.

**20.** So I ask about the image of Juno: now here we need a mystical interpreter. For Juno does not, like mother earth, show by her name what she signifies, or like the image of the sun or moon. The great secret of Juno, then — what secret, I ask you? "This," he says, "few know." Perhaps the many heard from those few and told it. Perhaps their own writings betray to those whom they do not wish what they worship, although our writings too betray to them what we worship, but we do not fear. Our books are publicly offered for sale: the light does not blush. Let them buy, read, believe; or buy, read, mock. That Scripture knows how to hold guilty those who read and do not believe. The book is carried about for sale, but he who is preached in the book is not for sale. Or is he too for sale, because he set himself to be bought by the nations at whatever price each one has? Zacchaeus bought him with half his patrimony, the widow bought him with two mites, the poor host bought him by holding out a cup of cold water[^48]; and he is possessed by all his buyers: to no one is he cramped, all he enlarges. Such is the one who is preached in the book. Buy you the book and read; we do not blush. You hide the books of your rites in secret caves: so great is the curiosity of men that it both searches out and betrays.

**21.** What, then, is Juno? "Juno," they say, "is the air." A while ago they were inviting us to worship the sea in Neptune, to worship the earth in the image of Tellus; now they invite us to worship the air. These are the elements of which this world consists. So, warning of this beforehand, the apostle Paul in his epistle says: *Beware lest anyone deceive you through philosophy and empty seduction, according to the elements of this world*[^49]. For he was touching upon those very ones who expound idols as if more prudently. Therefore, when he said, *through philosophy*, in the same place he says, *according to the elements of this world*. He admonished that they are to be guarded against, not as common worshipers of images, but as more learned interpreters of signs. "Therefore," they say, "Juno is the air." Let them call by whatever name a thing known to all: I will not worship the air, for it cannot even be cultivated as the earth is cultivated by farmers. For in the earth the farmer works, as I said above, not by adoring but by laboring, that he may find fruit. But this air can neither be cultivated thus nor is it to be adored. Praise the work, adore the maker. "That you may know," they say, "that the air is Juno, the Greeks call her so: she is called Hera in Greek; and aer, often repeated, sounds like Hera." But we, who say that the one Creator must be served in religion, do not adore Juno for this reason, that she is an image; we do not adore her for this reason, that she is a creature.

**22.** But this I ask of these men: if I worship God alone, is the air angry? for if I worship the air, God is angry; although he is always tranquil and administers and rules all that he created without any disturbance, yet in some unutterable way God is angry. He who, when he wishes himself alone to be worshiped, alone wishes this not out of pride: for he alone is worthy to be worshiped, and he wishes to be worshiped not for this, that he himself may be the more exalted, but that man may be the better. For he does not need worshipers, but you need the one worshiped. *I said*, says the prophet, *to the Lord: You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^50]. If, then, he alone, without pride, requires that he be worshiped, whoever else requires it and claims it for himself privately, that he may, as it were, be worshiped in his own right, who is not satisfied to be worshiped in him who created him, requires it out of pride. And the pride of the devil is to be avoided, that being humble we may come to the height of God. For the devil alone wishes to be worshiped in place of God, because he himself said: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^51].

**23.** For what are they going to say of the sign of Vulcan, for example, that we may recall all the elements — for they say there are four: earth, water, air, fire; and of heaven we have already spoken, when we spoke of the sun and moon? "Vulcan," they say, "is fire," not the ethereal fire, where the sun and moon and the other stars are, but this fire which is on earth, fitted to the uses of men. "For," they also say, "Vulcan is therefore painted lame, because the motion of this fire flickers as though limping." What then? Since a man has fire in his power, who kindles it and extinguishes it when he wishes, and uses it as he wishes, is the power of men over a god so great, and does he who blows on a lamp extinguish a god? I do not know whether it is worse to worship the image itself by itself, than such an interpretation of it. But what is it to us? For perhaps they may interpret even invisible things: for these are visible. Let us come quickly to the security of our faith, that we may not be corrupted by them at all.

**24.** "When I worship Mercury," he says, "I worship genius; genius is not seen, it is something invisible." We too plainly concede that genius is something invisible, and that it is so invisible and is such a thing that it is better than the sky, than the earth, than the sea and all these things that are seen; for an invisible substance, since it is a kind of life, is better than every visible substance, because everything visible is a body. And genius is a great thing: yet if you consider that very genius which they say they worship, what has it done? Do not many err with a great genius? And perhaps those most of all who think genius is to be worshiped in the image of Mercury. If, then, the human genius can err without God as its guide, when the human genius is sound, it does not want to be adored in place of God, but wants him to be adored by whom it wishes to be enlightened. For unless the human genius is enlightened by the enlightening God, it is dark in its errors. But I ask you, what do you worship in Mercury? "Genius," you said. Genius is a kind of middle thing, because genius either, turned away from the Creator, is darkened and becomes foolish, or, turned toward the Creator, is enlightened and becomes wise. But you said something middle, when you said you worship genius. "Yes," he says, "middle, for the very name of Mercury sounds, as it were, running in the middle." For Mercury is said to have been named as, it were, running in the middle. If, then, he is worshiped for this reason, that he is something middle, why is not he rather worshiped who calls back to himself the middle thing which you call genius, that, suspended from lower things and turned away, he may make it cleave to himself, such as are the geniuses of the saints, the geniuses of the martyrs, the geniuses of the angels? For if the genius be such as that of this angel whom I recalled, enlightened by God, it repels from itself the adoration of a man and admonishes its adorer rather to adore God. *Adore God*, he says, *for I am your fellow servant and of your brothers*[^52]. Ought you, then, to worship a middle genius, for which a better genius would reprove you? For if you worship a middle genius, the enlightened genius will reprove you. Why? Because the enlightened genius does not want genius to be worshiped, but the enlightener of geniuses. For by that very enlightenment it is kind and benevolent, and wishes all geniuses to be turned to him by whom it knows itself also to be enlightened. Or does perhaps a foolish genius want itself to be worshiped? Plainly this is true. Beware, then, lest, when you wish to worship Mercury as genius, you worship the devil, who has a foolish genius. For the devil too, when he said: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^53], was unwilling to worship God and wished to be worshiped in place of God, rising up against God. But because he departed, as it were, from the south to the north, his genius was made dark, and because dark, also proud, and because proud, therefore by you, deceived, he wishes Mercury to be worshiped, that, while you worship Mercury, you may worship the devil.

**25.** Let your Holiness attend, and from men see what we say. Direct your mind and understand. Every creature is either corporeal or spiritual. The Creator alone is spiritual; but the creature, as we said, is corporeal or spiritual. Attend, that you may have, according to our strength, as much as the Lord has helped, against the pagans, not just any sort, but those who think themselves more learned and interpreters of images, and are puffed up and puff up others, and, empty, mock the very name of truth. Every creature, as I said, is either corporeal or spiritual. Corporeal, such as the earth and all that is begotten in it, such as the sea and all that swims or creeps in it, such as the air and all that flies in it, such as the heaven and all that gleams in it. All this creature is corporeal. But the spiritual is better than the corporeal. What is the spiritual creature? That which is perceived not by the senses of the body, but by the understanding of the mind, such as are all souls, whether irrational as in beasts, or rational as in men, or failing as in the ungodly, or advancing as in the converted, or perfect as in the angels and archangels, thrones, dominations, principalities, powers of the higher dwelling. Since, then, a creature is either corporeal or spiritual, and no third can be found, the highest bodies, than which there are none better, are the heavenly. Now what is beyond — not by spans of place, but by force of nature — is not a body, but spirit: a kind of life, but a changeable life, because it is a creature; the true unchangeable life is the Creator. Changeable life, then, that is, the spiritual creature, is also formable and able to be enlightened, that is, it can be formed and enlightened; but the unchangeable life, that is, the very substance of the Creator, is the maker and the enlightener. The creature is formed and enlightened, but the Creator forms and enlightens.

**26.** Attend and understand. Every life, then, that can be enlightened and come to wisdom, when it is of good will, loves the God who enlightens it, and, turned toward him, advances, and, clinging to him, is formed unto the integrity and perfection of wisdom, and, in its own kind, unto the fullness of blessedness: the rational and intellectual life, namely, such as is that of the angels, such as is that of men. If, then, such a life, when it is praiseworthy, loves its enlightener, every such life that by a culpable will deserts the life that enlightens it is darkened and becomes proud, such as is the life either of an unrighteous angel or of an unrighteous man: for it does not want to cleave to God, but wants by a certain private haughtiness to be held in place of God. Into such a proud and unrighteous life having fallen and turned, the archangel and his angels was made the devil and those demons, and they received a certain space of this murky air, where they might rule over all unrighteous men. And therefore the Apostle says: *According to the prince of the power of this air, who now works in the children of unbelief*[^54]. And again, exhorting us to endure the persecutions of this world: *For we have not*, he says, *a wrestling against flesh and blood*, that is, against men, *but against the principalities*, he says, *and powers and rulers of this darkness, against the spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places*[^55]. For because he said that *the prince of the power of this air* works *in the children of unbelief*, lest they fix their attention on men and think they must struggle with them, through whom they suffered persecutions, and not behold in mind that enemy whom they ought to conquer in Christ: *You have not*, he says, *a wrestling against flesh and blood, but against the principalities and powers and rulers of this darkness, and against the spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places*. And indeed, lest, calling them *rulers of the world*, he should be thought to call them rulers of this whole fabric which God created, he showed of what world he spoke, by adding and saying: *of this darkness*. And what the darkness is you can recognize. For that the unbelieving are signified by the name of darkness the Apostle himself says, when he was speaking to the faithful: *For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^56]. So too what he said, *in the heavenly places*, must be understood as said not of those highest seats of the holy angels, where the luminaries and stars are also set, but because this air too is called heaven, whence also the birds are called birds of heaven.

**27.** Let your Holiness, then, attend, that the mercy of the Lord may enlighten you. For we sound in the ears; he works within, as we believe, as we hope, of the mercy of him to whom we sing praises. So, as I had begun to say, a soul turned away from God, as from the light of truth and as from the south, comes to be in the north. But the kingdom of the north is the kingdom of the devil who says: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*[^57]; there the hearts of men grow cold, and, made cold away from that fire of divine wisdom, they cannot savor spiritual things. And so they begin to think only of bodies, so that they seek even divinity itself in bodies, that is, in the sea, in the earth, in the air, and most of all in the heavenly bodies, as in the moon or the sun or the stars. For because the chief among the senses of the body is the sense of the eyes, whatever gleams to the eyes, if it also surpasses in place in the world, is thought great. But if anyone tells them that there is something great *which neither eye has seen nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^58], they say there is nothing that cannot be seen. Such hearts, then, have grown cold. If they have grown cold, they are in the north; if they are in the north, he rules over them who said: *I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like the Most High*. But those who in any way have raised up their hearts and, as much as they could, have sharpened them by zeal, that they might see something that cannot be seen by the eyes of the body, transcending — which was easy — the earth they trod and all that is in the earth, transcending also the sea and whatever swims or creeps in it, transcending the air too and all the birds — for this too is a corporeal element — transcending the whole ethereal heaven with all the luminaries that shine there or whatever others perhaps lie hidden, they saw that there is something that is not seen, such as is the mind, such as is genius, such as is reason; they saw also that this too is changed, and they sought something unchangeable, they passed by, in thought and in the keen edge of the mind, even the spiritual creature, and understood the Creator Spirit, and, as though beaten back by the unwonted brightness of wisdom, they returned, as if to rest in the darkness of their flesh, and some of them felt that there was need of a purgation of their soul, which, cleansed from all fleshly desires, might be made fit to obtain that whose ineffable light had grazed them.

**28.** Attend, my brothers, and mark it with grief. They saw that there was need of a purgation, that that light which cannot be grasped by the weak gaze of the mind might, when that gaze was healed and formed and strengthened, be grasped. They saw that there was need of medicine, and, as they sought that very medicine, the devil at once interposed himself, because they sought proudly, boasting, as it were, in their own teachings, especially because they could, those who could, by the sharpness of genius and the keen edge of the mind, having transcended all creatures both corporeal and spiritual, reach so far as to know that there is something both spiritual and unchangeable, and that from it are all these things that subsist either spiritually or corporeally; but that that Creator is not moved by places, not moved by times, whereas bodies are moved by places and times; but that the substance of the soul, that is, the spiritual creature, is indeed not moved by places, but yet is moved by times through the changeableness of its various affections, and therefore that it is something middle, because the highest God is moved neither by places nor by times, while the substance of bodies — that is, the lowest creature — is moved both by places and by times. Whence the soul is a middle thing, because it is not moved by places, as neither is God, but is moved by times, as bodies are. When, then, they saw and sought purgation, that proud devil forestalled them as they sought proudly and boasted proudly, and set himself before them as a mediator, through whom it might seem to them that their souls could be purged. He, persuading by certain signs of his pride that he was necessary, that it might seem to men that the soul which wishes to come to God can be purged through magic arts, instituted sacrilegious rites in the temples, which promise purgation to the sacrilegious. For many of the images, as Scripture says, were instituted out of the honors of men who were held great, whether absent or dead. All of which, in the name of Christ, now abolished by public laws, have ceased to be done publicly, since some of them were, as it were, public magic. But just as before there was private magic, so now these things are done secretly, after they were forbidden to be done publicly.

**29.** Since, then, that proud and unrighteous enemy of souls set himself before them as a mediator, hear the Apostle teaching why he set himself before them, namely, because their pride gave him a place. Whence he admonishes, saying: *Do not give place to the devil*[^59]. But how we may discover their pride, let the same Apostle say. And first let us attend to the fact that some even of them had come to the knowledge of God, whom yet he wished to be saved through Christ. For they had come to the knowledge of God, but they had not come to salvation. For it is one thing to come to the knowledge of God, another to come to salvation, where that knowledge itself also becomes full, when the knower cleaves to the known. When the Apostle was speaking among the Athenians as to pagan men, because they boasted of the height of their learning above the other nations, and among them there was, as it were, the highest philosophy — for there indeed arose the learned and wise of this world — when, then, the Apostle was speaking there, he did not bring forth to them testimonies from the prophets, but brought forth from their own, nor was he silent that they were theirs, not ours, because, even if something good is found there, many evil things are found there, not as our prophets, whatever they have in their Scriptures is good. So among the Athenians, when the Apostle spoke of God: *In him*, he says, *we live and move and are, as also some of your own have said*[^60].

**30.** But what those "some" were, and why they were disapproved, expounding in another place: *The wrath of God*, he says, *is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness*[^61]. What do we understand by *against ungodliness*, except both that of the Jews and that of the Gentiles? But lest it be said: "Why is *the wrath of God revealed against* the ungodliness of the Gentiles, since you said *against all*? For did the Gentiles receive the Law and become transgressors? Rightly is *the wrath of God revealed* against the Jews, to whom the Law was given and who refused to observe it; but to the Gentiles it was not given," behold, brothers, and understand how the same Apostle shows all to be guilty, and all to be in need of salvation and of the mercy of God. *For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men, of those who hold the truth in iniquity*[^62]. Understand, brothers, see how he did not say, "They do not have the truth," but, *They hold the truth*, he says, *in iniquity*. And as though you should ask, saying: "How can those who did not receive the Law have the truth?", *because what is known of God*, he says, *is manifest in them*[^63]. How could *what is known of God be manifest in those* who did not receive the Law? He goes on and says: *For his invisible things, from the foundation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made; his eternal power also and divinity*[^64], surely we are to supply, "is understood and seen." Through what is it seen? *Through the things that are made*.

**31.** For why should a man attend to the works, and not seek the maker? You attend to the earth bearing fruit, you attend to the sea full of its animals, you attend to the air full of birds, you attend to the heaven gleaming with stars, you recognize the changes of the seasons, you consider the fourfold year, the leaves falling from the trees and returning, the numbers given to their seeds, that there are measures for each thing, their weights[^65], that all things are administered by their orders, that the higher heaven has all its peace, the lower earth has the beauty proper to its own kind of things yielding and succeeding: beholding all these things, you see that all is now made alive by the spiritual creature, and you do not seek the maker of so great a work? But you say to me: "These things I see, him I do not see." Because he made for you one thing by which to see these things, another by which he himself is seen — to see these things he gave the eyes of the body, to see himself he gave the mind — you are not, then, permitted vainly to say: "I do not see him"; look at these things according to the understanding, and you will see the one who works. For neither do you see the soul of a man; for the soul of a man is not seen, but from the management of the body, when we behold him walking, working, speaking, moving all his members according to the proper functions of each, we say: "He lives, there is in him what we do not see," and we understand it through these things which we see. As, then, from the movements and management of the body you understand the soul, which you do not see, so from the management of the whole world, and from the governing of souls, understand the Creator.

**32.** But it is too little to understand; for they too understood, and see what the Apostle says, consider those very words that I had begun to say from the outset. *The wrath of God*, he says, *is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men, who hold the truth in iniquity, because what is known of God is manifest in them: for God manifested it to them*[^66]. And as though you should ask whence he manifested it, *his invisible things*, he says, *from the foundation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made; his eternal power also and divinity* — that is, is understood and seen — *so that they are*, he says, *without excuse*[^67]. Why *without excuse*? For because he had said, *They hold the truth in iniquity*, he showed how. *Because, knowing*, he says, *God* — he did not say, "Because they did not know God," but what does he say? — *because, knowing God, they did not honor him as God or give thanks, but they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^68]. By what desert, except of pride? For see what follows: *For claiming*, he says, *to be wise, they became fools*[^69]. For they ought not to have claimed for themselves what he had given, nor to have boasted of what they had not from themselves, but from him: which ought surely to have been rendered back to him, so that, to hold to what they could see, they might be healed by him who gave them to see. For if they had done this, they would have kept humility, and could have been purged and have clung to that most blessed contemplation. For to such would the true and truthful physician, the mediator, the conqueror of pride, the exalter of humility, have been revealed. But you will say: "Had he not yet been born in humility?" But he would have been revealed through prophecy, as he was revealed to Abraham — for the Lord himself said: *Abraham longed to see my day, and he saw it, and was glad*[^70] — as he was revealed to the other fathers and prophets. For so they were saved by faith in the one who was to be born and to suffer, as we are saved, being born and having suffered. But it is no wonder that he revealed himself to their humility and veiled himself from their pride. But because there was pride in them, the false and deceitful and proud one interposed himself, who would promise them that their souls would be purged through arts of pride or other, and he made them worshipers of demons, that is, of evil angels. Hence all the sacred rites that are celebrated by the pagans, which they say avail for the purgation of their souls.

**33.** And hear the Apostle saying this in consequence, that they received these things as the reward of their pride, because they did not so honor God as God is to be honored. *Their foolish heart*, he says, *was darkened. Claiming to be wise, they became fools*[^71], now indeed through that false mediator who rejoices in images, who rejoices when the unclean beasts are fed — that is, the swine that that younger son fed, having gone far from the father[^72]: for as *the Lord is near to those who have crushed their heart*[^73], that is, to the humble, so the Lord is far from those who have lifted up their heart, that is, the proud. *Claiming*, he says, *to be wise, they became fools, and they changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of an image of corruptible man*[^74]. Now they are images, and these indeed of all the Greeks and other nations, which have the likeness of men. But because there is no greater nor more superstitious idolatry than that of the Egyptians — for Egypt flooded the world with such figments as the Apostle next describes — when he had said, *into the likeness of an image of corruptible man*, he added: *And of birds and of four-footed beasts and of serpents*[^75]. For have you, brothers, in other temples seen images with a dog's head or a bull's, and the figments of the other irrational animals? Has anyone seen these in any temple, except in the temple of Isis? For they are the idols of the Egyptians. Embracing both kinds, the Apostle says: *Into the likeness of an image of corruptible man and of birds and of four-footed beasts and of serpents. Therefore God handed them over to the desires of their heart, to uncleanness, that they might dishonor their bodies among themselves*[^76]. These evils of theirs are from ungodliness. For the head was pride. But these subsequent sins are not only sins, but also punishments. For when he says, *God handed them over*, it is now of the vengeance of some sin, that these things should be done; but these punishments are still also sins. Why? Because from them one can still return, if he wishes. But when it has come to the punishment, return is no longer granted; that will be the punishment which is no longer called sin. For in these middle things there are both punishments and sins. The first thing, which is pride, is sin alone, not yet punishment. The things that follow are both sins and punishments. He who is unwilling to return from them will come to a punishment which is no longer sin, but the penalty of all sins. But because the first sin is pride, it is openly written elsewhere: *The beginning of all sin is pride*[^77]. And how is *covetousness the root of all evils*[^78]? Because to wish for more than God is covetousness, to wish for more than suffices is covetousness. For God alone suffices for the soul. Whence it is said by Philip: *Show us the Father, and it is enough for us*[^79]. But what is prouder than he who, presuming on himself, deserts God? What more covetous than he for whom God does not suffice? It is, then, that very pride which, as covetousness, is at the origin of sins. Therefore the soul, a fornicatress, having deserted the one true and as it were lawful husband, God, prostitutes itself to many false gods, that is, to demons, and is in no way satisfied.

**34.** How many gods the Romans worshiped! At first, as it were, their own, having dismissed the one true and unchangeable God, they worshiped, as it were, their own gods. They began to fight with other nations, and, believing those nations to be, as it were, defended by their gods, they pressed on and tried by certain sacrifices to turn the gods of those nations into transgressors, and they increased the number of their gods, by taking up the sacrilegious rites of the nations either subdued or to be subdued: so they invited in the dog-faced and bull-faced and serpent forms and the bird forms and all the monsters of the Egyptians, and so, as it were by propitiating them, they appeased them. For one reads in their own authors that the gods of the Egyptians were even hostile to the gods of the Romans not long before the time of the Lord's incarnation. For as the Apostle says of the Cretans: *A certain prophet of their own said: The Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy bellies*[^80], so we too can say: A certain prophet of their own said:  
*monsters of gods of every kind, and barking Anubis,  
against Neptune and Venus and against Minerva  
hold their weapons*[^81].  
For it seemed unworthy to this poet that the dog-heads of the Egyptians fought against the human images of the Romans. But the demons fought against themselves, those of the Egyptians for the Egyptians, and those of the Romans for the Romans. But that they might possess both nations together, they composed their differences among themselves, and by the Romans all began to be worshiped. For so the Apostle says: *Not that an idol is anything, but that what the nations sacrifice they sacrifice to demons and not to God. I do not want you to become partners with demons*[^82]. As these men do, deceiving themselves and saying: ... as though you despised the idol which the craftsman made, because it has no life: you would despise the idol, if you despised the demon that rejoices in the idol. In vain, then, do you interpret the image as a signification of the creature, because neither does the creature rejoice in such worship of venerating men, except a sinful creature which, proud, requires an honor not due, fallaciously terrifying human frailty. *God handed them over*, he says, *to the desires of their heart, to uncleanness, that they might dishonor their bodies among themselves, who changed the truth of God into a lie, and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever*[^83]. What is *they changed the truth of God into a lie? Into the likeness*, namely, *of an image of corruptible man and of birds and of four-footed beasts and of serpents*[^84].

**35.** And lest any of them should say: "I do not worship images, but what the images signify," he at once subjoined: *And they worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*[^85]. Understand prudently, dearest ones. For they worship either the image or the creature. He who worships the image turns *the truth of God into a lie*. For the sea is truth, but Neptune is a lie made by man, the truth of God being turned into a lie, because God made the sea, but man the image of Neptune. So God made the sun too, but man, by making an image of the sun, turned *the truth of God into a lie*. But lest he say: "I do not worship the image, but I worship the sun," let him hear what follows: *They worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*. Having dismissed the Creator, then, they fell to the creature. And, what is more shameful, they were not even content with that. For if, having dismissed God, they worshiped what God made, they would be detestable. But how much more detestable are they who, having dismissed even the things God made, worship the things that craftsmen made? Having dismissed the creature, you go to the image, and, confounded about the image, when you try to defend yourself, you return to the creature. Plainly, then, at some point, that you may truly defend yourself, pass over from the creature to the Creator. "But I cannot," he says, "come to him except through these things." Who says this? That learned man. Who says this? He who holds *the truth of God in a lie*. Who says this? He who, when he had known God, did not glorify him as God or give thanks: not he who did not know, but he who knew.

**36.** But of these proud men who *hold the truth of God in a lie*, there are two kinds. Some committed themselves to their own virtue, sought no helper, judging that their souls could be purged through philosophy, as though needing no mediator. But of these we need not dispute at present. We are now dealing against the sacrilegious rites of the pagans. But they said that no sacrifices helped them. They say Pythagoras was such a one. Let that boasting itself see what it promises itself from its own strength! He who presumes on himself presumes on a man. But the unhappy man, *who will free him from this body of death, except the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^86], through the one true mediator, God and man[^87]? Who, if he were only man, would not be a mediator; if he were only God, would not be a mediator. But if he were not a mediator, he would not reconcile man to God, who through iniquity had been far separated. And these men, too, hold *the truth of God in a lie*, because they presume on themselves, whereas *every man is a liar*[^88], and no one is freed except through the confession of sins, with a propitiator interposed. And these men, not indeed through sacrilegious rites, but in another way, that proud enemy of souls seduces, making them like himself through the haughtiness of human presumption, not seeking again with humility the one from whom they fell by pride. Such were all to whom Christ was not revealed, even if they wished to be purged by no false rites. For it is itself a great uncleanness of an unclean soul to think it can be purged by itself. But of those who worshiped no idols and bound themselves to no Chaldean or magic rites, nothing should be rashly said, lest perhaps it be hidden from us that that Savior was in some way revealed to them, without whom no one can be saved.

**37.** But there are others who, when they had seen or believed that there is a God to whom they are to be reconciled, and did not presume on their own strength, wished to be purged by rites, but, themselves too swollen with vain curiosity and preferring themselves to others by the teachings of demons, through that very pride gave place to the devil, and through the deceits and empty mysteries of the aerial powers, that is, of demons, judged that they could be cleansed. Such the Apostle recalled, when he said: *They changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of an image of corruptible man and of birds and of four-footed beasts and of serpents*[^89]. For some of them stretched themselves out and, the whole creature transcended, understood the Creator above all, but, remaining weak, even grew proud in him. And now such men say to us, or those who follow their authority: "We cannot be purged except through these mediators," powers, namely, of this kind. So they seek a mediator. Why? "Because the human mind, wrapped up and oppressed by its desires, has need of purgation. And unless it be purged, that which it sees to be unchangeable does not delight it, even if it can in some degree and in some way be seen." And that indeed they rightly perceive, that without a mediator no one can come to God.

**38.** But what the mediator is must be sought. For there is a false mediator, there is a true one. The false mediator, as we have often said, is the devil. He interposes himself with those who seek badly and wish to be proud, doing certain signs and miracles. For the magicians of Pharaoh too did the like, as Moses did, although they could not do everything[^90], because these do as much as the aerial spirits through whom they do it are permitted. But God did as much as it pleased him. Through certain signs, then, as I said, the spirits interpose themselves with the proud and promise them purgation. But the true mediator is the one Lord Jesus Christ, whom the humble of old also recognized through revelation, and through him wished to be purged. Before he was born of Mary, he was revealed to the worthy, that through faith in him who was to be born and to suffer they might be saved, as we are saved through faith in him who was born and has suffered. Therefore he came so humbly, to show that he does not purge and save any but the humble. For even in that time, before the Word was made flesh, he was revealed not only to the holy patriarchs and prophets among the Hebrew people, but examples are found also in other nations, since, whoever sought humbly, the humble mediator was not lacking to them, who alone reconciles to the Father and alone most truly says: *No one comes to the Father except through me*[^91], fitting his humility to them, that, persevering in humility, they might merit to be purged through him who is the humble mediator. For was Melchizedek of the people of Israel? And yet Scripture commends him as *a priest of the most high God*[^92], in prefiguration of that very mediator. For by him Abraham is even blessed[^93]. Was Job, that man so great, so mighty a wrestler with the devil and a victor, who, sitting in dung, full of worms, humble, conquered him by whom Adam, proud, with an intact body, in paradise, was conquered — was this man of the people of Israel? And yet in his words that true mediator is understood to be proclaimed and foretold. But as the mediator recalls certain ones, as much as was enough, so it is to be believed that the revelation of the mediator, through whom they might be purged, was present to all, even before he had appeared in the flesh, who humbly sought the purging mediator, without whom no one is purged. But no one, except one purged through the mediator, comes to that which, even though it can be gazed upon in some small part through a certain understanding of the soul, yet cannot be obtained except by the most purged. Therefore, even if some sought in such a way, as the Apostle says, that they perceived the invisible things of God through the things that are made, but in such a way that they held *the truth of God in a lie*, that is, that they called themselves wise and were puffed up through pride, not worthily honoring him from whom they had received understanding, to such, as I said, that mediator sets himself, proud to the proud, as humble to the humble, by certain congruities and by a certain ineffable and wondrous justice, remaining in the inmost places of God, which, though we cannot see it, let us honor. The proud mediator, then, meets the proud, the humble the humble, but to the humble he is humble for this, that he may lead them to the height of God; to the proud he is proud for this, that he may shut off from the lofty within them the height of God.

**39.** For see how the devil interposes himself as a mediator. He is not mortal in the flesh, but a sinner. But the Lord willed to be mortal in the flesh, but not a sinner. He shared death with men, not sins. For even if he were a sinner, he could not be a mediator. For if he were both mortal and a sinner, he would be what the rest of men are, and would no longer be a mediator, but would need a mediator. For any man is a sinner and mortal. But God is righteous and immortal. The humble mediator is righteous, mortal, and not righteous in any way whatever, but righteous because God, mortal because man. But the proud mediator is unrighteous and immortal: for he is not dissolved in body, because he was not clothed with flesh — in this respect I call him immortal; for true immortality is none but God's, according to what is said: *Who alone has immortality and dwells in light inaccessible*[^94], according to which the only Son too is immortal, because he and the Father are one, but he willed to be mortal, a man being taken up. The devil, then, unrighteous and in a certain manner immortal, interposed himself as a mediator with man, unrighteous, mortal. There are two things at the bottom, two at the top. But that the things at the bottom may be reconciled to those at the top, they need a mediator. What two at the bottom? Unrighteous, mortal. What two at the top? Righteous, immortal. The mediator, then, who purges and leads back, if he were both unrighteous and mortal, would not be a mediator, since he would not have one thing from the bottom and one from the top, but both from the bottom, both iniquity and mortality. Again, if he had immortality from the higher things and iniquity from the lower, he would have one from here and one from there, and through this would seem a mediator, but not one leading back, because not purging. For having iniquity in common with man, he would be held worthy of punishment with him, and so would be a mediator in such a way as to shut off the way to God. For sins know only how to separate from God; hear Scripture saying: *Has God burdened his ear, that he should not hear? But your sins separate between you and God*[^95].

**40.** Sins, then, shut off; but mortality does not shut off, because mortality is the penalty of sin, coming from the judgment of God. That shuts off which merited this penalty. We say this: not what God did to you shuts off your way to God, but what you did to yourself. For God made the mortality of your body, but you yourself made the sin. So that true and truthful mediator shared with you what God did in punishing you, but did not share with you what you did in sinning. He shared with you mortality, but did not share with you iniquity. For he was made in mortal flesh, yet was not made, through sin, a debtor to death. For *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men and found in fashion as a man*[^96]. Which is so said that we should not think him changed, but that he willed to appear humbly and in the manner of a servant, remaining in secret the Lord and God with God, the Son equal to the Father, through whom all things were made. So, mortality being taken up and the weakness of our penalty shared with us, he purges from sins and frees from mortality itself: therefore worthy, by dying, to slay death, because he suffered death unworthily. This is the true and truthful mediator, the humble and exalted mediator, the mediator who leads back to that from which we fell. But that proud mediator, the false and deceitful mediator, has iniquity in common with sinful men; but he does not have the mortality of the flesh, and therefore does not dissolve their mortality, while he offers iniquity to be imitated, because, just as the iniquity of the first life made the desert of this death, so the iniquity of this life, persevering, makes the desert of eternal death. Which the devil will indeed suffer in common with those whom he seduces, for whom he does not now take up the mortality which is carried out in the mortality of the flesh; and therefore he wishes to be a mediator, because he has one thing in common with them — that is, iniquity — but does not have the other — that is, mortality.

**41.** For this reason the proud one rather seduces the proud, because mortality offends the proud more than iniquity, and therefore they rather abominate mortality in the humanity of Christ than iniquity in the pride of the devil. And so he leads those puffed up with empty and false teachings through I know not what sacrilegious rites, promising purgation in the temples, and through magic consecrations and detestable secrets he draws them to astrologers, to soothsayers, to augurs, to diviners. He dares to boast himself stronger and better than Christ, because he was not born in the flesh of a woman, was not seized, was not scourged, was not spat upon, was not crowned with thorns, was not hung on a cross, did not die and was not buried: all of which the proud deride, which the humble mediator took up, not taking up iniquity with men but taking up humanity that he might heal them of the swelling of pride and make them victors over that false mediator, when they had learned to confess their sins, and through the righteousness of Christ might be purged from their own unrighteousness, and through the humble fellowship of his mortality might come to the sublime summit of immortality.

**42.** Let us therefore reject, brothers, the malign mediator, the false and deceitful mediator, the mediator not reconciling but more and more separating. Let no one promise you any purgation outside the Church, whether in temples or anywhere through sacrilegious rites; let no one outside unity, even through the Christian sacraments, because, although there is a sacrament outside unity — which we cannot deny nor dare to violate — yet the power and salvation of the sacrament, making one a coheir with Christ, is not except in unity and in the bond of the peace of the Church. Let no one seduce you from God, no one from the Church, no one from God the Father, no one from the Church the mother. That false mediator, transforming himself into an angel of light, wishes to separate you from God, and his ministers, transforming themselves into ministers of righteousness[^97]; if he cannot lead you to sacrilegious rites, he wishes to separate you from the Church, either to heresies or to schisms, that, by deserting the mother, the Father may be offended. We had two parents who begot us mortally, we have two who beget us immortally: God and the Church. Those begot successors to themselves, these beget those who remain with them. For to what else were we born of human parents, except that we should succeed the dead? But by God the Father and the Church the mother we are so begotten that we may live forever with our parents. Whoever goes to sacrilegious rites or to magic arts or to consult astrologers, augurs, diviners about his life or what pertains to this life, and the diabolical contrivances of this world, is separated from the Father, although he does not withdraw from the Church. But if anyone is separated from the Church by the division of schism, although he seems to hold the Father for himself, he most perniciously deserts the mother. But he deserts both who relinquishes both the Christian faith and the mother Church. Hold to the Father, hold also to the mother. You are a little one: cling to the mother. You are a little one: suck the milk of the mother, and, nourished with milk, she will lead you to the table of the Father.

**43.** Your Savior took up flesh, your mediator took up flesh, and by taking up flesh he took up the Church. He tasted beforehand, as it were from the Head, what he might offer to God, *a priest forever*[^98] and *the propitiation for our sins*[^99]. The Word took up human nature, and the two became one, as it is written: *The two will be in one flesh. This is a great sacrament*, he says, *but I speak of Christ and the Church*[^100]. The chamber of this marriage was the womb of the Virgin. *And he himself, like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, rejoiced like a giant to run his course*[^101]. *A giant* because strong, by weakness conquering weakness and by death slaying death. But he ran in the way: he did not stand in the way, lest he become a man who is signified as having stood in the way of sinners. For when the psalm says: *Blessed is he who has not gone in the counsel of the ungodly and has not stood in the way of sinners*[^102], it signifies someone who stood in the way of sinners. The Lord Jesus Christ, then, ran in the way of sinners, but Adam stood in the way of sinners. And because he stood, wounded by robbers, he fell and lay. But he who so walked this way as not to stand but to run, saw the wounded one, set him on a beast and handed him to the innkeeper[^103], because he ran the way, that he might fulfill what was foretold of him: *He will drink from the torrent in the way; therefore he will lift up his head*[^104]. For this age is a torrent. Torrent waters are those that flow from sudden rains, whether wintry or from floods, surely about to pass away quickly. So are all these temporal things: it is a torrent passing by, soon to cease. On this present day of the Kalends of January, those who rejoice in the luxuries and vanities of the world do not see the rush of the torrent sweeping them away. Let them call back, if they can, the day like it of the former year; let them call back at least yesterday. They do not see even their joys passing like a torrent, so that afterward they say: *These are they whom we once held in derision and as a byword of reproach. We, fools, esteemed their life madness, and their end without honor. How are they reckoned among the sons of God, and is their lot among the saints? Therefore we strayed from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness did not shine for us, and the sun did not rise for us*[^105]. What *sun*? Surely not this visible one? This rises for them daily. For of this the Lord says: *Who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil*[^106]. There is another who made this sun, invisible and intelligible, that sun of righteousness, of whom it is said in another place: *The sun of righteousness has risen for me*[^107]. He has not risen for these. And hear what they lament: *What did pride profit us, and what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All those things passed away like a shadow*[^108]. As if that torrent had now flowed away; but yet he who was born, suffered, was crucified, was buried, rose again: from that torrent he drank in the way, passing from here to the Father, with the faithful to follow, for whom from this torrent he drank in the way; therefore he lifted up his head, that is, himself.

**44.** For he himself is the Head of the Church[^109], who has already ascended into heaven and sits at the right hand of the Father[^110], showing us in his holocaust what we ought to hope for even of our flesh. For as the Apostle says: *For by hope we were saved, and we ourselves groan within ourselves, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^111]. For we are adopted, but he was born the only Son: *in the beginning the Word*, God *with God*, as the evangelist witnesses[^112], and equal to the Father, as the apostle Paul says[^113], yet he was made humble, that he might be *the one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^114]. But of what kind that evil and false mediator is who shuts off the way to God, of such kind do all proud men strive to be: wherever they are, they wish to be like their mediator. And as you see in men, so also in angels. He who willed to be the mediator and reconciler to God, of whom we have said much, that is, the humble and exalted mediator, the Lord Jesus Christ, took up the whole creature — for in man there is the whole: for we said above that the creature is either spiritual or corporeal — he took up, then, the whole man, taking the form of a servant; whatever is in the rational mind, whatever is in the soul, whatever is in the flesh, he took up wholly, except sin. So in him are all things, lest anyone should err in seeking, for his purgation, a creature as mediatrix and as it were cleanser; therefore that mediator has all, therefore that mediator took up all, because a creature can be such a cleansing mediatrix, able to cleanse from iniquities and to free from mortality, which does not presume itself to avail anything, but cleaves personally and in an ineffable manner is joined and commingled with God the Word, so that it could be said: *The Word was made flesh*[^115]. Whose humility was for this despised and exalted, lest humble men despair of themselves and the proud presume. When, then, proud men want themselves to be worshiped, and, if God is preferred to them, are angry, they are imitators of that deceitful mediator. On the contrary, holy humble men, when they are so honored by erring men who want to place their hope in a man, not attending to the divine Scripture that says: *Cursed is everyone who places his hope in man*[^116], grieve for them in their heart and faithfully admonish them, as much as they can, to hope in God, not in man, when not even the Apostle himself wished hope to be placed in himself, but in the truth which he announced. What was said through him was better than he himself through whom it was said, and he wished those who believed to place their hope in what was said through him, not in him through whom it was said. *Even if we*, he says — it is too little, *if we*, hear what follows — *or an angel*, he says, *from heaven should announce to you anything beyond what you have received, let him be accursed*[^117]. He saw that a false mediator could transform himself into an angel of light and announce something false. As, then, proud men want themselves to be worshiped in place of God, to claim for themselves whatever they can, to be named, and, if it can be done, to surpass Christ himself in glory, so do the devil and his angels.

**45.** My brothers, we speak to catholics. The Donatists hold Donatus in place of Christ. If they hear someone, a pagan, disparaging Christ, they bear it perhaps more patiently than if they hear someone disparaging Donatus. You know what I say, and you are compelled to experience this daily. They love Donatus so perversely that they prefer him to Christ. For not only do they have nothing to say, but they even know that they have nothing to say. For nothing holds them but the name of Donatus under the name of Christ; for the name of a man they have been seduced against Christ. For this reason they have contracted against us a most monstrous hatred, because we cry out to them: "Do not place your hope in man, lest you be cursed." They hate those who preach peace, and, if they suffer anything for so great a crime of theirs — and not for Christ, but for Donatus — they think themselves martyrs; and because we say to them: "Do not blow away the baptism of Christ, love peace, give yourself back to the whole world: Christ redeemed the whole with his blood, do not bring back to a part the buyer of the whole," for this they hate us and, if opportunity be given, kill us by the hand of the Circumcellions. But because the Lord helped us, we escaped, giving thanks to the mercy of the Lord. Whence we admonish and ask you to pray for us, that the Lord may always inspire in us confidence to preach his peace, and that we may not fear them, but rather love them and rejoice to have fulfilled in us what is written: *With those who hated peace I was peaceable; when I spoke to them, they fought against me without cause*[^118]. And if they cannot be healed otherwise, let them fight against us, strike us, kill us, and yet be healed.

**46.** But let us return, brothers, to what I had begun to say; for grief snatched me away from what I had intended. As there are proud men — they prefer themselves to be worshiped rather than God — so proud angels prefer themselves to be worshiped rather than God. And as there are holy men — they prefer God to be worshiped rather than themselves — so all the holy angels, which is also to be believed of the martyrs without any doubt, prefer God to be worshiped rather than themselves. They want him to be honored whom they honor; in him they choose to be loved; private honors from men they not only do not accept, but even reject. Whence we also gave an example, as much as the Lord granted, from that angel in the Apocalypse, who refused to be adored by a man, but God[^119]. Do not fear, then, brothers, lest perhaps, if you worship God alone with religious service, you offend some holy angel or some martyr. For if the prudence of the flesh persuades you of this, that, measuring from yourselves — if perhaps you delight to be honored by a certain private haughtiness of pride, not in God nor for the sake of God, but in yourselves for your own sakes, so that, if it is not done, you are indignant — you should thus think that either the holy angels or the holy martyrs rejoice in such homage of men and require for themselves the service that is owed to God, you will be deceived by a most easy seduction by the pagans, and will withdraw altogether far from the Lord your God, of whom we have received this command: *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve*[^120]. Do you wish securely to honor the holy angels and the holy martyrs? Worship him in whom alone they wish themselves to be honored. For if they are holy, they are angry with you, if you worship them privately and not him alone from whom they too received the grace of their blessedness. Because, then, they are holy, all the more do not offend them by wishing to worship them apart. For in worshiping God you worship everyone who, by pious love and holy devotion, cleaves to God. But if one is angry because he himself is not worshiped by some sacraments proper to him, he is now that proud transgressor and false mediator, transforming himself into an angel of light[^121]. And if he has turned you aside to himself, he has shut off the way: not only can you not come through him, but rather through him you are not allowed to come.

**47.** Let them not sell you their empty talk and say: "We worship the virtues of God, that through them we may come to God, and we make these rites to Saturn, those to Jupiter, those to Pluto, those to Neptune, those to Mars, those to Ceres." For this is why they do not all rejoice in the same rites, because they claim for themselves private and proper worships; and through this they are not sacred rites, but sacrileges. And in all these their prince exults and triumphs, delighted not so much in those rites as in the deception and error of men through proud malevolence. So let them not seduce you, when they say to you: "If you worship the martyrs and think yourselves helped by them before God, how much more ought we to worship the virtues of God, through which we may be helped before God?" For attend to the sacraments of the Church and see whether a sacrifice is offered to any martyr, so that we should exhibit one thing to this martyr, another to that one; but at all the memorials we offer one thing, and not to any one of them, but to the Lord of us all, in which sacrifice we honor the martyrs too according to their rank, not in themselves, but in him through whom they conquered the devil. And they are mindful of us so much the more dearly, the less we bring them private rites, because, in him alone in whom they have their joy, in him alone they have their honor. If anyone says to you: "Invoke the angel Gabriel thus, invoke Michael thus, exhibit this to that one, this to this one," do not be deceived, do not consent; nor let it deceive you for this reason, that the names of these angels are read in the holy Scriptures: attend rather to how they are read there, whether they required anything for themselves from men of private religion, and did not rather always wish glory to be given to the one God whom they obey.

**48.** But not only if a man, but if anyone as though an angel, whether by some visitation or through a dream, should wish to tempt you and say: "Do this for me, celebrate this for me, because I am the angel," for example, "Gabriel," do not believe it. Worship securely the one God, who is Father and Son and Holy Spirit. He rejoices in such worship of yours, if he is an angel; but if he is angry because you did not give something apart, he is now to be understood as the one of whom the Apostle says that *he transforms himself into an angel of light*[^122]: he wishes to shut off the way, he interposes himself wickedly; he is not a reconciling mediator, but rather a separating one. For that angel in the Apocalypse and the other such ones do not want to be adored, but God[^123]. They are messengers, announcing what they have received to be announced; they are ministers, doing what they have been bidden to do, carrying our prayers to God, not themselves requiring them in place of God. An angel says to a man: *I offered your prayer in the sight of the brightness of God*[^124]. Yet he was not asking the angel himself, and that angel was asking God: as a minister he offered his prayer. Did he say, as the perverse and corrupt ministers of certain powers do: "Give me something, if you want me to announce it, if you want me to admit you"? Our Lord does not have such a great house. His servants love him, his sons love him. If you should wish to corrupt any of them, as it were privately, that you may be admitted to their Lord, you will be driven far from that great house. For if they imitate the angelic life — because it is written: *There was one soul and one heart toward God, and no one had anything of his own, but all things were common to them*[^125], as we read in the Acts of the Apostles that so many thousands of men of the number of the Jews were converted[^126], to whom, possessing nothing of their own, out of the common stock *it was distributed to each as each had need*[^127] — if, then, these imitate the angelic life, and if to any of them some friend of his, whether brother or father or mother or any near one, should wish to offer something privately, he not only rejects it, but also admonishes that, if it is to be offered and received, it be offered to God, as those did who laid the prices of their possessions at the feet of the apostles[^128], as also those whom the Apostle exhorted to make some offering for the poor of the saints who were in Jerusalem[^129] — and this he teaches, that they may know they offer to God what they do — how much more those angels, surely more perfect, who in the heavenly hosts and in the great house and the heavenly city Jerusalem, the mother of us all[^130], serve God in most concordant love, want no honor to be exhibited to them privately and properly, but rejoice in that honor alone which is exhibited in common to God as God, and to the city itself as his temple, that is, to the whole Church.

**49.** And therefore, on account of the purgation which is made through the mediator, the one who is equal to the Father willed to be the one mediator and himself to become man, that through a kindred substance — which is man — we might come to the highest substance — which is God. And therefore he descended, because we were in the lower places, and ascended, lest we should remain in the lower places. That one mediator is true, who deceives no one, who, even though he is equal to the Father, willed for our sake also to be less than he, not losing what is equal, but taking up what is less. He has now freed even our flesh in his flesh. *Now he dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^131]. To him our prayers come, although in the sacraments of the Church they are directed to the Father. *Now he dies no more.* For he himself, the high priest, offers them, who offered himself a holocaust for us. He himself is the one who leads us through, interposing himself, not to shut us out, but to direct; not to separate, but to reconcile; not to hinder, but to break the hindrances. He himself is the one pontiff and the one priest, who was prefigured in the ancient priests of God. Therefore a priest was sought without blemish of body, because he alone lived without blemish of sin, even in a mortal body. For what was prefigured in their body was signified in his life. But all of us bishops are called priests for this reason, that we are set over the people. Yet the whole Church is the body of that priest. To the priest belongs his body. For the apostle Peter too says to that very Church: *A holy people, a royal priesthood*[^132].

**50.** Then one priest was anointed, now all Christians are anointed. The king was anointed, the priest was anointed; the rest were not anointed. The Lord bore both persons, not in figure, but now in truth, both of king and of priest. For for this reason, in king David himself, of whose seed he was made according to the flesh, both were figured. He was a king, which we know and which is manifest. There was prefigured in him also the person of a priest, when he ate of those loaves of proposition, which it was not lawful to eat except for priests alone[^133]: which the Lord himself also recalled in the Gospel[^134], so that by those who can understand he might be recognized as having been prefigured in David. For Mary herself too drew her origin not only from royal but also from priestly line. And from the royal line it is manifest; for which reason the Apostle says of the Lord himself: *Who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^135]. For although Joseph is called his father by the effect of love, yet he was not born of his seed, as is declared in the Gospel[^136]. It remains, then, that Christ is said to be of the seed of David on account of Mary, because she was of the seed of David, that is, of the royal line. But how do we find also the priestly line in Mary? It is written in the Gospel that the priest Zechariah had as wife Elizabeth of the daughters of Aaron[^137]. Therefore Elizabeth was of the priestly line: for Aaron, the brother of Moses, was a priest, and that whole tribe is Levi. But it is said in the Gospel to the virgin Mary by the angel: *Your kinswoman Elizabeth*[^138]. If, then, Elizabeth, one of the daughters of Aaron the priest, was kin to Mary, there is no doubt that the virgin Mary belonged not only to royal but also to priestly blood. Wherefore there is in the Lord, according to the man whom he took up, both persons, the royal and the priestly: the royal, leading us to every imitation of spiritual warfare, *until he puts his enemies under his feet*[^139], and *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^140] — for our king himself too was tempted by our enemy[^141], that the soldier might learn to do battle — but the priestly, by which, to expiate and purge our sins, he offered himself a holocaust. Therefore then, in figure, two were anointed: the king and the priest.

**51.** For this reason too there are two fish with those five barley loaves[^142]. For fish belong to the relish: but relishes are usually seasoned with oil. Two fish, then, with five loaves, that is, with the Old Testament, where the five books of Moses hold the chief place. But with the seven loaves, which are not called barley and now signify the New Testament, a few fish are said to have been[^143], because Christians had now begun to be few, that is, the anointed, out of whom the whole Church throughout the world would be filled. For the five barley loaves signify, covered as it were with little husks, those things which are to be understood spiritually in the Law itself, which, when expounded, the Lord taught the apostles. Whence there, from the remnants of the fragments, twelve baskets are filled[^144]. But the seven loaves signify the sevenfold operation of the Spirit in the New Testament, as John too says in the Apocalypse[^145]. For the seven spirits are understood to be recalled for the seven spiritual operations, of which Isaiah first had made mention: *of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and piety, and of the fear of the Lord*[^146]. And therefore from those remnants of the fragments seven hampers were filled[^147], by which number the whole Church is signified. Whence also the same apostle John writes to seven churches[^148]. And in the epistles of the apostle Paul the same number seven is found, not of epistles, but of churches. And many things in those sacraments, more diligently sought, are more sweetly found. But now I have recalled something from there on account of the anointing of the fish, to show that the royal and priestly person was signified by the two fish then, because two were anointed then, the king and the priest. But from "chrism," that is, from anointing, he is called Christ. But the body of Christ is the Church. And therefore all Christians are anointed, by a sacrament hidden from the rest, but known to the faithful. But the priest alone is the mediator himself, the Head of the Church without sin, through whom is made the purgation of our sins.

**52.** Whence it comes to my mind to recall, with great grief, that Parmenian, once a bishop of the Donatists, dared in a certain letter of his to set down that a bishop is a mediator between the people and God. You see that they set themselves up in place of the bridegroom: with sacrilegious adultery they corrupt souls not their own. This is no slight audacity, and it would seem to me altogether incredible, had I not read it. For if a bishop is a mediator between the people and God, since there are many bishops, it follows that many mediators are to be understood. So that the letter of Parmenian may be read, let the letter of the apostle Paul be deleted, who says: *For there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^149]. But between whom is a mediator, except between God and his people? Therefore between God and his body, since his body is the Church. So that pride is monstrous which dares to constitute a bishop a mediator, claiming for itself the marriage of Christ by adulterous deceit. Let us look at the friend of the bridegroom, zealous for the bridegroom, not setting himself up in place of the bridegroom. Does he say: "I have betrothed you to myself"? He can say this who says he is a mediator between the people and God, not this one who says: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^150]; not this one who says: *For there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^151]; not this one who says: *I have betrothed you, to present you to one husband as a chaste virgin to Christ*[^152]. And therefore that adulterer, who did not have the wedding garment, was cast out of the wedding feast[^153]: for he did not have the garment in which to honor the bridegroom, but through that attire sought his own honor at the bridegroom's feast.

**53.** Therefore we have one mediator, brothers, who is also our Head. But we, in the name of Christ, even if we are not set over the Churches together with you, are yet together with you members of the body of Christ: we have one Head, not many; for a body that wants to have many heads is now a monster. But we were speaking of anointing, because then the priest alone was anointed, and the king, but now all Christians. Hence see that you all belong to the body of the priest along with us, that is, because you are all faithful; yet especially are those called priests who are set over the Church, but not for that reason is the rest of the body not the body of the priest. And therefore in those old sacraments, because that one was prefigured, that is, our Lord the priest Jesus Christ, one priest entered into the holy of holies; but all the people stood outside. Are you now, while the bishops stand at the altar, outside, and do you not within see and hear and bear witness and receive? Then one priest entered into the holy of holies once a year. "Year" signifies the whole of time. Once, then, in the whole of time, our one priest, rising from the dead, the Lord Jesus Christ, entered into the holy of holies, not figured but true, beyond the veils of the heavens, offering himself for us. He entered, and is there. But the people still stand outside with us: we have not yet risen to go to meet Christ and to remain forever within with him, when he will say to the good servant: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^154]. This, then, was prefigured then by the one priest alone entering into the holy of holies, and the people standing outside, which is now fulfilled by our Lord Jesus Christ alone entering above the secret places of the heavens, and the people still groaning outside, made safe by hope, awaiting the redemption of its body, which will be in the resurrection of the dead. But yet we have a priest and pontiff who intercedes for us in the holy of holies, sitting at the right hand of the Father, that we may not fear in this our pilgrimage, if we do not stray from the way of truth, if we do not love another in place of him, but love one another in him, so that in every brother of ours walking in his ways we may behold him, honor him, receive him, *who was handed over for our trespasses and rose for our justification*[^155]; because he himself speaks in his saints, as the Apostle says: *Do you wish to receive proof of Christ who speaks in me?*[^156] And although he says, *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the growth*[^157], not because he wished himself, but because he wished him to be loved in himself, he bears witness to certain ones, saying: *You received me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus*[^158]. In all his saints, then, he himself is to be loved, because he says: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^159]. For he does not say: "You gave them," but: *You gave me*; so great is the love of the Head toward his body!

**54.** And so, because the mediator and priest himself alone entered into the holy of holies, but the Church — which is his body — still, as we said, groans outside in tearful prayers and laborious works, we do not find in the old books that a priest commended himself to the people, that prayer be made for him, because he figured our Lord Jesus Christ, for whom no one prays. For of whom was the priest a figure, for whom no one prayed, except of our Lord Jesus Christ, *who intercedes for us*[^160] and does not need our prayers for himself? Which he himself deigned to figure even on this earth, when he prayed alone on the mountain, while the disciples in the little boat were tossed by storms[^161]. And we too, as in a ship, in the Church are tossed by the storms of this age; but because he, as then on the mountain, so now in the heights of the heavens, *intercedes for us*, let us be secure.

**55.** The apostle John also says: *I write these things to you that you may not sin. And if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous: he is the propitiation for our sins*[^162]. See who John is? He who reclined on the breast of the Lord and drank from that breast at that feast what he might belch forth among the peoples: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^163]. Did that John, then, say: "And if anyone has sinned, you have me with the Father: I pray for you"? See who says what. For not only did he not say this, but even if he said: "And if anyone has sinned, you have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous: he is the propitiation for your sins," he might perhaps seem proud and arrogant. He did not say this. And if he did not say this, with how great pride would he say: "You have me as an advocate with the Father!", with how much more monstrous a sacrilege would he say: "You have me as a mediator with the Father!", so as to wish to interpose himself between sinners and God! And this these men neither fear nor blush to say, that a bishop is a mediator between God and men. Plainly he is a mediator, but on the side of Donatus, to shut off, not to lead through, as Donatus did: for he interposed his own name, to shut off the way to Christ. For from there they are unwilling to come to the Church, because Donatus shut it off, because... through a whitewashed wall: for it is earthly, but not by the Christian religion. He whitewashed it to deceive ... and those coming all the way to him could not pass over to Christ, that is, to the body of Christ which is the Church spread throughout the whole world. Behold how he made himself a mediator, how that proud angel too does, of whom we have already said much. But attend to that saying of John: *I write these things to you that you may not sin. And if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father*[^164]. He would not say *we have*, except because he knew himself well and humbly commended himself. *And he is*, he says, *the propitiation for our sins*[^165]. He did not say "your," as though making himself alien from sins. For if he said this, there would be recited to him his own saying in another place: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^166].

**56.** And so, brothers, give effort that you may not sin, strive mightily that you may not sin. But if anyone has sinned, he will expiate us who *is the propitiation for our sins*[^167]. Avoid all those evils through which the name of God is blasphemed[^168], so that, in your good conduct, it may be to the glory of the name of Christ that others are to be won. Avoid most of all those things which can separate you from the altar of God. But those sins which daily creep in through the custom of daily life, and, as from the waves of the sea of this age, through a certain weakness of ours cease not to steal in, bail out with good works, lest you suffer shipwreck. Let those daily wounds be healed by the daily medicines of alms, fasts, prayers. Be fervent in all these good works, and neither show to men what you do for the sake of your own glory, nor avoid the eyes of those wishing to imitate you, *that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^169]. Whatever you do, *do all to the glory of God*[^170], that he, the humble and exalted one, may make us exalted out of the humble. For so our alms will reach him who for our sake *became poor, though he was rich*[^171], as our fasts will reach him who for our sake fasted; so our prayers will reach him, even when in them we truly ask that *our debts be forgiven us, as we too forgive our debtors*[^172], as he too forgave his debtors, hanging on the cross and saying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^173]. Let him be blessed, who gave you to be able to do what you do. For if you attribute to yourselves and claim for yourselves your good works, so that in the very sacrifices of humility pride puffs you up, you will give place to that false mediator, that he may interpose himself and shut off the way to you. For he seeks all approaches and, through the good works themselves, tries to creep in by serpentine slidings. And if he finds a man as it were working well and attributing to himself what he does, and by a certain swelling lifting himself above one who does not, he sets himself before him at once, to deceive him in place of a mediator.

**57.** But as I had begun to say, we do not find any priest in the old books commending himself to the people, that it might pray for him, because they figured the one for whom no one was going to pray, that is, our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the one mediator and priest, not figured but true. But the apostle Paul, who knew himself to be of the body of the priest along with the other members, commends himself to the prayers of the Church, because *the members are anxious for one another; and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice together, and if one member suffers, all the members suffer together*[^174]. The Head intercedes for all the members; let the members intercede for one another under the Head. What, then, does the Apostle say? *Praying*, he says, *also for us at the same time, that God may open to us a door of the word*[^175]. And the Church prayed for Peter when Peter was in chains[^176], and was heard, as Peter too prayed for the Church, because the members pray for one another. These men do not call themselves mediators and pray for those by whom they wish to be prayed for, while those call themselves mediators, who, if they took themselves out of the middle, the two would become one, which a wicked pride has divided. Far be it, then, from the hearts of Christians what is shown in the pride of heretics! As we have said to your Charity, that first priesthood bore a certain figure: now what was then figured is fulfilled. We have a mediator and pontiff: he ascended into heaven, he entered within the veil, into that true, not figured, holy of holies. The sacrament of this is celebrated in the Church: within you pray with us, to the words of the bishop you answer "Amen." For so the people, as it were, subscribes, because all belong to the body of the priest. Let no one, then, as it is wont to be said, sell you smoke: we have one mediator, the Lord Jesus Christ; he is our prayer, *he is the propitiation for our sins*[^177]; securely let us hold to him.

**58.** For you see, brothers — that we may now return to what I was saying against the pagans — how you are fortified, that you may not only not be overcome by them, but — as much as can be done through you — may, by convincing them, win them to salvation and pray and fast for them, that they may know God and honor him as he is to be honored, not as the Apostle says of certain ones: *that, when they had known God, they did not glorify him as God or give thanks, but they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^178], so that they sought a superstitious and sacrilegious defilement under the name of purgation. For wishing to cleave to that thing which always is, which is always of the same kind, remains unchangeable, which by some sharpness of genius they were able to attain, but with a humble heart were unwilling to honor, they fell into that false mediator, envying the human soul and trying by all means lest it pass from the labors in which he rules to the rest where it would be more sublime than he, to whom belong all sacrilegious rites and the contrivances of the most deceitful iniquity of astrologers, soothsayers, diviners, Chaldeans.

**59.** But you, my brothers, who perhaps do not see with the keen edge of the mind what they saw, and are not yet fit to transcend in the thought of the mind the whole creature, not only the corporeal but also the spiritual, and to see the unchangeable God, *from whom and through whom and in whom are all things*[^179], do not be alarmed, do not despair, because he who willed to descend even to the lowest and the weak made himself the way for you. For what does it profit them that, proud, they see the homeland from afar? They do not find the way, since to that height of the homeland the way begins from humility. They see the homeland as it were from the mountain of pride, they see it from the contrary mountain. But no one ascends to it unless he first descends. They are unwilling to descend, that they may be able to ascend, that is, they are unwilling to be humble, that they may be able to be Christians. When they say to themselves: "And am I to be this, which my doorkeeper is, and not rather what Plato was, what Pythagoras?", with reckless mouth blowing out empty things, they are unwilling to descend, they cannot ascend. For our Lord descended to us from the height of his majesty, and these men are unwilling to descend to him from the swelling of their pride. He descended, then, that he might *choose the weak things of the world to confound the strong, and the foolish things of the world to confound the wise*, *and the ignoble things of the world and the contemptible he chose, and the things that are not, as though they were, that he might bring to nothing the things that are*[^180].

**60.** For for this reason he willed to become man, that God made man might teach humility, not by changing himself into man, but by taking up man. To this humility it would seem that nothing could be added, and yet even in human things he did not choose those of which men are swollen. For he willed to be born of a woman who was betrothed to a carpenter, lest anyone should boast against the righteousness of the poor and ignoble man about the nobility of his parents and be incurably puffed up. He did not even choose a noble city in which to be born — Bethlehem is of few men: he was indeed from there himself, of the tribe of David. Yet in that tribe, as in the rest, there were many poor and ignoble. And David himself, of whose seed the Lord Jesus Christ was made according to the flesh, did he not pasture sheep, before he was divinely chosen for the kingdom[^181]? For even in him he had chosen *the ignoble things of the world, that the noble might be confounded*[^182], and in him too was prefigured what would be fulfilled through his seed. He chose, then, also a humble line, from which to be born. But perhaps someone may say: "Even if he himself was born in humility, he wished to boast in the nobility of his disciples." He did not choose kings, or senators, or philosophers, or orators. Rather, he chose common folk, the poor, the unlearned, fishermen. Peter a fisherman, Cyprian an orator. Unless the fisherman had gone faithfully before, the orator would not have approached humbly. Let no one cast down despair of himself: let him only hold to Christ, and his hope will not be false.

**61.** For those who from afar, as I had begun to say, and from the contrary mountain of pride, see the homeland, spurn humility: therefore they do not hold to the way. For our way is humility. This Christ showed in himself. If anyone strays from this way, he will run into a mountain tangled and inextricable, interposing itself, into the devil interposing himself as a mediator perniciously and fallaciously through innumerable sacrilegious rites, through soothsayers, augurs, diviners, astrologers, magicians. For those given over to these do not descend to the way, but err on a certain wooded mountain, from which some of them lift up their eyes and see the homeland, but cannot come to it, because they do not hold to the way. But those who now hold to the way, that is, the true and truthful mediator, the mediator who leads through and does not shut off, the mediator who purges and does not entangle, walk perseveringly in what they hold. For some of them see the homeland, some of them do not see it. But let those not yet seeing the homeland not withdraw from the way, and they will come there where those also who see. For the keenness of the eyes is in some such that they can see from afar. To these it profits nothing to see where they go and not know by what road they proceed. But if they have come to know the way, it profits them not so much that they see from afar where they are to come, as that they have come to know whither they are tending. But those who have not such keenness, if they walk together, will come together. He of you, then, who can transcend the whole creature in mind and see the ineffable light of wisdom, when he sees it will see that it cannot be told, and will see that all things that have been said of it are unworthy of its greatness, although they are fitted to the little ones, who are nourished for this, that they may hear as it can be told, until they hear what cannot be told. But those who cannot transcend the creature and see the ineffable truth, let them hold to the mediator, whom, unless even those also hold who now see something unchangeable, they surely see in vain. In him we have the corporeal creature, which he took up even in flesh; in him also the spiritual creature, because there is the soul, there is the rational mind; in him the very Word through whom all things were made, because *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^183]. Holding to this way, let us not stray from it, lest we run into false mediators who promise us purgation and bring in hindrances.

**62.** For he alone is the mediator, *in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge*[^184], *who is the head of all principality and power*[^185]; through that which we hold, he leads us through to that which we ought to hold. For nothing so holds a man as human affection. Therefore he, God and man, through that which we hold, carries us across to that which is to be held. For, lest a man should think that the Word of God, through whom all things were made, is far from human nature ... but is near to the heavenly or supercelestial virtues, and therefore should wish those very virtues to become his mediatrices and cleansers unto that Word, and so through pride and vain curiosity should fall into the aerial powers which deceive and beguile the senses of human weakness with various and manifold errors, envying its passage to God and desiring to rule over the captured: therefore the Word itself through whom all things were made *was made flesh*[^186], that is, joined to itself even a fleshly nature from man, that thus man might learn that it is not far from his own nature, which he thought drew near to the heavenly things alone, and, purged through the known middle thing, might cleave to the unchangeable divinity. For this reason he deigned to do both earthly and heavenly miracles, to show that even those things were subject to him, by which men could be terrified in lying signs and wonders[^187]. He showed also that every spiritual power either fears Christ or loves Christ, so that what fears Christ may not be feared by a Christian, but what loves Christ may be loved in Christ.

**63.** Wherefore, dearest brothers, let not men frighten you on this score either, because they say that these powers of the elements, on account of the necessities of this life and on account of the temporal things that are necessary for our uses, must be appeased and honored with their rites: neither do they have any power in these things, except as much as they are permitted from above. Remember that holy Job could not be proved by such great temptations, unless the tempter received the power from the Lord God[^188]. And attend to the Apostle saying: *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, but will with the temptation also make a way of escape, that you may be able to endure*[^189]. Attend also to those who presume on such things and bind the souls of the light to the sacrilegious sacraments of demons and to magic arts, how they lose their salvation for nothing. For they do not fail to suffer the things that other men suffer, or even suffer worse, with the torment of a bad conscience: losses, diseases, condemnations, deaths, for the most part from the common scourge of the human race, for the most part also for their very deeds. See whether those who worship Neptune sail more happily than those who do not worship him; whether those who are bound to the temple of Tellus have more fruitful fields than those who are held by no such superstition; whether women who worship Juno give birth with less pain or peril than Christian women who detest her; whether those who worship Mercury are sharper than those who deride such figments, or, because he is also said to be the god of merchandise, whether those who sacrifice to him make more profits than those who contaminate themselves with no such sacrilege. And so, through all these temporal advantages, lead the prudent eye of the mind: you will find all things governed under the most high power of God, serving whom alone the Christian, and holding to the way, his mediator, does not care for the deceitful enticements and terrors of vanity, giving honor to the truest God, whether his temperance be proved by the prosperities of temporal things or his fortitude by adversities, because *God is faithful, who does not let us be tempted beyond what we can bear, but makes with the temptation also a way of escape, that we may be able to endure*[^190], consoling us in all things and filling us with the joy of a good hope, until he leads us where the way leads, which he deigned to offer to our weakness. Turning to the Lord, and so on.  
Here ends the treatise of Saint Augustine, delivered on the Kalends of January against the pagans, concerning the false mediator the devil and the true mediator Christ.
