# Sermon 136

**1.** We have heard the reading of the holy Gospel, which we are accustomed to: but it is good to be reminded; it is good to renew memory from the torpor of forgetfulness. In short, the most ancient reading, as though new, delighted us. Christ enlightened a man blind from birth: why do you wonder? Christ is the Savior: he restored by a benefit, what he had left undone in the womb. But when he left his eyes undone in the womb, he surely did not err; but deferred it to a miracle. You say perhaps: Whence do you know? From him I heard it: just now he said it; together we heard it. For when his disciples asked him, and said: *Lord, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?* What he answered, you heard with me: *Neither did this man sin, nor his parents; but that the works of God might be shown in him* [^1]. Behold why he deferred it, when he left his eyes undone. He did not do what he would do; he did not do, what he knew he would do, when it was fitting. And do not suppose, brothers, that his parents had no sin, or that he himself, when he was born, did not contract original sin, on account of which sin to be remitted little ones are baptized for the remission of sins. But that blindness was not on account of the sin of his parents, was not on account of his own sin; but that the works of God might be manifested in him. For all of us, when we were born, drew original sin; and yet we were not born blind. Ask diligently: we were born blind too. For who was not born blind? but in heart. But the Lord Jesus, because he had created both, cured both.

**2.** You have seen this blind man with the eyes of faith, you have seen too the one seeing from blind: but you have heard him erring. In what this blind man erred, I say: first because he thought Christ a prophet, he did not know him to be the Son of God. Then we heard one answer of his utterly false: for he said: *We know that God does not hear sinners* [^2]. If God does not hear sinners, what hope have we? If God does not hear sinners, why do we pray, and speak the testimony of our sin by the beating of the breast? Where surely is that Publican, who went up with the Pharisee into the temple, and, while that Pharisee was boasting, winnowing his merits, he, standing afar off, and with eyes fixed on the earth, and beating his breast, confessed his sins? And he who confessed his sins went down justified from the temple, more than that Pharisee [^3]. Surely God hears sinners. But he who said these things had not yet washed the face of his heart from Siloam. In his eyes the sacrament had preceded: but in his heart the benefit of grace had not yet been effected. When did this blind man wash the face of his heart? When the Lord, after he was cast out by the Jews, brought him in to himself. For he found him, and said to him, as we heard: *Do you believe in the Son of God?* And he: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?* Surely he already saw with his eyes: did he already see with his heart? Not yet. Wait: now he will see. Jesus answered him: *I am he, who speaks with you* [^4]. Did he doubt? At once he washed his face. For Siloam was speaking with him, *which is interpreted sent* [^5]. Who is the sent one, except Christ? Who often testified, saying: *I do the will of my Father, who sent me* [^6]. Therefore he himself was Siloam. The blind man approached with his heart, heard, believed, adored: washed his face, saw.

**3.** But those who cast him out remained blind, inasmuch as they calumniated the Lord, because it was the Sabbath, when he made clay from spittle, and anointed the eyes of the blind man. For when the Lord cured by a word, the Jews openly calumniated. For he did not work anything on the Sabbath, when he spoke, and it was done. The calumny was open: they calumniated him commanding, they calumniated him speaking; as though they themselves did not speak on the Sabbath. I can say that not only on the Sabbath, but on no day do they speak, since they have departed from the praises of the true God. Yet, as I said, brothers, the calumny was open. The Lord said to the man: *Stretch out your hand*; he was made whole, and they calumniated because he cured on the Sabbath [^7]. What did he do? What did he work? What burden did he carry? But now to spit on the earth, to make clay, and to anoint the eyes of a man, is to work. Let no one doubt, it was to work. The Lord broke the Sabbath: but not for that reason guilty. What is it that I said: He broke the Sabbath? He himself had come, the Light, he was removing the shadows. For the Sabbath was commanded by the Lord God, commanded by Christ himself, who was with the Father, when that Law was given: by him it was commanded, but as a shadow of what was to come. *Let no one, then, judge you in food, or in drink, or in respect of a feast day, or a new moon, or Sabbaths, which is a shadow of things to come* [^8]. He had come whom these announced as to come. What do shadows delight? Jews, open your eyes: the sun is present. *We know*. What do you know, O blind of heart? What do you know? *That this man is not from God, who so breaks the Sabbath*. The Sabbath, wretches, Christ himself preached the Sabbath, whom you say is not from God. You observe the Sabbath carnally, you do not have the spittle of Christ. Attend in the earth of the Sabbath even to the spittle of Christ, and you will understand Christ foretold through the Sabbath. But because you do not have the spittle of Christ in the earth upon your eyes, therefore you have not come to Siloam, and have not washed your face, and have remained blind; to the good of that blind man, rather now not blind, neither in flesh nor in heart. He received clay in spittle, his eyes were anointed, he approached Siloam, washed his face, believed in Christ, saw, did not remain in that very terrible judgment: *I came for judgment into this world, that those who do not see may see; and those who see may become blind* [^9].

**4.** A great terror! *That those who do not see may see*: good. It is the office of the Savior, it is the profession of medicine: *That those who do not see may see*. What is it, Lord, that you added: *That those who see may become blind?* If we understand it, it is most true and most right. Yet what is: *Those who see?* They are the Jews. Do they then see? According to their words they see, according to the truth they do not see. What is, then: *they see?* They think they see, they believe they see. For they believed they saw, when they defended the Law against Christ. *We know*: therefore they see. What is: *We know*, except: We see? What is: *That this man is not from God, because he so breaks the Sabbath?* They are seeing: what the law said, they read. For it was commanded that whoever broke the Sabbath should be stoned [^10]. Therefore they said this man was not from God; but, seeing, they were blind, because he came for judgment into the world, that judge to be of the living and the dead. Why did he come? *That those who do not see may see*: those who confess that they do not see, may be enlightened. *And those who see may become blind*: that is, those who do not confess their blindness, may be more grievously hardened. In short, it was fulfilled: *Those who see may become blind*; the defenders of the law, the treaters of the law, the doctors of the law, the understanders of the law crucified the author of the law. O blindness! It is that which came to pass in part on Israel. That Christ might be crucified, and the fullness of the nations might enter, blindness came to pass in part on Israel. What is: *That those who do not see may see?* *That the fullness of the nations might enter, blindness came to pass in part on Israel* [^11]. The whole world lay blind, but he came, *that those who do not see may see; and those who see may become blind*. He was not known by the Jews, he was crucified by the Jews; from his blood he made eye-salve for the blind. Made harder, made blind, those who boasted that they saw the light, crucified the light. How great blindness! They killed the light: but the crucified light enlightened the blind.

**5.** Hear the one seeing, who was blind. Behold against what a cross they wickedly stumbled, who refused to confess their blindness to the physician. The law remained to them. What does the law do without grace? O wretches! what did the law do without grace? What does the earth do without the spittle of Christ? What does the law do without grace, except make men more guilty? Why? Because hearers of the law and not doers, and through this sinners, transgressors. The son of the host of the man of God died, and a staff was sent through the servant, and was set upon his face, and he did not come to life [^12]. What does the law do without grace? The Apostle, now seeing, now enlightened from blind, what does he say? *For if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would altogether be from the law*. Attend: let us answer, and say: What is it that he said? *If a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would altogether be from the law*. If it could not give life, why was it given? Following, he added: *But Scripture shut up all things under sin, that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe* [^13]. The promise of enlightenment, the promise of love from the faith of Jesus Christ, that it might be given to those who believe, that Scripture, that is, the law, shut up all things under sin. What is: *shut up all things under sin?* *I did not know desire, unless the law said: You shall not desire* [^14]. What is: *Scripture shut up all things under sin?* It made the offender even a transgressor. For it could not heal the sinner. *It shut up all things under sin*: but in what hope? In hope of grace, in hope of mercy. You received the law; you wanted to do it, you could not: you fell from pride, you saw your languor. Run to the physician, wash your face. Long for Christ, confess Christ, believe in Christ: the Spirit comes to the letter, and you will be safe. For if you take away the Spirit from the letter, *the letter kills*: if it kills, where is hope? *But the Spirit gives life* [^15].

**6.** Let Gehazi, then, the servant of Elisha, receive the staff, just as the servant of God Moses received the law. Let him receive the staff, let him receive it, run, go before, go ahead, set the staff upon the face of the dead boy. And it was done: he received it, ran, set the staff upon the face of the dead boy. But for what? To whom the staff? *If a law had been given which could give life*, the boy would have been raised by the staff: but because *Scripture shut up all things under sin*, he still lies dead. But why *did it shut up all things under sin? That the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*. Let Elisha, then, come, who sent the staff through the servant to convict the dead one; let him himself come, let him himself come, let him himself enter the lodging of the woman, ascend to the boy, find him dead, conform himself to the members of the dead one, not dead but living. For this he did: he set face upon face, eyes upon eyes, hands upon hands, footsteps upon feet, he constrained himself, he contracted himself, though he was great he made himself small [^16]. He contracted himself; so to speak, he made himself less. Because: *Though he was in the form of God, he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant* [^17]. What is it: He conformed himself living to the dead one? You ask what it is? Hear the Apostle: *God sent his Son*. What is it: He conformed himself to the dead one? Let him say this, let it follow, let him say it himself: *In the likeness of sinful flesh* [^18]. This is for the living one to conform himself to the dead one: to come to us in the likeness of sinful flesh, not in sinful flesh. The dead one lay in sinful flesh, the likeness of sinful flesh conformed itself to him. For he died who had no reason why he should die. He died, in the dead alone free; since all the flesh of men was surely the flesh of sin. How would it come to life again, unless he who had no sin, conforming himself to the dead one, came in the likeness of sinful flesh? O Lord Jesus, who suffered for us, not for yourself, having no fault, and sustaining the penalty, that you might loose both the fault and the penalty.
