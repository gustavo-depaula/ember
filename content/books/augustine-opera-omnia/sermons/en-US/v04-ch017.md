# Sermon 107a

**1.** The Lord Jesus, the bestower of love, is the accuser of greed. For he wants to root out the bad tree and plant the good tree. From love of the world no good fruit springs. From the love of God no bad fruit. These are the two trees of which the Lord himself, speaking, says: *A good tree does not bear bad fruits. But a bad tree bears bad fruits* [^1]. Our sermon, then, when it is from the Lord God, is an axe to the root of the bad tree [^2]. That very sermon from the holy Gospel which sounded forth struck the bad trees. It prunes, it does not cut off. For what he who made you did not want you to have, know that it is not expedient for you. The Lord does not want us to have love of the world. Let no one, then, say: I seek my own, not another's. *Beware of all greed* [^3]. If you love too much your own things which can perish, you will truly lose your own things which cannot perish. I, you say, neither want to lose my own nor to take another's. This is the excuse of a certain greed, it is not the glory of love. Of love it was said: *It does not seek its own, but the things of others* [^4]. It does not seek its own advantages, it seeks the salvation of the brothers. For this man too who appealed to the Lord, if you attended, if you noticed, was seeking his own, not another's. For his brother had taken away the whole and had not given his brother the share owed. He saw the Lord just, for he could not find a better judge, and he appealed to him and said: *Lord, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me* [^5]. What more just? Let him take his share, give me mine. Neither I the whole, nor he the whole, because we are brothers. And the very thing they were seeking to divide they would always have whole if they lived in concord. Everything that is divided is diminished. If they were in concord in their house as they were while their father lived, each would even possess the whole. For example: if they had two farms, both would belong to both, and to one asking about each, they would say it is theirs. Whose is the farm? if you asked one of them: Ours, he would answer. Likewise the other, if you asked whose it was, similarly: Ours. But if each had taken one, the possession would be diminished, the answer changed. Then if it were asked whose this farm is, he would answer: Mine. Whose is that? My brother's. You did not acquire one, but lost one because you divided. Since, then, he seemed to himself to have a just greed because he was seeking his own share, was not desiring another's, as though presuming on the justice of his cause, he appealed to the just judge. But what did the just judge say to him? *Man*—for you do not relish the things of God, but the things of men—*who set me a divider of the inheritance between you* [^6]? He denied what was asked, but greater is what he gave than what he denied. The one asked for judgment about possessing the inheritance. The other gave counsel about not having greed. Why do you seek a farm? Why do you seek land? Why do you seek a share? If you have not greed, you will have the whole. See, you greedy ones, the one who had not greed, who said: *As having nothing, and possessing all things* [^7]. You, then, he says, ask of me that your brother give you the share of your inheritance. *I*, he says, *say to you: Beware of all greed* [^8]. You think yourself to beware of greed for another's property. I say to you: *Beware of all greed*. And you want too much to love your own, and on account of your own property, to put down your heart from heaven, and, wanting to lay up treasure on earth, you seek to oppress your soul; for the soul has its riches as the body has its own.

**2.** In the end a certain rich man is set forth as a warning to beware of all greed. What is, of all? Even of those things which are called yours. A certain rich man is set forth, whose *region had yielded* [^9], that is, his possessions in the region; abundant fruits came forth: for "yield" means prosperity. And he thought within himself, saying, what you have heard when the Gospel was read: *What shall I do, where shall I gather my fruits* [^10]? He had nowhere to put them. He had too much. He was cramped by plenty, not by want. How wretched he was, whom not want but abundance disturbed! As though he did not have anywhere to put it from where he would lose nothing! And he seemed to himself to have found great counsel: *I have found*, he says, *what I shall do. I will pull down my old storehouses and make new, larger ones, and fill them, and I will say to my soul: Soul, you have many goods for a long time. Rest, eat, drink, feast. But God said to him: Fool* [^11]—you seem wise to yourself because you found counsel for casting down small storehouses and making larger ones. You are a fool where you seem wise to yourself. Why did you do this, to say to your soul: You have many goods for so long a time? *This night your soul is required of you* [^12]. Where is the long time? *This night your soul is required*. These things you have prepared, whose will they be? Are you not vainly troubled? You lay up treasure and do not know for whom you gather it [^13]. He had nowhere to put it! And where were the poor? What the cramping of your storehouses no longer held, let your brother receive, let your Lord receive, who says: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me* [^14]. You commit it to storehouses, and will you not lose it? You migrate to heaven, and will you lose it? You have nowhere to put it? Dole it out, and await what is repaid you. You put it in the hand of the poor, you receive it from the hand of the rich one. The one who has not where—who gave it to you? He who gave it to you wants to receive something from what he gave. He who made you is in need from you. If for your good he is in need from you, give from what you have. For you have earthly fruits; do you have eternal life? How great a possession it is! At how cheap a price it is to be had! Do you want to know at how cheap a price it is to be had? Such is the possession, such a possession is eternal life, to which when you have come (O fool, who reckon on earth and will lose heaven!), such is that possession, to which when you have come, you cannot migrate from there, but will even possess it perpetually and without any end. How great it is, you see. At how cheap a price it is to be had, attend. It is worth as much as your storehouses do not hold, what they cannot hold, what overflows, on account of which you want to make them larger. But what is it? How great is the price of the possession? If it were given to the poor as porters? For you know and see that those to whom you give on earth walk about. What you give they carry to heaven, and when they have carried it to heaven, you do not receive back what you give. For in exchange for earthly things you are to receive heavenly, for mortal immortal, for temporal eternal. If you lent at interest and for so much silver received so much gold—for example, for a pound of silver a pound of gold—what would be richer than you? Would you contain yourself for joy, when it was permitted you to come to such interest? What is this interest? See what you give, what you receive. You give what here you let go, you receive what you never lose. For you give that from which you do not live (for therefore the Lord said, if you noticed, in that very reading of the Gospel: *A man's life does not consist in abundance from the things he has* [^15]), that you may receive that from which you always live. The riches of the flesh are gold, silver, grain, wine and oil, estates, possessions; these are the riches of the flesh. The flesh itself, how much does it have of them, when the belly has been filled? You see that the rest lie superfluous. If you were forced to eat as much as you have, would you not be forced to expire?

**3.** *Beware, then*, brothers, *of all greed* [^16]. On little is life lived on this earth, and great eternal life is bought at little. Did Zacchaeus seem to have bought it more dearly? For *he was very rich, a chief of the publicans* [^17]. And when the Lord entered his house, salvation was thus made there: *Half*, he says, *of my goods I give to the poor* [^18]. From much he bought much. Why the other half? *If I have taken anything from anyone, I restore fourfold* [^19]. Behold why the other half was kept, not that it might be covetously possessed, but that what was owed might be restored. From much he bought who had many riches and gave half to the poor. Whatever he gave, what is it? How many are the riches of any powerful man? What is the whole earth? What is earth and sea? Attend to heaven, attend to the stars, attend to the whole creation. If you despise a few things, you will possess the Creator himself for an inheritance. Your Lord says this to you: *Beware of all greed*. Beware of acquiring earthly things, and I will fill you. Answer and say: With what will you fill me? For this you were seeking, when you despised the few things, to be filled with greater things. Because you gave something of your fruits and heard from your Lord: I will fill you, you will say: He is going to fill my house with gold, with silver. I will fill you. You ask that I fill your house. I will fill you if you are my house. Acknowledge and love the one who made you, and he will fill you, not with anything of his, but with himself. You will have God, you will be full of God. These are the great riches of the soul. The riches of the body are superfluous, because our body seeks little to get by on. The riches of the soul are not superfluous. As much as God will give you, as much devotion as God will bestow on you, as much love as he will bestow, as much justice as he will bestow, as much chastity as he will bestow, whatever he will bestow of himself, cannot be superfluous. Your inner riches are great. What are they called? God. O poor man, then, do you have nothing, if you have God? O rich man, then, do you have anything if you do not have God?

**4.** Therefore, to return to the words of the Lord, let us beware of all greed. Of all? you will say. It is my property. He will say: Of all. For has it been said to you not to have your own property? Have it, but without greed. For so you will have it. But if you covet it, you will be had, you will not have. Do not love money, if you want to have money. For if you do not love it, you will have money. If you love it, money will have you. For you will not be the master of money, but the servant, and since a servant, where it drags you, you will follow. Are you not a servant when you are dragged by greed of it? Does its love not rouse you from sleep? If you were the servant of a man, perhaps he would allow you to sleep. If you do not have money and are greedy, out of that very greed you keep awake to have it. If you have it, out of fear you keep awake lest you lose it. You fear even lest through it you yourself perish: I think, when you had less, you slept secure.

**5.** *Beware of all greed* [^20]. May it profit you, brothers, that you are poor. Do not want to be rich. Let God suffice for you, because he does not desert you. He thought of you before you existed, and does he not think that you may live? You have already believed in him, praised him, hoped in him, and will what he knows to be necessary for us be lacking to you? What he gives to strangers, will he refuse to his own? Is he going to refuse his praisers, who gives to his blasphemers? When you have him, reckon that you have all things. *For your Father knows*, the Lord himself says in the Gospel, *what is necessary for you, before you ask him* [^21]. And of the things of the body he spoke thus: *Seek first the kingdom of God, and the righteousness of God, and all these things will be added to you* [^22]. But sometimes the just man suffers hunger, and sees the unjust man belching from indigestion. Do not wonder. The one is tested, the other condemned. He is tested who in that very want praises God. He is condemned who through that very plenty offends God. Scripture says: *The rich and the poor have met one another, but the Lord made them both* [^23]. Where have they met one another? On a certain way. What is that way? This life. Here the rich and the poor have met one another, because a rich man is born, a poor man is born. They have met one another. They have seen each other on the way. Both walk the way, one laden, the other relieved. But he who is relieved is hungry, he who is laden groans. Let him who is laden relieve himself. Let him give something of it to the one who met him, and neither shall the one be hungry, nor the other groan, and both arrive. Whence comes your groaning, O rich man? Because you have nowhere to put it? There is a place to put it. I do not want you to groan. Look on the hungry, and you have a place to put it. Do you fear lest you lose it? Then all the more you do not lose it.

**6.** Something that happened, both very pleasant and to be told to Your Charity. A certain devout man of middling means sold a gold coin for the needs of his house. And because he was devout, he took a hundred small coins from the top of the price and doled them out to the poor, so as to put the rest into his house for his needs. That he might be tested, a thief was let in upon him, and he lost the whole price of the gold coin. The devil did this so that he might repent of having given something to the poor, and might say: O Lord, none please you but those who do evil. Men do evil and acquire. I did good and lost everything. But he did not say this; for he was foursquare, he stood even when turned. So when he had lost the whole price of the gold coin from which he had given a hundred small coins to the poor: Wretched me, he said, that I did not give it all! What I gave, I did not lose; this I could lose, what I did not give. There came to his mind what he had heard from the Gospel or what he had read, and believed. For it is the very counsel of our Lord. Recall and see. *Do not lay up for yourselves treasure on earth, where moth and rust destroy, and where thieves dig through and steal. But lay up for yourselves treasure in heaven, where the thief does not approach nor moth destroy. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also* [^24]. The thief could take his money, his heart he could not put down from heaven.

**7.** Therefore have what you have, so as to provide for the needy. For when the Lord Christ had said: *Fool, this night your soul will be taken from you; these things you have prepared, whose will they be* [^25]? to a man not seizing another's, but immoderately loving his own, he added and said: *So is everyone who lays up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God* [^26]. Do you want to be rich toward God? Give to God. Give not from great means but from your own will. For not, if you give a little from the little you have, is the little you gave little accepted. God does not weigh means but wills. Recall that widow, brothers. You have heard Zacchaeus: *Half my goods I give to the poor* [^27]. He gave much from much, and bought that possession of the kingdom of heaven, as it were, at a great price. Considered as to how great it is, whatever you give is cheap to it. Yet he seems to have given much, because he was very rich. Recall that poor widow, who was carrying two mites [^28], and those who were present watched the rich casting in much into the treasury, and attended to the great things being put in. She came in, put in two mites. Who deigned even to see her? But the Lord so saw her that he saw her alone, and he himself commended her to those who did not see, that is, that she might be attended to who was not even seen. Do you see, he says, this widow, and then they attended to her, she put in more, he says, *into the gifts of God than those rich men* [^29], great things from much. They were gazing at the huge gifts of the rich and praising them. When did they see those two coins, even if they saw the widow herself afterward? She put in more, he says, *into the gifts of God than those rich men. For they* put in much from much, but she put in the whole that she had. She had much, because she had God in her heart. It is more to have God in the soul than gold in the purse. Who put in more than she who left herself nothing?

**8.** Yet, dearly beloved, attend, because we were speaking of buying that heavenly possession. Was it worth as much as Zacchaeus bought it for, and not worth as much as that widow had? Its price was what Zacchaeus gave, its price was also the two small coins. To one comparing half of Zacchaeus's goods and the two mites, there is no comparison. Compare the will of Zacchaeus and the will of the widow. There you will find them unequal, here equal. Let not your heart, then, be saddened, when from your little you give a little. What is little to the poor man is much to him who knows the poor and the rich. For God knows with what mind you give, with what will you give. Only, beware of all greed, and give whatever out of love. Do we find anything cheaper than two mites? A cup of cold water. He who *gives*, he says, *a cup of cold water to one of the least of mine, amen I say to you, will not lose his reward* [^30]. Now how much is a cup of cold water worth, for which not even wood was doled out to heat it? Therefore he did not say only a cup of water, but added "cold." You see how great it is, and how great is that which it bought. Is there anything cheaper than a cup of cold water? What? Nothing. If, then, there is, how is it nothing? It is both nothing and is nothing. There is what you give: but *peace on earth to men of good will* [^31]. This was bought at this price by the Patriarchs and remained for sale for the Prophets. Was it for that reason not kept for others? The Prophets bought it: they left it to be bought by the Apostles. The Apostles bought it: they kept it to be bought by the martyrs. The martyrs bought it, and it is whole for us to buy. Let us love and we buy. There is no reason for you to say: It is worth so much and I do not have it. Let me take so great a loan and from it pay it, as men are wont to say when they fix a certain price for a house or a possession for sale. Do not go round your chests, return to your conscience, where you find the price of the possession. If there be there faith, hope, love, give and you buy, and when you have given you do not lose. For not, having given faith, do you lose faith, or, having doled out hope, do you lose hope, or, when you have given love, will you remain without love. They are fountains: by flowing they abound.

**9.** Behold, you are poor and you build a church. Whence this, if you are poor, except that you are rich in soul? Act, then, with the Lord's help, so that you may complete it. For *God loves a cheerful giver* [^32]. When you give cheerfully, it is reckoned to you. But when you give with sadness, you both have nothing outside, and within, where there is sadness, there is anguish. The coin perishes, that is not bought, since good will buys it. Give little, give much, let the will be good and you buy. And in that you build a church, by God's favor, you build for yourselves. It is another thing that you give to the poor: and they pass and come. This you build for yourselves. It is the house of your prayers, where you may gather, where you may do the things that are divine, where you may say hymns and praises to God, where you may pour out prayers, where you may receive the sacraments. You see that it is the house of your prayers. Do you want to make it? Be yourselves the house of God, and that house is made. Amen.
