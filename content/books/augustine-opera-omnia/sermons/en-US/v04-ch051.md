# Sermon 132a

**1.** What kind of voice of the Lord have you heard inviting us? Who invited, whom did he invite, and what did he prepare? The Lord invited servants, and prepared for them as food himself. Who would dare to eat his Lord? And yet he says: *He who eats me, lives because of me* [^1]. When Christ is eaten, life is eaten. Nor is he killed that he may be eaten, but he gives life to the dead. When he is eaten, he refreshes, but does not fail. Let us not fear, then, brothers, to eat this bread, lest perhaps we finish it, and afterward not find what to eat. Let Christ be eaten: he lives when eaten, because he rose again slain. Nor when we eat do we make parts of him. And indeed in the sacrament it is so done, and the faithful know how they eat the flesh of Christ; each one receives his part, whence too the grace itself is called parts. By parts it is eaten, and remains whole entire; by parts it is eaten in the sacrament, and remains whole entire in heaven, remains whole entire in your heart. For he was whole with the Father, when he came into the Virgin; he filled her, nor did he depart from him. He came into the flesh, that men might eat him; and he remained whole with the Father, that he might feed the angels. For that you may know, brothers (both you who know and you who do not know, you ought to know), when Christ was made man, *man ate the bread of angels* [^2]. Whence, how, by what way, by what merits, by what dignity would man eat the bread of angels, unless the creator of the angels became man? Securely, then, let us eat: that which we eat is not finished; and let us eat, lest we be finished. What is it, to eat Christ? It is not only this, to receive his body in the sacrament; for many receive it unworthily, of whom the Apostle says: *He who eats the bread, and drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself* [^3].

**2.** But how is Christ to be eaten? As he himself says: *He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, abides in me and I in him* [^4]. If, therefore, he abides in me, and I in him, then he eats, then he drinks; but he who does not abide in me, nor I in him, even if he receives the sacrament, acquires a great torment. *He who abides in me*, then, which he says, in another place he himself says: *He who does my commandments, abides in me, and I in him* [^5]. See, then, brothers, that if you are separated, you who are faithful, from the body of the Lord, it is to be feared lest you die of hunger. For he himself said: *He who does not eat my flesh, nor drinks my blood, will not have life in him* [^6]. If, then, you are separated, so as not to eat the body and blood of the Lord, it is to be feared lest you die; but if you receive it unworthily, and drink unworthily, it is to be feared lest you eat and drink judgment. Great straits are upon you. Live well, and the straits are widened. Do not promise yourselves life while living badly: what God does not promise, a man deceives himself, when he promises it to himself. An evil witness, you promise yourself what the truth denies you. The truth says: If you live badly, you will die forever; and you say to yourself: Do I both live badly, and live forever with Christ? How can it be, that the truth lies, and you speak the truth? *Every man is a liar* [^7]. Therefore you cannot live well, unless he helps, unless he gives, unless he grants it. Therefore pray, and eat. Pray, and you will be freed from these straits. For he will fill you, both in acting well, and in living well. Let your conscience be examined. Your mouth will be filled with the praise of God and with exultation; and you will say to him, freed from great straits: *You have freed my steps beneath me, and my footsteps were not weakened* [^8].
