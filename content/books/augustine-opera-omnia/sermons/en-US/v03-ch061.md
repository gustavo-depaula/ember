# Sermon 94: On the Words of the Gospel, Matthew 25:24-30, Where the Lazy Servant, Who Refused to Pay Out the Talent He Had Received, Is Condemned

**1.** My lord brothers and fellow bishops have indeed deigned to visit and cheer us by their presence: but I do not know why they are unwilling to help me, weary as I am. I have said this to your Charity in their hearing, so that in a way your hearing may intercede for me with them, that when I ask them, they too may give a sermon. Let them pay out what they have received, let them deign to work rather than to excuse themselves. But from me, weary and scarcely speaking, gladly receive a little. For we have also a little book about the benefits of God given through the holy Martyr; let us listen to it together more gladly. What, then, is it? what shall I say to you? You heard in the Gospel both the merit of the good servants and the punishment of the bad. And the whole malice of that reprobated and gravely condemned servant was this, that he refused to pay out[^1]. He kept intact what he received: but his Lord was seeking profits. God is greedy for our salvation. If he who did not pay out is so condemned, what ought those to expect who lose? We, then, are stewards, we pay out, you receive. We seek profits: live well. For these are the profits of our payment. But do not think that paying out does not pertain to you too. You cannot pay out from this higher place, but you can wherever you are. Where Christ is reproached, defend him: answer the murmurers, rebuke the blasphemers, estrange yourselves from their fellowship. So you pay out, if you gain anyone. Take our part in your homes. A bishop is so called because he superintends, because by attending he cares. Each one, then, in his own house, if he is the head of his house, ought to have the office of bishop pertain to him—how those who are his believe, lest any of them run into heresy, neither wife, nor son, nor daughter, nor servant himself, because he was bought at so great a price. Apostolic discipline set the master over the servant, and made the servant subject to the master[^2]; yet Christ gave one price for both. Do not despise your least ones, with all vigilance procure the salvation of your household. If you do these things, you pay out: you will not be lazy servants, you will not fear that so detestable a condemnation.

[^1]: See Mt 25:25-30
[^2]: See Eph 6:5-9; Col 3:22-4:1
