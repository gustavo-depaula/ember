# Sermon 396: At the Taking Up of a Bishop

**1.** You indeed, brothers, seek a comforter: but we too must be comforted; and our comfort is no man, but he who makes man; for he who made us remakes us, and he who created us re-creates us. We cannot, through weakness, but be saddened; but through hope we ought to be comforted. We want all good people to live longer with us, and we do not want to be abandoned by our companions in this most harsh life; but those who went before us, who lived well, exhort us by their example, that whether we live here long, or go hence quickly, we should so live that we may come to them. Because to live here long is nothing else than to endure troubles long. But to live with God, and in God's presence, is to live without any trouble, and without any fear lest the happiness perish which has no end. Nor ought we to think that your bishop, our brother, went hence quickly and lived too little. For rightly there is no living too little, where, when much is said, there is no ending. For here even what is much, when it has been ended, will be reckoned as nothing. Nor yet did he live little here, if we think of his works, and do not count his years. How many others perhaps did not, in many years, do half of what he fulfilled in a few years? It would be nothing else, then, to want to keep him here, than to envy his happiness.

**2.** For in this we have sorrow over a man, as men do. What then shall we do, that we may not be men? As men, then, we are humanly saddened at the departure of a man: but how did we hear the divine reading, that *being made perfect in a short time, he fulfilled long times*[^1]? Let us, then, there reckon the times, as a day is reckoned. Whatever, therefore, he did with you by exhorting, by speaking, by setting himself forth to be imitated, for the praising and worshiping of God, hold it; and you will be his most adorned memorial. For it is no great thing to him to be laid in marble tombs; but to be laid in your hearts. Let him live buried in living tombs. For his burial is your memory. With God he lives, that he may be happy; among you let him live, that you may be happy. We could perhaps exhort you to faithful prudence with many words, were we too not scarcely allowed to speak for human grief. Accordingly, because God granted us to be present with the dying man at that time; since he granted us to escort his funeral—the escorting which is owed to love adds nothing to his happiness—he granted also that we should see your holiness, and address you, that, according to our small measure, we might comfort you who are to be comforted: whatever grief does not permit us to say, supply it by thinking; and our soul, in the remembrance of so great a man, though it has human grief, does not have unbelieving despair.

[^1]: Wis 4:13
