# Sermon 61/A: On What the Lord Says: "Ask and You Will Receive"

**1.** Since the Lord willed that I not depart from here a debtor, I recognize the time for repaying what I promised. And therefore I ordered that very chapter of the Gospel to be read today too, which was read when I made my excuse, so that what I then withdrew by necessity, I may now repay by love. And indeed for considering and treating all the words of that same chapter, neither does the time suffice, nor are our powers sufficient for it. Yet what is most necessary to say from it, the Lord granting, we say what we can.

**2.** The Lord exhorted us to ask, seek, knock, saying: *Ask and you will receive, seek and you will find, knock and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, he who seeks will find, and to the one who knocks it will be opened*[^1]. These first raise this question, which must be resolved according to our powers. We know that many ask and do not receive, seek and do not find, knock and it is not opened to them. How, then, does everyone who asks receive? For all this, although it seems to be said three times and in three words, is reduced to one petition: *Ask, seek, knock*, all this is "ask." Which we know from that conclusion, where he says: *If you, being evil, know how to give good things to your children, how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask him?*[^2] He did not say "to those who seek and knock," but he concluded all three into one, when he said, *to those who ask.*

**3.** Why, then, do many ask and not receive, if *everyone who asks receives?* Or do we err in thinking that we ask and do not receive? Besides the daily examples that we know, Scripture itself testifies that the apostle Paul asked that the angel of Satan depart from him, and did not receive it[^3]. And we find that the evil have asked and received, and the good have asked and not received. For what is worse than demons? And yet they asked for the swine and received them[^4]. And it is found that God did not do the desire of the Apostles, and fulfilled the desire of the demons. Or do we doubt that these belong to God and will reign especially with Christ, but the demons will burn forever with their prince the devil? What, then, do we say, except that: *The Lord knows who are his*[^5], and of them *everyone who asks receives?*

**4.** But still about the Apostle a scruple remains. For he himself was not outside those who are his, who said this sentence: *The Lord knows who are his.* So, all who are his ask and receive, and none of them asks and does not receive. But we ask: "What?" For those things which are asked on account of this temporal life sometimes profit, sometimes harm. And when God knows them to harm, he does not give them to his own who desire and ask, just as a physician does not give whatever a sick man asks, and out of love denies what, out of lack of love, he would grant. He hears, then, all his own unto eternal salvation. He does not hear all unto temporal craving. And therefore he does not hear unto this, that he may hear unto that. For indeed the sick man too, from whom we drew the likeness, when he asks of the physician what the physician knows to be harmful, desires health above all from the physician. The physician, then, that he may hear the sick man unto health, does not hear him unto his wish. Finally, consider the very words. When he did not receive that for which he asked the Lord three times, he said to him: *My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness*[^6]. Why do you desire of me that the thorn of the flesh, which you received lest you be exalted in your revelations, be taken from you? Surely you ask this because you do not know it profits you. Believe the physician. What he has applied is sharp, but useful; it causes pain, but brings forth health. See the end, and rejoice that it was denied, and understand what was given. What end? *Power is perfected in weakness.* Bear weakness, then, if you desire health. Bear weakness, if you desire perfection. Because *power is perfected in weakness.* For that you may know you are not forsaken, *my grace is sufficient for you.*

**5.** On account of this I first admonish your love, since I know—and we all know, and cannot dissemble it, for they strike the eyes even of the unwilling, the miracles of healings that take place here daily through the memorial of the most blessed and most glorious martyr present in this place—but without doubt some ask and do not receive. Let them not think themselves forsaken. Let them first question their own heart, whether they ask faithfully. Whoever asks faithfully, usefully receives, or usefully sometimes does not receive. When he does not heal the body, he wishes to heal the soul. Believe, then, that what he has willed is expedient for you, who has called you into the eternal kingdom. For what is this very thing you desire as a great thing? He promised you eternal life, he promised you a kingdom with the angels, he promised you rest without end. What is it that he does not give now? Is not *the salvation of men vain?*[^7] Will not all who are healed without doubt die? And when that death comes, all those past things vanish like smoke. But that life which was promised, when it comes, will surely have no end. For that he arms you, who now denies you something; for that he prepares you, for that he instructs you. But even if you have received health, since you believed and asked—for it is not asked improperly, even if sometimes it is not usefully given—receive it and use it well. For one who, when healed, begins to live in luxury, would it not be expedient for him to be sick? When, therefore, you have received temporal health, turn it to a good use, that from what he gave, he may be served who gave it. And do not prefer yourself over another who perhaps asked and did not receive, and say in your heart: "I am more faithful than he." For to this end you just now heard in the Gospel: *Do not judge, lest you be judged*[^8]. What is, *Do not judge*, except of hidden things? For who is forbidden to judge of manifest things, when Scripture says in another place: *The things that are manifest, to you. But the things that are hidden, to the Lord your God?*[^9] This is: the things that are manifest, leave to be judged by you; the things that are hidden, leave to your God. For whence do you know whether perhaps to that man who asked and did not receive, this temporal health was denied because he is stronger than you? He asked and did not receive. But what did he ask? Bodily health. Perhaps his faith is stronger than yours, and therefore you received because, if you had not received, you would have fainted. Nor have I affirmed this, but said "perhaps," lest I do what I forbid, so that I dare not pass sentence on hidden things. For sometimes one did not receive because he asked unfaithfully; sometimes he did not receive because he is stronger than you, to exercise patience in him, as we said of the Apostle. He was stronger, and yet not yet perfect, so that he heard: *Power is perfected in weakness*[^10].

**6.** We know that they—their writings proclaim it—healed the sick with a word. The apostle Paul himself said to a certain man: *Aeneas, rise, make your bed*[^11]. The man, sick for a very long time, rose at once healed, and made his bed. And yet the same one says of a certain disciple of his: *But Trophimus I left sick at Miletus*[^12]. You heal one unknown to you, to whom you come, and leave sick a disciple from whom you depart? What does he say of Epaphroditus? *He was sad*, he says, *because you had heard that he had been sick, for indeed he was sick even close to death*[^13]. What great thing was it for the apostle Paul to heal him too with a word, and not to allow him to come close to death? *But God*, he says, *had mercy on him, and not only on him, but also on me, lest I should have sorrow upon sorrow*[^14]. It seems that he wished him to become well. If he wished it, surely he also prayed, and yet praying he did not obtain. Yet, when he scarcely obtained it, he gave thanks, because at least with difficulty he obtained it. To blessed Timothy he gives counsel of medicine. He raised an old paralytic with a word; the stomach of his dearest and unanimous and, as he himself calls him, most genuine disciple he could not heal with that same word. And yet he says: *Do not drink water still, but use a little wine on account of your stomach and your frequent infirmities*[^15]. Let these things suffice on what I wished to admonish your love about, that you neither mock those nor think ill of those who perhaps have asked and not received, nor faint, you who perhaps ask and have not received, nor proudly prefer yourselves to those who do not receive, you who ask and receive.

**7.** What, then, is that which absolutely all who are his ask and receive, seek and find, knock and it will be opened to them? For unless there were something such, the Truth would not say: *For everyone who asks receives*[^16]. What is this? Where is it found? Let us seek in the very chapter, lest perhaps there we find what we seek. There you have it, plainly there you have it. Let us acknowledge ourselves, where we hear that we are evil. For he says: *You, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children. How much more will your heavenly Father give good things to those who ask him?*[^17] He called our Father good, and us evil. What, then? Is that supreme good God the father of the evil? Yet we cannot deny it, although it seems absurd. The Truth speaks: *If you, being evil*—why do we contradict the Truth?—*know how to give good gifts to your children.* We give those good things to our children which nevertheless do not make them good. If, then, we can give good things which do not make good, yet are good, what remains for us to ask of God, except the good things by which we may be good? For we were rebuked, when it was said: *being evil.* And yet our supreme good Father, who is in heaven, was shown to us. Are we not ashamed to be evil under such a Father? Or indeed would that Father wish to be the father of the evil, if he wished to leave us evil, if he wished us to remain evil forever? If, then, we are evil, and have a good Father, let us ask this, let us seek this, for this let us knock, that the good one may make us good, lest he have evil sons. And to what extent is anyone now made good? To what extent? However much he advances, he is going to fight against cravings, he is going to fight against lusts. However much he advances, even if anyone has peace from the things that are within or that are without, he will have war in himself, he is going to wage a contest in himself, nor will he cease to wage the contest, with him watching who is ready to help the one laboring and to crown the one conquering. But when all dissension and all the quarrel that we are has passed; for our languor and our quarrel is no other nature contrary to us, but in a way our accustomed nature is our languor. We were not so in paradise; nothing in us fought against us. We forsook him with whom we had peace, and began to have war ourselves with ourselves. And this is our misery. And it is a great thing in this life, in this war, not to be conquered. For we cannot be without an enemy in this life. But the last life will be, when we shall have no enemy, neither without nor within: *for the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^18]. Then, blessed, we shall dwell in the house of God, and forever and ever we shall praise him[^19]. Amen.
