# Sermon 328: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1. 1.** In the Psalm we said to the Lord our God: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^1]. Precious is the death of the holy martyrs; because their price is the blood of their Lord. For he suffered by his own passion, because they were to suffer after him. He himself went before, and many followed. For the way was very rough; but he made it smooth, when he himself passed before all. Therefore the rest did not fear to pass, because he passed first. For he died, and terrified his disciples. He rose again, and took away their fear, and gave them love. For when Christ died, the disciples trembled, and thought that he had perished. When they followed, there see the grace of God. Then the thief believed, when the disciples trembled. For there was one thief on the cross with him, and he so believed in him that he said: *Lord, keep me in mind, when you come into your kingdom*[^2]. Who taught him, but he who hung beside him? For he was fixed at his side: but he dwelt in his heart.

**1. 2.** But in this psalm, where we said: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints:* there is written also what you heard: *I said in my ecstasy, Every man is a liar*[^3].

**2. 2.** What do we say, brothers? *Every man is a liar.* Were the martyrs then liars too? But if the martyrs were truthful; how is it true: *Every man is a liar?* Scripture says: *Every man is a liar.* If we say, The martyrs were truthful; we make Scripture a liar. But if Scripture said truly, that *every man is a liar;* then the martyrs were liars. How then can we show both Scripture truthful and the martyrs truthful? Or perhaps the martyrs were not men? But if they were men, how is it true: *Every man is a liar?* What then shall we do? Shall we labor to show you that both Scripture is true, and *every man is a liar;* and the martyrs were truthful, because they died for the truth? For they are martyrs for this reason, because they suffered for the truth. For "martyr" is a Greek word, and in Latin it is called "witness." If, then, they were true witnesses, they spoke true things; and by speaking true things they received crowns. But if they were false witnesses, which far be it, they went not to crowns, but to punishments: because it is written: *The false witness will not be unpunished*[^4]. So let us show them truthful. They have already shown themselves so, when they were even willing to die for the truth. How then is Scripture truthful, which says: *Every man is a liar?* Let us ask our Lord Jesus Christ; and he himself will resolve this question for us. From where does he have to resolve it for us? From the Gospel, of which we were just speaking, when it was being read to you.

**3. 3.** For you heard, when the Gospel was read, that the Lord Jesus said to the martyrs: *When they hand you over, do not think what you may speak, or what you may say, for it will be given you in that hour what you may speak. For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father, who speaks in you*[^5]. Because if you speak, you say a lie: because *every man is a liar.* So the Lord himself saw that *every man is a liar,* and gave the martyrs his Spirit; that not they themselves should speak, but his Spirit: that they might not be liars, but might be truthful. Behold why they were truthful; because not they themselves were speaking, but his Spirit. And now what we speak to you, if we speak of our own, we say a lie. But if the things we say to you are of the Spirit of God, therefore they are true. And do you make progress: do not want to speak of your own, if you want to speak true things; that you may not remain men who are liars, but may be truthful sons of God.

**4. 4.** All heretics too suffer for falsehood, not for the truth: because they lie against Christ himself. All pagans, the impious, whatever they suffer, they suffer for falsehood. Let no one, then, exalt himself and glory in his suffering, but first show the truth of his tongue. You show the punishment, I ask the cause. You say, I have suffered: I say why you suffered. For if we attend to the sufferings alone, robbers too are crowned. Surely that man does not dare to say: I suffered such and such things? Why? Because it is said to him: For your evil deeds; you had a bad punishment, because you first had a bad cause. If glorying is to be from suffering; the devil too can glory. See how much he suffers, whose temples everywhere are overturned, whose idols everywhere are broken, whose priests and possessed everywhere are struck. Surely he cannot say: I too am a martyr, because I suffer so much! So let the man of God first choose for himself the cause, and securely approach the punishment. Because if in a good cause he approaches the punishment, after the punishment he will receive also the crown.

**5. 5.** So *the righteous man will be in everlasting remembrance, and will not fear the evil report*[^6]. For there comes the judge of all the living and the dead, as we read in the Gospel. And it is true, since those things which we now see were not, when they were said to be future. That you now see the name of Christ preached among all nations, men turned to one God, idols abandoned, demons abandoned, temples overturned, images broken; all these things were not yet, yet they were being said, and now are seen. In what Letters, then, are these things written which we now see (but they were written then, when they were not seen, but promised as future), in those same Letters we read what has not yet come. For the day of judgment has not yet come, the resurrection of the dead has not yet come, he has not yet come to judge, who had first come to be judged. Judged unjustly, about to judge justly. Deferring his power, when he wishes to show his patience. So he is to come, and as he promised that he would come with his angels, so he will come and appear in brightness to all, even those rising again.

**6. 5.** For each one is to rise again with his own cause. For such as he now is when he dies, and is received into the prison, such he comes forth to the judge. Now he needs to set his cause in order, shut up he cannot. Those, then, who have good causes are received into rest: but those who have bad causes are received into punishments. But they are to suffer greater punishments, when they have risen again: in comparison with which those are such which evil men who are dead suffer, as are the dreams of men who are tortured in their sleep. For their souls suffer, the flesh does not suffer. But the torment is greater, if one is tortured awake.

**7. 5.** So when all have risen again, and appeared before the just judge, as he himself foretold, he will separate them as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats: he will set the goats at the left, but the sheep at the right. And he will say to those who are at the right: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom, which has been prepared for you from the origin of the world*[^7]. At this voice those at the right rejoice, the righteous rejoice. But to those who are at the left he is to say: *Go into the eternal fire with the devil and his angels*[^8]. From this evil report the righteous man will not fear.

**7. 6.** So, though they have not yet received their fruits, the holy martyrs are now blessed, since their souls are with Christ. But what is prepared for them in the resurrection, who can unfold in words? *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love him*[^9]. If no one can unfold in words the great goods which the good faithful are to receive; not without cause are such rewards prepared for those who contended even to blood for the truth. The world did not entice them, terror did not break them, torments did not conquer them, flatteries did not deceive them. Their very bodies will have great adornments, in which they suffered great torments.

**8. 6.** But let us, if we love them, imitate them, so that first we choose a good cause, and for the good cause bear with an even mind whatever is troublesome in this world. For many did not choose the cause, and for a bad cause died bad. They lost patience because they did not hold wisdom. For it is not the punishment that makes the martyr, but the cause. For if the punishment makes the martyr, then the thief too, when he is killed, is a martyr. Do you want to know that it is not the punishment that makes the martyr, but the cause? Attend to those three crosses where the Lord was crucified in the middle of two thieves. The punishment was equal, but the cause separated those whom the punishment joined. One of those thieves, while he hung, believed in the Lord Jesus Christ. And he first said to his fellow—for the other thief, his fellow, insulted the Lord Jesus Christ and said to him: *Free yourself, if you are the Son of God*—and that other thief: *Do you not fear God?* he said to his fellow, *we suffer these things for our evil deeds, but this man is the holy one of God*[^10]. O confessing, for if he confessed, he was not hanging without cause! Then he said to the Lord Christ himself: *Lord, keep me in mind when you come into your kingdom*[^11]. What faith! Whom he saw crucified, he hoped would also reign. The thief did not despise the common punishment in Christ. He saw him dying like himself, and hoped he would reign over himself. Great this thief! He did violence and seized the kingdom of heaven. Where did he learn it? He was robbing in the pass, he was led to the judge, he received the sentence, from the pass to the judge, from the judge to the cross. Where did he learn what he said, except that the master who taught was beside him? For the Lord Christ, the master of all, hung at his side and taught in his breast. Why have I said these things, brothers? Because it is not the punishment that makes the martyr, but the cause. There were three crosses. The punishment equal, but the cause unequal. One to be condemned, the other to be saved, in the middle the condemner and the Savior. He punishes one, he frees the other. That cross was a tribunal.

**9. 6.** So, brothers, let us strive, while we live, to hold the true faith, to be in the true Church of God, to lead a good life, if we love the martyrs, that we may be able to imitate them, who had a good cause. But let no one say: I cannot be a martyr because there is no persecution now. The trials do not cease. The fight is on, and the crown is prepared. When, perhaps? Behold, to recall something—for it is a long matter to relate all the things in which the Christian soul is tried and, with God's favor, conquers and gains a great victory, no one seeing, shut up in the body; it fights in the heart, it is crowned in the heart, but by him who sees in the heart—behold, then, to say something: perhaps one of you falls sick. As human cases go, how many are in danger! And they come to him who lies in bed and say to him, or make for him, amulets or charms, I know not what, and he is tried and it is said to him: "Let this and that be done." All who do these things perish with the devil, because all these things are not the sacraments of angels but the contrivances of demons. Whoever, then, despises these things, and hears at some time: "Unless you do these things you will die," but answers him: "It is better for me to die than to do this," lies in bed and carries on a martyrdom. In bed, weary and worn out by fevers, he cannot move himself, and he wrestles. He does not move his limbs, and with the arms of faith he chokes the lion of whom the apostle Peter says: *Do you not know that your adversary the devil goes about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour?*[^12] He described the devil as a roaring lion going about and seeking to take something from the sheepfold or to harm it. He does not cease. Never until the end does he abstain from his plots. If, then, our adversary does not sleep, we fight daily. And we do not see our very adversary, and we conquer. Why do we not see him? Because we feel within and tame the thing by which he wants to conquer us. You do not see your enemy the devil, but you feel your avarice in you. You do not see the devil your enemy, but you feel your lust in you. You do not see the devil your enemy, but you feel your anger. Conquer what you feel within, and those who lie in wait outside are conquered. This, then, is to love the martyrs, this is to celebrate the days of the martyrs with devout godliness, not to gorge oneself with wine but to imitate their faith and patience.
