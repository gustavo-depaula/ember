# Sermon 153

**1. 1.** We have heard, and have answered in concord, and have sung to our God with harmonious voice, *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and teach out of your law*[^1]. If you grant silence, you will hear. Wisdom finds no place where there is no patience. We speak, but God instructs; we speak, but God teaches. For he is not called blessed whom a man teaches, but *whom you instruct, O Lord*. We can plant and water; but it is God's to give the increase[^2]. He who plants and he who waters works on the outside; he who gives the increase aids on the inside. What reading from the Epistle of the holy Apostle has been set before us to speak on—how difficult, how full of hidden places, how perilous (if it is not understood, or is misunderstood)—I think, brothers, indeed I know, that you heard, since it was recited along with us; and you wavered, if you noticed; or if some of you did understand, you saw without doubt how arduous it is. This reading, then, and that whole passage of the Apostle's Epistle, troublesome and obscure but wholesome to those who understand, we have undertaken, with the help of God's mercy, to expound by discoursing. I know that we are debtors to your Charity; I feel that you are the collectors. As we pray that you may grasp these things, so do you pray that we may be able to unfold them to you. For if our prayer agrees, God will both make you fit hearers and make us most faithful payers of this debt.

**2. 2.** *For when we were in the flesh*, says the Apostle, *the passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members, so as to bear fruit for death*[^3]. Here the Apostle seems (which, for those who do not understand, is the first and great peril) to rebuke and blame the law of God. Someone says: Far be this from the mind of any Christian whatever; who, even though mad, would dare suspect this of the Apostle? And yet, my brothers, these words, misunderstood, have furnished tinder for the madness of the Manicheans' fury. For the Manicheans say that the law given through Moses was not given by God, and contend that it is contrary to the gospel. And when one deals with them, they try, by these testimonies of the apostle Paul which they do not understand, to convict—what shall I call them?—Catholics who do not understand, or rather who are negligent. For it is no great thing, if one wishes to be diligent, after the slanders heard from the heretic, at least to consider in the codex the context of the reading. And if he does this, he will at once find there the means to refute the talkative adversary, the means to lay low the enemies and rebels against the law. For even if he is slow to understand the words of the Apostle, the praise of the law of God is plainly expressed there.

**2. 3.** See first, and notice. *For when we were in the flesh*, he says, *the passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work*. Here now the Manichean raises his neck, lifts up his horns, attacks you, makes his charge: Behold, he says, *the passions of sins, which were through the law*. How is the law good, through which the passions of sins are in us and are at work in our members, so as to bear fruit for death? Read, go a little further, hear it all patiently, even if not understandingly. For this which he says, *The passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members*, is much for you to understand; but first be with me a praiser of the law, and then you will deserve to become one who understands. You have a closed heart, and you accuse the key? Behold, meanwhile, let us set aside a little what we do not understand, and come to the praise of the law which is plain. *The passions*, he says, *of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members, so as to bear fruit for death. Now therefore we have been freed from the law of death, in which we were held, so that we serve in newness of spirit, and not in oldness of the letter*[^4]. Still he seems to charge, blame, condemn, detest the law; but only to those who do not understand. For when he says, *When we were in the flesh, the passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members, so as to bear fruit for death. Now therefore we have been freed from the law of death, in which we were held, so that we serve in newness of spirit, and not in oldness of the letter*, he seems, as it were, to accuse and blame the law. He himself saw this; he saw, he felt that he was not understood, and that the thoughts of people were being stirred against the obscurity of his words; he felt what you might say, he felt what you might say in objection; and he wished to say this first, so that you might not find anything to say.

**3. 4.** *What, then, shall we say*? he says. This follows: *What, then, shall we say? Is the law sin? Far from it*[^5]! With one word he absolved the law, condemned the accuser of the law. You were bringing forth the authority of the Apostle against me, O Manichean, and saying to me, as you reproached the law: Behold, hear the Apostle, read the Apostle: *The passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members, so as to bear fruit for death. Now therefore we have been freed from the law of death, in which we were held, so that we serve in newness of spirit, and not in oldness of the letter*. You boasted, you cried out, you said: Hear, read, see; you had said these things, and now, turning your back, you wished to go. Wait, I have heard you, hear me; rather, neither I you nor you me, but let us both together hear the Apostle, who frees himself and binds you. *What, then, shall we say*? he says. *Is the law sin*? This you were saying: *The law is sin*, this indeed you were saying. Behold, you heard what you were saying; hear what you should say. You were saying that the law of God is sin, when, blind and improvident, you reproached it. You erred; Paul saw your error. What you were saying, he himself said. *What, then, shall we say? Is the law sin*? Do we say what you were saying? *Is the law sin? Far from it*! If you followed the authority of the Apostle, weigh the word, and take counsel from it. Hear: *Is the law sin? Far from it*! Hear: *Far from it*! If you follow the Apostle, if you value his authority highly, hear: *Far from it*! and let what you thought be far from you. *What, then, shall we say*? What shall we say? That because I said, *The passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members, so as to bear fruit for death*; because I said, *We have been freed from the law of death, in which we were held*; because I said, *Let us serve in newness of spirit, and not in oldness of the letter: Is the law sin? Far from it*! Why, then, Apostle, did you say all those things?

**4. 5.** Far be it that the law be sin: *But*, he says, *I did not know sin except through the law. For I would not have known coveting, unless the law said, You shall not covet*[^6]. Now then I ask you, Manichean, now I ask you, answer me. Is the law evil that says, *You shall not covet*? Not even some lustful and worthless person would answer me this. For even shameful people themselves blush when they are reproached; and when they are among the chaste, they do not dare to be wanton. If, then, you say that the law is evil which says, *You shall not covet*, you wish to covet with impunity; you accuse the law because it strikes at lust. My brothers, if we did not hear the Apostle saying, *Is the law sin? Far from it*! but only recalling the words of the law, where it was said, *You shall not covet*; even though he did not praise the law, yet we ought to praise it—to praise it, and to accuse ourselves. Behold the law, behold from above the divine trumpet cries out to man, *You shall not covet. You shall not covet*: reproach it if you can; do it, if you cannot reproach it. You have heard, *You shall not covet*; you do not dare to reproach it. Because what it said is good, *You shall not covet*; it is evil to covet. The law blames the evil, the law forbids you from your own evil. Therefore the law blames the evil of coveting, the law forbids you from your own evil. Therefore do what the law commands, do not do what the law forbids, do not covet.

**5. 6.** But what does the Apostle say? *I would not have known coveting, unless the law said, You shall not covet*. For I was going after my coveting, and I ran where it dragged, and reckoned its allurements—soft and pleasant with carnal sweetness—a great happiness. *For the sinner is praised*, says the law, *in the desires of his soul; and he who does unjust deeds is blessed*[^7]. You find a person pursuing his carnal cravings, giving himself wholly as a slave to them, snatching at pleasures on every side, fornicating, getting drunk; I say no more—fornicating, I say, getting drunk. I have named these things, which are committed lawfully, but not by the laws of God. For who was ever brought before a judge because he entered a harlot's brothel? Who was ever accused in the public tribunals because, wanton and unclean, he dissolved himself among his lyre-girls? Who, having a wife, ever found a charge brought against him because he debauched his maidservant? But in the forum, not in heaven; in the law of the world, not in the law of the Creator of the world. The lustful, unclean, and wanton man is called happy because he abounds in pleasures, enjoys delights. And if he even gluts himself with wine, if he drinks measures without measure, it is too little that he finds no charge—he even receives the name of a brave man, the more worthless the more unconquered under the cup. When these things are praised, and it is said, He is happy, he is great, all is well with him; and not only is this not thought to be a sin, but it is even thought to be either a gift of God, or at least a sweet, pleasant, and lawful good: the law of God comes forth and says, *You shall not covet*. That man who thought it a great good, and reckoned it great happiness, not to deny his coveting what he could, but to follow where it dragged—he hears, *You shall not covet*; and he recognizes that it is sin. God spoke, man heard. He believed God, he saw his sin; what he thought good, he came to know was evil; he wished to bridle his coveting, not to go after it, he restrained himself, he tried, he was conquered. He who was before ignorant of his own evils became learned, and he is the worse for being conquered; he began to be not only a sinner, but also a transgressor. For he was a sinner even before; but before he heard the law, he did not know that he was a sinner. He heard the law, he saw the sin; he tried to conquer, he was overcome and laid low; he became a transgressor of the law too, who was before an ignorant sinner. This is what the Apostle says: *Is the law sin? Far from it! But I did not know sin except through the law. For I would not have known coveting, unless the law said, You shall not covet*.

**5. 7.** *But sin, taking occasion, worked in me through the commandment every kind of coveting*[^8]. The coveting was less when, before the law, you sinned without care; but now, with the barriers of the law set against you, the river of coveting was as it were checked a little, not dried up; but, the force that was leading you growing greater against no barriers, it overwhelmed you when the barriers were broken. Your coveting was less when it stirred your lust; but it is "every kind" when it transgresses even the law. Do you wish to know how great it is? See what it broke through: *You shall not covet*. No man said it; God said it; the Creator said it; the eternal Judge said it; it was no ordinary person who said it. Do, then, what he said. You do not do it? Beware of the one who said it as he judges. But what can you do, O man? You did not conquer for this reason: because you presumed upon yourself.

**6. 8.** Attend, then, now to the earlier words, which seemed obscure: *For when we were in the flesh*. Attend to the words we called the earlier ones, where the reading began that seemed obscure: *For when we were in the flesh, the passions of sins, which were through the law*[^9]. Whence are they "through the law"? Because we were in the flesh. What is, "Because we were in the flesh"? We were presuming upon the flesh. For surely the Apostle who was speaking had not already gone out of this flesh, nor was he speaking to those who had already gone out of this flesh by death? Not at all; but according to the manner of this life, both he who was speaking and those to whom he was speaking were in the flesh. What, then, is, *When we were in the flesh*, but: When we presumed upon the flesh, that is, relied upon ourselves? For it was said to man, and said of men, *All flesh shall see the salvation of God*[^10]. What is, *All flesh shall see*, but: Every man shall see? And what is, *The Word was made flesh*[^11], but: The Word was made man? For it was not that the Word was flesh, and there was no soul there; but by the name of flesh man was signified, in that which is read, *The Word was made flesh*. Therefore *when we were in the flesh*—that is, when we dwelt in the desires of the flesh, and placed all our hope there, as it were in ourselves—*the passions of sins, which were through the law*, were increased through the law. For by forbidding they made a transgressor of the law, because the one who was made a transgressor did not have God as helper. *They were at work*, then, *in our members, so as to bear fruit*—for whom, but *for death*? If the sinner was to be condemned, what hope has the transgressor?

**7. 9.** Therefore, O man, your coveting conquered you; it conquered you because it found you in an evil place; it found you in the flesh, and so conquered you. Move away from there; why are you terrified? I did not tell you to die. Do not be terrified because I said: Move away from the flesh. I did not tell you to die; rather, I dare to say, I told you to die. If you have died with Christ, seek the things that are above[^12]. Living in the flesh, do not be in the flesh. *All flesh is grass, but the Word of the Lord remains forever*[^13]. Let the Lord become your refuge. Coveting presses, urges you, has received great strength against you, has been made greater by the law's prohibition; you suffer a greater enemy: let the Lord be your refuge, a tower of strength against the face of the enemy[^14]. Do not, then, be in the flesh; be in the spirit. What is it to be in the spirit? To place your hope in God. For if you place your hope in the very spirit by which you are a man, again your spirit slips back into the flesh, because you did not give it to the One from whom it might hang. It does not contain itself, if it is not contained. Do not remain in yourself; transcend even yourself; place yourself in the One who made you. For if you have hope in yourself, when the law is received you will be a transgressor. The enemy finds you stripped of refuge, attacks you; beware lest perchance he seize you, like a lion, and there be no one to deliver you[^15]. Attend to the words of the Apostle praising the law, accusing himself, making himself guilty under the law, and perhaps transfiguring your person in himself, and saying to you: *I did not know sin except through the law. For I would not have known coveting, unless the law said, You shall not covet. But sin, taking occasion, worked in me through the commandment every kind of coveting. For without the law sin was dead*. What is, *was dead*? It lies hidden, does not appear at all; as though buried, it is not known. *But when the commandment came, sin revived*[^16]. What is, *revived*? It began to appear, began to be felt, began to rebel against me.

**8. 10.** *But I died*. What is, *I died*? I became a transgressor, *and the commandment that was unto life was found by me*. See that the law is praised, *the commandment that was unto life*. For what kind of life is it, not to covet? O sweet life! Sweet indeed is the pleasure of coveting—it is true, nor would people pursue it unless it were sweet. The theater, the spectacle, the wanton harlot, the most shameful little song—these are the sweet things of coveting; sweet indeed, pleasant, delightful: but *the unjust have told me of delights, but not like your law, O Lord*[^17]. They are pleasant, they are sweet, they are delightful; but hear better things: *The unjust have told me of delights, but not like your law, O Lord*. Happy the soul that is delighted with delights of this kind, where it is stained by no shamefulness, and is purified by the serenity of truth. But whoever is delighted by the law of God, and so delighted that it conquers all the delights of wantonness, let him not arrogate this delight to himself: *The Lord will give sweetness*[^18]. Which shall I name? Lord, give me that sweetness, or that other? *You are sweet, O Lord, and in your sweetness teach me your righteous decrees*[^19]. In your sweetness teach me, and you do teach me. Then I learn so as to do, if in your sweetness you teach me. But as long as iniquity flatters and iniquity is sweet, truth is bitter. *In your sweetness teach me*: that truth may be sweet, that by your sweetness iniquity may be despised. Truth is far better and sweeter, but to the healthy bread is sweet. What is better and more excellent than the heavenly bread? But only if iniquity does not blunt the teeth. For Scripture says, *As an unripe grape is harmful to the teeth, and smoke to the eyes, so is iniquity to those who use it*[^20]. What does it profit that you praise the bread, if you live badly? What you praise, you do not eat. When, then, you hear the word, when you hear the word of righteousness and truth, and praise it; it is far more praiseworthy if you do it. Do, then, what you praise. Or will you say: I wish to, but I am not able? Why are you not able? Because there is no health. Whence did you lose your health, but because by sinning you offended the Creator? Therefore, that you may eat his bread, which you praise, with sweetness—that is, with health—say to him, *I said, O Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^21]. Therefore, he says, *the commandment that was unto life was found by me to be unto death*[^22]. For before he was a sinner unknown to himself; he became a manifest transgressor. Behold, that which was unto life was found by him to be unto death.

**9. 11.** *But sin*, he says, *taking occasion, deceived me through the commandment, and through it killed me*[^23]. So it happened first in paradise: *It deceived me*, he says, *taking occasion through the commandment*. See the serpent whispering to that woman. He asked her what God had said: she answered, *God said to us, Of every tree that is in paradise you shall eat; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat. If you eat of it, you shall die the death*. This is God's commandment. The serpent, on the contrary: *You shall not*, he says, *die the death. For God knew that on the day you eat, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like gods*[^24]. *Taking occasion*, then, *sin deceived me through the commandment, and through it killed me*. By the sword you were carrying, the enemy killed you; with your own arms he conquered you, with your own arms he slew you. Take up the commandment: know that it is arms, not by which it may kill you, but by which the enemy may be killed by you. But do not presume upon your own strength. See little David against Goliath, see the small one against the huge one; but presuming in the name of the Lord. *You come to me*, he says, *with shield and spear; I, in the name of the almighty Lord*[^25]. Thus, thus; not otherwise: by no means otherwise is the enemy laid low. He who presumes upon his own strength is laid low before he fights.

**10. 12.** See, however, dearly beloved, see again and again that the apostle Paul, against the fury of the Manicheans, is a most open praiser of the divine law; see what he adds: *And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*[^26]. Can it be praised more richly? A little before, by that word which he said, *Far from it*! he had defended it from a charge, not praised it. It is one thing to defend from a charge brought, another to proclaim with due heralding. The charge brought was: *What, then, shall we say? Is the law sin*? The defense: *Far from it*! With one word truth is defended, because great is the authority of the Apostle defending. Why should he defend at length? It suffices. *Far from it! Or do you wish*, he says, *to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ*[^27]? But now: *And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*.

**10. 13.** *Did that which is good, then, become death to me? Far from it*! Because death is not good. *But sin, that it might appear as sin, worked death in me through what is good*[^28]. The law is not death, but sin is death. But he had said a while before, *Without the law sin was dead*. Where I had admonished you that *was dead*, he said, means: it lies hidden, concealed, does not appear. Now see how truly it was thus said: *Sin*, he says, *that it might appear as sin*. He did not say, That it might be; because it was even when it did not appear. *Sin, that it might appear as sin*. What is, *That sin might appear*? Because *I would not have known coveting, unless the law said, You shall not covet*. He did not say, I did not have coveting; but, *I did not know coveting*. So here too he does not say, That sin might be; but, *That sin might appear, it worked death in me through what is good*. What death? *That the sinner*, or sin, *might become beyond measure through the commandment*. Note: *the sinner beyond measure*. Why *beyond measure*? Because now there is also transgression. *For where there is no law, neither is there transgression*[^29].

**11. 14.** See, then, brothers, see that the human race flowed from that first death of that first man. For *through one man sin entered into this world, and through sin death, and so it passed into all men*[^30]. *It passed*—note the word that you heard; consider, see what is *it passed. It passed*: hence even the little child is guilty; he has not yet committed sin, but he has inherited it. For that sin did not remain in its source, but *passed*; not into this one or that one, but *passed into all men*. The first sinner, the first transgressor, begot sinners liable to death. The Savior came from the Virgin to heal them. Because he came to you, not by the way by which you came; for he was not from the concupiscence of male and female, not from that bond of concupiscence. *The Holy Spirit*, it says, *will come upon you*. This was said to the Virgin, said to one fervent with faith, not aflame with the concupiscence of the flesh: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you*[^31]. Who could have such an overshadowing while aflame with the ardor of lust? Because, then, he came to you not by the way by which you came, he frees you. Where did he find you? Sold under sin, lying in the death of the first man, inheriting the sin of the first man, having the guilt before you could have a will. Behold where he found you, when he found you a little child. But you have passed beyond the age of a little child: behold, you have grown, you have added many things to the first sin; you have received the law, you have stood forth a transgressor. But do not be anxious: *Where sin abounded, grace abounded the more*[^32]. Let us turn to the Lord, etc.
