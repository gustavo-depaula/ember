# Sermon 77/A: A Treatise on the Canaanite Woman According to Matthew

**1.** That woman, who cried out after the Lord, you have heard how she sought, asked, knocked, and it was opened to her. She teaches us, then, to seek, that we may find; to ask, that we may receive; to knock, that it may be opened to us. What is it, then, that the Lord was unwilling to grant what was asked? was he merciless? But he knew when to give, who deferred giving; for he did not deny his benefit, but exercised her desire. Let us cry out, then, to him what we just now sang: *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me, for in you my soul has trusted*[^1]. *Have mercy*, he says, *on me, O God:* why? *Because in you my soul has trusted.* This, he says, is the sacrifice I offer you, that you may hear me: *because in you my soul has trusted. Who has hoped in God, and been forsaken?* It happens even in great temptations; and however much we advance in God, we live under pardon. Did the Lord Jesus teach the little lambs, and not the rams themselves, to pray? His disciples, our Apostles, the very leaders of the flock, whose sons we are, of whom it was said: *Bring to the Lord the sons of rams*[^2]; those rams, then, he taught to pray, when he said to them that they should say: *Forgive us our debts*[^3]. If this prayer is daily, we live under pardon. Both all our sins were forgiven us in baptism, and we live under pardon. We advance, if our hope is nourished in God and strengthened with his help, that we may bridle every craving. Let us fight: our contest is known to him, who both knows how to watch and to help.

**2.** You heard, when the Apostle was read: *We know*, he says, *that the law is spiritual; but I am carnal.* See who says what. *The law*, he says, *is spiritual; but I am carnal, sold under sin; for what I work, I do not know*[^4]. What is: *I do not know?* I do not accept, I do not approve. *For I do not do what I wish, but what I hate, that I do. But if I do what I do not wish, I consent to the law that it is good*[^5]. What is: *I consent to the law?* Because what I do not wish, the law too does not wish: when, then, I do what I do not wish, and I do not wish this which the law does not wish, *I consent to the law* that it is good. But it is spiritual, I am carnal; what then will happen? We do what we do not wish; and if we do all evil things, shall we be unpunished? Far be it, do not promise yourself this, O man, attend to what follows: *But if I do what I do not wish, it is no longer I who work it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^6]. What is the sin that he calls it, except the craving of the flesh? And lest perhaps you say it does not pertain to you, therefore he said, *the sin that dwells in me.* And what is: *It is no longer I who work it?* I crave with the flesh, with the mind I do not consent. The flesh craves, the mind does not consent: behold the fight. Remain, mind, in your contest, and ask help from the Lord your God. Remain, mind, in your contest, and cry out what that woman cried: *Lord, help me*[^7]. Remain, mind, in your contest, and cry out what you sang: *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me*[^8]. Behold the sacrifice: *In you my soul has trusted.* In baptism iniquity is blotted out, but weakness remains; but in the resurrection there will be no iniquity, and weakness will be consumed. *When this mortal has put on immortality, and this corruptible has put on incorruption, then will come about the word that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your strife?*[^9] And if the strife of death is our contest, it is no longer I who work it, but the sin that dwells in me. He called the craving of the flesh sin; I crave, but with the mind I do not consent, and the craving does not cease to goad to evil. This is the strife of death. But then the outer enemy, the devil, will be under our feet, when the inner enemy, craving, will be healed, and we will live in peace. In what peace? Which *eye has not seen, nor ear heard*[^10]. In what peace? Which no heart conceives, no discord pursues. In what peace? Of which the Apostle said: *And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, guard your hearts*[^11]. Of that peace the prophet Isaiah says: *O Lord our God, give us peace; for you have repaid us all things*[^12]. You promised Christ: you repaid. You promised his cross, and his blood to be shed for the remission of sins: you repaid. You promised his ascension, and the Holy Spirit to be sent from heaven: you repaid. You promised the Church spread throughout the whole world: you repaid. You promised that there would be heretics for our exercise and our proving, and from their errors the victory of the Church: you repaid. You promised that the idols of the Gentiles would be abolished: you repaid. *O Lord our God, give us peace; for you have repaid us all things.*

**3.** Meanwhile, until we come to that peace, where we will have no enemy, let us fight long and faithfully and strenuously, that we may merit to be crowned by the Lord God. The apostle James says: *Let no one, when he is tempted, say that he is tempted by God.* He speaks of the temptation that is unto seduction. *God*, he says, *is not a tempter to evils, but he himself tempts no one; but each one is tempted, drawn away and enticed by his own craving. Then craving, when it has conceived, brings forth sin; but sin, when it has been completed, begets death*[^13]. So each one is tempted by his own craving; therefore let him fight, resist, not consent, not be dragged along, not let it conceive what it may bring forth. Behold, craving allures, goads, presses, demands that you do something evil; do not consent, and it does not conceive. If you think of it willingly, it conceives; it will bring forth, that you may die. See what the Apostle says: *Sin, when it has been completed, begets death.* Sin is sweet, but death is bitter: beware the sweetness of sin, lest you feel the bitterness of death; beware craving, and if not unto the deed, at least unto the word. You hear something willingly, which you ought not to hear, you say what you ought not to say, you think what you ought not to think. Nothing is swifter than thought, it has incredible wings; it shakes itself out of the heart, and outstrips the tongue; the evil is thought before it is spoken. Do not stand there. The thought has crept up: go away from there, proceed elsewhere, do not linger there. You do not intend to do evil: why do you willingly think what you do not wish to do? My brothers, to whom these sins are not great, hesitates to persevere in those things to whom the Lord says: *Say, forgive us our debts*[^14]. However much you advance, you have that craving in you. Before death is swallowed up in victory, therefore say: *Forgive us our debts.* Do not raise your head into pride, fear God: we live under pardon. Say with all your heart: *Forgive us our debts.* This of past things, this of deeds, of words, of thoughts: of future things, what? Hear, and say what follows: *Lead us not into temptation*[^15]. *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*[^16]. What is, to enter into temptation? To consent to evil craving. Have you consented? you have entered: at least go out quickly. Before you come to the sin, kill your consent; rejoice that you did not do it, let it repent you that you thought it.

**4.** Brothers, let us have a wise heart: let us fear God who promises great things, who threatens terrible things. This life must at some time be ended. You see that daily men pass away from here: death can be deferred, it cannot be taken away. Whether we will or not, this life must be ended; let us desire that life which has no end; to which life there is no way to pass over, except through death. Let us not, then, fear that which will at some time come; let us fear that which, if it comes and finds us in sins, drags us not to a temporal but to an everlasting death; which may God avert from all, both from us and from you. O man, do you not fear to die forever, who so act that you may be punished or die for an eternal time? Let the fear of this death teach you how the death to come is to be feared. You fear death: will you escape death? Whether you will or not, it is necessary that it come. You fear death, you ought to fear sin more; for through sin the soul dies, sin is the enemy of your soul. From sin, indeed, you will at some time be loosed, but, loosed from the corruptible fetters of the flesh, see lest you be bound by the fetters of hell. Loosed, you ought to be free, not a slave. Beware frauds, on account of that craving which is called avarice: beware base gains, on account of the craving which is called avarice; because avarice itself too *is the root of all evils*[^17], as Scripture says. Beware drunkenness, beware adultery, theft, lying, false testimony. Beware blasphemies, amulets, incantations, and the various superstitions. Beware usury and interest; do not have fellowship with usurers, leave them. The day will come, when it will be said to them: Let your money be with you unto destruction[^18]. The day of judgment will come, when for that money, and with that money, they will burn in everlasting fire, *where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^19]. That money will be for a testimony against them. Do not so give, do not so receive, lest you begin on the day of judgment to render a bad account of yourselves to God. For what does it profit them, except that for money, which either living they will lose, or dying they will leave behind, they lose their soul, which they cannot redeem? As the holy Gospel says: *What does it profit a man, if he gain the whole world, but suffer the loss of his soul? Or what will a man give in exchange for his soul?*[^20] Beware, then, my brothers, of usury and interest, and do not say: And whence shall we live? This is not to seek life, but death. Do not say: And whence shall we live? There are other ways by which men transact their living. What God forbids, do not do, do not live from it. O wretched and pitiable and unhappy man! You attend to what you live from, and do not attend to what you die from. And whence, you say, shall I live? This a procurer too can say to me, this a robber too can say to me; are robberies, then, to be committed, or pandering practiced, because those who do these things live from them? Woe to the wretches who live from such things, because from them they die. It is better to beg, than to live from the unlawful. Finally, it is better that a man die, than that, by living from the unlawful, it be brought about that he be tortured with everlasting death. This death ends pain: that death remains in eternal pains. Believe, understand, fear, abstain from every evil thing; be zealous for the word of God, love to hear what God wills, and what God promises to those doing his will. And that what he commands may be done, let God be entreated, and God helps.
