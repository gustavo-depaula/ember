# Sermon 97a

**1.** *Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick; I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners* [^1]. It is the Lord's voice; and he calls sinners to this end, that they may not always be sinners; lest perhaps men suppose, because the Lord loved sinners, that they should *always* want to have sins, so that Christ may love them. Christ loves sinners as a physician loves the sick man—but so as to kill the fever and save him. He does not want him always to be sick, so as to have someone always to visit; rather, he wants to heal him. Therefore the Lord did not come to call the righteous but sinners, that he might justify the ungodly. From an idolater he made a believer, from a drunkard a sober man, from a spendthrift a frugal man, from a miser one who does not give to huntsmen, applauding the devil, but gives to the poor and is to be crowned by Christ, acquiring for himself that which cannot pass away. What the Lord did was more difficult; for he who made the godly man out of the ungodly, will he not give a reward to the godly? Consider, my brothers: which is more incredible, to make a godly man out of an ungodly one, or to make an angel out of a godly man? The ungodly and the godly are contrary to each other; the godly and the angel are not contrary. Will he not fill you from what is near, who changed you from what was contrary? For once you have begun to be godly, you begin to imitate the angelic life; but when you were ungodly, you were far from the life of the angels. But when faith comes, you are justified, and you are humbled before God, you who used to blaspheme God; and you who had turned to the creature now long for the Creator.

**2.** Behold what he has bestowed on you: he has displayed the Church throughout the whole world; as he promised it, so he has displayed it. It was foretold that idolatry would one day be slain and taken away; our forefathers read it, and did not see it; but we read it and see it. For does the one who sees believe? He believes who does not see; it is one thing to believe, another to see. Believe what you do not see, so that by believing what you do not see you may deserve to see what you believe. The merit of vision is faith; the reward of faith is vision. Why do you seek the reward before the work? Believe, then, and walk in faith; your salvation is in hope. The best Physician, then, has begun to cure you, the Physician to whom no disease is incurable. Do not fear your past crimes, however monstrous, however incredible, that you may perhaps have committed; the diseases are great, but greater is the Physician. Do not, then, be anxious about the past; in one moment of the sacrament they will be forgiven, and absolutely all will be forgiven. Hear on this matter what was said by the Apostles to the Jews who crucified the Lord: *Repent, and let each of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, and your sins are forgiven you* [^2]. It was done: they were baptized, they believed, they approached the Lord's body, they came to drink the blood which they had shed. To all who had been made guilty, the forgiver of sins grants pardon—not the praiser of sins—he who did not come to call the righteous, but sinners.

**3.** Briefly, then, we say to Your Holiness: let every Christian make the effort, if he is still a catechumen, that his sins may be forgiven him. For already he bears the sign of Christ on his forehead, already he enters the church, already through him alone is the name invoked; but he still carries the burden of his sins; they are not yet forgiven, because they are not forgiven except in holy baptism. And let him not say to himself: I am afraid to become one of the faithful, lest I sin afterward. For that he may not sin afterward, he has it in his power; but that he may not have sinned, does he have that in his power? He has what he can do, so as not to sin; what can he do, so as not to have sinned? For what is done is done; the past you cannot make undone; but what is to come, you can refrain from doing. Why then is he led astray by the devil with perverse counsel? He fears future sins, which he has not yet committed; he does not fear the past ones, which he carries already committed. Those you have not yet done; these already press upon you; perhaps you will not do those—indeed, if you are unwilling, you will not do them—but these, even if you wish, you can blot out. I cannot, you say. Then flee to him who blots them out.

**4.** Come to grace. For you have received the power, since it is written: *He gave them power to become sons of God* [^3]. Begin, then, to be a son, you who were a wicked servant, though you had already begun to be in the great house. Where you began to be a servant, aspire to be a son; let the sins you carry be forgiven you. Why do you fear those that are not, and do not fear those that are? But when through the remission of sins you have been made new, all the past forgiven, if you receive here a generous span of living, so that good works may follow your faith, then live as one made a son of the household of so great a father, as one upon whom the name of God is invoked. Live thus: make progress, despise the present, hope for things to come; let temporal things grow cheap to you, let eternal things grow bright. Let us keep the Physician's precepts, that we may deserve to enjoy eternal health: for *whoever does the will of God abides forever, just as God abides forever for him* [^4].

[^1]: See Mt 9:12-13
[^2]: See Acts 2:38
[^3]: Jn 1:12
[^4]: See 1 Jn 2:17
