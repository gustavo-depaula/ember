# Sermon 335N

**1.** To despise the present life without fear, the blessed martyrs first chose a good cause and discerned and understood what they were losing and gaining, what they were spending and acquiring, what they were sowing and reaping. By loving life they despised life, and by fearing death they suffered death. For our age flies and everything passes; things being born succeed things dying, and things dying yield to things being born, like this river flowing from the hidden into the hidden, visible in between. The blessed martyrs understood this and, despising a few evil days, gained a day without sunrise or sunset. For they heard the Father calling and saying: *Come, children, hear me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord. Who is the person who desires life*—for what person does not love life?—*and loves to see good days?*[^1] Who does not wish to see good days? But if you seek to receive that reward, do not refuse the work by which it is reached. You have certainly heard the reward set before you: life and good days. Therefore this is not life, nor are these good days. For these days are undoubtedly evil, even when they seem good. Despise the world when it flatters, if you want to overcome it when it rages. In this age good days are the world flattering; evil days are the world raging. When the world flatters, it lies. If you want it not to break you when it threatens, let it not seduce you when it lies. Thus here both flattering days and harsh days are all evil days, because all are vain. Flattering days are full of fears; harsh days are full of torments. If you wish to give me a good time of life, give me security, if you can. Wherever you turn, whatever you hold, whatever you choose, everything is suspect. What is evil, you wish to pass; what seems good, you fear to lose.

**2.** Let us, then, become in a sense deaf to the flattering world and hear the one who says: *Who is the person who desires life and loves to see good days*? I know: all of you answer in your hearts, “I do.” *Who is the person who desires life*? The whole people silently answers me, “I do.” *Who loves to see good days*?[^2] I know; I hear your hearts. You cry out, “I do.” Hear, then, what follows too. You certainly desire life and love to see good days. *Keep your tongue from evil*. Now say, “I do.” Let the divine word find you here. You run toward what it promised; attend to what it commanded. You run toward life and good days. This is the road: *Keep your tongue from evil, and let your lips speak no deceit; turn from evil and do good*.[^3] Keep the order of a good life. First one must turn from evil, and then do good. How do you clothe the naked while still seeking to strip the clothed? You plunder much from whomever you can, give a little to the poor—if you even do that—and think you are buying off God as one bribes a judge. For someone who has much from plunder and has a bad case because of his plundering looks to a corrupt judge (†corrupticium†), takes something from the much he has stolen, and gives it to the evil judge, so that he may keep all he has stolen. Do you think God is like that, do you say God is like that? *Keep your tongue from evil; turn from evil and do good*. Look at Zacchaeus: *Half*, he says, *of my possessions I give to the poor*. See, he wants to do good. See whether he has turned from evil: *If I have taken anything from anyone, I repay fourfold*.[^4]

**3.** Whatever is unlawfully desired in this life, such as another's property or another's wife, and whatever is permitted to be lawfully possessed but is loved immoderately, such as your property or your gold, which you own with full legal right—even there fear the birdlime, fear even there. Fear the birdlime of desire, lest your wings stick and you cannot fly toward life and good days.[^5] For from whom had that rich man stolen, whose own property had yielded a crop and whom the abundance he had wished for wearied? He had things to gather but could find nowhere to put them. He was troubled by plenty and cramped by abundance. At last he seemed to himself to have found a plan and said: *I have found what to do. I will tear down the barns and build new, larger ones, fill them, and say to my soul: Soul, you have many good things stored up for many years; feast and rejoice*. *God said to him: Fool*, you thought you had provided wisely for yourself. *O fool, this night your soul will be demanded from you; whose will the things you have prepared be?*[^6] Does not *man walk in an image, yet trouble himself in vain? He stores up treasure and does not know for whom he has gathered it*.[^7] *Whose will the things you have prepared be?*[^8] Greedily keeping his own crops, he heard his own name. Brothers, if someone who keeps his own is rebuked in this way, how is someone who steals another's punished?

**4.** Whatever is unlawfully desired, whatever is lawful and permitted but loved immoderately, and finally whatever is naturally sought—the martyrs despised all these at one stroke. For I say to Your Charity, pay attention: how many things in this world people love! Yet in that life promised as a reward, these things will not exist. All this abundance of wealth will not be there, because true wealth will be there. Are the angels poor because they have no estates, silk garments, gold, silver, carriages, or mules? Are they poor? There are no possessions there, only ease. How are they poor when they have what they want? Finally, how are they poor when God is their wealth? Therefore these things will not be there. And whatever pleasure comes from corruption will not be there, where incorruptibility will be. I have spoken briefly, beloved, and briefly indicated countless pleasures. Whatever is delightful through corruption, whatever in lust or in food and drink produces pleasure through corruption, will not be in the land of incorruption. Among these things, some are unlawful for human beings, such as debauchery, drunkenness, and fornication; others are lawful, such as food and drink suited to health, and marriage for begetting children.

**5.** Neither those unlawful things nor these lawful things will be there. Let the unlawful be avoided; let the lawful be tolerated. Let nothing that will not be possessed forever be greatly desired. What, will life not be there? Certainly it will. And what was it we said: *Who is the person who desires life*? Will health not be there? Certainly it will. For life without health is not even life. There will be life; there will be health. These things are naturally desired. What else will there be? A friend. There will truly be a friend where there can be no enemy. Life, health, a friend, God. And God himself first of all as friend. I believe I am not insulting him. *I will no longer call you servants, but friends*.[^9] These things are naturally sought, and those who delight in them are not to be blamed. But what sort of life is this, destined eventually to end? What sort of health, exposed to countless hazards? Finally, how certain even is a friend in this life, whose face is open but whose heart is hidden? For him to be a friend, what is unknown is believed. I know that I love you; I believe that you love me. You know that you love me; you believe that I love you. You believe my love because you do not know it; I believe your love because I do not know it. I know my own love; you know your own love. And sometimes I suspect what is not in you; in turn, you suspect what is not in me.

**6.** *Judge nothing before the time*. Let the Apostle continue and teach us what friendship will be like there. *Judge*, he says, *nothing before the time, until the Lord comes and illuminates the hidden things of darkness and reveals the thoughts of the heart*.[^10] Then hold fast to a friend, when you do not fear an enemy. *And he will reveal the thoughts of the heart*, *and then each will have praise from God*. What does *from God* mean? Will there not also be praise of one's neighbor and from one's neighbor? Why, then, *from God*? Because he himself illuminates the secrets of darkness. He opens your neighbor's heart to you and your heart to your neighbor. There the situation addressed by the command given to us here cannot exist: *Let your lips speak no deceit*.[^11] It is rightly commanded here; let it be practiced here, where deceit is possible. For what is deceit but concealing one thing in the heart and uttering another with the mouth? That will not be possible there. If it could be done, these things alone would delight us here too: life, health, a friend. †Sic sic qualiscumque hic esse potest cui creditur et quod non videtur† [corrupt reading]. They are lawful, naturally desired; these things will be true, perfect, and eternal in the kingdom of heaven.

**7.** O holy martyrs! You despised everything unlawfully desired, despised everything possessed with permission and lawfully, and despised even natural goods. You rejected life, health, and a friend; you found life, health, and God as friend. How many friends said to the martyrs, “Deny him; God will forgive”! How can they be friends who are enemies to themselves? How often did wives' coaxing try to bring them down from heaven's citadel? How often did children's tears fail to turn them toward earth? How many parents spread their white hair before children about to suffer? Firm against everything, they loved by not loving, cherished by despising, lived by dying. Rightly the Church magnifies them; rightly most solemn days are celebrated for them. We do this in vain unless we choose to follow their footsteps and imitate them. Therefore, my brothers, advance step by step; I have arranged everything in my discourse as well as I could. Avoid in every way what is unlawfully desired; do not cling with excessive love to what is lawful and permitted. Lawful and natural goods remain: life, health, a friend. Despise these too. If some trial comes, spend your life. You spend life, but temporal life, which you cannot make everlasting. Why not expend it so that you may acquire everlasting goods? Despise present health to confess Christ. Do you confess the Savior and despair of salvation? Your health will be immortality. But those who wish you well coax you, though they wish badly, because unknowingly they wish you harm too. They hold you back from suffering, hold you back from enduring trial. As friends, they offer counsel to withdraw from God's command and gain a few days, evil and beggarly. Despise even your own people's coaxing; choose the angels' friendship. If, then, while living here you abstain from unlawful things; if you withhold your love from lawful and permitted things, which nevertheless will be possessed in that life; if in trials, when the crisis comes that you must either suffer for Christ or deny Christ, you despise even natural goods, he for whom you despise them will give you better. They will be the price. You buy something, and what you give as the price you will not possess forever. You give, and you will have what you can never lose. Heart is needed, deliberation is needed, wisdom is needed. For the martyrs were not really without heart, nor did they fail to love life or fear death. They truly loved life when they desired eternal life; they truly feared death when they refused to perish forever.
