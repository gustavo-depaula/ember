# Sermon 173A (Fragment; Direct Attribution Uncertain)

**1.** [...] the last, because he sees and believes.[^1] Let the one who hopes for an eternal age not fear time. Time postpones true\* life\*; it does not take it away. Indeed, it does not kill the soul's life. Time postpones the body's life, because time took away the body's life; the soul's life does not\* perish, even if there has been an evil life within it. But an evil life is not life; therefore the soul's life perishes, because it was not a good life. What does “its life perishes” mean? God does not give it life, because God is the soul's life. But a soul abandoned by God will consequently be miserable, because it can be happy only through God. And what use is life\* to it, when it does not die so that its misery may end, if an evil life has preceded?

**2.** Rightly, then, the faithful, when they leave the body, are believed to be helped through the services and devotion of their own people, alms, and the saving sacrifice. For according to how people live in this life—especially those who have passed through this world retaining the faith, yet entangled in certain desires—we ought to believe that they are helped\* by these services of their own people's\* devotion. We also find in the Scriptures, in the books of Maccabees, that certain sinners died and that a faithful decision was made to offer\* sacrifice to God for them.[^2]

**3.** There are two lives of ours in this world, one old and the other new: the old belongs to generation, the new to regeneration. If someone reborn departs immediately, we need have no anxiety about him; if he remains in this life, he must say, and say truthfully: *Forgive us our debts*.[^3] Therefore Christ displayed both lives by way of likeness. For by living before his Passion until the Passion, he carried through what was like the old life; but in the Lord and Savior it was not old. Where\* there was no sin, there was [not] a likeness of the old life. His Resurrection, however, was a likeness of the new life. Hence the Apostle said: *Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more; death will no longer have dominion over him. For the death he died,* 〈*to sin*〉 *he died once; but the life he lives, he lives to God*.[^4] He who was without sin, 〈how did he die to sin〉 except because of the likeness of sinful flesh? Hence it is said to the Hebrews: *For he experienced everything according to our likeness, without sin*.[^5] The same Apostle teaches what this benefits by saying: *So you too must consider yourselves dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus*.[^6] In dying to sin we imitate\* our Lord's Cross; in living to God we imitate our Lord's Resurrection. He died to sin 〈because of the likeness of sinful flesh; let us die to sin〉 because of sinful flesh. With the help of him who lives and reigns forever and ever. Amen.

[^1]: Cf. John 6:40.
[^2]: Cf. 2 Macc 12:39–45.
[^3]: Matt 6:12.
[^4]: Rom 6:9–10.
[^5]: Heb 4:15.
[^6]: Rom 6:11.
