# Sermon 199: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1. 1.** Recently we celebrated the day on which the Lord was born of the Jews: today we celebrate the day on which he was adored by the Gentiles. *For salvation is from the Jews*[^1]: but this salvation reaches *to the ends of the earth*[^2]. For on that day the shepherds adored, today the Magi. To the former the angels, to the latter the star, announced it. Both learned from heaven, when they saw the King of heaven on earth, that there might be *glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^3]. *For he himself is our peace, who made the two one*[^4]. Now from here the infant, born and announced, is shown to be that *cornerstone*[^5]; now in the very first beginning of his nativity he appeared. He began already to join two walls to himself from different directions, leading the shepherds from Judea, the Magi from the East: *that he might create the two in himself into one new man, making peace; peace for those who were far off, and peace for those who were near*[^6]. And therefore the former, drawing near from close by on that very day, the latter, coming today from afar, marked out two days to be celebrated by posterity, yet both saw one light of the world.

**1. 2.** But today we must speak of those whom faith led to Christ from distant lands. For they came and sought him, saying: *Where is he who is born king of the Jews? For we saw his star in the East, and we have come to adore him*[^7]. They announce and inquire, they believe and seek; as though signifying those who *walk by faith* and long for the *sight*[^8]. Had not other kings of the Jews already been born so many times in Judea? What is it, that this one is recognized by foreigners in heaven, sought on earth; gleams on high, lies hidden in the lowly? In the East the Magi see the star, and understand that a king is born in Judea. Who is this king so small, so great; not yet speaking on earth, already publishing edicts in heaven? Yet for our sake, to whom he willed to become known from his holy Scriptures, he willed even those very Magi, to whom he had given so clear a sign in heaven, and to whose hearts he had revealed that he was born in Judea, to believe nonetheless concerning himself from his prophets. For in seeking the city in which he was born, whom they desired to see and adore, they had to inquire of the chief men of the Jews; so that those, from the holy Scripture which they bore in the mouth, not in the heart, unbelieving, might answer the believing about the grace of faith, liars on their own behalf, truthful against themselves. For how great a thing would it have been, that they should become companions to those seeking Christ, when they had heard from them that, his star being seen, they came desiring to adore him; that they themselves should lead them to Bethlehem of Judah, which they had indicated from the divine Books, see together, understand together, adore together? But now, having pointed out to others the fountain of life, they themselves died of drought. They became to them like the stones at the milestones: to travelers walking they showed something, but they themselves remained stolid and immovable. The Magi sought, that they might find: Herod sought, that he might destroy: the Jews read the city of the one being born, did not understand the time of the one coming. Between the pious love of the Magi and the cruel fear of Herod, they vanished, pointing out Bethlehem: but the Christ who was born there, not then sought but seen later, they would deny, and would slay not then the infant but later the one speaking. Happier rather is the ignorance of the infants whom the terrified Herod persecuted, than the knowledge of these whom, disturbed, he consulted. The former could suffer for Christ, whom they could not yet confess: the latter did not follow the truth of the one teaching, whose city, when he was born, they could know.

**2. 3.** The Magi, indeed, that star led to the very place itself where God the Word was an infant. Here now let sacrilegious folly blush, and a certain, so to speak, unlearned learning, which thinks Christ was born under the decree of the stars, because it is written in the Gospel that, when he was born, the Magi saw his star in the East[^9]. Which would not be true, even if men were born under such a decree: because they are born not by their own will, as the Son of God, but by the condition of mortal nature. But now it is so far from the truth that Christ was born under a starry fate, that whoever rightly believes in Christ believes that no man is born thus. But let vain men say of the births of men what they foolishly opine, let them deny the will by which they sin, let them feign the necessity by which they defend their sins; let them try to fix even in heaven their ruined morals, through which they are detested by men on earth, and lie that these flow from the stars: yet let each one of them see how he thinks that not his life, but his household, must be ruled by some power of his own; since, holding such opinions, he is not allowed to beat his servants sinning in his house, unless he is first compelled to blaspheme his gods shining in heaven. Yet these men cannot, even according to their most empty conjectures and their books, not indeed prophetic, but plainly lying, think that Christ was born under the decree of the stars, because, when he was born, the Magi saw his star in the East. For hence rather Christ appeared not under its dominion, but as its Lord: because that star did not hold sidereal paths in heaven, but to men seeking Christ pointed out the way as far as the place in which he had been born. Whence it did not make Christ live wondrously, but Christ made it appear wondrously; nor did it decree Christ's wonders, but Christ displayed it among his wonders. For he himself, born of a mother, showed a new star from heaven to earth, who, born of the Father, formed heaven and earth. At his birth a new light was revealed in a star, at his death the ancient light was veiled in the sun. At his birth the things above shone with new honor, at his death the things below trembled with new fear, at his rising the disciples blazed with new love, at his ascending the heavens opened with new homage. Let us therefore celebrate with devout solemnity this day too, on which the Magi from the Gentiles adored the recognized Christ, just as we celebrated that day on which the shepherds from Judea saw the newborn Christ. For the Lord our God himself chose the apostles as shepherds from Judea, through whom he would gather to be saved sinners even from the Gentiles.
