# Sermon 136b

**1.** Whoever of you have heard the name of Christ and believed and are not yet baptized have anointed eyes. Wash and see, because this blind man too, not without cause, when he had been anointed, was sent to the pool of Siloam, *which is interpreted: sent*. Who is the *sent one*, except Christ from the Father? Therefore he washes his face in Christ who is baptized in Christ. But what had already happened to this man in body had not yet happened in heart. For all things are arranged by their degrees. It happened to him in heart then, when he acknowledged the Son of God and adored him. But when he still thought him a prophet, he had, as it were, anointed eyes of the heart and did not yet see. When the Lord said to him: *Do you believe in the Son of God?* that you may know that he did not yet see, he answered thus: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?* And the Lord: *You both have seen him and he who speaks with you is the one* [^1]; you have seen him with the eyes of the flesh, see also with the eyes of the heart. When did he see with the eyes of the heart? When he heard, and believed what he heard.

**2.** When, then, he had, as it were, anointed eyes of the heart and did not yet see, and so spoke, you have heard what he said: *We know that God does not hear sinners* [^2]. He would not say this if he saw this light in his heart. For if God did not hear sinners, that Publican would have gone down confounded from the temple. But he went down justified more than that Pharisee. But whence did this man go down justified? Because he did what Scripture says: *I acknowledged my sin and did not cover my iniquity. I said: I will declare against myself my offense to my Lord, and you forgave the iniquity of my heart* [^3]. Surely, then, does God not hear sinners? Therefore believe this which the now enlightened believe: God hears sinners. For that the Lord said he came for judgment into this world *that those who do not see may see and those who see may become blind* [^4] can move many who do not understand. For Christ came as Savior. In a certain place he too says: *For the Son of man did not come to judge the world but that the world might be saved through him* [^5]. If, then, for this reason he came, that he might save, it is acceptable that he said he came *that those who do not see may see*. But that is hard: *that those who see may become blind*. If we understand it, it is not hard but pure. But that you may understand how truly it was said, turn your eyes to those very two who were praying in the temple. The Pharisee saw, the Publican was blind. What is: "he saw"? He thought he saw; about his vision, that is about his righteousness, he gloried. But that one was blind because he confessed his sins. The one boasted of his merits, this one confessed his sins. *The Publican went down justified* more than that Pharisee, because Christ came into the world *that those who do not see may see and those who see may become blind*. Therefore when the Pharisees who then heard him speaking had said: *Are we too blind?* they were surely like the one who had gone up into the temple and said to God: *I give you thanks that I am not like the rest of men, the unjust, adulterers, plunderers* [^6], as though he said: I give you thanks that I am not blind, but I see, like the rest of men of the kind of that Publican. What did they say: *Are we too blind?* And the Lord to them: *If you were blind you would not have sin. But now because you say: We see, your sin remains* [^7]. He did not say: your sin comes; but: *remains*. For it was already there. For when you do not confess it, it is not taken away, but *remains*.

**3.** Let the blind, then, run to Christ that they may be enlightened. For Christ is the light in the world, and among the most wicked men. Divine miracles were done, nor did anyone do miracles from the beginning of the human race except him to whom Scripture says: *Who alone does wonders* [^8]. Why was it said: *who alone does wonders*, except because when he himself wants to do them, he does not need a man? But when a man does them, he needs God. He alone did wonders. Why? Because God in the Trinity is the Son with the Father and the Holy Spirit, surely one God *who alone does wonders*. But the disciples of Christ did wonders too, but no one alone. What kind of wonders did they too do? As it is written in the Acts of the Apostles: the sick desired to touch their hems, and touching they were healed; the sick lying down wanted to be touched by the shadow of those passing by. What wonders they did, and no one of them alone! Hear their Lord: *Without me you can do nothing* [^9]. Therefore, dearly beloved, let us love a patriarch as a patriarch, a prophet as a prophet, an apostle as an apostle, a martyr as a martyr, yet God above all, and let us without doubt presume that we shall be saved by him alone. The prayers of the saints, well deserving of God, can help us, yet with no preceding fruit of their own merits, since the merits of any saint are the gifts of God. God who works in the open, works in secret, works in things visible, works in hearts. He himself in his temple does his wonders when he does them in holy men. For all the saints are together kindled by the fire of love and make one temple for God, and individually they are a temple and all together one temple.

**4.** You have noticed what he said to holy Elijah: *Go to Zarephath of Sidon, there I have commanded a widow woman to feed you*. For there was a great famine, but it was no great thing, even without the ministry of any man, to feed his servant the minister. For why was he sent to that woman, and the raven did not bring him the food of God? But the raven by no fruit of its own. For not because by God's command it fed the prophet was the raven therefore going to reign at any time with Christ. He was sent to the widow for the good of the widow not of Elijah, not that he could not find food elsewhere, but that she might acquire for herself with God, by feeding a just man, a merit. He came, then, and found her gathering wood. He asks and demands food. She answered that she had nothing from which to make bread, for she said she had only what would suffice for herself alone and her sons, which when they had consumed, they would die. Yet before death what does she say: *I am gathering two sticks*. When she gathered two sticks, she was seeking the cross. But ought she to have excused herself to the man of God demanding food? Where is what the Lord had said to Elijah: *I have commanded a widow woman to feed you?* Now if she had a command she ought to have recognized the one coming and said: Lord, come, eat, for now the Lord has commanded me concerning you that I prepare you lodging and from what I do not have hold it out that I may have it for you. She does not say this, but you have heard what she said: *I have only what I shall consume alone with my sons, and I shall die*. And he: *Go, make a baking and bring me first. For the Lord says this, that until he gives rain on the earth, neither that flour nor the oil shall fail* [^10]. The whole patrimony of the widow woman was a little flour and a little oil. But no opulent estate is compared to a little jar hanging on a peg: for whatever that great estate was, at that time it thirsted, but that jar did not fail. She at once believed, went and did it, undertook to feed the man of God. This is what the Lord had said: *I have commanded*. What is: *I have commanded?* I prepared the will for faith. Thanks to his mercy, since *the will is prepared by the Lord* [^11].
