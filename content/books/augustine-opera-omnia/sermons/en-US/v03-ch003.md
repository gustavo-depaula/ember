# Sermon 53: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 5: "Blessed Are the Poor in Spirit," and What Follows

**1. 1.** On the solemnity of the holy virgin, who bore witness to Christ and merited a witness from Christ, openly slain, secretly crowned, we are prompted to speak to your love of that exhortation which the Lord just now brought forth from the Gospel, naming many causes of the blessed life, which there is no one who does not wish for. For no one can be found who does not wish to be blessed. But oh, if only men, as they desire the reward, would not refuse the work of the reward! Who would not run eagerly, when it is said to him, You shall be blessed? Let him gladly hear too when it is said, If you do this. Let the contest not be refused, if the prize is loved; and let the mind be kindled to eagerness for the work by the commendation of the reward. What we want, what we desire, what we ask, will be afterward: but what we are commanded to do, for the sake of that which will be afterward, let it be now. Behold, begin to recall the divine sayings, and the evangelical precepts, or gifts, themselves. *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^1]. Afterward the kingdom of heaven will be yours; now be poor in spirit. Do you wish that afterward the kingdom of heaven be yours? See now whose you yourself are. Be poor in spirit. You ask me perhaps what it is to be poor in spirit. Everyone puffed up is not poor in spirit: therefore the humble man is poor in spirit. The kingdom of heaven is on high: but *he who humbles himself shall be exalted*[^2].

**2. 2.** Attend to what follows: *Blessed*, he says, *are the meek, for they shall possess the earth by inheritance*[^3]. Already you wish to possess the earth: see that you are not possessed by the earth. You will possess it if meek, you will be possessed if unmeek. And when you hear the prize set forth, that you may possess the earth, do not stretch out the bosom of avarice, by which you wish now to possess the earth, shutting out even your neighbor in some way: let not that opinion deceive you. You will truly possess the earth then, when you cling to him who made heaven and earth. For this is to be meek, not to resist your God: that in what you do well, he himself may please you, not you yourself; in what you justly suffer ill, that he himself may not displease you, but you yourself. For it is no small thing that you will please him, displeasing yourself; but you will displease him, pleasing yourself.

**3. 3.** Attend to the third: *Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be consoled*[^4]. In the mourning is the work, in the consolation is the reward. For those who mourn carnally, what consolations do they have? Troublesome, fearful ones. There the mourner is consoled, where again he fears lest he mourn. For example, a grown son saddens, a newborn gladdens: that one death carried off, this one was received; in that one is sadness, in this one fear: in neither, then, is there consolation. Therefore that will be the true consolation, by which will be given what is not lost; so that they may afterward rejoice to be consoled, who now mourn that they are pilgrims.

**4. 4.** Let the fourth work and gift come on: *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^5]. You wish to be filled. With what? If the flesh craves fullness, when the fullness is digested, you will suffer hunger again. *And he who drinks*, he says, *of this water will thirst again*[^6]. The medicine that is put on a wound, if it heals, then no longer hurts: but what is put against hunger, that is, food, is so put that it relieves for a little. For when the fullness is past, hunger returns. The remedy of fullness comes daily, indeed, but the wound of weakness is not healed. Let us hunger, then, and thirst for justice, that we may be filled with that very justice for which we now hunger and thirst. For we shall be filled with that for which we hunger and thirst. Let our inner man hunger and thirst: for he has his food, he has his drink. *I am*, he says, *the bread that came down from heaven*[^7]. You have the bread of the hungry; desire also the drink of the thirsty: *For with you is the fountain of life*[^8].

**5. 5.** Attend to what follows: *Blessed are the merciful, for God will have mercy on them*[^9]. Do, and it will be done: do for another, that it may be done for you. Because you both abound and are in need: you abound in temporal things, you are in need of eternal things. You hear a beggar of a man, you yourself are a beggar of God. You are asked, and you ask. What you do with your petitioner, this God will do with his. You are both full and empty: fill the empty one from your fullness, that from God's fullness your emptiness may be filled.

**6. 6.** Attend to what follows: *Blessed are the pure in heart*, that is, those who are clean of heart; *for they shall see God*[^10]. This is the end of our love; the end by which we are perfected, not by which we are consumed. Food comes to an end, clothing comes to an end: food, because it is consumed by eating; clothing, because it is finished by weaving. Both come to an end: but this end pertains to consumption, that to perfection. Whatever we do, whatever we do well, whatever we strive for, whatever we praiseworthily burn for, whatever we blamelessly desire, when we come to the vision of God we shall require nothing more. For what would he seek to whom God is present? Or what would suffice him to whom God does not suffice? We wish to see God, we seek to see God, we burn to see God. Who does not? But see what was said: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.* Prepare that whereby you may see. For, to speak according to the flesh, why do you desire the rising of the sun with bleary eyes? Let the eyes be sound, and that light will be a joy; let the eyes not be sound, and that light will be a torment. For you will not be permitted to see with a heart not pure what is seen only by a pure heart. You will be driven back, you will be taken away, you will not see. For *blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.* How often has he now numbered the blessed? What causes of blessedness, what works, what gifts, what merits, what rewards? Nowhere was it said: *They shall see God. Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are the meek: they shall possess the earth by inheritance. Blessed are those who mourn: they shall be consoled. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice: they shall be filled. Blessed are the merciful: they shall obtain mercy*[^11]. Nowhere was it said: *They shall see God.* It came to the pure in heart, and there the vision of God was promised. Not without cause, but because there are the eyes by which God is seen. Of these eyes the apostle Paul, speaking, says: *The eyes of your heart enlightened*[^12]. Now, then, these eyes, according to their weakness, are enlightened by faith: afterward, according to their firmness, they will be enlightened by sight. *For as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord. For we walk by faith, not by sight*[^13]. But as long as we are in this faith, what is said of us? *We see now through a mirror in an enigma; but then face to face*[^14].

**6. 7.** Let no bodily face be thought of here. For if, kindled with the desire of seeing God, you prepare your bodily face for seeing, you will desire such a face of God too. But if you already at least understand of God spiritually, so as not to think God is corporeal (on which we treated at greater length yesterday, if indeed we accomplished anything); if in your heart, as in the temple of God, we have broken the idol of the human form; if it has now come well into your mind, and possessed your inner depths, where the Apostle detests those who, *saying they were wise, became fools; and changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of the image of corruptible man*[^15].

**7. 7.** If you already detest such an evil, if you turn away from it, if you cleanse for the Creator his own temple, if you wish that he come and make a dwelling with you: *Think of the Lord in goodness, and in simplicity of heart seek him*[^16]. See to whom you say it, if indeed you say it, if you truly say it: *To you my heart said, "I will seek your face."* Let your heart say it too, and add: *Your face, O Lord, will I seek*[^17]. For you seek well, because you seek with the heart. God's face is spoken of, God's arm is spoken of, God's hand is spoken of, God's feet are spoken of, God's seat is spoken of, the footstool of his feet is spoken of: but do not think of human members. If you wish to be the temple of truth, break the idol of falsehood. The hand of God, the power of God. The face of God, the knowledge of God. The feet of God, the presence of God. The seat of God, if you wish, is you. Or will you perhaps dare to deny that Christ is God? No, you say. Do you grant this too, that Christ is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God[^18]? I grant it, you say. Hear: *The soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom*. For where does God have his seat, except where he dwells? And where does he dwell, except in his temple? *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^19]. See, then, how you receive God. *God is spirit; in spirit and truth must God be adored*[^20]. Now let the ark of the testament enter your heart, if you please, and let Dagon fall[^21]. Hear now, then, and learn to desire God, learn to prepare yourself whereby you may see God. *Blessed*, he says, *are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^22].

**8. 7.** Why do you prepare the eyes of the body? If it will be seen so, what is seen will be in a place. He who is everywhere whole is not in a place. Cleanse that whereby he may be seen.

**8. 8.** Hear, and understand, if with his help I shall be able to unfold it: may he help us to understand how all the works and gifts mentioned above were fitly placed, in things that agree. For where was anything said of the reward that did not agree with the work, that did not harmonize? Because the humble seem as it were estranged from a kingdom: *Blessed*, he says, *are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^23]. Because meek men are easily shut out of their own land: *Blessed*, he says, *are the meek, for they shall possess the earth by inheritance*[^24]. Now the rest are open, clear, known of their own accord: they need no disputant, they have need of one to recall them. *Blessed are those who mourn.* Who that mourns does not desire consolation? *They*, he says, *shall be consoled. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice.* Who that hungers and thirsts does not seek satiety? And *they*, he says, *shall be filled. Blessed are the merciful.* Who is merciful, except one who wishes, from the very work, the turn to be repaid to him by God, that it be done about him which he does about the poor man? *Blessed*, he says, *are the merciful, for God will have mercy on them.* How through these individual things proper things were placed for each, and nothing was applied in the reward that did not agree with the precept! For the precept is that you be poor in spirit: the reward is that you have the kingdom of heaven. The precept is that you be meek: the reward is that you possess the earth. The precept is that you mourn: the reward, that you be consoled. The precept is that you hunger and thirst for justice: the reward, that you be filled. The precept is that you be merciful: the reward, that you obtain mercy. So the precept is that you cleanse your heart: the reward, that you see God.

**9. 9.** Do not, then, so understand these precepts and rewards that, when you hear: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^25], you think the poor in spirit will not see, or that the meek will not see, that those who mourn will not see, that those who hunger and thirst for justice will not see, that the merciful will not see. Do not suppose that only those who are pure in heart will, as it were, see, with the others separated from the vision. For all these are the very same persons. They shall see, and not for this reason shall they see, because they are poor in spirit, because they are meek, because they mourn, because they hunger and thirst for justice, because they are merciful: but because they are pure in heart. Just as if bodily works were fitted to bodily members, and someone said, for example: Blessed are those who have feet, for they shall walk: blessed are those who have hands, for they shall work: blessed are those who have a voice, for they shall cry out: blessed are those who have mouth and tongue, for they shall speak: blessed are those who have eyes, for they shall see. So, composing as it were spiritual members, he taught what pertains to what. Humility is fit for having the kingdom of heaven; meekness fit for possessing the earth; mourning fit for consolation; hunger and thirst for justice fit for satiety; mercy fit for obtaining mercy; a pure heart fit for seeing God.

**10. 10.** If, then, we desire to see God, by what shall this eye be cleansed? For who would not take care, who would not seek that by which he may cleanse the eye by which he can see him whom he desires with all his affection? The divine testimony has expressed this: *cleansing their hearts by faith*[^26]. Faith in God cleanses the heart, the pure heart sees God. But because faith itself is sometimes defined by men who deceive themselves as if it were enough merely to believe (for some promise themselves even the sight of God and the kingdom of heaven, believing and living wickedly); against these, kindled and as it were vexed with spiritual love, the apostle James says in his Epistle: *You believe that God is one*[^27]. You applaud yourself for your faith: for you notice that many impious men reckon there are many gods, and you rejoice in yourself, believing that God is one. *You do well. The demons too believe, and tremble*[^28]. Will these too see God? They will see who are pure in heart. But who would call unclean spirits pure in heart? And yet *they believe, and tremble.*

**10. 11.** Our faith must be distinguished from the faith of demons. For our faith cleanses the heart: but their faith makes them guilty. For they do evil, and therefore say to the Lord: *What have we to do with you?*[^29] When you hear the demons say this, do you think they do not recognize him? *We know*, they say, *who you are. You are the Son of God*[^30]. Peter says this, and is praised[^31]: a demon says this, and is condemned. Whence is this, except that the voice is equal, and the heart unequal? Let us distinguish, then, our faith, and let it not suffice to believe. It is not such a faith that cleanses the heart. *Cleansing*, it says, *their hearts by faith*[^32]. But by what faith, by what kind of faith, except that which Paul the Apostle defines, where he says: *Faith that works through love*[^33]? This faith distinguishes ours from the faith of demons, distinguishes it from the shameful and lost ways of men. *Faith*, he says. What faith? *That works through love*, hopes for what God promises. Nothing is more weighed than this definition, nothing more perfect. So those three are there. It is necessary that in him in whom there is *faith that works through love*, he hope for what God promises. Hope, then, is the companion of faith. For hope is necessary, as long as we do not see what we believe; lest perhaps, not seeing and despairing, we faint. It saddens us that we do not see: but it consoles us that we hope we shall see. Hope, then, is present, and is the companion of faith. Then love too, by which we desire, by which we strive to attain, by which we burn, by which we hunger and thirst. This too is applied: and there will be faith, hope, and love. For how will love not be there, since love is nothing else than charity? But faith itself was defined as *that which works through love.* Take away faith, and what you believe perishes; take away love, and what you do perishes. For it pertains to faith that you believe; to love, that you act. For if you believe and do not love, you do not move yourself to a good work: and if you move, you move as a servant, not as a son: fearing punishment, not loving justice. That faith, then, I say, cleanses the heart, *which works through love.*

**11. 12.** And now what does that faith itself do? With so many testimonies of the Scriptures, with so manifold a reading, with so varied and copious an exhortation, what does it do, except that we may see now through a mirror in an enigma, afterward face to face[^34]? But again do not return to that face of yours. Think of the face of the heart. Compel your heart to think of divine things, force it, urge it. Whatever like a body occurs to you as you think, cast it away. You cannot yet say: "This is it": at least say: "This is not it." For when will you say: "This is God"? Not even when you will see him: for ineffable is what you will see. The Apostle says he was caught up to the third heaven, and heard ineffable words[^35]. If the words are ineffable, what is that of which they are the words? As you think, then, of God, there occurs to you perhaps in human form some wondrous and most ample greatness: you have set it in the gaze of your thought, as something great, most ample, vast, spread out in an enormous mass. You have ended it somewhere. If you have ended it, God is not. If you have not ended it, where is the face? You think of a mass, and that you may distinguish members, you bound the mass. For you cannot otherwise distinguish members, unless you give the mass an end. What are you doing, foolish and carnal thought? You have made a great mass, and the greater the more you thought you honored God further. Another adds one cubit, and makes it greater.

**12. 13.** But I have read, you say. What have you read, you who have understood nothing? And yet, tell me, what have you read? Let us not drive away the little one playing in heart. Tell me, what have you read? *Heaven is my throne, and the earth is the footstool of my feet*[^36]. I hear; I have read it too: but you perhaps think yourself the better, because you have read and believed. I too believe what you have said. Let us believe together. What do I say? Let us seek together. Behold, hold what you have read and believed: *Heaven is my throne*, that is, my seat; for "throne" in Greek is "seat" in Latin: *and the earth is the footstool of my feet.* Have you not read this too: *Who has measured heaven with a span?*[^37] I think you have read it: you recognize it, and you confess that you believe it. For there we read both, both we believe. Now think, then, and teach me: I take you as teacher, and make myself the little one. Teach me, I beg you. Who is it that sits in his own span?

**13. 14.** Behold, you have drawn the figures and lineaments of God's members from the human body. But perhaps it has crept upon you to think that we were made to the image of God according to the body. Meanwhile I take it up to be considered, examined, inquired into, shaken out by disputing. If you please, hear me: because in what pleased you, I have heard you. God sits in heaven, and measures heaven with a span. Does the same heaven become broad when he sits, and narrow when he measures it? Or is God himself as great in sitting as in his span? If this is so, God did not make us to his likeness: for we have a span much narrower than the part of the body by which we sit. But if he is as broad in his span as broad in his sitting, he made our members unequal. This is not a likeness. Let such an idol, then, be ashamed in a Christian heart. Accordingly, take "heaven" for all the saints. Because "earth" too is said for all who are on the earth: *Let all the earth adore you*[^38]. If we speak well according to those who dwell on the earth, *Let all the earth adore you*: we speak well also according to those who dwell in heaven, Let all heaven bear you. For the saints themselves who dwell on earth tread the earth with the flesh, dwell in heaven with the heart. For not in vain are they admonished to have the heart on high, and when admonished they answer that it is so; or in vain is it said: *If you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^39]. Insofar, then, as they dwell there, they too bear God, and are heaven; because they are God's seat; and when they announce the words of God: *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^40].

**14. 15.** Return, then, with me to the face of the heart: prepare it. Within is he to whom God speaks. The ears, eyes, and other visible members are the dwelling or the instrument of a certain inner being. Within is the man where Christ dwells for now through faith: there to dwell by the presence of his divinity, when we have known what is the breadth, length, height, depth; and have known also the love of Christ surpassing knowledge, that we may be filled unto all the fullness of God[^41]. Now, then, if this understanding does not displease you, summon yourself to comprehend the breadth, length, height, depth. Do not run about with the imagination of thought through worldly spaces, and through the comprehensible greatness of this so great mass. In yourself attend to what I say. Breadth is in good works; length is in the longanimity and perseverance of good works; height is in the expectation of the rewards above; on account of which height it is said to you to have the heart on high. Work well, and persevere in good works, on account of the benefits of God. Esteem earthly things as nothing, lest, when this earth of yours is disturbed by some scourge of that wise One, you say you worshiped God without cause, did good works without cause, persevered in good works without cause. For by doing good works you had, as it were, breadth: by persevering in them you had, as it were, length: but by seeking earthly things you did not have height. Attend to the depth: the grace of God is in the secret of his will. *For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor?*[^42] And: *Your judgments are like a great deep*[^43].

**15. 16.** This conduct of working well, of persevering in it, of expecting things above, of God's giving grace secretly—wisdom, not folly; and of not finding fault as to why one man is thus, another thus; for there is no iniquity with God[^44]—this conduct, then, if you please, fit also to the cross of your Lord. For not in vain did he choose such a death, in whose power it was either to die or not to die. If it was in his power to die and not to die, why was it not in his power to die in this way or that? Not in vain, then, did he choose the cross, where he crucified you to this world. For breadth is in the cross the transverse wood, where the hands are fixed: on account of the signifying of good works. Length is in that part of the wood which tends from the transverse down to the earth. For there the body is crucified, and in a way stands: and that standing signifies perseverance. But height is in that wood which projects upward from the same transverse toward the head: that by it the expectation of things above is signified. Where is the depth, except in that part which is fixed in the earth? For grace is hidden, and lies in secret. It is not seen, but from it projects what is seen. After these things, if you have comprehended all these, not only by understanding but also by doing (for *a good understanding is to all who do it*[^45]); then now extend yourself, if you can, to recognizing the knowledge of the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge. When you have arrived, you will be filled unto all the fullness of God. Then will be that face to face. But you will be filled unto all the fullness of God, not that God be full of you, but that you be full of God. Seek there, if you can, a bodily face. Now let trifles be taken away from the gaze of the mind. Let the little child cast away his toys, let him learn to handle greater things. We too are little ones in many things: and when we were more than we are, we were borne with by the greater. Follow peace with all, and sanctification, without which no one will be able to see God[^46]. For by this too the heart is cleansed: because there is the faith that works through love. Hence: *Blessed are the merciful, for they shall see God*[^47].
