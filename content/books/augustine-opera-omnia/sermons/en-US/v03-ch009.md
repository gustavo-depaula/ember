# Sermon 58: Again on the Lord's Prayer in Matthew 6:9-13, to the Candidates for Baptism

**1. 1.** You have recited the Creed, in which the faith is briefly comprehended and contained. I have already told you before what the apostle Paul says: *How will they call upon him in whom they have not believed?*[^1] Because, then, you have both received and held and recited how God is to be believed in: receive today how God is to be called upon. The Son himself, as you heard when the Gospel was read, taught his disciples and his faithful this Prayer. We have hope of obtaining our cause, when such a jurist has dictated our prayers for us. The Assessor of the Father, as you have confessed, who sits at the right hand of the Father: he himself is our advocate, who is to be our judge. For from there he is to come to judge the living and the dead. Hold, then, this Prayer too, which you are to recite eight days from now. But whoever of you have not recited the Creed well, you have time, hold it: because on the Sabbath day, with all who will be present hearing, you are to recite it, on the last Sabbath day, on which day you are to be baptized. But eight days from this present day you are to recite this Prayer, which today you have received.

**2. 2.** Its head is: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^2]. We have found a Father in heaven: let us attend to how we live on earth. For he who has found such a Father ought so to live that he be worthy to come to his inheritance. But we say in common: *Our Father.* How great the condescension! This the emperor says, this the beggar says; this the servant says, this his master says. Together they say: *Our Father, who are in heaven.* They understand, then, that they are brothers, when they have one Father. But let not a master disdain to have his servant as a brother, whom the Lord Christ willed to have as a brother.

**2. 3.** *Hallowed be your name*, we say: *Your kingdom come*[^3]. The hallowing of the name of God is that by which we are made holy. For his name is always holy. We wish also that his kingdom come: it will come, even if we do not wish it; but to wish and pray that his kingdom come is nothing else than to wish from him that he make us worthy of his kingdom, lest perhaps, which God forbid, it come, and not come to us. For to many it will not come, which nevertheless is going to come. For it will come to those to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^4]. It will not come to those to whom it will be said: *Depart from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire*[^5]. When, then, we say: *Your kingdom come*, we pray that it may come to us. What is, that it may come to us? That it may find us good. This, then, we pray, that he make us good: for then his kingdom will come to us.

**3. 4.** We add: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth*[^6]. The angels serve you in heaven, may we serve you on earth. The angels do not offend you in heaven, may we not offend you on earth. As they do your will, so may we do it too. And here what do we pray, except that we be good? For when we do the will of God (for he himself without doubt does his own), then his will is done in us. And we rightly understand it in another way: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* We receive the precept of God, and it pleases us, it pleases our mind. For we delight in the law of God according to the inner man[^7]. Then his will is done in heaven. For our spirit is compared to heaven, but our flesh to earth. What, then, is: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth?* That as your command pleases our mind, so our flesh may consent to it; and that quarrel may be taken out of the way which is described by the Apostle: *For the flesh lusts against the spirit, but the spirit against the flesh*[^8]. When the spirit lusts against the flesh, his will is already done in heaven: when the flesh does not lust against the spirit, his will is already done on earth. But there will be full concord when he himself wills: let there be a fight for now, that there may be a victory. It can also be well understood thus: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth*: so that we take heaven for the Church, because it bears God; but earth for the unbelievers, to whom it was said: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^9]. When, then, we pray for our enemies, the enemies of the Church, the enemies of the Christian name, this we pray, that his will be done, *as in heaven, so also on earth*, that is, as in your faithful, so also in your blasphemers; that all may become heaven.

**4. 5.** There follows: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^10]. This prayer can be taken simply as our pouring it out for daily sustenance, that it may abound to us; and if it does not abound, that it may not be lacking to us. He said *daily*, as long as it is called *today*[^11]. Daily we live, daily we rise, daily we are filled, daily we hunger. Let him give us daily bread. Why did he not also say "and clothing"? For our sustenance is in food and drink, our clothing in raiment and shelter. Let a man desire nothing more. Since the Apostle says: *We brought nothing into this world; but neither can we take anything away: having food and clothing, let us be content with these*[^12]. Let avarice perish, and nature is rich. So if it pertains to daily sustenance—because this too is well understood, that we say: *Give us this day our daily bread*—let us not wonder if, bread being named, the other necessities are understood too. As when Joseph invited his brothers: *Those men*, he says, *will eat bread with me today*[^13]. Were they going to eat bread alone? But from bread alone the rest is understood. So when we ask for daily bread, we beg whatever is necessary for us on earth on account of our flesh. But what does the Lord Jesus say? *Seek first the kingdom of God and his justice, and all these things will be added to you*[^14]. It is also very well understood: *Give us this day our daily bread*, your Eucharist, the daily food. For the faithful know what they receive, and it is good for them to receive the daily bread necessary for this time. They ask for themselves, that they may become good, that they may persevere in goodness and faith and a good life. This they wish, this they pray: because if they do not persevere in a good life, they will be separated from that bread. Therefore: *Give us this day our daily bread*, what is it? Let us so live that we are not separated from your altar. And the word of God which is daily opened to you, and in a way broken, is daily bread. And as bellies hunger for that bread, so minds hunger for this. This too, then, we ask simply, and whatever is necessary for our soul and our flesh in this life is included in the daily bread.

**5. 6.** *Forgive us our debts*[^15], we say, and let us say it; because we say what is true. For who lives here in the flesh and has no debts? Who is a man living so that this Prayer is not necessary for him? He can puff himself up, he cannot justify himself. It is good for him to imitate the Publican, and not to swell like the Pharisee, who went up into the temple, and boasted of his merits, and covered his wounds. But the other knew why he had gone up, who said: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^16]. This the Lord Jesus—consider, my brothers—this the Lord Jesus taught his disciples to pray, those great first apostles of his, our rams. If, then, the rams pray for the forgiveness of their sins, what ought the lambs to do, of whom it is said: *Bring to the Lord the young of rams?*[^17] You know, then, that you recited this in the Creed, because among the other things you named the forgiveness of sins. There is one forgiveness of sins, which is given once; another, which is given daily. There is one forgiveness of sins, which is given once in holy Baptism; another, which, as long as we live here, is given in the Lord's Prayer. On account of which we say: *Forgive us our debts.*

**6. 7.** And God has entered with us into a pact and an agreement, and a firm bond, that we say: *As we too forgive our debtors*[^18]. He who wishes to say effectively: *Forgive us our debts*, let him say truthfully: *As we too forgive our debtors.* If he either does not say what is latter, or says it falsely, he says what is former in vain. Most of all we say to you, Forgive all things from your hearts, you who approach holy Baptism. And you, the faithful, who through this occasion hear this Prayer and our explanation, forgive everything whatever you have against anyone from your hearts: forgive there, where God sees. For sometimes a man forgives with the mouth, and holds it in the heart: he forgives with the mouth on account of men, and holds it in the heart, not fearing the eyes of God. Utterly forgive; whatever it is that you have held until these days, at least in these days forgive it. The sun ought not to have set upon your anger[^19], and many suns have passed. Let your anger too pass at last; we now celebrate the days of the great sun: of that sun of which Scripture says: *The sun of justice shall rise for you, and healing in his wings*[^20]. What is *in his wings?* In his protection. Whence in the Psalm it is said: *Under the shadow of your wings protect me*[^21]. But others, who on the day of judgment will be repenting too late and grieving fruitlessly, foretold by Wisdom, what will they then say, doing penance and groaning for anguish of spirit? *What did pride profit us? And what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All those things have passed away like a shadow*[^22]. And among the rest: *So we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of justice did not shine for us, and the sun did not rise for us*[^23]. That sun rises for the just: but this visible sun God makes to rise daily upon the good and the evil[^24]. To seeing that sun the just belong: for now that sun dwells in our hearts through faith. If, then, you are angry, let this sun not set in your heart upon your anger: *Let not the sun set upon your anger*[^25]; lest perhaps you grow angry, and the sun of justice set for you, and you remain in darkness.

**7. 8.** But do not think that anger is nothing. *My eye is troubled*, says the prophet, *for anger*[^26]. Surely, one whose eye is troubled cannot see the sun: and if he tries to see it, it is a punishment to him, not a pleasure. What is anger? The desire of revenge. A man wishes to be avenged; and Christ is not yet avenged, the holy martyrs are not yet avenged! Still the patience of God waits, that the enemies of Christ may be converted, that the enemies of the martyrs may be converted: who are we, that we should seek revenge? If God sought it from us, where would we remain? He who harmed us in nothing does not wish to avenge himself on us: and we seek to be avenged, who almost daily offend God! So forgive: forgive from the heart. You are angry, do not sin: *Be angry, and do not sin*[^27]. *Be angry* as men, if you are overcome; *and do not sin* by holding anger in your heart (because if you hold it, you hold it against yourselves), lest you not enter into that light. So forgive. What is anger? The desire of revenge. What is hatred? Inveterate anger. If anger has become inveterate, it is now called hatred. Which he seems to confess who, when he had said: *My eye is troubled for anger*, added: *I have grown old among all my enemies*[^28]. What was anger, when it was new, has become hatred; because it has turned into old age. Anger is a speck, hatred is a beam. Sometimes we reprove one who is angry, and hold hatred in our heart; and Christ says to us: *You see the speck in your brother's eye, and do not see the beam in your own eye*[^29]. Whence did the speck grow, so as to make a beam? Because it was not at once plucked out. Because you allowed the sun to go out and come in so many times upon your anger, you made it old; you drew on evil suspicions, and watered the speck, and by watering you nourished it, and by nourishing you made it a beam. Be terrified at least when it is said: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^30]. You did not draw the sword, you did not make a wound in the flesh, you did not slay the body with any blow: the mere thought of hatred is in your heart, and you are held a murderer; you are guilty before the eyes of God. He lives, and you have killed. As far as concerns you, you have killed the one you hate. Amend yourself, correct yourself. If there were scorpions or asps in your houses, how you would labor to purge your houses, that you might dwell secure! You grow angry, and angers become inveterate in your hearts, so many hatreds are made, so many beams, so many scorpions, so many serpents; and the house of God, your heart, you do not wish to purge? Do, then, what was said: *As we too forgive our debtors*: and securely say: *Forgive us our debts.* Because without debts you cannot live on this earth. But those are other things, the great crimes which it is good for you to have forgiven in Baptism, and from which you ought always to be estranged: others are the daily sins, without which a man cannot live here, on account of which a daily prayer is necessary, with its own pact, with its own agreement: that as it is cheerfully said: *Forgive us our debts*, so it may be truthfully said: *As we too forgive our debtors.* Then, these things we have said about past sins, what about the rest?

**8. 9.** *Lead us not into temptation*[^31]: forgive what we have done, and grant that we commit no others. For whoever is conquered by temptation himself commits sin. For, says the apostle James: *Let no one, when he is tempted, say that he is tempted by God. For God is not a tempter to evils: but he himself tempts no one. But each one is tempted, drawn away and enticed by his own desire. Then desire, when it has conceived, brings forth sin: but sin, when it has been completed, begets death*[^32]. So do not be drawn away by desire: do not consent to your desire. There is nothing for it to conceive from, except from you. You have consented: as it were, you have lain together in your heart. Desire has arisen: deny yourself to it, do not follow it. It is unlawful, it is wanton, it is shameful, it estranges you from God. Do not give the embrace of consent, lest you bewail the birth: because if you consent, that is, when you have embraced, it conceives. *When desire has conceived, it brings forth sin.* Do you not yet fear? *Sin begets death:* fear at least death. If you do not fear sin, fear whither sin leads. Sin is sweet: but death is bitter. This is the unhappiness of men: that for what they sin, they leave it here when dying, and carry the sins themselves with them. You sin for money, it must be left here: you sin for an estate, it must be left here: you sin for a woman, she must be left here: and whatever it is for which you sin, when you have closed your eyes in death, you leave it here, and carry the sin itself which you commit with you.

**9. 10.** Let sins be forgiven: let past ones be forgiven, let future ones cease. But you cannot live here without them: let them be either smaller, or slight, or light. But let the very light and small ones not be despised. From small drops rivers are filled. Let not even the smaller ones be despised. Through narrow cracks water seeps into the ship, the bilge fills, and if the bilge is despised, the ship sinks. But the sailors do not cease: their hands keep going: they go, that the bilge may be daily drained. So let your hands too keep going, that you may daily drain the bilge. What is, let the hands keep going? Let them give, do good works, let your hands keep going. *Break your bread for the hungry, and bring the needy without shelter into your house: if you see one naked, clothe him*[^33]. Do as much as you can, do whence you can, do cheerfully, and securely send forth your prayer. It will have two wings, the twin almsgivings. What are the twin almsgivings? *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given you*[^34]. One almsgiving is that which is done from the heart, when you forgive your brother his sin. The other almsgiving is that which is done from your substance, when you hold out bread to the poor. Do both, lest your prayer remain with one wing.

**9. 11.** So when we have said: *Lead us not into temptation*; there follows: *But deliver us from evil*[^35]. He who wishes to be delivered from evil testifies that he is in evil. Therefore the Apostle says: *Redeeming the time, because the days are evil*[^36]. But *who is the one who wishes for life, and loves to see good days?*[^37] When every man in this flesh has only evil days, who does not wish it? Do what follows: *Restrain your tongue from evil, and let your lips speak no guile; turn away from evil, and do good; seek peace, and follow it*[^38]: and you will be rid of evil days, and what you prayed will be fulfilled: *Deliver us from evil.*

**10. 12.** The three upper petitions, then—*Hallowed be your name, Your kingdom come, Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth*—are eternal. But the four following pertain to this life. *Give us this day our daily bread:* shall we ask daily for daily bread, when we have come to that fullness? *Forgive us our debts:* shall we say it in that kingdom, when we shall have no debts? *Lead us not into temptation:* shall we be able to say it then, when there will be no temptation? *Deliver us from evil:* shall we say it, when there will be nothing from which we need to be delivered? These four, then, are necessary for us on account of our daily life, but those three on account of the eternal one. But let us ask all things, that we may come to that life; and here let us pray that we be not separated from it. The prayer must be said by you daily, when you have been baptized. For in church, at the altar of God, this Lord's Prayer is said daily, and the faithful hear it. We do not fear, then, that you will hold it less diligently: because even if any of you cannot hold it perfectly, by hearing it daily he will hold it.

**11. 13.** Therefore on the Sabbath day, when we are to keep vigil in the mercy of God, you are to recite, not the Prayer, but the Creed. For now, unless you hold the Creed, you do not hear the Creed daily in church, among the people. But when you have come to hold it, that you may not forget it, say it daily; when you rise, when you settle yourselves to sleep, recite your Creed, recite it to the Lord, call yourselves to mind, do not be loath to repeat it. For repetition is good, lest forgetfulness creep up. Do not say: I said it yesterday, I said it today, I say it daily, I hold it well. Call your faith to mind, look at yourself: let your Creed be as a mirror to you. There see yourself, whether you believe all that you confess you believe, and rejoice daily in your faith. Let it be your riches, let these be, in a way, the daily garments of your mind. Do you not clothe yourself when you rise? So too, by calling to mind your Creed, clothe your soul, lest perhaps forgetfulness strip it, and you remain naked, and that come about which the Apostle says, which be far from you: *If indeed, having put it off, we be not found naked*[^39]. For we shall be clothed with our faith; and this faith is both a tunic and a breastplate: a tunic, against confusion; a breastplate, against adversity. But when we have come to that place where we shall reign, there is no need to say the Creed: we shall see God, God himself will be our vision; the vision of God will be the reward of this faith.
