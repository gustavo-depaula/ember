# Sermon 168

**1. 1.** By the readings, the songs, and the divine sermons, and—what is the chief thing—by his grace, may the Lord build up your heart; that what you hear as true, you may hear not unto judgment, but unto reward. He will do this, since he who promised is able also to do it. So Abraham believed, giving glory to God, whence also believing most fully that what he promised he is able also to do[^1]. Great is our joy; he promised us to Abraham; we are the sons of the promise. For when it was said to Abraham, *In your seed all nations will be blessed*[^2], we were promised. Therefore he himself made us sons of the faith of Abraham, who is able to do what he promised. Let no one say: I did it. For God does not promise, and you do it. But it can rightly be said that what you promise, God does. For you are weak, you are not almighty. When, then, you promise, unless God does it, your promise is empty. But the promise of God does not depend on you, but on him. But I, you say, believed. I grant it. You say true: you believed, but you did not give yourself faith. But whence did you believe, except from faith? Faith in you is the gift of God.

**2. 2.** Hear the Apostle himself, the disputant of faith, and the great defender of grace; hear him saying, *Peace to the brothers, and love with faith*[^3]. He said three great things: Peace, love, faith. He began from the end, he ended at the beginning. For the beginning is in faith, the end in peace. For that by which we believe is faith itself. But the faith of Christians ought to be such, not of demons. For, as the apostle James says, *The demons too believe, and tremble*[^4]. And the demons said to Christ, *You are the Son of God, we know who you are*[^5]; are they therefore going to reign with the Son of God? Far from it! The faith of demons, then, is to be distinguished from the faith of the saints. Plainly it is to be distinguished, vigilantly and diligently. For Peter too said this to the Lord asking, *Who do you say that I am? You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*. And the Lord: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah*[^6]. O Lord, the demons too said this to you; why are they not blessed? Why? Because the demons said it out of fear, Peter out of love. Therefore the beginning is from faith. But by what kind of faith? That which the Apostle defined: *Neither circumcision avails anything, nor uncircumcision, but faith*. Tell me, what faith? *That which works through love*[^7]. This faith the demons do not have, which works through love; but only the servants of God, only the holy ones of God, only by faith the sons of Abraham, only the sons of love, the sons of the promise; therefore it was said, *and love*. Those three things were said by the Apostle: *Peace to the brothers, and love with faith. Peace to the brothers*. Whence peace? *And love*. Whence love? *With faith*. For if you do not believe, you do not love. The Apostle said, then, thus beginning from the end and coming to the beginning: *Peace, love, with faith*. Let us say: Faith, love, peace. Believe, love, reign. For if you believe and do not love, you have not yet distinguished your faith from those who trembled and said, *We know who you are, the Son of God*. Therefore do you love; because love with faith itself leads you to peace. What peace? True peace, full peace, solid peace, secure peace; where there is no plague, no enemy. That peace is the end of all good desires. *Love with faith*; and if you say it thus, you say it well: Faith with love.

**3. 3.** The Apostle, then, mentioned great goods: *Peace to the brothers, and love with faith*; great goods. But let him say whence these goods are; whence they are, from us, or from God? If you say: From us, you glory in yourself, not in God. But if you have learned what the same Apostle says, *That he who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^8]; confess that peace, love with faith, is yours only from God. But you answer me: You say this, prove what you say. I prove it; I will call the Apostle himself as witness. Behold, you have it; the Apostle said, *Peace to the brothers, and love with faith*. He himself said it. What did he himself say? See, it follows: *Peace to the brothers, and love with faith, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ*[^9]. *What*, then, *do you have that you have not received? But if you have received it, why do you glory as if you had not received it*[^10]? For if Abraham gloried, he gloried from faith. What is full and perfect faith? That which believes all our goods to be from God, and faith itself. Again the Apostle says, *I obtained mercy*. O confession! He did not say: I obtained mercy, because I was faithful; but, *that I might be faithful, I obtained mercy*[^11].

**4. 4.** Let us come to his beginnings, let us see Saul raging, let us watch him furious, let us watch him panting with hatreds, and thirsting for blood. Let us watch him, brothers; a great spectacle. Behold, after the killing of Stephen, after the blood of God's witness was poured out by stones, where he kept the garments of those who stoned him, so that even in their hands he might stone, then were dispersed the brothers who had been gathered at Jerusalem; and he, raging—for whom it was too little to have seen and shed the blood of Stephen—received from the chief priests authority to go to Damascus, and whatever Christians he found there, to lead them away bound. And he was going. This was the way of Paul, whose way was not yet Christ; still Saul's, not yet Paul's. He was going. What did he have in his heart? What, but evil? Give me his merits. If you seek merits, they are of condemnation, not of liberation. He was going, then, to rage against the members of Christ, going to shed blood, going to be a wolf about to be a shepherd; so he was going. For he could not go otherwise to the things for which he was going. And as he thus walks, thinks, pants for slaughter; while anger leads his feet, hatred moves his members, while he goes on and walks, the chattel obeys cruelty; and behold, a voice from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*[^12]? Behold why he said, *I obtained mercy, that I might be faithful*. He was unfaithful; it is too little—he was, in that very unfaithfulness, cruel; but he obtained mercy, that he might be faithful. What will you say to God saying: I wish this? Therefore, Lord, the one who did so great things, who desired to do so great evils against your saints, you reckon worthy of such mercy? I wish this. *Or is your eye evil, because I am good*[^13]?

**5. 5.** Have faith; but that you may have faith, pray with faith. But you could not pray with faith, unless you had faith. For nothing prays but faith. *For how will they call upon him in whom they have not believed? Or how will they believe him whom they have not heard? But how will they hear without a preacher? Or how will they preach, if they are not sent*[^14]? Therefore we speak, because we have been sent. Hear us, hear him through us. So, someone says, we call upon God, that he give us to persevere in these goods which we have, and add the goods we do not have. The faith that asks, then, went before. Surely God gives all. For that he might give to me, I asked; that I might ask, I first believed. Therefore I gave to myself what I believed, and God gave what, believing, I prayed for. The question is resolved—for it is no small one. I see you saying this: that you first gave something to God, that he might give the rest to you. For you gave him your faith and your prayer. And where is what the Apostle says, *For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, and it shall be repaid to him*[^15]? Behold what kind you wish to be. Did you, then, first give to God, and give this which God did not give to you? Did you find from where you might give? O beggar man, whence did you have it? Whence, then, would you give? Did you have anything? *For what do you have that you have not received*[^16]? Therefore you give to God from God's; from what he gave to you, he receives from you. For your beggary, unless he had first given, would remain most empty.

**6. 6.** Hear whence you may prove this more evidently. Behold, you, because you believed, received; what do we say of those who have not yet believed, as Saul was, who had not yet believed? But he received that he might believe; after he believed in Christ, then he began to call upon Christ. From him he received that he might believe, and by believing call upon him, and by calling upon him receive the rest. What do we suppose, brothers? Saul, before he believed, did those who had believed pray for him, or not? Let it be said to me, if they did not pray for him, why did Stephen say, *Lord, do not lay this sin against them*[^17]? Prayer was made both for him and for other unbelievers, that they might believe. Behold, they did not yet have faith, and by the prayers of the faithful they received faith. They did not yet have what they might offer to God; because they had not yet obtained mercy, that they might be faithful. Finally, after this Saul was converted, struck down and raised up by one voice—struck down the persecutor, raised up the preacher; after he began to evangelize the faith which he once ravaged, what did he say of himself? *But I was unknown by face to the churches of Judea that are in Christ; they only heard that he who once persecuted us now evangelizes the faith which he once ravaged; and in me they magnified God*[^18]. Surely he did not say: And in me they magnified me? And in me, who evangelized the faith which I once ravaged, they did not magnify me, but God. Therefore he himself brought it about that Saul, his old tunic laid down—ragged with sins, bloody with slaughters—that, with this tunic laid down, he might receive the tunic of humility, and from Saul become Paul.

**7. 7.** What is "Paul"? "Least." *For I am the least of the apostles*[^19]. Behold what "Paul" is. For "Paul" in Latin is "little." So we speak when we say: After a little ("paulum") I see you; a little after ("paulo post") I do that. What is "a little after"? A small while after; after a little, after a small while. Why, then, Paul? Because little. Little, because last. *For I am*, he says, *the last of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God*[^20]. You say well; whence you ought to have been condemned, from him you received whence you ought to be crowned. From whom did you receive, whence you ought to be crowned? From whom did he receive? Do you wish to hear? Do not hear me, hear him: *I am not*, he says, *worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God; but by the grace of God I am what I am*. Therefore what you were, you were by your own iniquity; what you are, you are by the grace of God. *And his grace*, he says, *was not empty in me*. Behold, he evangelizes the faith which he once ravaged; nor is that grace empty in him, who says, *It was not empty in me, but I labored more than all of them*[^21]. Watch out, you have begun to raise yourself up. Where are you, Paul? Surely you were little. *I labored more than all of them*. Tell me, whence? *For what do you have that you have not received*[^22]? At once he looked back; and when he had said, *I labored more than all of them*; as though he were terrified at his own words, he at once subjected himself, humble Paul: *Yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^23].

**7. 8.** Therefore, my brothers, that you may know that faith too is ours from the Lord God, pray for those who have not yet believed. If anyone has an unbelieving friend, I advise him to pray for him. Is there really need for me to advise him? The husband is a Christian, the wife is an unbeliever; does he not pray for his wife, that she may believe? The wife is a Christian, the husband is an unbeliever; does the religious woman not pray for her husband, that he may believe? When he who prays prays this; what does he pray, but that God give him faith? Therefore faith is the gift of God. Let no one exalt himself, let no one arrogate to himself, as though he had given himself something. *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^24].
