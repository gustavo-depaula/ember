# Sermon 264: On the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** Many are the hidden sacred meanings of the divine Scriptures, whether those that we ourselves still have to seek, or those which the Lord has already condescended to reveal to our lowliness: but time does not suffice to open these to your Holiness. For I know that especially in these days the church is filled with such people who would rather depart than come; and they find us burdensome, if we ever converse rather long: who yet, at their dinners, to which they hasten, if they are kept until evening, neither labor nor refuse, nor at least ever depart with any shame. Yet lest we defraud those who come hungry, even if briefly, we will not be silent about the sacred meaning of this matter, that our Lord Jesus Christ ascended with that body in which he rose.

**2.** Surely it was because of the weakness of his disciples: for even in that number there were not lacking those whom the devil tempted with unbelief, so that a certain disciple of his, even in the very form in which he had known him, yet had no more faith in the living members than in the fresh scars: therefore for their strengthening he condescended after the resurrection to live with them for forty whole days, from the very day of his passion to this present day, going in and out, eating and drinking, as the Scripture says; confirming that this had been restored to their eyes after the resurrection, which had been taken away through the cross. Nevertheless he did not wish them to remain in the flesh, nor to hold him longer by carnal love. For they wished him to be always carnally with them with the same mind by which Peter too feared him to suffer. For they saw with them a master, a comforter and consoler and protector, a man, such as they saw themselves to be. If they did not see something of the kind, they believed him absent; though he is everywhere present by majesty. But he truly watched over them, as he himself condescended to say, like a hen her chicks. For as a hen, because of the weakness of her chicks, herself too grows weak. For if you remember, before our eyes are so many birds that make chicks; we do not see any bird grow weak with its chicks, except the hen: and therefore the Lord drew a likeness from her; because for our weakness he himself too, by the taking up of flesh, condescended to grow weak. But it was fitting that they be raised up somewhat, and now begin to think of him spiritually, as the Word of the Father, God with God, through whom all things were made: and the flesh which they saw did not allow them. It profited them, then, to be strengthened in faith through his company with them for forty days: but it profited them more that he should withdraw himself from their eyes; and that he who had lived on earth as a brother should help them from heaven as the Lord; and that they should learn to think of him according to God. For this the Evangelist John sounded: if anyone notices, if anyone understands. For the Lord says: *Let not your heart be troubled. If you loved me*, he says, *you would rejoice, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I*[^1]. And in another place, he says: *I and the Father are one*[^2]. And he claims for himself so great an equality, not by robbery but by nature, that he said this to a certain disciple who said to him: *Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us*: and he: *Philip, so long a time I am with you, and you do not know the Father? He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^3]. What does it mean: *He who has seen me?* If according to the eyes of the flesh, those too saw him who crucified him. What, then, does it mean: *He who has seen me*; except, he who has understood, who has seen with the eye of the heart? For as there are inner ears, which the Lord sought when he said: *He who has ears, let him hear*[^4], though no deaf man stood before him: so there is also an inner gaze of the heart, by which, if anyone has seen the Lord, he has seen the Father; because he is equal to the Father.

**3.** Hear the Apostle wishing to commend to us his mercy, because for our sake he grew weak, that he might gather his chicks under his wings, teaching also the other disciples, that they too should sympathize with the weakness of the weak, who from a common weakness had ascended to some firmness; when he came down from heavenly firmness to our weakness: he says to them: *Have this mind in you, which also was in Christ Jesus.* Deign, he says, to imitate the Son of God by sympathy with the little ones. *Who, since he was in the form of God.* Already by saying, *he was in the form of God*, he shows him equal to God. For the form is not less than him whose form it is. For if it is less, it is not the form. Yet lest anyone doubt, he added, and set down the very Word, by which he would shut the mouths of the sacrilegious: *Who, since he was in the form of God*, he says, *did not think it robbery to be equal to God.* What is it, dearest brothers, that the Apostle says: *He did not think it robbery?* Because by nature he is equal. To whom, then, was equality with God a robbery? To the first man, to whom it was said: *Taste, and you will be as gods*[^5]. He wished by robbery to reach out to equality, and by punishment lost immortality. For he to whom it was not a robbery *did not think it robbery to be equal to God.* If, then, not a robbery; it is nature, whole fellowship, and the highest comparison itself. But what did he do? *He emptied himself*, he says, *taking the form of a servant; made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man: he humbled himself, made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross*[^6]. It was too little to say death, and he showed the kind of death. Why also the kind of death? Because many are ready for death: for many say: I do not fear to die, but I would wish to die in my own bed, surrounded by sons, grandsons, the tears of my wife. These seem indeed not to refuse death, but, by choosing the kind of death, are punished by fear. But he chose a kind of death, but one that is worse than all. As men choose for themselves a better kind of death, so he chose a worse one, this which was execrable to all the Jews. For he did not fear to die through false witnesses, through the sentence of a judge, he who will come to judge the living and the dead: he did not fear to die through the ignominy of the cross, that he might free all believers from all ignominy. Therefore *made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross*: yet by nature equal to God; strong in the power of his majesty, weak in the compassion of his humanity: strong, to make all things; weak, to remake all things.

**4.** Attend, then, to what John says: *If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I*[^7]. How equal, as the Apostle says? As the Lord himself: *I and the Father are one*[^8]. And in another place: *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^9]. How here: *For the Father is greater than I?* This voice, brothers, as far as the Lord inspires me to notice, was in a way that of one rebuking and consoling. For they were fixed on the man, and could not think of God. For then they would think of God, if the man were taken away from them and from their eyes, that, the familiarity which had been made with the flesh being cut off, they might learn, even with the flesh absent, to think of the divinity. Therefore he says this to them: *If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father.* Why? That with him to the Father, you may be able to think of me as equal to the Father. For therefore *he is greater than I*: still, while you see me in the flesh, the Father is still greater than I. See whether you have grasped it: for they did not know how to think of anything but a man. I say this somewhat more plainly, because of our slower brothers: but those who have understood, let them bear with the slowness of the rest and imitate the Lord himself, *who, since he was in the form of God, humbled himself, made obedient unto death*[^10]. *If you loved me*, what is this? *If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father.* *If you loved me*, what else is it but, you do not love me? What, then, do you love? The flesh which you see. For you do not want it to depart from your eyes. But *if you loved me*; what is, *me? In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^11]: which the same John says. If, then, you loved me so, as he through whom all things were made, *you would rejoice, because I go to the Father.* Why? *Because the Father is greater than I.* Still, while you see me on earth, the Father is greater than I. I will depart from your eyes; let the mortal flesh be taken away from your gaze, which was taken up because of your mortality; begin not to see this garment, which I took up in humility: yet let it be lifted into heaven, that you may learn what you should hope for. For he did not lay aside here that very tunic, which he willed to be clothed with here. For if he had laid it aside here, all would despair of the resurrection of the flesh. For now he lifted it into heaven, and there are those who doubt the resurrection of the flesh. If God showed it in himself, will he deny it to man? For God took it up out of mercy, but man has it by condition. And yet he showed it, strengthened them, and lifted it up. But the carnal sight being withdrawn from their eyes, now they did not see the man. If anything had been drawn in their hearts from carnal desire, it was as it were saddened in them. Yet they were gathered into one, and began to pray. But he was going to send, after this, with ten days intervening, the Holy Spirit, that the Holy Spirit might fill them with spiritual love, taking away from them carnal desires. He made them now so understand Christ as he was, the Word of God, God with God, through whom all things were made. But they could not be filled with such understanding, unless carnal love had departed from their eyes. And therefore he said: *If you loved me, you would rejoice, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I.* According to man he is greater than I, equal according to God: equal according to nature, greater according to the mercy of the Son. For he humbled him, not only below himself, but also below the angels, as the Scripture says. He is not less; and if you see the Son to have somewhat withdrawn, by the taking up of flesh, from equality with the Father, from which he never withdraws: but by taking flesh (for he took up a man), he was not changed. As he who takes a garment is not turned into the garment, but himself remains whole, a man within: and if a senator takes a servile garment, if perhaps he cannot enter to console someone chained in prison with his senatorial garment, he takes the dress of the prison, the dress seems base through humanity; but within the senatorial dignity remains so much the more whole, the greater the mercy by which he willed to put on what was of humility. So too the Lord, remaining God, remaining the Word, remaining wisdom, remaining the divine power, remaining in the government of the heavens, remaining in the administration of the earth, filling the angels, wholly everywhere, wholly in the world, wholly in the patriarchs, wholly in the prophets, wholly in all the saints, wholly in the womb of the Virgin, to put on flesh, to join it to himself as a bride, that the bridegroom might come forth from his bridal chamber, that he might betroth the Church a chaste virgin. For this, then, less than the Father, because man: but equal to the Father, because God. Take away, then, from your midst carnal desires. As though he said this to his apostles: You do not want to let me go (as each one does not want to let go his friend, as if saying: Be with us a while, our soul is refreshed when we see you); but it is better that you not see this flesh, and that you think of the divinity. I take myself from you outwardly, and fill you with myself inwardly. For does Christ enter the heart according to the flesh and with the flesh? According to the divinity he possesses the heart: according to the flesh he speaks through the eyes to the heart, and admonishes from outside; dwelling within, that we may be converted inwardly, and made alive from him, and formed from him; because he is the unfashioned form of all things.

**5.** Therefore if he spent forty days with his disciples, not without cause did he spend forty days. Perhaps twenty would suffice, thirty would suffice: forty days is the dispensation of this whole age. We have discoursed on this at some time because of the number ten taken four times. I remind you who heard it. For the number ten signifies all wisdom. This wisdom has been dispensed through the four parts of the world, through the whole world: and the times are divided by a fourfold arrangement. For the year has four seasons: and the world has four cardinal points. Ten, then, taken four times has the number forty. But the Lord fasted forty days, showing us that abstinence from all corruption ought to belong to the faithful, as long as they are in this world. Forty days Elijah fasted, bearing the person of Prophecy, showing that this is taught in Prophecy too. Forty days Moses fasted, who bore the person of the Law, showing that this is taught in the Law too. Forty years the people of Israel was led in the desert. Forty days the ark floated in the flood, which ark is the Church, made of unrotting wood; the unrotting wood is the souls of the saints and the just: yet having clean and unclean animals; because as long as one lives in this age and the Church is cleansed through Baptism as through a flood, it cannot but have good and bad: therefore that ark had both clean and unclean. But after Noah went out from there, he made sacrifice to God only from the clean animals. From which we ought to understand that in this ark there are both clean and unclean animals, but after this flood God receives only those who have cleansed themselves. Therefore hold all this time that is seen, brothers, for forty days. All this time, as long as we are here, is the ark in the flood: as long as Christians are baptized and cleansed through water, the ark seems to swim in the waves, which was carried on the water for forty days. But the Lord, remaining with the disciples for forty days, condescended to signify that throughout this time faith in the Incarnation of Christ is necessary for all: which is necessary for the weak. If there were now an eye that could see: *In the beginning was the Word*, that could see, that could hold, that could embrace, that could enjoy, there would be no need that the Word be made flesh and dwell among us: but because for holding and enjoying that the inner eye was blinded with the dust of sins, there was now no means by which the Word could be understood; which condescended to become flesh, that it might be entrusted to that by which it could afterward be seen, what now cannot. Because, then, the dispensation of the flesh of Christ is necessary for the faithful of this life, through which they tend to the Lord: but when one has come to that vision of the Word, all carnal dispensation will not be necessary: therefore his company in the flesh after the resurrection for forty days was necessary, that he might demonstrate that faith in the Incarnation of Christ is necessary as long as in this life the ark is taught to float in the flood. See what I say, brothers: believe in Jesus Christ born of the Virgin Mary, crucified, risen. There is no need that we inquire after this age, because we have already received it in faith: we hold it; it is necessary for our weakness. Think, then, of the love of that hen, which protects our weakness; think that it is the beast of burden of that merciful one passing by, onto which he lifted the sick man who had been wounded. For he lifted him, where? Onto his own beast. The beast of the Lord is the flesh. Therefore when this age has passed, what will be said to you? Because you rightly believed in the flesh of Christ, now enjoy the majesty and divinity of Christ. The weak one was necessary for the weak, the strong one will be necessary for the strong.

**6.** Because you too have to lay aside that very weakness, according to what you heard in the Apostle: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality.* Because *flesh and blood*, he says, *will not possess the kingdom of God*[^12]. Why will they not possess it? because the flesh will not rise? Far be it: the flesh will rise, but what happens? It is changed, and itself becomes a heavenly and angelic body. Surely the angels do not have flesh? But this is the difference, that this flesh will rise, this very flesh which is buried, which dies; this which is seen, which is handled, which needs to eat and drink, that it may be able to last; which grows sick, which suffers pains, this very flesh has to rise, for the bad to eternal punishments, but for the good that they may be changed. When it has been changed, what will happen? It will now be called a heavenly body, not mortal flesh: because *this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality.* But they marvel whether God makes a heavenly body from flesh, he who made all things from nothing. The Lord, set in the flesh, made wine from water, and is it a marvel if he could make a heavenly body from flesh? Do not, then, doubt of God, because he is able to do it. That the angels might exist, they were nothing; but by his majesty they are what they are. He who was able to make you when you were not, cannot he restore what you were, and cannot he give the honor of brightness to your faith because of his very incarnation? Therefore when these things have passed, there will come to us that which John says: *Most beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we will be: we know that, when he appears, we will be like him, because we will see him as he is*[^13]. For this vision prepare yourselves; meanwhile, as long as you are in the flesh, believe in Christ incarnate; and so believe that you do not think yourselves seduced by some falsehood. For the truth never lies: for if it lies, where do we go for counsel? what do we do? whom do we believe? Therefore the truth, the true Word, the true wisdom, the true power of God: *The Word was made flesh*, true flesh. *Handle, and see*, he says, *that a spirit does not have bones and sinews, as you see me to have.* For there were true bones, true sinews, true scars: true whatever was touched, true whatever was understood. The man was touched, God was understood: the flesh was touched, wisdom was understood: weakness was touched, power was understood. The whole true. Yet then the flesh went before into heaven, that is, the head. The other members will follow. Why? Because it is fitting that these members receive sleep for a while, and all rise in their own time. If the Lord too wished to rise then, there would be no one in whom we would believe. Therefore he willed to free for God in himself the firstfruits of those who sleep, that, when you saw in him what was restored, you might hope in yourself for what is to be granted. The whole people of God will be made equal to the angels and associated with them. Let no one, then, say to you, brothers: Foolish Christians believe that the flesh will rise: who rises? or who has risen? or who has come here from there, from the dead, and told you? Christ came from there. O wretched man! O perverse and backward human heart! If his own grandfather rose, he would believe him: the Lord of the world has risen, and he does not want to believe.

**7.** Hold, therefore, my brothers, the true, genuine, Catholic faith. The Son is equal to the Father, the gift of God the Holy Spirit is equal to the Father, and therefore the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God, not three gods: not added to one another by degrees, but united by majesty, and one God. But yet the Son for our sake: *The Word was made flesh and dwelt among us. He did not think it robbery to be equal to God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, and found in fashion as a man*[^14]. And that you may know, brothers, that this Trinity is truly equal, and it was not said that *the Father is greater than I*, except because of the flesh which the Lord took up; why is it never said of the Holy Spirit, He is less, except because he himself did not take up flesh? See what I have said: search all the Scriptures, lift all the pages, read all the verses; you will never find that the Holy Spirit is less than God. He, then, was called less, who for our sake was made less, that through him we might be made greater.
