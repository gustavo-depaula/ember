# Sermon 336: On the Dedication of a Church

**1.** The celebration of this gathering is the dedication of a house of prayer. So this is the house of our prayers, but the house of God is we ourselves. If the house of God is we ourselves, we are being built up in this world, that we may be dedicated at the end of the world. The building, indeed the building-up, has labor, the dedication has exultation. What was done here, when these things were rising, this is done now when those who believe in Christ are gathered. For by believing, timbers and stones are cut, as it were, from the woods and mountains: but when they are catechized, baptized, formed, they are, as it were in the hands of craftsmen and workers, hewn, lined up, planed. Nevertheless they do not make the house of the Lord, except when they are joined together by love. These timbers and stones, if they did not cohere with one another in a certain order, if they did not knit themselves peaceably, if by cohering with one another they did not in a certain way love one another, no one would enter here. Finally, when you see in some structure stones and timbers cohering well with one another, you enter secure, you do not fear collapse. So the Lord Christ, wanting to enter, and to dwell in us, said, as though building: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*[^1]. *A commandment,* he says, *I give you.* For you were old, you were not yet making me a house, you were lying in your ruin. So that you may be drawn out of the oldness of your ruin, love one another. Let Your Charity consider, then, that this house is still being built throughout the whole world, as was foretold and promised. For when the house was built after the captivity, as another psalm has it, it was said: *Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^2]. What there it said, *a new song;* this the Lord said, *a new commandment.* For what has the new song, but the new love? To sing belongs to the lover. The voice of this singer is the fervor of holy love.

**2.** Let us love, let us love freely: for we love God, than whom we find nothing better. Let us love him for his own sake, and ourselves in him, yet for his sake. For he truly loves a friend, who loves God in the friend, either because he is in him, or that he may be in him. This is true love: if we love for the sake of something else, we hate rather than love. For *he who loves iniquity*: what does he hate? perhaps his neighbor, perhaps his neighbor woman? Let him be afraid, *he hates his own soul*[^3]. Hatred of the soul, love of wickedness. So against the hatred of wickedness, love of the soul. *You who love the Lord, hate evil*[^4]. God is good, evil is what you love, and you love your own evil self: how do you love God, when you still love what God hates? For you heard that God loved us: and it is true, he loved us; and what sort we were when he loved us, if we attend to it, we blush. But we do not blush for this reason, because, loving us such, he made us to be not such. We blush at the recollection of past things, we rejoice in the hope of future things. For why should we still blush for what we were, and not rather be confident because by hope we have been saved? Finally we heard: *Draw near to him, and be enlightened, and your faces will not blush*[^5]. If the light withdraws, you return to confusion. *Draw near to him, and be enlightened.* So he is light, we without him are darkness. If you withdraw from the light, you will remain in your darkness: but if you draw near, you will not shine from your own: *For you were once darkness,* says the Apostle to the faithful from unbelievers: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^6]. If, then, light in the Lord, darkness without the Lord. Further, if light in the Lord, and darkness without the Lord; *draw near to him, and be enlightened.*

**3.** Attend in the Psalm of dedication, which we just sang, to the building from the ruin. *You have torn my sackcloth*[^7]: this pertains to the ruin. What then to the building? *And you have girded me with gladness*[^8]. The voice of dedication: *That my glory may sing to you, and I may not be pricked*[^9]. Who is it that speaks? In his words recognize him. If I expound, it is obscure. So I will say his words, you will at once recognize the speaker, that you may love the one addressing you. Who is it that could say: *Lord, you have rescued my soul from hell*[^10]? Whose soul has now been rescued from hell, but that of which it was said in another place: *You will not leave my soul in hell*[^11]? A dedication is set forth, and a deliverance is sung: the song of the dedication of the house is jubilated, and it is said: *I will exalt you, Lord, for you have upheld me, and have not made my enemies rejoice over me*[^12]. Attend to the Jewish enemies, who thought they had killed Christ, conquered him as an enemy, destroyed him as a man like the rest and mortal. He rose on the third day, and this is his voice: *I will exalt you, Lord, for you have upheld me.* Attend to the Apostle saying: *Wherefore God exalted him, and gave him a name which is above every name*[^13]. *And you have not made my enemies rejoice over me.* They indeed rejoiced in the death of Christ, but in his resurrection, ascension, preaching some were pricked. So in his preaching and the proclamation through the constancy of the Apostles some were pricked and converted, some were hardened and confounded; yet none rejoiced. Now when the churches are filled, do we think the Jews rejoice? The churches are built, dedicated, filled, how do they rejoice? Not only do they not rejoice, but they are even confounded; and the voice of the one exulting is fulfilled: *I will exalt you, Lord, for you have upheld me, and have not made my enemies rejoice over me.* You have not made them rejoice over me: if they believe in me, you will make them rejoice in me.

**4.** Not to say much, let us come at last to those things which we sang. How does Christ say: *You have torn my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness?*[^14] His sackcloth was the likeness of the flesh of sin. Let it not grow cheap to you, that he says, *my sackcloth:* there was enclosed your price. *You have torn my sackcloth.* We escaped to this sackcloth. *You have torn my sackcloth.* In the passion the sackcloth was torn. How then is it said to God the Father: *You have torn my sackcloth?* How it is said to the Father, do you want to hear? *You have torn my sackcloth.* Because *he did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all*[^15]. For he acted through the Jews not knowing, whence the knowing might be redeemed, and the denying confounded. For they do not know what good they have worked for us by their evil. The sackcloth was hung up, and the impious one as it were rejoiced. The persecutor tore the sackcloth with the lance, and the Redeemer poured out our price. Let Christ the redeemer sing, let Judas the seller groan, let the Jew the buyer blush. Behold Judas sold, the Jew bought, they did bad business, both were damaged, the seller and the buyer lost themselves. You wanted to be buyers: how much better would you have been redeemed? That one sold, this one bought: an unhappy transaction; neither does this one have the price, nor does this one have Christ. To this one I say: Where is what you received? To that one I say: Where is what you bought? To this one I say: Where you sold, you deceived yourself. Exult, Christian, in the transaction of your enemies you have conquered. What this one sold and that one bought, you have acquired.

**5.** So let our head say, let the head say, killed for the body, dedicated for the body; let him say, let us hear: *You have torn my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness*[^16], that is, you have torn my mortality, and girded me with immortality and incorruption. *That my glory may sing to you, and I may not be pricked*[^17]. What is, *I may not be pricked?* Let the persecutor no longer bear the lance against me, that I may be pricked: *for Christ rising from the dead dies no more, death will no longer have dominion over him: for in that he died, he died to sin once; but in that he lives, he lives to God. So too let us,* he says, *reckon ourselves to be dead to sin, but living to God, in Christ Jesus our Lord*[^18]. So in him we sing, in him we are dedicated. For where the head went before, we hope the members too will follow. *For by hope we have been saved: but hope that is seen is not hope; for what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^19], by patience we are built up. But perhaps there too is our voice, if we attend well, if we gaze diligently, if we have a keen eye; not as the carnal lovers of bodies are wont: if, then, we direct the spiritual eye, in the very words of our Lord Jesus Christ we find ourselves too. For not in vain did the Apostle say: *Knowing that our old man was crucified together with him, that the body of sin might be done away, that we may no longer serve sin*[^20]. Recognize there your voice: *That my glory may sing to you, and I may not be pricked.* For now, while we carry these burdens of the mortal body, there is no lack of what may prick us. For if the heart is not pricked, why is the breast beaten? When, then, the dedication of our body too comes, which went before in the example of the Lord, then we will not be pricked. For the pricking, which we have from sin, the lance of the striker signified. Finally, since it is written: *From a woman was the beginning of sin made, and through her all die*[^21]: recall from what member she was made, and see where the Lord was pricked with the lance. Recall, I say, recall our first condition: for not in vain, as I said: *Our old man was crucified together with him, that the body of sin might be done away, that we may no longer serve sin.* Surely Eve, from whom the beginning of sin was made, was taken, to be formed, from the side of the man. He lay sleeping, when it was done: he hung dead, when it was done. Two kindred things are sleep and death, side and side, the Lord was pricked in the place of sins. But from that side was made Eve, who would put us to death by sinning; but from this side was made the Church, who would give us life by giving birth.
