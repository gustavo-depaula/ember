# Sermon 22

**1** We have heard, and we have trembled at, what is foretold in the words of the psalm. For it says: *As smoke vanishes, let them vanish; as wax flows from before the fire, so let sinners perish from before the face of God*[^1]. I do not doubt, dearest brothers, that the hearts of all of you have been shaken, and that no one's conscience stood unafraid beneath these words. For who will boast that he has a chaste heart? Or who will boast that he is clean from sins? And so, when Scripture says, *As wax flows from before the fire, so let sinners perish from before the face of God*[^2], who would not tremble, who would not leap up in dread? What, then, do we do, and what hope is there for us? For these things are not sung in vain. Or rather, when the prophet says these things, does he wish them upon men, and not rather foresee what is to come? In the words, indeed, the figure of one wishing appears, but the foreknowledge of one announcing is to be understood. For just as certain things in the Scripture of the prophets are narrated as though done in the past when things to come are being foretold, so certain things are spoken as though by the wish of one desiring. But those who rightly understand what they hear recognize the vision of one foretelling. Now long before the birth of the Lord's incarnation these psalms were spoken and written down. Not before Christ as God, but before the birth of Christ from the Virgin Mary. For surely father Abraham lived long before King David, in whose time these psalms were sung. But *the Lord said: Before Abraham was, I am*[^3]. For he is the Word of God, through whom all things were made. And he, filling the prophets, foretold that he would come in the flesh. To his incarnation, moreover, belongs his passion. For he could not have suffered the things written in the Gospel except in the mortal and passible flesh which he bore. And there indeed we read how, when the Lord was crucified, those who crucified him divided his garments among themselves, and when they had found among them a tunic woven from above, they would not tear it, but cast lots for it, so that it might come whole to whomever it should come, because it signified the love that cannot be divided. These things, then, though in the Gospel they are now narrated as done, were narrated as deeds done and accomplished long before, many years earlier, in the psalm, when they were being foretold as things to come. *They have pierced my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones. They themselves looked and gazed upon me; they divided*, it says, *my garments among them, and over my clothing they cast lots*[^4]. All are spoken as past, and they are foretold as things to come. As, therefore, things to come are signified as done in words of past time, so in the figure of one wishing, the mind of one foretelling is to be understood. So too of Judas, the betrayer of the Lord, the prophet, as it were, wishes upon him what he foretells will come to pass. And of the Jews themselves: *Let their table*, it says, *become a snare, and a trap, and a stumbling block*[^5]. That this was foretold of them, the Apostle expounds without any doubt, just as the apostle Peter recalls what was foretold under the same figure concerning Judas.

**2** And not without cause are the things that are to come spoken of as accomplished. For to God they are so certain that they are already held as done. And the prophet seems to speak as one wishing what he certainly foresees will come, showing nothing else, so far as it seems to me, than that the known sentence of God, which he has established as fixed and immovable, ought not to displease us. And therefore in the *Acts of the Apostles*, when a certain prophet named Agabus foretold that the apostle Paul would suffer many things from the Jews at Jerusalem and would come even to chains, and when, on hearing this, the brothers wished to call him back and hold him so that he would not go there, he said: *What are you doing, troubling my heart? For I am ready not only to be bound, but even to die for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^6]. And so, when the brothers saw the man's unshakable constancy to endure all things, they said: *The will of God be done*[^7]. Surely, then, because they said, *The will of God be done*[^8], did they wish upon the Apostle that he should suffer such things, and not rather most devoutly submit their own mind to the sublime and divine decree? So too the prophet, when he says, *As wax flows from before the fire, so let sinners perish from before the face of God*[^9], sees most certainly that this hangs over sinners, and what God has decreed pleases him, lest he himself displease God.

**3** What, then, do we do, brothers, except that, while there is time, we change our life and correct our deeds if any of them are evil? So that what without any doubt is to come upon sinners may not find us among those upon whom it comes—not because we will not exist, but that it may not find us such as those upon whom it was foretold to come. For this reason the judge threatens that he will come, that when he comes he may not find any to punish. For this reason the prophets sing that, that we may be corrected. If he wished to condemn, he would keep silent. No one who wishes to strike says, "Watch out." All that we have heard through the Scriptures, brothers, is the voice of God saying, "Watch out." And all that we suffer, the tribulations in this life, is the scourge of God wishing to correct us, lest he condemn us in the end. Though they are hard, though they are burdensome, though they make us shudder when they are described—the grievous things each one suffers in this life—yet in comparison with eternal fire they are not small but nothing. Whether, then, we are scourged, or when others are scourged, let us be warned. All these things, brothers, that are inflicted by the Lord in this life are warnings and goads of our correction. But the eternal fire will come, of which it will be said to those who are to be set on the left: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^10]. Then indeed they will do penance. For it is written in a certain book of Wisdom: *They will say among themselves, doing penance and groaning in anguish of spirit: What did pride profit us, and what did the boasting of riches bring us? All those things have passed away like a shadow*[^11]. There will be penance there, but fruitless. There will be penance there, but having grief, not having medicine. Now penance is fruitful, while correction is free. May you do penance at the voice of Scripture. For at the voice of the present judge, your penance will be in vain. Then he is now about to pronounce sentence. And there will be nothing for you to reproach when he is about to pronounce sentence. For he did not keep silent before the sentence. For he did not put you off, except that you might correct yourself, since indeed he permitted the thief hanging on the cross to change. For then the thief hanging with the Lord believed in Christ, when the disciples wavered about him. The Jews despised him as he raised the dead; the thief did not despise him as he hung beside him on the cross. There is, then, no reason why in the end it should be said to the Lord: "You did not permit me to live well"; or "You did not give me a postponement for correction"; or "You did not show what I should desire, what I should avoid." See that he does not keep silent, see that he delays, see that he coaxes, exhorts, threatens. He has set his word on high. Throughout the whole world it is recited to the whole human race. There is now no one who may say, "I did not know, I did not hear." What is said in the psalm is fulfilled: *Nor is there one who can hide himself from his heat*[^12]. Now, then, his heat is in his word. Be changed now by his heat, and you will not flow away like wax from his fire.

**4** For that thing, my brothers, will one day come, which now the impious laugh at, which now the scoffers despise, which they think is sung falsely. One day it will come. If the many things that were foretold have not come, let us despair that it too will one day come. But if we now see fulfilled all the things that were foretold to come concerning the Church, and they strike even the eyes of the blind, why do we doubt that those things too will come? When it was said that the Church of Christ would be throughout the whole earth, it was said by few and laughed at by many. Now what was foretold so long before is already fulfilled. The Church is spread throughout the whole earth. A thousand years ago it was promised to Abraham: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^13]. Christ came from the seed of Abraham, and all the nations are now blessed in Christ. Schisms and heresies were foretold to come. We see them. Persecutions were foretold. They were carried out by kings who worshipped idols. For those very idols, against the name of Christ, the earth was filled with martyrs. The seed of blood was scattered, the harvest of the Church arose. Nor did the Church pray in vain for its enemies. Even those who persecuted it believed. It was also foretold that the very idols would be overthrown through the name of Christ, for this too we find in the Scriptures. A few years ago Christians read those things and did not see them. They still awaited those things as future and so departed. They did not see them, but believing nonetheless that they would come to pass, they departed in faith to the Lord. In our times those very things are now beheld. All the things that were foretold concerning the Church we see fulfilled. Is the day of judgment alone not to come? Is it alone foretold and yet will not come? Are we so hard and stony of heart that we read the Scriptures and see that absolutely everything that was written has come to pass to the letter, and yet despair of the things that remain? For how little is what remains, compared with the things we already see displayed before us? God has shown so many. Will he cheat us of the remainder? The judgment will come that will render to all according to their merits, good things to the good, evil to the evil. Let us be good, and let us await the judge in security.

**5** My brothers, listen to me now most of all as I speak. I do not wish to reckon up the past with you. From this day change yourself; let tomorrow find you another man. But we, in our perversity, want God so to be merciful that he is not just. Others again, as though presuming on their own righteousness, want him so to be just that they would not have him merciful. God shows himself both, he furnishes both. Neither does his mercy set a limit to his justice, nor does his justice take away his mercy. He is merciful and he is just. From what do we prove him merciful? He now spares sinners, he gives pardon to those who confess. From what do we prove that he is just? Because the day of judgment will come, which he now defers, but does not abolish. And when it comes, he will render according to merits. Or do you want him to render to those turned away what will be rendered to those turned toward him? Brothers, does it seem just to you that Judas should be put where Peter was put? He too would be put there, if he had corrected himself. But despairing of pardon, he rather bound his own neck than implore the clemency of the King.

**6** And so, brothers, as I had begun to say, there is no ground for us to reproach God. There will be nothing for us to say against him when he comes to judge. Let each one think over his sins, and now amend them while there is time. Let the grief be fruitful, let the penance not be barren. As if God says this: "See, I have indicated the sentence, but I have not yet pronounced it. I have foretold it, not fixed it." Why do you fear, because I said, "If you are changed, it is changed"? For it is written that God repents. Surely God does not repent as a man does? For it is said: *If you repent of your sins, I too will repent of all the evils I was going to do to you*[^14]. Surely God does not repent as one in error? But repentance in God means a change of sentence. And that change of sentence is not unjust, but just. Why just? The guilty one was changed, the judge changed the sentence. Do not be terrified. The sentence was changed, not justice. Justice remains whole, because the one who is just ought to spare the one who has changed. As he does not spare the obstinate, so he spares the one who has changed. He himself is the king of pardons, who is the giver of the law. He sent the law, he came with pardon. The law had made you guilty; the one who gave the law absolved you. Or rather he did not absolve you, for to absolve is to judge one innocent. Rather he forgives the sins of the one who has turned. For all are guilty who are entangled in their sins. Let no one wish to be absolved. Let us all beg for pardon. But pardon is given to the one who has changed. And we shall be secure, when we hear: *As wax flows from before the fire, so let sinners perish from before the face of God*[^15].

**7** Surely, brothers, let sinners now perish from before the face of God. Let sinners now perish, and let sinners not perish. If they begin to live justly, sinners will indeed perish, but men will not perish. "Sinful man"—these are two names. "Man" is one name, and "sinner" is one name. In these two names we understand that one of them God made, the other of them man made. For man God made, but a sinner man made himself. Why, then, do you tremble when God says to you, *Let sinners perish from before my face*[^16]? God says this to you: "Let perish in you what you made, and let what I made be preserved." And now the fire burns in the heat of the word, the matter is in the fervor of the Holy Spirit, as we said a little while ago, because it is written in another psalm: *Nor is there one who can hide himself from his heat*[^17]. And that the heat is of the Holy Spirit the Apostle says: *Fervent in the Spirit*[^18]. So, in place of the face of God, set before yourself for the time being the Scripture of God. Melt before it. May you do penance when you hear these things about your sins. But when you do penance, and when you torment yourself under the heat of the word, when even tears run down, are you not found like wax melting and, as it were, running down into tears? Now, then, do what hereafter you fear, and you will have nothing hereafter to fear.

**8** Only let me not vanish like smoke. For you have both set there, perhaps not without cause, because there is also a difference among sinners. In that one verse the psalm set both: *As smoke vanishes, let them vanish*, and: *as wax flows from before the fire, so let sinners perish from before the face of God*[^19]. Who are they who vanish like smoke? Who are they, except the proud, who do not confess their sins but defend them? Why are they compared to smoke? Because smoke raises itself, lifts itself up as toward heaven. But the higher it goes, the more easily it vanishes and disappears. Again, consider what I said. Smoke is more solid when near the fire and near the earth. It has not yet so vanished, it is not yet so scattered into the winds. But when is it thinned out and vanishes and disappears? When it has lifted itself up much. Because, then, the proud man so raises himself against God as smoke against heaven, it remains that he so vanishes and, scattered as it were into the winds by the elation of his vanity, perishes, just as smoke that has risen disappears, puffed up with a swollen, not solid, greatness. For such is smoke. You see a great mass. You have something, as it were, to see, and you have nothing to hold. Such a punishment, then, brothers, above all detest, that you not defend your sins. And if you still commit them, do not defend them. Submit yourselves to God. And so beat your breasts that even the sins that remain do not happen. Strive not to do them, and if it can be, do none. But if it cannot be that you do none, let that devout confession at least remain. For there will be a regard of his mercy, so that, as you strive to destroy them all and destroy as much as he has helped you, concerning the rest that remain to you on the way, he may easily forgive what is found and caught up in your effort. Only strive to advance, not to fall back. If the last day does not find you a victor, let it find you at least a fighter, not captured and made over to the enemy.

**9** But most abundant is the mercy of God and generous his benevolence, who redeemed us with the blood of his Son, when on account of our sins we were nothing. For he himself did something great when he created man to his own image and likeness. But because by sinning we willed to become nothing, and we drew the strain of mortality from our parents, and were made a mass of sin, a mass of wrath, it pleased him nonetheless out of his mercy to redeem us at so great a price. He gave for us the blood of his only Son, born innocently, living innocently, dying innocently. He who redeemed us at so great a price does not want those he bought to perish. He did not buy them to destroy them, but he bought them to give them life. If our sins overcome us, God does not despise his own price. He gave a great price. Yet let us not flatter ourselves so much on his mercy, if we have made no effort against our sins. Nor, if we have done certain things, especially capital ones, let us hope that his mercy will be such that iniquity may be joined to it. For surely those who have done nothing as to how they might live corrected, but remained in obstinacy and hardness of mind, and even accused God by defending their sins—will he set them where he set the holy apostles, the prophets, the patriarchs, and his faithful who deserved well of him, serving him, walking in chastity, modesty, humility, giving alms, forgiving what they suffered from anyone? For such is the way of the just, such is the way of the saints, who hold to God their father, who hold to the Church their mother. Offending neither that father nor this mother, but living in the love of both parents and hastening to the eternal inheritance—the father not injured, the mother not injured—the inheritance is given to each one.

**10** Since two parents begot us to death, two parents have begotten us to life. The parents who begot us to death are Adam and Eve. The parents who have begotten us to life are Christ and the Church. And the father who begot me, Adam was to me; and my mother, Eve was to me. We were born according to this lineage of the flesh, by the gift of God indeed—because this gift too belongs to no other but to God—and yet, brothers, how were we born? Surely, to die. Our forebears begot successors for themselves. Did they beget those with whom they might live here forever? No, like men about to depart, they begot those who would succeed them. But God the father and the Church the mother do not beget for this. They beget for eternal life, because they themselves are eternal. And we have the inheritance promised by Christ, eternal life. According to the fact that *the Word became flesh and dwelt among us*[^20], he was nourished and grew. Having suffered, died, and risen, he received the inheritance, the kingdom of heaven. In that very man he received resurrection and eternal life. In that very man he received it. But in the Word he did not receive it, because unchangeably he abides from eternity to eternity. Because, then, that flesh received resurrection and eternal life, which rose and, made alive, ascended into heaven, this has been promised to us. We await that very inheritance, eternal life. For the whole body has not yet received it, because the head is in heaven, the members are still on earth. Nor will the head alone receive the inheritance and the body be left behind. The whole Christ will receive the inheritance, the whole according to the man, that is, head and body. We are, then, members of Christ; let us hope for the inheritance. Because when all these things have passed away, we are to receive this good that will not pass away, and to escape this evil that will not pass away. For both are eternal. For he did not promise his own something not eternal, and threaten the impious with something temporal. As he promised the saints life, blessedness, the kingdom, an everlasting inheritance without end, so he threatened the impious with eternal fire. If we do not yet love what he promised, let us at least fear what he threatened.
